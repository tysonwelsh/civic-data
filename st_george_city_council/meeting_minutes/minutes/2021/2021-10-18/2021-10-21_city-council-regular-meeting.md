     (Agricultural) to PD-R (Planned Development Residential) on approximately
     39.81 acres located east of 3000 East Street and south of Merrill Road. Case
     No. 2021-ZCA-071

      Planner Carol Davidson presented the request to change the zone from A-1 to PD-R
      on approximately 39.81 acres located east of 3000 East Street and south of Merrill
      Road. She provided a PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics: Aerial
      maps; General plan map; Zoning map; Proposal; Site plan; Amenities; Parking;
      Landscaping; Access plan; Phases 1 and 2; Sign variation request; Renderings;
      Elevations; Planning Commission recommendation with the condition the applicant
      meet all other requirements. She noted all of these units will be rentals and will
      include garages. The developer plans to keep the corridor from the Temple to Merrill
      Road open.

      Councilmember McArthur commented this type of housing is needed in the City. He
      asked about the property to the south of this proposal and the height of the
      proposed buildings as he is concerned because most of the area are two-story
      buildings.

      Ms. Davidson explained heights are not being discussed tonight, plans will come in
      the future. She stated that she understood the concern with the height of the
      proposed buildings.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard noted this is already an incredibly busy area. He asked
      if the proposed access along3000 East matches up to a platted intersection on the
      west side.

      Assistant Public Works Director Wes Jenkins stated a traffic study is being done. He
      explained a traffic signal that would line up with Woodlawn Patio Townhomes is being
      proposed if the study shows one is warranted.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard commented that he does not see how a traffic signal
      would not be warranted with the amount of traffic already on the road. He feels the
      developer will need to participate. If a signal is going to end up being the access the
      developer is showing as part of this property, he would like the commercial to tie into
      that road.

      Assistant Public Works Director Wes Jenkins explained staff met with the developers
      who have indicated that they recognize it would be good to tie into the commercial;
      they are looking at options to address this. Staff will look at the traffic study to see
      what is indicated. He noted that the preliminary plat for the commercial property will
      be on tonight’s agenda.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard commented there are concerns about the traffic as 3000
      East is already extremely busy. With the developments they are proposing in the
      area, the traffic will increase. The proposed developments will likely only get one
      more access onto 3000 East. He would like the development to participate with the
      traffic signal. Additionally, he is concerned with the commercial development and
      getting to the traffic signal.

      Dan McCay with SLR noted they share the City Manager’s concerns with regards to
      access and will work with staff on the issues. They plan on having a traffic engineer
      provide data to develop the intersection in a way that does not inhibit traffic.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard asked staff at what point would these concerns be
      addressed so that the ability for much needed road improvements are not lost.

      Assistant Public Works Director Wes Jenkins suggested the approval include the
      condition that before the construction drawings are approved for the commercial, it
      is determined what would trigger the signal if one were required.

      Mr. McCay noted they are committed to work out this issue with staff.

AMEND CITY CODE/ORDINANCE:
    Consider approval of an ordinance amending 10-7B-1 and 10-17A-3 of city
    code to clarify provisions regarding accessory dwellings units (ADU's)
    within a home's footprint as required by changes to the state code. Case
    No. 2021-ZRA-009

      Planner Dan Boles presented the request to amend 10-7B-1 and 10-17A-3 of city
      code to clarify provisions regarding accessory dwellings units (ADU's) within a
      home's footprint as required by changes to the state code. He provided a
      PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics: ADU definition; Single-family
      dwelling; Current use table; Proposed use table; 10-17A-3 Accessory dwelling unit –
      specific standards (current); and 10-17A-3 Accessory dwelling unit – specific
      standards (proposed). He noted the state legislators separated detached and
St. George City Council Minutes
October 21, 2021
Page Eleven

      attached structures. The legislators did not regulate detached structures.
      Additionally, the changes in state code did not amend heights for ADU’s. City code
      10-17A-3(2)(d) states: “The maximum height of a detached accessory building
      containing an accessory dwelling unit shall not exceed the height of the single-family
      dwelling on the property or exceed twenty-five feet (25') in height, whichever is
      more.”

      Councilmember Hughes commented that he does not always like to restrict
      homeowners; however, at times the ADU is much taller than the existing home.

      Councilmember McArthur agreed and noted that the City takes pride in the
      downtown area. He would like to see the ADU’s match the existing home.

      Mr. Boles noted the language in this section (10-17A-3(2)(d)) could be changed to
      “whichever is less”, rather that “whichever is more”. Making this change would keep
      the ADU from being larger than the main structure.

      Councilmember Hughes mentioned in some instances there may be a walkout
      basement or a slope lot where the ADU can start out 10’ below a home.

      Community Development Director John Willis explained in that case the
      measurement would be an average of the two (2) tallest sides. This could cause the
      ADU to be taller from the home from the street.

      After being asked by Councilmember McArthur, Mr. Boles outlined the standards for
      an attached ADU.

      Councilmember Curtis inquired if an applicant could apply for a special consideration
      if there are exceptions if the language is changed.

      Mr. Boles explained the ordinance would have to specify that.

      Community Development Director John Willis explained changing the language as
      suggested would make the ADU the same scale as the house. Setbacks would be the
      same as the primary house. There may be some scenarios where the ADU may be
      taller, but not too much when taking the averages.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard commented there are a number of circumstances that
      could arise.

      Community Development Director John Willis added was included in the proposed
      ordinance to say that the ADU cannot be bigger than the main house.

      MOTION:
          A motion was made by Councilmember Curtis to approve the
                    ordinance amending 10-7B-1 and 10-17A-3 of city code to clarify
                    provisions regarding accessory dwellings units (ADU's) within a home's
                    footprint as required by changes to the state code with the
                    modification to reword 2(d) to read “The maximum height of a
                    detached accessory building containing an accessory dwelling unit shall

                    exceed twenty-five feet (25') in height, whichever is less.”

PRELIMINARY PLAT:
     Consider approval of the preliminary plat for Palermo Pass at Divario, a 189-
     lot residential subdivision located within the Divario development at the
     intersection of the future extension of Alienta Drive and the future
     extension of Divario Canyon Drive.

      Assistant Public Works Director Wes Jenkins presented the preliminary plat for
      preliminary plat for Palermo Pass at Divario, a 189-lot residential subdivision located
      within the Divario development at the intersection of the future extension of Alienta
      Drive and the future extension of Divario Canyon Drive. He showed and outlined the
      preliminary plat. The developer will be required to install a sewer line down the
      future extension of Divario Canyon Drive and to construct the extension of Alienta
      Drive from the proposed subdivision to the intersection of Alienta Drive and Gap
      Canyon Parkway. Additionally, the developer will be dedicating property to the City
      for a community park. There will be double fronting lots, with landscape strips and
      privacy walls that their master HOA will maintain, on the road that leads up to the
      water tank. He noted the cul-de-sac area will need to meet City standards.

      A brief discussion took place regarding four (4) lots that seem to stand alone on
      Alienta Drive near the wash.

      Councilmember McArthur asked about trail systems and if the canyon is still
      accessible for recreation.

      Mark Teepen noted Gap Canyon will stay in its current condition and use.
St. George City Council Minutes

      Councilmember McArthur noted this area is worthy of preserving. He feels the
      homes that stand alone seem out of place for what is in the area.

      After being asked by Mayor Randall about the purpose of these stand alone lots, Mr.
      Teepen explained the development agreement was done years ago. The agreement
      laid out all of the parcels and identified the areas that were to be preserved. There
      is a 100’ cliff line setback included in the agreement. These lots are the only ones
      that can be built and stay out of the 100’ setback, protecting the area for its use.
      The areas between the lots adjacent to the road that will provide access can be
      landscaped with benches and parking stalls. Their intention is to keep the area as
      pristine as it has been.

      Councilmember Curtis concurred that these lots don’t seem to fit the rest of the
      proposed subdivision although he understands they may be within the development
      agreement.

      Councilmember Hughes agreed that these lots don’t fit in and look weird on the plat.

      Mr. Teepen stated the lots can be moved to the west, but that would make them
      encroach onto the 100’ setback. He noted they are constrained by an existing
      waterline, utilities, and the 100’ setback.

      Councilmember McArthur commented he does not want to have them encroach. This
      request wouldn’t be heavily scrutinized if this wasn’t a part of southern Utah that is
      worth protecting.

      After being asked by Councilmember Hughes, Mr. Teepen stated there is nothing
      proposed between the four (4) lots to the intersection to the south. Their intention is
      to provide trails and access to the canyon.

      Mayor Randall asked if the Council could approve the plat without the four (4) lots;
      Deputy City Attorney Paula Houston replied yes, however staff would have to review
      the development agreement as the developer would have a right to adjust for the
      density.

      Mr. Teepen stated he understands the concerns. They would like to keep the
      recreation area the way it has been, improving the area.

      Design Engineer Clayton Nelson explained in looking at the map, these lots would be
      pretty far away from Gap Canyon. What is being proposed is not much different
      than what is at the top. They are trying to keep the lots as close to 10,000 sq ft as
      possible.

      Mr. Teepen commented he is more than happy to work with the Parks Department to
      determine what amenities they would like to see on the cliff edge.

      Councilmember McArthur stated because of where this is, he would like to see the
      four (4) standalone lots be moved or if there is a way to make adjustments if the
      development agreement allows.
