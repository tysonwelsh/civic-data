  REPORT ANALYSIS

The applicant requests review and recommendation of proposed amendments to Chapter 26 changing the requirements
for Detached Accessory Dwelling Units. This includes reducing the lots size and restricting them on steep slopes.
There were also several DRC comments with opinions on different ideas.

Reduced Lot Size
The current Code requires a minimum lot size of 15,000 square feet, which is above the 14,520 square feet in a third
of an acre. City Council indicated their interest in changing the size to actually reflect third acre lots. This minor
change will not greatly increase the number of lots that qualify for a detached ADU.

Steep Slopes
City Staff has received several calls from residents of Traverse Mountain wanting to add a Detached Accessory Dwell-
ing Unit on their lots with steep slopes. Steep slopes increase danger to homeowners and their property. Planning Staff

also fears that Detached ADUs will not include the same level of engineering and grading work as developers do for
whole subdivisions, presenting an even greater risk than what normally exists in these locations. State Code does not
require cities to allow detached ADUs, so the City has full discretion in the requirements for them.

Again, Planning Staffs’ main concerns are the safety concerns that allowing these would cause. And if property engi-
neered to ensure safety, the cost will make the detached ADU very expensive, countering the goal of housing afford-
ability. The proposal is to allow detached ADUs on slopes less than 20 percent. A grading plan would be required on
slopes between 10 and 20 percent.

DRC Comments
The Power department had two comments
    1. Provide sufficient off-street parking.
    2. Grading plan on slopes greater than or equal to 5%

Currently we require two off-street parking spots for detached ADUs. We only can require one for internal ADUs.
The power department recommends we evaluate if that provides sufficient off-street parking.

Power also recommended that grading plans be required for anything with a slope equal to or greater than fiver percent,
instead of the ten to twenty percent proposed in the amendment.

Engineering had one comment
3. This section b) should not be added to the code. The "less than 20 percent" restriction seems arbitrary, and is an
    infringement upon the property rights of an owner. Just like with the construction of a home on a lot, the Build-
    ing Department requires adequate design submittals and inspections to ensure that DADu's are designed and
    constructed properly and with proper grading, drainage, etc.

The City can legally restrict detached ADUs, and especially on steeper slopes. The Planning Commission should
consider if detached ADUs should be allowed on any slope or only in flatter areas.

                          Detached Accessory Dwelling Unit Narrative

City Staff has started to receive calls from residents living in Traverse Mountain wanting to build
detached accessory dwelling units (DADUs) in their backyards. The challenge is many of these
areas include steep slopes. Any time a steep slope is graded back and retained there is an
increased potential for landslides, foundation failures, and other impacts to the homeowners and
surrounding property owners. There is heightened risk when any home is built, but especially
with DADUs where homeowners might do more of the work on their own or just hire a building
construction crew and not an earthwork/grading crew. To reduce this risk, Planning Staff
recommends prohibiting DADUs on slopes over 20 percent, and requiring a grading plan for
slopes between 10 and 20 percent.

The amendment also includes an update to change the size of the lot from 15,000 to 14,520
square feet. This is a change to exactly a third of an acre size lot.

Section 26.080. Architectural Considerations.
(New 11/18/08)
Exterior building materials including roofing and
architectural designs and colors used for accessory
buildings and structures shall be comparable to, or
architecturally harmonious with, the existing main
residential structure.

Section 26.090. Handicapped Access.
(Amended 11/28/06)
Uncovered handicapped access ramps are required to
comply with the provisions of Section 12-020(E)(1).
Covered handicapped ramps or any other covered
facilities for handicapped access that encroach into
required setback and yard areas may be authorized by
the Planning Commission as a Conditional Use by
following the requirements and procedures of
Chapter 9.

Section 26.100. Accessory Uses.
(Amended 10/14/03)
Tennis courts are required to maintain a minimum
setback from side and rear property lines of fifteen
feet and must not be located within any required
utility easements. Swimming pools are required to
maintain a minimum setback from side and rear
property lines of four feet as identified in Section
12.020(E)(2) and must not be located within any
required utility easements.
