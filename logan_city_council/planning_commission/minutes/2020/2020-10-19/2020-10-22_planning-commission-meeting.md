submitted. Motion seconded by Commissioner Croshaw. The motion was approved unanimously.

 PC 20-051 100 West Apartments – continued from October 8, 2020 [Design Review Permit]
 Justin Campbell-Cartwright/Gene Needham, authorized agent/owner, request to construct a
 55,460 SF 4-story apartment building with 37 2-bedroom units over a 42,480 SF 2-story parking
 structure with 74 parking stalls located on .60 acres at 162 South 100 West in the Town Center
 (TC) zone; TIN 02-046-0019.

STAFF: Ms. Rice outlined the new site plan and elevations as requested by the Commission at
the last meeting. The updates include:
  - Refuse storage located on the south side of the property as shown.
  - Designated bike parking to accommodate 37 bikes in the southeast corner of both levels of
      the parking structure as shown.
  - Revised setbacks to 5’ around the perimeter of the building except the front (west), showing
      4’ from the property line (3’ in front of architectural metal panels).
  - Architectural concrete detail identified as tilt-up concrete panel (precedent image shown).
  - Delineation of the three levels of the building to break up the bland wall flatness with vertical
      architectural detail and bump-outs, added vertical windows, and material variation.
  - The number of resident parking stalls remains at 74 to reflect the 37 2-bedroom units.
  - Covered pedestrian entrances.
  - Parking structure entry gates with keypad locations.

PROPONENT: Justin Campbell, the project architect, said the fenestration requirements have
been met. The parking structure will have finished concrete. Commissioner Newman asked about
the screening requirements. Mr. DeSimone said it is being required on the west side, the minimum
wall height will be 42” which will screen vehicle lights. Mr. Campbell said he has maximized the
parking within the constraints of the site. The Code allows for more density, but that cannot be
reached because appropriate parking cannot be achieved. He feels there is adequate parking for
the number of units proposed. Commissioner Newman said, because this is the first project like
this in the Town Center (TC) zone, he wants to ensure that the parking requirement is met so there
is no precedent set for future projects.

PUBLIC: Debbie Treasure is in favor of the goal to revitalize downtown. She did not have many
concerns with the Millcreek development, however, that development added to this development
will create many new homes in a small area and traffic will greatly increase. She is concerned with
safety. The footprint of the building seems quite large. Parking will be an issue, especially during
high school events. She asked if this will be low-income housing. She lives in the Woodruff
Neighborhood, which is mostly rental homes. She is concerned about the type of people who
could live near a high school and asked how this might affect property values in the area. She
would like to see how Millcreek does before this project begins. She appreciates the blighted area
being cleaned up.

Nyla Adams travels the area every day and says it is already busy and this seems like too much in
such a small space. Developers generally do not live where they develop so they do not have to
live with the consequences. The idea of revitalizing Logan is a good, but she is concerned with
this specific site. The amount of visitor parking seems too low.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Ortiz asked about the bicycle parking locations. Mr. Campbell said
the areas where the bike parking is located make sense.

Mr. Campbell confirmed for Commissioner Goodlander that it will be a gray brick. Buildings with
lighter toned colors feel less oppressing.

Mr. Campbell clarified for Commissioner Croshaw that there will be 37 2-bedroom units with no
studio apartments.

Commissioner Lucero asked how this project relates to the cultural connection to bolster downtown
as a hub. Mr. Campbell said density plays a big part in bringing people downtown. The elevated
outdoor terraces provide a nice atmosphere, as well as other amenities which encourage people to
socialize.

Chairman Nielson thanked Mr. Campbell for moving the building back from the sidewalk.

Mr. DeSimone said he can approve up to a 15% variation to parking with a submitted Alternative
Parking Plan.

Mr. Campbell requested the condition “Screen parking structure openings on the north and south
elevations with architectural metal panels, louvers, or remove windows” be deleted because of
the height of the openings which will screen vehicle headlights.

Chairman Nielson asked about a possible street median for right-in/right-out access. Mr.
DeSimone said it can be discussed with the Engineering Division.

Chairman Nielson appreciated the public comments. He reminded citizens that it is often difficult
for the Commission to review these projects and make appropriate conditions.


Commissioner Newman lives on this street and appreciates the idea of a possible median or
other traffic control measure. Mr. DeSimone noted that traffic will increase. 100 West will
eventually go south, across the river and connect to where McDonald’s is. The focus for traffic
and parking should be for this project and what can be done to mitigate and/or offset some of the
potential. The Commission discussed TC areas for quite some time because these areas feed
into downtown, there is a recognition of the balance between higher density land uses relative to
what adjoins it. The focus should be on what the Code allows. The discussion should be
narrowed down to specific issues for this project.

MOTION: Commissioner Goodlander moved to conditionally approve a Design Review Permit as
outlined in PC 20-051 with the amended conditions for approval as listed below. Commissioner
Ortiz seconded the motion.

 PC 20-052 Foothill Subdivision [Subdivision Permit] Nic Porter/Foothill Lofts Residential LLC,
 authorized agent/owner, request a 25-lot single-family subdivision located on 8.37 acres at
 approximately 1300 East 1300 North in the Neighborhood Residential (NR-4) zone; TIN 05-011-
 0005.

STAFF: Mr. Holley reviewed the request for a 25-lot single-family subdivision. The square-shaped
property slopes slightly downward from east to west and sits between older single-family homes to
the east and the Foothill Lofts Apartments to the west. To the south, sits two churches and partially
vacant land is to the north. The proposal has lot sizes ranging from 10,004 SF to 14,680 SF. The
layout creates an inner block with an outer perimeter of lots accessed by a looping 60’ wide road.
The proposed layout shows two street connections onto 1300 East.

This 8.37-acre piece was part of the 19.36-acre property that was historically used for gravel
extraction in association with the Legrand Johnson Construction Company. The split zoning
designations of Mixed Residential Medium (MR-20) and Suburban Neighborhood Residential (NR-
4) was established in July of 2017 by Logan City Council Ordinance 17-17, approving 9.0 acres of
MR-20 (180 dwelling units) and the remaining ~10 acres as NR-4 single-family residential zoning.
With the construction of the Foothill Lofts Apartment complex on the 9 acres and the street
dedication of 1300 East, the remaining 10-acre NR-4 area became 8.37 acres in size. This area
was approved for a 19-lot single-family subdivision with 60’ streets and 10,000 SF minimum lots
sizes in September of 2018 and has since expired. Most recently, the Logan City Council denied a
rezone request from this applicant from NR-4 to NR-6 on July 7, 2020. The Future Land Use Plan
(FLUP), within the Logan City General Plan, identifies this property as Detached Residential (DR)
which are described as single-family home developments.

PROPONENT: Nic Porter thanked staff for all their help. Originally the idea was for student
housing due to the proximity to USU, however, when that was not an option, a rezone to NR-6 was
submitted and subsequently denied by City Council, so this plan has been developed. The spirit of
three (3) connections has been provided as proposed. A connection on the northeast corner
makes sense, but the adjacent neighborhood opposed that idea. This design seems to work with
all the site challenges. Density is not being maximized, but the lots and area are very nice. He
disagrees with the sidewalk. Traffic will not be an issue because of the number of lots.

PUBLIC: Marc Leager lives just over the fence. He provided a brief history of the parcel. He is
disappointed with the proposal. He would like the Commission to consider the five lots in question,
and which way they will face. He thanked the Commission and staff for all the work.

Jack Peterson appreciates the Commission considering how this project impacts the area. This is
yet another attempt to alter the rules and set a precedent for. It appears that staff has shifted a
critical decision from their responsibility to the Planning Commission, rather than follow Logan’s
own rules, standards and specifications. On page 3 of the Staff Report the issue is outlined “The
Planning Commission will need to determine if the proposal of five lots along the west side are
serviced from 1300th and should be considered separately from the other 20 lots along the looping
street”. Table 2 on page 2 of the report indicates that the minimum number of street connections
by the size of a development greater than 20 should be three (3) connection points. The proposed
25 units require three connections, if this is changed, a precedent will be set. He spoke with
Bracken Atkinson, the prior owner of the property, who wanted additional lots but was unable to
make it work because of the access points and subsequently sold the property. Now, the new
owner is applying for something not allowed. Decisions should be consistent. He encouraged the
recommendation that there should only be 20 units with two (2) street connections.


COMMISSION: Commissioner Lucero asked for clarification on the Code section relative to
condition #3 “… determine if the proposed five (5) lots along 1300 East are being served by 1300
East street or not.” Mr. Holley said it is covered by:
                Table 17.30.170.E.2: Minimum Number of Street Connections by Size of
                                         Residential Development
               Number of Dwelling Units Served                          Number of Connections
                           Greater than 20                                            3
                                9-20                                                  2
                                 1-8                                                  1
               Figure 2 shows the LDC Street Connection Requirements for Residential Developments

Commissioner Goodlander asked if there are any structures on the other side of 1300 East. Mr.
Holley said it is planned for open space.

Mr. Holley confirmed for Commissioner Croshaw that lots 7 and 8 are corner lots. The lots are
large enough to accommodate quite a few different house plans. Commissioner Croshaw asked
how the lots will be addressed. Mr. Holley said the addresses will be assigned based on the front
of the home.

Chairman Nielson asked if this will be senior housing. Mr. Porter said that is still undecided, but he
thinks it may not be appropriate due to the larger lots.

Chairman Nielson said there are challenges with getting another access through the area. If 20
units is the cut-off; that is how it should be approved. He does not want to see an exception made.

Commissioner Croshaw would like three of the lots (except for the corner lots) oriented to 1300
East.

Commissioner Goodlander noted that there have been several different projects proposed on this
site. When it was rezoned previously, a 19-lot layout was presented, which seemed to be a good
compromise.

Commissioner Ortiz said the five lots in question should not be separated from the entire project
(25 lots proposed). He would like street connections better defined, but that is for a future
discussion.

MOTION: Commissioner Croshaw moved to conditionally approve a 20-lot Subdivision Permit as
outlined in PC 20-052 and as conditioned by staff. The motion died for lack of a second.

Commissioner Lucero said the idea that this is below the potential density does not justify the
reduction for street connections. She has a difficult time imagining homes facing either way as they
are both undesirable options. The Commission needs to determine if the proposal meets the Code
requirements. 1300 East should not serve only five homes.

Mr. Housley, the City Attorney, clarified that the number of street connections is not tied to a
subdivision, but to the “number of dwelling units served”. The Commission must determine what
“units served” means (e.g. 20 units on the loop road and five units on 1300 East, and whether
these should be considered different services). Every subdivision has an adjoining subdivision, so
a perimeter needs to be defined. He believes it is based on where the access is taken. A home
can be oriented one way with a driveway coming off another way.


Commissioner Newman said although this is a good proposal, it does not seem like the right way
to go about it. He would like to better understand what the Commission is hoping to achieve
before a decision is made. This seems to be a 25-unit subdivision with different lots being served
by two different streets. Mr. DeSimone explained that 20 lots can be proposed (with the western
section left open) which would meet the Code having two points of access. Five additional lots
could then be proposed, fronting onto 1300 East and can technically be approved. The question
and concern will be to ensure that those five lots will not impact future design. This can be done
procedurally with one action or it can be done in multiple steps to get the same outcome.

Chairman Nielson understands the concept but is frustrated that the Code can seemingly be
manipulated to get to the desired outcome. Mr. Holley explained that the difficulty with this project
is dealing with the existing area and constraints. The goal of the Code is to essentially connect
neighborhoods. In this instance, there is no way to add more connections.

Jack Peterson expressed frustration that a motion was about to be made and then was influenced
by Staff who began discussing alternatives. He said the idea that the Hillcrest neighborhood was
blamed for not putting in a stub is ludicrous. He gets upset when a peripheral group attacks,
challenges and/or manipulates a situation.

Commissioner Croshaw said he appreciated staff’s clarification of the issue.

Mr. Housley pointed out that the Fire Code requirements must be followed, after 30 units,
additional accesses are required. Chairman Nielson asked why the minimum number of units does
not match 30. Mr. Housley said it used to be 30 but was changed.

Commissioner Lucero asked for a better definition of “access” and “units served”. She can
envision a scenario where larger lots could be designed to fit within the “units served” definition.
She cannot see how having frontage on 1300 East, or within the center of the block, makes any
sense for all the reasons that have been discussed.

MOTION: Commissioner Newman moved to deny PC 20-052 as proposed. Commissioner
Goodlander seconded the motion.
Moved: D. Newman Seconded: S. Goodlander        Approved: 4-2

 PC 20-053 Dominion Energy ILI Receiver Facility [Conditional Use Permit] Kevin
 Mulvey/Questar Gas Company dba Dominion Energy Utah, authorized agent/owner, request an
 in-line inspection facility that will be operated once every 6-7 years for a 1-2-week period to
 inspect the natural gas lines in the area on .91 acres located at 450 South 300 West in the
 Neighborhood Residential (NR-6) zone; TIN 02-057-0011.

STAFF: Ms. Rice reviewed the request for an unmanned in-line inspection (ILI) facility consisting
of a 4’ x 5’ x 3’ above ground pigging facility, mainline valve assembly and 12” x 16” receiver
barrel. The facility operates 1-2 straight weeks every 6-7 years to inspect the natural gas lines in
the area. A 24’ drive lane will circle the facility to be used as an access road during inspections.
The property will be fenced for security with locked double-swing gates at the entrance to 300
West. The proposed location of the facility is on a .91 vacant parcel on 300 West near the Cache
Valley Fairgrounds in the NR-6 zone. The parcel abuts residential housing to the south and east
and the fairgrounds to the west. The property is relatively flat. The proposed location for ILI facility
is to the south of an existing small Questar Gas facility and shed


PROPONENT: Kevin Mulvey was present to answer questions

PUBLIC: Corey Hanson has property adjacent to the site, Kim Standard owns the adjacent
building and there is a 6’ fence to the property line with barbed wire on top which is an eyesore.
He is not opposed to the proposed development; however, he has been using the property for the
past few years for his horses. He questioned whether operations will be in the evening; Mr. Mulvey
said it will not, it will only be operated once a year and confirmed that .91 acres was purchased
(pointed out the area on the plat map).

COMMISSION: Chairman Nielson asked about fencing. Mr. Mulvey said security has become
more of a priority over the years, he is proposing a 7’ fence and does not like the idea of barbed
wire.

Commissioner Newman said a fence with slats, allowing a visual, might mitigate possible problems
if someone were to enter the area. Mr. Mulvey said many sites have gates in the front for that
reason, they are willing to comply with what the Commission deems appropriate.

Commissioner Lucero would like to see a solid surface fence with a gate.

    and the Municipal Code.
