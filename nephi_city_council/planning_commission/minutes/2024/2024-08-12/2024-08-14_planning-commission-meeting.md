2. Considering a vicinity (concept) plan for the Rollin’ Home Subdivision parcel
#XA00-2588-12, located at approximately 2888 S Four Mile RD (575 W). This is a
phased subdivision with one lot being created containing an existing RV Park
business, and two remainder parcels that will be deed restricted prohibiting
development until future subdivision occurs.

a. Staff Presentation
Mrs. Shauna Talbot presented a concept plan of Phase 1 (Plat A) of the Rollin
Home subdivision. The development consists of one parcel # XA00-2588-12, and
is located at approximately 2888 S Four Mile RD, and includes approximately
57.29 total project acres with around 12.58 acres in phase 1 (one lot). Chad Leevy
would only like a portion of the property, which is why the current parcel would
need to be divided. The development is in the HC1 (Highway Commercial 1)
zone. This phase is a one (1) lot subdivision with two remainder parcels left
behind for future phases of subdivision. The lot that will be created already has
an established RV business on it with some installed infrastructure.

The developer and landowner are working with the city to get a development
agreement to allow them to split the property into 3 parcels, which are deed
restricted, until the full subdivision process has been completed, which must
happen within an agreed upon time frame. This would allow the developer to
exchange property ownership expeditiously. The agreement would also include

3. Public Hearing: Considering a request from landowner Howard Chuntz to change
the zoning code in the R2-8 zone to allow for a per unit requirement of 5,000 sq ft
for twin homes or duplexes (the current requirement is 6,400 sq ft per unit).

a. Staff Presentation
Presenter: Shauna Talbot, Staff Presenter
Mrs. Shauna Talbot presented that the city has received a request from Howard
Chuntz, a developer in the community who has some 3-plex areas in Nephi City.
He would like to talk to the Commission about making a proposal about having a
zoning change. Currently, in this zoning it is 6,400 sq ft.

Staff suggests the Planning Commission recommends denial of the zoning code

b. Public Hearing
Mr. Howard Chuntz presented photos that currently show what he is proposing to
the city for the zoning adjustment. There is left over area, but the problem is
under the current requirements there isn’t enough room to build another unit. He

Chairwoman Ann Peterson inquired about changing that lot to a R-3 zone. Is that

clarified that yes it would be allowed in an R-3 zone, but it has been the desire of
the city to not spot zone. An R-3 zone could be useful, but that it would need to be
strategically done; but spot zoning leaves behind a lot of patchwork and isn’t
conducive to the zoning plan. More could be done by grassing this area, or
something that could benefit the residents of the area. The interpretation the staff
has received in the past from the commission and the community is that by
leaving these areas it makes it feel more rural.

areas not be consistent with piles of dirt and weed patches for the community. But

Mr. Seth Atkinson: Presentation of the Staff Report. I will cover some of this. In
the prior staff report from June. We discussed how Nephi was a perfect example

5. Discussing the possibility of a short-term rental ordinance.
a. Staff Presentation
Mrs. Shauna Talbot presented that this is a draft of a proposed City Ordinance
that was brought forward from the Staff. We have had many questions about
short-term rentals, there are many opinions on it. We are trying to do it flexibly;
we will primarily be concerned about parking. There are not a lot of restrictions
currently in this proposal. Some are occurring, this is a way to make this legal.

Rentals will be less than 30 days, they will need to pay taxes, and have a business
license, they would have the right to advertise on a website. With no ordinance
this is illegal, but with this we would know how many, how much taxes, to help
regulate. They are happening anyway, so this would like to have this on the books
and control.

There isn’t a limit on how many in the city, but someday they may. One of the
concerns is big companies coming in and buying housing and controlling the
availability for residential purposes. We are not a destination area currently, but
we would like to watch it. If there is proliferation of this, then we can amend the
code and limit it.

The expectations no IA-U, no mobile home, no accessory building such as a shed
with living space above. So, any CU-I and CU-2.

They will need two parking spots from the start, and then one for each room. So,

if you have 8 rooms you will need 10 spots off-street parking. It is forcing people
to be reasonable. Has to be rented one day in the month per year to qualify. Non-
compliance will be Class C as per our code.
