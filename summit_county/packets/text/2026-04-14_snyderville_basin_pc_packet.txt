Proposal 
The purpose of this work session is to  gather input from the Planning Commission on the overall 
direction, clarity, and intent of Chapters 7 and 8 of the draft General Plan.  
 
Background 
The Planning Commission has begun its review of the Draft Snyderville Basin General Plan. On 
February 10, the Commission reviewed Chapters 1 and 2 and provided direction to staff. The purpose 
of this work session is to continue that discussion and receive feedback on Chapter 7: Open Space, 
and Chapter 8: Community Health and Recreation.  
 
Chapter 7- establishes policy direction for the preservation, management, and long-term 
stewardship of open space within the Snyderville Basin. The chapter recognizes open space as a 
defining feature of the Basin’s character and quality of life, supporting wildlife habitat, watershed 
protection, recreation opportunities, and scenic resources. Policies emphasize protecting 
interconnected open space systems, encouraging compact development patterns that reduce 
pressure on sensitive lands, and coordinating conservation efforts with recreation planning and 
agricultural preservation. The chapter also highlights the importance of partnerships with land 
trusts, public agencies, and private landowners to maintain and expand space resources in a 
manner that supports environmental sustainability, community identity, and long-term economic 
vitality. 
 
Chapter 8 - considers how our built environment affects our physical, mental, emotional, and 
social health. The Element strives to influence built environments in ways that foster good health 
outcomes.  This Element is supplemented by the Summit County Rail Trail Corridor Plan, which 
specifically considers recreation along the Historic Union Pacific Rail Trail. The Rail Trail Corridor 
Plan will be an appendix to this General Plan. 
 
Recommendation 
Staff recommend the Planning Commission review the proposed Amendments to Chapters 7 and 8 
of the Snyderville Basin General Plan and provide staff with direction.

Chapter 7: Open Space 
 
The Open Space Element establishes policy direction for the preservation, management, 
and strategic use of open space within the Snyderville Basin. Open space plays a critical role 
in protecting natural systems, supporting recreation, maintaining community character, and 
guiding growth patterns. 
 
This Element supports the County’s broader land use strategy by encouraging compact 
development in appropriate locations while preserving signiﬁcant natural landscapes, 
wildlife habitat, and agricultural lands. Coordinated planning and long-term stewardship of 
open space help protect water resources, maintain scenic qualities, and provide meaningful 
recreational opportunities for residents and visitors. 
 
Context 
 
Open space is a deﬁning feature of the Snyderville Basin and plays an essential role in 
preserving the area’s environmental health, scenic character, and recreational identity. The 
Basin contains a network of protected lands, natural landscapes, and recre ational areas 
that support wildlife habitat, watershed protection, and year-round outdoor activities. These 
lands also contribute signiﬁcantly to the Basin’s quality of life and economic vitality. 
 
Preserving connections between open space areas is critical to maintaining ecological 
function, supporting wildlife movement, and providing accessible recreational 
opportunities. Open space also serves as a buffer between developed areas, helping 
maintain neighborhood identity and limiting the spread of low- density development into 
environmentally sensitive lands. 
 
Open space in the Snyderville Basin generally includes lands preserved for conservation, 
recreation, agricultural use, and environmental protection. These lands may be publicly 
owned or privately protected through conservation easements, deed restrictions,  
development agreements, or subdivision requirements. 
 
Conservation Lands : These lands, while designated as open space, are also legally 
protected by governing documents that ensure ongoing permitted uses of the land and 
prohibition of others.  Conservation Lands  are protected by, including but not limited to, a 
conservation easement, a deed restriction, a land use covenant, or can be encumbered by 
a development agreement or subdivision plat dedication. Along with legal protection, 
Conservation Values are used to deﬁne the ecological, social, or cultural signiﬁcance of a 
natural area.  The “Conservation Values” describe the importance by underscoring the 
unique biodiversity, habitats, ecosystem services, and/or other signiﬁcant features that 
contribute to the ov erall health of the landscape;  essentially, they represent the worth of a 
natural area in terms of its conservation potential.  
 
4
Qualifying Open Space for Development: Qualifying Open Space refers to a designated area 
of land within a development project that is considered suitable for preservation as open 
space, often meeting speciﬁc criteria like environmental sensitivity, recreational value, or 
aesthetic qualities,  and can be used to fulﬁll local zoning requirements for open space 
dedication within a new development. 
 
Recreation Types: 
 
Passive Recreation: Refers to recreational activities that do not require prepared facilities 
like sports ﬁelds, pavilions, or improvements like trails. Passive recreational activities place 
minimal stress on a site’s resources; as a result, they can provide ecosystem service beneﬁts 
and are highly compatible with natural resource protection.   Examples of Passive Recreation 
include camping, hunting, observing and photographing nature, picnicking, swimming, 
hiking, walking, and wildlife viewing.  
 
Active Recreation: Refers to recreation activities that are structured for individual or team 
activity and that require the use of special facilities, courses, ﬁelds, or equipment. These 
activities are primarily physical and are done for enjoyment, health, or relaxation. Examples 
of Active Recreation include hiking and biking trails, sports ﬁelds, swimming pools, 
playgrounds, basketball courts, tennis courts, dog parks, and community gardens. 
 
Non-Motorized Recreation: Non-Motorized Recreation deﬁnes a means of travel that does 
not use an engine, or motor, to propel movement. Areas designated as non-motorized may 
allow for travel, such as by foot, horse, or bicycle. 
 
Motorized Recreation: Motorized Recreation deﬁnes a means of travel that allows for an 
engine, or motor , to propel movement. Areas designated as motorized may allow for travel 
such as by vehicle, off-highway vehicle (OHV), motorcycle, electric bicycle or scooter.  
 
Existing Conditions 
 
A substantial portion of land within the Snyderville Basin is preserved as open space through 
public ownership, conservation easements, development restrictions, and other legal 
protections. These lands support wildlife habitat, watershed protection, agric ultural uses, 
and recreational opportunities.

generally considered undevelopable and are managed to preserve environmental, scenic, or 
agricultural values.

Community Engagement  
 
Public input consistently identiﬁed open space as one of the community’s highest priorities. 
Residents expressed strong support for protecting natural landscapes, wildlife habitat, and 
scenic views while maintaining access to recreational opportunities. 
 
Key themes from community engagement include: 
 
• Many respondents identiﬁed open space as land preserved primarily for wildlife 
habitat and environmental protection.  
• 83% of respondents indicated that open space is important for protecting 
ecosystems, native plants, wildlife, and water resources.  
• Residents value open space for both environmental beneﬁts and proximity to 
neighborhoods and daily recreation opportunities.  
• Preservation of open space was identiﬁed as one of the top long -term concerns for 
the future of the Snyderville Basin.

OBJECTIVE D: Comprehensively consider future growth and development needs when

Strategy 7.1.1: Consider creating overlay zones to offer incentives for 
preserving open space in predetermined areas.  
 
Policy 7.2: Create additional zoning districts that provide more variety in allowable 
densities and development patterns.  
 
OBJECTIVE F : Ensure an adequate amount of open space is required for all new

Strategy 7.3.1: Analyze the current open space requirements in the

Policy 7.4: Offer development incentives for preserving more open space than 
required and for preserving open spaces adjacent to existing or planned open space.  
 
Strategy 7.4.1: Establish clear guidelines for the types of incentives available

incentives might include density bonuses, expedited permitting processes, or

OBJECTIVE G : Consistently coordinate with non -proﬁt organizations dedicated to

Policy 7.5: Establish and maintain cooperative partnerships with non-proﬁt

Policy 7.6: Ensure that open spaces are protected and maintained in ways that are

resources, cultural resources, scenic resources, etc.) 
 
Strategy 7.7.1: Create a future conservation lands map to help connect open 
space preserved through new development. Be considerate that the open 
space map does not inﬂate or hinder the acquisition of open space by the

OBJECTIVE H : Create consistent deﬁnitions of for different types of open spaces and 
identify how different types should be prioritized. 
 
Policy 7.8: Create a County-wide open space management plan that distinguishes 
the types of open spaces, how the protection of those open should be prioritized and

Strategy 7.8.1: Update the Development Code deﬁnitions for open space so 
they are consistent with the deﬁnitions in the Context section of this Chapter.

Policy 7.9: Work with non -proﬁt organizations that conserve open space to ensure

Policy 7.10: Ensure that conservation easements, deed restrictions, trail easements,

OBJECTIVE I: Ensure that open space is appropriately owned and maintained.  
 
Policy 7.11: Update the Development Code to require open space management

Policy 7.12: Ensure open space management plans are publicly available and

Policy 7.13: For open space preserved through new development, create standards

Policy 7.15: Establish and maintain cooperative partnerships with local land trusts, 
other public and private entities, or qualiﬁed land conservation groups and non-
proﬁts to acquire and maintain open spaces.

Chapter 8: Community Health and Recreation 
The Community Health and Recreation Element consider s how our built environments 
affect our physical, mental, emotional, and social health. The Element strives to influence 
built environments in ways that foster good health outcomes.  
This Element is supplemented by the Summit County Rail Trail Corridor Plan, which 
specifically considers recreation along the Historic Union Pacific Rail Trail. The Rail Trail 
Corridor Plan is an appendix to this General Plan. Other related and supporting plans 
include: 
• Summit County Active Transportation Plan 
• Snyderville Basin Recreation 5-Year Trails and Open Space Plan 
• Summit County Health Department Community Health Assessment 
• Summit County Health Department Community Health Improvement Plan 
Context 
Recreation is a cornerstone of life for many and is a significant tourism attractor. The terrain 
and vistas of Summit County provide ample opportunities for hiking, mountain biking, skiing, 
snowboarding, and fishing. Destinations like Park City Mountain Re sort and the expansive 
trail system in the Snyderville Basin contribute significantly to the local economy and 
lifestyle. Increasing recreation demand and tourism has led to concerns regarding 
overcrowding, trail degradation, and environmental impacts, hig hlighting the need for 
sustainable management and the preservation of these resources.  
 
Recreation has been described as vital to the physical and mental wellbeing of the 
community. Basin residents have a strong preference for improving walking and biking 
options. Improved pedestrian pathways, bike lanes, and a connected system of parks and 
open space can support an active lifestyle and foster connections between neighborhoods.  
Most recreation programming and infrastructure in the Basin is managed by the Snyderville 
Basin Special Recreation District, which has a very strong presence. Residents also have the 
Park City Recreation facilities nearby. Ensuring that all communities have equitable access 
to recreation infrastructure and a sense of belonging within these activities is crucial.  
There are also disparate opportunities and access to health and recreation infrastructure 
based on income. Those living in affordable housing often live in overcrowded conditions. 
Other factors related to  income inequality have compounding effects on health outcomes.  
Land use decisions regarding transportation, housing, and amenities should 
simultaneously support and improve community health. With development pressures 
9
increasing, it is important to ensure that future growth incorporates elements that support 
healthy, active living. 
Existing Conditions 
The 2024 Summit County Community Health Assessment (CHA), utilized both quantitative 
and qualitative data collection, including surveys, focus groups, and secondary data 
analysis, to present a holistic picture of the community's health landscape. Summit County 
residents identified key strengths and health priorities that were used in the development of 
the Community Health Improvement Plan (CHIP). The CHA data and the CHIP will guide the 
Health Department’s efforts over the next five years and work in tande m with the goals and 
objectives of this general plan for priorities that are outside the focus of public health. 
Community Strengths: 
● Summit County's abundant access to parks, trails, and outdoor recreational facilities 
contributes positively to physical and mental well-being. 
● Community cohesion and active civic engagement were cited as strengths, with 
residents expressing optimism about the county’s resilience and capacity for 
innovation in addressing health challenges.

Top Health Priorities: 
• Affordable Housing: The lack of affordable housing emerged as the most significant 
issue, impacting residents' financial stability, mental health, and access to care. 
• Healthcare Access: While Summit County scored high in healthcare coverage, 
challenges remain in accessing culturally appropriate care, mental health services, 
and treatment for substance use disorders. 
• Behavioral and Mental Health: Mental health services are in high demand, with

• Chronic Diseases: Heart disease, cancer, and unintentional injuries are the leading 
causes of death. Cancer incidence is notably high, with melanoma, prostate, and 
breast cancer rates above state averages.

Community Health and Recreation Goals, Objectives, Policies and Strategies 
The Snyderville Basin Recreation Special Service District is the County entity responsible for providing and 
programming recreation in the Basin. For up-to-date lists of facilities, trails, future recreation 
development and goals, please refer to the Snyderville Basin Special Recreation District Trails Master 
Plan, Trails and Open Space Plan, and Strategic Action Plan.  
The Summit County Health Department oversees efforts to sustain and improve factors that influence 
health outcomes in Summit County. Detailed plans for how the health department will partner with 
community organizations to prioritize health, please see the 2026-2030 Community Health Improvement 
Plan (CHIP). The three priority areas for the 2026-2030 CHIP are: (1) Strengthen Partnerships to Impact 
Community Health Drivers; (2) Improve Food and Nutrition Access for an Evolving Community; and (3) 
Increase Immunization Coverage to Reduce Preventable Disease.

Analysis
 and Findings 
Per Section 10-3-18.D of the Snyderville Basin Development Code, the County Council is the 
land use authority for plat amendments that alter a public road shown on a subdivision plat, 
after receiving a recommendation from the Planning Commission. As such, the 
Planning Commission may 
recommend approval, approval with conditions, or denial of the 
amendment based on written findings of fact, in accordance with the following 
standards. It is the responsibility of the applicant to provide written and graphic 
evidence demonstrating compliance:    
1. If the co
unty manager or land use authority is satisfied that neither the public nor any
person will be materially injured by the proposed vacation, alteration or amendment ,
and that there is good cause for the vacation, alteration, or amendment, the county
manager or land use authority may vacate, alter or amend the plat, any  portion of the
plat, or any road or lot.
Analys
is:  There is good cause for the amendment as the propos al would formally
amend the Silver Creek Estates Unit I Block 9 Lot A Amended plat to reflect the as -built
relocation of Earl Street and a portion of Silver Creek Road  and dedicate them as public
rights of way. Staff finds no evidence that the public or any person would be materially
injured by the amendment. COMPLIES
2. No pla
t amendment shall be approved that results in an increase in density.
Analysis: The amendment would not result in any additional density. COMPLIES
