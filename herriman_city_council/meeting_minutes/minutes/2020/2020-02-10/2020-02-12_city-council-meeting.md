Public Works Director Edwards introduced Riley Peck with the Division of Wildlife Resources (DWR). He
indicated staff is requesting direction to move forward as there are options available with this recreational
amenity including: do nothing, not allow hunting within City Limits, or regulate the discharge of the weapons
through an Ordinance.

Mr. Riley Peck indicated he oversees the wildlife programs in the central unit of the state which creates some
unique scenarios. He explained the Division sought at incorporating an extended archery hunt in the area to
coincide with Herriman’s Urban Deer Program. He observed natural habitual tendencies of deer and noted the

natural pressure of the program keeps the animals higher in the South Hills. He shared the same concern of

    2.4. Outline of the Beef Hollow Conditional Use Permit History - Michael Maloy, City Planner
City Planner Michael Maloy informed the Council of the former land use regulation that allowed sand and/or
rock removal as an interim use which the Council found that because of the reasonably anticipated detrimental
effects of the removal on the City, surrounding neighbors and adjacent land uses, may be approved if certain
conditions are imposed to mitigate or eliminate such detrimental effects. In 2012, this interim use was removed
from City Code and mineral extraction was considered a prohibited use. He acknowledged incidental uses of
a development would be considered including the removal of dirt in a practical way from the site as depicted
in Development Agreements or other vested laws were granted for Wasatch. They had asked for additional
grading for a development within specified plans and allowed a limit of disturbance.

City Planner Maloy presented the primary exhibit and offered a history of the land use applications relating to
sand and/or rock removal as an interim use in the South Hills area. In 2011, Staker Parsons applied with the
Utah Division of Oil, Gas and Minerals (DOGM) showing future expansion outside of their current
conditional use permit limits of disturbance as approved by Herriman City. The City contacted DOGM to
inform them the City no longer allows this type of use, and to honor the limits set forth by the City. In 2019,
GOGM presented Staker Parsons with a letter indicating the “approval or acceptance of a complete notice of
intention does not relieve an operator from his responsibility to comply with the applicable statues, rules,
regulations, and ordinances of all local, state and federal agencies with jurisdiction over any aspect of the
operator’s mining operations. Specifically, the following condition applies: Surface disturbance at the Beef
Hollow quarry is limited to the “Current and Future Mining Area” as shown on Figure 7 of the Notice. Staker
Parsons Companies must also comply with all requirements of Herriman City’s May 29, 2009, conditional user
approval letter included in Appendix L.”

City Engineer Blake Thomas offered an explanation of the approved Master Site Plan for the Conditional Use
Grading Plan. The approved plan does not extend beyond the limits of disturbance and identifies potential
road alignments. He presented a cross section for the proposed large mining area and highlighted significant
cuts location within the area. In 2018, Sunrise Engineering was hired to conduct an analysis of the current
conditions as requested by the City Council. It was found only a small area had been over excavated outside
of the approved limit of disturbance.

City Planner Maloy indicated Staker Parsons has not applied for an expansion or design. City Manager Wood
added that the Council at the time was concerned the operation was expanding beyond the limit of disturbance
that had been approved in 2009. In conclusion, the issue had been resolved with the exclusion that some
reporting could be requested. The conditional use would continue to be monitored to ensure compliance.

    2.8. Herriman Business Park Community Development Area Review - Alan Rae, Finance Director
Finance Director Alan Rae noted the Herriman Business Park Community Development Area (CDA) was
created by the City at the request of Momentum Development and Rosecrest Communities in 2014 with
revisions conducted in 2016 and 2017. In 2017, the plan was revised to allow the City to be reimbursed by the
Tax Increment Fund (TIF) for the land donated to Wasatch Developers because the original plan did not allow
for reimbursement of land. A budget amount of $6,000,000 and land was added as an acceptable
reimbursement to the project area. The City also waived the impact fees on the RSL training facility which was
anticipated to be reimbursed to the City with the TIF.

The plan was for water storage, water lines, storm drain lines and roads to be installed. The estimated cost for
improvements were set at $65 million. The original estimate was the CDA would receive $42.9M from TIF
property taxes and the estimate would need to be reduced by $3.27 million due to the City withdrawing from
the Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area.

Director Rae reported the amount of revenue is approximate, and each Interlocal Agreement would have caps
on the amount of funds the CDA would receive. The higher the value of construction would reduce the amount
of time until all of the money has been received. Current reimbursable costs have been set at $32,944,862.
There has not been a verified amount as of yet and no funds have been distributed. The trigger date of the
CDA was dictated by the Interlocal Agreement with Salt Lake County and commenced in 2018.

6. Public Hearing
    6.1. (Continued from January 22, 2020) Public Hearing and discussion related to hunting on City
         Property and within City Limits - Justun Edwards, Public Works Director
Public Works Director Justun Edwards offered a background of hunting within city limits and the increased
desire for people to conduct this type of recreation in the area. Previously, the City Council decided not to
make a decision at that time and to monitor the hunting season to identity concerns that would need to be
addressed. They also wanted to allow enough time to receive public input. Director Edwards identified
concerns that had surfaced during the 2019 hunting season.

He presented the available options the Council could consider moving forward including maintaining a status
quo, prohibit hunting within city limits, or adopt an ordinance regulating the discharge of weapons. He
observed different buffer proposals for the Council to consider moving forward.

Mayor Pro Tempore Jared Henderson opened the Public Hearing.

Kathryn Fuller informed the Council she resides on Rose Canyon Road and relayed her concern of the lack of
education as to where hunters are allowed to hunt. She opined hunting was not a compatible use with the other
recreational uses being conducted in the area.

Patricia Stanko noted she lived in Hi Country, and suggested too many tags for the area had been released by
the Division of Wildlife which, essentially, has led to hunters trespassing onto private property.

Steve Slessinger explained he has actively sought solutions for this amenity to exist and recommended the
Council utilize the trail extension as the hunting boundary as it would be clearly identifiable for everyone.

Councilmember Smith moved to close the Public Hearing. Councilmember Ohrn seconded the motion, and all voted aye.

Councilmember Smith expressed his appreciation to those who spoke on the matter as the Council has been
looking for ideas to consider. He highlighted that hunting has always been part of the City and the Council is
working to find the correct balance to preserve the recreational sport.

Councilmember Ohrn noted the challenge was to figure out how to work collectively to ensure every
recreational amenity is available, including hunting. She recalled the extensive work meeting discussion, and
noted it was important to take all feelings into consideration. The Council believed they had the same
expectation as the Division of Wildlife, to ensure maps were correct and for hunters to be educated that Hi
Country was out of bounds.

Councilmember Shields reiterated appreciation to those individuals who spoke regarding this item. He
indicated he would not support trespassing or any illegal conduct, and stated that hunters have a responsibility

    8.1. Discussion and consideration of a request to rezone 4.53 acres of property located at 13494
         South 7530 West for Steven Ohlwiler from A-1 (Agricultural) to A-.25 (Agricultural, 10,000
         SqFt minimum) (File No. Z2019-092) - Michael Maloy, City Planner
City Planner Michael Maloy relayed the Planning Commission recommendation to approve the rezone request
for Mr. Steven Ohlwiler with a zoning condition of 1.9 units per acre, and the lots along the west side of the
property shall be one-half acre lots. He informed the Council of the density range within the zone to be 1.8-
2.5 units per acre, with a method outlined to which a property owner could reach the desired density. The
rezone request would be incompliance with the General Plan. If the applicant did not have a zoning condition,
the maximum possible lots in the development would total 14 if all of the criteria had been satisfied. If the
Council places the 1.9 zoning condition on the property, the developer could yield nine lots.

Applicant Steve Ohwiler addressed the Council and requested the Council consider raising the zoning
condition to 2.0 units per acre to get the development to ten lots. He raised a concern of a discrepancy of the
parcel acreage being, and would look to ensure the error was rectified. City Planner Maloy informed the Council
this issue would be resolved through the application process where actual parcel numbers would be utilized.
He noted the applicant requested a straight A-.25 zone, and noted the Council would be at liberty to amend
the zoning condition if desired.

Councilmember Smith relayed his willingness to consider, based on the criteria presented, the zoning condition
of 2.0 to allow the property the ability to determine the actual acreage. He stated he would not be in favor of
allowing more than ten lots in the development. Councilmember Shields concurred. Councilmember
Henderson highlighted the recommendation was not based on total acreage, and would consider the tenth lot.
