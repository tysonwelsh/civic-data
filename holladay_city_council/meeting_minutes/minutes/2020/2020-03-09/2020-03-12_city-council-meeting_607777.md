IV.    Consideration of Resolution 2020-13 Authorizing the Issuance of Sales Tax Revenue
       Refunding Bonds; and Related Matters.
Council Member Gibbons asked if the resolution pertains to the refinancing of a bond or the
issuance of a new bond. Alex Buxton from Zions Bank addressed the Council and confirmed that
the above matter involves the refinancing of an existing bond that was issued by the City in 2011.
The purpose of the refinance was to lower the interest rate and save tax dollars and interest. The
savings to the City was estimated to be $175,000.

Fotheringham, Quinn, Gibbons and Mayor Dahle in favor. Resolution 2020-13 was approved by a

V.       Consideration of Ordinance 2020-04 Adopting Amendments to Title 9.48-Noise
         Ordinance.
Jonathan Teerlink, City Planner presented the proposed amendments to the City Noise Ordinance.
He reported that the City of Holladay adopted the Salt Lake Valley Health Department’s Title 21 as
it relates to regulating types of noise intrusions into and across property lines. The current Holladay
Ordinance refers to a 7:00 a.m. start time and the proposed amendment changes the start time to
8:00 a.m. throughout the Ordinance for the allowance of construction noise. A second amendment
prohibits power-related equipment operation between the hours of 10:00 p.m. and 8:00 a.m. The

VI.     Consideration of Resolution 2020-14 Amending the Personnel Policy.
Manager Chamness introduced the proposed resolution and stated that the City was recently audited
by Utah State Retirement System (“URS”). The audit resulted in the City becoming aware of a
State law requiring the City to develop a list of positions that can be exempt from the URS system.
The City of Holladay will need to exempt two possible positions that include elected or appointed
positions. The recommendation was to exempt the City Manager position and a Council position
that was elected before July 1, 2011, to maintain a specified pool size. The proposal was to exempt
a future City Manager and not Ms. Chamness.

Ms. Chamness also discussed URS implications for officials elected after July 1, 2011. She asked
the Council for direction on the employer match contribution for Council Member 401(k) plans.
Mayor Dahle did not think the employer match should be allowed for Council Members but also
thought this is a matter that should go to a public hearing. The 401(k) contribution for elected
officials is a City of Holladay benefit and is not required by URS.

City Council Member Gibbons asked to ensure that the Council was not violating any conflict of
interest ethics laws. City Attorney, Todd Godfrey did not think it was a violation of the Ethics
Code for the Council to vote on this matter because no action can be taken without the vote of the
Council.

Council Member Fotheringham moved to approve Resolution 2020-14 Council Member Petersen
seconded the motion. The Council roll call vote was as follows: Council Members Petersen,
Fotheringham, Quinn, Gibbons and Mayor Dahle in favor. Resolution 2020-14 was approved by a
unanimous vote.

IX.     City Manager Report – Manager Chamness.
Ms. Chamness reported that she attended a presentation by the Governor of Utah earlier during the
day outlining steps to reduce the spread of the Coronavirus. The goal was to slow the rate of
transmission and keep the health system operating efficiently. The City was keeping in close
contact with its partners at the State and County level and will take the lead from them. The
Governor asked that all mass gatherings over 100 people be ended. Accordingly, the City has
canceled various events that would be in violation. The City of Holladay has also considered
declaring a state of emergency but decided not to recommend that now. Staff was working on a
plan of continuity in the City and working through challenges with how that will look.

Many staff functions can be completed from home and there will be more developments on staff
telecommuting later. The City Council is required by State law to meet once a month, but that can
be postponed if necessary. The ordinance also allows Council Meetings to be held remotely but it
does require an anchor location and at least one member present in City Hall. The public still has to
have the opportunity to participate. The City was looking at several options and developing steps to
move forward. The Planning Commission cannot meet remotely so that will be reviewed in the
next several days. In the immediate term, they will be working to delay items with applicants where
staff expects there to be a large number of the public wanting to be present.

The Mayor prepared a statement to communicate with residents that will assure them that the City is
following guidance from State and County partners to keep them safe as well as direct residents to
pertinent information.

Mayor Dahle reported that all public gatherings that involve ride-a-longs or precinct tours have
been canceled. An outline of emergency personnel guidelines and procedures was provided.
Mayor Dahle commended the Governor and Lieutenant Governor in the steps taken to limit the
spread of Coronavirus. He reiterated that the best defense to the virus is to contain it and limit the
spread. Discussions about the continuity of City business was ongoing. Mayor Dahle read the
statement that will be posted on-line to the Council. He complimented the actions being taken by
City staff in addressing the challenges. It was mentioned that cities that declare a State of
Emergency may be doing so because they have internal emergency services and need to access
FEMA funds. Holladay contracts for those services and so access to FEMA funds are not needed at
this time.

Council Member Fotheringham complimented the Mayor on the letter that will be posted. He
encouraged staff to continue to exhibit a calm demeanor.

        a.      Policy Discussion on Accessory Dwelling Units (“ADUs”).
Paul Allred, Community Development Director identified Accessory Dwelling Units (“ADUs”) as
an additional topic to be discussed by the Council. He explained that the Council needs to
determine what the procedures and processes will be. Mr. Allred presented the Council with a chart
comparing the use of ADUs and Detached Accessory Dwelling Units (“DADUs”) in other
jurisdictions. The Council discussed potential standards regulating ADUs including the relation of
the footprint of the primary residence or lot size and parking requirements.

Mr. Allred asked for direction from the Council on regulations for staff to develop a draft
ordinance. He suggested tying ADUs to the lot size of the subject property. Discussion ensued
regarding the Council’s preference for direction in drafting an ordinance.

        b.     Discussion on Proposed RI Zone.
Mr. Allred addressed the Council regarding a new zone to regulate in-fill housing similar to small
cottage type homes in the Residential In-Fill (RI) zone. The lots are 4,000 square-foot minimum
with single-story homes. An RI zone would be similar to an R1-5 zone. Council Member Petersen
commented that an RI zone might support the City’s compliance with SB-34. She commented that
