2. Administrative Reports
    2.1. Discussion relating to the proposed development on the Game Pointe property
         – Tami Moody, Assistant City Manager
Assistant City Manager Tami Moody reminded the Council of the Game Pointe project and
offered an overview of the entertainment facility timeline. The developer requested to
extend the repurchase agreement which was approved until January 2023 with a plan from
the developer brought to the City by October 2022.

Applicant Aaron Osmond offered insight indicating the team had plans ready to present just
prior to COVID which caused many setbacks including primary financing. Since this time,
another entertainment facility was constructed in West Jordan and one in Bluffdale causing
them to start over. Mr. Osmond offered an overview of the conceptual design including
restaurant pads, retail pads, and a small office Space. Developer Jared Osmond anticipated
this as a commercial use and wanted the facility to be aesthetically pleasing with a good mix
of tenants to ensure the development thrives. A large tenant like Game Pointe could work;
however, if it didn’t, it would place a large empty building in the Automall area. The area
currently had no anchor tenant, but the WinCo was down the street. The development team
looked to get conceptual approval from the Council to move forward.

Councilmember Shields asked for clarification about what would work for the development
team. Mr. Osmond explained busy roads and the anchor tenant would offer visibility to the
smaller businesses in the development. He noted he has a following for tenants from
previous projects he had been a part of.

Councilmember Hodges asked about the entertainment portion of the development.
Councilmember Henderson opined the City wanted an anchor tenant and suggested the
proposed development was similar to what was already in the community. Mr. Aaron
Osmond suggested a large anchor tenant would not perform well in the recession and noted
October 12, 2022 – City Council Minutes                                            Page 3 of 15


it could take years for a large tenant to become interested in the site. Councilmember
Shields recounted it was easier to fill 1,500-3,000 square foot buildings with tenants rather
than a single 10,000-15,000 square foot building. He specified a percentage of the area
should be dedicated to the entertainment industry.

                     Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn arrived at 6:02 p.m.

Mr. Aaron Osmond expressed the desire to show the Council the team was making process
and was committed to moving forward. Councilmember Ohrn expressed concern the parcel
was originally automall until the developer convinced the Council to consider an
entertainment center adjacent to the automall. She indicated the City was missing the
entertainment destination and the anticipated Game Pointe development filled the vacancy.
She relayed concern of veering from the original intent. Councilmember Shields responded
the conflict was concept vs. market conditions and he appreciated the effort behind the
alternative thinking. There should be some discussion about a potential 10,000 square foot
building to be part of the development. Mr. Jared Osmond mentioned he has a shooting range
tenant who would like to place him in a development. Mayor Palmer detailed the developer
brought a unique portfolio to the community and would like to see the variety of
entertainment options the project could offer.

Councilmember Shields specified the need for an unique attractant, not just entertainment.
He wanted to achieve the big draw to feed other businesses in the community and the
Council wanted assurances there would be a mixture of tenants. The Council understood
the pressure of the expiring contract and were amenable to extend the deadline slightly to
make things happen.

Mr. Aaron Osmond expressed their anxiousness to get the project up and going. It was
determined to bring it back to a future meeting for Council feedback.

    2.2. Discussion regarding driveway improvements to property located at 13633
         South Rose Canyon Road (6400 West) – Blake Thomas, Community Development

improvements for the property located at 13633 South Rose Canyon Road. He observed the

    2.3. Discussion pertaining to Pioneering Agreements – Todd Sheeran, City Attorney
City Attorney Todd Sheeran gave a brief explanation of what a pioneering agreement
included. The City and developer would sign an agreement for future developers to pay a
October 12, 2022 – City Council Minutes                                            Page 4 of 15


portion of public road cost. The City would charge a fee to the future developer and pay a
portion back to the original developer. The future developer could dispute these charges
which could hold up development and incur additional costs to the City. Attorney Sheeran
indicated the City currently does not have a policy to cover pioneering agreements and
recommended not pursing them. The Council agreed.

   2.4. Discussion of permitted and conditional land uses within the proposed Auto
        Mall, North of Miller, Crescent, and Garden Plots Master Development
        Agreements with Herriman 73 Partners LLC – Michael Maloy, City Planner
City Planner Michael Maloy presented the land use tables for the C-2, M-1, and AutoMall
zoning designations and wanted to change the list of permitted uses in the Automall district.
The development team for the Automall requested to allow those uses within the
development. In previous discussions, the uses would be conditional use with an
administrative review process.

City Planner Maloy identified prohibited uses including schools and churches. Staff inquired
if there were any other uses which would be deemed inappropriate. Council questioned
what kind of uses would be appropriate for flex spaces within the Garden Plot and Crescent
Piece development parcels. The developers listed ten uses including restaurants, office,
vehicle-equipment, repair-rental and storage, telecommunication facilities, light
manufacturing, wholesale and warehouse, retail, and flex space. Staff proposed to remove
vehicle storage yard and generic storage space. Councilmember Henderson cited anything
required to go through an administrative review process had the possibility of being
appealed.

Councilmember Shields voiced the conditional use was concerning as it did not outline
mitigation impacts for residents. The residents would not have a say in any conditional uses
and Councilmember Shields did not want their input excluded during the process. Director
Thomas mentioned other cities post a notice to the community highlighting the project
administrative review request and encouraged them to contact staff with concerns.
Councilmember Ohrn pointed out an internal review by staff could take a few weeks and
noted the process could take months going through Planning Commission. Councilmember
Shields affirmed a possible compromise to give citizens a voice would be to bring up their
concerns by using the appeal process.

Councilmember Shields acknowledged Council could not use the master development
agreement process to strip away commercial uses. Council recounted uses which would be
permitted and prohibited in the area.

9. Public Hearing
    9.1. Public Hearing and consideration of the creation of a proposed Public
         Infrastructure District and accompanying Governing Document for the
         Herriman Auto Mall – Blake Thomas, Community Development Director
Director Thomas oriented the Council as to the location of the Public Infrastructure District
(PID) for the Automall project which included the Crescent Piece, North of Miller, Garden
Plots and the Automall parcels. He provided the key elements of the PID which would allow
the developer to place a tax on the property to collect and issue bonds to gain the funds to
build the infrastructure necessary for the project. Adjacent parcels are proposed to be
annexed into the PID boundary, if acquired by the developer. He further discussed the
infrastructure improvements including Auto Row Drive and presented the anticipated
phasing plan for the infrastructure schedule. The method to generate revenue for the bond
repayment would be .010 per dollar of taxable value on property through the mill levy rate
and each bond issued would mature in 31 years.

Mayor Palmer opened the public hearing.

There were no comments offered.

Councilmember Shields moved to close the public hearing. Councilmember Henderson
seconded the motion, and all voted aye.

Councilmember Hodges asked for clarification of the parcels which may be included in the
future. Director Thomas explained the parcels would not be incorporated into the PID
boundaries if not purchased by the developer.

Councilmember Shields moved to approve Resolution No. R28-2022 providing for the
creation of the Auto Mall and retail public infrastructure district as an independent body
corporate and politic; delegating to certain officers of the City the authority to execute and

City Planner Michael Maloy oriented the Council of the proposed areas for consideration
and relayed the original intent of the development was to create an auto mall area being
comprised of new dealerships. The owner requested a text amendment within the AMSD

dealership within a 10-mile radius. City Planner Maloy expressed used car values have risen
and the recreation vehicle sales are up. Councilmember Ohrn recounted only new car
dealerships was purposed due to the City not wanting a bunch of small used dealerships as
the desire was to have an aesthetically pleasing development. Councilmember Shields
revealed the proposed used car dealership was a 10-million-dollar building, which was
something not been seen before.

Developer legal counsel Bruce Baird offered a brief overview to the design standards which
was one of the driving forces to maintain a visually appealing area. He expressed concern
about the term conditional use as the developer was adamant the primary uses with the
AMSD would be permitted and not conditional.

Legal Counsel Baird and the Council reviewed the proposed document changes surrounding
landscaping, vehicle storage, building height, non AMSD uses, infrastructure, office spaces,
building materials, and future property purchases.

Councilmember Henderson suggested he would consider an MDA; however, wanted
assurances the rest of the auto mall would be constructed. The Council deliberated on the
percentages of development which was commercial uses compared to auto mall specific
uses.
