ORDINANCE 21-12: AMEND TITLE 22 OF THE WEST VALLEY CITY
MUNICIPAL CODE TO EXCLUDE THE PARKING OF VEHICLES WITHIN
FIVE FEET OF A DRIVEWAY
Wayne T Pyle, City Manager, presented proposed Ordinance 21-12 that would amend Title
22 of the West Valley City Municipal Code to exclude the parking of vehicles within five feet
of a driveway.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       If approved, this ordinance will amend Subsection 22-2-117(2)(a) to include a
       prohibition on parking within five feet of a public or private driveway.

       (2) Standing or Parking Prohibited. No vehicle shall be standing or parked,
       whether occupied or not, except to temporarily pick up or discharge a passenger
       or passengers, provided the driver remains in the vehicle and the engine remains
       running:

       a. In front of or within five feet of a public or private driveway;

The City Council will consider Ordinance 21-12 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
April 6, 2021 at 6:30 P.M.

       - What are health disparities? Health disparities are more than adverse
          health outcomes. Although all health disparities are adverse health
          outcomes, not all adverse health outcomes are health disparities. A
          “disparity” implies that the difference is avoidable, unfair, and unjust.
          Health disparities are differences in health outcomes closely linked to
          economic, socio-cultural, environmental, and geographic
          disadvantage.
       - What is health equity? Health equity is the principle underlying the
          commitment to reduce and, ultimately, eliminate health disparities by
          addressing its determinants. Pursuing health equity means striving for
          the highest possible standard of health for all people and giving special
          attention to the needs of those communities at greatest risk for health
          disparities.

C.    CITY COUNCIL MEETINGS- ANCHOR/NO ANCHOR LOCATION
      Mayor Bigelow stated that he doesn’t want anyone to feel they need to share health
      information. He indicated that there are three Councilmembers in the Council
      Chambers this evening to test out technology. Mayor Bigelow stated that some
      individuals in the community and maybe on the Council or staff may opt to not be
      vaccinated. He indicated he hopes everyone does but there is no requirement that
      everyone must. Councilmember Christensen stated that he is in favor of returning
      to the Council Chambers in person and having face to face conversations. He
      indicated that he does know social distancing must still be implemented but noted
      that regulations are becoming more relaxed. Councilmember Buhler stated that he
      feels the City should follow the State and County mandates and guidelines. Mayor
      Bigelow agreed and noted that it’s important to make decisions on what is being
      directed from the health industry as well. He indicated that he has heard the term
      “herd immunity” where a percentage of the population is vaccinated and gathering
      as communities is deemed safe again. Wayne Pyle indicated that the City has
     MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – MARCH 23, 2021
                           -9-


       reached a level of stabilization but there are still active COVID cases occurring
       every day. He indicated that we are about where we were in May of last year.
       Wayne stated that the City hasn’t received guidance as of yet. He indicated that he
       would advise a good amount of vaccines be available for people, as lesser amount
       of active cases, and some guidance from the State level. He stated he would predict
       end of May. Wayne noted that the availability of the vaccine is not where it should
       be yet. Councilmember Buhler asked how the lifting of a mask mandate will affect
       the City. Wayne replied that the City can still control a mask mandate, but the
       challenge will be conflicting guidance and information from various entities.
       Councilmember Lang asked what the City’s liability is. Wayne replied that liability
       is undefined at this time. Councilmember Buhler expressed concern about pushing
       to be first in opening up and having a liability issue versus waiting for the
       appropriate guidance and mandates and reducing that risk. Councilmember
       Christensen stated that people would need to wear masks and a member of staff
       could clean off the microphone between each speaker. Mayor Bigelow stated that
       he feels staff should be looked to for guidance. Councilmember Huynh agreed and
       noted that he doesn’t see a rush. Councilmember Fitisemanu stated that West Valley
       City staff is very prepared and efficient and is one of the best he has seen. He
       indicated that herd immunity won’t be achieved until at least June and he would
       agree it not be evaluated until that time.
