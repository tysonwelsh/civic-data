         2.1. Feasibility Study and Market Survey for the Southwest Athletic Complex – Wendy
              Thomas, Assistant City Manager
      Assistant City Manager Wendy Thomas introduced Victus Consultant Walter Franco who led
      the efforts on the feasibility study and market survey for the Southwest Athletic Complex.
      Consultant Franco offered a background of the methodology of the project.

      Victus Advisors conducted telephone interviews with representatives from 20 groups to get
      an understanding of their interest in utilizing the proposed complex to meet their demand.
      Consultant Franco offered an analysis of strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats
      of a complex in the community. A proposed Southwest Athletic Complex Master Plan was
      presented to the Council along with concept renderings.

      Consultant Franco anticipated the cost of the complex was estimated to cost just under $45
      Million. It was also estimated that visitors from outside of Salt Lake County could account
      for about 27-percent of annual attendance at the Southwest Athletic Complex. Weekday
      usage would primarily be regional programming, including youth and adult sports whereas
      weekend use would primarily be dedicated to youth/amateur sports tournaments.

      Consultant Franco anticipated, after adjusting estimates of gross direct spending for
      displacement, estimated approximately 43-percent of direct spending associated with the
      annual operations of the proposed complex and would have a net impact on Salt Lake County.
      The Food and Beverage industry in Salt Lake County would benefit the most from the annual
      visitation impacts of the proposed Southwest Athletic Complex, followed by lodging,
      shopping and entertainment. Mr. Franco also estimated the complex would generate
      $790,000annually in incremental tax revenue for Salt Lake County. He also presented
      incremental annual hotel nights for the complex at buildout would be $49,062. Over a 30-
      year period, the proposed complex was estimated to have a net impact of $1.1 billion in direct
      spending, over 1.4 million in incremental hotel nights, and over $17.5 million in Salt Lake
      County tax revenues.

      Consultant Franco indicated the firm was also tasked with assessing the potential economic
      and fiscal impacts that could be generated within Salt Lake County if the Southwest Athletic
      Complex in Herriman and the Regional Athletic Complex in Salt Lake City partnered to host
      a large national tournament. The assumption of a large soccer tournament could include 500
      teams, 20 players plus 40 spectators per team, 30,000 total visitors for a three-day
      tournament which was estimated to bring in almost $6.5 million in direct spending, $108,000
      in estimated tax revenue, and about $2 million in incremental hotel room spending.

         2.2. Update and Discussion Regarding Private/Public Fiber Utility Partnership –
              Nathan Cherpeski, City Manager
      City Manager Nathan Cherpeski recalled the previous discussion held in March 2023 when
      Strata had been selected to partner with Herriman City to help move this project forward. He
      turned the time over to Strata to offer an update of the project.

      The preliminary design showed three different zones where robust fiber backbone
      electronics would be housed to provide critical services. The group presented a map along
      with the 10-year growth areas to project future needs to ensure the design could handle the
      load. The project timeline was summarized with construction commencing in 2027. Resident
      impacts and debarkation points were identified. The group looked at two different
      construction methodologies: directional drilling or microtrenching. The utility fees, opt-out
      percentages, bond and term agreements, and retail rates were observed.

      Microtrenching offered significant benefits over traditional excavation methods by
      minimizing disruption and decreasing construction timelines. Using a diamond-tipped large
      circular saw to cut a narrow trench, the conduit would be installed in the bottom of the
      trench, backfilled with a concrete fill mix, and then sealed. Directional Boring was a
      trenchless technique used to install underground utilities like pipes, conduits, and cables. This
      method involved using a surface launched drilling rig to create a shallow arc or radius along
      a designated underground route. Unlike traditional cut and cover methods, directional boring
      caused minimal disruption to the surface and offered significant environmental benefits. It
      would be useful when conventional excavation would not be feasible or if the goal was to
      minimize surface disturbance.

      Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn quested the standards and wanted to make sure the disturbed
      surface area would not be inferior to the rest of the road. City Manager Cherpeski informed
      Council the drilling would be set to standards set by the City and anticipated each section of
      the road would be evaluated and utilizing a mixture of construction methods.

      The Council discussion turned to the infrastructure being installed on private roads and in
      areas controlled by Homeowner’s Associations. It was noted Strata would work closely with
      the HOAs to have access granted.

      Councilmember Shields expressed his appreciation for the work getting it done. He suggested
      once the agreement had been tweaked, the project should move forward with bonding
      information.

          2.7. Presentation on Process to Vacate Property – Tood Sheeran, City Attorney
      City Attorney Todd Sheeran outlined the process needed to vacate property. He noted a
      petition must be submitted requesting to vacate a portion or all of a public street and
      highlighted the noticing requirements.

      If a property was dedicated to the city by a developer less than 15 years ago, the City must
      first offer the property back to the developer. After that specified time frame, the vacated
      portion of the right-of-way would be dedicated to each adjoining property owner. If the
      property was purchased by the City, the property may be disposed of as deemed necessary.
      In essence, the property could be sold for fair market value.

          2.8. Discussion of a potential Investment Policy for the City – Kyle Maurer, Finance
               Director
      Finance Director Kyle Maurer reported the City currently invests 100-percent of its idle
      funds in the Utah Public Treasurer’s Investment fund. He recommended having the flexibility
      to invest in alternative instruments to ensure the funds meet City objectives including safety
      of principal, mitigating investment risk, and return on investment. Director Maurer wanted
      to put rules in place. The Council agreed.

          2.9. Discussion of a proposed Fund Balance Policy – Kyle Maurer, Finance Director
      Finance Director Maurer relayed his desire to have a fund balance policy to alleviate concerns
      and confusion. The objective of the policy would to have sufficient cash flow for daily
      financial needs, secure and maintain investment grade bond ratings, offset significant
      economic downturns or revenue shortfalls, and provide funds for unforeseen expenditures
      related to emergencies.
