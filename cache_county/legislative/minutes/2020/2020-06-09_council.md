PUBLIC HEARING: June 9, 2020 at 5:30 p.m. — Ordinance No. 2020-07-North Pine Canyon
Ranch Rezone ~ Request to rezone approximately 40 acres from the Forest Recreation (FR40)
Zone to the Agricultural (A10) Zone, located on two parcels with split zoning within an approved
subdivision at approximately 5500 West 4600 South, Wellsville - Development Services
Director Harrild explained the rezone will make it simpler to calculate for development and is
consistent with current and historical use. The Planning Commission recommends approval.

Chairman Ward opened the Public Hearing and invited public comment.
Jarvis Maughan, landowner of one of the affected parcels, spoke in favor of the rezone.
There was no other public comment.

ACTION: Motion by council member White to close the Public Hearing June 9, 2020 at
5:30 p.m. — Ordinance No. 2020-07-North Pine Canyon Ranch Rezone. Zilles seconded
the motion. The vote was unanimous, 7-0.

PUBLIC HEARING: June 9, 2020 at 5:45 p.m. — Ordinance No. 2020-08-Pinnacle Estates LLC
Rezone — Request to rezone 30.10 acres from the Agricultural (A10) Zone to the Rural 2 (RU2)

Zone, located at 190 South 300 West, Mendon — Development Services Director Harrild indicted
the rezone would increase the number of allowable lots from 2-3 to 17 — a significant jump in
density. The owner's annexation application was denied by Mendon as not a good match for
the level of density being proposed. The Planning Commission recommends denial.

Chairman Ward opened the Public Hearing and invited public comment.

Andrew Lillywhite, Attorney for Pinnacle, said the thirty acres would only have fifteen lots and is adjacent
to a city so it fits in with the RU2 Zone. This is simply a rezone request. Any development will require a
subdivision request and the county could impose its standards. Mendon has had an extended
moratorium and it is imperative to protect the property rights of owners and allow development to occur.
The developer would create a road up to county standards and the extension of services would be
snowplowing about a quarter mile of road. It seems an ideal location for development to occur. County
Resolution No. 2015-20 restricts expansion of road services on roads that do not already serve an
existing home. This is on a road serving an existing home.

Tom Hill, Engineer, said the property owner has investigated ground water and drilled a test well which
produced 30 gallons per minute which is adequate. The well had good recovery and was under the
maximum contaminant levels. There has been current flooding through the property and infrastructure
would meet county standards.

There was no other public comment.

ACTION: Motion by Council member Zilles to close the Public Hearing: June 9, 2020 at
5:45 p.m. — Ordinance No. 2020-08-Pinnacle Estates LLC Rezone. Erickson seconded the
motion. The vote was unanimous, 7-0.

DEPARTMENT OR COMMITTEE REPORTS

Tourism — Julie Hollist-Terrill reported via phone that the situation with the tourism
industry is terrible. COVID-19 has slashed tourism income. Hotel and motel occupancy
rates have dropped dramatically and restaurant patronage was down 80-90% in the
several weeks of COVID-19 restrictions. Hollist-Terrill has run several radio ads urging
residents to “order out” for meals and participate in “staycations.” The tour bus revenue
is gone. There will be a few months where restaurant tax is lower, but Hollist-Terrill
believes we may see a return by late fall with numbers closer to normal by next spring.

Hollist-Terrill, in response to the Council’s question about summer citizens and TRT at
the May 26, 2020 Council meeting, said if individuals stay at a property longer than thirty
days, there is no transient room tax collected.

Council member White said he enjoyed her radio spots and Vice Chair Worthen said she
appreciates Hollist-Terrill’s creative genius in marketing Cache Valley.

Resolution No. 2020-10 - Declaring the County’s Intent to Pick Up Tier II Public
Safety Employees’ Contribution to the Utah State Retirement Systems — Human

Resources Director Amy Adams verified the Council had received their requested
information. Sheriff Jensen expressed deep dissatisfaction with Utah Retirement
Systems (URS). They seem to set the rules and no one is allowed to challenge them.
When Representative Potter was running a retirement bill for Jensen and asked URS for
some numbers, he was told it was too expensive to provide. Next year they can ask for
5%, then 7%. Where does their outreach stop? We don't get information; we are just
told what the cost is and what the values are and how it’s going to work.

Director Adams recommends approval to show appreciation for the Sheriff's office and
what they do for the community. The decision can be made today or later, but if it is not
in effect by the pay period including July 1, 2020, the employee will have to pay — it is not
retroactive.

Council member Borup commented that in today’s world, he doesn’t want any whiff of
defunding the Sheriff's Department.

Council member Zilles noted this is only for Public Safety Employees and the Council
may have to have a discussion about other county employees.

e Discussion: Cache County COVID-19 Status — Executive Buttars said he

understands there are some Council members who want to discuss recommending the
county go to a New Normal risk phase — green in the color coded health guidance
system. Any recommended change in risk category should go through the Health
Department. Executive Buttars observed the Council is aware of the current situation in
Cache County with 40-50 new cases being reported each day in the county. One
reason is because of increased testing as some of the industries are asking all of their
employees to be tested. If we move to a green status more personal responsibility will
have to be taken.

Vice Chair Worthen said one thing that has been difficult for her to figure out with the
COVID-19 response is the complete lack of models. There is a whole other side of
things going on — the impact on local businesses, hotels, restaurants, the arts, domestic
violence. Other numbers have to be looked at. According to Duke University studies for
every percentage point that unemployment rises there is a 6% increase the next year in
the rates of death of working males as well as an increase in minorities exhibiting
suicidal behavior. There are different sets of facts proclaimed by various studies.
Worthen quoted a UAC CEO who feels there is reason to move to a “green with
precautions” status. The 18-25 age group is unlikely to be hospitalized with COVID-19.
The problem lies with the 65 and older age group which needs to be protected. The
majority of deaths from COVID-19 have occurred in nursing homes. Worthen feels the
1.1% mortality rate of COVID-19 does not support remaining at yellow status. There is a
1 in 7 chance of dying of cancer, a 1 in 25 chance of dying from all preventable causes

Deputy County Clerk Hoth observed the restrictions were enacted to contain COVID-19

of it. We must consider those who are trying to care for the people. Hospitals only have
so many IC units. In her mind that was the major reason for trying to contain the virus.

Council member Erickson asked, “Where have we, in this nation, in this state, given so
much power to unelected individuals? Executive Buttars told us we can send a letter off
and the Board of Health will take it into advisement.” Buttars interjected, “No, you have
asay. That was evidenced by your desire to go to yellow.” Erickson continued that it is
critical that elected officers — you can listen to the science, you can listen to the doctors
— and we need to live by any decisions that are made. Erickson said we live with risk.
Every day, everywhere we go, whatever we do, there are levels of risk and he noted that
Vice Chair Worthen brought that up. Erickson knows of people who have COVID and
nothing is different in their lives. They are not experiencing any kind of symptoms. He
thinks what is happening is we are taking away something more valuable than blue,
green, red — whatever we decide to do. We need to empower the people and give
personal responsibility back to them. The value of self-worth — when you start pulling
that away from people, we get suicides and people not feeling good about themselves.
They need to have that choice. If we share and educate then they make the choice. If
they feel vulnerable, they know their circumstances and they choose not to, terrific — that
is their choice. Erickson observed that everything he has read says “guidelines” not law.
They are guidelines; it’s the individual's choice. He feels we are at the point where we
educate and if the individual is vulnerable, we allow them to abide by the guidelines or
not, Erickson believes hospitalizations are not up dramatically which tells him what was
thought would happen is not true. Throwing fear into people is harmful; we need to
show some confidence in people. We just heard the report tonight of the dire effects
COVID is having on tourism. That tells him how volatile that industry is. The county
needs to look ahead in its economic strategic plan and make sure we are producing
something more than entertainment. We need to produce some “hard” items that people
