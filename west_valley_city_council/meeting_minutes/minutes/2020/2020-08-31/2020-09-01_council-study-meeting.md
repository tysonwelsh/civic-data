RESOLUTION 20-145: APPROVE A PROFESSIONAL SERVICES AGREEMENT
WITH AVENUE CONSULTANTS, INC. FOR A STUDY OF TRAFFIC PATTERNS
AND FUTURE IMPROVEMENTS ON BANGERTER HIGHWAY
Russ Willardson, Public Works Director, presented proposed Resolution 20-145 that would
approve a Professional Services Agreement with Avenue Consultants, Inc. for a study of
traffic patterns and future improvements on Bangerter Highway.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       UDOT is converting all at-grade interchanges on Bangerter Highway to freeway
       style grade-separated interchanges. Please see the attached memo for additional
       information regarding UDOT’s Bangerter Highway schedule.

       Concerns have been expressed about the effects on the City’s transportation system
       and adjacent properties, including: effects on east-west traffic, possible closure of
       some current Bangerter Highway accesses, possible construction of one-way
       frontage roads, impacts on businesses and neighborhoods, loss of landscaping
       adjacent to the corridor, impact to Stonebridge Golf Course, coordination with
       active transportation plans crossing the corridor, and planning for new growth in
       Lake Park Business Center.

       These are all legitimate concerns which normally would be evaluated during the
       UDOT environmental process, at UDOT’s expense. Past experience on the
       Mountain View Corridor project has shown that City staff and officials would have
       numerous opportunities during the environmental process to express concerns and
       work with UDOT to find acceptable solutions. The City’s study would be at least
       partially redundant, since UDOT will conduct its own current traffic data collection
       and analysis and develop concept designs.

       The advantage for the City in conducting its own high-level study, would be to get
       a head start on the process, and enable the City to predetermine preferences and
       recommend solutions to UDOT.

Councilmember Buhler stated that the idea to do this wasn’t to do UDOT’s work but to get
ahead of them and to hopefully present something that is better received by the City and
the community. He asked if the City could provide goals for the Study to the consultant.
Russ replied that there are many challenges with the Bangerter Highway project, but the
City can ask the consultant to analyze these challenges and provide possible solutions.
Councilmember Buhler stated that he would prefer that less land be purchased from
residents and questioned whether a two-level highway was an option. Councilmember
Lang asked if UDOT will take information from this Study and potentially support it. Russ
replied that UDOT will conduct their own Study but noted that he believes they will take
this Study and goals of the City into account. Russ stated that he is concerned that this
Study may be conducted too early but noted that funding could also be moved up for the
project by the Legislature. Councilmember Lang asked how far out construction is
projected. Russ replied approximately 5 years to begin the planning process.
Councilmember Buhler and Councilmember Lang discussed the timeline and whether the
City can influence or impact the decisions UDOT makes. Councilmember Lang stated that
this Study may provide a creative and more proactive approach. Councilmember
Fitisemanu agreed and noted that he would prefer to be proactive than reactive. Mayor
Bigelow stated that meeting cost goals and specific requirements are the primary goals of
UDOT and they don’t always take neighborhood impact into account. He indicated he
would like to minimize impact on local neighborhoods, even if there is additional cost.
Mayor Bigelow noted that this Study could have an influence on UDOT and State Elected
Officials and some of their decisions. Councilmember Christensen stated that the City can
possibly mitigate impact to neighborhoods. Mayor Bigelow stated that good facts and
potential alternatives could lend support to better ideas. Councilmember Buhler stated that
he would like the consultants to provide information that the City wants as opposed to
information that they would otherwise provide to UDOT. Mayor Bigelow stated that
beautification should be a higher priority than it has been in other Cities. Mayor Bigelow
stated that local leaders, officials, and representatives should be provided the results of this
Study as well. Councilmember Buhler stated this will be a long lasting and impactful
project. Councilmember Lang suggested that the Council meet and discuss goals for the
Study. Wayne stated that he would suggest entering into the contract and providing input
as the process progresses. He noted that the consultant can provide updates during the
course of the Study as well. Mayor Bigelow added that the City can request smaller studies
on particular items as well. He added that he would like to discuss and evaluate sound
walls.

The City Council will consider Resolution 20-145 at the Regular Council Meeting

RESOLUTION 20-146: APPROVE A COOPERATIVE AGREEMENT BETWEEN
THE CITY AND THE UTAH DEPARTMENT OF TRANSPORTATION FOR
FUNDING FOR THE COMPLETION OF 2550 SOUTH
Russ Willardson, Public Works Director, presented proposed Resolution 20-146 that would
approve a Cooperative Agreement between the City and the Utah Department of
Transportation for funding for the completion of 2550 South.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       Under the 2018 Senate Bill 234 (Utah Inland Port Authority Bill), it was directed
       that the Department of Transportation shall fund, from money designated in the
       Transportation Investment Fund for that purpose, the completion of 2550 South
       from 5600 West to 8000 West, with matching funds from the county in which the
       road is located.

       Total funding was in the amount of $4,000,000, with funding coming from UDOT
       and Salt Lake County in equal shares.

       This agreement provides for the final $500,000 from UDOT.

       The Public Works Department will use the funds for the design and construction of
       2550 South. The first segment was completed in 2019. The next phase east of 6000
       West to 2400 South over Mountain View Corridor, is anticipated to be completed
       in 2021.

Russ stated this completes the obligation from the State but doesn’t complete the project
overall and the City may need to acquire additional funds to finish construction.

The City Council will consider Resolution 20-146 at the Regular Council Meeting
scheduled September 8, 2020 at 6:30 P.M.
