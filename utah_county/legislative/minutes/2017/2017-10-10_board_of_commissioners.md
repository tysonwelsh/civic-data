1.     APPROVE AND AUTHORIZE THE COMMISSION CHAIR TO SIGN AN AGREEMENT WITH
       ARTHUR GALLAGHER TO PERFORM BROKERAGE AND CONSULTING SERVICE FOR THE
       COUNTY’S GROUP EMPLOYEE BENEFITS PROGRAM (Continued from the October 3, 2017
       Commission meeting)
(Track A, 12:55)
Utah County Human Resources Director Cammie Taylor summarized the discussion from the previous Commission
meeting: the bid from Gallagher was $100,000 and was negotiated down by ten percent. The current county provider
has included HSA/FSA (Health Savings and Flexible Spending Accounts) and COBRA administration services at a
price of nearly $8,400 annually; when Ms. Taylor requested the same from Gallagher, they were unwilling to meet
the same condition. Ms. Taylor reminded the commissioners Gallagher was the outlying response in the county
request for proposals – originally at $100,000 compared to the $72,000 and $80,000 price points offered by two other
competing companies.

Commissioner Graves pointed out it was the county who omitted the COBRA requirement from the request for
proposal (RFP) and Gallagher is now being asked to include services outside of those parameters. Ms. Taylor
countered it is important to have flexibility in a partnership so both parties can benefit. Commissioner Lee mentioned
speaking with James Sterling, a representative of Gallagher, and their offer to halve the $8,400 expense. Both
Commissioners Lee and Ivie expressed that is a reasonable offer. Commissioner Graves expressed concern in placing
responsibility for a county mistake onto a contractor. Commissioner Lee clarified he did not request Gallagher split
the additional costs; they have made that offer on a ‘good faith’ basis. Utah County Deputy Attorney Robert Moore
reviewed contract language regarding compensation. They discussed financial savings to the county, and
Commissioner Graves commented on the county being difficult in business transactions.

           Commissioner Graves made the motion to approve the agreement with Arthur
           Gallagher to perform brokerage and consulting service for the County’s Group
           Employee Benefits Program, with updated language for half of the COBRA
           administration fees not to exceed $4,200, as discussed in Regular Agenda Item
           No. 1. The motion was seconded by Commissioner Ivie and carried with the
           following vote:
                                                                           AYE: William C. Lee
                                                                                  Greg Graves
                                                                                  Nathan Ivie
                                                                           NAY: None

(Track A, 35:50)
Commissioner Graves took a moment of personal privilege to comment on Regular Agenda Item No. 5, which was
stricken from the calendar today. He noted the Pay Plan was updated on March 21, 2017. The Board has received
multiple requests for step increases that would be outside of regular parameters, which are allowed under certain
conditions. One of those conditions is for the Utah County Human Resources Director to provide written justification
for the increase, but that is not the current practice. Commissioner Graves stated the policy should either be updated
to reflect county practice, or additional steps taken to ensure protection for the Human Resources Director.

                                         PUBLIC COMMENTS
(Track A, 40:45)
Provo resident Robert Stevens commented on most of the links for the Utah County Health Department website being
broken, and offered to fix the problem for the county. Mr. Stevens also referenced a statement by Commissioner
Graves at the previous Board meeting one week prior, regarding the Commission clarifying many regulations but not
depleting any in the last three years. Mr. Stevens suggested Brigham Young University students could create forum
software for the Commission agenda process through an internship – this would allow residents to search, browse,
add footnotes, and discuss in a public space proposed codes and ordinances. All three commissioners expressed
interest in the idea.
