3. Public hearing, Zoning Ordinance amendment, for height, parking, and design standards in the BEH zone.

  Ms. Tammy Door stated as a real estate developer, they are committed to bringing old buildings back to
  use. Their intent is to bring commerce and business back into the building and its surroundings.

  Mr. Cody Storey stated he has reviewed the recommendations of Staff and supports their
  recommendation. He reviewed the proposed amendments, stating they would allow a viable use of the
  existing building as well as the addition of new buildings which would complement the historic Stock
  Exchange building.

  Mr. Brierley stated there is a proposal to renovate the existing Exchange building, and in doing so would
  like to do some additions to the site to increase its economic viability. The proposed amendments
  would allow additions to the site as well as an addition to the rear of the existing building. He reviewed
  the purpose of the BEH zone stating any renovation should complement the existing building and be in


                                                                                              Page 5 of 18
  scale and architecturally compatible. The General Plan policies encourage economic incentives for
  qualifying historic properties. The application requests reduced setback requirements, noting the
  existing building is about 9.5’ from the side property line. As there is little purpose for a rear yard for
  commercial uses, Staff feels it is appropriate to reduce both the side and rear setback requirements to
  10’. As the Stock Exchange Building should remain as the focal point of the area, the intent of the
  existing code is to limit height to match the existing building. The proposal would increase the height of
  the existing building, but would be set back from the front so as to not be highly visible. While the
  proposed use would anticipate up to 200 parking spaces, Staff is suggesting the owners consider a
  parking management plan which would encourage ride-share, use of public transportation, walking and
  biking options in an effort to reduce the need for vehicle parking in the area. Consideration also is
  proposed relating to design standards, incorporating historic feature of the building in a more
  contemporary building. The proposal is to amend some design standards to complement features of
  the Exchange building and eliminate some standard which are not visible on the existing building. Staff
  feels the amendment would incentivize the renovation of the existing building while retaining its historic
  character and recommends approval of the amendments as proposed.

  Chair Sandau asked if there are any visuals showing what the historic building would look like if the
  proposed additions were approved. Mr. Brierley clarified the glass proposal is for the third floor addition
  which would be set back from the front of the building. Commissioner Schade expressed concern the
  glass structure could become up to five stories with the height as proposed. Commissioner Graf asked
  if the Commission would review the appearance of the historic site and building. Mr. Brierley indicated
  while a parking management plan would likely be reviewed, the appearance of the building does not
  require Planning Commission approval. As it is on the local register of historic resources, it will require
  approval of the Landmarks Commission.

  Mr. Brandon Cooper, Community and Economic Development director, stated the City has been trying to
  get a viable use for the Exchange Building for many years, noting the historic building is a key central
  part of the project and is important that its historic character be preserved. He stated as it has been
  vacant for so long, it is in poor condition and the proposal would allow an investment to preserve the
  building while enhancing potential uses. He requested the amendments be approved as proposed.

As there were no additional public comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to close the
   public hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Blaisdell and passed unanimously.

  Chair Sandau asked about the height of the proposed addition. Commissioner Schade indicated the
  plans show 55’ which would allow up to five stories, indicating if only three stories are proposed, the
  height should be reduced. Discussion continued relating to the renovation of the building and the need
  for design standards. It was indicated some incentive is warranted in order to allow the building to be
  rehabilitated. Commission expressed trust in the Landmarks Commission in reviewing the final
  appearance of the building.

  MOTION:       A motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to recommend approval of the proposed
                ordinance language to amend the height, setback, parking and design standards in the
                BEH zone as recommended by Staff based on the findings these are consistent with the
                purposes of the BEH zone and the General Plan. Motion was seconded by Commissioner
                Southwick and passed 5-1 with Commissioners Blaisdell, Graf, Safsten, Southwick and
                Sandau voting aye and Commissioner Schade voting no. Commissioner Schade stated he
                is not comfortable with the proposed height and felt the while the proposed building
                addition would make it more economically feasible, it does nothing to preserve the gem of
                the community.

4. Public facility site plan, Airport Terminal renovation, 3909 Airport Road.

   Mr. Shane Sanders stated this is a proposed remodel of the existing terminal building in order to
   improve access and service. The existing parking also will remain, while the entryway will be widened to
   accommodate two-way traffic. A drop-off and pick-up area will be added as well as additional parking.

   Ms. Patrick indicated this property is in the AC zone and the Commission is to determine whether the
   proposed addition complies with the ordinance as well as the General Plan. She stated the new building
   will provide enhanced security and provide an indoor baggage claim area as well as car rental services.
   She indicated the intent of the AC zone is to provide services supportive of the aeronautic industry. Staff
   recommends approval of the site plan as proposed.

   Ms. Patrick noted she had received an email from Mr. Hollingsworth expressing concern for the amount
   of money to be spent on the proposed terminal expansion, indicating it ignores the adopted airport
   master plan in relocating the terminal to the west side of the airport.

   Mr. Bryant Garrett, Airport Manager, stated the City had allocated funding to match Federal funding and
   approval was given to expand runways and taxiways at the west end of the airport. Improvements
   totaling $10 million are in the process in that area. This proposal is using new Federal dollars
   consisting of $1 million for five years. He stated the existing terminal building was constructed in 1948
   and a few improvements have been made recently, including a new roof and renovated restaurant, but
   parts of the building have been neglected over time. Because it needs to serve more than its original
   intent for the next 30-40 years, improvements are needed to comply with ADA standards, fire code
   regulations, making it a safer and more usable building. It also could accommodate more air carriers
   which would be needed before the FAA would approve a new terminal building at the west end of the
   airport. The proposed improvements would entice air carriers to come to Ogden, indicating the recent
   departure of airlines has not been because of the airport or the location, but is due to pilot shortages.
   He stated the existing building accommodates only 370 customers according to fire code, but there are
   only 204 seats. The proposed expansion will allow multiple carriers, providing four gates while the
   existing structure has only one gate. He felt the terminal expansion is a priority in an effort to attract
   airlines.

   Mr. Kelly Cazier expressed there is no need for a terminal upgrade. He felt Ogden does not need
   commercial air carriers due to its proximity to the Salt Lake airport. He felt the proposed expansion is
   not in the best interest of the public and felt the terminal would still not be used by air carriers or by
   Ogden citizens.

   Mr. David Shumway felt while the existing building needs some upgrades, the proposed expansion is a
   waste of Federal dollars and serves no purpose. He stated the City had designated Airport Road as an
   entryway to the City and had asked landscaping improvements be installed which were accomplished
   but many of the trees have been neglected over time and have now died.

   Mr. Ed McKinney stated the proposed expansion of the parking lot would eliminate existing hangars and
   felt improvements proposed would not generate more air traffic in this area. He felt the terminal should
   be relocated to the west end of the airport where commercial airlines could be serviced, and retain this
   area for general aviation according to the airport master plan. He stated some pilots do not favor
   Ogden due to its approaches and proximity to Salt Lake City airport.

   Mr. Gerald Crawford stated while there has been discussions relating to expanding the Ogden Airport for
   decades, but felt safety and use does not support the proposed cost of the renovation.

  determine whether the proposed site plan complies with the General Plan. Commission discussed both

  Plan.

  Mr. Garrett indicated the FAA has determined there is a need for 100,000 passengers in order to

5. Change of Nonconforming Use, to allow auto sales and auto repair, 1990 Wall Avenue.

  Mr. Samuel Cole stated this property has historically been used as auto repair but has since been
  rezoned to MU where the use has continued as nonconforming as it is not allowed in the MU zone. The
  request is to continue the repair component while selling used vehicles as a secondary or accessory
  use. He stated the RDA has acknowledged the overall plan for the area will not be realized for several
  years and has allowed existing businesses to remain. He stated the new use will improve the
  appearance of the lot as display vehicles will have better curb appeal than the existing auto repair use.

  Mr. Rodriguez indicated the request is to change the existing nonconforming use as auto repair to add
  auto sales. He stated the Commission is to determine whether the new use intensifies the use of the
  property and whether it is compatible with uses in the area. He stated the combination of uses will
  create a visual impact to the surrounding neighborhood and increase traffic in the area. The added use
  of auto sales could change the character of both the site and surrounding area. He felt the use is not
  compatible with the MU zoning but is more appropriate in the C-3/CP-3 zone as it is more auto related
  than pedestrian which is the intent of the MU zone. He stated Staff feels the addition of auto sales will
  intensify the use and is not compatible with the standards of the existing MU zone and recommends
  denial of the expansion.

  Commissioner Graf asked about the combined use, clarifying the existing use as auto repair may
  remain as a nonconforming right while the addition of auto sales requires the Commission’s approval.
  Commissioner Safsten expressed concern of the implementation of MU uses, and felt existing uses
  should have the opportunity to remain in the meantime. He felt the addition of auto sales to the existing
  use as repair is minimal. Chair Sandau asked how many vehicles would be offered for sale and
  displayed at any given time. Discussion continued relating to the existing uses and their longevity in
  relation to the MU zoning and River Master Plan implementation.

  Mr. Samuel Cole indicated he anticipates no more than 15 vehicles for sale at any given time, stating
  sales would remain as an accessory use to the auto repair business. He felt the continuation of viable
  businesses in the area is important to make the area attractive enough that it would be redeveloped as

                                                                                            Page 8 of 18
  proposed. The intent of the MU zone is to create a walkable community, but existing uses already are
  not walkable, including the juvenile court building and Wal-Mart. He stated the original use of the
  property was auto related and until redevelopment can occur, the use as it was originally built and
  zoned for should be retained.

  Commissioner Graf expressed concern that if a nonconforming use is continued, the property may not
  become available to include in a redevelopment project in the future. Commissioner Southwick
  commented this is a high traffic commercial area, with established businesses, particularly on the east
  side of Wall Avenue. He felt cars offered for sale would likely have a better appearance than those being
  repaired and felt the added use would not detract from the neighborhood. Commissioner Safsten
  commented if a viable MU project were proposed for the area, existing uses would likely be willing to sell
  and felt the interim use should be allowed to continue until a development project is anticipated.

  Mr. Gerald Crawford, 318 15th Street expressed support of the proposed use as it improves the
  appearance of that lot.

  Ms. Shauna Jensen stated she had been working with Mr. Montgomery and had understood the use of
  auto sales would be allowed as it was originally used. She felt the property should be a viable,
  productive use in order to be a good neighbor and felt the proposed use should be allowed.

  Mr. David Rawson felt the added use of auto sales to the existing repair business would be an upgrade
  and would be better for the surrounding community. The use of sales would increase the value of the
  business and encouraging investment in the area.

  Mr. Cole stated he is not opposed to redevelopment of the property as a mixed use project but in the
  meantime desires to operate both the repair and sales component of the business which would be more
  appealing for the appearance of the corner.

   MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Southwick to allow the change of nonconforming use
           to add auto sales based on the findings there is no adverse impact to the surrounding
           community will not intensify the use of the site or impacts to the neighborhood and shall not
           change the character o the building or site and can meet development requirements as
           much as possible. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Safsten and passed 5-1 with
           Commissioners Blaisdell, Safsten, Schade, Southwick and Sandau voting aye and
           Commissioner Graf voting no. Commissioner Graf felt the intended use as MU for this area
           should be supported, and indicated there are enough auto dealerships along Wall Avenue.
