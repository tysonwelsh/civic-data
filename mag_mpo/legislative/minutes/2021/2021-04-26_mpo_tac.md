Approve FY-22 Unified Planning Work Program (UPWP) Executive Summary - Shawn Seager, MAG The July 1, 2021 to June 30, 2022 (FY22) Unified Planning Work Program (Work Program) describes the transportation planning work undertaken by Mountainland MPO Staff, UDOT Staff and UTA Staff within the Mountainland MPO planning area. The Work Program is an annual budget. It illustrates to the members, Federal Highway Administration, Federal Transit Administration, and the public where the planning resources are allocated. It describes the planned work, budget, and funding sources. This FY22 UPWP $2,851,656 budget is about 8% larger when compared to last year’s $2,571,482 budget with a $203,771 increase from FY20. This is due to an increase in new studies/contracts and staff cost in COLA, merit, and equipment cost. It proposes to spend the remaining $2,200,156 or 77% of the total budget on thirteen full-time equivalents (FTE). This budget proposes to spend $651,500 or 23% on new special studies and contract including: 1. Joint Planning Studies with Wasatch Front Regional Council (WFRC), UDOT, and UTA 2. Travel Demand Modeling 3. Planning Opportunities Contingency 4. Project Management / Development Engineer Consultant MPO Technical Advisory Committee April 26, 2021 Page 3 5. Household Travel Survey / Street Light Data Revenue for this $2. 8 million budget is approximately 93% federal funds from the United States Department of Transportation while 7% is local funds form UTA, UDOT and our Local Governments. TIP Amendment – FrontRunner Extension Environmental Study - Chad Eccles, MAG Bob Allen reported that the FrontRunner Extension Environmental Study is a new project to be added to the TIP. The TIP selection process and policies state if a project needs to be added outside the normal two-year selection cycle, it must go through TAC and MPO Regional Planning Committee for approval. MAG, UDOT, and UTA in partnership with the cities in the south part of Utah County are currently participating in the South County Transit Study to assess and plan for future significant transit to extend from the south terminus of the Frontrunner Commuter Rail line. The same partners realize that the next step in moving this project forward is to conduct an environmental study. In the Regional Planning Committee held in March, some of the Mayors requested that MAG prepare a funding strategy to conduct the environmental study. The sense was that with the current federal administration preparing a large infrastructure funding program, the project should be “shovel ready” when funding becomes available. They felt it would be advantageous to move forward toward the environmental work and that MAG should set some funding aside to fund this important next step. Payson City offered to sponsor this project for the south county. MAG has determined that they are able to set aside $2M for an estimated $4M study with the rest of the cost to be shared. This amendment would preserve $2M for this study. Jill Spencer said the mayors in south Utah County see this is a logical next step and want to continue to identify transit options moving forward. Brad Kenison asked if the $2M being proposed for the environmental study will affect projects that have already been approved or their respective contingency funds. Bob Allen stated that three MPO Technical Advisory Committee April 26, 2021 Page 4 months ago, MAG was notified that they received $3.8M in federal fund from one of the COVID Relief Bills. MAG has a couple of years to spend the money and felt like it was logical to spend a portion of the funds to support the study. He stated that the project will not affect any of the contingency fund’s MAG has set aside. The reminder of the $3.8 M will go toward the next selection process. MAG Staff recommends that MAG TAC amend the TIP to include $2 million for environmental study for a Frontrunner extension into south Utah County. Ken Anson moved that the MAG TIP be amended to include $2 million to be put toward an environmental study for a Frontrunner extension into south Utah County. Richard Nielsen seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. SR92/North County Blvd Property Acquisition - Bob Allen, MAG Bob Allen reported that the intersection of SR92 and North County Blvd continues to be very busy. The eastbound right turn to southbound movement is the biggest issue for the intersection and is creating a need for future modifications to the intersection. The parcel at the southwest corner of the intersection is for sale. UDOT and Highland City both agree that it should be acquired to allow future intersection improvements. The Canal Blvd project within Highland City and Cedar Hills is nearing completion and has excess funds allocated to it. Highland would like to propose that $250K of those excess funds be used to acquire the SR92/North County Blvd parcel. The property does not fit into any MAG or UDOT ROW acquisition funding pools. Chris Trusty asked what will happen to the remaining portion of the property once the intersection is completed. Todd Trane said Highland City does not have a plan at this time. Eventually they would like to sell the remaining land. Whatever funds they receive will be treated like a normal acquisition process and will be returned. MAG Staff recommend the TIP be modified to move $250k from the Canal Blvd project to purchase the SR92/North County Blvd property. Chris Trusty moved that we recommend $250k be moved from the Canal Blvd project to purchase the SR92/North County Blvd property. Shane Winters seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.
