State Legislation impacting City zoning regulations
         Council Policy Analyst Watkins used the aid of a Story Map presentation to provide the group with an update on recent
State adopted legislation that will eventually result in adjustments to City land use ordinances; Utah Code 10-9a.534 has been
amended to outline specific building elements that cities are prohibited from requiring on single or duplex dwellings. According to
this new amendment, the City is prohibited from regulating the following design elements:
              1. Exterior color
             2. Type or style of exterior cladding material
             3. Style, dimensions, or materials of a roof structure, roof pitch, or porch
             4. Exterior nonstructural architectural ornamentation
              5. Location, design, placement, or architectural styling of a window, door, or garage door
              6. Number or type of rooms
             7. Interior layout of a room
              8. Minimum square footage over 1,000 sq. ft., (not including garage)
              9. Rear yard landscaping requirements
              10. Minimum building dimensions
              11. A requirement to install front yard fencing
         Chief Deputy Attorney Stratford and Plmming Manager Montgomery engaged in discussion with the group to help defme
some of the terms included in the preceding list of design elements.
         Mr. Watkins presented an image of a home that summarizes what the City may not require unless the City implements
design standard requirements in the following situations:
              1. Historic districts (including homes on the national and state registers)
             2. When necessary, under FEMA regulations (for flood insurance requirements)
             3. When required by the Utah Wildland Urban Interface Code
             4. When there is a development agreement
              5. In residential zones that were substantially developed before 1950

                   Mr. Watkins presented a map of the City to identify the locations in which design standards may be enforced; the City
         can impose design standards in various areas throughout the City, but there are large tracts of Ogden that are immune to such
         standards, particularly in older parts of town; the rules only apply to one and two-family homes. The City will need to update its
         zoning ordinance to conform with these legislative changes in the near future.
                   Mr. Watkins then discussed legislation regulating Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs); House Bill82 mandates that cities
         allow internal ADUs and gives cities the authority to license and regulate exterior ADUs in limited ways. An internal ADU is
         defmed as an "accessory dwelling unit created within a primary dwelling; within the footprint of the primary dwelling; and for the
         purpose of offering a long-term rental of30 consecutive days or longer." Basement aparhnents are a good example of an internal
         ADU. Mr. Montgomery swnmarized key points of the legislation as follows:
                        1. Internal AD Us must be permitted in all residential zones. (the bill allows college towns to prohibit lAD Us in up
                             to 67% ofland in residential zones)
                        2. Prohibits regulation of Internal ADU size, minimum lot frontage, or lot size. But can be prohibited on lots
                             smaller than 6,000 square feet.
                        3. Cities may require: a parking space; no· change to exterior appearance; requirement of business license; and
                             owner occupancy restrictions.
                        4. Cities may record a notice of Internal ADU on property, which then allows prohibition of short-tenn rentals
                             (less than 30 days).
                        5. Cities may prohibit Internal ADU rentals in non-owner-occupied dwellings.
                        6. Basements may be used as Internal ADUs.
                        7. HOAs cannot prohibit Internal ADUs.
                   Mr. Watkins noted these provisions went into effect on October 1, 2021 and Planning staff and the Planning Cmmnission
         are working on proposed ordinances that would update the zoning code to reflect these changes.
                   Mr. Watkins presented a map that identified residential areas that currently have ADU restrictions and Mr. Stratford
         clarified that since the City is not required to amend the map to change the areas in which internal AD Us are allowed; the City
         may restrict internalADUs in up to 67 percent of the area of the City because Ogden is a university-town. Mr. Montgomery stated
         that the Planning Commission is considering a plat amendment for the central bench neighborhood two blocks below Harrison
         Boulevard near Weber State University to remove the restriction on ADUs. He then facilitated discussion among the group
         regarding experiences of communities in other states responsive to similar legislative changes. There was a brief focus on the
         current nwnber of ADUs in the cmmnunity and the anticipated increase in the land use associated with the potential zoning
         ordinance amendments; there was also a brief focus on ADU licensing procedures and costs.
