    2. PUBLIC HEARING TO SUPPLY INFORMATION REGARDING THE 2016 COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT
       BLOCK GRANT (CDBG) PROGRAM, SOLICIT POSSIBLE PROJECTS THAT MAY BE ELIGIBLE FOR THE
       FUNDS, AND FIELD POSSIBLE SUGGESTIONS AND QUESTIONS ABOUT THE PROJECTS FROM THE
         CITIZENS

         Chad Meyerhoffer, of County Engineering, noted the purpose of this public hearing as stated above. He
         stated that the national objective is for grant money to be spent primarily to benefit low to moderate
         income persons but other national objectives include aiding in the prevention or elimination of slums or
         blight and to meet urgent needs because existing conditions pose a serious and immediate threat to health
         and welfare. Eligible activities are identified in the CDBG application guide available for review by
         interested parties. Examples for possible CDBG projects include ADA improvements, public works
         infrastructure projects, property acquisition, public services (i.e, food banks, homeless shelters), loan
         programs for private businesses that hire low income persons or those who rent to low income persons).
         Projects within the Wasatch Front region will compete between Weber, Morgan, and Tooele Counties and
         can expect approximately $1,000,000 in 2016 CDBG funds. Projects will be rated by a regional review
         committee made up of an elected official and a staff member from each county to determine what projects
         can proceed with final application. All pre-applications must be submitted to the Wasatch Front Regional
         Council by 1/29/2016. Unincorporated county does not currently have any active grant projects. The
         county as a whole has sponsored the Weber Housing Authority in a project that offers incentives for first
         time home buyers. The county has received grants for restroom modification for the County Library and
         an emergency housing rehabilitation project administered through the Weber Housing Authority,
         development of ADA compliant campsites, ADA-compliant door hardware installment at the fairgrounds,
         installment of storm drains in low to moderate income communities, wheelchair ramps installment at
         street intersections, as well as sponsoring non-profit agencies such as Your Community Connection,
         Ogden Weber Community Action Agency, and Catholic Community Services. All possible projects in
         which funding may be applied for are indicated in the regional Consolidated Plan and is available for
         review in the Weber County Planning Office.
         Mr. Meyerhoffer had not been contacted by any non-profit agencies that were at the CDBG How to Apply
         Workshop requesting the county to sponsor them or wanting to submit a pre-application for a grant.
