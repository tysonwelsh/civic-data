GPZ-6-2020
Russell Platt
4565 W 3500 S
General Plan change from general commercial to high density residential and a zone change
from C-2 to RM
3.07 acres

Russell Platt has submitted a General Plan/zone change application for a 3.07-acre parcel located
at 4565 West 3500 South. The property is currently zoned C-2 (general commercial) with a
General Plan designation of general commercial. The proposed zone is RM (multiple unit
dwelling residential) and the proposed General Plan designation is high density residential (12 to
20 units/acre).

Surrounding zones include R-1-8 (single unit dwelling residential, minimum lot size 8,000 square
feet) to the north and south and C-2 to the east and west. Surrounding land uses include a City
park to the north on the north side of 3500 South, the North Jordan Canal and single unit
dwellings to the south, self-storage units to the east and EZ Auto Sales and Advanced RV to the
west. The site includes a building first built in 1956 that has since been expanded and remodeled.
The property has been licensed as Pyramid Auto Sales since 2017.

The applicant is planning to demolish the existing showroom, detail shop and auto mechanic shop
and develop the property into a 50-unit townhome project. The density of the proposed project is
16.3 units/acre. The attached concept plan and letter from the applicant provide details on the
proposed project. Also included are renderings of the proposed 3-story townhomes. The applicant
has constructed similar townhomes within the Cobalt Landing development at 3595 South
Redwood Road. More details about the proposal are covered below under the development
agreement discussion.

According to the City’s zoning ordinance, zone changes to the RM zone can only be considered
by the City if the following four conditions are met:

   1. For properties without existing Structures which are proposed to be developed, the
      property must be a minimum of one acre and the Density must not exceed 12 units per
      acre. For properties with existing Structures proposed to be redeveloped, the property
      must be a minimum of 20,000 square feet and the Density must not exceed 20 units per
      acre. For properties that are west of Redwood Road and within one-half mile of the West
      Valley Central light rail station, the Decker Lake light rail station or the Redwood
      Junction light rail station, the Density may exceed 20 units per acre;

   2. The property shall meet any one of the following three requirements: (i) have access and
      Frontage on a Street with a planned Right-of-way width of at least 80 feet (for properties
      under two acres) or 100 feet (for properties two acres or larger) as indicated on the Major
      Street Plan; (ii) be adjacent to existing multifamily residential development on two sides;
      or (iii) be located west of Redwood Road and within one-half mile of the West Valley
      Central light rail station, the Decker Lake light rail station, or the Redwood Junction light
      rail station;

   3. A Development Agreement must be proposed with the zone change application that
      addresses Dwelling Unit sizes, exterior materials, architecture, Landscaping, and project
      Amenities; and

   4. A traffic impact study prepared by a registered traffic engineer is required for projects
      with 100 or more Dwelling Units.

This application meets the first condition in that the property is over 20,000 square feet, includes
structures proposed to be redeveloped and has a proposed density below 20 units/acre. For the
second condition, the subject property has access and frontage on 3500 South, which has a
planned right-of-way width of 115’. On the third condition, the applicant’s development
agreement standards are addressed later in this report. The fourth condition does not apply
because the project is under 100 units.
As the Commission evaluates this application, there are essentially two questions to consider. The
first question is are townhomes an appropriate use for this property? The second question is a
follow up question to the first. If townhomes are an appropriate use, is the proposed quality
sufficient to meet the goals of the City?

The points below may help the Commission answer the first question.

      There are no stub streets to the adjoining residential development to the south.
      The property has relatively narrow frontage (125’) for a commercial property and is 662’
       deep at its deepest point.
      The online real estate database company Zillow shows neighboring home values to the
       south ranging from the mid $200s to the low $300s. According to the applicant, the sales
       price of proposed townhomes would range from $335,000 to $395,000.
      3500 South is currently a five-lane arterial street that is serviced by UTA bus routes 35
       and 35M. The Regional Transportation Plan adopted by the Wasatch Front Regional
       Council shows this section of 3500 South being widened to 7 lanes and transit service
       becoming more frequent sometime between 2031 and 2040.
      Average annual daily traffic (AADT) on this segment of 3500 South was 30,000 for 2017.
       In 2004, AADT was also at 30,000 for this segment of 3500 South and has fluctuated
       between 25,000 and 30,000 since then. A five-lane arterial can handle a traffic volume of
       26,500 at a level of service (LOS) C and 30,500 at an LOS D. According to the Institute of
       Transportation Engineers, townhomes generate 5.81 trips per day.
      There are some commercial services like a convenience store, post office, dentist, Papa
       John’s Pizza and Family Dollar within walking distance (about ¼ mile) to the east and
       west along 3500 South.
      Staff recently compared the property and sales tax revenue generated from small used car
       lots with the revenue generated from townhomes. Depending upon the density and value
       of the townhomes, townhomes will generate roughly the same amount of revenue or more.
       Staff is quick to point out that this is just one consideration and should not be the sole
       basis for a land use decision.

If the Commission believes that townhomes are appropriate here, the quality of the proposal
should then be considered.

       Dwelling Unit Sizes
       The proposed unit sizes are 1,750 square feet for the west units and 1,950 square feet for
       the east units. Each unit will have three bedrooms and a bonus room that can be used as an
       office, bedroom or all-purpose room. The ordinance requires a minimum area of 1,400
       square feet for a three-bedroom townhome and 1,700 square feet for a four bedroom
       townhome.

       Exterior Materials
       The ordinance states the following concerning exterior materials: “All multi-unit
       residential Building exteriors shall be brick, stucco, stone, tile, metal panels or fiber
       cement siding. With the exception of brick or stone which may be used on the entire
       Building exterior, no single material shall comprise more than 60 percent of a Building’s
       exterior. Where stucco, tile, metal panels or fiber cement siding are used, at least 20
       percent of the Building’s exterior shall be brick or stone. Wood and vinyl may be used as
       trim, soffits and/or accent materials only.” The proposed materials are stucco, fiber cement
       siding and tile. Without a development agreement exception, a minimum of 20% brick or
       stone will be needed.

       Architecture
       The architecture will be subject to the City’s multi-unit residential design standards.
       Assuming this application is approved, a comprehensive review of the building plans will
       verify compliance with the design standards. Based on a preliminary review of the
       elevations by staff, there is an issue with the units along the east side of the property. The
       ordinance states: “No more than 50% of the first floor of the Primary Façade shall be
       devoted to garage doors or Carports. This percentage may be increased to a maximum of
       60% for garage doors if: windows are used in the garage doors; the garages are recessed at
       least two feet from the rest of the Primary Façade or other enhancements are made to the
       garage doors. Examples of other enhancements include using decorative wood or breaking
       up the mass of two car garage doors by using two separate single car doors or by using
       panels or other treatments to make the door appear to be divided into two sections.”

       It appears that the garages make up more than 60% of the first floor of the primary façade
       of the front-loaded units. The applicant has included some features like windows in the
       garage doors, offsets between garage doors, cantilevered living space above the garages,
       multiple balconies and covered entries to deemphasize the garages. An exception to the
       60% rule can be called out in the development agreement, or the City can simply stick to
       the 60% rule.


       Landscaping/Open Space
       Townhome developments require at least 50% of the site as open space. According to the
       applicant, the open space percentage is 57%, which includes the rooftop decks. Without
       the rooftop decks, the open space percentage is 45%. During the study session concern
       was expressed about the lack of green space at grade. As an alternative to the applicant’s
       proposal, the Planning Commission could recommend a development agreement provision
       that capped the number of units to 48. That would create more open space and break up
       the long row of units along the east side.

       Project Amenities
       The City’s multi-family residential design standards require projects with 50 to 75 units to
       include at least two amenities from a list included in the ordinance. The following
       amenities are ones proposed by the applicant that count toward the two required:
       courtyards, rooftop patios, tot lot/playground, walking trail, and BBQ and picnic facilities.

Parking was also discussed during the study session. There are 174 parking spaces on the concept
plan with 100 in garages, 40 in driveways and 34 in stalls along the west side of the street. The
resulting parking ratio is 3.5 stalls/unit (2 stalls/unit is required). For comparison, the Erin Hills
project on the southeast corner of 3500 South 5600 West was approved at 3.2 stalls/unit and the
Cobalt Landing project just south of Paras Furniture on Redwood Road has a parking ratio of 3.9
stalls/unit.

The City’s Major Street Plan shows the future right-of-way width along 3500 South as 115’. The
current right-of-way half width is 40’. Staff recommends that the development agreement include
a provision to require 17.5’ of right-of-way dedication along 3500 South.

If the Planning Commission supports this application, the following development agreement
provisions are recommended based on the applicant’s project description as well as suggestions
from staff:

      The maximum number of units shall be 50.
      There shall be two different types of townhome units within the development – units with
       front-loaded garages and units with rear-loaded garages. The minimum size for the units
       with front-loaded garages shall be 1,950 square feet above grade excluding the garage.
       The minimum size for the units with rear-loaded garages shall be 1,750 square feet above
       grade excluding the garage.
      All townhomes shall be built substantially like the renderings provided by the applicant.
       All requirements in Chapter 7-11 Part 4 of the Zoning Ordinance (multi-unit residential
       design standards) shall be met. This means that the proposed townhomes may need to be
       revised as part of the subdivision approval process to meet the ordinance standards. The
       following are two specific exceptions granted for this development:
           o Exterior materials for all townhomes shall be tile on the first level and fiber cement
               siding and stucco on the upper levels.
           o The garage door percentage of the first floor of the front-loaded garage units may
               exceed 60% as shown in the proposed renderings.
      Project amenities shall include courtyards, rooftop patios on all units, a tot lot/playground,
       a walking path and BBQ and picnic facilities.
      An entry feature shall be included at the entrance. The details of the entry feature shall be
       determined during the subdivision review process.
      All units with front-loaded garages shall include a driveway with a minimum depth of 18’.
      There shall be at least one parking space along the private street for each unit with a rear-
       loaded garage.
      The developer shall dedicate right-of-way along 3500 South in accordance with the Major
       Street Plan, which shows the future right-of-way on 3500 South at 115’.
      A vinyl fence or masonry wall shall be installed along the south property line.
      A 6’ tall masonry wall shall be installed along those property lines adjacent to EZ Auto
       Sales where such a wall does not already exist.
      Upon completion of all of the on-site improvements, the developer shall provide a letter to
       the City from an independent engineering firm which certifies that all of the on-site
       improvements are completed as shown on the plans approved by the City. For the
       purposes of this provision, on-site improvements shall include landscaping, irrigation,
       private streets, sidewalks, parking, lighting, entrance features, fencing, playground
       equipment, picnic tables and any other outdoor recreational amenity.

Staff Alternatives:
   1. Approval. This General Plan/zone change should be approved subject to a development
      agreement that includes the standards outlined in the staff report.

   2. Approval. This General Plan/zone change should be approved subject to a development
      agreement that includes the standards outlined in the staff report with the following
      changes:
          a. The maximum number of units shall be 48. This will allow for more open space
             and more parking.
          b. The exterior material requirements in the ordinance shall be followed with no
             exception.
          c. The garage door percentage of the first floor of the front-loaded garage units shall
             not exceed 60%.

   3. Continuance.

TerraForm Commercial Subdivision
4700 South 5600 West
M Zone
2 Lots
5 Acres


BACKGROUND:

Elliott Smith, representing Robert J. Defa et al, is requesting minor subdivision approval for the
TerraForm Commercial Subdivision. The subdivision is located on the northwest corner of 4700
South and 5600 West. The subdivision is bordered on the north by vacant ground zoned Agriculture
and the west by storage units.


STAFF/AGENCY COMMENTS:

As this application will be followed up by various site plan reviews, staff and agency comments
will be better addressed at that time. The purpose for the commercial subdivision application is to
simply divide the property into 2 lots for future development.


ISSUES:

The proposed subdivision is in a manufacturing zone located on the northwest corner of 4700 South
and 5600 West. The subdivision will create two lots which will be developed in the future. Lot 1,
or the corner lot, will be approximately 1 acre in size, with the remaining property, or lot 2,
containing approximately 4 acres in size.

The corner lot is being proposed for a new 7-eleven store which is currently being reviewed by
staff. Access to the corner piece will be from both 5600 West and 4700 South. UDOT approval
will need to be granted for the 5600 West access. Access to lot 2 will be gained from 4700 South.
At the present time, there is no proposed use for that lot. Based on the site plan for the 7-eleven, it
appears that access on 4700 South extends into a portion of lot 2. If this is correct, a cross access
easement will need to be illustrated on the subdivision plat.

As part of this application, the developer will be dedicating a portion of 4700 South. The
intersection of 5600 West and 4700 South was built to a 53-foot half width. It is not anticipated
that a 53-foot half width will be needed west of the 7-eleven. The applicant will need to coordinate
with the City Engineering Division what dedication will be needed along 4700 South and where a
possible transition should occur.

The subdivision plat will simply provide the mechanism to divide the property as required by the
applicant. The subdivision plat will contain easements and other information applicable to the
division of property. Separate applications will be submitted for permitted and/or conditional use
on the two new lots.


STAFF ALTERNATIVES:

   1.      Approve the TerraForm Commercial Subdivision subject to a resolution of staff and
           agency concerns.

   2.      Continue the application for reasons determined in the Planning Commission meeting.

               applications. He also stated that the subject property's access points would be
               spread out as far away from the intersection as possible. Elliott Smith, the

               Commissioner Winter's question. He explained the subject property was under
               contract a year ago and fell out of the contract due to UDOT not approving access

subject property was recently rezoned from the M zone to the R-1-8 zone. The proposed
subdivision is bordered on the north by a new Maverik store, the east by existing residential and the
south by Rushford Court and west by 6000 West.


STAFF/AGENCY CONCERNS:

Fire Department:
               Existing Fire Hydrants will suffice for this project.

Granger Hunter Improvement District:

               Project will need to run availability for water, sewer and fire protection.

               Subject to design and review inspections.

               Authorization required of ditch/water users for any abandonment, relocation, piping

      The developer is proposing a minor subdivision to be known as Sadeghi Subdivision. The

      Access to the subdivision will be gained from Rushford Court. This street did not exist until
       a couple of years ago. It was constructed as part of UDOT’s Mountain View Corridor
       project. The subdivision to the east of the proposed subdivision used to gain access to 4100
       South. However, this access was eliminated due to the interchange at Mountain View
       Corridor and 4100 South. Rushford Court was modified, and a new segment was built in
       front of the proposed subdivision. A future plat amending the Hunter Cove Subdivision to
       the east will be presented to the Planning Commission at a later date.
     The subdivision slopes downward from south to north. The downward slope may allow
      walkout basements, which may help in the reduction of grading and drainage problems that
      might otherwise occur. A soils report was submitted for the property. Ground water was
      not encountered to a depth of 12.5 feet.

     The subdivision is located to the south of the recently completed Maverik gas station. The
      property was not zoned for residential land uses at the time the City approved the Maverik
      store. Therefore, the masonry wall that was built adjacent to the residential homes to the
      east, was not built along the boundary of this property. The City does not require fencing
      on standard subdivision developments, but we assume that some type of fencing will be
      installed. Staff recommends that the developer coordinate fencing along the common
      property line with the Maverik owners. It is simply a suggestion to see what fencing options
      might be available to help reduce the potential for graffiti along the commercial side of the
      fence.
