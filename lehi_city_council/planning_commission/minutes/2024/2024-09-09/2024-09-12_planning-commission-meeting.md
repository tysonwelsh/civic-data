3.2) Public hearing and recommendation of Brad Tronson's request for review of the
Tronson General Plan Amendment on 0.66 acres located at 635 West State Street, changing
the designation from Commercial to Heavy Commercial.
Katie Bussell presented information on the agenda item. General Plan Amendment will be
consistent of properties. DRC has one comment, which does not recommend commercial
designation on this property.

Commissioner Eyre asked when General Plan Amendments can be submitted. Commissioner
Kunze asked if this was already being used as commercial, which it is.

The applicant, Brad Tronson, was present. There is no light or heavy commercial designation. He
would like to build a building for commercial use with interior office warehouse. He would like
to build something like Dennis Schoonover's establishment.

Commission Chair Jackson opened the public hearing.

Teresa Gonzalez, who is adjacent to the property, expressed her opposition to the GPA zone
change. She stated that it went against the adopted general plan, was adjacent to residential areas,
and would impact planned residential developments. She mentioned that the barrier and buffer
zone should have been addressed years ago but had not yet been resolved, despite previous
commitments. Ms. Gonzalez requested that the property remain designated as light industrial and
based on the recommendations of the planning committee and the general plan goals and
policies, urged that the item not be approved for a designation change. While she acknowledged
the potential for noise, she did not feel the area was appropriate for the new designation.

Katie read one online comment which was also left by Teresa Gonzalez.

Commission Chair Jackson closed the public hearing.

Brad Tronson mentioned that two nearby residents had spoken to him, and neither had an issue
with the construction of a building and the enclosure of materials. However, he noted that the
building they plan to construct does not meet the light industrial designation. He also stated that
there will be multiple tenants.

Commissioner Jones asked Katie to clarify the definitions of heavy commercial, commercial, and
light commercial use. Commissioner Jones expressed concerns that the proposed development
might not meet the criteria for conforming with the neighboring areas.

The Commissioners inquired whether the property currently meets the buffering requirements.
Katie Bussell noted that if the requirements were approved, they would be reviewed during the
site plan process. The commissioners questioned why the property had previously been approved
as an island. Kim Struthers explained that it originally matched the surrounding properties.
While the site plan had been approved for a previous applicant, the property was never
developed as planned.

Commissioner Eyre expressed concern that approving this could set a precedent for further
exceptions, especially since it does not comply with the current code.

Commissioner Jackson agreed with Commissioners Eyre and Jones. He sympathized with the
applicant, noting that the property is a prime piece of real estate.

                Motion: Commissioner Jones moved to recommend denial of Brad Tronson’s
                request for review of the Tronson General Plan Amendment on 0.66 acres
                located at 635 West State Street, changing the designation from Commercial
                to Heavy Commercial; including all DRC comments, noting the development
                did not meet the findings listed in the report. Commissioner Everett seconded the
                motion.

                Vote: Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Everett, yes; Commissioner
                Eyre, yes; Commissioner Kunze, yes; Commissioner Jones, yes. The motion
                passed unanimously.

3.3) Consideration of Texas Instruments request for approval of the LFAB 2 Site Plan
including exceptions to the design standards located at 4000 North Flash Drive.
Katie Bussell presented information on the agenda item, noting that the DRC had made four
redline comments. Kim Struthers stated that if the exceptions were not approved, the applicant
would need to revise their site plan for future approval.

Commissioner Everett inquired about the traffic calming survey and whether it was
discretionary, based on a recommendation from the City Traffic Engineer.
Commissioner Kunze asked if the proposal was intended to match the existing building. She
commented that it seemed like a lot of exceptions were being requested but noted that the
proposal matched what was already in place. Kim agreed with her assessment.

Commissioner Eyre asked about the landscaping and whether an appropriate amount was
planned for planting. Kim confirmed that the plan still met the code requirements and that it
included native trees and bushes.

Commissioner Everett asked about the reduction in bike parking, indicating that it did not seem
like an unreasonable request. TI had provided numbers for current pedestrians who were riding
bikes and using the parking. Everett also noted that the utility screening appeared to include the
planting of trees and a berm and asked if this required an exception. Kim stated that the situation
was unusual but that it still met the level of development required.

The applicant, Barry Mccoin from Page Supplement Architectural firm, acknowledged that there
were numerous exceptions but stated that they had reviewed the green space and buffering. He
noted that they had tried to address all concerns within reason. He explained that the building is
large, with varying elevations and facades that are stepped back, while maintaining the required
height. The utility boxes, which carry massive voltage, are located behind the building, serving
as a natural buffer. The building also functions as a sound barrier, with some of the dock
operations positioned on the east side to minimize disruption to nearby residential areas.

Barry mentioned that the glass designation was not feasible for the building’s intended use.
However, they were following the LEED requirements for bike parking, though the current
facility's bike parking is not heavily used. He clarified that the setback had been modified to
avoid the need for an additional request, and they were not encroaching on the setback as the
zoning lot line had been adjusted. He explained that the building could not be more than 50 feet
away, but without the exception, they would not be able to connect to the existing building.

Regarding landscaping, Barry stated that they proposed planting 1,200 trees, though they would
prefer to plant fewer. Kim Struthers noted that the code would require approximately 2,200 trees.
Commissioner Everett remarked that even 1,200 trees seemed excessive, especially since the
project was connected to an existing property. The applicant added that much of the property
would remain in its native state. After hearing the rationale behind the exceptions, the
Commissioners agreed that the applicant was making a good faith effort to meet and address the
code requirements.

Commissioner Eyre reviewed the possible findings on the item, and finding number two allows
the exceptions. Kim Stated all the exceptions are acceptable and can be allowed.

Commission Chair asked for further discussion.

                Motion: Commissioner Kunze moved to approve Texas Instruments request
                for approval of the LFAB 2 Site Plan including exceptions to the design
                standards located at 4000 North Flash Drive; including all DRC comments and
                findings stated in the report, also noting the exceptions for the design standard.
                Commissioner Eyre seconded the motion.
                Vote: Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Everett, yes; Commissioner
                Eyre, yes; Commissioner Kunze, yes; Commissioner Jones, yes. The motion
                passed unanimously.

3.4) Public hearing and consideration of Compass Billboards? request for conditional use
approval of pylon signs with electronic message displays for EV Auto located at 1060 West
State Street.
Katie Bussell presented information on the agenda item. There were 8 redline DRC comments.

Commissioner Everett asked for clarification on the total sign height.

The applicant, Braxton Shank from Compass Billboard, was present. He stated that they were
comfortable with a 40-foot height due to the freeway’s grade. Commissioner Everett inquired
about the electronic features of the sign, and the applicant explained that it was a standard sign
with automatic dimming to reduce light pollution. When asked by Commissioner Everett if the
sign could be turned off during non-operating hours, the applicant responded that the sign’s
location made it barely visible. Commissioner Eyre asked whether charging services would be
offered, as that could affect the hours of operation. He also mentioned that the hours of operation
could influence the conditional use. Commissioner Kunze raised concerns about light pollution
affecting nearby residents, but the applicant clarified that there were no homes currently on the
property. Kim Struthers noted that the site plan included charging stations, although they were
likely intended for inventory purposes. The applicant then inquired whether operational hours
were addressed in the code, and the commissioners clarified that they could impose regulations if
light pollution became an issue.

Commission Chair Jackson opened the public hearing.

Adam Olsen expressed concern about the automated sign, fearing it would impact his residence.
While he had no objection to the sign being installed, he was worried about potential light
pollution, particularly from flashing and animated elements, noting that an existing sign already
affected their homes.

Commission Chair Jackson then closed the public hearing.

Commissioner Everett suggested that a reasonable condition might be to reduce the sign’s
operating hours to mitigate the impact on residents. Commissioner Eyre agreed with the idea of
turning off the sign but questioned whether it was reasonable to impose such a condition.
Commission Chair Jackson pointed out that the Ken Garff sign, though taller, was located farther
away.

Mr. Shank acknowledged the concerns about how signs could affect residences and emphasized
that he did not want to be a nuisance to the surrounding community. He asked the commissioners
to consider either shielding the sign or setting conditions on hours of operation, noting that
another option could be to keep the sign static during non-operating hours.

Commissioner Eyre expressed his personal dislike of signs, viewing them as a blight on
communities, but also recognized their value for business advertising. He felt that requiring the
sign to be turned off entirely might be excessive but suggested that offering the option of either
shielding or turning off the sign could be left to the developer.

Katie Bussell clarified that the code specified 10:00 p.m. as the cutoff time for operating hours.

                Motion: Commissioner Eyre moved to approve Compass Billboards request for
                conditional use approval of pylon signs with electronic message displays for
                EV Auto located at 1060 West State Street; including all DRC comments and
                findings stated in the report, noting the exception for the height due to the grade
                of the freeway. Commissioner Kunze seconded the motion.

                Vote: Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Everett, yes; Commissioner
                Eyre, yes; Commissioner Kunze, yes; Commissioner Jones, yes. The motion
                passed unanimously.

3.8) Public hearing and recommendation of Lehi City’s request for review of a
Development Code Amendment to Chapters 12-A and 12-B, adding some additional
requirements to grading.
Katie Bussell presented information on the agenda item.

Katie pulled up the map to show some of the occurrences. Commission Chair Jackson asked for
clarification on misdemeanor charges and the associated penalties. Craig explained that the code
enforcement team worked with residents who had encroached on City property. He outlined the
justification for keeping these violations as misdemeanors, noting that it was not feasible for
every code enforcement violation to address all issues. The cases that were brought to
prosecution typically involved ongoing problems. Katie added that most complaints were
initially sent to the planning staff, who would issue a letter asking the resident to contact the City
for compliance. If the resident failed to comply, the case would then be forwarded to
prosecution.

Commission Chair Jackson opened the public hearing.

Brandon Rudolph spoke about the seriousness of each misdemeanor, noting that most cases were
sent to prosecution and resolved there. He confirmed that his experiences aligned with what
Craig had stated.

Commission Chair Jackson then closed the public hearing.

Commissioner Everett emphasized the seriousness of residents developing on City property.
Commissioner Eyre asked for clarification regarding the Jordan River Trail. Kim explained that
the trail was on Utah County property, so the county would need to take a position on the matter.
The Commission Chair inquired about the possibility of charging daily misdemeanors. Craig and
the Commission discussed the process of notifying the tenant and allowing them a grace period
to respond or contact the City. Commissioner Everett pointed out that while there was discretion
in enforcement, compliance often could not be achieved without penalties.

                Motion: Commissioner Jones moved to recommend approval of Lehi City’s
                request for review of a Development Code Amendment to Chapters 12-A and
                12-B, including all DRC comments, with the findings stated in the report. Noting
                a grace period of 30 days of notice before a misdemeanor Class B charge can be
                brought to prosecution. Commissioner Everett seconded the motion.

                Vote: Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Everett, yes; Commissioner
                Eyre, yes; Commissioner Kunze, yes; Commissioner Jones, yes. The motion
                passed unanimously.
