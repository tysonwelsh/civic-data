REGULAR AGENDA ITEMS FOR CONSIDERATION:

PRESENTATION: 2019 WASHINGTON COUNTY FAIR

Washington County Fair Director Susi LaFaele reported on the 2019 Washington County
Fair, stating that there were more cars on Wednesday and Thursday nights than in the
previous ten (10) years. The demolition derby was sold out on Saturday night and the
petting zoo was successful with the help of surrounding towns and cities. Final attendance
numbers are not yet available.

A replica of the Tomb of the Unknown Solider was brought in and sponsored by the
American Legion Post 90 and the St. George Exchange Club.

Ms. LaFaele mentioned there were over 400 volunteer hours logged during the fair and
expressed appreciation to everyone who contributed to the fair’s success.

CONSIDERATION OF RESOLUTION NO. _R-2019-2503, A RESOLUTION
APPROVING PUBLIC DEFENDER AGREEMENTS

County Administrator Nicholle Felshaw explained that the County was awarded a grant
from the Indigent Defense Commission. Part of the grant award included funding for one
(1) additional public defense service contract. The new contract will handle specialty court,
such as drug court, and arraignments. It is being recommended for Doug Terry and his firm
to take on the responsibilities of the specialty contract. This transition has opened up a
General Defender Contract, which was advertised and from the interviews held, the
recommendation is for Jacob Fisher to be awarded the general contract.

Deputy County Attorney Eric Clarke explained that the Southern Utah Shooting Sports
Park is anticipating to expand in size to include an additional twenty (20) acres adjacent to

an environmental assessment on the twenty (20) acre parcel.

It is recommended for Bighorn Archeological Consultants, LLC to perform these services
for the County as they submitted the lowest responsible proposal.

CONSIDERATION _ OF RESOLUTION NO. _R-2019-2509, A RESOLUTION
APPROVING PROFESSIONAL SERVICES AGREEMENTS FOR SCHOOL-
BASED THERAPY FOR VICTIMS OF CRIMES

Washington County Children’s Justice Center (CJC) Director Kristy Pike explained that
167 therapy hours have been provided to 57 individuals year to date.

The total amount received from the Victims of Crime Act (VOCA) grant for the fiscal year
is $235,753 which is 40% of the overall funding received by the CJC. The VOCA grant is
being used to provide mental health services in area schools to juvenile victims of crime
for the purpose of trauma-informed therapy.

Ms. Pike explained that these funds do not come from tax payer dollars but are set aside
from restitution paid by offenders.

It is being recommend that Tyler Gordon and Jaime Christiansen fulfill the two (2)
advertised positions for professional services.

MOTION: Motion by Chairman Cox to Adopt Resolution No. R-2019-2509, A
Resolution Approving Professional Services Agreements For School-
Based Therapy for Victims of Crimes. Motion seconded by
Commissioner Almquist and carried by unanimous vote, with
Commissioners Cox and Almquist voting aye.
