I.     Welcome – Mayor Dahle.
Mayor Dahle called the meeting to order at approximately 6:00 p.m. He read into the record the
Order on Public Meetings of the City Council of the City of Holladay.

II.     Introduction of New UPD Lieutenant – Chief Hoyal.
Chief Hoyal announced that Tyler Ackerman is the Department’s New Police Lieutenant after being
promoted from the position of Sergeant. Lieutenant Ackerman introduced himself and was grateful
for the opportunity to work for the City of Holladay.

Chief Hoyal provided a brief update on recent police activity in the City of Holladay.

IV.     Continued Public Hearing on Proposed General Plan Amendment for the Area Located at
        Approximately 2450 East and 4680 South, Currently Designated as MDR-S (Medium-
        Density Residential Stable) to LDR-S (Low-Density Residential Stable).
Mayor Dahle asked for additional public comments for the continued public hearing. He
acknowledged written documentation received from several constituents since the last meeting. He
clarified the Council’s plan to take public comment on the residential in-fill on September 17 and
continue the public hearing until that date based on a nexus between both topics.

Robin Huff - 2465 E Kentucky Ave. She commented on the proposal to change to a lower density
area. She read the names of residents in the Kentucky Avenue area who support the proposed change.

Greg Adcock - 2445 Kentucky Ave. He echoed Ms. Huff’s concerns. He stated that the residents
enjoy the low density and was concerned that higher density proposals will negatively impact the
character of the area.

V.      Consideration of Resolution 2020-27 Approving and Authorizing the Execution of an
        Interlocal Cooperation Agreement with the Town of Alta, the Town of Brighton, Bluffdale
        City, Copperton Metro Township, Cottonwood Heights City, Draper City, Emigration
        Canyon Metro Township, Herriman City, Holladay City, Kearns Metro Township, Magna
        Metro Township, Midvale City Corp., City of Millcreek, Murray City, Riverton City, City of
        South Salt Lake, and White City Metro Township Relating to the Conduct of the
        Community Development Block Grant Program, Emergency Solutions Grant Program, and
        the Home Investment Partnership Program.
Mayor Dahle reported that the above matter was being brought forward based on Coronavirus Aid,
Relief, and Economic Security (“CARES”) Act funds being administered through the County. He
stated that the processing of the funds must be set forth in an Interlocal Cooperation Agreement signed
by the participating entities. Council Member Fotheringham supported the proposed resolution.

Council Member Fotheringham moved to adopt Resolution 2020-27. Council Member Durham
seconded the motion. The Council roll call vote was as follows: Council Members Gibbons, Petersen,
Durham, Fotheringham, Quinn and Mayor Dahle in favor. Resolution 2020-27 was approved
unanimously.

VIII. Council Reports and District Issues.
Council Member Quinn gave an update on Wander Lane discussions she was having pertaining to
traffic studies.

Council Member Durham reported on the Tree Committee Tree Giveaway on Sept. 26.

Council Member Gibbons indicated a few constituents reached out to him regarding traffic issues on
Highland Drive. He suggested that it might be a good topic for the Council to discuss at a later date.

Council Member Fotheringham asked about staff communications with one of his constituents. He
also mentioned an email about an urban camper on Kings Road that had since been moved. He
reported that the Summer Concert Series was an extraordinary event.

Mayor Dahle asked the Council if there had been input regarding how things are going in the local
schools and if there is anything the Council should be aware of. Council Member Petersen raised the
issue of transportation and the school parking lot being used inappropriately. Otherwise, she felt that
school officials were addressing outstanding concerns. She had received positive comments from
parents regarding lighting situations. Council Member Durham concurred and had not heard much
from his constituents.

Mayor Dahle reported that he participated in a Board of Health Zoom call earlier in the day. There
was discussion regarding a vaccine being available in November but the priority would be first
responders, health care workers, and care facilities. It was not expected to roll out to larger
communities until after the first of the year.

Council Member Fotheringham moved to adjourn the Council Meeting and reconvene in a Work
Meeting. Council Member Quinn seconded the motion. The motion passed with the unanimous
consent of the Council.

IX.     Recess City Council to a Work Meeting.
        a.     Discussion with Tree Committee – Dennis Roach, Chair.
Council Member Durham reviewed the most recent Tree Committee Meeting. The Committee was
interested in discussing with the Council the possibility of hiring an Arborist to conduct a tree
inventory and develop a maintenance plan for the City. The Committee would also like to discuss a
review of the Tree Ordinance to address the development of new parking lots and the opportunity for
tree resources to break-up parking lots and commercial areas. He commented that the City is very
lucky to have such a well-structured group of citizens serving on the Committee.

Mayor Dahle reported that there are a substantial number of trees in the City that are very mature and
in need of constant care. If the Tree Committee would develop a job description for staff, he would

       b.       Pavement Review, Jared Bunch, City Engineer.
Jared Bunch, City Engineer discussed a pavement program outline he developed based on a budget
with Salt Lake County of about $245,000 in material costs. He reviewed pavement and street
conditions in the City and identified several streets that need overlay and provided a map identifying
the specific road for the Council to review. He noted that if the City wishes to proceed with overlay
work this fall, the County could place the work on the agenda for the fall. If the Council does not
want to address the matter in the current budget, the work could be done next June.

Council Member Gibbons asked about Frontier Road. Ms. Chamness stated that for historical reasons
portions of Frontier were never paved, which requires more than a simple overlay. Mr. Bunch agreed
to review Frontier Road.

Council Member Durham asked about a portion of Clover Lane. Mr. Bunch mentioned that the
proposed sites will address roads that need an overlay unless the Council wants to increase the budget
to cover additional areas.

Mayor Dahle suggested that staff move forward with the proposed suggestions for this fall’s timeline.
Ms. Chamness reported that staff will ask the County about adding Clover Lane if it is under $40,000.

        c.      Discussion on 6200 South Monuments – Manager Chamness.
Ms. Chamness reported that damage was done to the 6200 S Holladay Blvd island as a result of work
done on at the intersection and the recent damage to the monument at 6200 and Highland. The repairs
will involve concrete work and re-plantings and asked the Council for feedback on timing. There
was discussion regarding the various issues and the monument and if it should be relocated. Ms.
Chamness estimated the cost to be approximately $20,000 for concrete work and planting. Mr. Bunch
described the actual concrete work to be done which would include a 3ft border around to provide a
buffer to staff working on the island. The consensus was to keep the monuments where they are and
move forward with the repair work proposed for the 6200 S Holladay Blvd island. As for the one on
6200 S and Highland there was discussion regarding how to best recoup the costs from the damage
and to maybe wait until the Council discusses the Holladay Crossroads zone.

The Council asked about adding reflectors to the monuments or curbing to make it more visable.

        d.      Discussion on Storm Water Fee.
Assistant to the City Manager, Holly Smith introduced the subject and asked the Council for policy
guidance based on the memo provided in the packet. She explained that the Council needs to
determine how many areas should be included in the Equivalent Residential Unit (“ERU”)
methodology. The Council discussed various homeowner impacts on the stormwater system as well
as assessments for commercial properties. The Council agreed to the proposed methodology.
