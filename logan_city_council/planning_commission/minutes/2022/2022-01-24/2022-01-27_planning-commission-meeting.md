 PC 22-005 Westfield Lofts [Design Review] Tony Johnson/HRT Holdings, LLC, authorized
 agent/owner, request a design review permit to modify the previously approved commercial
 design to include 26 residential units on the upper floor. The project is located at 1115 West
 130 South in the Commercial (COM) zone; TIN 05-107-1026; -1027; -1028. (Ellis
 Neighborhood)

STAFF: R. Holey reviewed the request for a design review permit to modify two previously
approved commercial buildings located at 1115 West 130 South to include 22 commercial spaces
on the main level and 44 residential apartments on the upper floor. The proposed site plan shows
a total of 184 parking stalls. As proposed, this project requires 88 parking stalls for the residential
units. Commercial parking is assessed and required at the time of a business license or building
permit. Both buildings combined contain 33,000 SF of ground-floor commercial space. At the high
end (if all businesses are retail or similar), the commercial parking requirement would be 132 stalls.
At the low end (if all businesses are shop/contractor space), the commercial parking requirement
would be 66 stalls. The submitted site plan shows a total of 184 parking stalls, including the
accessory garage buildings, for both the residential uses and the commercial space. As submitted,
the 184 stalls are in between the low end (154 stalls) and the high end (220 stalls). The
commission will need to determine the number of stalls that will need to be met.

The applicant is proposing an alternative parking plan that proposes 126 stalls for this project. This
would be a 19% reduction for the low-end and a 43% reduction for the high-end. The LDC allows a
maximum reduction of 25% in parking requirements.

Both buildings are proposed with the same design, one of the buildings will need to have at least
three of the seven design variations listed in the Land Development Code.

PROPONENT: Tony Johnson talked about how there was a lot of interest in their previous 300
South (Borden Lofts) project that also has warehouse space on the lower floor and apartments
on the upper floor. Businesses may purchase the entire space, run the business on the main
floor and lease out the apartment above. He mentioned the future parks and trails in the area will
be an amenity to the residents.

Commissioner Croshaw asked what kind of businesses have shown interest in the spaces. Tony
Johnson mentioned that with the market study that was done there was interest in the spaces for
office use with a shop area, architects, a title company, daycare, and a small store. He


mentioned a project in North Logan that has the same setup, and how it is doing well, and all
units are full.

PUBLIC: No public comments were made.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Croshaw said this is a unique project with an attractive design.
Commissioner Dickinson asked about condition number 4 and if a sidewalk could be required on
1200 West to connect the parking lot across the street. Kymber Housley, City Attorney, stated that
the planning commission is not able to require a crosswalk. R. Holley stated that there has to be
connectivity from the parking lot to the business. Tom Dickinson, Assistant City Engineer, stated
that there is an unmarked crosswalk at the intersection of 1200 West and 130 South and that Public
Works would determine after the project is complete if the area warranted an additional crosswalk or
stop sign at that intersection.

The commission discussed the number of parking stalls that need to be required as well as the
connectivity from the offsite parking lot. Mike DeSimone, Community Development Director, stated
that at some point 1200 West will be further developed and at that time additional improvements will
be made. K. Housley stated that the existing unmarked crosswalk will take pedestrians to the front
of the building. Commissioner Croshaw Asked if the proponent had any solutions to the
connectivity/crosswalk issue. Tony Johnson said that they are willing to do whatever they need to
create a safe connection. The commission agreed to not accept the alternate plan and require the
184 onsite parking stalls. Heare said he is fine conditioning it as is with 184 required parking stalls.

 PC 22-007 Land Development Code Amendment-Short Term Rentals [Code Amendment] Mike
 DeSimone/Logan City, request an amendment to the Land Development Code to Chapter 17.08
 “Neighborhood Residential Uses”; Chapter 17.11 “District and Corridor zone uses”; Chapter 17.37
 “Additional Development Standards”; and Chapter 17.62 “Definitions” to include standards for short
 term rentals in residential and commercial zones.


STAFF/PROPONENT: M. DeSimone reviewed the request for a Land Development Code
amendment to chapters 17.08, 17.11, 17.37, 17.62, to define minimum operational standards for
Short Term Rentals and what zones they are appropriate to be located in. Currently, short-term
rentals are not permitted in residential areas. A short-term rental is a transient commercial rental
accommodation, that is rented out for a period not exceeding 30 days.

Short Term Rental Pros:
   • May contribute supplementary financial assistance to homeowners, especially elderly
       homeowners on fixed incomes, enabling them to stay in their homes longer;
   • Makes efficient use of space in a home that is otherwise empty or may go unused;
   • Enable travelers to find less expensive alternatives to a traditional lodging such as a hotel
       room;
   • Provide a unique “experience” for both host’s and guests that may not be available in more
       traditional forms of lodging (e.g., hosting international guests or guests experiencing a
       unique residential neighborhood);
   • Helps property owners maximize the economic potential of their property by renting out
       unused bedroom space;
   • Provide additional lodging during peak demand events;
   • Provide an incentive to keep properties from being blighted; and
   • Contribute more money into the local economy as it is believed individuals staying in STR’s
       end up staying longer and spend more money in the communities in which they stay.
Short Term Rental Cons:
   • Can lead to Changes in the Character of a Neighborhood
   • Nuisance Issues – Noise, Trash, Parking, People
   • May not Pay Fair Share of Lodging Taxes
   • Occupancy Issues – we regulate occupancy in residential uses, why should a Short-Term
       Rental get a free pass?
   • Negatively Impacts Affordability of Available Housing
   • Negatively Impacts Availability of Affordable Housing
Proposed approach to location of Short-Term Rentals:
   • Permit Short Term Rentals in MFR Zones – MR 20, MR-30 & CR-40 (proposed 17.08.040)
   • Permit Short Term Rentals in Commercial Zones – TC-1, TC-2, COM, MU, CC, & GW
       (proposed 17.11.040)
   • Prohibit Short Term Rental in the SFR Zones (NR-2, NR-4, NR-6) & less dense MRF Zones
       (MR-9 & MR-12)
Proposed approach to operational standards:
   • Parking: Res Zones – 2 off street parking stalls/unit Commercial Zones – 1 off street
       parking stall/2 bedrooms
   • Limitations on RV’s, trailers, ATV’s, boats, etc. parking
   • Standard Nuisance Ordinance Reference - more than 3 violations/year results in revocation
       of rental license
   • No Multiple Contracts for Same Period of Time
   • Comply with Building & Fire Codes – modern egress windows and safety inspection with
       initial license
   • Pets, Signs – per LDC or LMC

PUBLIC: Lyle Lundburg mentioned that the state has not required this amendment to be done,
and he feels that the code does not need to be altered regarding short-term rentals. The
proposed amendments are not what Logan City residents need or want and the amendments
have no benefit to the general plan. There do not seem to be any short-term rentals that are


causing a negative impact to the surrounding neighborhood, he can’t however say the same
about long-term rentals.

Aubree Hazard stated that she is opposed to the proposed standards. It is a great opportunity for
families to supplement their income to afford rising housing costs. The short-term rentals are
more likely to be well kept and maintained. There should not be such a blanket restriction
affecting the rights of local families trying to supplement their income.

Erin Bennett reflected on her own experiences, good and bad, staying in short-term rentals.

Troy Hazard stated that there has not been any evidence provided that shows a negative impact
from short-term rentals.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Guth mentioned that she would like the code to be less restrictive
and allow short-term rentals to be permitted in all zones. The code can always be amended in the
future if issues arise. She agrees that short-term rentals cause fewer problems and are better kept
than some long-term rentals.

Commissioner Croshaw agreed with commissioner Guth. Commissioner Heare said he agrees with
the public comments and feels as proposed it is too restrictive. Short-term rentals should be
permitted in all zones, and the occupancy limit should be raised. Commissioner Lucero agreed with
permitting in all zones and increasing the occupancy. She would also like to see recreational parking
required off-street.

Commission discussed changing the proposed amendment to allow short-term rentals in all zones.
The occupancy will allow for 2 individuals per bedroom, plus 2 additional individuals. Recreational
vehicles must be parked outside of the public right-of-way.

R. Holley mentioned that there is not a high vacation demand in the city. There is a high demand
for student housing, and keep in mind that this could end up being an occupancy issue if a
homeowner rents to 10-12 college students on a 29-day lease.

Based on the Planning Commission direction, the proposed short term rental code amendment
language being forwarded to the Municipal Council is as follows:

       §17.37.130. Short Term Rentals (Vacation Rental)

 Park Standards [Code Amendment] Mike DeSimone/Logan City, request an amendment to the
 Land Development Code to Chapter 17.11 “District and Corridor zone uses”; Chapter 17.15
 “Development Standards: Public Zones”; Chapter 17.37 “Additional Development Standards”, to
PROPONENT:
 remove RV parks and self-storage warehousing from the commercial services and industrial
 zones and include minimum development standards for RV parks.


STAFF/PROPONENT: Mike DeSimone reviewed the proposed code amendment to the Land
Development Code chapters 17.11, 17.15, 17.37 to eliminate RV & tent Campgrounds, and
Commercial Self Storage from the CS & IP zone, to change impound tow lot in CS from permitted to

conditionally permitted, and to include standards for new RV parks. The changes to the Land Use
Table are needed because the amount of vacant industrial land is declining therefore it is important
to high grade uses in the IP & CS zones in order to permit uses that provide more employment
opportunities and a better tax base for the community, consider the land consumption vs return of
benefits, and to permit RV & tent campgrounds in the appropriate zone, Recreation, not IP or CS.
The proposed Code Amendment also includes proposed RV park standards in 17.37.

Proposed RV park standards:
   • Minimum Land Area – 3 Acres
   • Maximum Density – 20 RV stalls/acre
   • RV Space Improvements – hardened surface, minimum space dimensions, hookups, parking
   • Park Setbacks & Buffer Area – 25’ Perimeter Open Space Setback and Buffer
   • Internal Roadways – hardened surface, min. widths, parking
   • Minimum Open Space on Overall Site – 20%
   • Landscaping Required per LDC
   • Management Stalls – 1 stall per 20 stalls can be designated for Management use & year-
      round occupancy
   • Length of Stay – 30 consecutive days Max & up to 20% of total stalls designated for long
      term occupancy up to 180 days
   • Limitations on Accessory Vehicle, Boat, ATV, etc., Parking
   • Signage – comply with LDC

Commissioner Guth asked if there are any RV Parks currently in the proposed restricted zones. Mike
said there is not.
Dickinson asked if there were any available recreational land for an RV park. M. DeSimone stated
that currently there isn’t any vacant and available land in the Recreation zone

PUBLIC: No public comments were made.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Guth stated that the land should be prioritized for the highest and
best uses. Commissioner Lucero and commissioner Dickinson agreed. Commissioner Dickinson
questioned Condition No. 3 requiring all RV spaces to be improved with a hardened surface
(concrete or asphalt), and at a minimum, hookups for water, sewer, and electric. Commissioner
Guth asked if there could be a percentage of spaces that would not require hardened surfaces or
the minimum hookups. M. Desimone said that parking lots are required to be paved, it's cleaner.
Commissioner Heare stated that we are in an urban setting and there are plenty of places nearby
where someone could park their RV in the dirt if they wanted to.

MOTION: Commissioner Guth moved to forward a recommendation of approval on PC 22-008 to
the Municipal Council with the findings for approval as listed below. Commissioner Lucero seconded
the motion.

RECOMMENDED FINDINGS FOR APPROVAL
1. Utah State Law authorizes local Planning Commission to recommend ordinance changes to the
   legislative body (Municipal Council).
2. The Code Amendments are done in conformance with the requirements of Title 17.51 of the
   Logan Municipal Code.
3. The proposed Code Amendments are consistent with the Logan City General Plan.
4. The proposed amendments provide reasonable standards for new RV Parks and clarify
   appropriate uses in the CS and IP zones.
5. No public comment has been received regarding the proposed amendments.
