9. Public Hearing(s) / Action Items

    a. Resolution No. 20-54 – Authorizing the Mayor to execute a Sales Tax Revenue
       Sharing Agreement with Costco Wholesale Corporation, as an Inducement for
       Costco to Construct a Wholesale Warehouse in Riverton

Ryan Carter, City Attorney, presented information from a Memorandum regarding a proposal to
construct a new wholesale warehouse at the approximately location of 13200 South and 4570
West. He said a component of Costco’s proposal to construct such a warehouse would include
an Agreement with the City to share sales tax revenue that a new Costco warehouse would
generate in Riverton. He said that Costco proposes to share, on a 50-50 basis, sales tax
generated at a Riverton Costco warehouse and remitted to the City by the State of Utah. The
amount to be paid to Costco would be capped at the amount of $6,250,000. He said the City’s
current sales tax estimates project that the capped amount would be reached approximately ten
years after a Costco warehouse opens in Riverton. However, the City expects to pay
approximately $625,000 to Costco annually while retaining the same amount. Mr. Carter said
that it was important to remain mindful that options were available for Costco to locate in the
vicinity, but outside of Riverton’s municipal territory. Therefore, any sales tax agreement should
be recognized as an inducement to bring a Costco warehouse to Riverton.
Mr. Carter then presented the following information regarding UCA statutes:

    “Pursuant to Utah Code Ann. § 10-8-2, the City Council may share sales tax revenues
    otherwise due to the City if the Riverton City Council finds and determines that the value
    which the underlying project provides to the citizens of Riverton equals the value of the
    sales tax provided to Costco. To explain, under § 10-8-2, a municipal legislative body
    may appropriate money for “corporate purposes only.” However a “corporate purpose”
    includes, “any purpose that, in the judgment of the municipal legislative body, provides
    for the safety, health, prosperity, moral well-being, peace, order, comfort, or convenience
    of the inhabitants of the municipality.” That said, the statute and opinions issued by the
    Utah Supreme Court require that the value of monies appropriated by a municipal
    legislative body must be at least equal to the value of the benefits which flow to the
    community by virtue of the appropriation. In balancing the values of the appropriation
    against the community benefit, the statute allows the municipal legislative body to decide
    what constitutes the value received, and the legislative body’s determination of the value
    received is presumed valid unless a person can show the determination was arbitrary,
    capricious, or illegal.

    Utah Code Ann. § 10-8-2 also requires that the benefits be compared with the value of
    the appropriation in some form of a study. In the present case, this means the City
    Council must approve a study that concludes the value of having a Costco warehouse
    locate in Riverton equals or exceeds the value of the sales tax which we provide Costco,
    as an inducement to locate in Riverton. Again, the City Council may take into account
    any number of tangible or intangible benefits which Riverton citizens receive from the
    location of a Costco warehouse in Riverton.”

Mr. Carter reviewed several of the benefits the community would receive if a Costco warehouse
were constructed in Riverton, under the inducement created by a Sales Tax Revenue Sharing
Agreement. He said that, if the Council adopts the findings and conclusions of the Memorandum
after holding a public hearing, it would have complied with the procedural requirements of Utah
Code Ann. § 10-8-2.

Mr. Carter said that the Memorandum demonstrated that the value of a Costco warehouse in
Riverton created a benefit that the community receives from gaining employment opportunities.
He said a Riverton Costco warehouse would greatly enhance the prosperity of the community,
which equates to a valid corporate purpose to appropriate monies in order to induce a major
employer to come to Riverton. He said additional benefits accrue for Riverton City when they
recognize that a Costco warehouse in Riverton would lead to a significant reduction in travel
miles for Riverton residents who enjoy a Costco membership.

Mr. Carter explained that, accordingly, the conclusion of the Memorandum was the approval of
Sales Tax Revenue Sharing Agreement, which would provide benefit to Riverton City that was
of greater value than the amounts appropriated under the Agreement.

Mayor Pro Tempore Stewart opened a Public Hearing and called for public comment; there
being none, he declared the Public Hearing closed.

Brian Wayland, Costco representative, thanked the City Council for their support of locating a
Costco Warehouse in Riverton City. He also thanked Staff for their excellent cooperation and
helpfulness. He said that work would begin on infrastructure but a start date had not yet been
determined.

Council Member Buroker MOVED that the City Council adopt Resolution No. 20-54 -
Adopting a study which demonstrates the community benefit for locating a Costco
Wholesale Warehouse in Riverton exceeds the cost executing a Sales Tax Revenue
Sharing Agreement with Costco; and approving a Sales Tax Revenue Sharing Agreement
with Costco Wholesale Corporation. Council Member Wells SECONDED the motion. Mayor
Pro Tempore Stewart called for discussion on the motion; there being none, he called for a roll
call vote and the vote was as follows: Council Members Buroker-Yes, McCay-Yes, McDougal-
Yes, Stewart-Yes, and Wells-Yes. The motion passed unanimously.

    b. Ordinance No. 20-14 - Adopting a General Plan Amendment, Land Use Map of the
       Riverton City General Plan, update initiated by Riverton City

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that, following public hearings,
forwarded a recommended draft Land Use Plan to the City Council. He explained that the
Planning Commission and the City Council differed in two areas of the General Plan map, which
he presented and explained as follows:

         The Elderly Housing Overlay was briefly discussed but will be addressed at a
         later date following additional research and review.

         The Autumn Hills area along 13400 South and 5600 East. The zoning
         recommendation to the Planning Commission was Community Commercial;
         however, the Planning Commission did not include that in their recommendation
         forwarded to the Council; they recommended 13400 South frontage only be
         designated Community Commercial.

Lengthy discussion was held in the July 21st City Council Meeting and it was the consensus of
the Council to designate the entire parcel as Community Commercial and delete properties if
needed once the Public Hearing had been held on August 18th.
