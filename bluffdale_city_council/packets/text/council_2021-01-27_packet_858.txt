 1   case, is the City Council and the staff is shared. The Agency creates project areas with the goal of
 2   the project area being to utilize its powers to create incentives and open up an area of the map that
 3   staff determines would be beneficial to incentivize. The area chosen could be a single developer
 4   or a broad multi-use development.
 5
 6   Mr. Springer explained that the idea of tax increment takes a piece of time and makes the decision
 7   to incentivize anything above it. This involves agreements with taxing entities to determine the
 8   time period and tax increases that will be used for the Agency powers. The more successful, the
 9   more development the greater the property tax and sales tax in some cases, and the more money
10   that goes to the Agency.
11
12   Mr. Springer stated that the idea is at that the end of the term, all of the different entities benefit
13   from the incentivization. The process involves creating a project area through the Agency,
14   attracting development, and administering the project area incentives to continue attracting more
15   development. The more times the City goes through this cycle, the more increment is generated,
16   and the more taxable value is ultimately created at the end of the term.
17
18   The tool is used as a catalyst for new development. If no development takes place it does not hurt
19   the Agency or the City. The Agency then distributes the funds and does the administrative work
20   of preparing the reports and deciding what activities are appropriate to incentivize. One of the
21   benefits of the Agency is that it has powers that the City does not. For example, the Agency can
22   distribute money to a developer. The City Council has control of how the Agency operates, what
23   is incentivized, and how it takes place.
24
25   Mr. Springer presented a list of different tools the funds can be used for. He stated that there are
26   some restrictions and things money has to be used for, such as housing. Development can be
27   incentivized by building a pad even if there is no developer or store. This is a way for Bluffdale
28   to keep up with the competition for economic development with surrounding cities. Some
29   examples of projects that could be incentivized in Bluffdale include auto retailers, power
30   infrastructure, Rio Tinto, and eBay.

       This Agreement is made and entered into among the Welby Jacob Water Users

                                         RECITALS

        A.     Applicant desires to obtain the Company’s consent to encroach upon its
Welby Canal to replace an existing turnout and add a rotating drum screen structure near
1500 West Jordan Narrows Road in Bluffdale, as shown in Exhibit Drawings 1-15. The entire
project described in this Agreement and Exhibit Drawings 1-15 are referred to herein as the
“Works.”

       B.      This Agreement preserves in the Company all of its rights to continue
operating and conveying water through its canal.

       C.       The Company has agreed to allow Applicant to encroach on the Welby Canal,
provided Applicant strictly adheres to the terms of this Agreement and provided the Works,
and any associated facilities, do not in any manner whatsoever interfere with the Company’s
use, operation, maintenance, and repair of its canal and related facilities.

                                       AGREEMENT

       In consideration of the payments and covenants set forth herein and for other good
and valuable consideration, the receipt, adequacy, and legal sufficiency of which are hereby
acknowledged by the Parties, the Parties agree as follows:

        1.      Purpose.
        The purpose of this Agreement is to set forth the conditions under which Applicant
may install the Works, while also protecting and preserving the Company’s present ability
and right to use, operate, maintain, and repair its canal and related facilities. This Agreement
is limited to said purposes.

         2.     Period.
         The term of this Agreement shall be 25 years, unless terminated earlier pursuant to
the Termination paragraph below. If no party terminates the Agreement prior to its expiration,
it shall automatically be extended for successive 25-year periods without any action of the
parties.

       3.    Termination.
       The Company, at its option, may terminate this Agreement if Applicant fails to begin

                                          Page 1 of 8
construction of the Works, as determined by Company, within two (2) years of the date this
Agreement is signed by both parties. Upon a good faith determination by the Company Board
of Directors that Applicant failed to strictly comply with the terms of this Agreement,
Company may, at its option, terminate this Agreement after notice of a period of 30 days to
cure the breach and Applicant’s failure to cure the breach within the 30 days, as determined
by Company.

       The notice required by this section shall be sent to Applicant at:

       Bluffdale City
       Attn: Daniel Tracer, P.E., Assistant City Engineer
       2222 West 14400 South
       Bluffdale, UT 84065

       Applicant shall update this notice information with Company if it changes.

       4.     Location.
       The Works shall be located on the Welby Canal in Bluffdale City, Utah, as shown in
Exhibit Drawings 1-15.

        5.      Design, Construction, and Installation.
        The Applicant submitted drawings of the Works to the Company, copies of which are
attached as Exhibit Drawings 1-20 and incorporated as part of the Agreement. The Applicant
shall construct and install the Works strictly in accordance with these drawings, any red-line
drawings or instructions, and the provisions of this Agreement, as determined by the
Company. Applicant has no right to make any other alterations or changes to the Company’s
canal or properties. Any notes or conditions included in the attached drawings are
incorporated herein as conditions of this Agreement. The Company and the Company’s
engineers shall under no circumstances have any responsibility for design or construction of
the Works. The Applicant must obtain Company’s written approval to deviate from the
attached drawings in Exhibit Drawings 1-15 or to alter the Works in any manner in
constructing the Works or after Applicant constructs the Works.

       6.      Rights Reserved; Continued Operation of the Welby Canal.

         a.     The Company reserves all of its rights necessary to continue operating and
maintaining its canal and facilities, including the section of the Welby Canal affected by the
Works. Company shall not be liable for any damages to the Works or caused by the Works
from operating its canal. Applicant’s construction of the Works shall not in any manner
whatsoever interfere with the Company’s use, operation, maintenance, and repair of its canal
and related facilities, including the section of the Welby Canal affected by the Works. This
Agreement, and any interest Applicant acquires by this Agreement, is subordinate and subject
to the rights of the Company to use, operate, maintain, and repair its canal and related
facilities. The Company reserves the right to allow others to encroach upon the canal property
to construct facilities that do not materially interfere with the Applicant’s Works.

convey water through its canal. Company shall also have full use of the Works to convey
water.

        c.      The Works built by the Applicant pursuant to this Agreement shall be owned

delivery of water in its canal.

wear of the Works that adversely affects or threatens to adversely affect the Welby Canal, as
determined by Company. The maintenance and repair of the Works and any and all associated
facilities shall be the sole responsibility of Applicant and shall not interfere with Company’s
delivery of water in its canal. If Applicant fails to meet the maintenance obligations of this
paragraph, Company may repair the Works to meet the requirements of this paragraph and
Applicant shall reimburse Company for all costs it incurs to repair the Works.

11.80.060.010: PURPOSE AND ZONE CHARACTERISTICS:

The R-SL Senior Independent Living Zone is established to provide areas within the City for
housing for residents aged 55 years and older who prefer to reside in independently owned
dwelling units within a larger community of common area, open space and amenities. Rezoning
to the R-SL Zone should be carefully reviewed to ensure compatibility with existing
development and adjacent neighborhoods.

11.80.060.020: PERMITTED AND ACCESSORY USES:

   A. Permitted uses allowed within the R-SL Zone are provided as follows:
         a. Multi-family residential dwellings providing two (2) or more attached units.
         b. Minor home occupations in compliance with BCC xxxx with no employees.

11.80.060.030: MINIMUM PROJECT AREA AND MAXIMUM DENSITY:

Each development project in the R-SL Senior Independent Living Zone shall be located on a
parcel containing not less than four (4) acres.

The density for any senior living project shall be a up to a maximum of 6.50 units per acre for any
R-SL zone.

11.80.060.040: OCCUPANCY QUALIFICATIONS AND RESTRICTIONS:

A. Dwelling units are restricted to a maximum unit size of two (2) bedrooms on the main floor. A
   bonus room or loft is allowed with a second-floor option over a garage.
B. The dwelling units are intended for, and to be owned and occupied by, at least one (1)
   person fifty-five (55) years of age or older per unit.

           b. A unit may be occupied by the management, medical, or custodial personnel of a
              qualifying resident.
           c. A homeowners’ association (HOA) shall be established for a development to

Senior living projects shall promote an active street frontage on all public streets. The creation

oriented toward, can also be an acceptable option for projects located on collector roads.

11.80.060.060: SETBACK AND DRIVEWAY REQUIREMENTS:

The following minimum setback requirements shall apply in the R-SL Independent Living Zone.
Each setback is measured from the property line of the lot or parcel.

A. Front Setback; Unit and Driveway Access: Each building or structure in the R-SL Zone
   adjacent to a public or private street shall have a minimum front setback of fifteen feet (15’)
   measured from the right-of-way, otherwise the front setback shall be a minimum of ten feet
   (10’).

B. Driveway areas shall be a minimum of twenty feet (20’) in length, exclusive of any sidewalk or
   walkways. If units are accessed by an alley or auto court, the width of the alley shall be a
   minimum of twenty-five feet (25’) and shall comply with all fire codes.

C. Side Setback; Corner Lot:

   1. Each building or structure in the R-SL Zone shall have a minimum side setback of ten feet
   (10').

   2. Each corner lot or parcel in the R-SL Zone shall have a minimum setback of twenty feet
   (20') along the street on the side of the lot not being used as the front setback.

D. Rear Setback:

   1. Each building or structure in the R-SL Zone adjacent to a public or private street shall
      have a rear setback of fifteen feet (15’) measured from the right-of-way, otherwise the
      rear setback shall be a minimum of ten feet (10’).

in the area with the Midvalley Bible Church immediately adjacent to the subject property, the North Star

available or will be provided with a proposed development.

The Planning Commission will also be the land use authority to approve the site plan application for the
future proposed project if the zoning is approved.

APPROVAL CRITERIA FOR THE APPLICATION:
Zoning Map amendments are a legislative decision of the City Council, after receiving a recommendation
from the Planning Commission. Broad discretion is given to the City’s Land Use Authorities when making
land use decisions. Consideration for amending the Zoning Map may include, but not limited to;
opportunities for future development to balance the goals of the City and property owners, adequate
infrastructure, and meeting the overall needs of the community for future residential and economic
growth are all valid considerations when making land use decisions.

The Planning Commission should establish adequate findings in support of a positive or negative outcome
and forward those findings to the City Council which may include, but are not limited to:
   1. That the zoning of the subject property to R-SL Senior Independent Living Zone provides a variety

       stages.
   2. That the zoning provides housing opportunities close to public and civic uses such as parks and
       recreation, schools, and other community uses.
   3. That the zoning provides an appropriate transition for residential uses to commercial uses and will
       reduce the impacts and buffer the adjacent lower density residential neighborhoods.

Design guidelines are a major priority of the City Council, so the proposed text amendments include
design guidelines that will ensure that this type of housing development is an aesthetic addition to
Bluffdale.

Parking requirements include a two-car garage and a 20-foot d1iveway. Additional guest parking
must be included at the rate of one stall per every four units. Landscaping requirements are also an
integral part of the R-SL zone. A perimeter fence will be required and must be constructed of
masonry or wrought iron, with no other options allowed. Fences can be up to eight feet tall. A
lighting plan is required. Common open spaces must constitute at least 30% of the property.
Potential amenities are enumerated, including trail connections, if possible. Signage will be limited
to a monument sign, with no electronic signage. Gates may be permitted with the approval of the
Fire Chief. Gated streets will be private. Street cross-sections have not been specified so on-street
parking may or may not be pennitted. Typically, the minimum width for a private street is 20 feet.

access by emergency service vehicles. Perhaps a prohibition of on-street parking would alleviate

                                 SL Residential Senior Independent Living Zone to provide the
                                 opportunity for future senior living projects with specific development

                                 apply the new R-SL Zone to 4.50 acres located at approximately 13975

Applicants:                      Howland Partners
Staff Presentation:              Jennifer Robison

RECOMMENDATION: To approve the Zoning Text Amendment to enact BCC 11.80.060 R-SL Residential
Senior Independent Living Zone and to approve the Zoning Map Amendment to apply the new zone to the
4.50 acres at 13975 South 2700 West (Application 2020-47) as recommended by the Planning Commission
on December 2, 2020.

BACKGROUND: The City Council approved the amendment to the City’s General Plan on April 22, 2020 for
Howland Partners with the condition that a subsequent application to rezone the property would be
submitted. The Council requested the applicant provide quality development standards and plans to
enhance this area of the community for the evaluation for the future zoning application.

The applicant worked with Staff and provided the proposed zoning structure and development standards
to enact the R-SL Residential Senior Independent Living Zone (R-SL). In evaluating the creation of a Senior
Living Zone, Staff reviewed many examples of all types of senior residential options. Within a 55+ or senior
living zone, the definition can be very broad and provide for various residential options such as
independent living, assisted living, nursing facilities, continuing care retirement communities,
alzheimer’s/memory care/dementia facilities, or others. Staff determined to focus on the independent
living options only at this time due to the potential complexity for all 55+ senior living situations. Creating a
specific R-SL zone will provide the tools to respond the applicant’s request, but also provide a for the
possibility of other future developments for senior independent living for all areas in the community. In the
future, Staff can address the other types of senior living options, if desired.

RECOMMENDATION OF APPROVAL INCLUDING FINDINGS FOR ZONING TEXT AMENDMENT:
Kory Luker moved to forward a positive recommendation to the City Council for the proposed Zoning Text
Amendment Application (2020-47) to enact BCC.11.80.060 for an R-SL Residential Senior Independent
Living Zone, including all development standards and requirements based on the following findings:
        1. The creation of a new R-SL Zone supports the City's General Plan to provide for a variety of
           high-quality housing options that are affordable for all segments of the population, at all life
           stages.
        2. The creation of the new R-SL Zone provides specific and enhanced development standards to
           provide for a quality senior residential project.

The R-SL Senior Independent Living Zone is established to provide areas within the City for

Mrs. Robison next read the specifications for the minimum size of property to be developed, along
with the maximum density to be allowed. Potential home occupations would be classified as Minor
Home Occupations per the new ordinance on Home Occupations. The number of bedrooms for
each unit will be limited to two on the main floor. At least one of the occupants must be at least 55
years old. Provisions are included in the event that the person who is 55 or older dies, leaving a
younger surviving partner. Mr. Sheeran said the CC&Rs would be reviewed before an application
was approved. It would be up to the HOA to enforce the age requirements.

Commissioner Walston asked about the requirements and rationale for requmng at least two
attached units but not having a maximum number of attached units to be allowed. Mrs. Robison
said that in general, the units she has reviewed are often four-plexes, but they could be limited to
two dwelling units. The Planning Commission could recommend a maximum number of attached
units to be allowed. In addition, if the Planning Commission felt that single dwelling units could be
permitted, they would have that option, though single dwelling units are not the norm in this type of
development. Mrs. Robison emphasized that the meeting packet contained just a draft of the
proposed R-SL zone and specifications.

The primary building will not have a basement or crawl spaces because the absence of a basement
would minimize the number of people who live in the dwelling unit. In response to Commissioner
Flynn's question regarding the option of having backloaded dwelling units, Mrs. Robison stated that
staff's review of this product led them to conclude that the homes should have driveways so that
they look similar to a typical single-family residence.

The site plan requirement will be included in the zone, with the Planning Commission being the
Land Use Authority to approve site plans for the R-SL zone.

Design guidelines are a major priority of the City Council, so the proposed text amendments include
design guidelines that will ensure that this type of housing development is an aesthetic addition to
Bluffdale.

Parking requirements include a two-car garage and a 20-foot d1iveway. Additional guest parking
must be included at the rate of one stall per every four units. Landscaping requirements are also an
integral part of the R-SL zone. A perimeter fence will be required and must be constructed of
masonry or wrought iron, with no other options allowed. Fences can be up to eight feet tall. A
lighting plan is required. Common open spaces must constitute at least 30% of the property.
Potential amenities are enumerated, including trail connections, if possible. Signage will be limited
to a monument sign, with no electronic signage. Gates may be permitted with the approval of the
Fire Chief. Gated streets will be private. Street cross-sections have not been specified so on-street
parking may or may not be pennitted. Typically, the minimum width for a private street is 20 feet.

Commissioner Flynn reported that he had concerns with the allowance of a Minor Home
Occupation due to potential parking problems it could pose. Mr. Sheeran explained that a Minor
Home Occupation allows no employees, only three vehicles per day to visit the home, and no on-
street parking. Commissioner Flynn also asked if the presence of a gate on a street would hamper
access by emergency service vehicles. Perhaps a prohibition of on-street parking would alleviate
any access problems.

excellent location for a 55 and older development. This project would provide an
tremendous tax base without the need for additional schools. Additionally, this
development would allow for older Bluffdale residents who wish to downsize, to stay in our
wonderful community. Finally, older residents make exceptional neighbors, have a low
crime rate and are active in their communities.

Since the completion of our building and campus more than 14 years ago we have enjoyed
an excellent relationship with our neighbors and community. Our facility has been made
available for the Utah Philharmonic Orchestra to practice in and have public concerts, Public
Voting, the Up With Kids program, emergency shelter for North Star
Academy, FireFighterand Police Officers Appreciation nights, Support Groups and other
events. Our commitment is to this community both now and in the future.

I would love to be able to write more, but in the interest of time, I will simply close with a
sincere request: As a congregation, we would be beyond grateful if this request were
approved. I believe this development would be an tremendous addition to the Bluffdale
community and its approval would be a win for everyone.

B. Hearing.
        1. Once an applicant submits a complete application and payment of its associated
           fee to the City Recorder by certified mail, the City Recorder shall submit the
           complete application to a Hearing Officer and the Hearing Officer shall
           schedule a hearing date.
        2. At least five (5) days before the hearing, the City may, at its discretion, file a
           responsive brief regarding the City’s position on whether the requested
           accommodation should be granted or denied, in whole or in part.
        3. The hearing will not be open to the public but the applicant and the City may
           bring its attorneys or witnesses, if so desired. A witness, including the applicant,
           may only speak at the hearing if a witness statement for that witness was
           submitted in the application (if by the applicant) or responsive brief (if by the
           City).

           accommodation to the applicant, witnesses, or the City.

1.140.010 Computation Of Time(Added)

Unless expressly stated otherwise, computation of time shall be as follows:

     A. Time periods stated in “days” are calendar days.
     B. The date of the trigger event is excluded.
     C. When the due date falls on a weekend or legal holiday, the due date shall extend to the
        next business day.
     D. Any submissions or filings are due before City Hall closes.
