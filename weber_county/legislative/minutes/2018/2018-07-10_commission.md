E. PRESENTATION - UPDATE FROM UTAH GEOLOGICAL SURVEY ON OGDEN VALLEY HYDROGEOLOGY STUDY.
   Lucy Jordan, Hydrogeologist, with Utah Geological Survey (UGS), presented the major findings of this 3-year
   study. She showed maps and graphs of the study area that comprised the principal aquifer potentiometric surface,
   stable isotopes of water, sub-watershed water budget components, and predicted septic tank impacts in future
   growth scenarios. There are about 2,200 septic tanks in Ogden Valley (Valley). The entire drainage basin was
   studied. The study overview included a comprehensive hydrogeologic study, use of chemistry and environmental
   isotopes, water levels, stream gaging and existing data analysis to understand the extent of the surface
   water/groundwater interaction, to estimate water budget components by sub-watershed and to evaluate impacts to
   water quality. When the Ogden City wells were sampled, they were found to contain a very tiny amount of tritium
   and UGS’s hypothesis included that water may be getting to those wells recharged at the surface from the mountains
   to the west of Pineview Reservoir or water may be leaking through the confining unit. According to the data, 1.7
   people per household was estimated on average in the Valley (Weber County’s average is 3 people/household), and
   that permanent residence is underestimating the number of people that is actually contributing to the nitrate levels
   in the Valley. Regarding the predicted septic tank impact in future growth scenarios, UGS took a guess that second
   homes were used 50% of the time and the result was 2.4/people/household and by building more houses on septic
   tanks there could be about 4.4 acres/household/septic tank if the county wanted to have 1 milligram/liter degradation
   as the average. She cautioned to not do that because once houses are built there may actually be 3/people/household
   and suggested putting 3/people/household on all the existing houses and start adding more septic tanks which would
   be another 2,000, and that the county may want to limit development to about 5.8 acres/households on septic tanks.
      Ms. Jordan responded the following to questions: from Miranda Menzies, with Wolf Creek Water & Sewer
      District, that 3.5 acres was not very protective for the very long term and this was ignoring any second
      homeowners and recreational use in the Valley; from Pen Hollist, of Liberty, that nitrates can travel through
      the confined aquifer to the Ogden City well field and eventually end up in the Reservoir but the level is low
      because it has so much clean surface coming in; from Steve Waldrip, of Eden, regarding nitrates, that no mass
      balance had been done on converting farm land to homes with septic tanks and USU did a lot of work on this;
      UGS believes that septic tanks are the main contributor of nitrates; from Kim Harvey, of Eden, that UGS did
      a good cross-section sampling that included 57 wells for general chemistry, a subset for filtered metals, 43
      samples for nutrients, 224 samples of water isotopes, and about less than 10% of those wells were deeper than
      200 feet; from Zane Froerer, of Mountain Canal Irrigation, that UGS did a seepage run on the Ogden Valley
      Canal in 7/2016, measuring the flow and documenting and measuring any take-outs from that quantifying what
      was going in and out of the Canal, and also comparing Weber Basin Conservancy District’s measurements
      (from several places along the canal), and where both entities had measurements, UGS was within 10% of
      those. Overall, where the Canal takes off from South Fork ending at the highway to Powder Mountain, there
      were 18 cfs of loss along the canal (about ½ of the canal’s flow or 3,300-acre ft./year) that is not take-offs for
      irrigation; from Commissioner Jenkins, that UGS took samples from South Fork and nitrate measurements
      were low. Both commissioners expressed concern with the assumption of 2.4/people/household and that the
      Valley would develop at the remaining county rate. Ms. Jordan believes that the permanent resident figure is
      reasonable at 3/people/household. UGS can adjust the figure for the remaining households on developed
      parcels if there is better data available; they were trying to add in all the recreation (i.e., parks & campgrounds).
      Jim Richardson, of Mountain Canal Irrigation, said that about four years ago they had a big discussion with
      Weber Basin regarding leakage on their canal and confirmed what UGS found. They have plans to pipe but
      for this huge project it will be years down the road. Recharge should then decrease a little bit. Chair Harvey
      requested that as much in-depth information be posted as possible on this item. Today’s presentation is to be
      posted by County Planning on its webpage and UGS will release the study when it has been published; it will
      be available on its website and the county can link to it.

G. ACTION ITEMS:
    1. LOCAL TRANSPORTATION FUNDING AGREEMENT WITH MARRIOTT-SLATERVILLE CITY FOR 1200 W. PROJECT.
         Brooke Stewart, with County Community Development, stated that the project is to widen/improve 0.6 miles
         of 1200 W. from 400 N. heading North to the Farr-West City boundary, which ends approximately 86 feet just
         before Billy Bailey Drive. BDO donated a 30-foot buffer from the edge of the right-of-way, which will allow
         the project to include a meandering pathway with pathway lights on one side of the street. Total project cost is
         estimated at $6,779,300 and the project has been awarded $3,762,110 in STP funds from WFRC. The project
         was recommended by WACOG on 12/4/2017 to receive $3,017,190 in sales tax funds, and it was approved by
         the Commission as part of the 2017 priority list on 12/12/2017.
         Commissioner Jenkins moved to approve the Local Transportation Funding Agreement with Marriott-
         Slaterville City for the 1200 West project; Chair Harvey seconded.
         Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Harvey – aye
    2. LOCAL TRANSPORTATION FUNDING AGREEMENT WITH MARRIOTT-SLATERVILLE FOR THE PIONEER
       ROAD (PHASE 1) PROJECT.
         Brooke Stewart, with County Community Development, stated that the project is to improve the road by
         enlarging the turning radius, improve the design speed, raise the road, improve the bridge at Warren Canal to
         avoid future closures due to flooding, and to bring the roadway into compliance with the American Association
         of State Highway and Transportation Officials’ geometric design standards. In 2015 this phase of the project
         was awarded $250,000 in corridor Preservation Funds and $1,950,000 in Sales Tax Funds to realign the first of
         the two phases. In 2017 Marriott-Slaterville City submitted a second request seeking additional funds to address
         recommendations from the geotechnical report and the U.S. Army Corp of Engineers to include a retaining wall
         and for over-excavation to a depth of 5-feet throughout the wetland area. The project was recommended by
         WACOG on 12/4/2017 to receive an additional $246,000 in Corridor Preservation Funds and $902,918 in Sales
         Tax Funds to cover the unexpected costs. This project was approved by the Commission as part of the 2017
         priority list on 12/12/2017.
         Commissioner Jenkins moved to approve the Local Transportation Funding Agreement with Marriott-
         Slaterville City for the Pioneer Road (Phase 1) project; Chair Harvey seconded.
         Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Harvey – aye

         Brianna Sederholm, County Purchasing Agent, stated that this provision allows the county to hire contractors,

         work that is reasonably related to the original project without going through a procurement process. The

         Harvey seconded.
