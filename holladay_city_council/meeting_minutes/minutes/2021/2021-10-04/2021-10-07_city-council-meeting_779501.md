III.    Public Comments.

IV.     Public Hearing on Proposed Amendments to Title 13 Regarding Sensitive Lands
        Development Standards Update - FEMA Floodplain and Fault Study.
Planning Manager, Jonathan Teerlink reported that Federal Emergency Management Agency
(“FEMA”) updated its Flood Insurance Rate Maps (“FIRM”) for Salt Lake County. The last time it
was been done was in 2009. Mr. Teerlink noted that the latest update involved one panel, which was
a section of Big Cottonwood Creek where some floodplains had been updated. In the ordinance where
FIRM is referred to, there was a revision date listed. That needed to be updated to November 2022.

Utah Geologic Survey (“UGS”) in collaboration with the Utah Division of Emergency Management
released a New Seismic Report consisting of updated fault mapping that spans the length of the
Wasatch Front Fault Zone. The new study expanded the mapped faults from 451 to 739 miles. Much
of the new fault lines targeted the Salt Lake Valley, specifically the east bench along Millcreek, the
City of Holladay, Cottonwood Heights, and Draper. Mayor Dahle wondered what that meant for the
City. Mr. Teerlink explained that it would expand the areas where they will be required to do a
geotechnical study. He noted that a lot of the technologies for studying fault areas had evolved. He
also discussed the process and procedures involved with fault study report reviews. Council Member
Durham wondered if there should be similar processes for the floodplain. Mr. Teerlink explained that
there were State-mandated processes in place for that.

City Attorney, Todd Godfrey reported that much of the fault study and appeal provisions in the
ordinance are required by State Code. They are not required for other sensitive lands, because those
processes are expensive and imposed expense for the City. The Legislation that required some of the
appeal procedures was a reaction to certain development situations that occurred in Salt Lake County.
The intention was to balance municipality reactions to existing faults and landslides with private
property owner rights.

Mayor Dahle opened the public hearing. There were no comments. The public hearing was closed.

V.     Public Hearing on Proposed Amendments to Title 13.77 Regarding Parking Lot Shade Tree
       Standards.
Mayor Dahle reported that the above item relates to proposed amendments to Title 13.77. He
explained that the Parking Lot Shade Tree Standards were part of the discussions during the creation
of the Holladay Crossroads Zone Small Area Master Plan. Members of the Tree Committee were
present and an expanded discussion would take place during the Work Meeting.

Mayor Dahle opened the public hearing.

Travis Jones - 4733 South Ichabod Street. He is speaking on behalf of the Tree Committee. He noted
that the proposed amendments were due to Staff efforts as well as feedback shared by members of
the Tree Committee. The Committee intended to preserve the trees but it was also important to
consider businesses and storefront appeal. The Parking Lot Shade Tree Standards were not intended
to block businesses. Mr. Jones explained that the proposed amendments have the full support of the
Tree Committee.

There were no further comments. The public hearing was closed.

VIII. City Manager Report – Gina Chamness.
        a.      Update on 3900 South.
Ms. Chamness shared updates related to construction projects in the City. She noted that the Highland
Drive Project was initially anticipated to be completed by August 2021 to minimize the impacts to
the schools off of Highland Drive. However, the Highland Drive and the 3900 South Projects were
impacted by the Utah Department of Transportation (“UDOT”). Ms. Chamness explained that UDOT
was managing the projects on behalf of the City and had made a decision in April 2020 that City was
not aware of until very recently. UDOT issued communications in April 2020 that stated any delay
in existing contracts would be excusable. As a result of the COVID-19 pandemic, both of the projects
had been delayed.

The contractors on the Highland Drive Project missed the anticipated deadline of August 2021 but
were looking to complete the project by the end of October 2021. Ms. Chamness explained that it
was possible that some micro-surfacing would need to be done in the Spring of 2022.

Ms. Chamness reported that the original plan for the 3900 South Project was to complete work by the
end of the season. In mid-September, the contractor informed the project team that work would likely
not be completed by the end of the season. Public Services Director, Jared Bunch pushed back against
the extended deadline and asked that the contractor provide a plan to complete the work during the
current season. Two weeks earlier, the contractor provided that information but gave Staff three hours
to decide whether a complete shutdown between 2300 East and 2700 East would be possible. The
reason for the quick turnaround was that they needed to line up their subcontractors and materials.
Ms. Chamness thanked Mayor Dahle, Council Member Durham, and Council Member Quinn for
their prompt responses. She explained that there would be a temporary closure in mid-to-late October

IX.     District Issues and Board Reports.
Council Member Fotheringham reported that the Holladay Arts Council had an event scheduled to
take place on the weekend. The Plaza Fest would happen on October 9, 2021, from 9:00 a.m. to
1:00 p.m. He also reported that the Unified Fire Authority (“UFA”) was hosting the Firefighter Chili
Cookoff. It would take place on October 9, 2021, at South Town Mall from 11:00 a.m. to 3:00 p.m.

Council Member Durham noted that there had been a lot of questions related to 2300 East recently.
He wondered if there could be discussions about those issues during a future Work Meeting.
Ms. Chamness confirmed this. She noted that there would be a challenge with the crosswalk because
it was so close to the intersection at 3900 and 2300. However, there were options to consider, from
removing the crosswalk entirely to providing a pedestrian refuge in the middle of the street. Council
Member Durham asked for an update on the arborist position. Ms. Chamness stated that based on the
conversations that the Council and Tree Committee had in the spring, the order of priority was tree
maintenance, tree inventory, and then the arborist position. Staff met and reviewed those priorities
recently and tree maintenance was planned to be completed within the next month.

Council Member Durham reported that he attended the Utah League of Cities and Towns (“ULCT”)
conference and there was a presentation on middle-end housing. He felt that public education was an
essential part of that discussion. It was important for the public to understand that everything that
was not single-family housing was not automatically high-density housing. Council Member Durham
wondered if the Council would be open to doing some public education related to density.

Council Member Quinn reported that there had been a neighborhood meeting in District 4 regarding
the frequent water main breaks in the northeast corner of the City. Four representatives from Salt
Lake City Public Utilities presented information. Council Member Quinn explained that Salt Lake
City Public Utilities was aware of the situation but since that meeting, there had been two more major
water main breaks. She would be reaching out again to see what could be done. Council Member
Quinn stated that she had also attended the ULCT Conference, which focused not only on housing
issues but also on drought issues as well.

Council Member Fotheringham moved to Recess the City Council Meeting and Reconvene in a Work

       a.      Discussion on Previous Public Hearings.
There was discussion regarding the FEMA Floodplain and Fault Study. Council Member Quinn
asked about the definition of well-constrained. Mr. Teerlink explained that well-constrained meant
there was evidence of fissures or fractured lines on the fault study. Those were areas that were known
to be highly susceptible to finding new fault fractures. Mayor Dahle noted that there would be a
chance to further discuss the item at the October 14, 2021, City Council Meeting.

Mayor Dahle discussed the proposed amendments to Title 13.77. He believed they initially intended
to address the Parking Lot Shade Tree Standards at the same that the Holladay Crossroads Zone was
being considered. However, they wanted to expand the standards to include other areas. Mr. Teerlink
stated that if there was a development or redevelopment of commercial property and/or the
reconstruction of a parking lot, there were currently no requirements regarding how many trees need
to be placed or replaced. The proposed amendments would create a new standard, which would create
significant canopy coverage. There were some elements of landscaping requirements included as
well. Mr. Teerlink felt the amendments established regulations that could be enforced.

Council Members discussed landscaping requirements. Mayor Dahle wondered if there were
landscaping requirements related to the addition of grass. Mr. Teerlink explained that there was
nothing specific to grass. He noted that Mayor Dahle previously expressed concerns about
environmental sustainability issues that could prevent a property owner from trying to achieve water
efficiency standards. The landscaping definitions did not specifically state that grass was needed and
there was flexibility as it related to landscaping. Mr. Teerlink noted that there was a provision to
replace 50% of the required landscape area with other elements such as rocks, boulders, or ornamental

features. In the draft before the Council, that section was proposed to be removed. He felt that in
light of recent conversations, that the language should be left in the final draft.

Council Member Durham noted that the Tree Committee debated tree plantings in front of businesses.

       b.      Discussions on Priorities for Proposed Federal Lobbyist Request for Proposals.
Ms. Chamness stated that she was looking for guidance from the Council as she prepared a request
for proposal (“RFP”) for a Federal Lobbyist. In the budget, $50,000 was allocated for Federal
lobbying. That line item had been a long-standing item in previous City budgets but had been
eliminated four years ago when it was clear that earmarks at the Federal level had disappeared.
Assuming there would be a change in that process, the budget had added the funds back in.
Ms. Chamness reported that as she prepares to write the RFP, she wanted to know if there was
anything in particular that the City Council would like her to highlight. She shared several examples.

Council Member Gibbons wondered what kinds of Federal grants had been received in the past.
Ms. Chamness reported that a major project that was funded with Federal dollars related to utility
relocation and road reconfiguration. The Council suggested that the focus be on infrastructure.
Additional suggestions included economic development, possible redevelopment areas, affordable
housing, and sustainability. Ms. Chamness noted that she would include those items in the RFP.
