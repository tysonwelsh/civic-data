Council Business
        Council Calendar: The Council began a brief review of calendar items for the remainder of the year 2020.

Fiscal Year (FY) 2021 Quarterly Financial Report
          Ogden City is in a state of emergency due to the Coronavirus pandemic (COVID-19). Because economic impacts of
COVID-19 were unknown during the budget process, the City Council was conservative in appropriating the FY2020-21 budget.
The Council committed to regularly evaluate revenue source data over time as it becomes available. Because revenue data lags, it
will be difficult to determine the full impacts of COVID-19 for some time-especially considering the on-going state of
emergency. This report will summarize various fund expenditures, revenues, and general financial information.
          Comptroller Stout showed aPowerPoint presentation to provide a General Fund revenue summary as of September 30,
2020. The total budgeted revenues for the entire FY are $60 million; to date the City has received $10 million, or 16.5 percent.
She noted that she can provide a comparison with last year's revenues to date to give the Council an indication of how the current
FY compares with the last FY. Revenues are received throughout the year and on varying schedules. For example, Weber County
transmits the majority of the City's portion of property tax at beginning of the calendar year. Payments that are submitted to the
County late, may be received throughout the year as the County receives them. Sales tax is received from the State of Utah
monthly but lags sixty (60) days from when vendors submit taxes to the State.
          There was discussion between the Council and Ms. Stout regarding how Ogden compares with other communities in
terms of economic conditions and budget revenues. Ms. Stout indicated the Conference Board's Consumer Confidence Index
increased to 101.8 in September, up from 86.3 in August. The index gauges consumers' optimism about the economy and is
closely watched because consumer spending accounts for 70 percent of economic growth in the United States. In February 2020,
the index was at 132.6 before decreasing to below 70 when COVID-19 hit. She stated there is still some uncertainty and economic
risk related to the presidential election this year. The economy has been artificially inflated due to higher unemployment benefits,
CARES Act support, and tax payments. The United States deficit is at an all-time high right now.
          Chief Administrative Officer Johnson noted that he feels Utah is better poised than several other states from an economic
standpoint, but it is necessary to wait until after the presidential election to learn the impacts on the economy. Once there is a clear

    Council Business - continued
              Council Calendar: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith reviewed the Council's meetings scheduled for the remainder
    of the month as well as for November.
              Council Business: Chair Choberka reported she has heard a complaint from a constituent about noise generated at a
    nearby carwash. She indicated she will forward the complaint to City Administration.
              Council member Stephens mentioned the growing number of vacant commercial buildings in the City, specifically along
    Washington Boulevard. He asked that Administration evaluate the economic viability of the spaces to detennine if there are
    opportunities to attract new businesses to the community. Mr. Johnson stated that the number of commercial vacancies is directly
    related to COVID-19. Businesses were struggling or there were opportunities to transition to a work-from-home arrangement and
    he is unsure how quickly those spaces can be filled; however, the Community and Economic Development Department have done
    a great job at attracting businesses to the community that serve as job creators and economic drivers for the broader community.
              Council member White reported she has received more complaints in the last two weeks than her entire five years as a
    Council member regarding cars speeding in residential neighborhoods. She indicated many people feel an increased police
    presence is needed in these areas, especially around high schools. Mr. Johnson said he has heard there are speeding issues in many
    communities across the state and it likely increased when school resumed. He will speak with the police chiefabout opportunities
    for increasing enforcement.
