City Planner, Lisa Halversen presented the Staff Report and stated that the matter
pertains to the Starbucks Coffee Amended Site Plan. She explained that the Iceberg
Drive-In is an existing building located on the south side of 13400 South 5057 West. The
existing building is on Lot 3 of Monarch Meadows Phase 10, which is a commercial
subdivision. The parcel is zoned SP-C (Monarch Meadows Specific Plan Commercial)
as were the adjoining properties to the east and west. As a general design principle,
buildings in the zone need to be compatible. The existing building stands out the most
from others in the area. However, Starbucks Coffee wanted to open a location and make
changes to the exterior of the building.

Ms. Halversen reported that notices were sent out but no public input was received. She
shared the zoning map with the Commission and identified the location of the subject
property. To the north of the subject property is Herriman, which also designed its
buildings on 13400 South to have red brick. Images of the south and east sides of the
existing building were shown. She identified a waiting area, benches, and entry doors.
There were also two brick towers. One of the towers was taller than the other. White
stucco was on the top part of the walls and red brick around the lower portion. She

tower. This means that the roofline would not be as varied. Ms. Halverson shared the
proposed elevation for the south and east sides. She reported that the stucco would be

The Site Plan was reviewed. Ms. Halversen explained that in Monarch Meadows, there
is a special code that asks commercial buildings to be designated as either “federal” or
“traditional” style. Staff believed the Starbucks proposal could be considered a “federal”
architectural style. Some common elements of “federal” architecture include simple
square or box-like shapes with understated and simple design elements. Certain
architectural features are required in the SP-C Zone. Each project needs to have five.
Staff believed the argument could be made that the design meets the following:

      Decorative block or brick;
      Towers and vertical features;
      Trim, keystones, and shutters on windows and doors;
      Brick accent walls; and
      Architectural wall variations.

Ms. Halversen stated that an argument could be made that the proposal meets the Code.
Alternatively, an argument could be made that the proposal does not meet the Code due
to the lack of red brick. According to the SP-C code in Monarch Meadows, exterior
surfaces need to have brick, tile, or decorative concrete on the lower wall. Ms. Halversen
pointed out that no color was specified. The remaining portions of the building could be
stucco. She shared images of the surrounding neighborhood for additional context about
exterior design.

The Planning Commission needed to determine whether the style, as proposed, meets
the requirements of the SP-C Zone for Monarch Meadows. Ms. Halversen shared

4.     DISCUSSION ITEMS

       A.     Planning Commission Training – Ryan Carter.

Commissioner Cannon reported that Commissioner Breinholt would be leaving the
Planning Commission shortly. As a result, he recommended that the training wait until a
new Commissioner was added to the Planning Commission. There was support for the
suggestion. Mr. Carter asked the Commissioners to read the case study he provided in
preparation for the next meeting. He overviewed reasonable accommodations, which
would be discussed in more detail when the full Planning Commission training takes
place.

Commissioner Rushton asked that a summary of legislative changes related to planning
and development be added to the Planning Commission Training. Mr. Carter reported
that the Utah Municipal Attorneys Association convenes each May. At that time, there is
a legislative recap. Once the COVID-19 pandemic hit, the conferences started to shut
down. A certain amount of work now needs to be done in person, so the conferences
were starting to take place again. He would be attending one in May 2023. After that, a
summary of information would be shared with the Planning Commission.

Mr. Carter suggested that discussions about affordable housing be postponed until after
the conference. The State has reporting requirements that cities need to comply with but
the State was dissatisfied that affordable housing was not rolling out onto the market.
There seemed to be an expectation that cities can control how houses are built and the
price points. That was not the case. Cities can control zoning and provide infrastructure
to keep costs down. Ultimately, it is up to a developer to decide to put in affordable
housing. That was not happening in general but especially in a community like Riverton.
Typically, for affordable housing to be effective, it needs to be close to amenities such as
public transportation, which Riverton does not have. Some more difficult reporting
requirements were coming as a result of the latest Legislative Session.
