Visit Ogden Annual Report

The City Council has requested to receive information from local foundations and nonprofit organizations that have a
contractual agreement with Ogden City. These presentations help the Council to become more familiar with the objectives and
efforts of the organizations.

Sara Toliver, President/Chief Executive Officer for Visit Ogden, used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to describe
their organizational mission and goals, successes and challenges over the past year and any future plans for their organization.
This included a review of the 2022 Annual Report, 2023 Strategic Plan, and Destination Development.

Discussion among the Council and Ms. Toliver centered on partnership between Visit Ogden and other entities in the
community focused on tourism and economic development matters; the demand for lodging in the community; and the increase in
the number of events hosted in the Ogden community over the last 12 months. The Council thanked Ms. Toliver and her entire
staff for the positive impact they have on the community.

Proposed Historic Designation — 136 West 2nd Street
Planning Manager Brierley explained petitioner Terry Lanier, owner of the house at 136 West 2nd Street, located within
the Bingham area, is nominating their house and detached garage to be listed on the Ogden City Register of Historic Places. The
designation would require any significant changes proposed for the building, including demolition, to be approved by the
Landmarks Commission through the approval of a Certificate of Historic Appropriateness. The designation would help ensure the
preservation of the property and would help to ensure that any changes proposed would maintain the character and historicity of
the home and property. The City’s Landmarks Commission has reviewed the proposed designation and has forwarded a
recommendation of approval finding that the building meets both the historical and architectural criteria listed in the City’s code
for local designation.

| Proposed Amendment to Notification Requirements
Planning Manager Brierley explained that based on recent state law changes, cities are no longer required to provide
public notice using the newspaper for land use applications. Instead, state law generally requires posting notices on the City’s
website, on the Utah Public Notice website, posting in at least three public places, and, if applicable, mailing to affected entities as
required by both state and City code. As such, the proposed amendments remove the newspaper noticing requirements for street
name changes, subdivision code section changes, street and alley vacations, zoning ordinance changes and rezones, general plan
amendments, and designations to the local historic register.
The Council heard from City Recorder Hansen regarding ongoing public noticing practices for the variety of items that
are submitted to the Council. The City posts a notice on the Utah Public Meeting Notice and residents can subscribe to the website
in order to be notified any time there is a notice published for Ogden City.
Council Business
Ogden School District Property: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith stated that the purchase of land is an
administrative function and the City Council does not have the authority to purchase land independent of City Administration; if
the Council does desire to purchase one of the properties declared as surplus by the Ogden School District, the only authority the
Council has is to set aside funding for the property and to request that Administration move forward with the purchase. Council
member Lopez stated this issue was discussed during the last work session meeting and if there are enough Council members
interested in purchasing the property, but Administration chooses not to proceed, he wondered how all of the residents interested
in purchasing the property will be impacted. He feels strongly that a budget amendment should be considered by the Council to set
aside funding. Council member White stated she supports setting the funding aside, but she is not sure that the funds should be
dedicated to the Grandview Elementary property. Council member Lopez stated that he would like to hear from the Mayor, on the
record, about his plans relative to the Grandview property. Chair Choberka stated that the Mayor may not have verbally
communicated his feelings about the Grandview property, but he has sent written communication to the Council, which is public
communication. She then stated she is also comfortable setting the money aside, but she does not want to give residents false hope
that means the City will be purchasing the property. She added she is not sure that purchase of the Grandview property is the best
use of City funds, but there may be other properties available for purchase in the future and the funding could be used for that. The
SD Council then engaged in discussion and debate regarding the proper balance between preserving open space and preparing for
other economic development opportunities.
Property Tax Increase: Council member Hyer stated that he has been in touch with County Treasurer Bond about
property taxation in Weber County and he has offered to attend a future work session meeting and provide information about the
property tax formula and the impact the inflation in property value has on a resident’s property tax bill.
