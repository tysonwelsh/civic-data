Mrs. Robison presented the Staff Report and identified the subject property on an aerial map
displayed. The area shown in red was identified as Heavy Commercial with Light Industrial shown
in gray. Most of the I-1 area is filled in with the exception of the small portion of the Mountain
Point development. The applicants approached the City in 2013 and received Site Plan approval.
Currently, there are two existing buildings along 14600 South. They are proposing to place a third
and possibly fourth building on the subject property. Mrs. Robison explained that the Industrial
zone is built out with the exception of a small parcel adjacent to the Heavy Commercial. The area
has been looked at by the City for potential improvements.
The developer is asking to change the building height. The ordinance was adopted in 2000 and
since then some construction and uses have changed. The applicant’s desire was to allow the
building to be a bit taller. It took time for the applicant to get the project going due to water and
other issues that have arisen over the years. The applicants are asking for a height increase to 45
feet to be consistent with the adjacent Heavy Commercial zone. In 2017, the hotel was approved.
The language specifies that heights should not exceed 35 feet or be lower than 10 feet unless
reviewed and approved by the Fire Chief/Fire Marshall and the City Council. At that time, the City
Council approved all site plans but does not any longer. In 2017, the ordinance language was
reviewed that states that for Heavy Commercial, all buildings and structures must adhere to all
Building and Fire Codes. The language was removed because it was specific to who had to grant
the approval and does not apply to site plans today.
Mr. Crowell stated that it was a subjective sentence and that applying the Code is objective since
the criteria are known. Mrs. Robison suggested that the Commission consider changing the
language in the I-1 zoning to reflect what exists currently in the Heavy Commercial zone. The
building standards were compared to Light Industrial and Heavy Commercial with the only
difference being building height. She felt that changing the building height now would not likely be
detrimental to what currently exists. The intent was to accommodate these types of flex spaces to
allow for more economic growth in the community. The Site Plan that was approved in March
2013 was presented and included several buildings planned for the site. The applicants have done
two phases so far and received Preliminary Plat approval. The Final Plat was approved at staff

7. PUBLIC HEARING, CONSIDERATION, AND RECOMMENDATION _on_a

Proposed Text Amendment to the Bluffdale City Land Use Ordinance 11.350, Land

Use Tables, to Allow Kennels in the GC-1 Zone, Tracie Kay, Applicant. (Application

2024-07) — Staff Presenter, Ellen Oakman.
Ms. Oakman presented the Staff Report and stated that the request is for a Text Amendment to the
Land Use Code to add the definition of kennels as permitted in the GC-1 zone. The intent was to
update the Land Use Table standards to allow kennels where they previously did not exist.
Currently, kennels are not listed in the Land Use Table but are found in the definitions Section of
Chapter 20. They are allowed in the Special District zone in the Ranch; however, kennels are not
allowed in most zones, which is why they are defined in the definitions chapter. She explained that
“kennel” is defined as a commercial establishment with three or more dogs or cats for the purpose
of boarding, breeding, buying, grooming, letting for higher, training for a fee, or selling. The
proposed text amendment is to add “kennel” or “kennels” to the Land Use Table and permit them in
the GC-1 zone.
Chair Cragun stated that a kennel is described as having three or more animals and asked if there is
language specifying a maximum number of animals. Ms. Oakman was not aware of any. Chair
Cragun commented that the health and safety of the animals that come to the facility could be
dependent on how crowded the kennel is. Ms. Oakman stated that a footnote could be added to
specify a maximum.
Commissioner Flynn asked if it should be considered as being similar to a stable for horses and
asked what zoning would allow for that use. Mrs. Robison stated that kennels are not allowed in
any other commercial zone currently with the exception of a kennel that is allowed in the Gateway
Commercial zone.
Commissioner Kraupp asked if there is any background or history as to why kennels have never
been included. Mrs. Robison stated that one reason is that people worry about being near barking
dogs and the animals being outside. This is not the first time staff has been approached about
allowing kennels but they have simply never been allowed anywhere else. The Gateway
Commercial zone specifies that kennels include inside boarding only and limit outdoor activities.
