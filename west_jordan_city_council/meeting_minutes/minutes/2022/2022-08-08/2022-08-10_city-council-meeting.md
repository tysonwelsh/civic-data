          Larry Gardner oriented the Council with proposed Article M of Title 13 Chapter 5
          regarding “Integrated Housing”, and proposed amendments to Section 13-5-1 “Zoning
          Districts” to add two zones: Integrated Housing-Limited Density (IH-L) and Integrated
          Housing Development (IH-D).

           Chair Whitelock opened a public hearing at 8:13 pm

           Comments:
           None

           Chair Whitelock closed the public hearing at 8:13 pm

           Vice Chair Green thanked staff for working on the Code, and said he felt middle housing
           had not been included for a long time. He said he knew from experience that a duplex
           could be in the middle of single-family homes and work as a neighborhood.

           Responding to a question from Council Member McConnehey, Vice Chair Green said the
           intent of the language was that if a developer was going to donate property for
           community housing, the City could verify the land transfer and waive impact fees. Scott
           Langford said the waived impact fees could total $12,000 for one lot. City Attorney Rob
           Wall clarified that impact fees could not legally be waived and said impact fees would
           be recognized as an in-kind off-set rather than “waived”. Some consideration would
           need to be shown. Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck added that the offset
           was required to be paid to the Impact Fee Fund by the General Fund. Mr. Gardner
           suggested staff revise the document prior to Council approval.

           Chair Whitelock said she was not comfortable with asking residents to pay the impact
           fees for higher density. Vice Chair Green suggested striking the line referring to waived
           impact fees, acknowledging that the issue could be revisited in the future if required by
           the Legislature. Council Member Bloom said she was comfortable with the suggestion.

           Council Member Jacob suggested changing the language to state all impact fees would
           be paid by the City. Chair Whitelock said she was not in favor. Council Member
           McConnehey said he would want an exception or qualifier stating the City may choose
           to pay the impact fees if funding was included in the budget approved by the Council.
           Mr. Wall commented that land trusts often required city participation. The issue could
           be considered by the Council on a case-by-case basis and included in a master
           development agreement.

 8. BUSINESS ITEMS
       a. Ordinance No. 22-41 setting the 2022 property tax rate.
          Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck oriented the Council regarding proposed
          Ordinance No. 22-41 which would set the West Jordan property tax rate at 0.001476, a
          5.6% increase over the certified tax rate. The proposed rate would be the lowest in at
          least ten years.




City Council Meeting Minutes              Wednesday, August 10, 2022                           Page 7
           Vice Chair Green noted that the Council eliminated the Solid Waste transfer to spread
           the expense over a larger base. He said he was disappointed and believed the Council
           could have done more to get the property tax rate a little lower. Vice Chair Green said
           he believed the City had done a good job with use of one-time funds.

           Chair Kayleen Whitelock noted that in May the Mayor presented the Council with his
           budget with a 10% property tax increase. The Council had approved a significant pay
           increase in August of 2021 to retain and attract police, at that time our Council was told
           it would require a 10% property tax increase. The Council spent many hours with Ms.
           Steck and her team reviewing the budget and finding areas where we could cut the
           Mayor’s budget and save taxpayer dollars. We found we had a 2015 bond that we could
           pay off early with one-time money and save money on the bond as well as decrease the
           property tax increase. We took away the transfer from solid waste to the general fund
           and will decrease the solid waste fee by the same amount. The Communications budget
           has decreased as we have moved the funds to the departments it is being used for, and
           we hope to be able to share more information via electronic means which will help cut
           the costs. The proposed tax increase is now down to 5.76%. In this budget no personnel
           have been added to the Council's staff, and all city employees will be receiving a COLA,
           and some based on a market study will see a change in their wages. The Council is
           cognizant of the many asks on this year's property tax statement and have worked hard
           to only ask for the amount needed to maintain our staff. This increase will be somewhere
           between $20 and $40 per household per year. Again, we understand that even this can
           be hard. We thank the residents of the city for their input and support of our city.

           Council Member Worthen commented that the Council would experience the same tax
           rate increase as the rest of the City. She emphasized that a majority of the City’s property
           tax revenue paid for City employees and expressed support. Council Member Worthen
           thanked everyone involved in putting together the FY 2023 Budget, and said she thought
           they did a really good job.

           Council Member McConnehey said he would support the rate proposed, but felt the rate
           should be higher, with a decrease in the utility rate transfer. Council Member Bloom
           commented on the need to get the Legislature involved in the conversation and
           encouraged everyone to speak to their Legislators. She spoke of the need to work
           together as a community to help those who struggled as prices went up. Council Member
           Pack said he was grateful for the work and collaborative efforts in putting together the
           FY 2023 Budget.

           MOTION: Council Member McConnehey moved to APPROVE Ordinance No. 22-
           41 setting the 2022 property tax rate. Council Member Worthen seconded the
           motion.

           Council Member Pack said he had found the allowance to be a lot of work to use. Mr.
