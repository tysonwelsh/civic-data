I.     Welcome – Mayor Dahle.
Mayor Dahle called the meeting to order at approximately 6:00 p.m. He read into the record the
Order on Public Meetings of the City Council of the City of Holladay.

II.    Public Comments.
There were no public comments.

III.    Consideration of Resolution 2020-24 – Approving a Development Agreement with
        Holladay Hotel, LLC.
Council Member Gibbons suggest an amendment to Recital D – change granted Site Plan approval
to any future Site Plan approval.

Council Member Petersen moved to approve Resolution 2020-24 with the proposed amendment by
Council Member Gibbons. Council Member Quinn seconded the motion. The Council roll call
vote was as follows: Council Members Fotheringham, Gibbons, Durham, Quinn, Petersen and
Mayor Dahle in favor. Resolution 2020-24 was approved by a unanimous vote.

IV.    Consideration of Resolution 2020-23 Granting Advice and Consent for the Appointment
       of Members to the Planning Commission.
Manager Chamness introduced Howard Layton from District 5. Mr. Layton stated he has lived in
Holladay for 30 years and is active in real estate. He is looking forward to serving. Council
Member Gibbons feels his broad background in real estate and development will be an asset to the
Commission. Mr. Layton is doing mostly consulting and commercial real estate so there should be
no conflicts of interest.

Chris Layton, no relation, has been recommended for re-appointment and is willing to continue on
the Commission.

Council Member Gibbons moved to approve Resolution 2020-23. Council Member Fotheringham
seconded the motion. The Council roll call vote was as follows: Council Members Fotheringham,

V.      Consideration of Resolution 2020-25 Extending the City Manager’s Emergency
        Declaration and all Associated Addenda.
Manager Chamness reported that in March they approved an Emergency Declaration to receive
federal funds and that declaration is now expiring. The proposed resolution extends the declaration
through the end of 2020.

Council Member Petersen moved to approve Resolution 2020-25. Council Member Fotheringham
seconded the motion. The Council roll call vote was as follows: Council Members Fotheringham,
Gibbons, Durham, Quinn, Petersen and Mayor Dahle in favor. Resolution 2020-25 was approved by
a unanimous vote.

VI.    Quarterly Reports.
       a.      Unified Police Department (“UPD”) – Chief Hoyal.
Chief Justin Hoyal presented a quarterly report for the Unified Police Department from April
through June of 2020. The report addressed crime trends, call volume, and other relevant statistics
within the City. There was a brief discussion about responses to burglar alarms, traffic stops, and
canine services. Chief Hoyal provided the Council with an overview of recent citizen
communications.

       b.     Unified Fire Authority (“UFA”) – Captain Dan Brown.
UFA Captain Dan Brown provided a presentation of responses that took place in the last quarter.
Ms. Chamness thanked Captain Brown for securing masks for City residents to be distributed on
August 20. Captain Brown informed the Council that he was asked to address COVID-19 logistics.
There was a brief discussion about recent grass fires.

VII.   Consent Agenda.
       a.     Approval of Minutes – May 28 and June 4 and 18, 2020.
Mayor Dahle asked for clarification on the second page, the second paragraph of the May 28
minutes regarding the rezone and the driveways facing Butternut and 4370 South. It was agreed the
minutes were written correctly.

Council Member Fotheringham moved to approve the Minutes of May 28 and June 4 and 18, 2020.
Council Member Quinn seconded the motion. The motion passed with the unanimous consent of
the Council.

VIII. City Manager Report – Manager Chamness.
Ms. Chamness reported that the City of Holladay has completed the Safe Sidewalk Project on
Gunderson Lane and 2000 East in time for back to school. She also reported that the slurry seal
work is underway in Districts 2 and 5 and should be completed in the next few weeks.

Ms. Chamness informed the Council that she has addressed an issue regarding raccoons with
Council Member Durham. About five years ago the City entered into a contract with Animal
Services. Animal Services contracted with the United States Department of Agriculture (“USDA”)
to perform raccoon removal, however, the City was unable to determine how often the services

been reported by additional residents. Ms. Chamness would work to obtain updated data regarding

IX.    Council Reports and District Issues.
Council Member Petersen asked if there were any updates regarding the Cottonwood Mall. Mayor
Dahle reported that issues are being addressed currently regarding various agreements but the atter
should move forward in the next few weeks.

Council Member Quinn reported a concern raised by a constituent regarding safety for pedestrians
walking on Wander Lane between Murray Holladay Road and 4500 South. It was suggested that
parking be restricted along the west side of the street, however, the issue was delayed due to
COVID-19. Council Member Petersen suggested reminding parents that Wander Lane is not part of
the safe route for students walking to school but to also resend a link to remind parents of the Safe
Walking Routes.

Council Member Fotheringham asked about the bike lane issue. Ms. Chamness stated that staff met
to address the matter and determined that there are complications regarding the logistics of the bike
lane. Staff was currently exploring possible solutions to address the concerns and should have a
recommendation by the end of the month. Council Member Fotheringham also inquired about an
update regarding a boarding house concern in his district and broken speed sign. Ms. Chamness
stated that she would follow-up on both matters.

Council Member Gibbons reported on the gravel pit being proposed for development. The proposal
is to open a new entry road on Wasatch Blvd that the Council should be aware of. He also
mentioned a recent conversation about Tanner Ditch where a proposal was made to create a special
service district for the landowners who have open ditches in the network. He suggested the district
create a line item on the property tax bill for the City and possibly cover other costs.

Mayor Dahle informed the Council of recent updates on COVID-19 statistics and stated that the
County is trending down. Statewide the Governor has reported that Utah is doing better. Mayor
Dahle mentioned that the Granite School District Board voted to open up on August 24 with a full
schedule but with an option for families to opt-out. The decision raised safety concerns within the
community.

Council Member Fotheringham moved to recess the City Council Meeting and reconvene in a Work
Meeting. Council Member Petersen seconded the motion. The motion passed with the unanimous
consent of the Council. The Council convened in a work meeting at approximately 8:06 pm.

X.     Recess City Council to a Work Meeting.
       a.      Discussion on Storm Water Fee Timeline.
Assistant to the City Manager, Holly Smith reported that the Council previously decided to move
forward with the Storm Water Fee sooner rather than later. She presented a timeline regarding the
process and stated that the data will be reviewed with the Council on August 27. Collection of the
fee will begin in about one year. Mayor Dahle mentioned that public education will be very
important and asked if the issue should be introduced in the September newsletter. Ms. Smith
agreed and stated that project launch information will be included in the September newsletter. She
concluded that there will be some flexibility in the timeline if needed.

        b.     Update on CARES Budget and Plan.
Ms. Chamness reported that the City is still negotiating a contract with Utah Community Action in
terms of rental assistance. Staff met with the County in terms of the County’s willingness to act as
an agent for Holladay for business outreach and loans. The County indicated that they would be
willing to do so but staff was still waiting to hear on a final decision. Ms. Chamness also informed
the Council that staff is planning to do resident mask pick-up on August 20 and business masks and
hand sanitizer on August 27.

       c.      Discussion on City Marketing for Face Masks.
Council Member Quinn commented that it would be helpful for businesses to know that the City
supports the mask mandate. She suggested distributing flyers about the City’s stance in support of
the mandate. Council Member Fotheringham had heard that once the County came out in support
of the mask mandate that public disgruntlement seemed to be resolved in one particular instance.
There was discussion that people are not objecting as much now as they were previously. It was
suggested that CARES Act funds be used to market the City of Holladay’s support of the mask
mandate. Council Member Petersen suggested discussing the topic with the Chamber prior to
spending funds. It was agreed that the funds should be used in a manner that is most beneficial to
the businesses and residents of the City. Mayor Dahle agreed to reach out to discuss the issue with
the Chamber.

        d.     Discussion on Holladay Boulevard Speed.
Council Member Petersen commented that it is a serious matter to reduce the speed in an area. She
would support the reduction if it is only in the Cottonwood Elementary area, beyond that the road is
meant to be an arterial and it should remain at a higher speed. She supported an additional traffic
study in the next six to 12 months. Council Member Gibbons agreed and stated that the change in
the school area was universally desired. He suggested a boundary line of 5255 South or the
intersection of Hillsden Drive and Holladay Boulevard or 5380 South to the east. Ms. Chamness
mentioned that there is currently a traffic sign near the school, however, staff will have further
discussions on the speed with the County.

        e.     ADU Policy Direction.
Council Member Fotheringham raised the possibility of addressing acreage in terms of a lot relative
to the zone that it is in as opposed to a hard and fast half one-acre condition. There was discussion
regarding how the process would look to address this concern. The Council discussed the proposal
and agreed that it should be part of the policy and guidance will be given to staff.
