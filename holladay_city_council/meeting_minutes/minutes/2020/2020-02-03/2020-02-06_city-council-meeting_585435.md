IV.   Public Hearing on Proposed Rezone for Property Located at 1306 East Spring Lane from
      Public (P) to Residential Multi-Family (RM).
Mayor Dahle reported that the proposed rezone has been reviewed by the Planning Commission.

Paul Allred, Community Development Dir., reported that the rezone request involves 1.19 acres on
the corner of Spring Lane and 1300 East. The property is currently occupied by church buildings
belonging to the Christ Scientist Church. It is proposed that the property be rezoned from the
Public Zone, which is used for schools, churches, parks, etc. The Planning Commission
unanimously recommended approval. There was significant public comment most of which was
strong opposition. The main concerns pertained to access and traffic safety. Mr. Allred explained
that the majority of the comments were received from the residents of Tobermory, which is also
zoned RM.

Mr. Allred reported that the Planning Commission was of the opinion that the request complies with
the General Plan. The suggested zoning for the land use designation is RM. If there were no
church on the property it would not have been zoned for single-family. The natural land use on the
subject property is an extension of Sand Piper and the Tobermory land use.

Mr. Allred reported that many of the concerns identified by the neighbors pertained to issues that
would typically be addressed at the next stage in the process. The issue tonight was to determine

the proper land use. The Planning Commission will deal with the specifics at a later stage if the
zoning is granted.

In response to a question raised by Mayor Dahle, Mr. Allred stated that the maximum allowable
residential density is 16 units per acre. Legitimate concerns were raised about traffic turning and
traveling eastbound from 1300 East onto Spring Lane. The issue of how to address access off of
Spring Lane was discussed.

Jeff Kruckenberg- Applicant, .from Alan Kruckenberg Construction - 5411 South Vine Street, Suite
4-A. He stated that the proposed change is consistent with the 2019 General Plan update, which
addresses the need for more affordable housing in the City. Increasing the density in the proposed
location and creating an infill development will allow for an affordable residential product in the
City of Holladay. It will not be substantially different from the surrounding uses as evidenced by
Wolf Hollow, Sand Piper, and the Tobermory developments.

Mayor Dahle opened the public hearing.

Doug Beck, a Tobermory resident. He had no issue with the proposed zone change but stated that
the applicant did not address the idea that there is a 22-foot entrance into the church property.
Currently, it is not possible to pull onto Spring Lane and make a right turn into a residence on
Tobermory Ridge if a car is parked there. He stated that there is not enough room for two cars. Mr.
Beck explained that on Spring Lane there are 16 guest parking stalls. He saw no reason for vehicles
to park on Tobermory Way. What is proposed is a recipe for disaster. Specific traffic issues were
described.

Kristine Goddard - 5061 South Oban Court. At the last Planning Commission Meeting it was
stated that there will only be one access, which she found upsetting. She discovered that there is a
fence with a locked gate between the end of the entrance that is intended to serve as the secondary
exit. In the event of an emergency, there would be no way to get off of Tobermory Way because of
increased traffic, which was of concern to her.

Peggy Scheltzback, a Tobermory Way resident. She commented that the most significant problem is
the lack of a sidewalk on Tobermory Way, which is less than 15 feet wide and cannot be widened.
If there were trucks coming out of the right-of-way, it would prevent residents from getting in or
out. They were informed by the HOA property manager that they have rights associated with their
right-of-way to maintain access regardless of who owns the property. She indicated that she has a
key to the locked gate and stressed that the secondary access is strictly for use by the Fire
Department. She did not feet it was logical for the City to allow the secondary access to be used.
She pointed out that all of the homes on Spring Lane are one level.

Mayor Dahle clarified that the purpose of tonight’s meeting was to consider the appropriateness of
the zone and not an approval of the twin homes. There will be another opportunity for public
comment when the specific proposal comes forward. It was anticipated that the matter will be voted
on by the Council on February 20.

There were no further public comments. The public hearing was closed.

V.     Quarterly Reports.
       a.     Introduction of New Emergency Management Coordinator.
Manager Chamness introduced the City’s New Emergency Management Coordinator, Julie Harvey.
This year the Unified Fire Authority began a new program to coordinate emergency management
and allowed cities to opt-in for an additional fee. The City purchased two days of time per week
and Ms. Harvey began work on December 2. The City was very pleased with the scope of Ms.
Harvey’s experience in emergency management. Ms. Harvey worked for 29 in various types of
service in the Air Force and received her emergency management background in the State of
Alaska.

Appreciation was expressed on behalf of the City for David Chisholm for the many years he
donated his time toward this effort.

        b.     UPD – Introduction of New Code Enforcement Officer – Chief Hoyal.
Chief Hoyal reported that since 2016 there have been significantly fewer calls for service in the
City. The fourth quarter compared to the previous year was down 164 calls. Response time data
was presented with Priority 1 response times being two to three minutes. Chief Hoyal highlighted
the SWAT team, which is highly trained and specialized to respond to dangerous situations. The
SWAT team consists of 40 officers and responded to 77 operations throughout the year. During the
last quarter of 2019, they responded to 20 UPD operations. They are highly trained, very
specialized, and a great asset to the City.

Chief Hoyal reported that in October 2019 there were 113 citations issued, 253 in November, and
166 in December. He noted that the average is 150 to 200 per month.

Council Member Quinn reported that she received comments from constituents who are concerned
about traffic on their respective streets. She asked about the best means of communication to reach
the UPD. Chief Hoyal stated that there is a link on the City’s website where traffic-related issues
can be reported.

Another Council Member had a constituent raise a question about property damage citations. Chief
Hoyal stated that whenever there is vandalism to private property, an officer can take an initial
report and start an investigation.

Chief Hoyal reported that to date he has received one response for the UPD Training scheduled for
February 28.

        c.       UFA – Captain Brown.
Captain Brown presented the quarterly report for the fourth quarter of 2019 and noted that the
Holladay Fire Station typically responds to five calls per day. Crews are most often dispatched on
sick person calls and falls. Response time data was presented and specific calls described. It was
clarified that the report shows only calls that were generated within the City of Holladay.

Mayor Dahle commented that it might be important to address the VECC issue since it is affecting
response times. Ms. Chamness offered to conduct a more complete discussion at a future meeting.

VI.    Consideration of Ordinance 2020-01 Amending Title 13 Pertaining to Maximum Heights
       Allowed within the Holladay Village Zone.
The issue of the building height maximum was addressed. The Mayor commented that if the intent
was to go 14 feet on the elevator height, it would be to accommodate accessory uses on the roof.

Council Member Petersen moved to adopt Ordinance 2020-01 with the following additional
language:

      1. 14 feet or less for an elevator only when the elevator provides access to rooftop amenities.
         Maximum 2,500-pound elevator capacity rating.

Council Member Fotheringham seconded the motion. The Council roll call vote was as follows:
Council Members Gibbons, Quinn, Fotheringham, Durham, Petersen and Mayor Dahle in favor.
Ordinance 2020-01 was adopted by a unanimous vote.

IX.    Consideration of Resolution 2020-09 Adopting an Interlocal Agreement with Salt Lake
       County for Aerial Photography.
Mr. Teerlink reported that the City has not had an update to its aerial imagery since 2015. Usually,
imagery is obtained from Google Earth, however, the Salt Lake County Recorder’s Survey
Department provides its own aerial imagery and each individual municipality can share in the
purchase of the photography. It provides a resolution that is similar to military-grade 10 or 15 years
ago and is helpful in infill ordinances.

Council Member Petersen moved to approve Resolution 2020-09. Council Member Quinn
seconded the motion The Council roll call vote was as follows: Council Members Gibbons, Quinn,
Fotheringham, Durham, Petersen and Mayor Dahle in favor. Resolution 2020-09 was approved by
a unanimous vote.

XI.     Council Reports and District Issues.
Council Member Gibbons addressed speed limit and safety issues on Holladay Boulevard and near
Cottonwood Elementary. There is a residential neighborhood between 4700 South and 6200 South
where there are no businesses or offices with a 40 MPH speed limit. His understanding is that it is a
City road and traffic engineers will not recommend the speed limit be more than five MPH different
than the 85th percentile of the average speed. Council Member Gibbons questioned whether a speed
limit of 35 MPH might be more appropriate and if there are traffic calming efforts that could be
implemented such as solar-powered speed signs.

Mayor Dahle commented that solar-powered signs would be best addressed during a budget
discussion. With respect to lowering the speed limit, Ms. Chamness stated that there is State
guidance based on street types. If that is a step the Council would like to take, Ms. Chamness asked
that she be given additional guidance on the parameters. Mayor Dahle recommended it be placed
on the Work Meeting agenda as a discussion item before moving forward. Council Member
Petersen commented that reducing the speed limit has been discussed in the past so some data may
already be available.

Council Member Gibbons also commented on the issue of disposable single-use plastic bags. It was
noted that they significantly impact recycling contamination rates. It is also an environmental issue.
HB 320 would have imposed a State-wide moratorium on municipalities but died. It was noted that
at least three municipalities have passed a ban on single-use disposable plastic bags. It was
suggested that the issue be coupled with other east side municipalities such as Cottonwood Heights
and Millcreek.

Council Member Fotheringham reported that Principal Adamson and Mark Kindred, who serves on
the Spring Lane Elementary Community Council, were disappointed in the quality of plowing on
school days. Council Member Fotheringham agreed to register a complaint with the City’s service

XII.    Recess City Council to a Work Meeting.
        a.      Presentation on Canal Trails Study – Jay Bowinkle.
Jay Bowinkle described his work and the topography of the site. He reported that their surveyor
prepared an analysis and it was determined that the canal is owned by Salt Lake City who will allow
access if the City agrees to accept the liability.

Mr. Bowinkle addressed ownership and title issues. He reported that two parcels on Walker and
Cottonwood Lanes do not have clear title so the canal company does not know if there is clear
access. One option was to construct a trail next to the road. He explained that Salt Lake City would
like to have an access road along the canal since some areas need to be maintained. In some areas,
trees are impeding the canal and restricting the flow of water. Access issues were addressed as well
as options for providing a trail through the area.

Mayor Dahle was interested in speaking with Murray City officials about how they worked with the
property owners. He commented that a trail would be a phenomenal asset to the City and a great
opportunity. Mr. Godfrey commented that legally it will be an extensive and lengthy process.

Council Member Petersen recommended forming a neighborhood committee to address the matter
further. Mr. Bowinkle commented that several Holladay residents were surveyed and 80%
indicated that if they owned property on the canal, they would be willing to put a trail through.
When surveys are conducted on parks and recreation matters, trails are always the most desired
amenity. The next step was to schedule public meetings in the spring.

Mr. Allred commented on the importance of the section between VanWinkle Parkway and Highland
Drive since it would easily connect to the section going up to Murray Holladay Road from Walker
Lane. He stressed the importance of interconnectivity. Council Member Petersen stated that it
could also be connected to Knudsen Park where there is parking and easy access to the trail.

        b.     Grants Update.
Ms. Chamness commented on the Holladay @20 and how much has been done with grant funds. A
list was prepared of long-term grants in the City in terms of their overall success. Ms. Smith stated
that grant writing is labor-intensive and it can take 40 to 80 hours to prepare a grant application.
Various members of staff, the Mayor, and the Council have been involved in making the City’s
grant program a success. The Grant Committee monthly to review upcoming grant applications,
grants that have been submitted, what has been funded, and the status of the various projects. They

have worked diligently to build relationships with regional agencies and organizations that make
funding decisions. Ms. Smith reported that approximately eight grant applications are submitted
every year with the City being awarded more than half. They are selective in terms of the grants

       c.      Discussion on Enforcement Issues with Short-Term Rentals.
Ms. Chamness reported that in addressing enforcement, there had been a discussion with the Chief
and several officers that suggested the ordinance be clarified to make enforcement easier. Language
was added clarifying that any violation would constitute a Class B Misdemeanor and any rental
income collected during a period of violation would be remitted to the City.

Mayor Dahle explained that there is an ordinance in place dealing with short-term rentals but
enforcement is an issue. Mr. Godfrey suggested that the permitting of short-term rentals be
addressed in greater detail. He explained that a web listing cannot be used as the sole means of
enforcement. It is a criminal matter but the City also has a civil right to provide enforcement.
