Budget Presentation FY 2024/2025 – Mayor Holly H. Daines (18:35)

Mayor Daines addressed the Council and presented the following.

At Logan City, our guiding budget philosophy is to budget for revenues conservatively,
and we continue with that practice. Conservative revenue projections cause us to
examine proposed expenditures carefully. Furthermore, when actual results exceed our
conservative projections, those funds in turn are dedicated to the next most critical
capital project, which further reduces pressure on future operating budgets. Conservative
projections pay dividends in the future, while overly aggressive projections can actually
borrow from an uncertain future.

In the general fund, which is shared by many departments including police, fire, public
works, and parks and recreation, as well as all other general government services we
provide, we have continued with a collaborative approach to the Target Based Budgeting
principles we have used for many years. The goal is to give departments more flexibility
to address what they see as their most critical budgetary needs. Departments have been
asked to examine their budgets carefully looking for ways to streamline services and
reallocate budgeted amounts, so we might better utilize our scarce resources.

After several years of growth in sales tax revenues, we are projecting the fiscal year 2025
sales tax to remain relatively flat. Sales tax is the most significant source of revenue to
the general fund and can vary significantly with the flow of the overall economy. It is
important that we watch this revenue source carefully and respond conservatively when
sales tax growth flattens, as we are seeing now.
As you may be aware, property tax revenues to the City do not necessarily go up as
property values rise. While it is true that a specific property valuation done by Cache
County may influence the specific amount a certain taxpayer might pay in property tax
from one year to the next, the total amount of property tax revenue that comes to the City
from existing taxpayers remains the same. This is mandated by the state legislature. In
                                                                                   3 | Page
              Logan Municipal Council Minutes ~ Logan, Utah ~ April 16, 2024
other words, unless the City raises the property tax rate through a process the legislature
calls “Truth in Taxation,” property tax revenue from existing taxpayers remains flat.
Logan City has not raised the general fund property tax levy since 2010 and has not
raised the library property tax levy since 2017. We believe it is a better policy to raise the
property tax levy through small incremental increases, than through large property tax
rate corrections done periodically. To that end, we are proposing to raise the general
fund and library property tax levies by approximately 3 %. It is our intent to continue this
practice in future budgets.

Emergency management is a critical function for any city, let alone a city the size of
Logan. For many years, Logan has relied upon our departments to act cooperatively to
prepare for emergencies without a dedicated full-time position being named as
Emergency Manager. While this approach has served us well, it is apparent that we are
not as prepared for emergencies as we need to be. In the FY 2025 budget, we are
proposing to add a full-time position to act as Emergency Manager for the City and to be
tasked with preparing and coordinating a unified City response to emergencies.

The electric fund is requesting a new apprentice lineman position. Adding this position
will allow our line crews to operate at recommended staffing levels.

The EMS fund is requesting to convert several part-time positions into a full-time
position. It is anticipated that converting part-time funding to a full-time position will
stabilize turnover and increase reliability.

Our employees are a critical asset for the city and we must reward and retain them.
Although amounts may vary, we have generally budgeted for a 3 % Pay for Performance
increase.

One item with a huge impact on our budget and on our ability to give adequate raises is
the cost of healthcare. The increase in our insurance premiums for this coming fiscal
year is 29 %, compared to 15 %. 7%, and 5 % in the three previous fiscal years. Health
care premiums for employees are paid entirely by the City, so this is a significant benefit
for our employees. This approach is particularly beneficial to those employees on the
lower end of the pay scale. For instance, the increase in family health care premiums
alone is equivalent to a 14 % increase in pay for an employee making $50,000 per year.
We believe our employees value and appreciate our approach to funding employee
healthcare, and we sincerely value and appreciate our employees.

In summary, my number one goal as Mayor is wise fiscal management. We are
accomplishing a wide variety of capital projects and investment in infrastructure, but we
do so in a fiscally responsible way.

As Mayor -- along with my outstanding team -- we pledge to do our very best to provide
excellent services and quality of life for our citizens as we use our resources wisely and
meet the challenges ahead.

Holly H. Daines, Mayor

PROJECT
The proponent (Logan City) is requesting to rezone these four sites to match the
appropriate use. Three are new public facilities and are currently zoned TC-1. Those are
proposed to be rezoned to Public. The fourth is the existing (old) fire station in the City
that will be sold and redeveloped. This site is proposed to be rezoned from Public to TC-
1.

GENERAL PLAN
The Future Land Use Plan (FLUP), adopted in 2008, identifies these four sites as Town
Center (TC). The General Plan, a nonregulatory visioning plan, describes TC areas as
being a hub of the City and Valley and reflects a vibrant downtown, with a variety of
commercial, mixed-use, and institutional uses and activities. The application of the Public
Zone onto public uses in the Town Center is consistent with patterns of historical uses
and compatible with the range of current uses in the adjacent blocks.

Land Development Code (LDC) - Zoning
Both zones encourage walkable development in the downtown area. TC-1 encourages
dense, walkable commercial and residential development in the downtown area with
unique and interesting building designs. The Public zone is being applied to new public
facilities while the TC-1 zone is being applied to the existing fire station site which is
expected to be sold and redeveloped into a commercial center.

SUMMARY
This zoning action is considered "clean-up" zoning to reflect new construction and
changed land uses.
