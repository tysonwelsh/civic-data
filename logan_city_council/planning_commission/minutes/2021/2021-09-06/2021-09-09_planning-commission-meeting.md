 6000 sq ft apartment buildings that include eight townhomes with three levels, three bedrooms

 Residential (CR) zone; TIN 06-058-0006, with alternate parking on 06-058-0005; -0004; -0003.
 (Adams Neighborhood)

STAFF: R. Holley reviewed the request for a design review permit. The proposes project includes
the demolition of one existing home and the construction of two new multi-family student housing
structures on a .45-acre lot at 655 East 400 North. The proposed three-story buildings have four
units each. Each unit has a ground floor 2-car garage, a second-floor kitchen/living room, and a third

The frontage is required to be 75% in the CR zone, this project is at 71%. The Commission will need
to determine if the 71% is adequate.

properties will have one parking stall per one occupant. The remaining cross-access property is a
duplex at 425 North 700 East This duplex property only associates seven parking stalls with a
maximum possible occupancy of 12 tenants. An occupancy restriction will need to be recorded on
both Old Ephraim Apartments and the Duplex as they have the potential for more occupants than
parking stalls.

 PC 21-055 Serenbe Rezone [Zone Change] Riley Duke, owner/authorized agent is requesting
 a zone change of 7.25 acres from Traditional Neighborhood Residential (NR-6) to Mixed
 Residential Transitional (MR-9) to create a new subdivision located at approximately 600 North
 200 West; TIN 05-045-0016. (Bridger Neighborhood)

STAFF: R. Holley reviewed the request for a rezone of 7.25-acres at the northwest corner of 600
North 200 West from Traditional Neighborhood Residential (NR-6) to Mixed Residential Transitional
(MR-9) This property is currently vacant with numerous trees and fencing. Historically the property
has been used for agricultural purposes.

The Future Land Use Plan (FLUP) identifies this property as Detached Residential (DR)
which plans for detached single-family homes developed at a density range of 4-6 units per
acre.

The current zoning designation is NR-6, which is a detached single-family residential zoning
district with a maximum density of six (6) homes per acre. Minimum building lot sizes are
6,000 SF with a minimum 50-foot width. The proposed rezone to the MR-9 allows a range
of housing options. Attached multi-family homes are allowed in this zoning district at a
maximum of 9 units per acre, a minimum lot size of 4,000 SF, and a minimum of 40 feet in
width for single-family homes. This zone intends to function as a transition zone between
commercial or other higher-intensity zones to lower-density areas. MR zones are often
placed near job centers and major corridors to offer more walkable options.

The surrounding zones are NR-6 directly surrounding this property, with Mixed Residential
Low (MR-12) further to the Southeast and Mobile Home (MH) further to the Northwest.


The surrounding neighborhoods have a mix of single and multi-family structures with a
scattered zoning past. This was one of the main reasons, along with school district pressure
to provide less dense single-family homes, why large portions of the Bridger Neighborhood
were downzoned in 2007. This downzone was intended to stop future multi-family
development and increase the balance of single-family housing stock. The proponent’s
description to create less of a “tract home” feel indicated on the application can be
accomplished in the current NR-6 zoning district with larger lot sizes and less overall density.
Staff recommends denial of this rezone request.

 PC 21-051 LDC Amendment Chapter 17.08, 17.11, 17.37, 17.62 [Code Amendment] Mike
 DeSimone/Logan City, request to amend chapter 17.08 “Neighborhood Residential Uses”; Chapter
 17.11 “District and Corridor Zone Uses”; Chapter 17.37 “Additional Development Standards”; and
 Chapter 17.62 “Definitions” To include standards for community agricultural uses, urban
 agricultural uses, and the keeping of domestic fowl, bees, and livestock in urban areas.

STAFF: M. DeSimone review the proposed code amendments

Chapter 17.37: Additional Development Standards
Purpose:
   • Establish minimum standards for keeping domestic fowl (chickens), bees, & livestock in
      residential areas
   • Clarify the distinction in types of “Agriculture” that are appropriate for Logan – community
      gardens, community-supported gardens, market gardens, commercial/industrial agriculture
   • Minimize negative impacts of keeping fowl, bees, & livestock on adjoining properties

Proposed 17.37.080 – Chickens
  • Numbers of chickens tied to minimum lot size
  • Only permitted on a lot with a detached SFR
  • Roosters, geese, turkeys, peacocks only in limited areas
  • Confined to a coop and/or run – no free-range chickens
  • Minimum setbacks of chickens from P/L and dwellings
  • Located in fenced rear yard
  • Minimum size & maintenance standards for coops & runs
  • Health and Sanitation Standards – odors, rodents, insects, vermin, pests, diseases

Proposed 7.37.090 – Bees
   • Numbers of beehives tied to minimum lot size
   • Only permitted on a lot with a detached SFR
   • Beekeeper has to be registered with Utah Dept. of Ag
   • Operational & Maintenance Standards – condition & water
   • Minimum setbacks of bees from P/L and dwellings
   • Flyaway Barrier – direct bees on flight path & elevation

Proposed 17.37.120 & 17.62 – Community Agriculture
   • Community Agriculture versus Agriculture
   • Community Gardens – nonprofit entities or collectives
   • Community Supported Gardens – “CSA” or collective types of small gardens less than 2
      acres where produce is grown for members/shareholders
   • Market Gardens – ½ acre gardens where produce, flowers, plants are grown for off-site
      distribution and sale (pumpkins/flowers)


   •   Urban Agriculture – food production within an urban area on a scale that is for personal use
       and consumption
