1. Welcome Breakfast
   Mayor Johnson welcomed everyone and noted that all Councilmembers were present, and that
   Councilor Albrecht joined virtually. Mayor Johnson offered the prayer.

2. Keynote Speaker and Council Training- Cameron Moon with Franklin Covey
   Mr. Moon facilitated the training “Trust and Inspire”.

3. Lunch and Miscellaneous items
   Matt Dalton, with the Water Department, gave an update on the water and snowpack this year
   which is above average. He noted that the reservoir’s storage capacity is still very low. Mr.
   Dalton stated that it’s still important to keep conservation efforts going with public outreach.
   There was discussion about changing the water phases. Matt stated that there is no reason for
   residents to water more than 3 times per week.

   Andy Gowans, Management Analyst, gave an update on the Legislative Session. He stated that
   key messages for the Legislators include that the State has a surplus but cities do not and that
   we want the State to partner with local government. Some bills that are concerning include land

4. Citizen Survey Results
   Emily Schill, with Y2 Analytics, presented the annual citizen survey results. She stated that
   growth is again a major concern for residents and the things that come with it. Water
   conservation is also a concern. They’ve seen a downward trend since 2021 in the overall
   satisfaction that’s consistent across high growth communities. Generally, residents are still
   highly satisfied. Snow removal saw the most improvement from last year.

5. Financial Update
   Dean Lundell, Finance Director, gave an economic update. He stated that inflation was up
   6.45%. Retail sales numbers have been consistent. Big items for next year’s budget will be
   Station 83 construction and staffing, along with the new Library and City Hall. He reviewed the
   utility funds and suggested rate increases for some funds such as pressurized irrigation due to
   the operating losses in that fund. There will need to be some rate discussions on pressurized
   irrigation to determine when to implement usage rates and what those rates look like. It’s
   reasonable to expect that these rates will increase from year to year. He would prefer that rate
   increases are more frequent but small. Small increases were recommended for garbage, and
   storm drain. TSSD is hopeful that they will have a place to take the green waste soon. Mr.
   Lundell also suggested that they may need to increase the green waste rates. Power had an
   operating loss last year, but they had cash reserves to help with that and they implemented a
   rate increase.

   Mr. Lundell reviewed the City’s financial policies, which are necessary for a good bond rating.
   There was discussion about adjusting the targeted general fund reserve from 20-25 to 25-30
   percent. It was also suggested that they add that the reserves could be used for specific purposes
   such as an economic downturn.

8. Family Park Concepts
   Steve Marchbanks presented the proposed Family Park concept plans.

9. Elections Updates and Information
   Teisha Wilson gave an update on important election dates and information.

6. Council Goals Discussion
   The Council reviewed their goals and priorities. Transportation was discussed as a priority
   including public transit. Other priorities included a plan for downtown sidewalk improvements
   and restoration, code enforcement support and staff support for parks and recreation. Councilor
   Koivisto suggested building another rec center with a general obligation bond that would go to
   the voters. Councilor Hancock suggested that they be more aggressive with non-resident fees
   for recreation and the Legacy Center. There was also discussion about expanding or renovating
   the Legacy Center.
