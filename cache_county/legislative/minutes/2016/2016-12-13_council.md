ITEMS OF SPECIAL INTEREST

> Bear River Health Department Annual Report — In response to Chairman Merrill's
request, Director Lloyd Berentzen explained the health department serves three counties
and was put together through an interlocal agreement in 1971. Funding from each
county and fees help support the department; however, grants are a major portion of
their funding. The governing body consists of elected officials from each county, a
physician and a legal representative.

Jill Parker reviewed the ten essential public health services Bear River Health
Department provides.

Council member Potter asked what steps the Health Department will use to keep the
public informed on air quality. Parker replied there is an app — Utah Air — individuals can
download to their phones and receive real time numbers. Director Berentzen noted that
he has seen improvement in air quality from 2004 to the present. Berentzen believes
Tier 3 gasoline (lower-sulphur gasoline required by the EPA in the near future) will
significantly reduce pollution.

Council member Zilles asked how a Cache County citizen applies for the grant money to
cover costs of required repairs for emissions. Berentzen said they are still receiving
details on how that is going to work.

(Attachment 3)

ACTION: Motion by Council member Potter to approve the Bear River Health Department
Annual Report. Yeates seconded the motion. The vote was unanimous, 7-0.

Vice Chairman Erickson said he has received new information about where the funding
for the elected officials’ salaries comes from and asked for additional discussion on the
issue.

A 2% increase for eight elected officials equals $15,661.00 with $5,600.00 coming out of
Assessing and Collecting and around $10,000.00 out of the General Fund. Council
member Potter asked if more of the Assessing and Collecting funds can be used for

Assessing and Collecting. Presently, there are no Auditor salaries coming out of that
allocation.

Referring to the Council's feeling that elected officials’ salaries should only be addressed
prior to the election cycle for that position, Merrill asked that a comprehensive study be
made to determine how Cache County salaries compare to other counties.

officials’ salaries when compared with other counties.

Council member White said the only way to raise wages is to raise taxes. Every county
cannot be equal. Do they deserve a raise? Yes, that is more than fair, but the county is
already drawing from the reserve fund and is in a deficit budget situation.

Re: 2014 RAPZ Allocation

Dear Craig,

The purpose of this letter is to request an extension of time for expending RAPZ funds awarded
to Nibley City by Cache County in 2014.

In 2014, the Cache County Council allocated $40,000 in RAPZ funds to Nibley City for the
Heritage Park — Phase 5 project. The purpose of this project is to construct tennis courts, as
there are currently no tennis courts in Nibley.

As you are also aware, in 2014, Nibley City and Cache County entered into talks related to
recreational property and funding swaps related to the development of Green Canyon High
School in North Logan on land owned by Cache County. As a result of those discussions,
Nibley and Cache County entered into an agreement in which Nibley purchased 20 acres of
land adjacent to Heritage Park. Due to this significant change in Nibley park development, the
Nibley City Council decided to update the City’s Parks Master Plan prior to completing either of
these projects. This has caused a delay in expending the RAPZ funds, which has necessitated
this request for an extension. The master plan is expected to be complete by April 2017, after
which the City will proceed with both projects.

Thank you for your consideration.
