ORDINANCE 22-10: AMEND TITLE 2, CHAPTER 3 OF THE WEST VALLEY
CITY MUNICIPAL CODE, MAKING ADJUSTMENTS IN THE WEST VALLEY
CITY COUNCIL DISTRICT BOUNDARIES
Steve Pastorik, CED, presented proposed Ordinance 22-10 that would amend Title 2,
Chapter 3 of the West Valley City Municipal Code, making adjustments in the West Valley
City Council District Boundaries.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       Section 10-3-205.5, Utah Code Annotated, requires all municipalities, with some
       or all council members being elected from districts, to “make any adjustments in
       the boundaries of the districts as may be required to maintain districts of
       substantially equal population.” In accordance with State law, City staff has
       evaluated existing council districts and has determined that adjustments are
       necessary.

       2020 Census data was utilized by staff to create two options for proposed council
       boundaries. The population of each proposed district varies by no more than 1%
       from the ideal population, which is one fourth of the total City population. In
       addition to balancing population between districts, staff also kept district
       boundaries to major streets. The table below lists the population of each proposed
       district for the two options and the percentage by which each district varies from
       the ideal population.

           District         Option 1         % Change          Option 2         % Change
                                             from Ideal                         from Ideal
                                             Population                         Population
               1                  35,018          -0.11%             34,907          -0.43%
               2                  34,761          -0.84%             35,209           0.43%
               3                  35,279           0.63%             34,942          -0.33%
               4                  35,172           0.33%             35,172           0.33%

Councilmember Harmon stated that he would like to see at least 29 feet of pavement
in any type of street that is approved. Councilmember Huynh stated that he does
not want to see the City take over existing private streets. He indicated, however,
that he would like to see private streets be better constructed in the future.
Councilmember Nordfelt stated that private streets with an association can be
troubling and he would like to limit that in future developments. Mayor Lang stated
that many associations don’t have wide enough streets so parking flows out into the
neighborhoods. She indicated that she would prefer all development to have public
streets except for rear loaded alleys. Councilmember Nordfelt stated that residents
who live in HOA’s are paying the same amount of taxes as those who don’t and
they don’t get their roads maintained by the City. He added that he is worried about
pavement falling into disrepair and the HOA not having funding to fix them. Mayor
Lang suggested allowing rear load alleys with a long private driveway and limiting
that by length. Councilmember Harmon stated that the Council is mostly concerned
about association type situations over flag lot type scenarios. Councilmember
Harmon stated that design flexibility doesn’t benefit the residents or the City and
only benefits developers. Councilmember Whetstone stated that he has seen private
HOA’s push snow from private streets to public streets and asked if this is
permitted. Coby Wilson, City Engineer, stated that he isn’t aware of any restriction
in code but noted that some smaller streets would be nearly impossible to get snow
removed based on their size and the orientation of the stub streets. Councilmember
Harmon stated that many people park on Daybury Drive because the nearby streets
are private. Mayor Lang stated that she would like to ask staff for an alternative to
require public roads in new developments and to change standards to allow
driveway spacing to be closer. Councilmember Fitisemanu clarified this would be
for future development. Mayor Lang replied yes. Councilmember Nordfelt asked if
this would work for City snowplows. Coby replied yes, as long as the design of the
streets were in a circular pattern and there are no small dead-end streets or stubs.
