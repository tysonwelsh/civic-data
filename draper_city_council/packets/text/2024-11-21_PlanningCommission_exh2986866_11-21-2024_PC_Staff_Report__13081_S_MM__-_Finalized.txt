SUMMARY AND BACKGROUND
This application is a request for approval of a Site Plan Amendment and Conditional Use
Permit for approximately 2.93 acres located on the east side of Minuteman Drive, at
approximately 13081 S. Minuteman Drive (Exhibits B and C). The property is currently
zoned CR. The applicant is requesting to modify the site layout, build a new office building
and obtain a conditional use permit for an outdoor storage yard.

The applicant intends to develop the vacant east side of property with an office buildling
that can later be subdivided into commercial/office condominium units to be sold to
individual owners. The western portion of the site is already developed with a large
warehouse/office building currently occupied by Utah Disaster Kleenup (UDK). The

Parking. According to the site plan (Exhibit F), there will be three access points to the
development. One existing access point is located along Minuteman Drive and there is one
along 13065 South. A new access point will be located along 13065 South at the far east
edge of the property. The existing parking areas will be modified to meet current code
requirements and a new parking area will be provided for the proposed additional
commercial/office development. Based on the existing and proposed uses on the site, a
range of between 144 and 176 parking stalls are required. The applicant has provided 145
parking stalls shared between all uses on the site which is at the low end of the 10% natural
adjustment range allowed per Section 9-25-090(A) of the DCMC. While the site does meet
minimum parking requirements, staff is concerned with future uses on the site. The
applicant has indicated that the existing warehouse/office use will not be changed and all
of the proposed uses have been calculated at the parking requirement for office uses. This
means that future uses in individual units will be limited to office use or other uses that are
permitted within the zone with a lesser parking requirement. Staff will not be able to
approve any business licenses unless it can be shown the entire site meets the minimum
parking requirements. Because of the provision of parking on the low end of the natural
adjustment, staff has included a condition of approval stating that each business license
application will need to show that minimum parking requirements for the entire site are
maintained. In addition, some of the minimum required parking for the site is located
within the proposed outdoor storage yard. Staff has included a condition of approval that
the proposed storage yard be limited to employee parking for the existing
warehouse/office as this area will be gated for security and may not be accessible to other
owners or patrons of the other businesses located on the property at all times.

As part of the review for the Site Plan application, Planning staff found that there is no

current Conditional Use Permit for use of the portion of the property as a storage yard for
the current tenant of the exiting warehouse/office building. Staff asked the applicant to
submit the application so that a ‘construction sales and service’ storage yard could be
legalized for the property.

As can be seen from the aerial of the property (Exhibit J) this portion of the site has been in
use by the current tenant, UDK, as an outdoor storage yard since around 2007. The
proposed application will slightly reduce the size of the current area utilized for the storage
yard. Staff does not see any significant issues with this area being used for storage as long
as the design and use of the area complies with the terms of Section 9-27-230(A) for a
storage yard which states:

A.      Screening: All outdoor storage shall be in conjunction with and subordinate to a legally
        established business and shall be allowed only after approval of a conditional use permit
        by the planning commission. The following screening regulations shall apply to open
        storage areas:
        1.      An opaque fence shall be required to screen the view of all open storage areas.
                a.      For screening views from public and private streets the fence shall be
                        constructed of masonry, metal, or the same materials as the adjacent
                        building.
                b.      For screening views from neighboring properties along the side and rear
                        property lines, inclusive of a gate, the fencing may be constructed of the
                        same materials listed in subsection A(1)(a) of this section or chain-link with
                        privacy slats.
        2.      Outdoor storage areas shall not be located closer than twenty feet (20') to a
                residential use or zone.
        3.      Fences used to screen open storage shall be ten feet (10') high unless a lower
                height is approved by the Planning Commission as part of the conditional use
                permit. In approving reduced fence height the planning commission may not
                approve a fence lower than six feet (6') in height and must find that the reduced
                height will not create additional impacts to surrounding properties.
        4.      Materials may not be stored or stacked higher than the fence.
        5.      The planning commission may condition or require that specific materials, colors,
                or design elements to be used in the construction and installation of the screening
                fence or wall, other than those proposed, upon finding that he specific material,
                color, or design element will better mitigate existing or potential impacts to
                surrounding properties.

As indicated above, the fence used to screen the storage area shall be a minimum of 10
feet tall unless a lower height is approved by the Planning Commission. A location for the
installation of a fence and sliding gate accessing the area has been identified on the site
plan, but the applicant did not provide specific details for the construction of the proposed
fencing. For the side facing the public street the fencing will be required to be constructed

E.      Standards For Approval: The following standards shall apply to the approval of a site

STAFF RECOMMENDATION
Staff recommends that the Planning Commission review the requests based on the
suggested findings and the criteria for approval, or denial as listed within the staff report.

comment prior to making a decision.

use permit requests, staff recommends they include the following conditions of approval:

1.      That all requirements of the Draper City Engineering, Public Works, Building,
        Planning, and Fire Divisions shall be satisfied throughout the development of the
        site and the construction of all buildings on the site, including permitting.

        development of the site and the construction of all buildings on the site.

Fire Division Review.
1.      Fire Department Access is required. An unobstructed minimum road width of 24
        feet, exclusive of the shoulders, and a minimum height of 13’6” shall be required.
        The road must be designed and maintained to support the imposed loads of
        emergency apparatus. The surface shall be able to provide all weather driving
        capabilities. The road shall have an inside turning radius of 28 feet. There shall be a
        maximum grade of 10%. Grades may be checked prior to building permits being
        issued.
2.      Fire Hydrants are required. Hydrants are required for this project. They shall be,
        protected with bollards if susceptible to vehicle damage. Fire Flow for the buildings
        shall be a minimum of 2,000 GPM @ 20 p.s.i. residual pressure.
3.   Fire Hydrants. Fire hydrants are required to be operational and
     unobstructed at all times throughout the project. If at any time a hydrant
     needs to be shut down and made inoperable the Fire Marshal shall be
     notified immediately. Just a reminder these fire hydrants shall be operational
     prior to any combustible construction materials arriving on site.
4.   Hydrants and Site Access. All hydrants and a form of acceptable temporary
     Fire Department Access to the site shall be installed and APPROVED by the
     Fire Department prior to the issuance of any Building Permits. If at any time
     during the building phase any of the hydrants or temporary Fire Department
     Access becomes non-compliant any and all permits could be revoked.
5.   Utilities. All utilities including Fire hydrants within the required width of fire
     apparatus access roads shall be installed prior to introducing combustible
     materials to a site or commencing vertical construction. Any excavation in
     the required fire department access roadway after combustible materials are
     on site shall be performed in a manner to maintain access to the site and
     shall be coordinated with the Fire Marshal. Combustible material shall not
     be placed in any way that will impede fire apparatus access to any site.
6.   Site Access Required During Construction. Prior to and throughout
     construction a form of acceptable temporary Fire Department Access to the
     site shall be installed this includes turn arounds. The required access for fire
     apparatus access shall have a base material capable of supporting a 75,000
     lbs vehicle, during most-weather conditions, installed and compacted to 95%
     relative compaction at a minimum of 26 feet wide. There shall be no parking
     of construction vehicles, sub-contractor vehicles within the required fire
     access they shall be parked on the non-hydrant side of the road to allow for
     emergency vehicles to still access the project. If at any time during the
     building phase any of the hydrants or temporary Fire Department Access
     becomes non-compliant any and all permits could be revoked.
7.   Access and Fire Protection During Construction. All buildings under
     construction shall meet the requirements of IFC Chapter 33, Fire Safety
     During Construction and Demolition as well as all of the sections detailed in
     this document.
8.   IFC SECTION 3310 ACCESS FOR FIRE FIGHTING DURING CONSTRUCTION
     a.      3310.1.1 Required access. Approved vehicle access for firefighting shall be,
             provided to all construction or demolition sites. Vehicle access shall be
             provided to within 100 feet of temporary or permanent fire department
             connections. Either temporary or permanent roads, capable of supporting
             vehicle loading under all weather conditions, shall provide vehicle access.
             Vehicle access shall be, maintained until permanent fire apparatus access
             roads are available.

            -      Until permanent fire apparatus access roads are constructed, fire-
                   fighting vehicle access is the means by which fire fighters gain access
                    to the construction or demolition site and building for fire suppression
                    and rescue operations. Such access is an integral component of the
                    fire prevention program. The site superintendent or other person
                    responsible for construction and demolition operations is responsible
                    for maintaining and policing fire-fighter access routes, as provided in
                    Section 3308. Fire apparatus must be able to get within 100 feet of
                    any installed fire department connection supplying water to
                    temporary or permanent fire protection systems. Access roads must
                    support the weight of the heaviest vehicle that might respond. The
                    weight requirements are available from the local fire department. All-
                    weather surfaces are required because the responding fire
                    department should not waste time moving snow or trying to get out
                    of mud.

      entrance to the building and will be within 100 feet of the fire hydrant. The FDC will
      be located to ensure connected hoses which supply the system will not obstruct
      access to the buildings for other fire apparatus. The final location of FDC will be
      approved by the Fire Marshal.

CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT QUESTIONAIRE
4. The following factors shall be reviewed and considered in determining whether a conditional use
permit application should be approved, approved with conditions, or denied because reasonable
conditions cannot be imposed to achieve compliance with applicable standards:



a. The harmony and compliance of the proposed use with the objectives and requirements of
the city's general plan and this title:

While outdoor storage is generally restricted within the regional commercial zone, we believe the
conditional permit should be approved based on the long-standing use of the secure storage yard,
operational for over 28 years. Our proposal aims to enhance site harmony by reducing the storage
yard's size to accommodate the development of the new Hutch Cove Building on the same lot.



b. The suitability of the specific property for the proposed use:

The conditional use permit for secured outdoor storage is well-suited for the existing office
warehouse, which has been in operation for over 28 years. The success of UDK and future tenants
relies on the availability of a secure outdoor storage yard. The property’s existing infrastructure,
strategic location, and long-standing use make it ideal for this purpose.



c. Whether the proposed use or facility may be injurious to potential or existing development
in the vicinity:

We have assessed the impact of the proposed use on nearby developments and are confident it will
not harm surrounding properties. Our proposal is designed to complement and enhance the area,
preserving the value and integrity of neighboring developments. The reduction of the secured
storage yard, combined with the addition of the new Hutch Cove Building, will improve site
functionality and aesthetics, benefiting the entire community. This plan aligns with the existing RV
and travel trailer storage of Camping World and the self-storage facility across the street.



d. The economic impact of the proposed facility or use on the surrounding area:

We have evaluated the economic impact of potentially removing the secured storage yard. UDK has
indicated that without a secured outdoor storage yard, the building’s value to them is diminished,
and they have expressed concerns about reducing its size. We believe the proposed reduction,
balanced with additional employee parking, is a viable compromise that will support UDK’s
continued success and that of future tenants. This balance also allows for 11 new professional
office-oriented businesses in the Hutch Cove Building. Approving the conditional use of the existing
storage yard will enhance the office warehouse's longevity and value for current and future tenants.
e. The aesthetic impact of the proposed facility or use on the surrounding area:

We have considered the aesthetic impact of the proposed facility and designed it to blend
harmoniously with the existing environment. The reduction of the storage yard and the
incorporation of the new Hutch Cove Building are intended to enhance the site's overall
appearance. The updated layout and landscaping will improve visual appeal, positively contributing
to the neighborhood’s character and making it more attractive for businesses and the community.



f. The safeguards proposed or provided to ensure adequate utilities, transportation access,
drainage, parking, loading space, lighting, screening, landscaping, open space, fire protection,
and pedestrian and vehicular circulation:

We have planned comprehensive safeguards to ensure that all infrastructure and services are
adequately addressed. Our proposal includes provisions for utilities, transportation access,
drainage, parking, loading spaces, and lighting, meeting or exceeding current standards. We have
also incorporated effective screening, landscaping, and open spaces to enhance site functionality
and aesthetics. Fire protection measures have been carefully integrated, and pedestrian and
vehicular circulation has been designed for efficient movement throughout the site. These
safeguards ensure the development is both functional and beneficial to the surrounding area.



g. The safeguards provided or proposed to prevent noxious or offensive emissions such as
noise, glare, dust, pollutants, and odor from the proposed facility or use:

The secured outdoor storage yard and employee parking area will be fully developed with asphalt,
concrete, and landscaping to prevent dust pollution. This area will be used for safely storing
company trucks outside business hours and protecting materials and equipment.



h. The impact of the proposed facility or use on the health, safety, and welfare of the city, the
area, and persons owning or leasing property in the area:

The primary impact of the proposed facility is the replacement of the current use, which has been in
place since the building's construction in 1996. At that time, the facility and storage yard would
have been approved by city staff and met code standards. Our proposal aims to enhance the city's
welfare by creating infill opportunities, such as the Hutch Cove Building. Although the development
will reduce the size of the existing storage yard to add more parking stalls, the remaining storage
area will remain highly valuable to UDK. Enhancing the UDK site will provide Draper with increased
tax revenue and contribute positively to the neighborhood’s character, making it a more attractive
place for businesses and the community.
