CONSENT AGENDA APPROVED:

  Councilor Jones moved to approve the consent agenda which included the approval of the minutes of
the regular council meeting held on 9-15-20, the work session minutes 9-15-20, the work session
minutes 9-22-20, and a list of claims on the warrant register dated 10-6-20 Councilor Ostler seconded
the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

BUSINESS LICENSE APPROVED:

   Councilor Jones reviewed two business license applications for Nephi Handyman LLC, and Sandalfoot
Music & Mobile Recording. The owner of the recording business, Mr. Chris Martinez answered some
questions about the nature of his business. Councilor Jones moved to approve the two business
licenses. Councilor Worwood seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

PUBLIC COMMENT:

   Mr. Alex Johnson addressed the council and expressed his disapproval of Section 9 of the Biglow
Annexation Agreement. Section 9 dealt with requiring Mr. Johnson to clean up his property before
annexation could be approved. Mr. Johnson stated that his property is a construction yard and the
materials on the yard were necessary for conducting his business. Councilor Seely said that he thought
some middle ground could be found between the two parties. Councilor Seely expressed his desire to
have inviting entrances into the city. Councilor Worwood agreed with Councilor Seely but did not want
the requirements to be such a burden as to stunt the annexation. Councilor Ostler thought that the
annexation area in question was going to be Nephi’s growth area and thought it needed to be cleaned
up. Councilor Jones asked Mr. Johnson what changes in the agreement he would be in favor of. Mr.
Johnson said that the fence requirement needed to be eliminated. He asked the council what
constituted a “clean yard.” The council and Mr. Johnson agreed to have City Attorney Kasey Wright,
Public Works Director Kyle Marchant and Mr. Johnson meet and work out some language that both the
city and Mr. Johnson could agree upon.

PROPERTY SOUTH OF THE CITY AND WEST OF THE NEW WATER TANK CHANGED TO A LEVEL 2 ON THE
ANNEXATION AREA MAP:

   City staff had been contacted, through Brent Boswell, by a business owner in Alpine, Utah. The
business owner owns an excavation business and would like to move his operations further south due to
the expansion of residential development in Alpine. His desire is to acquire property near similar
industries and away from residential development. He is currently looking at purchasing the property
located adjacent to the city’s southern water tank. He would also like to prepare to annex this property
in order to receive city services. This property is currently designated as a Level 3 in the city’s
annexation policy plan. He would like to request that this property be moved to a Level 2 in the city’s
annexation area map. This change in Level would prompt the Level 2 analysis found in the city’s
annexation policy plan. This analysis would determine how to supply services to the property as well as
how this property would fit into the overall General Plan.
   In reviewing this request, staff had determined that the business would fit with businesses in the
area. A level change in the annexation area map would allow for a more in-depth analysis of the
property and would assist with a recommendation if another level change is warranted. Councilor Seely
moved to change the area from Level 3 to a Level 2 on the annexation area map. Councilor Worwood
seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

UDOT COOPERATIVE AGREEMENT ADDENDUM #1 APPROVED:

  Public Works Director Kyle Marchant reviewed with the council that in May of 2017, the city entered
an agreement with UDOT to place signs on the 1-15 freeway restricting engine brakes. The city agreed
to purchase the signs and UDOT placed them on the freeway at certain mile markers. Recently UDOT
had received the request to lengthen the restricted section of the freeway. UDOT has relocated the
south sign to expand the length to approximately 3 miles. UDOT would like the city to agree to this sign
relocation and restriction expansion. Councilor Worwood moved to authorize the mayor to sign the
agreement addendum with UDOT. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion passed on a
unanimous vote.

WATER PROJECT CONTRACT AMENDMENT #3 APPROVED:

    Public Works Director Kyle Marchant updated the council that the water project is entering its latter
phase and will be wrapping up soon. As staff had evaluated the project scope, an item had arisen that
needs to be addressed by the Council. The water project originally contained a booster pump station on
the south end to help feed the south water tank when the Worwood Well is not functioning,
intentionally or unintentionally. The booster pump station was removed from the project due to cost
considerations. Now that the project is in the tail end of its construction process, staff would like to add
this booster pump station back into the project for several reasons.
   First, the Worwood Well contains some sand and needs to be pumped to waste for roughly 24 hours
to clean out the sand prior to having the water pumped to the south water tank. This needs to occur
each time the well is shut down. If the well should shut down due to a power outage, lightning strike, or
for servicing, the water would need to be pumped to waste for 24 hours prior to being put into the
tank. Due to system friction losses, the tank would fill to inadequate levels without the Worwood Well
replenishing the supply. A booster pump station would be able to fill the tank from the distribution
system.
    In the summer months, the well will run constantly to keep the tank full and to avoid the pumping to
waste issue. The volume will fluctuate depending on the draw from the tank. During the winter months
when the water from the canyon is more than sufficient to supply the system, it would help the system
if the city had the option to take the Worwood Well offline and supply through the booster pump
station.
   Secondly, there has been interest in new development and with existing businesses in the area
around the south water tank to purchase water from the city. In order to supply water to these
developments, a booster pump station will be needed. Impact fees from these businesses will assist in
covering the costs of this booster pump station.

CITY ATTORNEY KASEY WRIGHT CONFLICT OF INTEREST LETTER AND WAIVER APPROVED:

   City Attorney Kasey Wright presented a Conflict of Interest Disclosure and Consent Letter that
disclosed him representing the Juab School District, Central Valley Medical Center, B. Jackson
Construction, and Rock Canyon Bank. The letter stated that “In the event there is a legal matter that
involves the City and one or more of my current clients, I will assess the City’s interest in the matter in
relation to the other client’s. If I determine that the City and the other client’s interests are either
aligned or neutral to each other, then I will continue to advise both clients on the matter. If the City and
the other client’s interests are adverse, then I will advise the City Administrator of the conflict and
provide neutral advice. In the event that more than neutral legal advice is needed, then I will
recommend the City retain independent counsel on the matter.” Councilor Ostler moved to authorize
the Mayor to sign the letter stating that the council approved Kasey Wright representing the City
consistent with the procedures outlined in the letter. Councilor Worwood seconded the motion. The
motion passed on a unanimous vote.

discuss the character and competency of an individual. Councilor Seely seconded the motion, which
passed on a unanimous vote.
