       The purpose of the Joint Work Session was to review the agendas for the City Council, Redevelopment Agency, and
Municipal Building Authority meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.; discuss the Fiscal Year 2021 budget specific to General
Fund revenues and Business Depot Ogden lease revenues; discuss Board/Council/Trustee business; and hear
Board/Council/Trustee comments.

Agenda Review
         Chair Choberka and members of Council staff briefly reviewed the agendas for the Special City Council, Regular
Redevelopment Agency (RDA) and Special Municipal Building Authority (MBA) meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m. There
was a brief focus on the City Council agenda item relating to adoption of the Fiscal Year (FY) 2021-2025 Consolidated Plan and
FY 2021 Annual Action Plan and the changes that have been made to the documents since publication of the packet materials
responsive to COVID-19.

Fiscal Year 2021 Budget
           General Fund Revenues: Comptroller Stout and Treasurer Johnson used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide
an overview of revenue projections in the Mayor’s proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2021 budget; total general fund revenues are
reduced by $4,030,150. This amount is made of decreases in nearly every General Fund revenue source, including general sales
and use tax revenues; license and permit revenues; fines and forfeitures; events, parks, and recreation revenues; and all other
charges for services. The significant decreases are due in large part the COVID-19 pandemic. The presentation also provided a
comparison of proprietary fund revenues for the current FY and the next FY; revenues for all funds totaled are expected to
decrease by 2.41 percent.
           Council member Nadolski asked if administration has an idea when the City will have access to data illustrating the
actual impact that COVID-19 has had on the economy. Ms. Stout stated sales tax is on a one-month lag; the City is just now
receiving sales tax reports for March 2020, so administration would be hesitant to make any adjustments to revenue projections
until this fall. Chief Administrative Officer Johnson added that he believes the City will not understand the true economic impact
of the pandemic until late fall or winter.
           Business Depot Ogden (BDO) Lease Revenues: Comptroller Stout provided an overview of the proposed BDO lease
revenue appropriations for FY2021. Current ordinance and agreement requirements mandate the decision of $1 million to the
City’s Quality Neighborhoods Initiative, $200,000 to the City’s HELP Loan program; and $250,000 for Central Business District
(CBD) mall district debt service. She added that $200,000 is allocated for arts programming, including arts grants, public arts
projects, and public arts maintenance. Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) appropriations total $1,422,650, with the remainder of
BDO lease revenue appropriated to non-CIP projects and/or expenditures. The total use of fund balance/BDO lease revenue is
$4,564,425.
         There was a brief discussion among the Council and staff that centered on opportunities available to the Council and
Mayor for consideration with regard to additional adjustments to the budget responsive to the COVID-19 pandemic. Council
members expressed their gratitude to the City’s financial staff for working hard to adjust the budget as information becomes
available regarding fluctuations in revenue sources.

Board/Council/Trustee Business
          Council member Stephens stated he continues to hear feedback from constituents regarding the recent development
trends that have led to an increase in the number of apartment units that are being constructed in the community. He is concerned
that the City is being inundated with apartments. Planning Manager Montgomery referenced an informational item provided to the
Council several weeks ago regarding construction of apartment projects throughout the City; his concern relates to the fact that
most of the apartment development is occurring in commercial zones of the City. This is related to a lack of standards in
commercial zones that leads to easier development. He stated a temporary ordinance has been enacted that would halt construction
of apartments in commercial zones for a six-month period to give the City time to determine the appropriate design standards for
that type of development in commercial zones. The Council will have the opportunity to consider the revisions to those standards
in the coming months. This led to a discussion regarding tax revenue implications of allowing residential development in
commercial areas; commercial is taxed at 100 percent of their property valuation, whereas residential uses are taxed at 55 percent
of their valuation. Additionally, the City loses the opportunity to generate sales tax revenue when commercial property is
consumed by residential uses.

         The meeting adjourned at 5:08 p.m.
