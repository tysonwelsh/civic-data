                                 PLANNING COMMISSION MINUTES
                                          Meeting of January 23, 2020
                            Logan City Council Chambers (290 N 100 W Logan, UT 84321) www.loganutah.org
 Minutes of the meeting for the Logan City Planning Commission convened in regular session on
    Thursday, January 23, 2020. Chairman Nielson called the meeting to order at 5:30 p.m.

Commissioners Present: Roylan Croshaw, Regina Dickinson, Sandi Goodlander, Jess Lucero,
Dave Newman, Tony Nielson, Eduardo Ortiz

Staff Present: Mike DeSimone, Russ Holley, Aaron Smith, Kymber Housley, Paul Taylor, Bill
Young, Tonya Rice, Jess Bradfield (Council liaison)

Minutes from the January 9, 2019 meeting were reviewed. Commissioner Newman moved to
approve the minutes as submitted. Motion seconded by Commissioner Goodlander. Approved
unanimously.

PUBLIC HEARING

 PC 20-004 Rosine Daycare [Conditional Use Permit] Mistafa Karamoko, authorized
 agent/owner, request a permit for an in-home daycare for up to 16 children located at 443
 Oakwood Drive in the Neighborhood Residential (NR-4) zone; TIN 02-152-0060.

STAFF: Mr. Smith reviewed the request for a daycare with a maximum of 16 children. The Land
Development Code (LDC) allows daycare with a maximum of 16 clients in the Neighborhood
Residential (NR-4) zoning district as a Conditional Use. Affoue Rosine Nguessan currently
operates a daycare, with up to 8 children, at the location, which operates from 5:00 am to 5:00 pm
with limited weekend use. As a daycare with 1-8 children is a permitted use, the Commission is
specifically reviewing the impact of a daycare use in a residence with up to 8 additional children.
The concerns with daycare use in a residence are typically parking, access, noise, and safety.

PROPONENT: None

PUBLIC: None

COMMISSION: Mr. Smith clarified for Commissioner Croshaw that there can be no more than two
(2) clients at a time for drop-off prior to 7:00 a.m.

MOTION: Commissioner Ortiz moved to conditionally approve a Conditional Use Permit as
outlined in PC 20-004 with the conditions and findings for approval as listed below. Commissioner
Newman seconded the motion.

CONDITIONS OF APPROVAL
1. All standard conditions of approval are recorded and available in the Community Development
   Department.
2. No outside activities permitted earlier than 7:00 am.
3. One dedicated off-street parking stall within the driveway shall be provided during business
   hours for employee parking.
4. Drop-off and pick-up times shall be scheduled and staggered so that no more than three (3)
   client vehicles are at the home at any one time.


5. A written parking policy shall be submitted to Logan City that limits scheduled drop-off and pick-
   up to no more than three (3) client vehicles at time, and no more than two (2) client vehicles at
   a time prior to 7:00 am. The policy should also specify preferred parking area adjacent to the
   home, and state that sidewalks, corners, and driveways shall not be blocked.
6. The proponent shall comply with all State and local regulations and licenses regarding in-home
   daycare facilities for up to 16 clients.
7. Prior to issuance of business license, the Director of Community Development shall receive a
   written memorandum from each of the following departments or agencies indicating that their
   requirements have been satisfied:
     a. Fire
          i. Inspection required prior to opening for 16 children.
     b. Engineering
          i. No on-street parking shall be allowed for drop-off or pick-up along Oakwood Drive, or
              within 20’ of the ADA radius curve start on Three Point Ave. Parking will not be
              allowed along the ADA corner.
     c. Water/Cross Connection
          i. A hazard assessment should be done at this property to look for the possibility of a
              cross connection to ensure the safety of the water for the homeowner, the children,
              and the City’s water supply.

FINDINGS FOR APPROVAL
1. This daycare is compatible with surrounding land uses and zoning designations and, as
   conditioned, will not interfere with the use and enjoyment of adjoining properties.
2. As proposed, the streets providing access to the subject property have adequate capacity for
   the proposed use and parking/drop-off/pick-up management will be provided and regulated.
3. Other infrastructure to the property has adequate capacity, or suitable levels of service, for the
   proposed use.
4. The project conforms to the requirements of Title 17 of the Logan Municipal Code as an
   identified conditional use.
5. The project was noticed as required by State and City regulations and proper procedure was
   followed in processing this request.

Moved: E. Ortiz Seconded: D. Newman Approved: 7-0
Yea: Croshaw, Dickinson, Goodlander, Lucero, Newman, Nielson, Ortiz Nay:             Abstain:

 PC 20-005 L59 (Redesign) [Design Review Permit] Beth Larchar/Trent Cragun, authorized
 agent/owner, request a redesign which is nearly the same as the previously-approved
 application, but the apartment building will be reduced by one (1) story and two units. This will
 be a 4-story building with 108 units. The ten (10) townhomes on 100 East have not changed.
 The project is located on 2.89 acres at 159 South Main in the Town Center (TC-2) zone; TIN 02-
 047-0021;-0023;-0005;-0017;-0019;0020.

STAFF: Mr. Holley reviewed the redesign of the previously-approved L59 residential project. The
ten (10) townhomes along 100 East are unchanged. The main apartment building is proposed to
eliminate a floor, to 4-stories, and reduce the number of units from 120 to 108. The 2,500 SF of
commercial space near the west entrance is proposed to be replaced with on-site amenities such
as a fitness room and leasing office. The 10 townhomes, two buildings, each containing 5 units,
are oriented towards and align along 100 East. The two-story townhome structures have rear-
loading, double-wide, garages and usable rooftop patios. The remainder of the project site is
proposed as surface parking lots and landscaping.


The Land Development Code (LDC) 17.10.060 requires 1.5 parking stalls per every studio or one-
bedroom dwelling unit and 2.0 parking stalls per every two-bedroom or larger dwelling unit within
the TC-1 zone. The TC-1 zone also requires one visitor parking stall per every 10 stalls and bike
racks at a ratio of one slot per 0.5 bedrooms. The proposed apartment building contains 78 one-
bedroom/studio dwelling units and 30 two-bedroom dwelling units for a parking requirement of 177
stalls. For 177 stalls, 17 visitor stalls would be required for a grand total of 194. The proposed
project provides 153 parking stalls on-site, leaving a shortfall of 24 parking stalls (14% below the
Code requirement). The Code does allow for up to a 25% reduction of parking. An alternative
parking plan has not been submitted.

PROPONENT: Beth Larchar stated that the owners of the surrounding lots (except for Chuck-A-
Rama) are participating in the project and are on board with the boundary line adjustment.
Commissioner Lucero asked about the 20 additional spaces. Ms. Larchar said Chuck-A-Rama is
subject to the shared parking requirements as outlined in the CCR’s. Commissioner Croshaw
asked if the current parking requirement could be met. Ms. Larchar said it cannot and that is why
they are asking for a 15% parking variance. She confirmed for Commissioner Goodlander that
there are two (2) entrances on the north side, however, that may depend on how the leasing office
is configured. She outlined possible plans for connectivity. Commissioner Newman asked if each
apartment would have designated parking. Ms. Larchar said that can be done.

PUBLIC: Gayle Yost lives one block south of the proposed project and is concerned that the gap
between the townhomes and the parking lot is not a safe place for families. If the parking lot will be
full, how will cars pull out of the garages safely. There is a problem with the way the parking lot is
structured. To the south of the complex is a church, if there is not enough parking, tenants may try
to use the church parking lot. She is concerned that this will create parking problems, like in the
Adams neighborhood. She has a difficult time getting out of her driveway because of the traffic.
She is concerned about the price of the apartments and suggested making more units that could
house more roommates to share the rent.

Jess Bradfield said the Council is excited about this development and has had a very lengthy
discussion about the parking. The CCR’s needing to be amended may be too vague with relation
to the parking concern.

Randy Penrod said there is quite a bit of traffic in the area and he is especially concerned with
pedestrians.

Gary Saxton, representing the Logan Downtown Alliance Design Committee, encouraged the
Commission to use its discretion and consider LDC 17.31.40.G “The number of off-street parking
spaces specified in this Chapter shall not be exceeded, nor reduced by more than twenty-five
percent (25%) of the minimum parking requirements where authorized”. Paragraph G provides a
workable solution for the L59 Redesign and allows the Commission to grant, at its discretion, an
allowance of up to a 25% reduction in off street parking spaces. He encouraged the Commission
to not make a decision based on emotional talking points. He would like to see the Downtown
Specific Plan be executed and there is a need for this type of housing downtown. Much of the
traffic on 100 East is commuter traffic. This project that will be good for the City.

LouAnn Sakaki lives two blocks south of this proposed project. She was hit by a car on 100 East
last March. This type of project may be needed; however, it seems too big for the area.

COMMISSION: Mr. Holley confirmed for Commissioner Goodlander that they will move the
property line out toward the parking lot (common owners); the boundary line adjustment will require
signatures from all the owners. The adjustment has not been finalized yet, but will be reviewed and
approved by the City Engineer.

Mr. Holley clarified for Commissioner Dickinson that TC-1, if directly on Main Street, requires
ground floor commercial. Since the building does not front Main Street, commercial is not required.

Commissioner Newman asked if the potential 20 off-site shared stalls available are reserved for
this development. Mr. Holley said none of the stalls have been signed or specifically designated.
The visitor stalls could be satisfied with these available stalls.

Mr. Holley confirmed for Commissioner Lucero that the Code does not specify overnight visitor
parking.

Mr. Holley confirmed for Commissioner Ortiz that amenities should not require additional parking
since they are for the tenants, who have parking, however, there may be a need for parking in
relation to the leasing office. The Code does not address this specifically.

Chairman Nielson questioned the interior parking lot landscaping requirement. Mr. Holley said the
islands along the south and to the north meet the 18 SF of landscaping (as conditioned in 12b).

Mr. Housley clarified that visitor parking is not limited to only 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m.; visitors can
park during the evening hours as well (no overnight). The parking to the northwest is private
parking.

Chairman Nielson said the Commission cannot, and should not, redesign the project. He said
there should be adequate connectivity throughout the project. Traffic and safety are concerns;
however, he feels the biggest concern is parking.

Commissioner Dickinson asked if the east side will be a right-turn only. Mr. Holley said it is
planned to be a full movement intersection. Commissioner Dickinson asked if there was any way
to make it a right-turn only exit. Mr. Young, the City Engineer, said there are no restrictions being
put in place, however, that can be changed if determined necessary. A consultant will be hired to
consider the crosswalk at the bottom of the hill and to determine whether to put in some type of
traffic control device for pedestrians to cross at Pioneer Street. A traffic signal will be put in this
summer at 300 South 100 East that will hopefully create gaps along 100 East to create better
access for vehicles getting in and out. Commissioner Dickinson questioned whether the additional
traffic accessing Main Street will create problems. Mr. Young said that is an existing access, if a
problem arises, UDOT will review it.

Commissioner Croshaw believes in this development, parking may dictate who lives there, the
project is needed in downtown and he thinks the Commission would be wise in allowing some
leniency regarding parking. Commissioner Dickinson disagreed with any leniency on parking.

Mr. DeSimone said if there is a shared parking agreement with the local business owners, that
agreement will be required to be recorded. It would be better if an alternative parking plan,
indicating how parking will be managed, had been submitted by the applicant.

Commissioner Newman was never in favor of this project, however, at this point, he is more
favorable than he has ever been. The only concern is parking. The hope for how the future may be,
cannot override the need for the present. Allowing a parking reduction would be a disservice to
everyone involved.

Commissioner Lucero supports the vision of this type of housing downtown and is comfortable with
a parking reduction if the CCR’s can be amended to include visitor parking. She would like to see
more specificity in an alternative parking plan.


Commissioner Goodlander has a problem with projects that come in with ideas about what might
happen. To feel comfortable approving a project like this, she would like to see the boundary
adjustment completed, the CCR amendment, and the connectivity design. She does not agree to
a reduction of parking.

Commissioner Ortiz said although this is an important project for downtown, parking is a very
important element. The Code requirement should be followed.

Mr. Holley asked if the Commission would make a judgment call about visitor parking; the Code
does not specify that it must be 24/7 so there is some ambiguity. If visitor parking should not be
counted with the shared parking stalls that are available only during the day, then the need for
parking could change.

Chairman Nielson said some visitor parking should be overnight. Commissioner Dickinson agreed
that millennials often stay the night at friends’ homes. Mr. DeSimone noted that the benefit of a
shared parking agreement with different types of businesses allows for different hours of need.
Commissioner Goodlander would like to see the CCR’s designate which 20 spots could be utilized.
Mr. DeSimone said that could be included in an alternative parking plan.

Commissioner Ortiz asked how the introduction of any type of commercial element would affect
parking. Mr. Holley said it would be handled at the application of a business license, however, the
way this project is being constructed would make that quite difficult.

Commissioner Lucero would like to see the CCR amendment and a specific parking plan.
Commissioner Goodlander agreed, she would like to see a complete plan before any approval.

MOTION: Commissioner Goodlander moved to continue discussion of PC 20-005 to the February
13, 2020 meeting with the parameters that the applicant submit an alternative parking plan that
meets the requirements of the Code, the amended CCR’s, and a design plan for connectivity.
Commissioner Newman seconded the motion.

Moved: S. Goodlander Seconded: D. Newman Approved: 7-0
Yea: Croshaw, Dickinson, Goodlander, Lucero, Newman, Nielson, Ortiz Nay:            Abstain:

 PC 20-006 USU Credit Union [Design Review & Conditional Use Permit] Anderson Wahlen &
 Associates/Goldenwest Federal Credit Union, authorized agent/owner, request a single-story
 2,942 SF commercial building for a financial institution on 1.01 acres located at 220 East 1400
 North in the Commercial (COM) zone; TIN 05-014-0068.

STAFF: Mr. Smith reviewed the request to construct a 2,942 SF credit union building on a 1.01-
acre (44,000 SF) parcel. The development will include the one-story building, drive-through lanes,
surface parking, and landscaping. The development connects to 1400 North via a shared access
between the credit union and Culver’s to the west. The proposal includes a Conditional Use Permit
request to allow front yard parking in the Commercial (COM) zone.

PROPONENT: David Butterfield said they are asking for an allowance of 15 parking stalls in front
of the building since 30 stalls are necessary. The unique circumstance is based on the narrow and
deep 1-acre lot, which works for the use of the business, however, if only 6 spots are allowed in the
front, the remainder of the parking would have to be in the back, which would mean that the drive-
thru would have to be moved to the front. This would not provide a good presentation and would
further congest the area due to the design. Landscaping will help mitigate the front parking and
keep with the integrity of the property. They are planning on deeding the sidewalk to the City.


PUBLIC: None

COMMISSION: Mr. Smith clarified for Commissioner Lucero that only 50% of the required parking
can be placed in front of the building; which would allow 6 parking stalls to be permitted in front of
the building for this project. The proposal includes 15 parking stalls in front of the building. To
approve more than 6 stalls in front of the building, the Commission would need to find that excess
parking is required due to unique circumstances. The findings are required to demonstrate that the
excess parking demand is ongoing and not based on peak day, holiday, or seasonal events.

Commissioner Dickinson asked why so many parking spaces are needed. Mr. Butterfield
explained that this is a branch that will grow significantly. The current branch inside Lee’s will close
and move to this location and the branch at 1000 North 700 East may become more limited. He
said there will also likely be some overflow parking from other businesses.

Commissioner Newman asked how many employees are anticipated. Mr. Butterfield said there
may be 4-7 at any given time.

Commissioner Croshaw likes the raised berm and landscaping and sees no problem with the front
parking. Commissioner Goodlander agreed and appreciates the layout.

MOTION: Commissioner Goodlander moved to conditionally approve a Design Review &
Conditional Use Permit as presented and outlined in PC 20-006 with the front yard parking as
proposed and the conditions and findings for approval as listed below. Commissioner Lucero
seconded the motion.

CONDITIONS OF APPROVAL
1. All standard conditions of approval are recorded and available in the Community Development
    Department.
2. 49% building frontage at the front setback is approved by the Planning Commission.
3. A minimum of 12 parking stalls is required on site.
4. A bike rack that accommodates a minimum of two (2) bikes shall be installed.
5. The width of the shared driveway access onto 1400 North shall be narrowed to 16’ for the
    ingress lane and 12’ for the egress lane and designed with a physical barrier to prevent left
    hand turns. The access shall be signed right turn only.
6. A minimum 4’ wide contiguous pathway shall be provided along the west side of the building
    that is adjacent to all parking along the side and provided access to the rear parking.
7. A performance landscaping plan, prepared in accordance with LDC §17.32, shall be submitted
    for approval to the Community Development Department prior to the issuance of the building
    permit. The plan shall include the following:
      a. A minimum of 4,400 SF of both open and usable outdoor space, for a total of 8,800 SF,
           shall be provided.
      b. A total of 20 trees and 50 shrubs, flowers and ornamental plants shall be provided.
      c. Minimum of two (2) tree species is required.
      d. A minimum of 25% of plant material shall be evergreen trees and shrubs.
      e. Street trees shall be provided every thirty (30) feet on center and outside of site-distance
           triangles near intersections or driveway access points or otherwise determined by the City
           Forrester.
8. Exterior lighting shall be concealed source, down-cast and reviewed and approved prior to the
    issuance of a building permit and shall comply with current LDC regulations.
9. Rooftop mechanical and/or building wall mechanical equipment shall be placed out of view
    from the street or screen from view from the street.
10. No signs are approved with this permit. All signage shall be approved and permitted by staff in
    accordance with the LDC.

11. No fences are approved with this permit. All fences shall be approved and permitted by staff in
    accordance with the LDC.
12. Prior to issuance of a building permit, the Director of Community Development shall receive a
    written memorandum from each of the following departments or agencies indicating that their
    requirements have been satisfied:
     a. Engineering
           ii. Provide the City with water shares or in-lieu of fee for increased demand on existing
               City water rights
          iii. Provide on-site retention of the 90% storm utilizing Low Impact Design practices.
               Provide storm water flood control detention/retention per Logan City Storm Water
               Design Standards.
          iv. Currently sidewalk along the south side of 1400 North is on Goldenwest property, may
               want to consider deeding this sidewalk to Logan City.
           v. Provide CC&R’s for subdivision shared/common accesses, utilities and easements.
          vi. Shared access to 1400 North shall be designed and restricted to a right-in and right
               out only access.
     b. Water/Cross Connection
            i. The building’s water main needs to have its own RP (ASSE1013) installed and tested
               on the water main as it enters the building before any branch offs or connections. This
               is for containment protection only (City’s protection only). Points of use protection will
               also be needed (occupant’s protection).
           ii. All landscape irrigation systems fed from Logan City water must have a high-hazard
               backflow assembly installed/tested. No dual feed systems allowed without City prior
               approval and installation criteria (RP & swing joint) are met and inspection is passed.
               All backflow assemblies must be tested within 10 days of turning on water to them
               and annually thereafter.
          iii. Fire suppression systems connected to Logan City water must have a minimum DC
               (ASSE1015) installed and tested. Fire risers and B/F assemblies must be installed as
               per Logan City standards.
          iv. All points water use must comply with the 2018 IPC and State of Utah Amendments
               during and after construction.

FINDINGS FOR APPROVAL
1. Project is compatible with surrounding land uses and will not interfere with the use and
   enjoyment of adjacent properties due to building design, materials, landscaping, and setbacks.
2. Front yard parking at the proposed site is compatible with adjoining properties, is consistent
   with surrounding land use and development patterns, provides enhanced pedestrian
   functionality and walkability in relationship to the adjoining street, will not compromise future
   projects or design, and included substantial landscaping adjacent to the parking area.
3. As conditioned, the project conforms to the requirements of Logan Municipal Code Title 17.
4. The project provides adequate open and useable outdoor space in conformance with Title 17.
5. As conditioned, the project provides adequate off-street parking.
6. 1400 North provides access and is adequate in size and design to sufficiently handle
   automobile traffic related to the land use.
7. The project meets the goals and objectives of the COM designation within the Logan General
   Plan by providing services near high-capacity roadways and is designed for easy circulation of
   both pedestrian and vehicles.
8. The project met the minimum public noticing requirements of the LDC and the Municipal Code.

Moved: S. Goodlander Seconded: J. Lucero Approved: 7-0
Yea: Croshaw, Dickinson, Goodlander, Lucero, Newman, Nielson, Ortiz Nay:               Abstain:


 PC 20-003 Future Land Use Plan (FLUP) Amendment [Code Amendment] Logan City
 proposes amendments to the Logan City Future Land Use Plan. Some of the proposed
 changes are based on long-range planning and others are because of City jurisdictional
 boundary changes, street alignment changes and public ownership changes.

STAFF: Mr. Holley reviewed the request for a Map Amendment to the current Future Land Use
Plan (FLUP) for several areas in and around Logan City. Some of the proposed changes anticipate
future residential, commercial and industrial growth areas that are currently located in
unincorporated areas outside the perimeter of the City, and other changes represent boundary
changes with North Logan City. This proposal also amends areas that have recently been rezoned
to conflicting zoning districts and changes the designation on properties that have been purchased
by Utah State University near campus.

An overview of the key was discussed. DR – Detached Residential (single-family housing); DX
suffix is an overlay that suggests possible critical lands (wetlands, slopes, etc.); MR – Mixed
Residential; CR – Campus Residential; MUC – Mixed-Use Center; NC – Neighborhood Center; TC
– Town Center; Com – Commercial; CS - Commercial Service; IP – Industrial Park; APP – Airport;
GW – Gateway; RCA – Resource Conservation; Rec – Recreation, PUB – Public; RRA – Rural
Reserve (sensitive, low-density, agricultural areas).

MOTION: Commissioner Goodlander moved to continue PC 20-003 to the February 13, 2020
meeting. Commissioner Dickinson seconded the motion.

Moved: S. Goodlander Seconded: R. Dickinson Approved: 7-0
Yea: Croshaw, Dickinson, Goodlander, Lucero, Newman, Nielson, Ortiz Nay:          Abstain:
