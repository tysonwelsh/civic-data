Washington County Planning Commission Meeting

The Washington County Land Use Authority Meeting was held on Tuesday, December 10, 2019 in the
Council Chambers of the Washington County Administration Building at 197 E. Tabernacle, St. George, UT.
Chairman Slade Hughes opened the meeting at 1:30 p.m. He led the Pledge of Allegiance and then explained
meeting protocol and how the public hearing would be conducted.

Commissioners present: Brandon Anderson (excused for a portion of the meeting), Dan Briggs (Alternate
acting as a voting member), Kevin Jones, Mark Owens, Reed Scow (Alternate acting as a voting member),
Doug Solstad, and Jon Walter.

Excused: Shayne Durrant.

Staff present: Scott Messel, Community Development Director; Natalie Nelson, Deputy County Attorney;
Brian Graff, Deputy Attorney; Doreen Bowers-Irons, Administrative Assistant; Todd Edwards, County
Engineer; Gil Almquist, County Commissioner.

4.  DAMMERON VALLEY CONCEPT MASTER PLAN REVIEW. Review of the proposed
Dammeron Valley Concept Master Plan. The applicants are Dammeron Valley Corp, and Brooks and
Andrew Pace.

Chairman Hughes explained that this item is for the planning commission to review and make
recommendations to the plan. It is not a public hearing however, one will be scheduled in the future. Mr.
Messel confirmed that to be correct.

Mr. Messel provided the submitted proposed development maps of the Dammeron Valley area. He reported
that the remaining vacant SITLA parcels of land in Dammeron Valley are planned for future development.
They will go through the PD (Planned Development) review and the master plan approval process. Several
months ago, the Planning Commission held a public hearing on the proposed master planned development.
The residents raised several concerns in the lengthy public hearing. The concept plan for Dammeron Valley
is before the planning commission at this time for review and comment. A public hearing is not required at
this step on the Master Planned PD (Planned Development) process but one will take place in the future.

Mr. Messel reviewed the submitted documents of the plan and explained the requirements the county would
like to see. Currently, they are calling out the roads as open space, which he did not believe should be
considered open space. The applicants will need to address that issue. He noted there were several emails
and letters of concerns submitted by residents of the area and were given to the commissioners.
Commissioner Solstad commented it appeared those documents had been received within the last 24 hours

Recommendation:

Staff recommended that the Planning Commission review and provide feedback on the Concept Plan. The
developer can then take those comments into consideration when preparing the development agreement,
project plan, and zone change.

Andrew Pace from Dammeron Corporation addressed the commission to answer their questions. He pointed
out the number of homes proposed and the updates they plan for the open space areas.

The commissioners had several questions and requested information on the water issue, fire flow,
sewer/septic system concerns, landscaping, open space, trails, flooding, and various other information. Mr.
Pace provided answers to their concerns and questions.

Todd Edwards, County Engineer updated the commission regarding funding from the National Resources
Conservation Service (NRCS) the entity who funded all of the erosion control on rivers after they flooded
years ago. The county applied for and received a grant from them for a watershed for the Dammeron and
Diamond Valleys and the Shivwits areas. That funding was approved at 100 percent. There will need to be
assessment studies and designs that will take up to a minimum of 18 months before any work can start. If
the applicant wants to start before the county is able to do the work, they will need to address the flooding
concerns on their own. The commission and Mr. Edwards discussed the water flows and flooding issues of
the Dammeron Valley area.

Commissioner Jones adamantly stated that he was against taxpayer funding for problems developed by a
developer. If problems do arise from the development, then the developer should be the one who pays for
the costs, not him and not the taxpayers.

A lengthy discussion regarding the project between staff, the commission, and the applicant transpired. The
commission provided direction to the applicant that needed to be addressed to meet the standards. They also
requested more detail of the plan. Mr. Pace indicated they would work on the direction and standards
provided.



5.  PUBLIC HEARING AFFORDABLE HOUSING. Hold a public hearing and review the Affordable
Housing Element to the Washington County General Plan.

Mr. Messel reported on the Washington County Affordable Housing Element to the Washington County
General Plan. He stated there is a need for affordable housing throughout the State. Affordable housing is
not being addressed in the communities. The state legislature have provided guidance to address the housing
needs. An amendment to the General Plan is needed to meet the State’s direction. We are now proposing
some amendments to the General Plan. However, a public hearing is required.

Chairman Hughes asked what attainable housing was. Commission Walter explained that the concept is
based on the ratios to wages and housing, which is one third. With the housing market right now and the
costs of the homes, it is hard for people to afford homes either renting or buying them.

Mr. Messel pointed out that 80% of the residents of Washington County are at or below the median income.
He reported they are trying to find solutions to address affordable housing.

Commissioner Walter pointed out that part of the problems are the high impact fees being charged.

The commission and staff discussed the affordable housing issues and the documents that were provided.
Commissioner Walter stated he would like to see in the plan areas called out for affordable housing with
amenities that are close to the areas.

Nate Wiberg, 5 County Association of Government reported on what they have been doing to address
affordable housing indicating that he worked with Washington City on an affordable housing analysis. He
provided information on the criteria used to identify areas locations affordable housing could be developed.

Chairman Hughes opened the floor to the public hearing.

John Leavitt of Veyo stated Chairman Hughes asked what is affordable housing. He wanted a definition of
that. What is the housing part of that? Mr. Messel answered he would provide Mr. Leavitt a copy of the
plan.

Mr. Leavitt then asked if there was a definition of what affordable housing is. Mr. Messel answered yes
there is. Mr. Leavitt then asked if it was an R.V. Mr. Messel answered, it is a bunch of different things. Mr.
Leavitt stated he just was asking the question. Hypothetically, if it is a R.V. and a developer brings a piece of
property in, gets it through one part but the other part shuts it down, how do you handle that?

Mr. Messel stated he, Mr. Wiberg, and Shirlayne Quayle of St. George City are spearheading a group called
H.A.C. It is a part of the intergenerational poverty committee and they are looking at solutions. Education
and celebration are part of the solution.

David Miller, St. George stated affordable housing is generally subsidized by the government. You can have
affordable housing areas but transportation is a huge issue when they need to get to home, work, or medical
appointments and have no transportation to get there.

Mr. Wiberg explained there are several definitions of what attainable housing is. As far as the general plan
and the document before the commission, it equates to not spending more than one third of the annual salary
on housing plus utilities. That number would apply to everyone.

Commissioner Walter spoke of the annual salary numbers that would need to be made in order to purchase a
$270,000 home, which would require approximately $60,000 to make a $1,300 monthly payment. That is
what rents are currently starting at.

David Buckingham stated he lived in Washington and supported the continued education of this idea. He is
in Human Resources of Washington County and the people who work for the county do not make anything
near $60,000 as an individual.

6.   PUBLIC HEARING ZONE ORDINANCE AMENDMENTS. Hold a public hearing and review
updates to County Ordinances: Title 10, Chapter 1-14: Changes and Amendments and Title 10, Chapter 2-
3: Public Hearings.

Mr. Messel reported often times outside sources interpret the county code differently than the County’s
intent. So, we are constantly making changes for clarification. He turned the floor over to Ms. Nelson.

Ms. Nelson reported on the proposed changes to the code. She pointed out that currently, the code indicates
for any amendments to Title 10, a public hearing must be held, not only at the planning commission meeting
but also during the county commission meeting. As a practice, we have only held the public hearing during
the planning commission meetings who would then make a recommendation to the county commission. In
addition, the County Code currently requires a 14-day notification of public hearings. However, State Code
only requires 10 days. We are clarifying the code to match that of the State and for the intent of public hearings.
In addition, should and item be tabled for any reason, the county commission has the authority to pull the
item from the planning commission and approve it.

The Commission and Ms. Nelson reviewed and discussed the current county and state codes and the
suggested changes.

Chairman Hughes opened the floor to the public hearing.

John Leavitt cautioned the commission of giving three people more power than this body. It was pointed
out that they already have it. He asked who would define the tabling of a project or item before the county
