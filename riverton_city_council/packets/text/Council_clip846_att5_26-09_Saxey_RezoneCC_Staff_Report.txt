    To:            Honorable Mayor and City Council
    From:          Planning Department
    Date:          May 19, 2026
    Subject:       Ordinance 26-09, “Saxey Rezone” An application to rezone approximately
                   2.82- acres located at 12317, 12325 and 12341 South Redwood Road,
                   currently zoned R-4 and RM-6-SD to RM-14-D, and to amend the General
                   Plan from Medium-High Density Residential to High-Density Residential.
                   Applicant- Eric Saxey


    On April 23, 2026, the Planning Commission held a public hearing to review this item. Minutes
    from that meeting are included below. The Planning Commission voted to recommend
    approval, but with the recommended zone at R-6 rather than RM-14-D as requested. The
    Commission recommended the following motion:

    I move the City Council ADOPT Ordinance 26-09, rezoning 2.82-acres located at 12317,
    12325 and 12341 South Redwood Road from R-4 and RM-6-SD to RM-6, as shown in
    Exhibit “A”.

    Background

   This item is a public hearing for a Rezone of three adjoining parcels located on the east side of
   Redwood Road at 12317, 12325 and 12341 South. The parcels are narrow and deep with
   dimensions of about 80-ft wide and 480-ft deep. Each parcel has a small house located near the
   front of the lot.

   Current Zoning
   The Current Zoning for the two (2) northern parcels is RM-6-SD, which was established by City
   Ordinance 22-04 in January 2022. The RM- 6 Zone only allows single family detached housing on
   lots of at least 5,000-sf and density not to exceed 6 units per acre. The “SD” added requirements:
1- No more than 4 lots adjacent to the north boundary, and lots must align as closely as possible to the
   adjacent existing lots to the north. And
2- A masonry fence will be installed along the south property line prior to issuance of any building
   permits on the property. (See attachments for Ordinance 22-04)

   The third parcel in this rezone request is currently Zoned RR-22 which is a single-family residential
   zone with ½-acre lots and animal rights. The other parcels similarly situated on the east side of
   redwood road are also zoned RR-22.

   Zoning in the area is a mix of residential zones with some Commercial Downtown zoning nearby. To
   the east is R-3, to the north is R-4, across Redwood to the west is also R-4.
General Plan
The General Plan for properties on the east side of Redwood Road from about 12310 South to about
12530 South (north of the church) designates all these properties as Medium-High Density
Residential (5-8 Dwelling units per acre) and includes a Historic Preservation designation. The
Medium-High Density designation suggests the properties could be zoned to RM-6 or RM-8 zones.
Both zones allow single family detached housing on small lots, the RM-8 Zone also allows attached
housing such as townhomes or condominiums. The General Plan designation for Historic
Preservation was put in place in 2020 with an idea to consider the existing small homes facing
Redwood, but without specific requirements.

Requested General Plan and Zoning
The applicant is requesting the three subject parcels be rezoned to RM-14-D, which requires the
General Plan be amended to High Density Residential. The RM-14-D zone is a multifamily zone that
allows a variety of housing types such as small lot detached, townhomes, and condos with a
maximum density of 14-units per acre.

Applicants Concept Plan
The Applicant included a concept plan with his application. The concept plan is useful to see how a
project might be laid out using the zoning the applicant is requesting. However, it is not a required
part of a rezone application, and is not being recommended by staff, nor can it be approved as part of
this action. If a rezone was ultimately approved by the City Council, the applicant could apply for a
site plan that looks different from this concept plan.




Public Notices and Response
Notices were sent to all property owners within 1,000 feet of this site and posted on the City’s
webpage. Staff received several public comments related to this rezone application. Comments
made up to the time this report was published are included below.

The Planning Commission voted to recommend approval of the rezone, but with the zoning at
RM-6, rather than the RM-14-D as requested by the applicant.

Comment 3 – Saxey Rezone - MacKay


Dear Planning Commission,

I am writing to express my strong opposition to the proposed rezoning of properties 12317, 12325,
and 12341 from RR-22 to RM-14-D. As a resident directly impacted by this proposal, I am concerned
that moving from a low-density residential designation to a high density multi-unit classification will
alter the safety and character of our neighborhood.

My primary concerns regarding this proposed change include:
1. Neighborhood Character and Scale
The leap from RR-22 to RM-14-D is a drastic shift. The introduction of large multi-unit buildings would
be visually jarring and out of scale with our existing homes. To maintain the livability of this area, any
zoning changes should be gradual rather than a sudden spike in density that disregards the current
neighborhood aesthetics.
2. Infrastructure and Public Safety
Our current infrastructure, specifically water, sewer, and storm drainage, was engineered for the low-
density RM-6 or RR-22 standards. Doubling this capacity would place an immense strain on these
systems. Furthermore, I am concerned about:

   •   Emergency Access: Increased density brings higher call volumes for EMS and fire services.
       The narrow streets or limited access points may make it hard for firetrucks and other
       emergency vehicles to reach residents quickly, especially if there is not enough parking
       causing people to park on the streets.
   •   Traffic and Road Safety: The large increase of vehicle trips and turning movements will affect
       the already busy roads and intersections nearby.

3. Environmental and Quality of Life Impacts

   •   Stormwater Runoff: Replacing natural ground cover with the pavement and roofing required for
       RM-14-D will lead to increased impervious surfaces, heightening the risk of local flooding and
       erosion.
   •   Noise and Light: The shift to a high-density environment will naturally increase nighttime traffic
       and ambient noise, stripping the neighborhood of it's quiet residential nature.

4. Planning Precendent
Approving this rezoning sets a concerning precedent for future "spot zoning" in our area. It
undermines the city's long-term master plan and the land-use expectations that many of us relied
upon when choosing to invest in our homes here.
I urge the commission to consider the long-term strain on our schools, roads and utilities. Please
maintain the current RR-22 zoning to protect the integrity of our community.
Sincerely,
Robin Mackay, 1627 W Riverton Chase Dr Riverton, Utah 84065

To the Riverton City Council and Planning Commission,

I am a resident of the area impacted by the proposed rezone to allow 8–14 housing units on
the parcel addressed in the current public notice. I am writing to formally oppose the
density requested.

Let me be clear: I am not against developers providing additional housing in Riverton.
Growth is part of our community’s future, and I support responsible development that
meets housing needs. However, the proposed 8–14 units on this site is excessive for the
infrastructure and character of the neighborhood at this time.

My concerns are twofold:

Infrastructure capacity
The numbers and rate of development proposed will overwhelm existing infrastructure.
Sewerage systems, stormwater management, traffic ﬂow, and parking are not designed for
this density. Adding 8–14 units all at once risks system strain, backups, and long-term
maintenance costs that ultimately fall on residents. Phased, lower-density development
allows the city and utilities to keep pace.

Quality of space utilization
This parcel cannot absorb 8–14 units without compromising setbacks, open space, and
neighborhood scale. The resulting density changes the character of the area and reduces
livability for both current and future residents. A limit of 5–7 homes would better align with
existing lot sizes, traffic patterns, and the quality of space we value in Riverton.

I respectfully request that the City deny the rezone as proposed and cap any approval at 5–
7 homes maximum. This is a discretionary matter, and I urge you to exercise that discretion
in favor of sustainable growth that protects infrastructure and community character.

Thank you for your service and for considering resident input.

Sincerely,
