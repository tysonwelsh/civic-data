Proposed Interlocal Agreement – Landfill Post-Closure Care Permit at
910 West 21st Street
          Public Services Executive Director Anderson explained Ogden City and West Haven City are intending to enter into an
interlocal agreement for the purpose of a Post-Closure Care Permit for the ongoing monitoring and maintenance of the Moulding
and Sons Landfill located at 910 West 21st Street. The landfill was closed in 2008, and the property donated to Ogden City and
West Haven City shortly thereafter. The landfill cap had been inspected over the years and determined to be functioning as
expected. Though inspected by the state, no Post-Closure Care Permit was filed. As the current owners, Ogden City and West
Haven City are responsible for the post-closure care and for applying for the permit. Ogden City owns approximately 80% of the
landfill property with 20% owned by West Haven City. The permit application is being proposed as part of an interlocal
agreement between Ogden City and West Haven City with Ogden City being the lead agency. The agreement sets forth the
responsibilities of each party with regard to post-closure care. The agreement and the permit provide background on what the
property may be used for and what is required with the post-closure care. The post-closure maintenance is typically required for a
period of 30 years; however, the transmittal documents indicate that the City will apply for early termination after one year. The
post-closure plan provides additional background on the property and what is required with regard to inspections and reporting.
This includes quarterly inspections, annual reporting, facility maintenance, quarterly settlement measurements to ensure
stabilization, and adequate record keeping. The plan also sets forth uses for the site. The site is currently being used for storage of
rock, gravel, road base and other fill materials. No excavation is taking place on the property. Moving forward, the property may
be used for a limited number of uses including recreational and green spaces for public use, municipal uses, and specialized
structures and facilities. Any modification of the site, including new vegetation and trees, require review and approval to ensure
there is no disturbance of the materials under the cap. The permit also provides estimates for post-closure care costs. The plan
provides an estimate of approximately $11,500 of annual cost between the two entities.
          Discussion among the Council and staff centered on the value of the land and potential reuse of the land; collaboration
between West Haven and Ogden City to determine post-closure care of the property; and the risk to Ogden City involved in
accepting responsibility for the property. Chair Richey concluded action on the interlocal agreement will be scheduled for the
November 12, 2024, meeting.
Proposed Rezone – 139 North Wall Avenue
          Planning Manager Brierley used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss a petition submitted by Craig Butters to
rezone 0.56 acres from R-1-6 (Single-family residential) to R-3/CO (Multiple-family Residential with a conditional overlay) with
a development agreement. The subject property has recently been added to the Fenway Fields Subdivision so that two single-
family lots can access Fenway Fields Drive, as UDOT will not allow access to Wall Avenue at this location. The applicant has
sufficient acreage in the R-1-6 zone to develop two single-family lots, however, the rezone is necessary to ensure that the zone
boundary follows the property lines. In addition to the single-family lots, the applicant proposes tenant garages for the Fenway
Fields townhome residents on the subject property. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the
meeting.
          Discussion among the Council and Mr. Brierley centered on the development concept for the site; whether the housing
units will be for sale or for rent; location of the garages/storage areas on the site; and the potential future use of the Fenway Fields
subdivision.
          Chair Richey invited input from the petitioner. Mr. Butters approached and stated he owns the Fenway Fields project
area; he noted there are no storage sheds on the site, rather there are garages and there will only be eight or nine depending on the
total number of units on the site. There is a fairly small building window on the site, and he is working to include the subject
property in the homeowner’s association (HOA) for the project. He discussed parking requirements for the site and indicated he is
attempting to meet the minimum standards of the City while also complying with UDOT relative to access to the site and on-street
parking. The current renderings are fairly rough, and he will work to refine them once he has received approval of the rezone.

Council Business.
          Council Executive Director Eller-Smith reported on efforts of Council staff to plan a Council visit to Hof, Germany
next summer as part of the City’s sister-city relationship. She indicated she will keep the Council apprised of changes to the
travel plans in order for them to take the steps necessary to secure needed travel documents, clear their schedule, and consider
exploring other areas near Hof.
