The purpose of the joint work session was to discuss Fiscal Y ear 202 1-2022 Budget Amendments pertaining to Marshall
White Center design; Fiscal Year 2022-2023 City Budget pertaining to the City Council, Public Services, Public Utilities, and
Capital Improvement Plan; Council business; and hear Council comments.

Fiscal Year 2021-2022 Budget Amendment — Marshall White Design
Comptroller Stout explained Administration is proposing an amendment to the Fiscal Year (FY) 2022 Budget to
recognize and appropriate funds from General Fund Fund Balance for transfer to the Capital Improvement Fund project RG003 to
cover design costs for the new Marshall White Center (MWC) Facility. Funding for demolition, site work, and construction will
be appropriated at a later date. She indicated this is the first budgeting step in the MWC project; a budget of $23 million has been
recommended by Administration with funding sources as follows:
e ARPA Funds $ 5,000,000
e Donations $ 1,500,000
e RAMP Grant $ 1,500,000
e Bond Proceeds $15,000,000
Council member Hyer asked if those dollar amounts — specifically the amount assigned to donations — are confirmed. Ms.
Stout answered no, they are placeholders; there is potential for the City to raise more than $1.5 million in donations and if that
occurs, the amount of the bond to be issued will be adjusted. There was brief discussion among the Council and staff regarding
the City’s historic RAMP grant awards and the potential for the City to receive the amount requested for the MWC project. Public
Services Director Lowder indicated that the MWC project will be considered the City’s ‘legacy project’ for the purposes of the
RAMP grant program and there should be additional RAMP funding available for other City projects.

Fiscal Year 2022-2023 City Budget - Departmental Budget Review

City Council: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the budget
proposal for the City Council and Mayor’s Office; the Council office is funded through the General Fund and significant changes
in the proposed FY23 Council Office expenditures include additional wage and benefit costs in the amount of $228,625 to cover
cost-of-living increases for Council members and staff. There is also an additional $1,000 added to the tuition reimbursement
program and $10,000 added for a grant program to assist with costs for larger events that do not qualify for Neighbor-Up grants.

from the City Council relating to the Marshall White Center (MWC) project; in FY2022, the Council amended the City ordinance
governing the management of Business Depot Ogden (BDO) lease revenue funds to set aside $1 million dollars each year for five
years to provide funding for construction of a new Marshall White Center. This was first funded in FY2022. The proposed
FY2023 budget also includes the $1 million dollar funding for FY2023. Note that this funding is not currently proposed for

There was discussion among the Council and Mr. Lowder regarding large capital projects; the increase in the homeless
population in the community; and opportunities for saving costs associated with refuse hauling services and increasing
participation in City’s curb side recycling program.

Utility Fund Analysis and Financial Plan: Laura Lewis, Lewis Young Robertson Young (LY RB), the City’s Financial
Advisor, used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide budget proposals relative to the City’s enterprise fund utilities,
which includes: Water, Sanitary Sewer, Storm Sewer, and Refuse Utilities. These utility funds are managed under the Public
Services Department. She also discussed the financial health and planning for each utility fund and an analysis of the utilities’
performance related to the financial principles adopted by the Council with the 2012 Utility Rate Study. She discussed the
performance and cash reserve of each enterprise fund and provided rate model assumptions after which she indicated LYRB
recommends increasing utility rates based upon the blended inflation adjustment (BIA), which also contemplated increases in the
consumer price index. The BIA increase for proposed FY2023 is 7.73 percent for water, sewer, and storm and 7.1 percent for
refuse. Additionally, LYRB recommends continuing with the update of master plans for each of the utilities and performing an in-
depth analysis of the storm enterprise fund equivalent residential unit allocation.

The Council, Administrative staff, and Ms. Lewis then engaged in philosophical discussion about continued potential
increases in future years and whether inflationary increases will be sufficient to cover needed infrastructure projects; they also
discussed whether the proposed rate increases are appropriate at this time given the impact of other economic factors on household
budgets. There was also a brief focus on opportunities to extend secondary water infrastructure to all areas of the City at some
point in the future to reduce the usage of culinary water.

Public Services — continued: Deputy Public Services Director Anderson then reported on the sidewalk work performed
in 2021; he presented a map identifying the clustering of completed sidewalk repair projects. He noted that in 2021, 44,200 square
feet of sidewalk were replaced/repaired, and he presented photos of the ‘top 10’ sidewalk projects that have been completed. The
City’s 50/50 sidewalk replacement program was utilized on four projects; the City had $52,000 in funding available, and only
$2,716 was spent from that budget. He then reported on major sidewalk install projects in 2021, including the 36" Street sidewalk,
26" Street sidewalk, Ogden Avenue sidewalk, and the sidewalk at Taylor Avenue and Boughton Street. The total cost of all
sidewalk projects completed in 2021 was $1,375,000; this covered 110,000 square feet of sidewalk. He then reported on sidewalk
projects planned for 2022, most notably including sidewalk along Polk Avenue, 32™ Street to T.O. Smith Elementary on Monroe
Boulevard, 2" Street to Heritage Elementary on Wall Avenue, and a shared use path from Beus Pond to Skyline Drive.

Discussion among the Council and Mr. Anderson centered on efforts to advertise the 50/50 program to residents
throughout the City and other planned improvement projects that compliment the sidewalk projects, such as curb/gutter projects
and traffic signalization.

General Fiscal Year 2023-2027 Capital Improvement Plan Discussion: Deputy Executive Director Symes reported on
the annual process for reviewing the five-year Capital Improvement Plan (CIP); he invited the Council to review the Plan and
reach out to Council staff with any questions about any project so that those questions can be forwarded to City Administration.
Council member White stated she has requested an electric vehicle charging station plan for the City and she asked if any CIP
project includes infrastructure that would accommodate electric vehicle charging stations. Public Services Director Lowder
indicated the City partnered with Weber State University (WSU) on phase one of that type of plan and the City is working with
Rocky Mountain Power to pursue funding for continuation of the plan and identifying the appropriate locations for level three and
level two chargers.

General Fiscal Year 2022-2023 Budget Discussion: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith facilitated discussion
among the Council regarding their ideas for additional budget adjustments; she has heard from some Council members about their
desire to reduce the wage and benefit line items for Departments and holding those dollars in a reserve account to be reallocated
when they are needed. This would address the concern about the amount of ‘reserve funding’ each Department has access to when
wage funds are not used due to a position vacancy.

The Council then referenced the focus on homelessness issues during the Public Services budget presentation; they
indicated a willingness to consider policy adjustments and budget increases to secure bathrooms and other City facilities that are
commonly occupied and/or damaged by homeless individuals.

Relative to all wage scales, the Council indicated they would like to vote separately on the wages for City Council, the
wage for the Mayor, and the wages for all other employees of the City. This led to discussion of the benchmarking practices for
elected officials’ salaries and whether the benchmark entities were valid for determining the wages of City Council members.

The Council indicated support for the Management Services Department to pursue security measures for publicly
accessible areas of the Municipal Building.
