1. Presentation Regarding Massage Therapy Business Ordinance; Suzanne Dunbar
Suzanne Dunbar thanked the Council for letting her come and present, as well as the Planning
Director for his work. Dunbar introduced herself to the Council and said that she has operated
and owned a massage business in the City for 14 years and teaches massage therapy. She
proposed changes to the City’s code: 1) add a definition of Massage Therapy to Chapter 19.04
Definitions; 2) align definition of Reiki in Chapter 19.04 Definitions with the DOPL Massage
Therapy Board Position Statement on Reiki; and 3) a) add Massage Therapy into
Chapter19.04.393 Office Use alongside similar businesses. b) Strike the word Massage from

Millcreek City Council Meeting Minutes 14 May 2018 Page 2 of 14

Chapter 19.44.020, Table 19.44-1 and the related footnotes. c) Strike the word Massage from
Chapter 19.60.030, Table 19.60-2.

She said the rationale for the first two proposals is that massage therapy and reiki are not the
same. She continued to read through her handout which included state definitions, links to other
cities codes, and information from the Division of Occupational and Professional Licensing. The
rationale for the third proposal is that all major massage organizations define massage therapy as
a healthcare and wellness profession.

Mayor Silvestrini said the goal is to make sure those performing massage therapy are licensed by
the State and not engaged in illegal behavior. Dunbar said that there is a provision in the City’s
ordinance that does not allow massage therapy businesses within 500’ of each other. She said
this is limiting for business suites in one building.

Mayor Silvestrini said there have been problems with massage-type businesses and maybe
proximity restrictions are not the way to resolve the problem. Council Member Jackson
appreciated the time and effort Ms. Dunbar put into researching the issue. Mayor Silvestrini said
he would like to put together a stakeholder group with massage professionals and law
enforcement to address the problems the City has been experiencing.

Joe Holland said he is the President of Property Management Systems that has owned the
building that houses Dunbar’s business since 1997. They manage about 700 units and they
enforce the rules and HOAs. He said the company has lost $4,400 since the ordinance was
approved in January due to vacant space.

Mayor Silvestrini asked the audience who would be in favor of the ordinance changes. The
majority of the audience rose their hands and no hands were raised for being against the changes.
He said if people are interested in participating in the stakeholder group, that they should talk to
Francis Lilly.

3. Discussion on Direction of Town Center Overlay Ordinance and Master Development
Overlay Ordinance
Mayor Silvestrini said there were some questions about why the City is doing overlays when he
was at the last Planning Commission meeting. He said it may make sense to do a joint session
with the Commission on these ordinances. The idea of the town center overlay ordinance allows
the City to design something in the potential town center (the wedge). The City received a grant
to study the area and do more public engagement. He said the City has been working on public
engagement with the general plan since the City was incorporated and is almost ready to release
a draft of the plan for public review. Through that process, the public has said they would like a
city gathering place, and the area between 1300 E. and Highland Dr. (the wedge) is where they
want one. He said there has been a great deal of interest by developers to build in this area. He
said the City needs to help plan the growth in the area, so it is not haphazard. A city center public
meeting was held on April 30" to get public feedback.

Francis Lilly said there is currently a draft ordinance and the spirit of it is to be restrictive. The
ordinance will restrict uses the City does not want in the area and creates design standards.
Mayor Silvestrini said he sees a need to enact the town center overlay zone to act as a place-
maker for the City to be able to plan. If there was time, he would rather the general plan be done
first, but the City may lose opportunities before the plan is done.

Council Member Catten said the zone allows time for input from the residents. Council Member
Uipi said she wanted to be thoughtful and would like to know what the developers are bringing
to the table. Lilly said developers are bringing 188 apartments on Gunn Ave. and Richmond St.,
a 12-plex, and a 40ft tall project. These developers have agreed to wait and see what the City
does so they are more in line with the plan. Council Member Uip?i is in favor of giving positive
directions to continue with the zone. Lilly said holding a joint session with the Council and
Planning Commission would be a terrific idea. He would like to get an ordinance concept to the
community councils in June. Council Member Jackson said she loves the idea of creating design

standards because the Villa Theater is one of the few historic buildings in the City, and it would
be nice to highlight it. Council Member Marchant said he would like to move forward with the
town center overlay zone. The Council decided they would give direction to the Planning
Commission to move forward with the zone even though the general plan is not approved yet.

Mayor Silvestrini said the master development overlay ordinance would give the City the
flexibility to modify the zoning ordinance in response to a project; to make exceptions to the
requirements on a case-by-case basis. He used made-up examples about the old meeting house
on Highland Dr. as an example for potential property uses. He said this ordinance allows the City
to make concessions, to trade with the developer. The idea is to give the City the ability to allow
something that is not normally allowed if there is a trade off that benefits the City. In order for a
developer to propose this type of thing, the developer must bring in a rigorous plan that shows
what the City would be getting for making the trade-off.

Mayor Silvestrini asked if the Council wanted to give the Planning Commission reassurance if
this ordinance is a good idea or not. Lilly said he has worked on two projects like this, and they
do take a lot of time. He said what the City gets from the developer is a specific design standard,
etc. and it becomes a code. He said Daybreak was a big project done with this kind of concept.
He said the ordinance is the right tool for the right project for the right space. Council Member
Catten asked if it is anticipated that it would be used before the general plan is done. Lilly said he
would not be in a hurry to implement this adoption until after the general plan is done. The
Council said they were in favor of approving the zone in conjunction with the general plan.

4. Discussion on Alternate Planning Commissioner Participation

Mayor Silvestrini said he received a complaint from an applicant about there being too many
Planning Commissioners. There is a question about whether alternates can participate in the
discussion and not the voting because it was in the provisions with the County but is not in the
City’s code.

Tom Stephens, Planning Commission Chair, recommends the Council amend the bylaws to make
it explicit that alternates can participate but not vote, or have nine regular members and no
alternates.

Council Member Marchant said it is important for the audience to know who the alternates are
during the meeting and to for them to know that they will not be voting. Council Member Catten
asked if the background experience of the current alternates has swayed the votes of the regular
commissioners. Stephens said it has happened. Mayor Silvestrini thinks its problematic
influencing the decision but not voting and thinks the Council could amend the Commission to 9
members. Johns Brems said the options are to make the Commission 9 members or let the
alternates speak. Council Member Uipi said the expectation has been set at 9 commissioners with
the alternates speaking and voting. Stephens dislikes having a bare quorum making decisions and
appreciates more commissioners’ input. Council Member Catten asked about district
representation. Mayor Silvestrini brought up that 2 terms are expiring next month. He said the
Council would solicit applications, and when sorting through them would try to get a good
distribution from around the City. Lilly said both expiring term appointments have expressed that
they would like to stay for another term. The Council was in agreeance to give direction to staff
to amend the ordinance to 9 members.

3. Financial Matters
3.1 Presentation of FY 2018-19 Tentative Budget
Mayor Silvestrini said the City has accomplished a lot in the last year, and went through
some of them: opened a new city hall, recruited and hired 25 employees, continued
funding for community events, facilitated public engagement for the general plan, held a
city center stakeholder meeting, conducted 4 land use hearings for special permits,
adopted 3 new zones, corrected sales tax on 195 businesses in the City, established the
Community Reinvestment Agency, completed the Community Wildfire Preparedness
Plan and received a grant to provide for a preparedness event, purchased a fleet, kicked
off the Upland Dr. sidewalk project, acquired an easement for 25.5 acres of open space
on Grandeur Peak, and obtained a WFRC grant.

Mayor Silvestrini went over some noteworthy changes for the FY 2018-19, which are:
the City exited the SLVLESA Taxing District and created a Millcreek Community Crime
Suppression Unit, will look to adopt a property tax increase to pay for UPD contract
costs, hiring for 4 new positions with pay increases to the current employees, and the
budget contains a separate Capital Improvement Project Fund. He then went over the key
components in comparison from the FY 17-18 Adopted Budget, FY 17-18 Amended
Budget and FY18-19 Tentative Budget. Mayor Silvestrini discussed the savings for
bringing services in house and said the total budget is $23,489,545. The Mayor discussed
the fund balance and the projected balance for FY 2018-19 is $4,080,422 or 17.37%.
Council Member Jackson asked if that amount shows that the fund balance will be
applied to pay off the debt for police services. Laurie Johnson said the key is negotiating
the borrowing costs and there will be a conversation about using the funds to pay down
the debt. She also mentioned that there is some fund balance that has not been recognized
yet from SLVLESA.

Mayor Silvestrini said the budget contains the city manager’s budget statement and he
endorses it. The document also contains a budget calendar of events. The final tentative
budget will be approved May 29, 2018. He said the budget does include an increase in the
number of employees to handle the workload. He said the current property tax was a
judgment levy property tax and it generated $350,000 this past fiscal year. The next fiscal
year property tax will have to pay for the police services contract at $10,357,000, so it
will look like the property tax has increased by 2,859%. He said it will reflect that
Millcreek was not really levying a property tax before because the amount was so small.

The Mayor briefly went over the general fund revenue, which included: taxes, licenses

4.2 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution 18-22, Authorizing Study of
Extending S-Line Streetcar into Millcreek

Mayor Silvestrini said this Resolution is to authorize to fund a study to extend the s-line
streetcar into Millcreek. He said in discussions with the Salt Lake County Council on
boundary adjustments, one council member said they would like the City to offer to put
the tax money Salt Lake City would otherwise get into a fund to get the street car there.
He said this study is to look at the prospect of it, but the project would not actually be
done for years. He said to adequately address the issue, a study needs to be done. Mike
Winder said this gives the City the authority to start looking at options and having
conversations with Wasatch Front Regional Council and Utah Transit Authority. The
price of the study will be determined by the RFP’s.

Council Member Jackson moved to adopt Resolution 18-22, Authorizing Study of
Extending S-Line Streetcar into Millcreek. Council Member Catten seconded. Mayor
Silvestrini called for the vote. The motion passed unanimously.
