1.      WELCOME, INTRODUCTION AND PRELIMINARY MATTERS

Chair Barbieri called the meeting to order at 6:30 p.m. and welcomed those in attendance. A roll
call was conducted wherein all councilmembers were present.

        1.1      Opening Ceremonies – Pledge/Reverence – Councilmember Cochran (Opening
                 Ceremonies for October 5, 2022 to be arranged by Taylorsville Youth Council

Councilmember Cochran directed the Pledge of Allegiance before commenting on the recent
passing of Dale Kehl who had served the city in various capacities for many years. Councilmember
Cochran read Mr. Kehl’s obituary and described him as a true friend and mentor. A moment of
silence was then held in Mr. Kehl’s honor.

        1.2      Mayor’s Report

      Mayor Kristie Overson explained that in commemoration of the events of September 11,
2001, a day of service had been held earlier in the month. It was known as the largest annual day
of charitable service in the country. She had witnessed an amazing amount of community service
throughout the city. She explained that there had been a great deal of laughter and hard work and
she loved seeing everyone working together to commemorate 9/11 in a meaningful way.

The previous week, the mayor had been fortunate enough to represent Taylorsville and attend a
naturalization ceremony as 39 people swore an oath of allegiance and became United States
citizens. She appreciated that Salt Lake County representatives had invited her to the occasion.

Arts, Parks and Utah Skies was a collaborative event with the Salt Lake County Parks and
Recreation Department and the Clark Planetarium. It was designed to connect the community
through the wonders of space and using public art to create and activate our imaginations. Three
locations were chosen in which to display new pieces of art and one of those locations was the
Taylorsville Recreation Center. The new piece was a sun dial called Sun and Moon, and Mayor
Overson appreciated having the opportunity to speak with the artist.

Harmon’s was celebrating its 90th year as a grocery store and over 30 years in Taylorsville.

The Volunteer Appreciation Picnic had turned out wonderfully and she enjoyed hearing two of the
longest tenured volunteers share their stories. Dale Kehl had been missed, and the mayor thanked
Councilmember Cochran for mentioning him earlier in the meeting since he had been an important
part of Taylorsville. Kris Heineman, Jen Andrus, Jean Ashby, and the facilities crew had done a
fantastic job on organizing the picnic.

Mayor Overson explained that a resident had recently received a citation from TVPD Officer Bang
and when he came to pay the citation, he wanted to thank him for his kindness in doing his job.
He expressed his appreciation to Officer Bang for the kindness he expressed while citing him. He
appreciated all that the police department did.

Taylorsville’s SWAT team was joining forces with the SWAT team of West Valley Police. She
explained it would provide both agencies with more resources and manpower than each would
otherwise have. She was very excited for the collaboration.

Earlier in the day, interviews for prospective Youth Council members had taken place. A decision
would be made the following Friday. The Youth Councilmembers would be sworn in at one of the
October city council meetings.

The mayor had recently attended a building inspection with Building Official Steve Porten and
found it fascinating. The city was currently hiring a Building Inspector, Judicial Assistant, a
Judicial Service Representative, Crossing Guards and a Senior Van Driver. Taylorsville was a
great place to work, and she invited anyone interested in the positions to apply.

Chair Barbieri explained that she had been invited by the American Preparatory Academy to read
the Constitution on Constitution Day (September 17). She also mentioned her great appreciation
for first responders and everyone who had participated in the previously mentioned day of service.

3.      REPORTS

        3.1 Taylorsville Green Committee Report – Mark Wendel

       Green Committee Chair Mark Wendel introduced himself and listed the other committee
members. He then described some of the committee’s activities over the previous year. They had
hosted two Localscapes classes by Cynthia Bee of the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District
(JVWCD) and Dan McDougal of the Taylorsville Bennion Improvement District as
Councilmember Burgess had mentioned in the briefing session, another would be taught the
following Friday. The class was designed to address Utah’s unique climate which presented
various challenges. The class was free and those interested in attending could sign up through the
city’s website.

The annual community-wide cleanup had taken place on June 18th. It was held at Taylorsville
High School and over 500 residents had participated. The following services had been provided:
   • Document shredding
   • Household hazardous waste disposal
   • Prescription medication disposal
   • Electronic waste disposal
   • Glass recycling
   • Bulk waste disposal
   • Yard waste disposal
   • Donations of gently used items were taken to Deseret Industries and Goodwill.

The cleanup was made possible because of city funds, a grant from Salt Lake County and the use
of a shred truck from Certified Shred.

Later in the summer the Green Committee had sponsored a booth at Taylorsville Dayzz. Prizes
were distributed, as was literature pertaining to recycling, waste disposal and water conservation.

Committee volunteers and Councilmember Burgess had participated in the 9/11 Day of Service.
They had assisted Elsa Elizondo in her efforts to clean up I-215 and had helped at St. Matthew’s

        7.2      Resolution No. 22-29 – A Resolution of the City of Taylorsville Approving an
                 Interlocal Cooperation Agreement Between the City and Salt Lake County for
                 UPDES Media Campaign Cost Sharing – Ben White

      Mr. Ben White explained that the interlocal agreement represented a collaboration between
Salt Lake County, Taylorsville, and essentially every other city in the Salt Lake valley. He stressed
the importance of keeping the public informed and educated about the keeping the storm drain
system clean and clear. Taylorsville’s portion of the public education campaign would be $9,000
which would go to Salt Lake County who would then administer the program. The funds would
go towards things like the Salt Lake County Stormwater Coalition which provided a great deal of
educational material for the public. The Coalition also sponsored science fairs and aired public
service announcements on local media.

        7.3      Resolution No. 22-30 – A Resolution of the City of Taylorsville Approving an
                 Interlocal Cooperation Agreement between the City and Salt Lake County
                 for Funding a Portion of the Construction of the Utah and Salt Lake Canal
                 Trail. – Ben White

      Mr. White explained that the proposed agreement involved the city’s contribution of
$140,000 towards a trail to be constructed along the Utah and Salt Lake Canals. It involved
Taylorsville, Kearns, Salt Lake County, and the Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT).
UDOT would contribute $2.7 million, and the remaining entities would contribute 20% of the total
cost.
