1.0     WELCOME AND INTRODUCTION OF THE MASTER OF CEREMONIES
        (KAREN MORGAN) – City Manager, Jared Gerber.

City Manager, Jared Gerber, called the meeting to order at 3:00 PM and welcomed those present
and recognized their shared commitment to public service, civic engagement, and the future of
Cottonwood Heights. He expressed gratitude to the City’s elected officials, staff, family members,
community residents, and others who took the time to be part of the swearing-in ceremony. He
then introduced Master of Ceremonies, former State Senator, Karen Morgan.

Senator Morgan welcomed those present and stated that those involved in Cottonwood Heights’
incorporation had high hopes, but the City exceeded their greatest dreams.

3.0     PLEDGE OF ALLEGIANCE – Council Member Matt Holton.

The Pledge of Allegiance was led by Council Member Holton.

4.0     REMARKS– Council Member Suzanne Hyland.

Council Member Hyland stated that it was wonderful to see so many people in attendance at the
ceremony. Last year, unconscionable political violence happened in Utah, and it was refreshing
to participate in a peaceful transition of power and be reminded of the 250th anniversary of the
United States, a great nation that celebrates freedom, equality, and the pursuit of happiness.

In her two years on the City Council, Council Member Hyland has been grateful for the Council’s
civil and dignified discussions. She expressed gratitude for her Council colleagues, as well as
former Mayor Mike Weichers and Mayor-Elect Bennion, and their commitment to acting with
civility. Every human life deserves dignity, and she asked that everyone continue to respect and
give each other grace. She looks forward to serving the City together with her Council colleagues.

In her four years on the Council, Council Member Birrell has seen a great deal of blight in
Cottonwood Heights due to residents struggling with the cost of water and other essential services
and goods. Together, the community can find ways to meet the extreme challenges of the 21st
century. She worked in the ski industry for 20 years and understands what challenges like the
warmer-than-average December mean for the industry. Humans must face the fact that air, water,
and soil are necessities. Economic development and other ways to bring prosperity to the
community are important, but it is equally important to not lose sight of the things that sustain all

       6.3       REMARKS – Mayor Gay Lynn Bennion.

Mayor Bennion thanked everyone for their support. This was her fourth election in six years, and
many of those present had participated in those efforts. She thanked all constituents who had
called and emailed with questions, as those interactions made her a better, more informed person.
Whether residents supported her in the past or not, she will be their Mayor and her office will
always be open to them. She will hold open office hours every Friday from 2:00 p.m. to 5:00 p.m.,
and all citizens are welcome to attend and share their concerns with her.

Mayor Bennion thanked her family. She and her husband have been together for almost 40 years,
including 26 years in the Air Force. She is also grateful for her children and grandchildren, as well
as her parents, Donald and Joy Chisholm, and siblings. Her parents taught them to work hard,
play hard, forgive each other, and embrace life fully. Alain de Botton said that evidence of good
parenting is that your child has no desire to be famous. She has no desire to be famous but she
does have a desire to serve and make the community better for everyone, and she has been able to
succeed due to the support of the people around her.

When her family moved back to Utah 14 years ago, a neighbor, Suzanne Mulet, introduced Mayor
Bennion to the Women’s State Legislative Council of Utah, where she served for seven years and
learned about the state legislature. When Representative Marie Poulson announced that she was
retiring, people encouraged Mayor Bennion to run for office. Groups like the Women’s State
Legislative Council are desperately needed, as they allow citizens to learn more about the process
and become involved. Members of the group include Cottonwood Heights residents Bette
Voorhees, Donna Murphy, and Lorraine Turner.

Mayor Bennion thanked the City’s past mayors. Kelvyn Cullimore worked with minimal staff to
carry the City through very important times and had the vision to create the City Hall building.
Mike Peterson led Cottonwood Heights through the pandemic and was instrumental in completing
Big Cottonwood Canyon Trail. Mike Weichers led the effort to begin work on the Town Center,
which has been part of the General Plan for 20 years. She also thanked the City Council, who will
guide and help her as she learns more about running the City. She knows they will collaborate and
have great unity and strength together.

As Council Member Birrell mentioned, Mayor Bennion sees the area’s changing climate as one of
the City’s greatest challenges. She believes the City can rise to the challenge of addressing water
conservation to preserve the Great Salt Lake and can make its buildings and homes more fire-
resistant. Another challenge is that of misinformation and artificial intelligence. It is important to
double-check information against trusted sources to ensure that we make good choices for our
families and community and are not being led astray by misinformation.

Cottonwood Heights is an amazing community, and some of the brightest individuals in the state
live here. She is honored to serve as Mayor. However, the City faces a financial challenge. For
the past seven years, reserves have been accessed to balance the budget. The City is at a point

The individuals who laid the foundation for the City include Karen Morgan, who was the area’s
State Representative in 1999. At that time, the Salt Lake County Commission was working to
incorporate all unincorporated areas, and surrounding cities were considering annexing parts of
what would become Cottonwood Heights. In 1999, Gordon Nicholl, then-president of the
Cottonwood Heights Community Council, and attorney Kit Lindsey spearheaded the effort to
conduct a feasibility study to incorporate the City. In partnership with Senator Morgan, they
worked against opposition to gather signatures and convince the County Council to conduct the
feasibility study. Senator Morgan said, “I will never forget how excited the three of us were as we
finally delivered all the necessary information to the Salt Lake County offices by the deadline. We
were relieved when our request for a feasibility study was granted. We were thrilled in May 2002
when the County’s final report indicated that it was feasible for our area to incorporate. An
incorporation committee was established to move the potential city’s creation forward. In May
2004, nearly 85% of the citizens in this area voted overwhelmingly in favor of incorporation, and
Cottonwood Heights was incorporated on January 14, 2005 with Kelvyn Cullimore as the first
mayor.” Mayor Bennion hopes to continue fostering that type of citizen involvement, as citizens’
expertise and engagement will help the City Council.

Plaques were presented honoring Mr. Nicholl and Mr. Lindsey’s contributions to the City.

Mayor Bennion thanked Senator Morgan for being an example to women in leadership. Society
has been led by men for centuries, and only in the last 50 years have women moved into leadership
positions. Senator Morgan opened the door for Mayor Bennion and other women to serve.

Mayor Bennion thanked Mr. Nicholl for his contributions to the City. He was one of the first
paramedics in Utah and served as president of the Cottonwood Heights Community Council. His
daughter, Kris Nicholl, was sworn in earlier that day as a Sandy City Council member.

Mr. Nicholl stated that he and Mayor Cullimore worked closely with other members of the
incorporation committee and spent many hours working to form the City of Cottonwood Heights.
They love the community and want it to thrive and grow.

Senator Morgan remarked that she believes Cottonwood Heights is the greatest city in Utah, and
that is due to the efforts of the City’s leaders and staff. She thanked Mayor Bennion and the City
Council for their willingness to serve, dedication, and commitment to the community.
