1. 6:00 – Work Meeting (Fort Herriman Conference Room)
Chair Berbert called the meeting to order at 6:06 p.m.

    1.1. Review of City Council Decisions – Michael Maloy, City Planner
Planning Manager Spencer had no updates.

    1.2. Review of Agenda Items – Planning Staff
Planning Manager Spencer requested Commissioners to include specific direction to City Council, with a
recommendation. The options for action were to approve as drafted, approve with changes or lack of a
recommendation would be consider denial by default and forwarded to Council. Any additions should be
included in a very specific motion to City Council. Commissioners discussed Olympia and offered the opinion
it was not ready, as they felt more information was needed. Commissioners expressed the opinion it was being
rushed to meet the timeline and they were not comfortable recommending it with missing appendices. City

Attorney Andrizzi explained the requirements and process for development agreement applications. Some
elements, such as financing, were not part of Commission review. He recommended the Commission identify
deficiencies and request specific remedies as they make a recommendation to Council. He recommended the
Commission include issues with recommended updates as part of their motion. Chair Berbert emphasized this
may be their last review of the development and expressed a need to clarify requirements for it tonight. City
Attorney Andrizzi explained the development would either incorporate or file to be annexed into an existing
city, upon issuance of 1,500 building permits. Commissioners asked for the legal description. City Attorney
Andrizzi identified two separate legal descriptions, one of which included school districts. The land use map
plan included the acreage. Anything missed and/or not addressed in the agreement would revert to city
requirements per state law. Olympia design standards were originally negotiated with the County.
Commissioners requested the open space requirement be re-written to clarify the intent to include twenty acres
of open space for every 100 acres developed. City Attorney agreed to identify all the red line changes during
the regular meeting. Commissioners asked at what point the document would no longer be labeled draft. City
Attorney Andrizzi responded they were identified as drafts until final approval by Council. City Attorney
Andrizzi recommended each Commissioner identify specific issues, then itemize them for inclusion in the
motion. Commissioner Ferguson recommended adding parking behind the buildings and removing it from
areas adjacent to the roads, to contribute to the neighborhood feel. He recommended no parking within thirty
feet of the property line. He further recommended entrances every fifty or 100 feet. He requested requiring
balconies on all apartment buildings. All properties should have an entrance onto the street to activate the
street and prevent dead space. Chair Berbert added that no AC units or storage may be allowed on balconies.
Commissioner Sickles requested decreased block sizes and perimeters to encourage walkability. He suggested
around 300-400 feet range for block length with a maximum perimeter around 1,600 feet, as corners provided
respite areas.

The work meeting was adjourned by consensus at 7:06 p.m.

Legislative items are recommendations to the City Council. Broad public input will be taken and

    4.1    Request: Approval for the Master Development Agreement and Design Standards for
           the Proposed Olympia Development at approximately 7051 West 12600 South with ~
           933 Acres (Public Hearing)
           Zone: N/A
           Applicant: Ryan Button (legal representative)
           Acres: 933
           File Number: M2021-075
City Attorney Chase Andrizzi reviewed the location, history, and procedural timeline of the request. He pointed
out recent changes to the design standards and master development agreements. Commissioners were
encouraged to state specific recommendations for Council consideration when making a motion. City Attorney
Andrizzi informed the Commission meeting notes from each meeting were available on the City website and
no public comments were offered at the latest public hearing.

Applicant Representative Ryan Button expressed appreciation to staff and the Commission for input received
at a previous work meeting. He offered to answer questions.

Chair Berbert reviewed the public hearing guidelines.

Chair Berbert open the public hearing.

No comments were offered.

Chair Berbert closed the public hearing.

City Attorney Andrizzi reviewed the redline changes from the July 21, 2021 Commission work meeting and
encouraged them to voice additional recommendations. Chair Berbert requested a balanced distribution of
housing units to prevent all apartments in the same zone. Single-family homes should not be replaced with
multi-family units. City Attorney Andrizzi responded the design guidelines and setback requirements prevented
the movement of units between zones. The traffic impact study looked at roads within the development and
reviewed impacts on roadways near the development. If new impacts were determined to increase the total
number of vehicles, an additional traffic impact study would be required. He identified the Independent Review
Committee (IRC) section updates, which reflected changes suggested by the Commission. They further
requested specific administrative roles be designated by City Council Resolution. Commissioners requested to
be involved in the four-year annual review process and debated which items would need further Commission
August 5, 2021–Planning Commission Minutes                                            Page 6 of 8
review. Some items may need administrative staff approval, while others needed additional input. They
emphasized the importance of public involvement, a proactive approach, and a transparent IRC process.

Applicant Representative Ryan Button spoke. From their perspective, if the review complied and was a
straightforward process then streamlining the process could be beneficial for some elements of the
development. Perhaps a matrix or table could categorize issues toward the appropriate review process. Applicant
Representative Bruce Baird acknowledged the Commission concerns and recommended creating a mechanism
for public input in a referral to City Council. He added they have provided the City with multiple levels of input
for functional control.

Commissioner Palmer proposed community outreach meetings for citizens to voice concerns openly and on
record. Commissioner Sickles concurred. City Attorney Andrizzi continued with the development agreement
red line review of the open space process. Commissioners recommended unique large open space opportunities,
and dedication requirements. Land use place type changes had been updated based on previous Commission
input. Ideal commercial center and town center use locations and amounts were debated. Cul-de-sac locations
were limited and would only be placed in topographically steep or other appropriate locations. Commissioner
Sickles recommended small block sizes. He was of the opinion smaller blocks were an element that helped
create more walkable communities. He recommended a maximum block length of 400 feet for town center,
village center, and neighborhood areas, maximum 1600 feet perimeter. Chair Berbert agreed small blocks and
trails had a more walkable feel than long blocks. Commissioners discussed attributes of walkable cities. They
debated the effect of increased roads on open space areas and potentially increased roadway maintenance costs.
Commissioners debated elements that created walkable areas and lot size ranges.

Applicant Representative Ryan Button stated he did not share the vision for small blocks as the maintenance
and snow removal fees would increase, but he welcomed the recommendation. The additional upkeep and
maintenance of small blocks would add financial burden to the city or HOA. Herriman had no block length
requirements and addition of such would hold them to a higher standard than the rest of the City. The opinion
was offered that pedestrian walkways and trails were walkable and safe for children. Commissioners discussed
ways to promote walkability and connectivity. Text changes, storm drains, farm or agricultural uses and land use
definitions for landscape areas required updates. Setbacks, utility easement, and transition spaces from private
to public locations were discussed. Commissioner Ferguson was in favor of an addition to section one, with
regards to apartments, stating surface parking lots shall not be provided within thirty feet of a front or side yard
property line adjacent to a major or minor street. Apartment surface parking not permitted within the first six
feet of properties fronting private streets and must be separated from the street by a six-foot-wide berm that
was a minimum of 24 inches in height. He recommended pedestrian entrances from the street, with parking
locations behind the buildings, to promote the street front feel and energize the streetscape. Under section 6.7
he recommended a minimum of one pedestrian entrance to a public, commercial space lobby or residential unit
shall be provided for every fifty feet of building frontage along major streets and 100 feet on minor local streets,
with correlated street parking. He was also in favor of all properties with frontage on major or minor streets in
the town center and village center zone, providing a pedestrian entrance that fronts the street. These provisions
would not apply to commercial centers with a drive thru. Commissioner Ferguson was also in favor of minimum
fifty square feet balcony spaces shall be provided for minimum fifty percent of all single-family residential units
for buildings above two stories, with ten feet of vertical clearance from below. If they extend into the streetscape
they should be cantilevered. Balconies shall not be used for storage. City Attorney Andrizzi provided a review
of definitions and updated language to ensure consistent wording within the guidelines. Commissioners
reviewed their list of recommendations and reviewed the list as written by Planning Manager Clint Spencer.
Staff requested more detail regarding items which may be removed from IRC review and switched to
administrative, staff or Commission approval. The process options were discussed at length. Community

provided specific recommendations for administrative approval items.

Chair Berbert asked if he could make the motion for this item. City Attorney Andrizzi responded he may if no

Chair Berbert MOVED to refer to City Council item 4.1 Approval for the Master Development Agreement and Design
Standards for the Proposed Olympia Development at approximately 7051 West 12600 South with ~ 933 Acres (Public
Hearing) with the following recommendations:
    1. Work with Staff to implement more transparent public process than currently provided including amending the IRC
        process with the following options:
             a. Remove IRC; identify items for PC and Staff review
                        i. PC review:
                                1. Subdivision
                                2. Conditional Use for Home Occupation
                                3. Commercial, mixed use and multi-fam site plans
                       ii. Staff review:
                                1. Single family
                                2. Conditional Uses
    2. Add language to identify “unique” open space in 6.2.1
    3. Remove agricultural from open space.
    4. Amend open space definition to allow private land as well.
    5. Reducing the block sizes to promote walkability as proposed by Commissioner Sickle; and/or
             a. Possibly allow larger blocks if transected by pedestrian path which creates a 400 foot spacing for pedestrians; and
             b. Consider areas in the Village Center / Community Commercial to require alternative pedestrian path materials
                  including wider bridge connections
    6. Adopt language as presented by Commissioner Jackson regarding multi-family and commercial design excluding
        Commercial Centers and cantilevers on balconies.
    7. Allow increased distance from park to half mile if park is considered “large”, or “unique”.
    8. Encourage underground parking in multi-family, mixed use and commercial areas.
    9. Decrease the number of units allowed in the overall development, or bring a more substantial benefit to the community
        such as a larger park, or unique features that provides a benefit to a large portion of the community.
