Mariah Hill said the process of adopting the fiscal year 2026 budget requires several steps
that you, as a City Council, are required to take to be compliant with state statute. UCA
10-6-111(1) requires the Budget Officer to present a tentative budget for each fund within
the City. The budgets must be submitted no later than the first regularly scheduled
meeting in May. I will present budgets for each fund on Tuesday, May 6th on behalf of
the Budget Officer (City Manager). At that time, I will brief you on fiscal year 2025 revenue
estimates, provide a brief overview of expenditures, and present budget highlights along
with any proposed program changes.

UCA 10-6-111(3) further requires the City Council to adopt the tentative budget at this
meeting. The Council will then review and amend, if necessary, the budgets prior to their
final adoption. As a reminder, the purpose of a tentative budget is to have a working tool
from which we can start the formal adoption process. The budget document can be
amended prior to final adoption. At the time of final adoption, all budgets must be balanced
with expenditures equaling revenues.

          CITY PARK RENTAL AGREEMENT BETWEEN MIDVALE CITY AND THE
          UTE CONFERENCE INCORPORATED YOUTH FOOTBALL PROGRAM.
Matt Dahl said in 2020, Midvale City (City) entered into an agreement with the Ute
Conference Incorporated Football Program (the Conference), allowing the use of
designated areas within City Park for youth football programming. That agreement
expired on April 30, 2025.
                                           36
Proceedings of City Council Meeting

Following confirmation from the Ute Conference of their interest in renewing the
partnership, Midvale City has drafted a new five-year agreement. If approved, the
agreement would extend through April 30, 2030, and would permit continued use of
designated areas within Midvale City Park by the Conference for youth football activities.

Notable updates in the proposed agreement include:

   •   Adjustment of Rented Area: A provision under Section 1(a) “Property” allows the
       City to adjust the designated rented area of the park with four weeks’ written notice
       to the Conference. This flexibility is intended to accommodate potential
       construction activities during the agreement term.
   •   Adjustment of Use: Section 1(b) “Permitted Uses” has been revised to reflect that
       the Conference may be required to adjust its use of the rented area if the city
       modifies the designated space.

The city will continue to receive $5.00 per youth participant from the Conference. The
financial impact of the agreement is considered negligible.

Council Member Paul Glover asked if the Ute Conference is aware that they may need to
adjust its use of the rented area if the city modifies the designated space.

Matt Dahl said yes, they are aware, and he doesn’t see that there would be disruption
during this upcoming season.

Mayor Gettel asked if there was something in place in case of injuries.

Matt Dahl said yes, they are required to hold their own insurance policy.

Erinn Summers said prior to 2012, Midvale Elementary School was located at 332 and
362 W Center Street, on what is now part of Midvale City Park. In 2012, the school was
relocated to a new building at 7830 Chapel Street, and the original building was
subsequently demolished. On January 3, 2012, the Midvale City Council authorized
Mayor JoAnn B. Seghini to enter into an interlocal cooperation agreement with Canyons
School District to lease the former school property (the Property) to expand Midvale City

                                           43
Proceedings of City Council Meeting
May 6, 2025


Park to Center Street. This agreement granted Midvale City full management and
maintenance responsibilities for a 15-year term, ending in July 2027.
Over the past 13 years, Midvale City has used this space to provide recreational and
community benefits to residents. The City has maintained the property and added several
amenities, including pickleball courts, a basketball court, a tennis court, and a splash pad.
This area has become a vital extension of Midvale City Park, offering free, active space
for families and community events such as sporting activities and Harvest Days
celebrations, including the annual fireworks display.
In the last year, Canyons School District (District) has indicated that its Board of Education
is interested in selling the approximately 10.41-acre parcel currently under lease and
managed by Midvale City. The Property has been appraised at $4,933,000.00 and the
District is seeking to sell it at fair market value. Recognizing the value of preserving this
space for public use, Midvale City Council set thew parameters for the issuance and sale
of up to $55,000,000 in Sales and Franchise Tax Revenue Bonds on April 15, 2025.
These funds will, in part, support the purchase of the Property.
To ensure long-term preservation of this essential community space, Midvale City Staff
have prepared a purchase agreement with Canyons School District for the acquisition of
the Property. Key terms of the agreement include:
       •   Purchase Price: $4,933,000.00.
       •   Earnest Money: $49,330.00 due within five days of execution.
       •   Closing Date: Expected on or around June 16, 2025.
       •   Due Diligence: Waived, as the City has managed and maintained the property
           for over a decade.
       •   Additional Costs: Midvale City will cover the cost of a title report, ALTA survey,
           and title insurance.

IX.     DISCUSSION ITEM
        A. DISCUSS JORDAN RIVER TRAIL LIGHTING ISSUES AND REPAIR
            OPTIONS.
Jerimie Thorne said an inspection/evaluation of the Jordan River Trail (JRT) lighting from
7800 S to the South lift station and from the FL Smidth trailhead to the Trax crossing has
revealed significant vandalism and theft. Feeder and secondary pole wires have been cut
and stolen, junction boxes are filled with dirt, and lids are broken, posing safety risks.
Additionally, six light fixtures between FL Smidth and the Trax crossing have been stolen
and require replacement. A quote has been received to repair all lights and wiring from
7800 S to FL Smidth. Residents have expressed an interest in repairing the lighting.
However, staff is concerned about ongoing investment resulting from vandalism due to
the trail’s secluded nature and lack of patrols.

Fiscal Impact:
       • Bid to repair current damages: $72,853.00
       • Annual repair/maintenance costs of $3,000-15,000

OPTIONS FOR CONSIDERATION:
     • Fund Ongoing Repairs: The City continuously funds
       repairs/replacements to maintain operable lights.
     • Remove Lights: The City removes all lighting infrastructure and end
       ongoing maintenance.
     • Transfer Responsibility: The City offers to transfer
       ownership/maintenance of lights to adjacent HOA(s) or property owners

Staff was seeking Council guidance on preferred options and next steps.
