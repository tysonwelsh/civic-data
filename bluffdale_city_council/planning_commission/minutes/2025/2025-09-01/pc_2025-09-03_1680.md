7. PUBLIC HEARING, CONSIDERATION, AND RECOMMENDATION on_a
Proposed Text Amendment to Section 11.80.060 R-SL Senior Living Zone to Allow
Basements within the Zone and to Remove the Requirement that at Least One
Resident be 55 Years Old or Older. (Application 2025-35). Applicant, Howland
Partners, Inc., represented by Gary Howland and Tina Franco. Staff Presenter,
Caitlyn Tubbs.
Ms. Tubbs presented the Staff Report and indicated that the Applicant’s property is located off
2700 West, near Saddleback Pointe, Midvalley Bible Church, and Wardle Fields Park. Howland
Partners, Inc. applied for a General Plan Map amendment in 2020 from Very Low Density
Residential (“VLDR”) to Multi-Family Residential (“MFR”) in order to facilitate a zone change.
The R-SL Zone was later approved in February 2021 as the City’s first senior-targeted residential
zone. In September 2021, the Planning Commission reviewed the Site Plan and Plat for the Reagan
Cove development, which includes 28 attached units, private and public streets, and common-area
amenities.
Ms. Tubbs reported that the private street network had been installed, and one duplex had been
built. However, the Applicant had difficulty marketing the units due to the current point of access

through Saddleback Pointe and believed that the proposed changes to the R-SL Zone would expand
the pool of potential residents.
Proposed changes included the removal of the restriction on basements. This restriction was
originally included to prevent additional residents from living in the neighborhood, as well as to
assuage any concerns about potential accessory dwelling units (“ADU”). Ms. Tubbs reported that
it would be difficult and likely impossible to establish an ADU in the development. All property
lines are at the exterior walls of the condominium units, and as such, the required off-street parking
spaces could not be provided.
The Applicant also proposed the removal of the requirement that at least one resident must be 55
years of age or older, so they could market the units to younger families and other age groups.
However, the City had identified a need for senior housing developments, and residents have
expressed interest in this type of development. The R-SL Zone was the City’s only senior-focused
residential zone.
Notice of the public hearing was provided per Bluffdale City Code § 11.30.060. No public input
was received.
Chair Cragun asked how the amended zone would compare to the higher-density zone in
Independence. Ms. Tubbs clarified that the units are larger, and the largest building is a fourplex.
In Independence, pods of six to eight homes are allowed. The Applicant proposed changing the
zone from senior living to independent living to accommodate larger homes for people who want
to bring their growing families to the community but may not want a large yard.
In response to a question raised by Commissioner Flynn, Ms. Tubbs clarified that the restriction
on basements was not based on geotechnical issues and the homes would qualify for basements
under the Building Code. The restriction was intended to limit the number of residents in the
development.
The Applicant, Gary Howland, stated that they had not intended for the only access to the
development to be through an industrial park. That access was a result of the roundabouts on 2700

Commissioner Flynn stated that the original rezoning for senior living was a hard-won battle. The
City has acknowledged that it needs more senior housing. Removing that requirement and
allowing basements would completely change the subdivision and would not encourage public
support of similar projects in the future. He noted that access was mentioned as a reason for the

for the proposed Amendment of Section 11.80.060 R-SL Senior Living Zone. Commissioner
Flynn seconded the motion. Vote on Motion: Ulises Flynn-Yes, Erik Swanson-Yes, Kori
Luker-No, Chair Cragun-Yes. The motion passed by a vote of three-to-one.
Chair Cragun explained her reason for voting to forward a negative recommendation. Once
constructed, projects never look the same as they do when the Planning Commission first reviews
them. Flexibility and adaptability are required due to market changes, traffic changes, topography
issues, etc. Sometimes the Planning Commission decides that it cannot be flexible because the
request would substantially change what was first approved, and she believed that applied to this
request. The R-SL Zone should remain a senior living zone.
Ms. Tubbs reported that the item would be noticed and placed on the September 24, 2025, City
Council meeting agenda. Mr. Crowell added that Zone Map Amendments require a mailing, but
Zone Text Amendments do not.

Jeremy Searle of Wall Consulting Group presented the Active Transportation Master Plan and
indicated that a website was created to summarize key points of the plan. The website can be used
by residents and developers to obtain an overview of the City’s Active Transportation Plan without
having to review the full 100-page document. The plan was created with input from key
stakeholders such as residents, cycling groups, and the Healthy Bluffdale Coalition. .

Mr. Searle reviewed maps of the existing and recommended Active Transportation Networks,
including different types of bicycle lanes, trails, and improved road crossings. A map of the
existing sidewalk inventory was also reviewed, indicating areas where sidewalks are missing,
planned for the future, or only on one side of a roadway.
Traffic stress levels were considered. Many families are comfortable with low-volume roads and
separated trails, but only highly confident cyclists are comfortable riding on Redwood Road, for
example. The goal was to make the network as comfortable as possible for residents. Several
existing routes are higher stress, but that is anticipated to improve as trails are added and road
facilities are improved.
The Future Active Transportation Network includes protected, buffered, and standard bicycle
lanes. In response to a question raised by Chair Cragun, Mr. Searle stated that the question about
the definition of a bicycle is widely discussed. E-bikes are very popular now, and bicycle lanes
are appropriate for most of them. There are different classes of E-bikes; some are more like
motorcycles and are not appropriate for trails. He noted that the Jordan River Trail has some E-
bike restrictions. Mr. Crowell added that many cities are adopting E-bike policies. Some vehicles
that are referred to as E-bikes are actually motorcycles whose riders are required to meet all motor
vehicle requirements, but those were not considered bicycles for the purpose of the Active
Transportation Master Plan.
Chair Cragun stated that the general public may not understand the difference between E-bike
classes and what is and is not allowed in different areas. Mr. Crowell suggested that the City could
potentially have an educational campaign or install clarifying signage. Mr. Searle added that the
Active Transportation Master Plan includes E-bike information and policy recommendations.
The existing and recommended trail network was reviewed next, and Mr. Searle indicated that
many trails follow the canals. Canals typically have very few roadway crossings and ample land
available for trails, as long as the canal company’s cooperation can be obtained.

The active transportation crossing map was discussed. Recommended improvements include
better crosswalks and bulb-outs, as well as grade-separated crossings in high-traffic areas like the
Jordan River Trail crossing at 14600 South. In response to a question from Chair Cragun, Mr.
Searle stated that there is an existing grade-separated crossing on Redwood Road, as well as some
signaled crossings. Assistant City Engineer, Addison Mitton, clarified that the proposed trails that
will cross Redwood Road were not identified on the map. Mr. Crowell added that Redwood Road
is a high-speed, high-volume, state-controlled road, and grade-separated solutions can be
expensive. The undercrossing at Rose Creek was designed when the road was widened and is
smaller than some newer crossings, like the one at 2100 North.
Mr. Searle stated that a future trail crossing is recommended in southern Bluffdale. Mr. Crowell
noted that it is an existing wildlife crossing underneath Redwood Road that is smaller than the one
at Rose Creek, but it could potentially be modified.
Commissioner Flynn asked if the Jordan River Trail crossing at 14600 South would be installed
when the road is widened. Mr. Searle reported that the City is currently conducting a feasibility
study regarding widening 14600 South, and opportunities to grade-separate the trail crossing are
being considered in the study. Mr. Crowell stated that the Transportation Master Plan
acknowledges the need to increase capacity on 14600 South, and the feasibility study would be
presented to the Planning Commission once it was completed.
Mr. Searle continued his presentation. A safety analysis was conducted, which identified active
transportation accidents and accident patterns in the City. Approximately 95% of people involved
in a 20-mile-per-hour accident survive, but at 40 miles per hour 85% do not survive.
Recommendations included adding grade-separating crossings on higher-speed roadways to
increase safety.
