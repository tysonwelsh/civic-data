Development Proposal

The applicant is proposing a mixed use development that would include:

      a 6-story, 55-unit for-sale condo/hotel building to accommodate overnight stays for

Issues to Consider

In reviewing this application, staff suggests that there are two primary questions to answer:

   1. Is this the right location for this type of a mixed use development?

   2. Assuming the location is appropriate, does the quality of the project further City goals?

These two questions are explored below.

Is this the right location for a mixed use development?

Since the MXD zone has only been used once in the City, staff believes a brief explanation of the
MXD zone is warranted here. Unlike other zones, the MXD zone has very few standards spelled
out in our ordinance. For example, there are no setbacks, height restrictions, or maximum
building coverages. The lack of standards was intentional to allow for design flexibility for
applicants who could essentially propose their own standards through a development agreement.
Another unique aspect of the MXD zone is that all allowed uses within the zone, except for
public utility installations, are reviewed as permitted uses and not conditional uses. That means
that the site plan approval process will be handled only by staff. These unique aspects of the
MXD zone make detailed development agreements for developments within the MXD zone
essential in staff’s view.

ZT-5-2023
West Valley City
A zone text change to the Zoning (Title 7) and Condominium (Title 9) Ordinances to
comply with State code changes from HB 406 and SB 174.

City staff is proposing an ordinance change to amend the Zoning (Title 7) and Condominium
(Title 9) Ordinances to comply with State code changes from HB 406 and SB 174. Both HB 406
and SB 174 were passed by the Utah Legislature in the 2023 General Session of the Utah
Legislature and later signed by the governor. Copies of these bills are included with this report.
Among other changes, the bills prohibit cities from requiring an applicant to post an
improvement completion bond for landscaping on private property (HB 406) and prohibit the
legislative body of a municipality from reviewing subdivision applications for single-family
dwellings, two-family dwellings, or townhomes (SB 174).

Instead of having a separate subdivision process for subdivision applications for single-family
dwellings, two-family dwellings, or townhomes, staff is proposing that the subdivision review
process be amended to not require the approval of the City Council for any subdivision,
especially since these decisions are administrative.

Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval. The ordinance should be approved as proposed by staff to comply with State
      law.

   2. Continuance.

               speaking with the City Manager, he said staff has decided to continue this

               Commissioner Winters said he would assume the issue with the landscaping bond
               would be being unable to open a business because the weather prevents them
               from installing it. Steve said that the bills passed by the Utah Legislature prohibit
               the City from posting a bond on finishing the required landscaping. Therefore, the
               landscaping would be code enforcement issue.
               Commissioner Winters asked Steve what the benefit would be for subdivisions

               pressure the Council feels to deny a subdivision from those who speak in
               opposition during the meetings because it is an administrative decision. Therefore,
               the City can’t deny it if it meets the minimum requirements.

BACKGROUND

Trevor Gasser, is requesting a plat amendment for lot 4A of the Shoppes at Lake Park Phase 2
Subdivision. The purpose for the amendment is to create an additional building lot from the last

STAFF/AGENCY CONCERNS:

There are no staff or agency concerns with this application.

ISSUES:

The Shoppes at Lake Park Phase 2 was recorded with the Salt Lake County Recorder’s Office in
June 2007. Lots 4 and 5 were platted as part of that subdivision. In 2016, a boundary adjustment
was approved between said lots to increase the size of lot 5. The adjustment allowed for the
Burlington clothing store to be constructed. The result of that adjustment left lot 4A with an area
of approximately 4.3 acres.
Mr. Gasser is now proposing to add an additional building lot and is seeking a plat amendment of
lot 4A. The proposed plat amendment will consist of 2 lots on 4.3 acres. Lot 4B will comprise

This property was originally approved for a AT&T fiber optic cable switching facility in 2000
(C-5-2000). This was under the original R-1-8 zoning with the original home in a 20’ x 20’
fenced area. Since that time the home was destroyed by fire (2005) and the property was rezoned

property in 2012 and at this time would like to demolish the structure and expand the utility
facility on the lot with additional equipment including a backup generator.

The RB zone requires a 25’ front yard setback and that area will be landscaped in a similar
manner to the existing site with lawn, trees and shrubs. The access points and spacing is
currently being reviewed by Public Works for the change of use. Also, sometime in the last year

trees may be relocated outside of the fenced in area to spaces that are more visible. Screening is
also required adjacent to the residential uses. Since those areas are also designated as General
Commercial in the General Plan the application is requesting to do a solid Trex composite fence
in lieu of a masonry wall.

A backup generator is also proposed for this facility. That equipment can not be located in the
10’ setback adjacent to residential and staff also recommends that this equipment be spaced as
far away as possible to the adjacent residential uses and should be located more central to the
facility.

Lastly, all lighting throughout the facility and must be designed to meet section 7-9-113 of the
City Code and not disturb the adjacent residential uses.
