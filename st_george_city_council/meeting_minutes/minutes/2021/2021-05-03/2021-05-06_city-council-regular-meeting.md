ADOPT THE PRELIMINARY FISCAL YEAR 2021-2022 BUDGET/RESOLUTION:
     Consider approval of a resolution accepting and adopting the Preliminary
     Fiscal Year 2021-2022 Budget of the City of St. George and to set the dates
     for the required public hearings.

      Budget and Financial Planning Manager Deanna Brklacich briefly outlined the budget
      process. Recently, the City received the Distinguished Budget Award for the second
      year. She noted there are significant increases in the recommended budget; this is a
      catch-up year. The FY2021-22 budget is $415,321,000, a $10 million increase from
      last year’s budget. She outlined projects that were cut out of last year’s budget that
      have been put back in to the preliminary budget after which she outlined the
      requested and recommended positions. A property tax increase is not being
      requested; however, some City fees are proposed to increase. The preliminary
      budget will be available for viewing on the City’s website tonight She recommended
      setting the public hearing dates for the final budget as June 3rd and June 17th,
      2021.

      Councilmember Curtis thanked Ms. Brklacich and her team for their work.

      Mayor Randall wanted to let people know that the City cut many things due to Covid
      so this is a catch-up year to put those projects back into the budget.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard noted the budget is a policy document. He mentioned
      that the City has not increased property taxes in many years; however, he
      anticipates that there will be a need for an increase, particularly with public safety, in
      the future. A tax increase will be proposed only if absolutely necessary.

      MOTION:
          A motion was made by Councilmember Hughes to set the public
                     hearings for June 3rd and June 17th, 2021.
      SECOND:
          The motion was seconded by Councilmember Larkin.

ZONE CHANGE/ORDINANCE:
     Consider approval of an ordinance changing the zone from RE-37.5
     (Residential Estates 37,500 Sq. Ft. lot sizes) to R-1-10 (Residential with a
     minimum lot size of 10,000 sq. ft.) on .076 acres located on the corner of
     Del Mar Drive and Damascus Drive; the project is known as Lot 97
     Bloomington Ranches Subdivision No. 3. Case No. 2021-ZC-032

      Planner Mike Hadley presented the request to change the zone from RE-37.5 to R-1-
      10 on .076 acres located on the corner of Del Mar Drive and Damascus Drive; the
      project is known as Lot 97 Bloomington Ranches Subdivision No. 3. He provided a
      PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics: Vicinity map; Zoning map;
      General plan map; and photos of the property. The Planning Commission
      recommended approval. The discussion during the public hearing at the Planning
      Commission meeting was that with an R-1-10 zoning, the applicant could get three
      lots and not just divide the property. He explained that is a possibility in an R-1-10
      zone if setbacks are met.

      Deputy City Attorney Paula Houston explained an agreement would have to be
      entered into to restrict going to three lots.

      Mr. Hadley commented the plan is to split the lot into two lots so that another family
      member can build a home on the vacant lot. This request had been heard
      previously.

      Councilmember McArthur is concerned that approving this request would set a
      precedent. This is one area the people expect to be larger lots; this is one area he
      believes should be preserved. If there is an interest in adding additional homes, he
      believes this can be addressed with ADU’s.

      After being asked by Mayor Randall, Mr. Hadley stated if a home was built behind
      this one, it would front Del Mar Drive. He then outlined adjacent zoning.

      Councilmember Hughes commented there are some ways to do accessory dwelling
      units; however, it might be better to have the two lots on this particular parcel.

      Mayor Randall noted she does not vote; however, she commented that other large
      lots in the Ranches can only have an accessory dwelling unit if they wanted a home
      behind due to a fire truck trying to get back there. This is the only lot that would not
      be a flag lot.

      Mr. Hadley noted the property does have frontage along Del Mar.

      After being asked by Councilmember Larkin, Community Development Director John
      Willis explained the request was denied previously due to the character of the area
      and animal rights. It was a similar conversation at the previous meeting.

      A gentleman from the audience asked to speak. Mayor Randall noted this item is not
      a public hearing.
