GPZ-3-2020
Joe Salisbury
2892 S Decker Lake Dr.
General plan change from commercial/light manufacturing to high density residential and
zone change from A and M to RM
16.05 acres

This application was first submitted last year. At the request of the applicant, this application was
continued to no date certain to allow the applicant time to revise the proposal.

Joe Salisbury with DAI has submitted a General Plan/zone change application for 16.05 acres of
property located at 2892 S Decker Lake Dr. The property is currently zoned A (agriculture,
minimum lot size ½ acre) and M (manufacturing) with a General Plan designation of
commercial/light manufacturing. The proposed zone is RM (multiple unit dwelling residential)
and the proposed General Plan designation is high density residential (12 to 20 units/acre).

Surrounding zones include A and RM to the west, A and M to the north, M to the east and M and
RM to the south. Surrounding land uses include the E Gate Apartments, Decker Lake Youth
Detention Facility and Decker Lake Park to the west and north and office uses like Petzl and the
Department of Homeland Security to the east and south. The subject property is currently vacant.

The latest proposal includes a total of 286 rental housing units including 98 rear-load, 3-bedroom
townhomes and 188 stacked flat apartments (94 1-bedroom units and 94 2-bedroom units) that
can also function as townhomes. The overall project density is 17.8 units/acre. As seen in the
floor plans, a 2-bedroom stacked flat unit above a 1-bedroom stacked flat unit can be combined
or rented together to create a 3-bedroom townhome unit. All buildings are 2 story buildings.
Each townhome has a 2-car garage and each stacked flat unit has a 1-car garage. The attached
concept plan, application materials, and presentation from the applicant provide details on and
support for the proposed project.

By way of background, a General Plan/zone change request was made on this same property
back in 2013 by DAI for a 368-unit apartment development with a density of 23 units/acre. The
Planning Commission recommended denial to the City Council and the City Council denied the
application.

In reviewing this application, staff suggests that there are two primary questions to answer:

   1. Is this the right location for an apartment development?

   2. Assuming the location is appropriate, does the quality of the project further City goals?

These two questions are explored below.
Is this the right location for an apartment development?

When considering this question, there are many aspects to consider including recent code
changes, transportation infrastructure, neighboring land uses and balancing City objectives
across the City. Each of these topics is addressed below.

Recent Code Changes

One consideration is that last year, the City amended the criteria to determine whether a property
would be eligible to apply for the RM zone. Prior to this change, the subject property was not
eligible to be rezoned to the RM zone. The recent code change made properties located on the
west side of Redwood Road and within ½ mile of the Decker Lake or Redwood Junction light
rail stations eligible to be rezoned to RM. The subject property is within ½ mile of both stations.
While the property is eligible for RM zoning, the City may still deny requests for RM zoning.

Transportation Infrastructure
One of the other code changes made last year requires a traffic impact study prepared by a
registered traffic engineer for projects with 100 or more dwelling units that are requesting RM
zoning. The traffic impact study submitted by the applicant is included with this report. This
study was done based on the concept plan from last year which included 320 units. The key
findings from the traffic impact study were:

      “All study intersections are currently operating at an acceptable LOS during the evening
       peak hour in existing (2020) background conditions.
      All study intersections are anticipated to operate at an acceptable LOS during the evening
       peak hour with project traffic added.
      All study intersections are anticipated to operate at an acceptable LOS during the evening
       peak hour in future (2025) background and plus project conditions.”

Neighboring Land Use

Neighboring uses that seem the most compatible with the proposed residential development
include the E Gate Apartments and Decker Lake Park. The Decker Lake Youth Detention
Facility and neighboring office space seem more compatible with the current General Plan
designation of commercial/light manufacturing.

Another land use consideration for the area is the number of existing or proposed apartments.
The neighboring E Gate Apartments include 304 units and the Apartments at Decker Lake on the
northeast corner of Decker Lake Dr. and 3100 S include 104 units. The City also recently
approved a General Plan/zone change application for the property on the northwest corner of
Decker Lake Dr. and 3100 S where 225 apartment units are proposed. Assuming the recently
approved apartment development is constructed along with the project proposed on the subject
property, there will be 919 units within ¼ mile of each other.

Balancing City Objectives Across the City

When reviewing a General Plan/zone change for apartments in a specific area of the City, the
Commission should consider not only site-specific issues but citywide goals as well. Given the
regular inquiries staff receives regarding apartment developments, it seems clear that apartments,
if allowed, would be built just about anywhere in the City. However, doing so would preclude
other important objectives from being accomplished and would put our housing stock out of
balance. Therefore, deciding where to approve zoning for new apartment developments takes
careful consideration of both area specific issues and citywide goals.

Below are statements within the General Plan in quotations that highlight the need for a
comprehensive view when considering a zone change for apartments along with commentary
from staff:

      “In areas where retail development is aging (at least 30 years old) and underperforming
       and where transit is readily accessible, consider the addition of some multi-family
       residential as part of a redevelopment strategy to add more retail demand.” This objective
       was part of the reason for approving apartments on the old Kmart site on Redwood Road
       as well as the apartments at Valley Fair Mall.

      “Maintain zoning in areas currently zoned for office or industrial development to allow
       opportunities for new commercial development.” This seems to be the part of the reason
       that a request for apartments on this site was denied in 2013.

       “West Valley City should work to maintain at least one job for each person in the labor
       force within the City.”

      “When considering rezone requests for high density residential development, the
       proximity to transit, the size of the project and the proposed level of quality (i.e. exterior
       and interior finishes and amenities) should be primary factors for evaluation along with
       other site-specific considerations.” The proposed level of quality is addressed later in this
       report.

      “Redevelop blighted multi-family residential with higher density residential.” Examples
       of this would be the redevelopment that occurred on Harvey Street and in Fairbourne
       Station. There are other areas in the City where this approach will be needed.

In summary, when considering where to approve apartment developments, the City should
balance considering area specific issues which have already been discussed, providing retail
support within retail areas, preserving land for job creating uses, raising the bar on the quality of
new development, supporting transit and redeveloping blighted areas. This site has no
neighboring retail, would consume land that could be used for job creating uses and is vacant or
not needing redevelopment. The proposed level of quality is addressed later in this report. A
residential use on this site would likely support transit to some degree.

Does the quality of the project further City goals?

City Code requires requests for the RM zone to be accompanied with a development agreement
proposal that addresses dwelling unit sizes, exterior materials, architecture, landscaping and
project amenities. Each of these items is addressed below.

Dwelling Unit Sizes

The proposed project includes a mix of one, two, and three-bedroom units. The table below
compares the proposed unit sizes with the minimum sizes required in the ordinance.

Exterior Materials

For materials, the City’s multi-unit residential design standards state: “All multi-unit residential
Building exteriors shall be brick, stucco, stone, tile, metal panels or fiber cement siding. With the
exception of brick or stone which may be used on the entire Building exterior, no single material
shall comprise more than 60 percent of a Building’s exterior. Where stucco, tile, metal panels or
fiber cement siding are used, at least 20 percent of the Building’s exterior shall be brick or stone.
Wood and vinyl may be used as trim, soffits and/or accent materials only.”

The applicant is proposing a combination of fiber cement siding and at least 20% stone. The
fiber cement siding would comprise more than 60% of the exteriors, so a development agreement
exception would be needed here without introducing a third material or using more stone.

Architecture

Staff conducted a preliminary review of the proposed architecture. Given the style of the
buildings, the proposed roof pitch is 3:12. The minimum roof pitch in the code is 6:12 so the
applicant is requesting an exception in the development agreement.

The ordinance also requires each unit to have its own patio or balcony of at least 60 square feet.
All units are labeled as having over 60 square feet of patio space in the form of porches.
However, the two-bedroom units that are not on the building ends have a 4’ to 4½’ wide by just
under 13’ deep space by the front door that is functionally too narrow for a patio without
blocking the front door. Staff suggests a balcony on the back of the second floor to create a more
functional outdoor space for these units.

Landscaping

Earlier this month, the City adopted updated standards for landscaping and recreation space for
multi-unit residential developments. For projects under 20 units/acre, the minimum landscaping
and recreation space percentage is 50%. The latest concept plan shows a total of 42%. Through a
development agreement, a percentage less than 50% may be approved.
If the Commission believes that the amount of landscaping and recreation space should not be
reduced, this percentage could be increased by reducing the number of driveways to individual
units, reducing the amount of parking, and/or reducing the number of units.

Project Amenities

As mentioned previously in this report, the proposed project amenities are unique and extensive
and exceed the City’s requirements for amenities. For a project of this size, the ordinance
requires five amenities. The proposed project includes 11 amenities listed in the ordinance:
outdoor pool, courtyard, club house, fitness room, community game room, tot lot, outdoor fire
pit, hot tub, walking/exercise trail, electric vehicle charging stations, and outdoor covered
pavilion. In addition to the items listed in the ordinance, the project also includes a boulder cave,
waterfall, lazy river, and water slide associated with the pool; a dog park; lawn gaming area; a
hammock lounge; a bike maintenance station; and a 6’ vinyl fence around the perimeter except
along Decker Lake Drive. The applicant is also offering to invest $540,000 in amenities to
improve Decker Lake Park. Specific improvements proposed include 2 pickleball courts, a ropes
tower, 4 picnic pavilions, and parking lot striping.

Interior finishes and amenities include solid surface countertops, wood-style flooring, smart
home technology (smart light switches, programmable smart thermostat, and smart door lock), 9’
ceilings on main floors; 96% efficient furnaces, low-flow toilets, and garages that are pre-wired
for an electric vehicle charging station.

Other Considerations

Parking

For parking, the ordinance requires 2 spaces per unit but allows the Planning Commission to
reduce the amount of parking to 1.5 stalls per unit for one-bedroom units and 1.75 stalls per unit
for two-bedroom units if the Commission finds that reducing the parking “is not detrimental to
the surrounding areas and will enhance the proposed development”. The applicant is proposing
to meet the required 2 stalls per unit and provide additional parking in the form of driveways for
an additional 237 driveway stalls (overall 2.9 stalls per unit).

While staff believes that a sufficient amount of parking is proposed, the distribution of parking
can be improved. Some of the stacked apartment buildings don’t have all of the required parking
within close proximity. If this application is approved, the parking distribution should be
addressed as part of the conditional use review.

GPZ-7-2021
Utah Auto Brokers
3229 S Redwood Road
General plan change from low density residential to general commercial and zone change
from R-1-12 to C-2
1.29 acres

             Thiago Ferreira, with Utah Auto Brokers, has submitted a General Plan/zone
             change application for two parcels totaling 1.29 acres located at 3229 S Redwood
             Rd. The parcels are currently zoned R-1-12 (single unit dwelling residential,
             minimum lot size 12,000 sq. ft.) with a General Plan designation of low density
             residential (3 to 4 units/acre). The proposed zone is C-2 (general commercial) and
             the proposed General Plan designation is general commercial.

             The surrounding zoning includes C-2 to the west, RM to the north, R-1-4 to the
             east, and R-1-12 to the south. Lester Street is adjacent to these two parcels on the
             east. Surrounding land uses include a Beto’s restaurant and an asphalt paving
             business to the west, the Vivante multi-family housing development to the north,
             and single unit dwellings to the east and south.

             The subject parcels include a single unit dwelling located on about 0.18 acres in
             the south-west corner, and the remainder of the property has been used as a
             construction equipment and supply yard for the adjacent asphalt paving business.
             In 1993 the property to the west was granted legal non-conforming status by the
             Board of Adjustment (BOA) for the asphalt paving use, which had been in
               operation since the 1960’s. The BOA also granted an expansion of the non-
               conforming use onto the subject property. The area is generally not improved. A
               masonry wall was required and constructed along the eastern boundary of the
               property as a condition of the non-conforming use expansion.

               Included with this report is a letter and concept plan from the applicant describing
               the proposed use as well as reasons to support the application. The applicant is
               proposing to develop the subject property, as well as redevelop a majority of the
               adjacent property to the west, for a used auto sales complex. In the proposal, the
               existing restaurant along Redwood Road would remain, as well as an existing
               accessory storage building along the northern property line. There is also an
               existing single unit dwelling south of the Beto’s that would remain and be
               converted into a sales office. Four additional office buildings would be
               constructed throughout the site. The applicant plans to use one of the buildings,
               and the other four would be leased out to other auto sales businesses. Access to
               the property would be from Redwood Road. The existing access from Lester
               Street would be closed.

               During the study session the Planning Commission had a couple of
               comments/questions about this application. Staff spoke with the applicant after
               the meeting and those items are addressed below:

       There was some concern about commercial zoning extending all the way to Lester
        Street. Some of the Commissioners asked if the applicant has considered doing single
        family dwellings along Lester Street. The applicant indicated that he has not considered
        this for a couple of reasons. First, he said that the cost of the land is such that developing
        for single unit dwellings does not make sense. He also said that residential real estate
        development is not something he is familiar with.
       Another comment or concern was how well an auto sales use would do on a property
        with not much visibility or frontage on Redwood Road. The applicant indicated that
        while some visibility is important, their company’s auto sales generally stem from
        online activity. He said that online sales had already been increasing steadily over the
        past few years, and that the pandemic has only accelerated this.

If the Planning Commission feels that a change in zoning is appropriate for this property, there
are several site issues that staff recommends be addressed in a development agreement. These
items have been included under staff alternative 1 below.

Staff Alternatives:

    1. Approval. This application should be approved subject to a development agreement that
       includes the following standards:
           a. The Redwood Road Streetscape shall be installed by the applicant along the entire
              frontage of the proposed project area, including in front of the existing single unit
              dwelling.
           b. The exteriors of the existing buildings that are to remain shall be painted and/or
                 repaired where needed.

    2. Continuance. This application should be continued for reasons determined during the

    3. Denial. The R-1-12 zone and low density residential General Plan designation should

ISSUES:

To address the many issues related to this subdivision, staff would like to focus on the following:

Overall Subdivision design:

       The subdivision consists of 20 lots on 6.4 acres. This equates to an overall density of 3.1
       units per acre. Lot sizes range from 8,000 square feet to 11,118 square feet. The average
       lot size has been calculated at 8,775 square feet.

       The subdivision design and street pattern takes advantage of the existing stub street to the
       east and will provide two stub streets to the west for future development. Due to the
       locations of these streets and the corresponding lot patterns, lot depths in some locations
       are rather shallow. Staff has been working with the developer and future builder regarding
       setbacks and home designs.

       The subdivision is amending certain lots in the Brookfield Subdivision to the east. The
       reason these lots are being incorporated into the subdivision is to resolve boundary issues
       regarding the existing fence. Rather than remove fences that have been in place for over
       30 years, the developer is willing to give up a few feet of property so the fences can stay.
       Incorporating these lots into the subdivision would be easier than individual boundary
       agreements with each of those property owners. These owners have been contacted and
       agree to them being incorporated into the subdivision plat.

Access:

       Access to the subdivision will be gained from an existing street in the Brookfield
       Subdivision to the east. For the first phase, the subdivision is planned to only have 20 lots.
       The Fire Code allows you to have 30 homes on a single point of access. The proposed
       subdivision will provide additional access points to the west for future development. The
       street cross section will meet today’s 54-foot right-of-way standard. However, as streets
       in the Brookfield Subdivision to the east were not constructed to the standards that are used
       today, there will be slight transition to make this connection work. This will be coordinated
       with the City’s Engineering Division.
      All new streets will be dedicated and improved to the City’s standard 54-foot right-of-way.
      This cross section includes a 5-foot sidewalk and 5-foot parkstrip. The street network in
      this phase as well as future phases will follow this same standard. Dedication and
      improvements along 4100 South will also be required per City ordinance.

Grading/Drainage:

      The proposed subdivision slopes downward approximately 30 feet from the railroad to
      4100 South. However, the drop is over a distance of approximately 900 feet. To address
      the grade difference between lots, individual lot grading plans may be required prior to the
      issuance of a building permit. Individual lot retention will be required to help alleviate
      potential runoff problems from one lot to the next.

      The City Engineering Division will thoroughly review the grading and drainage plans as
      the subdivision moves through the review process. Should there be problems or concerns
      with regards to existing or proposed grades, staff will address these with the developer and
      builder during final plat review. During the course of final plat review, the engineering
      office will work with the developers engineer to understand how the site will be contoured
      and what retaining methods if any, will be used to ensure slope stability between
      residences.

      Parcel A is shown as a detention basin. Staff is recommending that the developer and
      Engineering Division see if there is a way to avoid the basin altogether. These basins are
      difficult to maintain and are generally and eyesore in the subdivision. If on site retention
      is being done and other low impact development options are available, staff would like to
      see those explored.

Groundwater:

      There has been no new development in this area since the Brookfield subdivision was
      developed in the late 80’s and early 90’s. There are no indications in those subdivisions of
      any groundwater. In addition, groundwater was not encountered in the Park Vista
      Subdivision to the north of 4100 South to a depth of 11 feet. However, in order to
      determine if basements will be allowed a ground water investigation will be needed and
      required as part of the final plat application.

Fencing:

      The west boundary of the subdivision is adjacent to agriculturally zoned property. City
      ordinance will require a 6-foot chain link fence to be installed along these parcels.
      However, since property to the west is currently being considered for a rezone to the RE
      zone (if approved by the City Council), staff would not require fencing with this phase, but
      will with future phases to the west as they would be adjacent to the A zone.
      Zoning Ordinance requires a 6’ tall masonry wall for double frontage lots with a rear yard
      adjacent to arterial streets. Since the City recently installed a consistent style masonry
      wall along 4100 South between Bangerter Highway and 5460 West, staff recommends
      the same style of masonry wall be installed for this subdivision.

Railroad:

      An existing rail line is located at the south boundary of the subdivision. While developing
      the Park Vista Subdivision to the north of 4100 South, a letter was submitted to the City
      when that property was proposed for rezoning. The letter expressed concerns by Union
      Pacific about new residential development near the rail line. Primarily, the concerns are
      noise, vibration, trespassing along the railroad etc.

      Staff has conveyed this information to the developer. Although these concerns are valid,
      there is nothing that would prohibit residential development from locating here. Staff will
      recommend that a note be placed on the plat similar to what the City requires for
      agricultural properties. This note will simply identify this area as being next to an active
      rail line and that there may be noises, vibrations etc., that could affect residential living.

Overpressure Zone:

      The proposed subdivision is located in the outer ring of the overpressure zone. City
      ordinance will require that certain construction standards, specifically special windows be
      installed on all new dwellings. This condition will be addressed with the building permit
      for all single-family dwellings.

proposed on the walls of the building as well.
