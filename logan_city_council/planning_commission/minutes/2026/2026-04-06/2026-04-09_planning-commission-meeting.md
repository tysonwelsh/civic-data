PUBLIC HEARING:

PC 26-014 Take 5 Oil Change [Design Review] Jared Whatcott/Blue Lube Properties LLC,
authorized agent/owner is requesting a Design Review Permit to construct a new 1,900 square foot
commercial building located at 1190 South Main Street in the Commercial (COM) Zone; TIN 02-088-
0021, in the Woodruff Neighborhood.

Staff: Community Development Director Russ Holley presented the proposal for a 1,900 sq. ft.
vehicle-oriented service building (oil change facility) where customers remain in their vehicles while
services are performed. The building includes three service bays, an office, supply room, and
restroom. The Commercial zone permits vehicle service uses without proximity or spacing
restrictions.

R. Holley noted that the north and south facades do not meet fenestration and articulation
requirements as proposed.

The Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT) required removal of the Main Street access. Existing
easements between the subject property and adjacent parcels to the north and east may affect
access. The applicant must review these easements, and any changes to site layout or access will
require Planning Commission review.

The proposed stormwater detention area may count as useable open space if designated with a
maximum 3:1 slope and landscaped with grass.

Proponent: Jonathan Bingham stated that revisions were made to the north and south façades to
meet fenestration requirements and asked for clarification on frontage and stormwater landscaping
requirements.

Public: Alex Bearnson stated that an existing easement allows ingress and egress to Main Street for
the properties to the east and questioned UDOT’s authority to remove access. Commissioner Lewis
clarified that easement matters are between property owners and UDOT, not the City.

Brice Cutler (Jiffy Lube) raised concerns about shared access and asked why proximity restrictions
for similar businesses are not applied, as with short-term rentals.

Commission: R. Holley confirmed the building meets frontage requirements and reiterated
stormwater design standards. He also noted fencing is permitted if it does not encroach on
easements or right-of-way.

In response to questions about proximity restrictions, R. Holley explained that such regulations would
require a code amendment and would only apply to future projects.

Commissioner Lucero suggested clarifying that any access changes resulting from easement review
must return to the Planning Commission for approval.

The commission amended Engineering Condition #33 to add:
“Access to the site is currently under the jurisdiction of a private easement. If the design of the project
changes based on resolution of that easement with UDOT, the project shall return to the Planning
Commission for review.”

PC 26-015 Delta Hangar Expansion [Design Review] Cory Goettsche/Logan Cache Airport
Authority, authorized agent/owner is requesting a Design Review permit to construct six to eight new
airplane hangar buildings at the Logan Cache Airport, located at approximately 2500 North 900 West,
in the Airport (AP) Zone; TIN 04-062-0050, in the Bridger Neighborhood.

buildings accommodating up to 16 hangars, along with a new taxiway, on approximately three acres
in the southwest portion of the airport. Building sizes range from 4,500 to 11,825 sq. ft.

discouraging birds and wildlife. In place of traditional trees and shrubs, materials such as rock or
mulch ground cover, decorative boulders, or native grasses and plants are recommended. One

R. Holley noted that an additional design feature is required on the south façade of the buildings
adjacent to 2500 North to meet design standards.

on the airport hangar waiting list.

Andy Checkets stated they will work with the building manufacturer to incorporate the required design
feature on the south façade

airport expansion and to serve those on the waiting list.
