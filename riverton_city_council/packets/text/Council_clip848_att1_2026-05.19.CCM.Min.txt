36          Stormwater Discharge 12600 South CRA Project
37
38   Kevin Hicks, City Manager, presented a preliminary proposal to allow a developer within the 12600
39   South CRA Project to direct storm water from a future development into an existing City retention
40   pond that has excess capacity. He explained the developer would pay all associated infrastructure
41   costs and that future agreements would address maintenance, engineering standards, and liability
42   protections. Councilmembers and the Mayor supported the concept, viewing it as an efficient use of
43   existing infrastructure that could encourage desirable development in the area, while also
44   discussing the need for proper safeguards and confirming that City engineers had determined the
45   pond has adequate capacity.

15            Main Park Entrance Discussion
16
17   Cary Necaise, Public Works Director, discussed potential improvements for the vacant area at the
18   entrance to the Main City Park following removal of the skate park, emphasizing the desire to
19   eliminate the current dirt lot and create an attractive, functional space before upcoming summer
20   events. He presented eight conceptual options ranging from simple sod replacement and additional
21   trees to decorative landscaping, pathways, parking, recreational courts, community gardens,
22   cornhole areas, and a conservation garden. He noted that the simplest options could potentially be
23   completed before Town Days and would provide additional seating space for attendees.
24
25   Councilmembers generally favored moving forward with Option #1 as a temporary but visually
26   appealing placeholder while longer-term plans for the area are considered. Several
27   Councilmembers expressed interest in exploring future recreational amenities such as futsal or
28   street hockey courts if outside funding became available. Mr. Necaise confirmed that simpler
29   options could likely fit within the current parks budget, whereas more elaborate proposals would
30   require future budget planning and additional Council discussion.

33            Park Water Use and Service Level Discussion
34
35   Mr. Necaise City explained that parks are among the City’s largest water users and emphasized the
36   need to balance conservation efforts with community expectations. His plan is to prioritize
37   preserving trees, athletic fields, gathering spaces, and cemetery landscaping, while reducing
38   irrigation in lower-use areas such as park strips, detention ponds, and passive open spaces. He
39   noted that recent upgrades to the City’s irrigation system, including weather-based controls and
40   moisture monitoring, have already improved efficiency and are expected to help meet reduction
41   goals. Parks and landscapes were categorized into four service levels, with irrigation reductions
42   ranging from 10% in high-priority athletic and civic areas to 25% in low-priority open spaces and
43   retention areas.
44
45   Councilmembers discussed how the City should manage splash pads during drought conditions,
46   noting that splash pads use culinary water that is not recycled. Staff proposed reducing operating
47   hours and potentially alternating operating days at certain splash pads, especially the less-used Old
48   Farm location, while still maintaining recreational opportunities for families. Councilmembers
49   generally supported reducing hours to approximately noon to 8 p.m. and exploring staggered
50   schedules between splash pad locations. Councilmembers emphasized the importance of
51   communicating the City’s conservation efforts clearly to residents and supported using maps and
     Page | 3 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1   visuals to show how water reductions are being implemented across parks and public spaces.

 3   City Council Agenda Review
 4
 5   Mayor Buroker suggested that the Council could discuss, in advance of the formal public hearing, a
 6   proposed budget adjustment for police compensation that had been brought forward by the police
 7   chief following internal review and discussions with officers. The revised proposal would provide
 8   officers with a 1.5% cost-of-living adjustment and a 4% step increase, totaling a 5.5% increase
 9   funded through the police fund rather than the general fund.. Councilmembers responded positively,
10   emphasizing appreciation for the flexible and transparent budget process and noting that it allowed
11   adjustments based on updated information without disrupting the overall budget timeline.
12
13   Mr. Necaise reported that a City monitoring system detected a reverse flow alert in the culinary
14   water system, which led crews to investigate and discover an illegal cross-connection between a
15   property’s irrigation system and the drinking water supply. The connection was immediately shut off,
16   and in coordination with the Division of Water Quality, the City issued a precautionary boil order
17   while water samples are tested, with no adverse results confirmed yet due to standard testing
18   timelines. He emphasized the issue was caught early through upgraded technology and that all
19   residents must follow the boil order because the system is interconnected, including areas partially
20   served by wells. He also noted that future prevention efforts may include installing additional
21   backflow protection devices, such as dual check valves or upgraded meter yokes, to better isolate
22   systems and reduce the risk of similar incidents.

34            Tentatively Adopted Budget
35
36   Nick Geer, Administrative Services Director, discussed an amendment to the tentatively adopted
37   budget to incorporate a revised compensation proposal for the Riverton Police Department. The
38   proposal would provide all officers with a 1.5% cost-of-living adjustment and a 4% step increase,
39   resulting in a total 5.5% pay adjustment. He explained that the adjustment would be funded
40   through the City’s RLESA fund, reducing its fund balance from about 5% to 1.5% for the year,
41   while still maintaining some capacity for unexpected needs or future adjustments.
42
43   Council members expressed support for the change, noting its role in retaining officers,
44   maintaining competitiveness in the job market, and appropriately valuing current staff.
45
46   Councilmember Pierucci MOVED to amend the tentatively approved budget to include the
47   revised COLA and step adjustments for the Riverton Police Department. Councilmember
48   Smith SECONDED the motion. Mayor Buroker called for a vote; the vote was as follows:
49   Haymond-Yes, Johnson-Yes, Pierucci-Yes, and Smith-Yes. The motion passed unanimously.
