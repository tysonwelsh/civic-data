 1.     TEMPORARY HOUSING ACCOMMODATION DISCUSSION (Public Meeting –
        Administrative). Discuss the extension of the temporary housing accommodation
        program and proposed changes.

       Mr. Messel led a discussion on the temporary housing accommodation pilot program. He
       provided that this program was introduced to help with housing affordability which allowed
       family members to temporarily live in an RV parked at a family members home. The
       unintended consequences were unknown at the time it was adopted. It became apparent that
       this temporary housing pilot program was not as popular as it was intended to be.

Mr. Messel invited Mr. Reid Walkenhorst (Planner and staff member) to the podium to
provide data on the program. Mr. Walkenhorst provided that for 2024, the number of entitled
lots or buildable lots that are zoned residential is 4,300 - these lots could have a single-family
residence and an accessory dwelling, and the number of vacant lots are 900. Mr. Walkenhorst
provided the number of accessory dwellings that have been permitted is just over 150. He
pointed out that there is a large disparity between lots that have homes and could have
accessory dwellings on them, but they don’t as the owners are not building those accessory
dwellings. Mr. Walkenhorst explained that this program was an economical way to get
someone into a housing situation, if they were on owner-occupied property. Mr. Walkenhorst
introduced that the new draft is proposing to extend this program and include some language
around tiny homes. Furthermore, he shared that the international building codes are starting
to recognize tiny homes as residential living spaces and have dedicated sections in the code
on tiny homes.

Mr. Walkenhorst explained that for this program, you either had to have a home that was
already built on the property or have an active building permit. He explained that if there
was an active building permit, one was permitted to live in an RV on the property whilst it
was being built. However, he expressed that there are many who have purchased property
but do not have the financial resources to build a home or get a building permit. He vocalized
that there is a demand for people who own property to be able to live in an RV or a tiny home
on their property.

Mr. Messel adjoined by stating that there are two types of tiny homes: 1) Tiny homes on
wheels (THOW) and 2) Tiny homes on foundations (THOF). Mr. Messel mentioned that they
have been in communication with representatives from the Tiny Home Foundation or
organization and have been looking at industry standards. He expressed that at present a
THOF is allowed because it would be a home or accessory dwelling. Mr. Messel conveyed that
what has not been included in many jurisdictions and code is the THOW and there is a market.
Mr. Messel talked about the disparity of the number of accessory dwellings verses the number
of lots – one of the causes of this is the cost. He posed the question of whether a THOW is
more affordable. Mr. Messel wanted to bring this up for discussion with the commissioners
to see what their thoughts were on THOWS and temporary housing accommodation. He
provided them with different options or scenarios.

A discussion ensued amongst the commissioners, Mr. Messel, Ms. Hales, Mr. Walkenhorst and
Mr. Kurt Dampf (Building Official) regarding the temporary housing accommodation pilot
program. Queries and issues were brought up such as the difference between an RV and a
THOW and the unintended consequences that may arise in the unincorporated parts of the
County should THOW’s be permitted. Sunset timing and temporary nature of utilities were
raised. A pedestal inspection, septic requirements, and utilities connections as per current
code was discussed. Septic limitations, costs and underlying concerns were raised that would
be a challenge to the pilot program. It was discussed whether the pilot program should be
extended another year or so with modifications of adding THOWS as another option to RV’s.
Another modification to the program that was brought forward was whether the temporary

2.    AGRITOURISM DISCUSSION (Public Meeting – Administrative). Review Agritourism
      draft language and discuss supportive measures to encourage growth in this market
      segment.

     Mr. Reid Walkenhorst stated that staff are currently working on a draft about agritourism
     and would like the commissioner’s input. Mr. Messel introduced Mr. Matthew Watson
     (Community Park Relations Manager – Greater Zion Tourism Office) to the commissioners
     and verbalized that they have been in discussions with him and are working on improving
     relations and working with the tourism department. Mr. Messel advised that Mr. Watson is
     incredibly involved at the State level and our department shares the common goals the state
     has with respect to agritourism and Astra tourism.

     Commissioner Kelsch commented against using terms ‘agritourism’ and ‘Astra tourism’,
     preferring the terms ‘farm tourism’ and ‘open sky tourism’ so it would be easier for people to
     understand.

     Mr. Walkenhorst explained that when attempting to define some of the key aspects of what
     we consider ‘Farm Tourism’ or ‘Agritourism’ to be - some of them would be more commercial
     in nature. Examples he gave were businesses like an overnight stay, a wedding venue, or a
     farm-to-table dinner venue for large groups of people. He continued by saying staff thought
     this would need to be some form of commercial agritourism. Mr. Walkenhorst stated that
     staff envisioned having a pathway for residents to monetize their agricultural properties and
     make it an easier process. Mr. Walkenhorst stated that at present agritourism is a permitted
     use in our code, but defining what those uses are is what needs to be done. He said that if
     residents wanted to monetize their agricultural properties our department did not want to
     burden or force them to go through a conditional use permit or a zone change process or
     necessarily get a county business license. He explained that staff produced the idea of listing
     these uses as permitted with standards and he provided an example. He stated that there
     could be instances where conditional use would be more appropriate and invited the
     commissioners to provide feedback on this topic.

     Mr. Walkenhorst said that some of these activities might involve serving alcohol in the
     vineyards, orchards and those types of winery tours are the types of things Washington
     County is trying to promote. He pointed out that another restriction for these properties that
     are potentially good for agritourism is that they are currently operating as Short-Term
     Rentals. In our code short-term rentals are not permitted to have special events.

     Mr. Scott added that the reason this was put in place was due to the Sky Ranch rental and all
     the parties that were happening there which caused a lot of complaints from the neighbors.

However, we are looking at different paths or silos where we could fit each thing having
different standards. Mr. Messel brought up the terms ‘Farm Stay’ or ‘Country Stay’ which
were being used a lot. He explained what this type of stay was, which was overnighting at a
vineyard, a dude ranch or some of these agricultural facilities. Mr. Messel suggested that for
these types of overnight stay places, we could keep them out of short-term rental and call it
a ‘Farm Stay’ as it is attached to these ag uses. In that way we are not making our STR code
more complicated. Mr. Messel provided an example of a resident that was unable to have a
dinner gathering event on her property in the country due to how our code is written.

Ms. Victoria Hales added that from a legal standpoint these can also turn into fake event
venues instead. She explained that we are trying to support family farming, not turn every
farm into an event venue. Ms. Hales stated the goal is to assist families in monetizing their
farms without flipping them into something that is no longer agriculture. She advised putting
in regulations that will keep a percentage of the venue as agricultural, so it does not turn into
just a wedding venue.

Commissioner Simonsen added that on paragraph b on the front page the term
‘Complimentary’ is used and he viewed that as part of the problem. He suggested that it needs
to be based on a percentage of revenue. He commented that if it brought in more than 50%
of the revenue it should be identified as a ‘Conditional Use’ not a ‘Farm’.

Mr. Messel brought up that there is a silo for this which mayhap could be called ‘Commercial
Agricultural’. Ms. Hales brought forward that it has always been considered in community
development that every farm is ‘Commercial Enterprise’. She advised that the problem is that
a lot of farmers here farm because of their heritage and do not break even, so they are looking
for another way to make money. She provided examples of them having jobs and having
farms. Ms. Hales if these are some ways where they could live on their farm and monetize it
to help support their farm uses. She gave an example of horse trailers, where people travel
with horses and have nowhere to stay with their horses. This is an example where existing
corral and farm could have some ‘Farm Stays’ and allow them to pay them money to stay
overnight and use the corrals for their horses.

Mr. Messel shared that they have had a lot of discussions with ranchers and equestrian people
about this very aspect and there is a big demand. Between Montana and Las Vegas and
California, there is a huge demand for equestrian stay facilities.

Commissioner Mark Owens read the USDA definition for ‘Agritourism’. He stated that he is
in favor of using the term ‘Agritourism’. He pointed out lines 13 & 14 and has the term
‘recreational’ and ‘national tourism’ and was not sure if these fell within the definition of
‘Agritourism’. He mentioned if line 13 is eliminated, then line 43 makes more sense. He also
pointed out lines 43, 65 & 70. He asked about line 70 and questioned the 5-acre set back and
whether it impacted residents who sold their goods at the junction of Pine Valley Highway
and 18 as well as the people who have fruit stands or stands in the front of their house.

     Mr. Messel explained the reason for the 5-acre setback was to stop this from happening in
     subdivisions.

     A discussion ensued between Mr. Messel, Ms. Hales, Commissioner Owens and Commissioner
     Kelsch regarding the sizes of lots and sale of produce on lots less than 5 acres. Agricultural
     footprints were raised along with how much can be produced in a greenhouse on a smaller
     piece of land. The legal question though was determining where allowances could be made
     for agrotourism sites where tourists would come to farms and pay money for other such uses
     as discussed earlier. Smaller sized lots would not be an option for this due to the close vicinity
     of neighbors. Examples were provided to clarify this. Therefore, the right zones need to be
     determined for this type of use. Major and Minor Home Occupations were talked about and
     how visibility of goods is not endorsed by the County. The issue of retail sales was brought
     up along with it the effects on neighborhoods - thus major and minor home occupations were
     brought to code to protect and keep the neighborhood from too much traffic and by turning
     into a full-blown retail business. In the upcoming year, when code is being redrafted, Mr.
     Messel and Mr. Walkenhorst will be coming up with different zones that allow retail in
     neighborhoods such as the ‘Village Commercial’ zone which brings the ‘Live work, work live’
     a possibility.

     Mr. Walkenhorst summarized that different formatting has been applied, the goal is to make
     it easier for someone to pick up the code and know what they are able to do with their
     property. Also coordinate with what the State and Tourism use with regards to terminology
     and staff would like for there to be as much flow and continuity as possible among the three
     entities.

     Mr. Matthew Watson talked about the State efforts wanting the agritourism site with a
     dashboard that lists many adventures throughout the State. There is a push on the state level
     to highlight agrotourism a little bit more.

     Mr. Walkenhorst stated that we live in a unique area within the country where we still enjoy
     dark skies and many communities around us are trying to leverage that. Astro tourism has
     generated a lot of interest and a lot of work being done by the Utah State extension office to
     promote Astro tourism and working with the Colorado Plateau. He explained that the state
     has a tremendous number of resources dedicated to Astro tourism. Ms. Hales brought up the
     four overlays that we have in place with additional regulations for lighting and we could start
     with those overlays which are: The Scenic Byway Overlay, The Pine Valley Overlay, New
     Harmony Valley Overlay, and the Kolob Overlay.

     Commissioner Owens suggested Colorado as another resource.
