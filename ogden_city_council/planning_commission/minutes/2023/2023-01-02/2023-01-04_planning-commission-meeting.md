   the R-2EC zone to a duplex. While the use of a duplex is permitted, the lot size remains
   nonconforming. She reviewed the history of the property, indicating the building was
   constructed in 1968 at which time the existing zoning was R-4 and allowed office
   buildings. It is now a nonconforming use as the zone has changed and the use of offices
   is no longer allowed in residential zones. Some of the interior rooms have already been
   remodeled to accommodate a residential use. She reviewed the standards the
   Commission is to consider when a change of nonconforming use is desired, indicating
   while the residential use is occupied continuously, the existing office was open only
   during business hours of 8am until 5pm. While the residential use is more conforming to
   the existing zone, the lot size is limited. She stated the use appears to be compatible
   with the surrounding area, but some of the parking in the rear yard should be removed to
   provide yard space for tenants. Remaining parking stalls should be clearly marked and
   any trash receptacle is to be screened. Staff recommends approval subject to the
   removal of asphalt in the rear to be replaced with landscaping as well as satisfying
   Ordinance requirements as follows:
   1. there needs to be more connectivity between the first floor and the first floor addition;

4. CBD Site Plan, for Apartments at YCC Family Crisis Center, 460 23rd Street.

  Mr. Tyler Hollen stated the YCC desires to remove their existing thrift store and office
  building and construct apartments for transitional housing. The building would
  accommodate 14 units with some flex units, allowing opportunities for different sizes of
  families. It will also accommodate some office space.

  Mr. Simpson stated this review is governed by the existing CBD Ordinance which requires
  the Planning Commission to determine whether the building material and architecture fit
  with the character of the downtown, but as the Make Ogden Plan Ordinance has been
  approved by the Commission but not yet codified, its regulations also should be considered
  as it is a pending ordinance. The project proposal is for apartments for the crisis center to



                                                                                             7
be located on two acres once existing buildings and parking are demolished. The proposed
building will be brick with metal and concrete accents, and is to be placed at the front
property line, satisfying the theme of the Make Ogden Plan. Parking is provided in the rear,
to be accessed from 23rd Street. Landscaping requirements can be satisfied and the
proposal shows a main covered entrance with access to the parking lot. Fencing will be
wrought iron and masonry to match the proposed building, providing needed security to the
site. He stated the code also requires additional doors, with Staff proposing exit only doors
being installed at both Ogden Avenue and 23rd Street. The architecture and finish material
fits with the downtown, with vertical columns of brick and punched windows. HVAC
equipment is to be located on top of the structure, and will be screened with a parapet wall.
Any additional utilities as well as trash receptacles are required to be screened. He also
noted existing shipping containers on the site are required to be removed prior to permits
being issued for the new construction. While the parking is short one stall according to the
current code, it meets the regulations of the pending code, and the Commission has the
ability to reduce required parking within the CBD.

He then reviewed standards of the pending Make Ogden Downtown Code, stating it calls for
increased windows along the street frontage and doors facing the street. The applicant has
prepared a revised façade, but Staff has not yet determined whether it would meet the
pending code. This plan also shows a mural, relocating doors to be closer to the corner as
well as increased fenestration. Staff is recommending additional entry doors be added at
both 23rd Street and Ogden Avenue, suggesting due to security, these could be exit-only,
prohibiting entry from the outside. The Make Ogden regulations for the Commercial Mixed

floor, but anticipates commercial uses on the ground floor, while this building has no
commercial space, noting the intent is that buildings have a presence on the public street.
Due to the need for security, the Commission may consider some flexibility regarding these
regulations. Staff is also recommending the Commission approve the reduction of one
parking space to meet current code requirements. Staff recommendation is for approval
subject to the following conditions:
1. Any on-site dumpsters be enclosed with a 6’ tall brick screening wall with a concrete cap

3. All existing shipping containers re to be remove form the site prior to permits being
    issued for the project;
4. The parking requirement under the current code be reduced from 21 stalls to 20; and
5. All departmental staff review comments be satisfied.

5. Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance Amendment, to add allowed uses in the Airport (A-C)
   Zone.

  Mr. Brad Morton and Jake Johnson stated they represent the property owner, Asilia
  Investments and had originally partnered with Kemp for development of property in this
  area. Once the Kemp portion was completed, the property has remained vacant and they
  have been unable to attract tenants. Their request is to amend the Airport Commercial
  section of the Zoning Ordinance to allow development of a business park with uses which
  are not necessarily aeronautically related but could still support uses in both the airport and
  the surrounding community, suggesting there is some ambiguity in the existing regulations.

  Mr. Rypien explained the request is to amend the AC Code to allow specific additional uses.
  He reviewed the current Code as well as the Airport Master Plan stating while the Code
  directs towards aeronautically related uses, many uses listed as permitted or conditional
  are not related to the industry. The petition is requesting specific uses be considered as
  well as eliminating the limited size restriction. He reviewed the petition and the changes
  proposed and felt the uses are appropriate. He also indicate the area zoned as AC is limited,
  with most of it being owned by Ogden City, and the City has the opportunity to prohibit a use
  on property they own if they desire. Staff recommends approval of the amendment to the
  language as proposed.

  Commissioner Safsten asked if the limited uses are tied to regulations of the FAA. Mr.
  Rypien answered the main restriction is on building height rather than particular uses. Mr.
  Brierley clarified if funds are awarded to the Airport from the FAA, there may be use
  restrictions, but that is not the case relating to this particular area.

As there were no public comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to close the
public hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Blaisdell and passed unanimously.

  Commissioner Graf asked if the Airport Manager is in attendance and whether they were
  supportive of the Code changes as proposed. Mr. Brierley stated while the City would love
  to have ownership and control of this property, it is now in private ownership and its ability
  to be leased is desirable. The decision of the Commission in considering the proposed
  amendment to the Code would be to determine whether it is consistent with the General
  Plan.

  MOTION:     A motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to recommend approval of the
              Ordinance amendment as proposed based on the findings it is consistent with
              the General Plan, the purposes of the Zoning Ordinance and State and Federal
              laws. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Aaberg and passed 8-1 with
              Commissioners Aaberg, Blaisdell, Graf, Safsten, Shale, Shinoda, Southwick and
              Sandau voting aye and Commissioner Schade voting no. Commissioner
              Schade explained he felt the City had been trying to limit warehousing in this
              area for many years and felt it is not a good use.

6. Request for Street Name Change, from 1355 South to 1425 South within the Canyon
   Terrace Condominium project.

  Mr. Russ Eldredge, City Engineering, explained the Canyon Terrace project was approved
  and recorded in the 1980’s, but only the first phase was constructed at that time. The plat
  identified the street serving the second phase as 1355 South but does not match the
  coordinates of the surrounding community and could create confusion for people seeking
  access to the new units. He recommended the street name be changed to 1425 South
  which would better match the grid system of roads in the community.

  Ms. Girardo stated this property is within the Horace Mann Community and is currently
  under construction as phases 3 and 4 of Canyon Terrace. She presented the recorded plat
  in relation to the surrounding community, indicating the road to the west is named 1450
  South and changing the street number to 1425 south would tie into both the Canyon Terrace
  community and the surrounding area. She has contacted Ogden City Police and Fire and
  they support the name change, indicating it would likely improve their ability to serve the
  tenants of the area. She stated as the project is still under construction, there are no
  existing residents which will be affected by the name change and it will improve its ability to
  be served by utilities and delivery services. Staff recommends approval of the name change
  as requested.

  MOTION:     A motion was made by Commissioner Schade to recommend approval of the
              proposed name change as requested based on the findings it will not create
              negative impacts on emergency services, deliveries, the traveling public or
              residents of the street. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Safsten and
              passed unanimously with Commissioners Aaberg, Blaisdell, Graf, Safsten,
              Schade, Shale, Shinoda, Southwick and Sandau voting aye.

7. Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance Amendment, to eliminate newspaper notification

  Mr. Brierley stated the State law was amended by eliminating the need for newspaper
  notification for land use items as people are now using technology rather than newspapers
  for their information. While the Planning Commission had approved language in June 2021
