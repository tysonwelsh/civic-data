Washington County Land Use Authority Meeting
January 11, 2022

The Regular Meeting of the Planning Commission of Washington County was called to order by
Chairman Anderson at 1:30 p.m. on January 11, 2022, in the Washington County Commission
Chambers and via Zoom, at 197 East Tabernacle Street in St. George, Utah. Commissioners in
attendance were Frank Leuck, Todd Jones, Brandon Anderson, Kevin Jones and Slade Hughes.
County Staff in attendance were Scott Messel, Community Development Director; Victoria Hales,
Deputy Attorney; Todd Edwards, County Engineer; Laura Thurber, Planning and Permits
Specialist; Kurt Gardner, Building Official; Brent Cardall, Code Compliance Specialist.

Chairman Anderson welcomed everyone to the meeting and led the Pledge of Allegiance.

He explained that Item 3 on the agenda is tabled, excused Commissioners Scow and Owens, then
explained meeting protocol.

irrigation transmission line from Ash Creek Reservoir to the proposed Toquer Reservoir
and a pressurized irrigation project for the Pintura Irrigation Company from the South Ash
Creek Tributary near the Pintura and Anderson Junction areas. The applicants are

administration decision.

Creek pipeline is coming down from Ash Creek Reservoir and will also go through the
Town of Pintura to provide pressurized irrigation to the customers of the Pintura irrigation
system.

CONCEPTUAL PLAN. Review a proposed project plan for a recreational vehicle resort on
Parcels 1157-B-1-HV, 1157-C-HV and 1156-C-HV for a proposed future zone change from
the A-20 (Agricultural 20 acre minimum lot size) zone to the PDC (Planned Development
Commercial) zone for the Millstream Resort generally located along State Route 9, near
Rockville and Virgin. The applicant is Millstream Properties, LLC.

Mr. Messel explained the location of the property between Rockville and Virgin. Past
proposed development concepts have brought up concerns with the proximity to the
floodplain. He stated that the applicants are aware of the complexity of crossing the river
and will need to construct a bridge. The proposal is in a secluded area on the south side
of State Route 9 for a Recreational Vehicle resort with several swimming pools and
amenities. He stated the applicants have developed RV parks throughout Utah and the
nation. Large irrigation and septic systems will be constructed throughout the project.
Mr. Messel explained that the applicant is proposing 110 RV pads, 2 homes and casitas
with 4 pools. He went on to explain the Planned Development Commercial (PDC) approval
process and that this is the concept review at this time only. Commissioner Hughes asked
if the current STR code would allow this applicant to operate the proposed STRs. Ms. Hales
explained that the STR code does not apply to commercial zones and the proposed nightly
rentals would be part of the PDC process. This is an RV park with transient rental of the
PDC products.

Commissioner Kevin Jones commented on the complexity of the project and asked about

the RV rental times. Discussion was had about the RV code and the differences between

residential and commercial zones. Mr. Messel explained that at this time, the planning

commission only gives feedback to the developer. This is a preliminary review of the

DISCUSSION ITEM FOR VARIOUS ADDITIONS AND REVISIONS TO TITLE 10, ZONING
REGULATIONS, INCLUDING ADDING PLANNED DEVELOPMENT RESIDENTIAL - SHORT
TERM RENTAL PROVISIONS, ADDING CONCENTRATED ANIMAL FEEDING OPERATIONS
(CAFO) PROVISIONS, ADDING WATER AND UTILITY REQUIREMENTS, REVISING APPEAL
PROVISIONS, REVISING ENFORCEMENT PROVISIONS, AND OTHER MISCELLANEOUS
CHANGES. Review and discuss various changes to Title 10, Zoning Regulations to plan for
and allow planned developments with short term rental uses, to plan for CAFOs and to
comply with new State law, to plan for water and utility infrastructure to support zone
changes and future development, to revise the appeal provisions related to Title 10
appeals, to provide more clarity and certainty regarding the enforcement of zoning
regulations, and other miscellaneous changes. Proposed additions and revisions to Title
10 will be discussed, but no public hearing will take place, and no action will be taken on
this item.

Ms. Hales explained Concentrated Animal Feed Operations (CAFOs) and Animal Feeding
Operations (AFOs) and the requirements by State law that every county must identify an
area unless you can exempt yourself. She went on to explain the definitions of a large
CAFO. She stated that every county needs to identify an area where a large CAFO is
allowed. There are conditions to exempt yourself, which is the amount of required land
in the county related to the population density. There are no specifics set in place by the
state for exemptions. The state also requires the county to specify fees and setbacks from
schools, churches, municipalities, and the need for roadway access. Ms. Hales went
through the proposed definitions of small, medium and large CAFOs and AFOs. She
explained that this will not be a zone but will be a designated area. This will not change

the amount of large animals allowed on a parcel for regular farm operations (where
animals are penned for fattening for 45 days or less). She stated that this only pertains to
largescale operations and not a typical farm. Commissioner Hughes explained that
different counties in the state were banning large scale operations, so the state stepped
in to protect agriculture and industrial farming. Ms. Hales explained that a lot of counties
are creating an IA (Intensive Agriculture) zone to allow for businesses with a sole purpose
of penning animals specifically to raise and sell for meat or other production. Ms. Hales
stated that the IA zone will be created, and individuals may request IA zoning and ask for
CAFOs or AFOs under that zone.

Matt Rhodes, a small AFO operator in Iron County spoke to the needs of keeping this
ordinance simple and that limiting CAFOs to one area may not be in the best interest for
ranchers. He stated that agriculture is important to Utah, and it should be kept easy for
future generations to continue operating CAFOs. Ms. Hales went on to explain that State
law only regulates large CAFOs and small AFOs might not need a zone change within the
county and might be allowed in other agricultural zones.

Commissioner Kevin Jones asked what if someone requests CAFO zoning outside of a
designated area or is the designation only within these areas or will the county be
allowing individuals to request zone changes outside of a designated CAFO area? Ms.
Hales explained that for health and safety reasons related to health impacts, water and
water table impacts, a CAFO or AFO might need setbacks from schools, churches, etc.
CAFOs, and will need wide open spaces, which we don’t have a lot of in Washington
County, also the consideration of SILTA land for CAFOs and AFOs may be an option. Ms.
Hales explained that a public hearing will be held later when drafts of the ordinance are
ready.

Ms. Hales went on to state that the county will revamp changes to Title 10, Chapter 2.
The county wishes to create a Hearing Officer for administrative appeals. The state allows
the counties to set up a process for appeals and there is a benefit to having a third party
hearing officer rather than your own public bodies making appeal decisions. Definitions
of new hearing officer, appeal authority were discussed. The County Commissioners will
hire hearing officers, based on expertise in certain areas. For example, if there is a dispute
over fines or fees, a hearing officer experienced in the applicable laws will be assigned to
that particular appeal. Ms. Hales explained the proposed appeals process. A hearing
officer would be a hired independent contractor, not a county employee. She stated that
this process would be clearer and more efficient. Commissioner Todd Jones asked if this
extra cost would benefit the county’s budget. Chairman Anderson asked if some of the
cost would be offset with an appeal fee.

Ms. Hales then explained that the county is looking to create a STR Planned Development.

It would be a zone change request for a Planned Development Short Term Rental (PD-

STR) and this would be a legislative discretion item. All lots in the PD-STR could be short

term rented, privately owned, or developer owned, with no owner occupancy

requirement. A PD-STR would have not the same size limits as a typical STR and the
planning commission could use discretion on the size. There will most likely will be some
sort of amenity requirements, and Scott Messel explained a few options. Discussion was
had about amenity options.

Commissioner Kevin Jones questioned whether this would only apply for new
developments and construction only. Ms. Hales clarified that it would be difficult for an
existing development to request the zone change and the need to have approval from the
original owners of the plat, otherwise you are creating spot zoning. So, it will be drafted
for new development only.

Ms. Hales went through a list of changes and fixes to Title 10 ordinances which would
include creating a hearing officer, code enforcement changes to include a three strikes
rule, deletion of the code that states random STR inspections are allowed by the county.
She also stated that there will be fixes to other miscellaneous provisions like weed, noise,
and number of dogs ordinances and clarifying the code as far as living in trailers. She went
on to explain that the county is out of step with zoning as far as infrastructure
requirements. A fix to this may be to request for proof of water and sewer before
approving zone changes to mitigate concerns regarding the ability to build once a zone
change has been approved. It is not a good idea to allow zone change after zone change
without any matching of the infrastructure constraints. She explained that we need to get
smarter about legislative discretion, otherwise we are setting up the individuals who buy
those lots to fail because they don’t realize that the water or septic is not there.
Commissioner Kevin Jones expressed positive feedback about the need to use legislative
discretion in the zoning process with growth occurring so quickly.
