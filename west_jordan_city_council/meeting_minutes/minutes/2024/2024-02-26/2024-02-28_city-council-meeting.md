 2. PLEDGE OF ALLEGIANCE
     Lt. Bigelow led participants in the Pledge of Allegiance.

 3. SPECIAL RECOGNITION
       a. West Jordan Police Department Badge Pinning
          Deputy Police Chief Jeremy Robertson supervised a badge pinning ceremony for newly
          promoted Sgt. Lofgren and Sgt. McMullin, and new officers Agent Archibeck, Patrick
          Pullen, Crystal Grady, Tabitha Grant, Clayton Brough, David Headrick, Cameron Lee,
          Christopher Muse, Sabrina Garcia, Bryan Samon, and Genika Workman. The Council took
          the opportunity to congratulate the officers and take photographs.

 4. PUBLIC COMMENT
     Chair Jacob opened the public comment period at 6:11 pm.
     Comments:
     Amy Mota, Salt Lake City resident and volunteer for Bailing out Benji, spoke of differences
     between responsible dog breeders and puppy mills. She encouraged the Council to pass a
     humane pet store ordinance.

     Heidi Smith said she was protesting the granting of an easement on her private property to
     Century Link with no notice to her and no permission from her. She said big holes were dug on

     in the neighborhood. She said Century Link did a lot of damage, and said something should be
     done.

     Sam Winkler, Public Engagement Manager for UDOT, provided an update regarding the UDOT

 5. REPORTS TO COUNCIL
       a. City Council Reports
          Council Member Green spoke of volunteering at Oquirrh Elementary, and said it was a
          good experience. He suggested the Council get involved, and mentioned the upcoming Dr.
          Seuss Day at Heartland Elementary.

           Council Member Shelton spoke of a recent visit to a fire station. He thanked Government
           Affairs Manager Tauni Barker for her abilities and support during the Legislative Session.
           He reported on a recent Utah League of Cities and Towns (ULCT) Legislative Policy
           Committee (LPC) meeting and Legislative Roundup.

           Council Member Bedore spoke of a recent airport open house, and the success of the
           Grace Company’s soft opening.

       b. Council Office Report
          Council Office Director Alan Anderson provided an update regarding ongoing efforts to
          update PrimeGov for the year and noted several items being addressed to finalize the City
          Hall remodel.

       c. Mayor’s Report
          Mayor Dirk Burton provided an update regarding Code Enforcement, spoke of the Grace
          Company opening, and said UTA continued to work on bus service along 5600 West.
          Mayor Burton reported the Copper Hills High School Wrestling Team took first place in
          State, and spoke of upcoming events in the community. He provided an update regarding
          Jordan School District and noted their goal to increase communication and meet with the
          City Council on a more regular basis.

       d. City Administrator’s Report

           City Administrator Korban Lee reported success of the recent Small and Tall Ball, and
           reported the move back to City Hall was ongoing.

           Utilities Manager Greg Davenport reported on a planned water tank project and current
           sewer project noting the sewer project was progressing well and may finish ahead of

           Community Engagement and Government Affairs Director Tauni Barker provided an
           update regarding communications with the community, the current Legislative Session,
           and answered questions from the Council.

       e. Cultural Arts Society of West Jordan Annual Report
          Janessa Kimball, Cultural Arts Society of West Jordan Executive Director, presented an
          Annual Report for the Cultural Arts Society, including an overview of programs,
          sponsorships and grant outreach, participation numbers, and a financial report for the
          previous fiscal year.

           Responding to a question from Council Member Whitelock, Ms. Kimball said the Senior
           Band would no longer be associated with the Cultural Arts Society of West Jordan. Council
           Member Whitelock asked what the Cultural Arts Society was doing to increase
           participation of West Jordan residents. Ms. Kimball spoke of local marketing efforts and
           welcomed suggestions from Council Members.

           Ms. Kimball presented a grant application for FY 2024-2025. She reported that the
           Cultural Arts Society would be implementing a new ticketing program, which would
           improve data collection, and announced planned programs.

           Council Member Green asked about the City’s relationship with the Choral Society and the
           sub-granting of funds. Mayor Burton said staff could review the contract. Council Member
           Green expressed concern with the 25% increase in the Cultural Arts Society budget.

           Chair Jacob thanked Ms. Kimball for the presentation.

 6. PUBLIC HEARINGS
       a. Resolution No. 24-005 amending the Consolidated Fee Schedule
          Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck explained proposed amendments to the
          Consolidated Fee Schedule to increase fees for road closures and road obstructions. She
          noted that calculated fees were meant to incentivize construction companies to stay out
          of the way of traffic as much as possible, and to complete work in a timely manner. Ms.
          Steck said Resolution No. 24-005 also included a proposed increase in the passport photo
          fee from $12.00 to $15.00.

           Responding to a question from Council Member Green, she explained that the fee
           schedule changes could be passed by resolution under State Statute.

           Chair Jacob opened a public hearing at 7:41 pm.

       b. Resolution No. 24-008 requesting the City Council waive dumpster rental fees in the
          amount of $825 for a neighborhood cleanup day
          Council Office Director Alan Anderson explained the request for dumpster rental fee
          waiver for a neighborhood cleanup day (11 residents at $75 per dumpster, totaling
          $825.00). He said the neighborhood cleanup day was scheduled for May 18, 2024.

           Chair Jacob opened a public hearing at 7:45 pm.

           Comments:
           None

           Chair Jacob closed the public hearing at 7:45 pm.

           Council Member Lamb said he thought the neighborhood cleanup day was a great event
           for the community, but acknowledged the costs involved and hoped the City did not
           experience a snowball effect of such requests.
