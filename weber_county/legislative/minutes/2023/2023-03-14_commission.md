D. THOUGHT OF THE DAY: Commissioner Bolos: My thought of the day comes from a presentation called
”Compassionomics: The Revolutionary Scientific Evidence That Caring Makes a Difference” by Stephen Trzeciak.
I’ve always taught my children to be resilient, to handle what comes at you and move on. This author says “the key to
resilience is relationships”. The more relationships we foster the better we can be resilient.

E. PUBLIC COMMENT:
   1. Washington Terrace Mayor, Mark Allen: Today I am here for a special reason. I would like give a huge thank
      you to our RAMP board. Washington Terrace received a substantial RAMP grant this year to continue phase
      two of a large project. I know we need to get RAMP funding back on the ballot in 2024. We could not as a
      community do the parks and recreation projects without RAMP grants. I encourage you to approve those
      RAMP projects before you today.
   2. Daniel Bartson, Ogden: I was fortunate enough to visit Portland earlier this year. I noticed there were junior
      constables assigned a three block radius to help clean up, dispense medical supplies. There are not enough
      signs in the city directing people to positive things the city has to offer. We could use more shared garden
      spaces. A European city provided citizens with opportunity to receive three free chickens and it had an
      unexpected impact on the local economy.

     1. APPROVE REQUEST FROM THE RECREATION, ARTS, MUSEUMS & PARKS BOARD FOR
        APPROVAL OF 2023 RAMP GRANT RECOMMENDATIONS.
            Shelly Halacy (Commission Office): I want to echo Mayor Allen’s words. The RAMP committee takes very
            seriously, sometimes with 11 hour days at a nonpaying job to make decisions.
            Lacy Richards (RAMP Chair): We encourage those we couldn’t fund to reapply. We could not fund all the
            great projects submitted. We had funding available for only 53% of Major category requests, 66% for Arts &
            Museums requests, and 70% for Parks & Rec requests.
         Chair Froerer: Can you explain where RAMP funds are sourced?
         Mrs. Richards: RAMP funds are derived from a fraction of a percentage of collected sales tax.
         Chair Froerer: That means those visiting from out of town contribute to those programs.
         Commissioner Bolos: Can you explain why some money is leftover and not spent?
         Mrs. Richards: We have to know how much we can spend before we learn how much sales tax revenue is
         available to spend. Once in a while a project comes in under budget or is cancelled with excess funds coming
         back. We have encouraged those who could not be funded to reapply next year. We had an excess of great
         projects and limited funding.
         Chair Froerer: This program works because of your leadership. You take the time to make sure the funds are
         given equitably and with great thought.
         Commissioner Harvey moved to approve request from the Recreation, Arts, Museums & Parks Board for
         approval of 2023 RAMP grant recommendations; Commissioner Bolos seconded.
         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Bolos – aye; Chair Froerer – aye

           2.      Public hearing to consider and take action on a requested rezone, including a rezoning ordinance and
                   zoning development agreement contract, to rezone property from A-1 to RE-15 on approximately
                   14.93 acres, located at approximately 4175 W 1400 S, Ogden, ZMA 2022-05.
                   Steve Burton (Planning): This rezone will amend zoning from A1 to RE-15 for the subject property.
                   37 lots with average sizes of 13k square feet. Frontages will vary between 70 feet to 150 feet. The
                   developer has agreed to public trail and street connectivity per our request. Developer has also
                   agreed to contribute toward open space via donating $2000 per lot to West Weber Parks District.
                   This proposal fits in the General Plan. In February, the Planning Commission unanimously
                   forwarded a positive recommendation and Staff recommend approval with items added to the
                   development agreement which items are agreed to by the developer. The developer, per the
                   development agreement, has asked for 8 foot side yard setbacks, in lieu of 12 foot side setbacks, on
                   each side for narrower lots.
