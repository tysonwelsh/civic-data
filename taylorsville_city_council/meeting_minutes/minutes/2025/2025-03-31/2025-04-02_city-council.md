         1. 2   Mayor' s Report



 El Mayor Overson delivered her report, highlighting several key points:

         A ribbon cutting was held for the remodeled Fantastic Sams in Taylorsville Town Center,
         in partnership with Chamber West.
         April was National Child Abuse Prevention Month. The Youth Council planted pinwheels
         outside City Hall to raise awareness.
         The Mayor served on the Tourism, Recreation, Culture, and Convention ( TRCC) board
         and was looking at grant opportunities for parks funding.
         Progress was being made on the wall project on 2700 West.
         The Mayor praised the fire depaitment' s fire school for new recruits and explained that
         one of the police depai tinent' s civilian employees would soon participate in it.
         Chief Cottam had recently made a very well-thought- out budget presentation regarding
         what his department would need in the coming year to continue to be successful. The
         Mayor anticipated having many more conversations in the coming weeks.
         The Bus Rapid Transit( BRT) project, now called MVX for MidValley Express, has been
         in development for 24 years. The mayor shared artwork designs for the new bus stations.
         She thanked Council Member Harker for inviting her to speak to her third- grade class
         about local government and parks.

                1. 2. 1   Introduction of Council Member Harker' s 3rd Grade Class and Parents

mentioned the classroom curriculum that was involved from discussing how city parks were made,
from concept to the final construction. The students had then designed their parks in groups and
presented their final designs to Mayor Overson.

Gilchrist, Director of Research who then presented the results of the 2025 Taylorsville Resident
Survey. Key findings included:

         86% of residents approve of the mayor and city council

         The average quality of life score was 75 out of 100
         55% of residents believe Taylorsville is heading in the right direction, an increase from

         2024

         The top areas of concern were crime, traffic, and housing
         Residents were most satisfied with fire/ EMS, garbage collection, water, snow removal,
         and police services

         6. 1   Public   Hearin, and Resolution      No.     25- 05 —
                                                                   A Public Hearing Regarding
                Resolution No. 25- 05, Approving the One- Year Action Plan for the Fifty-First Year
                of the Community Development        Block Grant ( CDBG) Funding and Home
                Investment Partnership Funding ( 2025- 2026); and Approving an Interlocal

                Cooperation Agreement Between the City of Taylorsville and the U. S. Department
                of Housing and Urban Development— Ken Donarski, CDBG Consultant


Ken Donarski presented information on the CDBG funding recommendations. He noted that the
funding amount was currently estimated to be the same as last year, with final numbers expected
in May. Ken Donarski provided an overview of the five-year consolidated plan, highlighting
housing affordability as a major issue in Taylorsville. He noted that 26% of households pay more
than 30%    of their income for rent. The plan also addresses homelessness and the need for home
rehabilitation programs.


Chair Harker opened the public hearing. However, there was no one present who expressed a
desire to speak, so she then closed the public
