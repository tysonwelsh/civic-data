        SUBSECTION 1-6-J, RELATED TO NONCONFORMING LOTS OF RECORD (Continued from the

(Track A, 49:00)
Brandon Larsen of the Utah County Community Development Office explained Ryan Schramm, the applicant for this
ordinance amendment, currently possesses property near Elk Ridge which is just short of acreage size to qualify for a
dwelling. Mr. Larsen commented on boundary line agreements within subdivisions impacting road right-of-way and
dedications, and Commissioner Graves mentioned city concerns surrounding General Plan annexations.
Commissioner Lee talked about limitations within the different zones and Mr. Larsen noted the Utah County Planning
Commission did not have a recommendation for the Board on this item. The commissioners discussed variances on
existing ordinances and suggested the applicant approach the Utah County Board of Adjustment for an exception on
the property. Mr. Schramm came forward to explain the lot already has a well and is completely ready for

6.     APPROVE AND AUTHORIZE FUNDING AND IMPLEMENTATION OF THE COMPENSATION
       STUDY COMMITTEE’S RESULTS AND RECOMMENDATIONS, EFFECTIVE PAY PERIOD 15,
       2018 (OR OTHER PAY PERIOD AS DETERMINED BY THE COMMISSION)
(Track A, 1:04:45)
Utah County Human Resources Director Lana Jensen summarized it would cost just under $2,000,000 to implement
the most recent market study for Utah County employees effective Pay Period 15. The commissioners thanked her
and the Human Resources Office staff for their research, with Commissioner Graves noting the Board spoke to each
Department Head or designated representative of the department heads and all concur this is the best result of this
process. The commissioners discussed their commitment to the employees, previous problems in disseminating
information, and the open dialogue of this process, including conversations about whether or not to address back-pay
as part of the implementation.

(Track A, 1:34:10)
Utah County Deputy Attorney Robert Moore noted if the question of back-pay is a ‘full’ implementation without it,
versus a partial implementation with retroactive salary, the response from county employees may change.
Commissioner Lee noted the full implementation would be delayed if the back-pay is included. Commissioner Graves
pointed out the delay would last until September. Utah County Sheriff James O. Tracy expressed concern that he was
asked to provide feedback from employees regarding the compensation study without those employees having access
to the specific numbers. He also commented on the disparities in job descriptions and position titles within Utah
County compared to other entities. Commissioner Lee mentioned the same concerns and Commissioner Graves
clarified the study which was provided to the county employees in December 2017 was not accurate raw data; it had
been manipulated. Sheriff Tracy also talked about the hardship of finding money within existing budgets – the
increases for wages and benefits since 2008 have come at the expense of operational funds and Utah County is nearing
being at risk of diminished services. He mentioned employee workloads are at a maximum.

(Track A, 1:48:15)
                 Commissioner Lee made the motion to accept the market study numbers with
                 some variations yet to be determined, contingent upon sending updated
                 information to county employees and continuing to work forward on
                 implementation and funding as discussed in Regular Agenda Item No. 6. The
                 motion died for lack of a second.

Commissioner Graves pointed out concerns with a handful of positions wherein the current incumbents have recently
received salary adjustments and may be omitted from this implementation; Utah County Human Resources Director
Lana Jensen noted that discussion to identify those exceptions can occur within the next couple of days. Commissioner
Lee explained it does not make sense to state the county will implement the salary study before identifying the funding
sources. Utah County Deputy Attorney Robert J. Moore asked for clarification on the positions which may be
exempted from implementation, and Commissioner Graves summarized the market study essentially went into effect
for several people earlier this year based on the need to retain those employees – they already “fit in” with the
Compensation Study Committee’s results.

(Track A, 1:53:55)
                 Commissioner Graves made the motion to approve and authorize the funding
                 and implementation of the Compensation Study Committee’s results and
                 recommendations, effective Pay Period 15, after the Commission has reviewed
                 the adjusted salaries which have been previously placed on market and adjusted
                 for the compensation study; and that this study is released to the employees.

There was some discussion at the bench to clarify deadlines within the motion, and Commissioner Graves amended
the motion to reflect:
