CONSIDERATION OF RESOLUTION NO. _R-2020-2667,_ A RESOLUTION
PROVIDING CORONAVIRUS RELIEF FUNDS TO PURCHASE LIVE
SCANNING WANDS FOR WASHINGTON COUNTY LIBRARIES

Ms. Felshaw stated that the third installment of Coronavirus Aid Relief and Economic
Security (CARES) funding has yet to be received by the County at this time.

Library Director Joel Tucker explained that scanning wands would be used to help find
lost materials, missing items, claimed returns, inventory management, and overall
management of the collection with minimal contact of the collection as possible. Additional
smart bins would be used at each branch to allow for a quicker check in process.

Commissioner Cox questioned the direct relation of the smart collection bins to the CARES
Act.

Deputy County Attorney Brian Graf clarified that in order to qualify for CARES funding,
the primary use of the bins would need to reduce handling or promote cleaning of the
collection.

Commissioner Almquist explained that if approved, funding would be contingent upon
receipt of the final CARES payment being received by the County.

MOTION: Motion by Commissioner Almquist to Adopt Resolution No. R-2020-
2667, a Resolution Providing Coronavirus Relief Funds to Purchase
Live Scanning Wands and Smart Bins for Washington County
Libraries.

Commissioner Cox stated that he supported the scanning wands, however, could not
support approving the smart bins at this time.

CONSIDERATION _OF RESOLUTION _NO._R-2020-2668, A RESOLUTION
APPROVING A GRANT DONATION AGREEMENT BETWEEN WASHINGTON
COUNTY _AND_ ZION NATURAL HISTORY ASSOCIATION DBA ZION

FOREVER PROJECT

Mr. Graf explained that the CARES Act has certain criteria in order for funding to be
approved which includes expenditures related to provision of grants to small businesses to
reimburse the costs of business interruption caused by required closures. Closures may not
have been state mandated but could have been voluntary in order to promote social
distancing.

The Zion Forever Project (ZFP) is a non-profit organization asking for funding to cover
expenses incurred from March 1, 2020 through September 30, 2020 for continued
operation of the Project including the Zion Canyon Visitor Center frontline team and

support staff.

ZFP Director Mark Preis explained that the ZFP along with Zion National Park was closed
for thirty-nine (39) days in order to prevent the spread of COVID-19. Safe resumption of
park operations were immediately discussed following the closure. There has been an
estimated loss of $2.2 million in revenue directly related to the COVID-19 impact of

closure.

It was clarified that the ZFP previously received funding for PPE, and this funding would
not be used for PPE, but rather for frontline staffing.

MOTION: Motion by Commissioner Cox to Adopt Resolution No. R-2020-2668, a
Resolution Approving a Grant Donation Agreement Between
Washington County and Zion Natural History Association DBA Zion
Forever Project in the Amount of $400,000 Contingent upon Final
Receipt of CARES Act Funds. Motion seconded by Commissioner
Almquist and carried by unanimous vote, with Commissioners Iverson,
Almquist, and Cox voting aye.

CONSIDERATION OF RESOLUTION NO. R-2020-2670, A RESOLUTION
APPROVING THE AMENDED WASHINGTON COUNTY MOHAVE DESERT
TORTOISE HABITAT CONSERVATION PLAN

Habitat Conservation Plan (HCP) Director Cameron Rognan explained that the HCP
renewal has been in the process since 2015, wherein, the permit allows for the development
of private property in compliance with the Endangered Species Act. The original Plan was
approved in 1996 as a way to facilitate new growth within Washington County. The Red
Cliffs Desert Reserve was established as an exchange for developable land.

County Attorney Eric Clarke explained that wildland fires were not addressed in the
previous Plan, but are addressed in the proposed renewal. Due to the recent fires over the
summer, it is imperative to implement the renewal as quickly as possible.

In August 2018, the Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT) applied for a right-of-
way for a northern corridor route that would extend Washington Parkway from the
northwestern edge of the Green Springs area to the Red Hills Parkway. The proposed right-
of-way would traverse approximately four (4) miles of the Red Cliffs Desert Reserve with
two of those miles also traversing the Red Cliffs National Conservation Area.

The renewal would be in place for an additional twenty-five (25) years. In order to meet
the current federal timelines for environmental review, the Fish & Wildlife (FWS) and
Bureau of Land Management (BLM) should make a decision on the County’s permit
renewal and State’s right-of-way application before January 15, 2021.
