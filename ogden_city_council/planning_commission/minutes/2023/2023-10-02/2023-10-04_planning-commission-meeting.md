3. Conditional Use Permit, to allow drinking establishment with dancing at 366 36th Street.
   Mr. Pablo Hinojosa asked Staff make their presentation.

  Ms. Patrick indicated this is a request for a drinking establishment with dancing, also classified as a
  cabaret in the CP-3 zone and identified in the Ordinance as a conditional use. As the building has
  been vacant for more than one year, a new conditional use permit is required. She stated the
  application proposes the use be open Wednesdays thru Friday evenings from 9pm until 2am. The
  previous use of this building was a drinking establishment and is surrounded by commercial uses. In
  considering a conditional use permit, the Planning Commission is to determine whether the use is
  detrimental to persons or property, consistent with the General Plan, whether the design and use are
  compatible and whether it complies with regulations of the Ordinance. She reviewed these,
  indicating as other surrounding uses close before 7pm, there would be little effect from this use. The
  parking lot will be well lit which should discourage damage to property. An existing alley divides the
  property from a parking lot to the east, and neither of these may be used for parking. She suggested
  the applicant place signage and mark where patrons may park in order to mitigate potential effects to
  adjacent properties. She also suggested the owner work with the H&R Block owners to the east to
  assure parking does not extend into their lot, which might include signage identifying it as a tow zone
  after 7pm. The use can be compatible with the General Plan which indicates landscaping be installed
  along major commercial corridors, and consist of water-wise landscaping. The regulations of the CP-
  3 Ordinance indicate a 15’ landscaped setback be required for parking adjacent to the street. Due to
  the limited hours of operation and work which is proposed to improve both the building and the site,
  Staff feels the use can be compatible with the area. Staff recommends approval subject to operation
  hours be limited to 9am-2am, installation of a 15’ wide waterwise landscaping for the parking lot
  facing 36th Street, existing landscaping be cleaned and trimmed prior to the business opening and
  parking for patrons be clearly marked on site to prevent parking on adjacent properties. She also
  noted the Commission had received an email from owners of the H&R Block property indicating
  parking from the previous use often extended onto their property to the east.

  Commissioner Southwick asked if water is available to service required landscaping, and suggested
  waterwise landscaping be considered. He also asked if there is adequate room on the site to allow
  for both the 15’ landscaped setback and parking along 36 th Street, considering backing requirements
  needed for parking. Commissioner Shale asked if handicapped parking is available and conveniently
  located near the building entrance.

  Mr. Hinojosa stated he operates a similar facility in Salt Lake City, and stated the days of operation on
  the application were incorrect, and would be Friday, Saturday and Sunday evenings from 9pm until
  2am. He clarified while no alcohol may be sold past 1am, the extra hour gives patrons time to
  complete their drinks and exit at closing time. He stated the main entrance to the use is from the
  rear, where handicapped parking is available, and there is no increase in floor area from the previous
  use. He indicated the previous owner had informed him there had originally been a 15’ landscaping

  maintained in the front it might be changed to angle parking which would reduce the required backup
  separation. Discussion continued among the Commission relating to the Ordinance regulations

  the previous use should not be applicable. It was suggested the applicant could work with Staff in
  order to identify waterwise landscape options, but the Ordinance maintains the requirement for an

  Mr. Nick Milford, 2510 Washington Blvd. indicate any use is preferable over leaving a building vacant.

4. Conditional Use Permit, to allow two group dwelling projects at 105-235 W North Street

  Mr. Mike Bastian requested Staff do their presentation.

  Mr. Simpson reminded the Commission this area had been rezoned from R-1-6 to R-2 earlier this year
  with the owner committing to creating owner-occupied duplex and single-family homes. Because
  there are more than five acres of property, the owner is proposing two stand-alone group dwelling
  projects, which will function as a single development. He reviewed the group dwelling standards and
  indicated some revisions are required, including relocation of the entry drive connecting to the UDOT
  property in order to meet the 8’ setback requirement. He stated while the applicant has not yet
  identified whether the units would each have roll-out trash cans or be serviced by a dumpster, all
  refuse and utilities are to be screened from the view of the street. Individual cans would be required
  to be stored inside the garage, and as the streets are to be private, will not be serviced by Ogden City.
  He stated while each unit is proposed to have a two-car garage, it needs to be able to accommodate
  both two cars and garbage cans. Guest parking to accommodate 13 stalls also is shown on the plan.
  One vehicle charging station also is an ordinance requirement for each project. The developer may
  choose to locate these in the surface parking or could provide wiring in each of the garages to
  accommodate future installation of EV charging units. Bicycle storage also is required, which also
  may be within garage space so long as the garages are sufficient in size. He then discussed on-site
  amenities required, including gathering areas available to each project itself, stating no details are
  shown for these. He stated while rock mulch with plantings is acceptable, there is a standard for
  plantings to achieve 50% coverage within three years of planting, and the design needs to assure this
  can occur. Each unit also is required five 5-gallon shrubs, with some to be flowering shrubs as well
  as two trees. He stated on-site connectivity is required, with walkways from units to both parking
  areas and amenities. Each sidewalk is to be a minimum of 6’ in width while the plan shows 5’, and
  any gathering area needs to be identified on the plan. He stated the existing homes on North Street
  are included in the project and will remain as single-family homes, but should be brought to code
  requirements by installing a solid driveway and side-by-side parking for two vehicles. He then
  reviewed the building plans, stating the gabled roof and large porch fit with the character of the
surrounding community. While the access to the garage is via an alleyway, a door is located on both
the front and rear of the buildings so guests may enter at either location. Each unit is required to
have a minimum of 20% glazing and the same material extended on all four sides of each building.
Floor plans must be provided to assure all ordinance provisions are accommodated, including
laundry hook-ups, adequate area for storage in the garages, and other details. He also indicated as
the owner had committed to these being for-sale units, a condition also is attached that the lots be
subdivided as a private subdivision. Staff recommendation is for approval, subject to both projects
being platted so that all of the individual units in the projects can be independently owned, and
obtaining and satisfying all department staff review comments which include:
1. Installing an entrance monument sign at the main entrance on North Street.
2. The applicant submits plans and information that addresses all of the preliminary group dwelling
    review requirements prior to going to the planning commission for final approval.
3. Detailed landscaping and irrigation plans be provided that meet all code requirements –
    landscaping and open space amenities be installed on both projects sites that meet code
    requirements.
4. All exterior lighting will be contained on the property.
5. All utilities be screened from view along the private driveways and North Street.
6. A 6’ tall vinyl fence be installed around the combined parameter of both projects, excluding the
    front setbacks along North Street.
7. Required parking be installed on the existing single-family home properties along North Street.
8. A financial guarantee contract be established with Ogden City for landscaping and engineering
    improvements.
9. 6’ wide sidewalk be installed throughout both projects that connects the front doors of each unit
    with on-site parking, open space amenities, and the sidewalk along North Street and include
    crosswalks at the driveway connection points.
10.The buildings meet the minimum 20% glazing requirements and the rock wainscot on the duplexes
    be extended on all sides of the buildings.
11. The 20’ wide private driveway along the west property line be shifted out of the required 8’
    setback.
12.The 30’ rear yard setback for lot 101 be met.
13. Bicycle parking and electric vehicle charging station requirements be met for both projects.
14. Laundry hook-ups be provided in each dwelling unit.
15. Storm water pollution prevention (SWPP) requirements be met.
16. All sewer and storm water requirements be met.
17. All requirements established by the irrigation company for the existing ditch that runs through the
    property be met.
18. Building division and engineering addressing review requirements be obtained and satisfied.

proceed with both funding and more detailed drawings. The second step, or final group dwelling
approval, then becomes a check-off to assure conditions of preliminary approval are then satisfied.

5. Public Hearing, Proposed Zoning Ordinance Amendment for uses in C-2/CP-2 when adjacent to
   residential uses.

  Mr. Rypien reminded the Commission this is to consider ordinance regulations for uses listed in the
  C-2/CP-2 Ordinances when adjacent to residential uses. He reviewed the purpose of the C-2/CP-2
  regulations, stating it is to provide a variety of commercial goods and personal services for the
  surrounding community, and these are typically located along major streets where there is little
  conflict with residential uses. This review is in response to a pending regulation requested by the
  Commission asking for review of uses listed as either permitted or conditional in the C-2 zones.
  Several scenarios have been considered, including prohibiting high-impact uses when within 100-300
  feet of residential uses, identifying mitigation measures to help them blend better with their
  surroundings, or any combination of these.

  Staff reviewed uses listed in the C-2/CP-2 code and identified uses which might create a high impact
  to surrounding residential uses. They then reviewed the existing zoning map, identifying the location
  of C-2/CP-2 areas. It was found most of these are adjacent to single-family neighborhoods, and any
  separation requirement would, in effect, prohibit many of these uses. No uses previously listed in the
  Code are proposed to be removed, but those identified as high-impact uses have a footnote added
  which requires additional mitigation standards if within 100’ of a residential use. An automatic car
  wash is identified as high impact if within 500’ of a residential zone boundary and should be pushed
  towards the interior of commercial zones. If beyond 500’ from a residential boundary, the provisions
  would not be applicable. The proposed ordinance would not affect existing uses and would not be
  effective when adjacent to vacant or non-residential uses in the residential zone. In addition, the
  ordinance is proposing a requirement for an on-site manager for any automatic carwash, a noise
  study be required which limits noise to less than 65 decibels at the property line, including noise from
  vacuums and dryers, and that all equipment be properly maintained. He also clarified different
  standards relating to a manual car wash if within 100’ of a residential boundary indicating these also
  may have noise from vacuums. A second option is proposed at a 300’ distance from a residential
  zone. Hours for all high impact uses are to be restricted to be from 8am until 8pm. Staff
  recommends approval of the ordinance amendment as proposed, allowing the Commission to
  identify whether Option A or Option B should be forwarded to the City Council.

  Mr. Chris Ward, 1655 Kiesel Avenue, expressed appreciation to the Commission on behalf of the
  citizens, stating as his home is near an existing automatic car wash, he has noticed noise from
  vacuums as early as 5am. He expressed appreciation for noting his comments from the previous
  meeting, stating the noise study maintenance of equipment are important factors. He can often hear
  noise as customers crank up their radio when vacuuming as far as several blocks away and asked if
  the ordinance also could also address noise from customers. Commissioner Graf thanked Mr. Ward
  for his interest and insight into this issue and asked if the requirement for a 300’ separation would
  make a difference. Mr. Ward answered his property is within 300’ of the existing use on Washington
  but if the separation were 350’ from a residential use, the impact to his home would have been
  reduced. He appreciated the requirements for maintaining equipment, but suggested there might be
  a separate decibel standard when noise is constant or high frequency rather than occasional noise.

  Mr. Nick Milford, 2510 Washington Blvd. expressed concern relating to some uses which are
  identified as high impact uses when they might be beneficial to citizens of the community. He felt if
  the concern for these uses is traffic and parking, that standards be added to limit parking areas
  rather than requiring all mitigation measures to be implemented for all high impact uses.

As there were no additional public comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to close
  the public hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Graf and passed unanimously.

  Mr. Rypien stated the intent is to continue to allow all uses as currently exist, whether they be
  permitted or conditional uses, but to protect the surrounding residential uses by implementing
  specific mitigation measures such as increased landscaping or setbacks. He stated the Commission
  could add that signage be required to educate users of an automatic car wash and request them to

6. Public Hearing, Proposed Subdivision Ordinance Amendment

  Mr. Brierley stated this is an amendment to the Subdivision Code in response to recent State
  legislation relating to the subdivision approval process. HB174 restricts cities from having more than
  one land use authority to approve subdivisions. The current process in Ogden is that both the
  Planning Commission and Mayor review subdivisions, with the recommendation of the Planning
  Commission forwarded to the Mayor who makes the final decision. Staff is suggesting the Planning
  Commission be identified as the land use authority with their action being final when relating to
  subdivisions. He also reviewed the appeal process, stating language is proposed which makes all
  appeals to the same body. He reviewed other details of both the legislation and proposed ordinance
  language and recommended the proposed language be adopted in order to comply with State law.

As there were no public comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Graf to close the public
  hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Safsten and passed unanimously.

  MOTION:       A motion was made by Commissioner Shale to recommend approval of the proposed
                ordinance language based on the findings it is consistent with the General Plan, the
                purposes of the subdivision ordinance and State and Federal laws. Motion was
                seconded by Commissioner Blaisdell and passed unanimously with Commissioners
                Aaberg, Blaisdell, Graf, Safsten, Shale, Shinoda, Southwick, Williams and Sandau voting
                aye.
