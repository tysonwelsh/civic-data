verbal commitment from HUD for funding for the Continuum of Care 
program, or if people could be without money to pay their rent in a few days.
Ms. McFadden stated contracts for HUD funding have been initiated, but 
agencies do not yet have those contracts.  The Continuum of Care funding 
from HUD goes toward rental assistance.  Without that funding, there would 
be an impact on the ability of Continuum of Care to pay rent.  Continuum of 
Care also receives funding from the State and private organizations, but those 
funds are used for other pieces of the program.       
Mayor Jennifer Wilson  stated she was alarmed.  A week ago, she was told 
there was certainty on the funding, but now, she was hearing that funding 
might not be there.  She wanted to make sure people were not without money 
to pay their rent.
Ms. Erin Litvack , Deputy Mayor of County Services, stated when Housing 
Connect was in Washington D .C. for a conference, it met with HUD 
representatives and was told the contracts should be there in the next month 
or two.  She sat on the Housing Connect Board of Commissioners, which 
met last week and discussed keeping people housed for up to two months 
while waiting to hear from HUD.  She explained that through Continuum of 
Care, HUD funds one program that Housing Connect does.  Housing 
Connect also receives funds through HUD, which funds Housing Choice 
Voucher Program Section 8 and other things.  If a lot of HUD funding went 
away, it would have a significant impact on the community.

interlocal agreement (the “TIF Interlocal ”) with the Redevelopment Agency 
of Salt Lake County (the “County RDA ”) whereby Salt Lake County agreed

as the successor party to the TIF Interlocal and, accordingly, by consenting 
to the above-described assignment.

RESOLUTION NO. 6278
A RESOLUTION OF THE SALT LAKE COUNTY COUNCIL 
DISSOLVING THE SALT LAKE COUNTY JUSTICE COURT 
EFFECTIVE JUNE 30, 2027, AND PROVIDING FOR RELATED 
MATTERS
WHEREAS, Utah Code Ann. § 78A-7-123 provides that a county shall 
obtain legislative approval to dissolve a justice court if the caseload from that 
court would fall to the district court upon dissolution; and
WHEREAS, Utah Code Ann. § 78A-7-123 further provides that in order to 
obtain approval of the Legislature to terminate a county justice court, the 
governing authority of the county shall petition the Legislature to adopt a joint 
resolution to approve the dissolution; and
WHEREAS, Utah Code Ann. § 78A-7-123 also requires that a county shall 
provide notice to the Judicial Council not later than July 1 two years prior to 
the general session in which the county intends to seek legislative approval 
for the dissolution of a Class I justice court; and
WHEREAS, on June 21, 2022, the Salt Lake County Council approved 
Resolution No. 5986, which resolution provided notice to the State of Utah, 
the Judicial Council, and the Utah Administrative Office of the Courts of Salt 
Lake County ’s intent to dissolve the Salt Lake County Justice Court, a Class 
I justice court; and
WHEREAS, Resolution No. 5986 further provided that the County would 
petition the Utah Legislature during the 2025 general session for a joint 
resolution approving the dissolution of the Salt Lake County Justice Court, 
as authorized by Utah Code Ann. § 78A-7-123; and
WHEREAS, on June 24, 2022, the Salt Lake County Council Chair issued a 
Page 12 of 16
County Council Meeting Minutes March 25, 2025
letter to Chief Justice Durrant, Chair of the Utah Judicial Council, which letter 
specifically informed the Judicial Council of the Salt Lake County Council ’s 
approval of Resolution No. 5986, and of Salt Lake County ’s intent to 
petition the Utah Legislature for approval to dissolve the Salt Lake County 
Justice Court during the 2025 general session; and
WHEREAS, Salt Lake County has timely complied with the notice 
requirements of Utah Code Ann. § 78A-7-123; and
WHEREAS, the City of Millcreek withdrew its caseload from the Salt Lake 
County Justice Court in 2023, greatly reducing filings at the Salt Lake County 
Justice Court; and
WHEREAS, the 2024 Utah Legislature recently adopted H .B. 35, which 
legislation converted certain metro townships located in Salt Lake County 
that are currently being served by the Salt Lake County Justice Court to 
municipalities, thereby providing those newly converted municipalities with 
greater autonomy to determine whether and how to provide justice court 
services for their residents; and
WHEREAS, the 2024 Utah Legislature recently adopted H .B. 330, which 
legislation provided legal mechanisms by which various unincorporated areas 
of Salt Lake County will be annexed or incorporated into municipalities by 
2027; and
WHEREAS, the cumulative effect of these legislative enactments is likely to 
be a substantial reduction in both the population and geographical areas of 
Salt Lake County that are currently being serviced by the Salt Lake County 
Justice Court, and a corresponding decrease in the Salt Lake County Justice 
Court’s caseload; and
WHEREAS, on October 8, 2024, the Salt Lake County Council approved 
Resolution No. 6243, which resolution formally requested that the Utah 
Legislature adopt a joint resolution approving the dissolution of the Salt Lake 
County Justice Court during its 2025 general session, in a manner consistent 
with Utah Code Ann. § 78-7-123, and provided notice to the Utah 
Legislature; and
WHEREAS, on or about March 5, 2025, the Utah Legislature approved 
S.J.R. 3, “Joint Resolution Dissolving Salt Lake County Justice Court, ” 
Page 13 of 16
County Council Meeting Minutes March 25, 2025
which joint resolution approved Salt Lake County ’s dissolution of the Salt 
Lake County Justice Court, with an effective date no later than June 30, 2027; 
and
WHEREAS, S .J.R. 3 further provided that any residual caseload of the Salt 
Lake County Justice Court will fall upon the Third District Court in Salt Lake 
County upon the dissolution of the Salt Lake County Justice Court; and
WHEREAS, the provision of justice court services is a municipal -type 
function that is fundamentally misaligned with Salt Lake County ’s 
presently-existing and future statutory functions, revenue sources, service 
delivery model, and role as a regional government; and
WHEREAS, the presently -existing facilities of the Salt Lake County Justice 
Court located at the Salt Lake County Government Center in Salt Lake City 
are scheduled to be closed in 2027, and it is in the best financial interest of 
the county and its residents to avert unnecessary and imprudent capital 
expenditures associated with developing and maintaining court facilities at the 
County’s future government center offices that will be located in Midvale; 
and
WHEREAS, the Salt Lake County Council has previously determined and 
does now reaffirm that it is in the best interest of the County and its residents 
to dissolve the Salt Lake County Justice Court, and to allow its residual 
caseload to fall to the Utah Third District Court as may be necessary; and
WHEREAS, subject to the authorization provided by S .J.R. 3 and consistent 
with the substantive provisions of Utah Code Ann. § 78-7-123, the Salt Lake 
County Council now finds it expedient to dissolve the Salt Lake County 
Justice Court, effective June 30, 2027;
NOW THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, by the Salt Lake County Council 
that the Salt Lake County Justice Court is hereby dissolved, with an effective 
date of June 30, 2027, and that upon dissolution of the Salt Lake County 
Justice Court on that date, its residual caseload will fall upon the Utah Third 
District Court located in Salt Lake County; and
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that during the intervening period between 
the effective date of this Resolution and June 30, 2027, Salt Lake County will 
encourage and cooperate with its partner governmental entities in the Salt 
Page 14 of 16
County Council Meeting Minutes March 25, 2025
Lake Greater Municipal Services District to explore alternative arrangements 
for the provision of justice court services to the municipal areas that are now 
being serviced by the Salt Lake County Justice Court; and
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that during the intervening period between 
the effective date of this Resolution and June 30, 2027, Salt Lake County will 
undertake all appropriate budgetary and personnel actions that are necessary 
for an orderly and timely dissolution of the Salt Lake County Justice Court 
consistent with this Resolution and applicable law; and
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that notice of this Resolution shall be 
provided to the Utah Judicial Council upon its approval.
