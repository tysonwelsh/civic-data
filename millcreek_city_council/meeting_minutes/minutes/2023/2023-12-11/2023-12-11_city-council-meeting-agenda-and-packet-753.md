Mayor Silvestrini called the work meeting to order.

1. Millereek Arts Council Year in Review; Mitchell Vice, Millcreek Arts Council Chair
Mitchell Vice acknowledged Arts Council board members: Kate Scribner, Rachel
Whittekiend, Brenda Martinez, Rachel Becker, Kaleb Claypool, Eric McKenna Spreng, Angel
Vice, and Heidi Jentsch. He noted the Arts Council’s funding from the year: $19,020 in
grants, $1,550 cash/donations ($1,765 in 2022), and $16,040 Arts Fest fees ($1,950 in 2022),
and $16,040 paid to artists ($13,500 in 2022). There were 10 Summer Jam in the Park events
with approximately 3,200 attendees (9 in 2022). He reported on the success of the “Getting
Visual at the Library” events and two art classes at Millcreek Common by Durga Ekambaram.
The Millcreek Arts Fest included over 90 local artists, 11 stage performers, and over 4,000
attendees. He showed the council a video highlighting art around the community and various
events from the year. He said the future vision for the Arts Council included a performing and
practicing arts center, paid directorial staff, public art installations throughout the city, artist
residencies, a mural festival, and having Millcreek attract artists and generate revenue from art
lovers, art tourists, and art collectors from around the state and globe. He thanked the council

1.2 Unified Police Department Millcreek Precinct Officer of the Month for
November 2023

Chief Petty-Brown introduced Sgt. Scott Wilberger to the council as a newly promoted
sergeant. She announced Officer Tiffany Twitchell-Smith as the Officer of the Month for
November 2023. Officer Twitchell-Smith temporarily took over a shift lead when it was
needed. She helped a victim of a domestic violence incident and was able to quickly
respond to another incident. She responded to the second scene and located critical
evidentiary items of the suspect which helped to identify the suspect.

2. Planning Matters
2.1 Discussion and Consideration of Ordinance 23-46, Rezoning Certain Property
Located at Approximately 2888 E 3300 S from the C-2 (Commercial) Zone to the R-
M (Multifamily Residential) Zone

Millcreek City Council Meeting Minutes 11 December 2023 Page 3 of 5

Mayor Silvestrini said the council had a first reading of the item at their last meeting.
Carlos Estudillo gave a brief overview of the proposed project associated with the rezone.
It would consist of 6 townhome units capped at 35 feet high and provide 40% open space
without amenities. Every unit would have a two-car garage, there would be three guest
parking stalls, water-wise plants would be planted, and most architectural requirements of
the project had been met. He showed the council the proposed site plan, landscape plan,
ALTA survey, renderings of the proposed buildings, and floor plans. The development
agreement would address uses, height, setbacks, parking, landscaping and open space,
exterior lighting, materials, right-of-way improvements, street lighting, and utilities. He
noted that he had added an owner occupancy section to the agreement since the last
meeting, as requested by the council. He recommended approval of the ordinance subject
to the development agreement presented. Council Member DeSirant asked about the
ADA parking stall. Estudillo said there was one stall, but it was not counted towards the
total. Mayor Silvestrini asked for public comment. There was none. Council Member
DeSirant appreciated the owner occupancy.

Council Member DeSirant moved to approve application file number ZM-23-006, to rezone
a property on 2888 E 3300 S to the residential R-M Zone, subject to a development
agreement, as presented - Ordinance 23-46. Council Member Jackson seconded.

Council Member Jackson noted commercial development did not work well for this property and
felt the project was a good fit.

The Recorder called for the vote. Council Member DeSirant voted yes, Council Member
Jackson voted yes, Council Member Uipi voted yes, and Mayor Silvestrini voted yes. The
motion passed unanimously.

3. Reports
3.1 Mayor’s Report
Mayor Silvestrini announced a Hanukkah lighting event at Millcreek Common on
December 14". He and staff would be meeting with Representative Burgess Owens’
office that same day. He reported on a meeting with the Governor and state leadership
about homelessness and some initiatives to augment funding for resources. If the
initiative was approved by the legislature, it would create an ongoing funding stream to
support the temporary overflow homeless shelters. The goal would be to acquire a
permanent overflow facility. The state leadership also discussed an initiative to promote
the construction of starter homes with smaller square footage on smaller lots. If cities
made these lots available for development, a square footage maximum may be put on
them. Council Member Jackson asked about other alternatives, such as building
materials.

Mayor Silvestrini announced that city hall would be closed on December 13" for a staff
holiday party. He announced the Holladay Hills Apartments would be shifting from an
affordable project to market-rate housing project, which would be a loss to the city for
affordable housing. He would be asking the property owners if they would be interested
in selling the project so it could be maintained as affordable. The city was trying to help
the residents find alternate affordable housing. He reported that he had also been meeting
with officials on the UPD governance agreement which would reinstate the interlocal
agreement in which UPD existed but without the County. The goal would be to get a new
agreement approved by March 2024.

3.5 Unified Police Department Report

Chief Petty-Brown reported the November crime statistics. There were 2,165 calls for
service, 770 total cases, 404 total citations, and 28 booking arrests. There were 27
assaults, 9 burglaries, 13 drug offenses, 69 family offenses, 76 larcenies, 3 robberies, 3
sex offenses, and 23 stolen vehicles. She announced that Detective Ryan Warner was
named the Millcreek Precinct’s Officer of the Year for 2023. Detective Warner is the
Domestic Violence Investigator for Millcreek and has served as the domestic violence
detective in Millcreek for the last 18 months. Detective Warner’s dedication, compassion
for victims, work ethic and expertise are widely recognized by fellow officers who seek
his guidance in handling difficult domestic cases. Detective Warner investigates
approximately 500 cases a year. In the month of November, Millcreek’s Community
Crime Suppression Unit had 8 arrests, 151 traffic stops, 12 stolen vehicle investigations,
5 recovered stolen vehicles, and 14 drug investigations, and 97.4 grams of meth, 13 pills
of buprenorphine, 1.3 grams of marijuana, and 201 pills of fentanyl were seized. In the
month of November, officers responded to 20 calls of transient activity, 69 mental health
calls, 157 traffic accidents, and 20 hit and runs.
