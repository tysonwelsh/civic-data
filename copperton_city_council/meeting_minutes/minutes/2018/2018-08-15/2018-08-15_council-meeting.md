Fiscal Items – Approval of Expenditures

                Council Member Bailey, seconded by Council Member Pazell, moved to approve
the legal invoice for June and July. The motion passed unanimously.

             Council Member Bailey stated the town council would like to request funding that
has been budgeted for their activities board.

                Mayor Clayton will send forms to the Community Council for Town Days and
activities account. He also noted that despite repeated requests, he had not received the P-card
from Salt Lake County. Copperton has the required P-card policy, but still no card has been
received. If the card is not received by the next meeting he will make a motion that Copperton
pull every last cent of its budget out of the County and put it in its own bank account.

              Council Member Pazell asked if Copperton was allowed to do that.

              Mayor Clayton said he did not care. He will push the button to force the issue.
This is not the money Copperton pays to the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District
(GSLMSD) for interlocal services. That is just the Council’s administrative budget.

              Nathan Bracken asked that the application forms be sent to him.

                Mayor Clayton stated when the County Auditor and Treasurer came to a Council
meeting they said it could be done. He is at the point where Copperton cannot get financial
information from the County. He has received a 600-page pdf copy of expenditures that he has
to fish through. Copperton tax revenue goes directly to the GSLMSD. This can only be changed
during the legislative session. He wanted Copperton to control its own money and allow the
treasurer to do her job.

Update of Unified Police Department (UPD)

                Council Member Pazell stated Riverton is currently in negotiations with UPD to
decide if they will stay with UPD. During one such meeting, Copperton was inappropriately cited
as an example of how Copperton is not working with the UPD model. He followed up to correct
this misconception.

He toured the Copperton Improvement District building. A UPD auxiliary substation will move into
the front of the building and the Improvement District will move to the back.

                Mr. Scott Carver, District Administrator, Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement
Service Area, reviewed the history of UPD and SLVLESA and the need for the new District
Administrator position. He started on June 24, 2018, and has focused on learning about the
property tax notice and other related issued. He also reviewed various issues facing SLVLESA
such as cities choosing to leave the district, the possible incorporation of Brighton, and a proposed
tax increase.

Storm Water Impact Fee

             Mayor Clayton stated an impact fee is different than storm water fee. An impact
fee is money held in escrow from a developer for infrastructure, repairs, etc. on a particular
development. It is held for six years. If nothing is used, the money is returned. The administrative

Municipal Services District (MSD)/Copperton Audit Update

                 Mayor Clayton stated the audit has not been signed by the GSLMSD chair
because it was not done in accordance with auditing standards. The metro townships are listed
as component units of Salt Lake County, which is not true. It appears that the auditor missed that
point or was misinformed. This should have been caught, but the census has classified metro
townships the same way. The law is clear; metro townships are individual entities. For that
reason, the General Manager of the GSLMSD has not signed the audit. Mark Anderson, legal
counsel to the GSLMSD, will write a rebuttal letter. It is unlikely that the audit firm will reissue the
audit; it will mostly likely remain unsigned.

Bingham City Cemetery

                Tessa Stitzer, Cemetery Board, stated she has still not been able to obtain a P-
Card from Salt Lake County. Because there is no P-Card, she requested an account be set up
with Diamond Rental as a backup plan. Within the cemetery, mapping and zoning remain the
top priority. She reviewed other repairs and projects.

              Mayor Clayton proposed having a petty cash box with about $1,000.00. The
township tax exempt number would have to be used and receipts would be required. Council
Member Bailey, as Treasurer, would oversee the fund.

             Ms. Stitzer stated she has asked the County to provide monthly statements. She
has made over $14,000 in deposits and has not seen a receipt of deposit or statements.

              Nathan Bracken stated for the record that he was concerned that $14,000 in
cemetery operating revenue was given to the County and they did not receive confirmation that
the money was received. Copperton and the cemetery board did not know where that money
was going. This is a basic auditing problem. There is potential liability on Copperton’s part if they
do not know where the funds are going. He recommended that the Cemetery funds be moved
out of the County.

               Council Member Pazell, seconded by Council Member Bailey, moved that the
Council begin the process to move the cemetery funds away from the County with a deadline of
two weeks. Mayor Clayton amended the motion to indicate that the service provider provide an
accounting of the monies. The motion passed unanimously.

Planning & Zoning

    Historic Preservation District Chapter

Public Comment

               Candice Carter stated this proposal is extremely restrictive and is going above
and beyond historic preservation. She may support protections against demolishment, but she
was not in favor of the remodel provisions.

              Tessa Stitzer stated she also opposed this proposal. It does not only apply to
new construction and is extremely detailed. She does not support the requirement to get approval
to make changes to existing homes.

               Doug Green, Chair, Copperton Planning Commission, stated the intent of the
Historic Preservation District was not to determine the color a house could be painted. He grew
up in Grantsville but would not move back there because it has changed. He wants to preserve
the Copperton community and he encouraged people to attend Planning Commission meetings.

                Council Member Bailey stated perhaps the approach is to only focus on new
construction.

                Council Member Pazell stated he went on record as being opposed to any
restrictions on the ability to use property. However, he does not like the character of Copperton
changing. He is philosophically opposed to this concept and will vote against it.

              Council Member Bailey expressed support for zoning for new development to
make sure that it conforms with the character of Copperton. They must set a standard on new
construction.

              Deb Riddle stated if there are a lot of different viewpoints, a community task force
can be created to work things out.
