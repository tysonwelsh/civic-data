1. Downtown Site Plan with exceptions, Angry Goat Rooftop, 2570 Washington Blvd.
    Mr. Dan McEntee, representing Angry Goat stated approval had been given and permits obtained for a
    rooftop patio to be constructed, but due to time constraints in obtaining UDOT approval and installation of
    fire sprinklers to meet building safety standards, the approval has now lapsed. A new layout design has
    been prepared and submitted for approval with a pending stop-work order issued by the City as the
    original permits had expired and the encroachment permit for the sidewalk patio had not been completed.
    Ms. Patrick explained while approval for a rooftop patio was granted in 2020 under the CBD regulations,
    the zoning has since changed to C-MU and new standards adopted with that change. In addition, the
    application has changed as the total size is increased and the proposed roof design and material has
    changed. She stated while Planning Commission approval had been given for the sidewalk encroachment
    permit, the process was not completed and the final permit was not issued. She reviewed the existing C-
    MU criteria and stated while flat roof designs are standard for the downtown, the proposed butterfly roof
    design helps to transfer the load of the roof. Staff suggests the slope of the roof be reduced to 8.3-10% as
    the proposed roof appears to be 16%. She stated the proposed cedar building material is compatible with
    the downtown, and the use of both the rooftop and sidewalk meet the intent of the General Plan in creating
    desirable street presence in the downtown. Staff recommends approval of both the encroachment permit
    and the proposed new rooftop design subject to reducing the slope of the inverted roof to 8.33-10.04%, the
    applicant complete the encroachment permit process with City Engineering and all departmental
    corrections be made to the site plan drawings to finish the site plan review process prior to permits being
    obtained.

    Commissioner Graf asked about the need to change from the originally approved design and whether the

    rooftop. He also asked if reducing the pitch of the roof would affect the load capacity of the roof. Mr.

    on the 2nd floor of the adjacent building. He also asked the rooftop design be separated from the sidewalk
    encroachment so work will not be delayed, indicating the owner had understood the approvals had been

    must be approved in order for building permits to be obtained and should include the entire site

2. Discussion of C-2 zone conditional uses
    Mr. Rypien reviewed the purpose of the C-2/CP-2 zoning regulations and indicated the City had initiated a
    pending ordinance process which limits new uses in those zones when next to residential zones in an
    effort to determine whether uses may or may not be appropriate as permitted or conditional uses in that
    zone. He indicated he has reviewed all uses in the C-2/CP-2 zones and has found some which are high
    impact and may not be appropriate adjacent to residential properties. These include batting cages, animal
    hospital with an outdoor kennel, automobile service station, bus terminal, ambulance base station,
    automotive lube and oil center, car wash, convenience store, gymnasium, health club, heliport, drive-in
    restaurants, manufacture of goods, etc. He identified the location of existing C-2/CP-2 zones, indicating
    they are typically adjacent to residential zones. By eliminating these uses, they would not be allowed in
    any C-2 or CP-2 zone, which would limit these commercial uses which are typically community commercial
    uses. He suggested it may be as effective to find ways to mitigate potential impacts such as lights, noise
    or traffic for some of these uses rather than eliminating them from the list of uses allowed. He stated many
    uses have created ways to limit impacts of their use by creating efficient vehicle circulation by creating
    multiple stacking lanes and increased distance of a drive approach. Other methods might be to adjust
    buffering and screening, restrict hours of operation, and to use building materials which would absorb
    noise created by the use. He stated there should be equity in how uses are treated and suggested
    potential mitigation impacts of noise, light, smell and traffic should be identified. He suggested standards
    should be created to mitigate the impacts of high-impact commercial uses to make them more compatible
    with surrounding residential uses.
    Commissioner Southwick commented he had understood the intent of the pending ordinance was to
    remove some uses which are now identified as conditional uses next to residential zones rather than
    finding ways to mitigate potential uses. Commission discussed various uses and potential impacts as well
    as mitigation measures which could be universal to the identified impact, despite the particular use. The
    height of buildings also was discussed, with concern that no one wants their view blocked, and the code
    does identify some increased setbacks when next to residential zones. Discussion continued relating to
    whether uses should be eliminated or mitigation standards identified. If uses are eliminated, they would

3.   Discussion of M-1/M-2 zone permitted and conditional uses and standards
     Mr. Brierley indicated he had been asked to review existing uses in the M-1 and M-2 zones in an effort to
     determine whether they bring value to Ogden City as well as to identify whether standards for uses could
     be implemented to reduce negative impacts. He reviewed the purpose of these zones and indicated they
     are typically west of Wall Avenue around the railyard areas. The M-1 zone is light manufacturing and is
     limited to uses which do not increase traffic hazards, create dust, fumes, vibration, etc. while the M-2 zone
     allows a broader range of industrial uses. Each zone and many uses create employment opportunities for
     the community as well as increased property taxes, as well as providing services to the community, often
     serving other nearby industrial uses. However some uses have low taxable value, create few jobs and
     could create environmental damage to the community. These are typically outdoor uses such as junk and
     salvage yards. While there is a need for personal self-storage and RV storage, these should be limited as
     they bring little economic benefit to the City. He stated Ogden has 15 existing self storage locations, and
     as density increases, the need for storage increases. He suggested new facilities be allowed only if
     removing an existing nonconforming self-storage use or as infill to an existing storage lot. New storage
     also could be allowed on upper stories of buildings as the population grows. He identified other uses now
     allowed or not allowed in the industrial zones and suggested those that create residential traffic such as
     gymnasiums or dance studios should not be allowed, but could be located in existing commercial space.
     Auto related uses such as a go-cart track would be allowed. While some uses are a benefit, they also may
     create noise or odor and should be limited to the M-2 zone, measures should be implemented to reduce
     the impact of the use to neighboring uses or to the community. Some assembly uses such as churches or
     schools should not be allowed, but if other assembly uses such as adult live entertainment businesses are
     allowed, a church also must be allowed. He suggested sexually oriented businesses be prohibited in the
     to M-2 zones.
     Staff also is suggesting adding or amending some design standards, such as eliminating the maximum
     impervious surface and rear setback requirements in the M-1 zone, establishing height limits in the M-2,
     creating better buffering and requiring a solid base for these buildings in an effort to provide better
     durability. Design features might be required such as providing an entry feature, eliminating overhead
     doors on the façade, eliminating front yard parking, adding window openings, brick or roof pitch, or items
     such as an art feature or variety of building materials or colors. He suggested design standards could set
     a bar for future development but felt each owner might choose from a laundry list of standards to become
     compatible. He also suggested upgrades should be required to better meet current codes, but done in
     relation to the amount of expansion proposed at a particular time.
     Commission asked about signage and lighting and whether these need to be addressed. Commissioner
     Safsten suggested a minimum landscaping requirement might be eliminated in favor of providing visible
     entryway landscaping or better designed landscaping or buffering.
     Mr. Brierley stated the intent is to provide language to be adopted at the August meeting and indicated if
     there were any additional comments or concerns to contact him.
