                                                    BRIEFING SESSION – 6:30 P.M.

       1.        The briefing session to review the Agenda was conducted by Jim Spung and Karyn Kerdolff (Planning Staff).
                       On the Agenda was a Zoning Map amendment by Utah Land Company for the properties at 3399 West
                 and 3397 West 4700 South for a change from Single Family Residential (R-1-8) and Limited Commercial (LC) to
                 RM-8. This item was previously heard on May 12, 2020 as a General Plan amendment from medium density
                 residential to high density residential. That proposal was tabled by the Commission to allow the applicant to
                 review suggestions made by the Commission. Also, on May 12, 2020, the applicant proposed a Zoning Map
                 amendment to rezone the same properties from R-1-8 and LC to RM-12, which was also tabled by the
                 Commission to allow the applicant to review suggestions made by the Commission. The applicant has decided
                 against seeking the General Plan amendment and to change the Zoning Map amendment to allow less density.
                 The new proposal was discussed at length by the Commissioners with Staff, especially addressing access,
                 number of units, etc. Mr. Spung advised most of the issues would be addressed under the subdivision process.
                 Commissioner Willardson asked the question if he was allowed to talk in person with the neighbors separate
                 from this meeting. Commissioner Wendel advised that it was her understanding that was not allowed and
                 Commissioner Burggraaf suggested that a legal opinion be obtained from the City’s Attorney and some training
                 provided to the Commission in that regard. Mr. Spung advised he would take care of that.

                 Commissioner McElreath opened the public hearing at 7:00 p.m. and turned the time over to Staff for
                 presentations.

                                                ZONING MAP AMENDMENT


 3. 7Z20 – Utah Land Company - Recommendation to the City Council for a Zoning Map Amendment for Property at
    3399 West and 3397 West 4700 South from Single Family Residential (R-1-8) and Limited Commercial (LC) to RM-8.
    (Karyn Kerdolff/GIS Planner)


3.1         Ms. Kerdolff presented this item.

            3.1.1      She explained that Utah Land Company is requesting a zoning map amendment to facilitate the
                       development of a twenty (20) unit townhome style subdivision along 4700 South. That Robert
                       Thompson representing Utah Land Company, has come before the Development Review Committee
                       (DRC) to discuss this proposal. Mr. Thompson came to the first meeting with a general site plan and
                       concept. City Staff directed Mr. Thompson to apply for a General Plan amendment and Zoning Map
                       amendment. Staff also directed Mr. Thompson to further develop the proposed site plan concept. The
                       second DRC meeting focused on addressing concerns with the Fire Department access to the site, road
                       widths, and other development standards.

          3.1.2        That Mr. Thompson has been working with both parcel owners and has acquired parcel #21-08-226-
                       002-0000) already for the project. Mr. Thompson appeared before the Planning Commission on May
                       12, 2020; at which time it was suggested by the Commission for Mr. Thompson to revise the proposal
                       to fit within the current density range allowed within the Medium Density Residential General Plan
                       designation. Mr. Thompson worked with Staff to revise the application and site plan to meet that
                       recommended density. He is now proposing a twenty (20-unit development instead of a twenty-nine
                       (29) unit development.

3.2         FINDINGS:

            3.2.1      This application was initiated by Robert Thompson on behalf of Utah Land Company.
            3.2.2      This is a Zoning Map amendment from Limited Commercial and R-1-8 to RM-8 for the parcels generally
                       located at 3399 W 4700 S (Parcel ID 21-08-226-002-0000) and 3397 W 4700 S (Parcel ID 21-08-226-001-
                       0000)
            3.2.3      The proposed subdivision will have twenty units, varying in size.
            3.2.4      The proposed zoning Map amendment places the property in residential use, which all surrounding
                       property is residential.
            3.2.5      The proposed Zoning Map amendment aligns with the General Plan.
            3.2.6      A Zoning Map amendment must be approved or denied by the City Council.
            3.2.7      The applicant’s request provides additional residential housing types, helping to provide greater
                       housing diversity.
            3.2.8      The applicant’s request improves a currently underutilized property along 4700 South.
            3.2.9      The applicant appeared before the Planning Commission n May 12, 2020 and has modified the
                       application to address comments brought up by the Planning Commission during that meeting.
                       Note on Findings: Open space and amenities will be addressed in the Subdivision/Site Review Process.

3.3         STAFF RECOMMENDATION: Staff proposes a positive recommendation be sent by the Planning Commission to
            the City Council for a Zoning Map amendment from Limited Commercial and R-1-8 to RM-8 based on the
            Findings in the Staff Report.

3.4         APPLICANT ADDRESS:           Mr. Thompson stated that Ms. Kerdolff did a great job explaining this proposal,
            which includes a significant reduction in density from 29 units to 20, along with adding more parking. He
            advised he would be happy to answer any questions the Commissioners may have tonight and that he looks
            forward to moving ahead with this project.        Commissioner Quigley         wondered if by decreasing the

3.5        Commissioner McElreath opened the public hearing and asked if there were any comments. Ms. Kerdolff said
           that she had received one from Janet Stewart and read the statement in its’ entirety, which substantially said
           that she expressed objection and opposition to this proposal because there still would not be sufficient parking
           available and people will park along the streets. There would also be an increase in traffic in general, along with
           the fact that townhomes do not attract long-term residents, so there would be a constant turnover. She did not
           feel this is a good location for townhomes and felt allowing this would be detrimental to the existing
           neighborhood. Her preference would be a single-family residential subdivision there. She also objected to
           giving up her view of the mountains when this development goes in.

3.6        There being no further comments, Commissioner McElreath closed the public hearing and opened the meeting
           for discussion and/or a motion by the Commissioners.

3.7        DISCUSSION:

           3.7.1      Commissioner Willardson said he was not in favor of developers buying land and wanting to maximize
                      their profit by negatively impacting the surrounding area. He did not have a problem with housing
                      going in on this site but felt it is not a good fit to put multi-family housing in there. He felt realistically
                      the future plan would be to buy a few of them and rent them out to people wanting to utilize the new
                      Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints Temple, to which he was opposed.

           3.7.2      Commissioner Burggraaf             felt that the applicant has done exactly what the Commission
                      recommended in the last meeting. He appreciated his efforts and for Staff in working with the
                      applicant. The General Plan is the aspiration that is the achievement goal and what the applicant is
                      doing is fixing a zone that doesn’t currently reach that aspiration. The applicant is asking to change it
                      to fix within those parameters and he felt Staff was correct in their Findings as far as, number one it
                      meets some of the moderate incoming housing needs, even with the potential that some of the units
                      are slightly more expensive than they would have been at 29 units on this lot; they still are cheaper
                      than a single family home. He felt it is a good project that is located right along a main transit corridor
                      and was an appropriate legislative recommendation being made to the City Council because the
                      applicant is making the effort that maybe should have been done before in bringing this area into
                      compliance with the General Plan or if the Commission does not like what is being proposed, then to
                      perhaps look at making a change to the General Plan.

           3.7.3      Commissioner Barbieri         expressed her appreciation to the applicant for their time and energy in
                      working this out. She knows that can be costly and that there are limitations in scope as to what can
                      be done. She did appreciate them taking into consideration the Commission’s previous comments.
                      She felt what is being proposed is better for the community that is being produced, as well as the
                      community that surrounds this. She was hopeful that there will not be an HOA saying that there is no
                      street parking allowed within the development. She hoped that on-street parking will be allowed in
                      this development, which will help with the concern neighbors have that parking will spill onto their
                      streets. She felt the development, being right along the transit corridor is important. She addressed
                      Commissioner Willardson’s comment that people may be renting those out to people going to the new
