Public Hearings

         Ordinance No. 22-01 – Amending Section 10.10, Parking, of the Riverton City
         Code, relating to unlawful parking in Riverton City, Amendment proposed by
         Riverton City

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that Riverton City Code Section
10.10 regulates parking within Riverton City municipal boundaries. This text amendment,
proposed by Riverton City, makes several clarifications to that section. One specific
amendment clarifies parking restrictions during winter months. The current language implies
that no on-street parking is permitted from November 1 to March 1 of the following year,
regardless of weather. The proposed amendment states that on-street parking is prohibited in
those months during a snowstorm, or when snow has accumulated in lanes of travel. One
additional amendment addresses parking a vehicle on a City street for the purposes of living
within that vehicle, which the amendment prohibits.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments there being none, he closed the Public Hearing.

Councilmember Stewart MOVED that the Riverton City Council adopt Ordinance No. 22-01
– Amending Section 10.10, Parking, of the Riverton City Code, relating to unlawful
parking in Riverton City. Councilmember McDougal SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs
called for discussion on the motion. There being none, he called for a roll-call vote. The vote
was as follows: Buroker-yes, McCay-yes, McDougal-yes, and Stewart-yes. The motion passed
unanimously.

         Ordinance No. 22-03 – General Plan Amendment and Rezone of Approximately
         4.07-Acres at 2356 West 11800 South from RR-22 to R-4, Single Family Residential
         with 10,000 Sf Minimum Lot Size. Applicant, Ben Howell.

Mr. Lethbridge explained that this public hearing was for a General Plan Amendment and
Rezone of 4.07-acres at 2356 West 11800 South, the rezone area includes four parcels, 27-21-
478-002, -019, -013, and -012. The properties have frontage on 11800 South and extend north
to Midas Creek. The subject property is currently zoned RR-22, which is a single-family
residential zone with half- acre lots and animal rights. Properties to the east are also zoned RR-
22 and are mostly developed. To the west is an undeveloped 5-acre parcel which is zoned R-3.
R-3 also extends north across Midas Creek into existing subdivisions. Further west across the
canal is Midas Crossing Subdivision by Ivory, which is zoned R-4. The Canal and Midas Creek
are not bridged and no roads are aligned in anticipation of a crossing. The General Plan
designation for the area is Low Density residential, this designation covers all the area of
Riverton north of 11800 South and east of the canal. Low Density Residential is equivalent to
the R-3 Zone. The applicant has requested R-4, allowing ¼ acre (10,000 sf) lots.

Ben Howell, Applicant, explained that this property is very unique in shape and size and is not
easy to develop. He stated that when this rezone went to the Planning Commission that some
of the commission members were worried about water rights. He has done everything he can to
make sure the water right issue is resolved. He has also reached out to the property owners
west and they are not ready to work with him at this time.

Councilmember Stewart asked for clarification on ingress and egress. In response, Mr. Carter
said that the property to the west may one day develop and create a loop opportunity and he
believes the City can solve this problem. Mr. Lethbridge commented that irrespective of zoning,
there will be a requirement for interconnectivity in these developments.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments.

Randy Moore commented that he represents the landowners to the west of this development.
He said they do not want to develop their land right now but are concerned about access into
the development because it is only 32 feet wide. He does not oppose the rezone but would like
to look at access once the development plans continue.

Lisa Stocking commented that she is opposed to one-fourth acres and believes it should be
one-third acres.

Ben Howell, Applicant, believes that he can work with staff and overcome the concerns that
have been expressed with access. He said that the homes adjacent to Midas Creek will still
need to be one-third acre even if it is rezoned to R-4 because of the way the property is shaped.

Mayor Staggs called for additional public comments; there being none, he closed the Public
Hearing.

Councilmember Buroker explained that residents that live east of this property are concerned
that they will have multiple homes backing their properties.

Councilmember McDougal commented that he prefers the City maintains the integrity of the
existing community and would like the surrounding properties zoned to 1/3 acres.

Councilmember Stewart said he doesn’t like doing rezones in pieces when issues aren’t
resolved as to access. In response to Councilmember Stewart’s concern, Mr. Lethbridge
displayed the developer’s concept plan and explained that this development plan has a private
lane which does meet the City’s standards for a private lane development. There would be
language on the plat which would address the cross access issue with the property to the
adjacent property.

Mayor Staggs asked if this rezone came with approval from the Planning Commission. Mr.
Lethbridge said that the Planning Commission did recommend approval. He said that the road
connection would look the same whether the lots were 1/3 acre or ¼ acres.

Councilmember Stewart expressed concern about compelling a private landowner to address
the cross access issue. Mr. Lethbridge said from a planning perspective he agrees and
understands this could be a complicated development. Councilmember McDougal asked if the
developer to the west would be required to have a private lane. Mr. Lethbridge said that is true
and he does not believe a public right of way is an option on this property.

Councilmember McCay commented that this rezone should be approved, with one-third acre
lots, even if there are access issues because the property owner has the right to develop their
property.

Ryan Carter, City Attorney, explained that the City can use development agreements as a
development tool. He said that development agreements are one of the best tools to solve
unique problems.

Councilmember McDougal said he agrees with Councilmember McCay that the City should not
limit the rights of this developer and appreciates Mr. Carter’s recommendation for a
development agreement.

Mayor Staggs open the public hearing again.

Randy Moore expressed concern about the private lane option creating problems in the future.

Ben Moore, Applicant, explained that with the unique shape of this land, it will be very difficult to
develop this property as 1/3 acre lots which will have a lot of unused grass and side yard.
Mayor Staggs asked if the lots adjacent to Midas Creek would be 1/3 acre and Mr. Moore said
that was true. Mr. Moore said that none of the lots to the east conform to the R-22 zoning and
are much smaller and similar to what he is trying to develop.

Mayor Staggs closed the Public Hearing.

Councilmember Buroker said that in looking at the map, it makes sense to zone this to 1/3
acres, however, this would be a much nicer looking product to have ¼ acre lots and she would
be supportive of this rezone.
