Wall Avenue Corridor Plan
         The City published a request for proposals (RFP) in June of 2024, requesting proposals from qualified groups to develop
a comprehensive plan for the entire Wall Avenue Corridor, with the intent to establish a plan to improve and better serve existing
and future land uses and to operate as a transportation corridor designed for a variety of transportation modes. The Utah
Department of Transportation (UDOT) has offered to contribute funding and is a partner with the City in creating the plan.
Kimley-Horn and Associates Inc. was selected as the project consultant. The consultant services include project management,
public engagement, plan review, data collection and analysis, refining street cross-sections and intersection improvements,
preparation of the draft and final corridor plan, and preparation of a corridor agreement with UDOT. The Planning Division and
consultant are requesting to discuss the project overview, and to provide the Council with an opportunity to ask questions about
the project at the beginning of the planning process.
         Jacob Farnsworth, Kimley-Horn and Associates, used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the project
background, vision and purpose of the study, and provide an overview of the scope of work and workplan. The vision for Wall
Avenue is to:
              • Better connect Union Station to 25th Street;
              • Serve as a multi-modal corridor;
              • Reduce vehicular speeds;
              • Enhance the sense of pedestrian safety; and
              • Ensure consistent character along the Union Station Neighborhood and beyond.
         For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
         Discussion among the Council, Senior Planner Rypien, and Mr. Farnsworth centered on public engagement and
stakeholder involvement in the study process and Corridor Plan development.

Proposed Rezone – 340 Washington Boulevard
          The Planning Division has proposed to rezone approximately 7,000 square feet from R-1-6 (Single-family Residential) to
C-3 (Regional Commercial). The property is the old Fire Station 3 and is currently owned by the City. The property (0.43 acres) is
split by zoning, with the fire station building being in the C-3 zone and the station parking lot located in the R-1-6 zone. If the
proposed rezone is approved the entire property will be in the C-3 zone.
          Planning Director Brierley used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to explain the proposed rezone. He noted that the
City owned building is no longer being used as a fire station and its split zoning may hinder the City’s ability to sell the property.
The Planning Division feels the proposal complies with the goals and policies of the General Plan and the Hillcrest/Bonneville
Community Plan by making the zone boundary follow the property lines and by continuing to support the major commercial node
near the subject property. A 12-foot wide cross-access easement is used to access the parking lot from Ogden Avenue. The
easement runs between two single family dwellings and will be eliminated if the property is sold to another party, as it was only
meant to be used for fire protection and emergency services. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information
packet for the meeting.
          Discussion among the Council and Mr. Brierley centered on the zoning and existing uses of surrounding properties and
nearby neighborhoods and permitted uses in the R-1-6 and C-3 zones. Additionally, Fire Chief Mathieu provided a brief history of
the subject property. Chair Richey concluded the application will proceed to the December 3rd City Council meeting.

Proposed Campaign Finance Amendments (Continued from October 8,
2024)
         Based on the Council’s input on the proposed campaign finance amendments provided on October 8, 2024, the
administration has modified the initial request to amend the Municipal Code related to campaign finance. The proposal to amend
Ogden Municipal Code §2-6-13 related to political activity has been withdrawn. The campaign finance proposal repeals Municipal
Code §1-8 in its entirety and readopts Municipal Code §1-8 to align with current state code by adopting Utah Code §10-3-208 and
§20A relating to elections. In addition to state code requirements, the proposal sets contribution limits at $5,000 for mayoral races
and $1,500 for City Council races (the levels currently in City code), requires that political advertising contain a disclosure by the
responsible candidate, and establishes a penalty and enforcement process.
         City Recorder Hansen used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the proposed code changes intended to
codify the Council’s direction from the October 8th meeting regarding campaign finance amendments as well as to bring the
City’s regulations in line with State Code. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
         Discussion among the Council and Ms. Hansen centered on her office’s process for accepting and publishing financial
statements reports submitted by candidates.

Proposed Fiscal Year 2024-2025 Budget Amendment – Mid-Year
Amendments
          Finance Director Sorensen used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the proposed Fiscal year (FY) 2024-
2025 mid-year budget amendment totaling $2.9 million. The proposed amendment recognizes revenues from various sources in
the General, CIP, Airport, Parking and Mobility, Business Depot Ogden (BDO) Operations, Storm Sewer, Medical Services, Fleet
and Facilities, and Miscellaneous Grant Funds. Sources of revenue vary from use of fund balance, use of contingency funds,
transfers from other funds, use of retained earnings, and grants. Appropriations are as follows:
              • General Fund - part-time wages, justice court equipment, Union Station improvements, transfers to Airport and
                   Fleet and Facilities, and sustainability;
              • CIP Fund - general facilities and 20th Street improvements;
              • Airport Fund - training and terminal upgrades and modifications;
              • Parking and Mobility – contingency for parking plan;
              • BDO Operations – transfer to the CIP and Airport Funds;
              • Storm Sewer – system improvements;
              • Medical Services – equipment purchases (vehicle);
              • Fleet and Facilities – new and replacement vehicles; and
              • Misc. Grants – part time wages and grant expenses.
          For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
         Throughout Mr. Sorensen’s presentation, he engaged in discussion with the Council regarding the specific purpose of a
few of the appropriations listed above, such as funding for staffing positions, the scope of maintenance and improvement projects,
equipment purchases, and grants.

Proposed Fiscal Year 2024-2025 Budget Amendment – Forest Service
Building Purchase
          Finance Director Sorensen explained City Administration has proposed that the Redevelopment Agency Board authorize
the purchase of the Forest Service Building for $3.6 Million Dollars. The proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 Redevelopment
Agency budget amendment recognizes funds from the Adams Community Reinvestment Act (CRA) fund balance and loan
proceeds and appropriates those funds for the 10 percent earnest money payment and estimated maintenance costs for two years.
An additional budget amendment will be required prior to closing to fund the remaining purchase price. It is anticipated that
proceeds from a private placement bond issue will reimburse the Agency for the earnest money payment and purchase of the
building.

Administrative Update
          Public Services Executive Director Anderson and Parks, Cemetery, and Trails Director Stewart addressed the Council
regarding parks and cemetery maintenance programs and summarized the prioritization of parks maintenance and improvement
projects.
          Council member Graf then referenced a recent media article that mentions exploration of properties for a 1,200-bed
homeless shelter; he asked if the group working on that initiative is exploring properties in Ogden. He also asked for an update on
the potential use of the jail facility to the west of the municipal building to be used as an Immigration and Customs Enforcement
(ICE) facility. He also asked for an update on the Brown Ice Cream building. Chief Administrative Officer Brown first referenced
the homeless shelter question and indicated it is her understanding that the group working on that project is looking at property in
Salt Lake County that could serve as a centrally located homelessness facility. They need a 30-acre contiguous parcel of property
and Ogden City does not have a property that meets that criterion. She then discussed the Brown Ice Cream building; the owner
recently informed the City they have prepared architectural plans that are ready for the City to review in the next two weeks
before they proceed with the permitting process. She concluded by addressing the question regarding the status of the jail building
next to the municipal building; City Administration has been working to secure approval for the use of American Rescue Plan Act
(ARPA) funds to purchase the old jail building, with plans to demolish it. She is not aware of any plans on the part of the Weber
County Sheriff’s Department to repurpose the building for an ICE facility.
