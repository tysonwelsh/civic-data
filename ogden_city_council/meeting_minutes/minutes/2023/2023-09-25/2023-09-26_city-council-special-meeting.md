A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed vacation of a portion of
River Drive east of Washington Boulevard came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated a multi-family residential
and commercial development located at approximately 18" Street and Washington Boulevard is being proposed which would
contain up to 115 units in two 5-story residential buildings and a small commercial building where the Pioneer Heritage building
(Piccadilly’s building) is located. The overall project approvals include a rezone to C3/CO, a street vacation of River Drive east of
Washington Boulevard, and property sale and/or transfer from the City, Redevelopment Agency (RDA), and Utah Department of
Transportation (UDOT).

Deputy Planning Manager Simpson summarized the memo and stated in order to make the site work for the proposed
development, the petitioners have requested the vacation of the River Drive right-of-way east of Washington Boulevard. River
Drive itself continues east of the remaining dedicated right-of-way and connects to Adams Avenue just south of 17th Street. The
portion between the dedicated right-of-way and Adams Avenue was previously vacated and is currently owned by Ogden City.
The request is to vacate the remaining dedicated right-of-way, which is about 140 feet in length and about 40 feet in width. He
reviewed the conceptual plan for the project, focusing on the area of the project that will be located on the vacated portion of
River Drive.

Chair Choberka asked if the street vacation will create any emergency access issues for any surrounding properties or the
proposed project. Mr. Simpson answered no; one of the contingencies of the street vacation, however, is that the petitioner provide
the City with easements for the underground utilities that will be located in the plaza area. Additionally, the street vacation will
limit the number of vehicular access points from Washinton Boulevard to two, which will also improve vehicular safety in the
area. The development will also enhance public trail access in the area. The Planning Commission has recommended approval of
the street vacation, finding that River Drive is underutilized, and the area has become more pedestrian oriented. ( |

Fiscal Year 2024 Annual Action Plan Amendment #1
Project Coordinator Fuentes explained as a requirement to receive federal funding from the U.S. Department of Housing
and Urban Development (HUD), the City’s Community and Economic Development Department prepares and submits to the
Council what is known as a five-year Consolidated Plan (ConPlan) and an Annual Action Plan. The Annual Action Plan (AAP) is :
used to outline how federal Community Development Block Grant funds, HOME funds and other federal and non-federal program
funds will be used by the City in the upcoming fiscal year. When the Annual Action Plan is submitted to HUD each year, specific
dollar amounts for program income funds and carry-over funds are estimated for the remainder of the fiscal year. Part way through
the fiscal year, after the specific dollar amounts are known or when program income from loans and other programs are received,
an amendment must be made to the plan to update the Annual Action Plan budget. Often when the mid-year amendment is
proposed, changes in funding levels within certain programs and line items are proposed as well. The amendments proposed with
this mid-year update address the following:
e Adopt the 2023 HUD HOME Homeownership Value Limits - $380,000 to $428,000;
e Increase the Own in Ogden HOME budget by $250,000 with an additional $100,000 to the HOME match funds
with an increase to the goal for down payment assistance to 50 per year;
e Adopt a one-year CDBG overall Low- and Moderate-Income Benefit Certification Period; and
e Adopt an overall decrease of $34,385 to the budget.
Ms. Fuentes used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the specific factors that haver contributed to the
proposed Plan amendments and she engaged in discussions with the Council throughout regarding program guidelines for the
HOME, Own in Ogden, and HOME match housing programs.
