LIBRARY DIRECTOR RUTH BONZO BRIEFS COUNCIL ON LIBRARY PROGRAMS:

   Ruth Bonzo, Nephi City Library Director, reported to the council that this year's Resource Fair held at
the Hive was a huge success. There were 44 vendors at the fair, up from 24 vendors the first year. There
were 570 attendees. Ms. Bonzo also shared statistics of library programming and patrons using the
library this past year. The council thanked Ms. Bonzo for all her good work she is doing for the library.

PUBLIC COMMENT:

   The Nephi Farmers Market organizers Jacque Thorn, Linda Steele, and Terry Cook presented a
PowerPoint that reported on last summer's Farmer’s Market. The group would like to continue the
market this next summer and requested the use of the park again. Ms. Thorn said that the organizers
were looking into adjusting the schedule to 9-12 instead of 9-1. Ms. Thorn reported that they had
started with 17 vendors and grew to 60 vendors by the end of the season. She asked the council if they
could have food trucks at the farmer’s market. The council said they would talk about it among
themselves and get back to her about the food truck issue. Councilor Skip Worwood mentioned that
they could not hold the market on the Ute Stampede weekend. The mayor said he had attended the
market every Saturday and had enjoyed it. Councilor Cowan said she liked the market’s Facebook
presence even after the market ended. The mayor mentioned that free internet access is coming to the
park and that should be helpful to the vendors. Terry Cook asked what it would take to advertise on the
freeway billboard. The council answered that it would not be possible due to the Travel Council rules.

  Glade Nielson addressed the council; said he has his citizen hat on and thanked the council and staff
for all they do. He then said he was putting his Sunrise Engineering hat on and stated that Sunrise
Engineering looks forward to doing more good work with the city.

OUT-OF-STATE POLICY UPDATE ADOPTED2

   Mr. Atkinson updated the council about their previous concerns with the Out-of-State Travel Policy
that had been discussed in a previous meeting. He said that the concern about insurance coverage
while traveling out of state was a non-issue and he added a line item 3 of the policy that assures that the
City Administrator would be notified when a department travels to Mesquite, Nevada.

The policy presented for approval included the following:

    1. City departments are required to present their request to travel out of the state of Utah for
       training, conferences, certification courses, or other purposes to the City Council at a public
       regular or work session meeting.
    2. Funding for any travel out of the state of Utah will be approved through the budget process.
       Any travel out of the state of Utah not approved through the budget process must be approved
       through an amended budget.
    3. Due to the number of events that occur in Mesquite, Nevada for city departments, this location
       will be assumed to be within the state of Utah for the purposes of this policy. Department
       heads will notify the City Administrator when there is any event in Mesquite, Nevada.

Councilor Parady moved to adopt the new Out-of-State Travel Policy. Councilor Skip Worwood
seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.

MASTER FEE RESOLUTION 02-04-2025 -NATURAL GAS DIGGING FEE APPROVED:

   Mr. Atkinson explained to the council that currently, subdivisions being developed have an option to
select a private company or the city to dig the trenches to install natural gas service. The city had based
their fee on costs while private companies typically include a profit margin. In order to utilize our
natural gas crews more efficiently in the work that they do and encourage the use of private companies
for digging services, the Natural Gas Superintendent has proposed a fee for digging trenches in
subdivision developments. This fee would incorporate the costs associated with digging the trench but
also the opportunity costs associated with being unable to complete work in other areas. This will
encourage the use of private companies for this service but will still enable a developer to choose the
city’s services if needed. Resolution 02-04-2025 established a fee of $1,500.00 for digging services in a
subdivision. Developers will still be required to provide sand for bedding the natural gas pipe. Zach
Larsen, the City’s Natural Gas Superintendent, recommended this fee during his presentation of his
department budget requests.
