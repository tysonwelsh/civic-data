Regional Growth Forum (00:03:03) Kevin Feldt outlined a proposed Regional Growth Forum, a more independent forum (not tightly tied to the MPO structure) to tackle the county’s major growth challenges, especially the imbalance between strong population growth and comparatively slower employment growth. Kevin Feldt noted that while Utah County will approach Salt Lake County’s population, its employment base is not keeping pace, driving regional transportation and land use pressures. The forum would bring together elected oﬃcials, local government staff, industry professionals, and appointed oﬃcials (e.g., UTA, UDOT), with MAG staff serving as the support staff to the forum. Kevin Feldt reviewed survey results from 15 respondents (elected oﬃcials and staff), which showed transportation and economic development together as the top concern, with land use implementation, transportation-supportive preservation, and population growth close behind. Next steps include: continuing to collect survey responses, planning an initial forum meeting near the end of summer, and returning later with an update to MPO TAC and the MPO Board once the structure and schedule are more fully developed. Corridor Preservation Update (00:08:02) Cody Christensen has been working with the corridor preservation committee/working group focusing on revising the scoring system used to evaluate corridor preservation funding requests and on where the process stands. Cody Christensen explained that the working group has met three times and hopes the upcoming Thursday meeting will finalize the framework. The proposed scoring breaks projects into high, medium, and low priority categories. High-priority measures include connectivity (e.g., arterial-to-arterial or collector-to-collector), timing (with a “sweet spot” around 6–10 years before project construction), and pre ‑ planning/environmental work. Medium-priority measures look at eﬃciency (strip purchase vs. full parcel and resale), multi ‑ modal elements, and roadway functional class. Low-priority measures include funding history (rewarding jurisdictions that have not received funds in the past ~5 years), multi ‑ jurisdiction coverage, and local match level. Cody Christensen emphasized that projects will not compete directly against each other; instead, each project will attempt to reach a threshold score (still to be set). He also noted recent experience where a January application was recommended by MPO TAC but denied and tabled by the MPO Board, leading the applicant to withdraw and sell to a private party—underscoring the importance of refining the process. MPO TAC members raised questions about timing (buying early while land is cheaper), regional significance vs. number of jurisdictions, and the treatment of phase ‑ one vs. later ‑ phase projects. Cody Christensen and MAG staff acknowledged this Page 2 of 4 feedback, stressed that this is a new system likely to be adjusted over time, and outlined next steps: finalize the scoring and threshold with the working group on Thursday, bring it back to MPO TAC in May for recommendation, then to the MPO Board in May for approval, and reopen corridor preservation applications in May with an updated application form and website materials. 2055 RTP: Project List Update (00:24:26) Kendall Willardson reviewed how the regional roadway project list has been refined since earlier MPO TAC workshops and outlined the path toward a draft RTP for public comment. Kendall Willardson explained that after the January and February workshops and additional technical review, MAG staff have narrowed and cleaned up the original, very broad project list, including removing or consolidating lower ‑ priority or overlapping projects and focusing more closely on projects supported by census data and prior analysis. The updated “project screening list” in the MPO TAC packet shows what has changed from the original workshop map and how projects now align with the draft RTP structure and phases. Kendall Willardson emphasized that this is still a working list, not a final program, and that MPO TAC feedback is critical before anything goes to public comment. He walked through the timeline: April–May for MPO TAC and local staff to review the maps and lists (including more detailed zoomed ‑ in maps at the back of the packet and, soon, a web map), early June to lock in a cleaned ‑ up list for the June 3 public comment period, and then continued work through the year so that by November MAG can settle on the final RTP project list and plan document. MPO TAC members were encouraged to review the materials closely, verify project locations and needs in their jurisdictions, and send comments or corrections before the maps are made public. 2055 RTP: Prioritization Overview (00:34:52) Kendall Willardson turned the time over to Alice Beatty and Crystal Fernandez with High Street Consulting to explain how project prioritization will work for the new RTP. They outlined a shift to a more formal, performance-based, data-informed framework aligned with federal best practices and constrained funding realities. The approach uses Multiple Objective Decision Analysis (MODA) to score projects against MAG’s RTP goals and objectives (e.g., access, safety, mobility, clean air), with clearly defined criteria and datasets for each goal area. Projects are evaluated and normalized across these criteria, then combined into a single score using goal weights that MAG and committees like MPO TAC will help set, allowing members to see how scores and rankings change as priorities shift. They emphasized that MODA is a methodology, not a software tool, and it does not replace policy judgment or automatically select projects; instead, it provides a transparent, repeatable ranking to inform final, fiscally constrained project decisions. Next steps include drafting specific criteria tied to the adopted RTP goals, testing them with MAG data, and bringing draft criteria and weighting proposals back to MPO TAC over the coming months for review and feedback. MAG Data Products Overview (00:48:00) Tim Hereth walked MPO TAC members through the key online GIS tools and datasets available on www.magutah.gov under the Planning → Data & Maps section, emphasizing how cities can use them for planning, analysis, and public communication. Tim Hereth highlighted Community Profiles, which compile census, forecast, transportation, land use, housing, and economic data for each community; the Wasatch Choice Vision Map, showing regional centers, land use, transportation vision networks, economic opportunity areas, and Olympic venue layers; the RTP and TIP maps, which display long ‑ range projects and currently funded projects; Page 3 of 4 and the Traﬃc Forecast application, which shows existing and 2050 forecast volumes and growth. He also demonstrated parcel ‑ level building stock data used in the travel model, the general plan land use layer, an internal-style “all layers” map, and an active transportation map, along with a more technical model output viewer that exposes link ‑ level volumes and speeds for different model years and scenarios. Discussion focused on traﬃc count accuracy and update frequency, with Tim Hereth explaining that UDOT’s program relies on continuous count stations and periodic tube counts (typically on a multi ‑ year cycle), and that local cities can share their own counts to improve the regional dataset. He stressed that more eyes on the data will help refine it, and invited jurisdictions to contact the MAG GIS/modeling team for help using or correcting any of these products. UPWP Update (01:12:08) Kevin Feldt reviewed the annual Unified Planning Work Program for the MPO and outlined planned revisions for FY 2027. He explained that the UPWP is a federally required document that identifies the core work activities, budgets, and funding sources for MAG staff, UDOT, and UTA—covering long ‑ range planning (including the 2050 RTP), short ‑ range planning, travel demand modeling and economic forecasting, technical assistance to local governments, and program administration/coordination. For FY 2027, MAG will align the UPWP more tightly with the 2050 RTP update, refresh the organizational chart and narrative sections, and use updated revenue and budget forecasts. Key tasks called out for FY 2027 include: completing the 2027 TIP, conducting an air quality conformity analysis for the RTP and land use plan, assisting with the North County PEL study, coordinating with UTA on long-range transit planning, and advancing development of an activity ‑ based travel demand model. Kevin Feldt noted that a notice and draft had already been sent to local staff and to FHWA/FTA for review, that the public comment period is beginning now, and that the schedule is to brief the MPO Board in April, return to MPO TAC in May for recommendation, and seek MPO Board approval in June, after which the final UPWP is due to FHWA/FTA by July 1.
