Proposed Shopping Cart Ordinance
Community and Economic Development Deputy Director Johnson explained abandoned shopping carts have become an
increasing nuisance on public and private properties throughout the city. Code Enforcement Services and Public Services are
spending considerable amounts of time picking up and returning or disposing of the carts. Code Enforcement Services has reached
out to most applicable store managers to discuss the challenges of providing and maintaining shopping carts. The unlawful
removal of shopping carts from their properties is detrimental to their business. All contacted retailers were in favor of some
regulation of shopping carts, and appropriate, relevant feedback has been considered and incorporated into the proposed
ordinance, which he summarized as follows:

Discussion among the City Council and Mr. Johnson centered on hypothetical scenarios involving the use of a shopping
cart by a homeless individual and the potential response of the City. Mr. Johnson assured the Council that Code Enforcement staff
will not be targeting homeless individuals and that any interaction with such individuals will involve homeless advocates. He
indicated the main goal of the proposed ordinance is to provide a mechanism for handling abandoned shopping carts, returning
them to the correct retailer, and recouping any costs associated with that service.

Fiscal Year (FY) 2022-2023 First Quarter Financial Report

Comptroller Stout provided an overview of the City’s first quarter FY2023 Financial Report. This report summarizes the
City’s budgetary expenditures, revenues, and general financial information. City Administration anticipates meeting annual
budget projections, as many funds are impacted by timing of collection and/or expense. Items within the General Fund, for
example, such as Taxes and Licenses & Permits are largely not collected until December and January, respectively.

Council Business

Ogden School District Surplus Property: Council member Choberka stated that residents have advocated for the City’s
purchase of the Grandview Elementary property to be left as green space or programmable space. She asserted that the taxpayers
of the City already own that property and she wondered why it would be necessary to purchase the property in order to preserve it.
This led to discussion of the mechanics of the ownership and future use/maintenance of the property; the discussion expanded to
the City’s current stock of programmable space and facilities and the demand for increased space and programming. The Council
reiterated the position they took during the November 29 meeting, with Council member Hyer noting that he wants to honor the
School District’s desires for the property and future improvements to District facilities. Vice Chair Lopez stated he agrees it is
important for the City to be a good partner to the School District, but he is struggling with the same concern as Council member
Choberka regarding the ownership of the properties already lying with the taxpayers. Council Executive Director Eller-Smith
stated that Council staff will confer with the City’s legal counsel to determine the City’s position on ownership of the property.
