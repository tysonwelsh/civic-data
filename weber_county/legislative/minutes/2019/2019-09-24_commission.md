G. ACTION ITEMS:
      1. FIRST READING OF AN ORDINANCE SETTING A FOUR-YEAR LIMIT FOR PROPERTY TAX REFUNDS.
            Courtlan Erickson, Deputy County Attorney, stated that this item is to clarify some confusion over the last few
            years about how far back the county can or should go for property tax refunds. There is a 4-year State statute of
            limitations that governs this issue and based on discussions by the Tax Review Committee and guidance from the
            County Commission in the past, a decision was made to put this into ordinance. Property owners receive valuation
            notices and tax bills every year and have the responsibility to check the information and to appeal each year. He
            proposed language to 2-11-7(c) to state that the county will not change the taxable status of a parcel of property
            for a previous tax year unless a timely appeal is filed with the Board of Equalization by its filing deadline. Most
            counties in the State do not go back the four years. Commissioner Harvey concurs with following the statute.
            Commissioner Harvey moved to approve the first reading of an ordinance setting a four-year limit for property
            tax refunds, including the language to 2-11-7(c) pertaining to time limits as noted by Mr. Erickson; Commissioner
            Froerer seconded.
            Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Froerer – aye; Chair Jenkins – aye
      2. FIRST READING OF AN ORDINANCE GOVERNING THE PREPARATION, REVIEW, ENACTMENT AND
         PUBLICATION OF ORDINANCES.

            Courtlan Erickson, Deputy County Attorney, stated that this is a revision to existing county Title 2-8. The current
            code includes a process, including a lot of responsibilities for the Clerk’s Office in preparing ordinance paper
            copies. Over time, the paper copies have become somewhat less important as everything is stored online and
            readily available to anyone. Additionally, there was some concern regarding whether first readings were required,
            and there was lack of following the ordinance process as written. This proposal clarifies the previous ordinance
            and the process. It includes that the Commission shall adopt a policy to govern the process, which will soon be
            presented to the Commission.
            Commissioner Froerer moved to approve the first reading of an ordinance governing the preparation, review,
            enactment and publication of ordinances; Commissioner Harvey seconded.
            Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Froerer – aye; Chair Jenkins – aye
    3. FIRST READING OF AN ORDINANCE GOVERNING GRAMA APPEALS.
         Courtlan Erickson, Deputy County Attorney, stated that the GRAMA appeals process has suffered from lack of
         clarity in the county code because it is a several-step process and many questions have arisen. Additionally, the
         GRAMA appeals board has been unable to convene because of unavailability of a board member. This ordinance
         provides for alternate members and clarifies the process.
         Commissioner Froerer moved to approve the first reading of an ordinance governing GRAMA appeals;
         Commissioner Harvey seconded.
         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Froerer – aye; Chair Jenkins – aye

    5. A $70,000 DONATION OF IMPACT FEES TO WEBER PATHWAYS FOR THE PINEVIEW LOOP PATHWAY
       (WINTERS GROVE TO OLD SNOWBASIN ROAD).
         Sean Wilkinson, County Community Development Director, stated that Weber Pathways is requesting an impact
         fee donation of $70,000 for engineering and construction-related expenses for replacing a bridge and putting new
         asphalt on the trail connecting Huntsville Town to Highway 39. These funds will come from the Ogden Valley
         Impact Fee Fund, they are specific for trails and must be used within a certain time period. Pathways has
         donations to cover the remaining costs. An MOU signed on 6/18/2019 allows the county to donate to Pathways
         without approving a new agreement each time.
         Commissioner Froerer moved to approve the $70,000 donation of impact fees to Weber Pathways for the
         Pineview Loop Pathway (Winters Grove to Old Snowbasin Road); Commissioner Harvey seconded.
         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Froerer – aye; Chair Jenkins – aye
