         The purpose of the joint work session was to review the agenda for the Redevelopment Agency and Special City Council
meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.; receive the annual report from the Ogen Pioneer Days Foundation; discuss proposed
amendments to the infill housing guidelines; discuss a proposed concept plan for 605 North Jackson Avenue; discuss proposed
Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 budget amendments pertaining to American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds; discuss Council/Board
business; and hear Council/Board comments.

Agenda Review
        Chair Richey and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council meeting
scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Annual Report – Ogden Pioneer Days Foundation
          The City Council has requested to receive information from local foundations and nonprofit organizations that have
contractual agreements with Ogden City. These presentations help the Council become more familiar with the objectives and
efforts of the organizations.
          Steve Handy, Foundation Chairman Elect, used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide the annual report; he
discussed the Foundation’s organizational mission and goals, successes and challenges over the past year, and future plans for the
Foundation. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.

Proposed Amendments – Infill Housing Guidelines
         City Administration is proposing changes to the Infill Housing Program guidelines. The current guidelines allow the
Administration up to $250,000 in property acquisition costs without going before the City Council for approval. The proposed
changes include increasing the property acquisition amount to $400,000 and adding language to clarify accessibility and fair
housing requirements when federal funds are used on a project.
         Community Development Director Smith used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the proposed
guideline amendments. The current guidelines are missing language required by the Federal Department of Housing and Urban
Development (HUD) regarding Fair Housing when federal funds are used for infill housing projects. The new language clarifies
what developers and contractors must do to comply with accessibility and fair housing options throughout the development
process. Additionally, the current guidelines require property acquisitions over $250,000 be brought to the City Council for
approval and Administration is requesting updating that amount to $400,000 to reflect the market. He concluded by providing
information about infill projects that have been completed in the past. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the
information packet for the meeting.
          Discussion among the Council and Mr. Smith centered on the specifics of the fair housing language to be included in the
infill housing guidelines document; any return on investment realized on past infill projects; and options for eliminating
competition between the City government and private developers and whether infill housing projects may be market driven in the
future. Mr. Smith concluded that a major positive aspect of City infill housing projects is that homes are for sale products, rather
than for rent, and the homes are accessible to those in the moderate-income range.

Proposed Concept Plan – 605 North Jackson Avenue
           The Community Development Division has requested that the City Council review and consider approval of the final
concept plan for the 605 North Jackson infill development site. The concept plan is for a residential subdivision consisting of 29
single-family lots with a minimum lot size of 5,000 square feet. The current infill housing guidelines require the Council’s
approval of final development concepts and financing on projects exceeding 10 homes.
           Community Development Director Smith used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to present the concept plan for the
subject property; it includes 29 single family home building lots, completion of curb and gutter and repaving of Jackson Avenue,
new public streets, piping the canal, and providing pedestrian access connecting to the existing neighborhood. The property was
purchased using Quality Neighborhood Initiative funds of $1,075,000, as well as a future Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) request
to fund new public streets, utilities, and completion of Jackson Avenue. The home construction is to be financed with the existing
Synchrony Line of Credit, with proceeds of the sale to be used to pay construction financing, carrying costs, etc. He presented an
illustration of the proposed architecture of the homes in the project area, as well as three different development options that would
include anywhere from 27to 32 homes on the subject property. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information
packet for the meeting.
           Discussion among the Council and Mr. Smith centered on opportunities for making the detention basin in the project an
amenity rather than a detriment to the neighborhood; connectivity throughout the new neighborhood and existing neighborhoods
and options for limiting the number of cul-de-sacs in the project; sewer infrastructure in the project area; the ability to place a deed
restriction on the homes to require owner-occupancy; building styles that are suitable for this area; and options for rolling the costs
of public infrastructure improvements into the cost of the development rather than paying for the improvements with CIP funding;
and whether it would be appropriate to prevent an individual or company from purchasing more than one unit in this and/or other
infill projects in the City.
           Council member Hyer disclosed that he owns property that shares a property line with the subject property, but he does
not believe that will impact his ability to be objective regarding this matter.
           Council member Graf acknowledged the need for low to moderate income housing throughout the community, but noted
he also feels that the City needs more market rate housing that could be accessible to those with professional-level income. This
will improve the local economy because more residents of the City will have disposable income to spend in Ogden each month.
This led to discussion among the Council and staff regarding the appropriate types of housing projects for the City to pursue; Mr.
Smith stated there are no federal income restrictions for these types of projects, but due to the current market conditions, a
moderate home will cost approximately $450,000. This is a purchase price that may be high for even a professional-level income
family to afford. Vice Chair White suggested that the appropriate term for this type of housing is the ‘missing middle’ rather than
‘moderate’. She agreed with Council member Graf that the City is at a critical stage relative to housing availability; the Council
needs to carefully consider the appropriate ratio of low to high income housing units in the community. Mr. Smith agreed and
noted that many households that find themselves in a position to purchase a more expensive or larger home have found it difficult
to do that in Ogden. Council member Choberka stated that may be the case, but she would be uncomfortable utilizing any City
funding to subsidize a housing project to build homes in the range of $700,000 to $800,000.

Proposed Fiscal Year 2024-2025 Budget Amendment – ARPA Funds
(Continued from December 3, 2024)
          Finance Director Sorensen reported Administration is proposing the reappropriation of funds from the American Rescue
Plan Act (ARPA). The City received $25,612,825 in federal ARPA funds and an additional $4,000,000 in state ARPA funds for a
total of $29,612,825. The Council has previously allocated ARPA funds for public safety radios, community grants, community
cleanup, Marshall White Center construction, the 36” canyon waterline replacement, and 4th Street Park netting. The remaining
funds were allocated to a general government operations account. As a policy, the City does not use one-time funds to cover
ongoing expenses such as wages, so the funds allocated to general government operations are available for reappropriation to
other projects. The Administration is recommending that the funds be reappropriated as follows:
              • Lester Park Improvements
              • Union Station Museum/Campus
              • Acquisition and Demolition of County Jail (Municipal Block)
              • General Park Improvements (All Abilities Park)
              • 4th Street Park Netting
          Additional funds from General Fund fund balance are proposed for transfer to the Fleet and Facilities equipment
replacement reserve. The total proposed budget amendment is $18,530,850. All ARPA Funds must be encumbered by December
31, 2024, and expended by December 31, 2026.
          Mr. Sorensen stated that this item was first discussed during the December 3, 2024, meeting, but the Council has several
questions and concerns about the proposal. He expressed a willingness to discuss those concerns this evening. Vice Chair White
stated that she supports the use of the ARPA funds, but feels more discussion is needed on some of the intended uses of the funds.
She wondered if there is some mechanism to place the ARPA funding in a holding account associated with the Capital
Improvement Plan (CIP) and move money from that holding account to specific projects in the future when decisions are
finalized. Council Executive Director Eller-Smith stated that is an option and is something that Council staff has been exploring
with Administration.
           Council member Graf expressed hesitation regarding the allocation for the demolition of the County Jail; he would rather
use more of the funding for the Lester Park project because he feels more money is needed to complete that project as designed.
He supported Vice Chair White’s suggestion for a holding fund for the ARPA monies, rather than deciding upon the allocations as
listed in the staff memo.
           The Council, Council staff, and Mr. Sorensen engaged in discussion about the options for allocating the ARPA monies as
directed by the Council. Mr. Sorensen concluded that he would create multiple budget amendment scenario documents for the
Council to consider during the December 17 meeting.
           Vice Chair White noted that the Council has not voted on the purchase of the jail and it may be premature to allocate
funding to the demolition of the building until the acquisition has been finalized. Council member Graf asked for input from City
Administration regarding the status of that issue. Chief Administrative Officer Brown noted Administration is working to secure
an appraisal for the building as well as estimates for demolition costs for the building; updated information will be provided to the
Council when available. She thanked the Council for their feedback regarding the use of ARPA funds and noted that she believes
Administration can develop a funding plan that will allocate the appropriate amount of money to the Lester Park project, while
still addressing other needs throughout the community.


         The meeting adjourned at 5:34 p.m.
