BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY
This application is a request for approval of a Final Subdivision Plat for approximately 9.22 acres located,
at approximately 425 West 11400 South (Exhibit B). The property is currently zoned R4 (Single-family
Residential, 10,000 square foot lot minimum). The applicant is requesting that a Final Subdivision Plat be
approved to allow for the development of 29 new single-family residential lots.

This property is part of the overall Big Willow Creek development. This development originally obtained
approval in January 2017 for 115 dwelling units on 49.75 acres. The approval included a Development
Agreement limiting the density to a maximum of 2.3 du/ac (dwelling units per acre), and requires a
variety of lot sizes from 20,000 square feet to 10,000 square feet in size. Preliminary plat approval for all
115 lots was approved by the Planning Commission on July 18, 2017. Phases 1A and 1B obtained final
plat approval on September 18, 2018, with an amended plat approved for these phases on November 21,
2019.

In April 2019 the applicant amended the development agreement (1st Amendment Development
Agreement) in order to add 18.1 acres of property into the Big Willow Creek Development. The 1 st
Amendment of the Development Agreement rezoned some of the additional 18.1 acres of property to the
RM2 zone, but limited any development to a maximum of 9 du/ac. The remainder of the property was
rezoned to R4.

On December 10, 2019 the applicant obtained a rezone and land use amendment for an additional 4.12
acres of land located adjacent to the development. This area was not included in the development

ANALYSIS

General Plan and Zoning. The Land Use Map of the General Plan calls for the Residential Medium
Density land use designation for the subject property (Exhibit C). This category is characterized as
follows:




The property has been assigned the R4 zoning classification, supporting up to four dwelling units per acre
(Exhibit D). According to Draper City Municipal Code (DCMC) Section 9-8-020 the purpose of the
R4 zone is to “permit medium to medium-high density residential developments with single-family
homes. These zones are intended to foster thriving and well maintained neighborhoods that allow upscale
single-family homes on smaller lots that require less maintenance and provide a balanced lifestyle.” The
R4 zoning abuts the subject property on all four sides.

Subdivision Layout. The proposed plat will add 29 new lots into the Big Willow Creek development

(Exhibit E). Per the original Development Agreement, six of the lots are required to have a minimum lot
size of 13,000 square feet, with the rest having a minimum of 10,000 square feet. The proposed plat
complies with this standard. The development will connect to two existing stub streets within Phase 1B,
and create three new stub roads. Two of the stub roads will be to future phases to the east, and one will
lead to future phases to the north of the Phase 2 boundary. In addition, a new roadway connection from
the north in Phase 6 will run through Phase 2 and connect to Phase 1B. All of the roadways are proposed
to be public and comply with the 60-foot width cross section standard for local roads.

2nd Amendment Development Agreement. The Big Willow Creek development has one access point from
11400 South and a second access point to the south through the Jenson Farms development. The Jenson
Farms connection has been delayed pending the design of a bridge over Willow Creek. This has restricted
Big Willow Creek to the 30 lots that were approved with the Phase 1B plat as it only has one access until
the bridge is built. In anticipation of the bridge crossing obtaining approval later this year, and to help
facilitate construction of Phase 2, the applicant obtained a 2nd amendment to the Development Agreement
(Exhibit F). This amendment allows the applicant to obtain approval for Phase 2 and begin construction
of the public infrastructure before the bridge in Jenson Farms is completed. It does set specific standards
as to when the plat can be recorded and does not allow the Phase 2 plat to be recorded, or any building
permits to be applied for, until the bridge construction drawings are approved and a bond posted for the
work, or the bridge has been constructed and accepted by the City. This will allow the applicant to work
on the installation of utilities and the roads while the bridge is constructed. The intent is that once the
bridge is constructed, the applicant will be ready to record the plat and pull permits.

Criteria For Approval. The criteria for review and potential approval of a final subdivision plat request is
found in Section 17-4-060 of the DCMC. This section depicts the standard of review for such requests as:

          “Within a reasonable time following the recommended approval of the final plat by the Zoning
          Administrator, the final plat shall be submitted to the Planning Commission for its review and
          consideration. A courtesy notice may be provided for the meeting in accordance with section 17-
          1-085 of this title, but is not required. The Planning Commission shall not be bound by the
          recommendations of the Zoning Administrator and may set its own conditions and requirements
          consistent with this title. If the Planning Commission determines that the final plat is in
          conformity with the requirements of this title, other applicable ordinances, and any reasonable
          conditions as recommended by the City's staff and Zoning Administrator or on the Planning
          Commission's own initiative, and that the Planning Commission is satisfied with the final plat, it
          may approve the final plat. If the Planning Commission determines that the final plat is not in
          conformity with this title or other applicable ordinances or any reasonable conditions imposed, it
          may disapprove the final plat specifying the reasons for such disapproval. No final plat shall have
          any force or effect unless the same has been approved by the Planning Commission and signed by
          the Mayor and City Recorder.”
