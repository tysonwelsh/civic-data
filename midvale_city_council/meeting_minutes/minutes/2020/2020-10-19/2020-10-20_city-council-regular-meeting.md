with Agricultural Overlay (SF-1/AO) to the Multifamily Residential - Medium Density Zone

(RM-12). This rezone is intended to allow for construction of a 128-unit residential master

planned development project. The property currently contains several single-family

homes and a church building. The proposed redevelopment would demolish the existing
buildings and construct 78 new single-family detached dwellings and 50 new townhomes.

This request is a revision of an earlier rezone request Mr. Nash submitted in 2019,

reflecting changes made in response to comments received from the Planning

Commission, City Council, and surrounding neighbors. Minutes from the December 3,

2019 Council meeting, where this earlier iterator was considered, have been attached.

The Planning Commission considered both the request for a rezone and a Large Scale

Master Plan for the proposal in a public rein on May 13, 2020 and a follow-up public

meeting on September 9, 2020. The Commission adopted decisions to not recommend

approval of the rezone to the City Council and table the Large Scale Master Plan pending

a decision from the Council on the rezone. A copy of the Planning Commission minutes

for these meetings as well as letters of support from the applicant and Mountain View

Christian Assembly have been attached to this Report.

Rezones are legislative decisions required lhenever a change in designation on the

City’s Zoning Map is requested. The Zoning Ordinance includes requirements for findings

and procedures necessary to consider and adopt a rezone. The General Plan provides

direction on the City’s future land use goals and must be considered whenever a rezone
request is made. |

ORDINANCE CONSIDERATIONS: |

Section 17-3-1 of the Midvale City Municipal Code requires the Planning Commission

hold a public hearing and adopt a written recommendation to the City Council, advising

the Council to approve, disapprove, or micay request on all zoning map amendments.

The City Council may only approve a rezone request if it determines, in written findings,

that the proposed rezoning is consistent with the policies and goals of the general plan

and that the applicant has demonstrated the following:

Ts The proposed rezoning is necessary either to comply with the general plan
proposed land use map or to provide land for a community need that was not
anticipated at the time of adoption of the general plan:

Bn The existing zoning was either the result of a clerical error or a mistake of fact, or
that it failed to take into account the constraints on development created by the
natural characteristics of the land inquuding but not limited to, steep slopes,
floodplain, unstable soils, and inadequ te drainage; or

oh The land or its surrounding environs nae changed or is changing to such a degree

The discussion in the General Plan Considerations section below addresses how the
proposed rezoning is consistent with the 2016 Midvale City General Plan. |
The most applicable criteria for this request is the third. The existing lots were originally
created to be used for agricultural purposes in the 1950s. In the years since, the area
transitioned away from an agricultural use to a higher density residential use piece by
piece until even the subject properties, the last of the large lots in the area, no longer see
regular agricultural use. With the other projects that have been developed along 8000
South in the decades since, the surrounding environs have changed and continue to
change. Rezoning from SF-1/AO to RM-12 will make the zoning designation on these
properties consistent with other properties in the area and encourage redevelopment of
underutilized property. Tying this rezone request to the Large Scale Master Plan and
Development Agreement guarantees the rezone responds to concerns raised during the
public process and that the project proposed is the project constructed.
GENERAL PLAN CONSIDERATIONS:
The 2016 Midvale City General Plan locates the subject properties in a Stability Area,
near the border of the Middle State Street and South State Street Opportunity Areas. The
proposal to rezone the subject properties from SF-1/AO to RM-12 is generally supported
by the General Plan because it allows for redevelopment in a manner consistent with the
surrounding neighborhood and satisfies the land use goals for Stability Areas in general.
The General Plan states that:
“In Stability Areas, the current overall land use mix is desirable and preservation of these :
areas’ character and function are the desired future condition. These areas are nearly
fully
developed and have little foreseeable change in the types or intensity of land uses in the
future.
In Opportunity Areas, minor-to-major changes in current land uses are likely to occur due
to market forces, increasing land values, and opportunities to optimize land uses to take
advantage of transit and other public investments. Projects in Opportunity Areas are
anticipated to be at higher levels of density than current land uses and should be carefully
planned and designed to integrate into the fabric of the area and minimize impacts on
adjacent and nearby existing land uses.”
The subject property is in a neighborhood that has been transitioning over the past two
decades from single family detached residential to medium density residential uses in the
form of townhome and duplex style developments. 8000 South is lined with projects using
this medium density style of development, including White Pines, Creekside Townhomes,
Auburn Townhomes, Pinstripe Cove, and others. The broader area also has seen this
shift to medium or higher density developments; nearby projects not located on 8000
South include the Candlestick Apartments complex and the Station Place Condominium
project. A few single-family dwellings remain scattered throughout the area but are
generally located on property that is challenging to redevelop individually, requiring
consolidation or coordination between multiple property owners, and are outnumbered by

The subject properties fall outside the boundaries of the Middle and South State Street

Opportunity Areas, which both have goals supporting substantial redevelopment and

intensification of uses along State Street. However, due to their general proximity to the

eastern boundary of both Opportunity Areas, redevelopment of the subject parcels
strengthens the buffer between the higher intensity uses in the Opportunity Areas to the

| north and west and the lower density residential uses to the south and east of the subject
properties, consistent with Stability Area Goal #2. Due to the open space requirements
for master planned developments, the rezone would also create more park space for
residents of the new development to use and provide additional trail opportunities and
users in the area. |

The rezone and associated project are rath supported by the City’s Housing Plan,

adopted in 2019 as an appendix to the fener Plan. The Housing Plan adopted 6 goals

to guide decision making on housing-related di cisions:

1. Maintain and strengthen stable neighBarhoods. This goal includes preserving the
quality and character of existing neighborhoods; providing neighborhoods with
better connectivity and access to recreational amenities; and ensuring that infill
and adjacent development is compatible with the existing neighborhood.

2. Maintain and improve the quality of existing housing stock in Midvale and revitalize
the physical and social fabric of mesg kee reteset that are in decline.

2. Expand the variety of housing opportunities to allow for more choices in types and
locations of residences. This includes providing for a mixture of housing sizes,

j densities, types and affordability in eacl area of the City.

4. Support the development of more affordable housing in appropriate locations, i.e.
near transit, retail, commercial, schools and recreational amenities. l

5, Encourage higher density residential in appropriate locations in opportunity areas
to create the market needed for viable commercial development.

6. Continue to encourage a variety of housing types, sizes, and pricing with new

This proposed rezone and project support these goals. Existing neighborhoods in the

area will benefit from the improvement of under- and unutilized property. The proposed

project includes a mix of housing types that are similar to all of the surrounding

neighborhoods. Existing housing stock will face pressure to stay in prime condition in

order to compete with new development nearby. In response to comments and concerns

from public meeting attendees, the project has been revised to include two housing types,

including one that is less common in Midvale (single-family detached units on small lots).

This property’s location places it near existing schools and commercial opportunities

along State Street. It also provides additional pressure for future commercial projects to

develop sooner in response to an expansion of the available customer market.

constructed, Staff recommends the Council table a decision on the requested rezone until

the same time.

A. CONSIDER ORDINANCE 2020-0-18 TO ENACT MIDVALE MUNICIPAL

CODE SECTION 3.02.075 CARES ACT EXPENDITURES
Lisa Garner said on March 27, 2020, the Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security
Act, commonly known as the CARES Act, was signed into law. The CARES Act provided
funds to state and local governments to cover costs that “1. are necessary expenditures
incurred due to the public health emergency with respect to the Coronavirus Disease
2019 (COVID-19); 2. were not accounted for in the budget most recently approved as of
March 27, 2020 for the State or government; and 3. were incurred during the period that
begins on March 1, 2020, and ends on December 30, 2020.” Midvale received $1,987,354
under the CARES Act.
As a result of the COVID-19 pandemic, the City has taken necessary steps to protect its
staff and members of the public. These steps initially included actions such as
encouraging staff to telecommute, providing personal protective equipment for
employees, and hosting public meetings electronically. As the COVID-19 pandemic has
continued, City staff has identified additional needs that would facilitate continued
protection and improved service for the City and its residents under the current COVID-
19 conditions. Some of these needs—such as the small business loan program,
emergency tree maintenance, and a cloud-based telephone system—have already been
completed or are in the process of being completed. So far, approximately $99,500 of
Midvale’s CARES Act funds has been spent. An additional $714,540 has been earmarked
for 18 different projects to be completed before the end of the year.
Approximately 12 additional COVID-19 related needs have been identified by City staff.
However, with the deadline of December 30 quickly approaching, it is unlikely that the
City will be able to fulfill these needs under the City’s current procurement requirements.
Under Midvale Municipal Code 3.02.060, any expenditure over $25,000 is required to go
through the formal bidding process. This process generally takes at least two months to
complete because the projects must be put out to bid. With a deadline of December 30,
a project put out to bid today would only allow a vendor 10 days in the middle of the winter
holidays to complete the project. 2
Due to these unique circumstances, City staff is requesting that City Council adopt
Section 3.02.075 CARES Act Expenditures. The proposed ordinance would allow City
staff to expediate the procurement of these projects by allowing CARES Act-funded
projects to go through the informal bidding process rather than the formal bidding process.
This process would still be competitive but may not provide as many responses as the
formal bidding process. City staff would be required to seek written bids from at least
three vendors who provide the needed item or service. The vendor would be required to
provide the item or service before the CARES Act deadlines of December 30. The
department head and the procurement officer would identify the responsible responsive |
bidder. All other requirements of the City’s procurement code would remain in effect
including ethical and approval requirements.

C. DISCUSSION TO ALLOW THE MAYOR TO ENTER INTO A BOUNDARY

LINE AGREEMENT WITH PARTIES TO CORRECT MISTAKE IN LEGAL
DESCRIPTION INVOLVING RIO GRANDE STREET .

Lisa Garner said Midvale City has been made aware of several errors in legal
descriptions, affecting many different pieces of properties, that have existed for many
years in the area of Rio Grande Street and Seventh Avenue. It appears that there was a
legal description recorded some time ago that overlapped onto an adjacent parcel and
shifted the boundary lines to all successive parcels. However, the actual physical
boundaries of these properties have been recognized by the property owners for over 20
years by fence lines. To clean up this issue, the property owners will need to enter
boundary line agreements with the neighboring properties to make the legal descriptions
match the actual and historical boundaries.
According to the Salt Lake County Recorder, a portion of Rio Grande Street is currently
owned by Chick & Jack Repairs & Distributing, Inc., who own the property east of Rio
Grande Street. As part of that same parcel, there is a small piece of property also included
in the Rio Grande Street right of way that should be owned by LeBaron Company, LLC.
It is believed that this mistake originated when the City was dedicated the right of way for
Rio Grande Street. As the City has no need for this property, and as it was clearly an error
in the legal description at the time of the dedication, it is the recommendation of staff that
Midvale City enter into a boundary line agreement with both property owners: Chick &
Jack Repairs & Distributing, Inc. and LeBaron Company, LLC.
Additionally, it was discovered that Salt Lake County acquired a portion of Rio Grande :
Street from a tax sale back in 1938. Salt Lake County has no desire to keep this small
portion of Rio Grande Street and will be either deeding this to Midvale City or entering
into a boundary line agreement to correct this error. _
