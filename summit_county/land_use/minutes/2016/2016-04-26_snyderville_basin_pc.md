APPLICANT’S PRESENTATION 
 
Mr. Tyler said because of the input they have received from the SPBC, he believes they 
have been able to come up with a better design.  He described the process and timeline 
to get to this point.  He said they made an effort to meet early in the design process with 
Planner Northrup-Robinson and Director Putt.  He gave the dates of the different 
meetings they had with Staff.  He feels there has been successful collaboration along the 
way.  They have tried to incorporate the feedback into a successful design. 
 
Mr. Tyler next addressed the engineering of the project.  He said they came up with the 
following five things that they feel are critical from an engineering perspective:  
 
1. The municipal separate storm system.  They have been working with the 
Engineering Staff and the Master Developer.  The Canyon’s SPA was designed as its 
own storm water system with off-site retention for each parcel.  Staff is in the 
Snyderville Basin Planning Commission 
April 26, 2016 
Page 4 of 43 
 
process of reviewing the submitted information and working out the specific 
requirements.  The completion of the storm system will be a condition of the 
building permit.    
 
Mr. Tyler noted that not all sites in the Resort Core have the ability to detain or 
retain water.  This is especially true for projects where the property line is the 
footprint of the building.  As a result, the SPA process came up with a system that 
designated flow areas around the Canyons and designated detention and retention 
areas that would collect storm water and allow it to percolate through before it is 
discharged.  This is something the Master Developer has taken care of.   
 
2. They had a geotechnical analysis study completed.  He said the State Code limits 
slopes to 3-to-1.  The SPA allows a 2-to-1 slope because it recognizes that the 
majority of the development is on a hillside.  They are showing 2-to-1 slopes with 
the understanding that when they start digging, a geotechnical consultant will be 
present on an almost daily basis during excavation with the intent to reduce the cuts 
as much as possible.  Information will be sent to the Engineering Staff who will 
indicate if the slope can be changed to the 1-to-1 slope.  Staff seems to be 
comfortable with this arrangement.  Mr. Tyler said until they cut the ground, they 
won’t know the specific makeup of the ground.  They believe they will frequently be 
able to utilize 1-to-1 slopes.   
  
3. Mr. Tyler said he has had a lot of conversations with the RVMA and the Master 
Developer about connectivity.  Their intent is to handle the pedestrian connections 
within the resort core as part of the master plan redesign.  The RVMA has two 
concerns about the sidewalk.  One is the long term maintenance of the sidewalk.  
Once it is installed, someone has to clear it, salt it, and make sure no ice is left on it.  
That is compounded by the second issue which is that High Mountain Road has a 
significant slope.  They are not sure what the solutions or compromises might be.
