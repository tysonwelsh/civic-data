Background

This is a public hearing for a Conditional Use for a Home Occupation “Voyagers in Learning,” an
in-home kindergarten for children to be located at 2363 West Frontier Street. The property is .27-
acre in the southernmost center of the city. The subdivision is Homestead Estates and the
property is zoned R-4. The lot has approximately 67 feet of frontage on Frontier Street. The
home is a split-level and has 2700- sf above grade with 440- sf in the basement. The attached
garage is 2-car with an extra-wide driveway.
The applicant is requesting approval of a conditional use home occupation permit for in-home
kindergarten classes. She is asking for permission to have 12 children per day. Classes would
be Monday through Thursday from 9 am to 3 pm. Ms. Chase is a certified, licensed teacher with
extensive teaching experience.
She does not anticipate traffic being an issue. Children would come and leave at staggered times
(a 15-minute window) to avoid traffic congestion. There will be no on-street parking, she has
sufficient driveway and parking space on her property. Ms. Chase’s traffic plan shows vehicles

entering and exiting from Frontier Street and utilizing her driveway for drop-off and pick-ups.
Child kindergarten classes are considered a child group activity and therefore have special
allowances in our City Home Occupation Ordinance. Child group activities are allowed more
visitors per day and more vehicle trips per day than a typical home occupation as approved by
the Commission. She anticipates having no more than 24 total vehicle trips per day for her 12
students.
The home is owned by applicant’s parents who support her business plan. Their letter of
permission is included. The backyard is fenced for play time. Diagrams also show the areas of
the home that are to be used for childcare.
This application comes to the Commission because ordinance states any child day care or other
child group activity that exceeds 8 children/students per day or any use that exceeds 8 vehicle
trips per day is a conditional use.

Recommendations

Staff is recommending approval of the home occupation with conditions listed above, including
allowing a maximum of twelve (12) children per day and a maximum of 24-vehicle trips per day.
All customer and parent parking must be off-street.

Notices were sent to all property owners within 300 feet of this site and posted on the City’s web-
page. At the time of this report, no public comments had been received.

Ordinance and Issues

Home based business may be allowed in any residential zone, if the business is able to follow the
rules and requirements of the Riverton City Home Occupation Ordinance found in Title 18, Chapter
190.

Riverton’s Ordinance has three (3) levels of Home Occupations based on intensity of use. As the
intensity of use increases, the level of scrutiny increases. Most home occupations are reviewed
and approved by City Staff. The Planning Commission only sees applications that are requesting
permission for certain things as defined by ordinance, or if the application doesn’t fit within the
ordinance that allows staff to approve it, or if there is public clamor about a certain application.

The Ordinance in 18.190 gives staff and the Commission criteria for evaluating home-occupations.
Staff can approve some home occupations that have customers or employees if the proposed
business can meet all of the Fixed Standards (18.190.060) and all of the Qualifications (18.190.070)
set forth in the ordinance.

If an application can meet the Fixed Standards, but not meet all of the Qualifications it must be
reviewed by the Planning Commission. Additionally, some uses are listed as Conditional Use (see
18.190.080) which automatically come before the Planning Commission. Any home occupation that
cannot meet the fixed standards can be denied, and the ordinance expressly prohibits some uses
(see 18.190.090).

The Commission cannot alter a Fixed Standard but can alter any of the Qualifications if the
Commission finds a less strict rule does not harm the neighborhood or create a greater impact.
Conversely, the Commission may tighten a Qualification in order to protect a neighborhood based
on the particular use or setting.

        18.190.080 Conditional use permit required.

        The following uses are appropriate as licensable home occupations only if they are determined
        to be compatible with residential neighborhoods after full conditional use review by the planning
        commission, all of the fixed standards, all the qualifications that have not been granted an
        exception through the conditional use process and additional regulations set forth hereafter:

        (9) Child day care and other group child activities (e.g., dance schools, preschools, music
        classes, other care, or instruction for children) that are expected to generate or exceed eight
        children/students any day. The following guidelines shall be used by the planning commission to
        determine the maximum number of students/children permitted:

        (a) A traffic plan has been reviewed and approved by the planning department, which includes
        acceptable traffic flow, drop-off and turnaround areas.

        (b) A maximum of 12 students/children per session and a maximum of 24 students/children per
        day shall be permitted.

        (c) A maximum of three sessions per day may be permitted.

        (d) All sessions combined shall not generate more than 24 vehicular trips per day.

        (e) The total number of students/children shall include the licensee’s and any employees’
        children if they are under six years of age and are under the care of the licensee at the time the
        home occupation is conducted.

        (f) No child day care or group child activities, conditional use home occupation may be
        established within 300 feet as measured from property line to property line of another child day
        care or group child activities, conditional use home occupation.
