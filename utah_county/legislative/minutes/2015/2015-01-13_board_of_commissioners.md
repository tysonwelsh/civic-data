3.     DISCUSSION REGARDING BUS RAPID TRANSIT (BRT) FINANCIAL OBLIGATIONS
Commissioner Lee noted his intention for an open public discussion regarding the proposed Provo/Orem Bus Rapid
Transit (BRT) project. He mentioned commitments made to constituents during the election process, and the Utah
County financial obligation of $65,000,000 for BRT which would need to be bonded for sometime during this year.

Commissioner Lee questioned the false rumor that a former legislative body can bind the vote of a future legislative body,
asking whether it is true that last year’s Board of Commissioners could bind the actions that must be taken by this year’s
Board. Utah County Civil Division Chief Deputy Attorney E. Kent Sundberg responded that some people would argue
that yes; a prior Commission can bind a future Commission in a couple of ways. One of those ways could be through
bonds because of their long-standing nature. Mr. Sundberg was not sure if interlocal agreements can also be binding in
long-term situations, but clarified that the previous Board did enter a contract with the Utah Transit Authority (UTA)
promising to issue $65,000,000 worth of bonds from the Section 2218 tax revenues if UTA receives a ‘Small Starts
Grant’ from the federal government. The interlocal agreement between the county and UTA would be terminated if the
federal grant is not awarded, but there could be consequences if the Board does not proceed with bonding in that case.

Commissioner Lee asked about the wording within the Utah Transit Authority agreement in which “the County agrees
to promptly issue bonds,” and the difference between a binding vote and contract. Kent Sundberg pointed out the Board
could disagree with a previous Commission contract and decide not to follow through, but may be responsible for costs
or damages incurred by UTA. Commissioner Lee asked the Board members for their opinions and Commissioner
Ellertson stated the ground work has been laid in order to move forward if the federal grant is awarded. Commissioner
Lee asked if they could vote against the agreement, and Commissioner Ellertson reiterated Mr. Sundberg’s explanation:
yes, but there would be the risk of consequences. Commissioner Graves added that Utah County has committed per the
terms of the contract.

Commissioner Lee asked about the overall financial obligation of Utah County in bonding for $65,000,000. Kent
Sundberg explained how the dedicated revenue stream for this project already exists, and Commissioner Ellertson
confirmed there is sufficient funding in that source for this bond. Commissioner Ellertson noted that source has been
used for road work for several years, and this decision was made with majority support from mayors and other elected
officials in the county. Mr. Sundberg clarified how statute requires an MPO (Metropolitan Planning Organization)
recommend uses for the Section 2218 quarter-cent sales tax; that vote must take place before the Commission can take
any action through an interlocal agreement with UTA. Sufficient revenue has been projected for the bonds, operation,
and maintenance expenses. There was some discussion regarding a separate $10,000,000 in-kind contribution match
from the cities and the Utah Department of Transportation.

Commissioner Lee paraphrased comments from financial advisors indicating “we are maxing ourselves out,” and finding
ourselves “dangerously on the edge of our bonding.” Commissioner Ellertson disagreed, stating there must have been
some miscommunication because a presentation would be forthcoming from the same financial advisors to demonstrate
the opposite. Commissioner Lee also expressed concern about expanding public transit only in Provo and Orem while
there is so much growth happening in Eagle Mountain and Saratoga Springs. Commissioner Ellertson reiterated his
differing opinion, emphasizing there is no general obligation debt in Utah County - no property taxes go to pay debt.

Commissioner Graves commented on the different percentages of growth for the Eagle Mountain and Saratoga Springs
areas versus Provo and Orem; it is comparing a beginning base of 25,000 residents to one of 250,000 residents. More
than forty-five percent of the county reside in Provo and Orem and there is a great need for transportation. Commissioner
Graves reminded the Board that he initially addressed bonding during his campaign for office, and Commissioner
Ellertson mentioned ongoing projects and revenue streams that would be available for additional transportation.
Commissioner Lee repeated his concern over future unfunded projects, stating he is worried about the county as a whole
and leveraging too much to serve properly.

                                            PUBLIC COMMENTS
Orem resident Wayne Burr commented on Work Session Item No. 3 related to the discussion of Bus Rapid Transit. Mr.
Burr asked if the federal government has sent the money to Utah Transit Authority, and Commissioner Ellertson
responded no; they are still in the process. Mr. Burr requested the Board write to the federal government and ask them
not to send those funds. He also asked the commissioners to consider spending $10,000,000 on any potential lawsuits
in order to save Utah County $40,000,000, and Commissioner Ellertson explained how the funds earmarked for
transportation could not legally be used for such matters. Mr. Burr asked the Board to give the public a break.

Orem resident Bob Womack read a prepared statement, using the adage “figures lie and liars figure” to express
opposition to the Bus Rapid Transit project. He provided statistics of residents per square mile in Orem and Provo
compared to major density areas like New York and San Francisco, stating BRT would be marginally beneficial at best.
Mr. Womack said the project would not positively impact air quality or traffic issues, only straining the budget for many
years to come. He suggested that avoiding a vote on the issue may allow additional negotiation on the bonding, and
asked the Board to “take my money carefully; spend my money wisely.”

Grant Pearson is a Lehi resident concerned about BRT. He mentioned specific bus lanes in West Valley City, stating
the lanes are often idle and an annoyance to other drivers. Mr. Pearson expressed the lane would be much better used
for traffic, and does not want Utah County to spend so much money on an unnecessary project. He advised against
making a rushed purchase simply because the funds are available, and asked the Board to reconsider approval.

Springville resident Ken Bowers approached the bench. He noted there is no denial that Utah County is growing in
population. Mr. Bowers pointed out that if the bonding for Bus Rapid Transit is denied, roads will become more
clogged. This will force people to carpool and encourage companies to step forward with privately funded transit
options. Mr. Bowers stated government should not be establishing private enterprises; the roads may be temporarily
congested, but the market will eventually find a solution. He encouraged the Board not to get into the habit of bonding
the county into perpetual debt. Mr. Bowers stated the BRT will never pay for itself and will require future tax increases.

Jennifer Baptista is a Pleasant Grove resident who has researched the Utah Transit Authority. She is concerned that Utah
County is partnering with UTA when there was a 200-page audit submitted to the legislators regarding their business
practices. Ms. Baptista mentioned the low tax base of Pleasant Grove, and the reluctance of their citizens to bond for
$12,600,000 Public Works Building project; how can the city now be asked to bond for a major project that will not even
benefit them? She stated that Pleasant Grove City will not be inclined to participate in this deal. Lastly, Ms. Baptista
asked the Board not to get involved with the proposed recycling program as outlined in Work Session Item No. 1 on
today’s calendar. She disagrees with Richard Guernsey’s attempt to subsidize the program through Pleasant Grove City.

Lewis Billings, former Provo City Mayor, came forward to state financing for BRT is not the issue that should be before
the Board today. The community decided years ago that mass transit alternatives should be used for the growing
population, but it is critical to address mass transit timely and in a way that is financially sustainable. Mr. Billings
encouraged financial prudence and the importance of helping people through the best possible value. He noted the
proper alignment for Bus Rapid Transit has not been fully determined, mentioning University Avenue is already a high-
stress corridor and will now have trip and vehicle interruption. Mr. Billings has asked for incurred vehicle trip statistics
before and after the implementation of BRT but they have not been made available to him. He cautioned against turning
Provo’s University Avenue into Orem’s State Street once the trees have been removed and the buildings are at the
minimum setback requirements. Mr. Billings commented on the importance of quality of life and suggested alternate
routes for BRT, stating if something is going to work, it needs to be the right thing at the right time.

Loy Brunson is a Provo resident who attended the swearing-in ceremony when the commissioners took the Oath of Office
on January 5, 2015. He reminded the Board that they swore to God and to the people to uphold the Constitution, and
asked them to look at Amendments 5, 9, and 13 again before violating civil and private property rights by approving the
Bus Rapid Transit project. Mr. Brunson talked about just compensation and action that could be brought against the
Board if they force something on others when they do not want it; he exclaimed the Board should not deny or disparage
the rights of the people.

Orem resident Sharon Anderson came forward in agreement with previous agreements against the Provo/Orem Bus Rapid
Transit project. She is worried that actual costs for BRT may have been underestimated. Ms. Anderson quoted articles
related to financial consequences for local government when misled on long-term projects. She is concerned about
transparency between levels of government, and information being withheld from City Council members. Ms. Anderson
noted Commission meeting minutes and associated documents related to the interlocal agreement with Utah Transit
Authority were not available online, and suggested they are being hidden from the public. Ms. Anderson repeated
citizens are either manipulated or uninformed about the facts on BRT, and encouraged the Board to vote against it.

Julie Reneer and Janet Russon are both Provo residents. They approached the bench together to speak in opposition to
the Provo/Orem Bus Rapid Transit project. Ms. Reneer stated she learned about BRT by accident even though she lives
on the proposed route for travel. She does not think the Utah Transit Authority has adequately notified the public
regarding this project. Ms. Reneer has been raised with a “no waste, no debt” philosophy, and is concerned by the empty
busses that now pass her home without any passengers. She believes the current system can accommodate inevitable
community growth. Ms. Reneer pointed out that Brigham Young University is preparing to offer their own shuttle
service, which will further decrease the ridership on UTA busses. She mentioned the inconvenience of using public
transit even when located near a bus stop, and suggested the funds for BRT be spent in outlying areas such as Saratoga
Springs where growth is more likely to occur. Ms. Reneer reiterated that the current busses should be filled before
adding more, and Janet Russon concurred with Ms. Reneer’s statements. Ms. Russon also added comments about heavy
traffic along 900 East potentially disrupting emergency response teams.

Orem resident Barbara Petty read a statement from Erin Madsen, wife of Utah State Senator Mark B. Madsen, against
the Provo/Orem Bus Rapid Transit project. The letter identified bad financial planning and lack of service to the North
and South ends of Utah County as problems with BRT, and called the decision to spend so much in bonds for a small
percentage of the constituency ‘baffling.’ Ms. Petty asked the commissioners to reconsider and vote against BRT.

Alpine resident Ed Barfuss explained eighty-four trees would be removed for the implementation of Bus Rapid Transit.
The trees would be valued at approximately $10,000 each, and can add between six and eighteen percent value to
property. They are also good for the environment. Mr. Barfuss noted these as additional considerations in providing
public transportation for residents.

Derek Whetten lives in Orem. He initially voted to support BRT through the Orem Transportation Committee, but is
concerned about management by the Utah Transit Authority. Mr. Whetten also would prefer the route run through
Brigham Young University campus, but overall finds BRT to be the right compromise for cost and mass transit. He pays
taxes and entrusts the elected leaders to provide municipal services and to proactively plan for the future, and therefore
supports BRT.

Pam Openshaw, Provo resident, is grateful for the new commissioners that will fight to stop Bus Rapid Transit if it is
not economically feasible. A lot of people will need to ride BRT in order to make a profit. Ms. Openshaw has read
reports indicating the amount of riders will increase if the busses travel more frequently, but does not believe this will
be the case because busses are still empty even in large cities. She agrees with previous comments that mass transit
should be a private enterprise.

Orem City Councilmember Hans Anderson has also been denied certain information related to Bus Rapid Transit by the
Mountainland Association of Governments (MAG). Mr. Anderson pointed out only fifteen percent of the project will
pay for itself; for each five-dollar fare purchased by a bus rider, the general public would be expected to contribute $4.25.
He also indicated that the likelihood of Utah Transit Authority suing Utah County for breaking any contract would be
slim. Mr. Anderson stated this agreement was made behind closed doors because the public would have voted against
it had they been given the opportunity to do so. He encouraged the Board to deny BRT.

Lehi resident and State Representative Jake Anderegg serves as the Vice-Chair for the House Transportation Committee,
and is sickened by the potential of increased taxes for road maintenance. He is concerned that bonding is not being used
optimally, mentioning that Bus Rapid Transit would better serve an East-West route for northern Utah County than
downtown Provo City. Mr. Anderegg also expressed discomfort with the approving vote for the project being one of
the final actions of the previous Commission, preventing them from publicly facing the ramifications of that decision.

Larry Ballard, Utah County resident, has been attending the MPO (Metropolitan Planning Organization) meetings and
does not think Bus Rapid Transit is ready for implementation. The regional board seems to be gaining power and
influence over government decisions. Mr. Ballard noted false assurances given to city leaders that BRT would not impact
any federal funds for future road projects. Mr. Ballard talked about the transition of city road to county road near his
home on the border of Salem City, and the dangers of speeding cars passing the irrigation canal. He questioned what
should be expected for the solution to local road issues if funds will be redirected for BRT.

Commissioner Ellertson thanked everyone for their public comments, and recognized the differences in opinions
expressed today. He clarified that there has been no intent to hide information. Commissioner Ellertson also disagrees
that the project has been hurried through, noting federal program deadlines. He reminded the audience the
commissioners are not the only ones who made this decision to move forward with Bus Rapid Transit; there were public
meetings, involvement from other cities, and a process through the Mountainland Association of Governments.
Commissioner Ellertson noted that taxes pay for roads; there are certain things that the private sector will not provide,
and some needs are fulfilled by the government.

Commissioner Lee asked the Utah County Attorney’s Office to begin researching the possible outcomes and penalties
of cancelling the current contract in place with the Utah Transit Authority.
