37      a. House Bill 411 – Community Renewable Energy Act: A Review
38
39          Rocky Mountain Power re. HB 411
40
41   Brad Knowles, Community Manager Rocky Mountain Power, presented information
42   regarding HB 411 and said that in the spring of 2019, the Utah Legislature enacted the
43   “Community Renewable Energy Act” (HB 411), which will create a framework for municipalities
44   like Salt Lake City, Park City and Moab to work with the power company toward the goal of
45   providing net 100% renewable energy by 2030. He said this would mean Rocky Mountain
46   Power would produce at least as much renewable energy as the city uses in a year. The
47   purpose of the Community Renewable Energy Act is to enable an electrical utility provider, such
48   as Rocky Mountain Power, to restructure its power generation system for delivery of electric
49   power sourced from renewable energy facilities. This naturally leads to the future construction of
50   power generating facilities that do not rely on fossil fuels.
51
     Page | 2 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     December 3, 2019



 1   Mr. Knowles said that HB 411 calls for, but does not require, municipalities to opt into a city-
 2   wide renewable energy program. He reviewed costs for the early implementation of the
 3   program. The cities that have opted in by December 31, 2019 will determine items such as what
 4   the anticipated rates, impact to customer rates would be, also the estimated participation of
 5   residents in the community into the program, and the cost of additional the renewable energy
 6   that would replace or early retire some of the thermal generation assets. He said the costs were
 7   unknown at this time; they will be studied, developed, and known later on in the process. Some
 8   of the considerations that a community might put forth in asking themselves whether this is a
 9   good fit are the estimated rate of participation. He said the cost would be solely born by the
10   municipalities that are in the program; they would not be spread out among all the rate payers at
11   Rocky Mountain Power.
12
13   Council Member Stewart expressed his concern with the “Opt-Out” option and the risk
14   associated with that, and Mr. Knowles addressed questions from the Council.
15
16   Council Members and Mr. Knowles discussed some of the towns and cities in Utah that have
17   expressed an interest in the program. They also discussed the need for consultants and
18   attorneys to help with the initial steps in setting up the parameters, regulations, rates, etc.
19   Interested cities and towns will each be asked to contribute for the consulting fees.

22       a. Proposed Ordinance No. 19-31 – Rezone of 3.18 Acres located at Approximately
23          10327 South Redwood Road from RR-22 (Rural Residential ½ Acre lots) to RM-6-
24          SD (Residential Multi-Family 6 Units per Acre, with Specific Development
25          Designations Restricting Unit Count and Lot Size, Ryan McDougal, Applicant
26
27   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that the rezone request was made
28   by Ryan McDougal, on behalf of his property and one adjacent property also participating in the
29   rezone. He said the proposed rezone area is on the east side of Redwood Road at
30   approximately 3027 South. The property is just north of the newly aligned Riverton Ranch Road.
31   There are contiguous properties which add up to the 3.22 acres. Like many parcels along the
32   east side of Redwood Road, these parcels were part of the UDOT Redwood Road Widening
33   Project and were vacant remnant pieces sold by UDOT and acquired by the applicants.
34
35   Mr. Lethbridge further explained that current zoning in the area was almost exclusively single
36   family residential, primarily RR-22, (Rural Residential, ½ acre lots with animal rights) along both
37   sides of Redwood and extending eastward, with some R-4 (Residential 10,000 sf lots) west of
38   Redwood. He said a notable exception in the area was the C-PO (Commercial Professional
39   Office) Zoning for the old Beckstead-Butterfield home, which was being restored and renovated
40   as a small office.
41
42   Mr. Lethbridge said that Redwood Road was a distinct boundary in the General Plan. He said
43   that on the east side of Redwood Road from 12800 South to the Bangerter Highway the
44   General Plan designation for the vast majority of property was Estate Density Residential
45   (minimum ½ acre lots). He said the property in the rezone request was also Estate Density
46   Residential.
47
48   Mr. Lethbridge said that the requested RM-6 zone was a residential multi-family zone, which
49   allowed for single family detached housing on small lots or attached housing such as
50   townhomes, twin-homes, or condos. The applicant proposed SD designations that allow the City
51   to include limitations on an underlying zone. The applicant also proposed limiting development

 1   of the property to single family, detached housing on individual lots. Lots adjacent to Redwood

12       a. Proposed Resolution No. 19-80 – Establishing a Community Renewable Energy
13          Program; Authorizing the Riverton City Manager to Explore Opportunities with
14          Rocky Mountain Power to Create a Community Renewable Energy Program; and
15          Related Matters
16
17   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, explained that in the spring of 2019, the Utah Legislature enacted
18   the “Community Renewable Energy Act” (HB 411), which is enclosed following the proposed
19   Resolution in the Council’s packet. The purpose of the Community Renewable Energy Act is to
20   enable an electrical utility provider, such as Rocky Mountain Power, to restructure its power
21   generation system for delivery of electric power sourced from renewable energy facilities. This
22   naturally leads to the future construction of power generating facilities which do not rely on fossil
23   fuels.
24
25   Mr. Carter said the legislation called for, but does not require, municipalities to opt into a city-
26   wide renewable energy program. If the Riverton City Council is interested in launching this
27   program, it must state its interest in seeing a supply of renewable energy be provided to
28   Riverton Residents by adopting a resolution which “states a goal of achieving an amount
29   equivalent to 100% of the annual electric energy supply for participating customers from a
30   renewable energy resource by 2030.” This opt-in resolution does not carry with it any financial
31   obligation. The Community Renewable Energy Act requires the Council to approve such a
32   resolution by December 31, 2019, if the Council elects to support such a program.
33
34   Mr. Carter explained that sometime following the adoption of the resolution, the City would then
35   be presented with an agreement that does financially obligate the City to pay for “third-party
36   expertise contracted for by the [Utah Division of Public Utilities], and the [Office of Consumer
37   Services], for assistance with activities associated with initial approval of the community
38   renewable energy program.” He said it was premature to know what the costs of such a
39   contract would be, but adopting a resolution did not require the City to execute the agreement.
40   Following adoption of a resolution and approval of an agreement to pay for expenses described
41   above, the City would also need to adopt an ordinance that enforces the agreement and any
42   additional terms or conditions required by the Utah Division of Public Utilities. Ultimately, the
43   costs of delivering power sourced from renewable energy, if any, would be borne by rate payers
44   within Riverton City.
45
46   Mr. Carter explained that the latter components (i.e. the agreement and ordinance) for launching
47   a community renewal energy program may take another year to develop. Therefore, the costs
48   associated with entering an agreement and the ultimate costs that ratepayers would experience
49   by funding renewable energy remains to be determined. In the event the costs become too
50   high, he said that adoption of the proposed resolution would not prevent the City from opting out
51   of the program in the future.
