IV.     Public Hearing on Proposed Amendments to Title 13 Regarding Accessory Dwelling Units.
Mayor Dahle reported that the above item related to Accessory Dwelling Units (“ADU”). The packet
included the Staff Report and a PowerPoint presentation outlining the specific changes made to
Title 13. Community and Economic Development Director, LaNiece Davenport shared the Staff
Report with City Council Members. She reported that staff had been considering a new ADU
ordinance for the City. This was the result of Legislation that came out of the 2021 General Session.
House Bill (“HB”) 82 requires all cities and counties to permit ADUs in all of the primarily residential
zones.

Staff had been looking at the ordinance and had opened it up to include External Accessory Dwelling
Units (“EADU”) and Internal Accessory Dwelling Units (“IADU”). Ms. Davenport shared the
PowerPoint presentation with Council Members and discussed the timeline. City staff originally
sought policy direction from the City Council and received direction from the Planning Commission.
They were now seeking a final review as well as additional direction and input from the public as it
related to the final draft ordinance. The next step would be to take action and adopt the ordinance at
the end of the month, assuming everything went according to plan.

Ms. Davenport reported that at the June 3, 2021, City Council Meeting, the City Council directed
staff to do the following:

   •   Not exclude IADUs in any of the City’s primarily residential zones;
   •   Not prohibit IADUs on small lots;
   •   Consider areas that may pose safety issues if an IADU is permitted; and
   •   Look at modifying the EADU ordinance in tandem with the IADU ordinance.

In terms of direction from the Planning Commission, much of the dialogue pertained to the permit
and licensing process. There was a considerable amount of feedback related to what the fees should
be. Ms. Davenport explained that a Fee Table was included in the Staff Report to show the possible

Ms. Davenport overviewed the purpose of an ADU with the City Council, which was as follows:

   •   To assist in the creation of new housing units; support more efficient use of existing housing
       stock and infrastructure; and provide housing that responds to changing family needs, smaller
       households, and increasing housing costs within the City and not a response to supplemental
       income or vacation rental opportunities.

She felt that statement accurately and appropriately designated the purpose of an ADU, but staff was
open to hearing additional feedback on the statement. Ms. Davenport believed that the City of
Holladay was doing its part as it relates to the housing supply issues that the State of Utah was dealing
with. They wanted to ensure that those who want to live in Holladay can do so.

Staff had decided to tackle the ordinance by looking at all of the definitions. Within Code Section
13.04.040, several different areas were addressed:

   •   Added new ADU definition - Pg. 1, lines 24-28;
   •   Added new IADU definition - Pg. 1, lines 29-31;
   •   Added new EADU definition - Pg. 1, lines 32-33;
   •   Deleted Pg. 1, lines 18-23 – redundant definition;
   •   Edited Dwellings (single-family, primary and rental) – Pg. 1, lines 37-39; Pg. 2, lines 1-4;
   •   Edited Family definition to remove “unrelated” – Pg. 2, lines 13-17; and
   •   Edited Guesthouse Caretaker Quarters – Pg. 2, lines 22-25.

Ms. Davenport explained that the Table of Allowed Uses was highlighted in Code Section 13.14.020.
The table specified that an IADU was permitted in any residential zone, EADUs were added to the
Table of Allowed Uses and the guesthouse/caretaker information was deleted as it was redundant. In
terms of violations, staff felt it made sense for ADUs to have the same violations that any home
would, so they had referred to Code Section 13.14.030 and 13.14.040. Any ADU that does not meet
the City Code must pay a fine and that fine would be based on the fee schedule.

IADUs were discussed in further detail. Ms. Davenport reported that Code Section 13.14.030 added
a new IADU section. She explained that most of the section was based on H.B. 82. It met the
Legislative requirements and also prohibited or regulated based on the Legislative requirements.
Some examples of things that would be prohibited or regulated included:

   •   Require a permit and license;
   •   Prohibit short-term rentals;
   •   Require off-road parking spaces; and

All of those items were spelled out in the ordinance to make it clear what is required of an applicant
when they apply for an ADU. The items would also be included on the annual license application as
a way to add further clarity for the applicant.

Ms. Davenport discussed EADUs further. She noted that staff had looked at the existing ordinance
and determined what needed to be modified based on direction from the City Council. There had
been some changes to the Definitions of Terms. For instance, guesthouses and caretaker quarters

language was modified to allow up to two units on any lot in single-family zones and also allowed
ADUs to be rented. Currently, the ordinance does not allow ADUs to be rented.

Ms. Davenport asked that the Council Members provide direction on two possible options for
EADUs:

   •   Option #1: Open up the ordinance to allow EADUs on all lots and possibly prohibit the size
       of the EADU based on lot size; and

   •   Option #2: Allow EADUs on lots that could be subdivided based on their lot size and acreage,
       as determined within the existing ordinance.

three main areas. Chief Hoyal reported that there were five patrol shifts with three officers on each

not change their response to calls, but they would be able to proactively patrol areas on an individual
level. He noted that this approach had worked in other areas and there had been a reduction in crime.

share updates with the City Council and Council Members were free to reach out with any concerns.

Mayor Dahle noted asked how often each of the areas will be patrolled. Chief Hoyal explained that

cover section 1(c). The second zone may be the afternoon shift or night shift, depending on the shift
bid assignment. Mayor Dahle wondered whether the shifts rotated so areas would be patrolled at

started in August and went through December. That was how long each of the officers would be
assigned to a particular area. Over the next several months, they would become very familiar with
the area. When the new bid took effect in January, the officers would be rotated and another shift
would move into each area. That means that each area would be monitored at different times of the
