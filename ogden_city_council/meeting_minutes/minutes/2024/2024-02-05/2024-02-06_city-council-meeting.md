Public Comments
          David Timmerman spoke to his desire for improved transparency and suggested the City’s website be updated and
improved. He also expressed his concern about the ‘looming eviction’ of the Northern Utah Wildlife Rehabilitation Center
(NUWRC); it is his understanding that the NUWRC is facing penalties if they are not completely out of the building by March 7,
but he would like to know why the deadline is so important and what the penalties are. He also still does not understand why the
NUWRC is being evicted and what project will be completed once the Center is closed. He concluded by offering comparisons
between the eviction of the NUWRC and the development of the MAKE Ogden Plan; he suggested that the City spend as much
time on other projects and services as they have on the NUWRC and the MAKE Ogden Plan.
          David Carlson discussed his history at the Ogden Airport as a hangar owner; all improvements he has made have been
done with approval of the Airport Manager, but in March of 2022, he learned that he would be shut down and his lease would be
terminated in November of 2022. He has been trying to communicate with the City since that time with no success, but he has
paid improper invoices for outstanding fees. He received a call today from someone from Breeze Airlines who asked for his help
with maintenance and repair of the ground equipment at the Airport and he had to inform the individual that he has been shut
down by the Ogden City Attorney and the Ogden Airport Manager.
          Kerry Wayne discussed outstanding issues with standing water on 1200 West, the road west of Business Depot Ogden on
the Marriott-Slaterville border. He also encouraged the Mayor and City Council to reconsider the eviction of the NUWRC. He
added that City Attorney Williams was the City Attorney under former Mayor, Mike Caldwell, and he suggested that Mayor
Nadolski consider terminating his employment; many of the bad decisions that were made in the past that have caused harm to
residents and business owners have had the support of the City Attorney’s office.
          Linda Sanchez thanked former Chair Choberka for always being responsive to the residents of Ogden. She welcomed
newly elected Council members as well. She reminded the elected officials of the City of their role and to always consider if
actions they are taking are constitutional. She then discussed recent actions taken by the Weber County Commission that deal with
water preservation and development in unincorporated areas of the County and suggested the City Council consider similar
measures. She thanked Council member Myers for the questions he asked during tonight’s work session meeting. She also
addressed matters of equality in the 2023 Utah Legislative Session.
          Teresa Bramwell also thanked former Chair Choberka for her kindness and responsiveness to residents. She then
discussed the definition of the real estate term “highest and best use” and asked everyone to consider who the term should apply
to. She believes that it should apply to the owner of a property, who should be able to realize the highest and best use of their
property and receive commensurate value. When the government uses the term, that means that politicians get to decide a
property’s highest and best use.
          Heath Satow addressed new information regarding the Brown Ice Cream building, which is in a far worse state of blight
than it was two years ago. He cited statements he made in the past regarding the manner in which the value of the building was
determined and how it was marketed for sale; he also expressed concern about the actions of City staff in recommending the sale
of the building to the developer selected by City Administration. He concluded the broker that created the lowered valuation of the
building now owns the building under a newly created LLC name; he is hopeful that with a new Director of the Community and
Economic Development the City will not be party to such ‘shady dealings’ and he asked the City Attorney to consider whether the
new property owner will be held to the original contractual deadline of November 2024 for renovations to be completed. The
argument provided by City Administration in the past regarding the lowered valuation of the building was to expedite the removal
of blight, but it has been 27 months and blight is only becoming worse. He hopes the City Council and Mayor will work together
to instill policy that will prevent something like this from happening in the future.
           Angel Castillo congratulated Chair Richey and Vice Chair White for their election to the leadership roles. She also
commented on her appreciation for the discussion during tonight’s work session regarding the Mayor’s efforts to work with the
State Legislature on many matters. She then addressed the Council’s discussion of the group dwelling ordinance during the
January 23, 2024, work session; she is pleased that smaller lots will be allowed, but she is concerned about the minimum dwelling
size of 2,500 square feet. This will result in the construction of $500,000 homes, which are not affordable by the ‘missing middle’.
She suggested the minimum unit sizes be reduced to make them more affordable, especially on smaller building lots. She then
stated she is also concerned about the NUWRC and the lack of plans for the reuse of the land on which the Center is currently
located.
           Adrianne Zubler also asked questions about the NUWRC; she thought the matter had been resolved months ago and she
does not understand what has changed. She inquired as to the differences between the Dinosaur Park and the NUWRC;
improvements that will be completed on the NUWRC land; why the Dinsoaur Park is given preference over the NUWRC; and
why dead animals are more important than live animals.
           Travis Pate commented on the success of recent special events in the community and thanked the Mayor and City
Council for participating in those events; he is concerned about preserving the history of the community and he encouraged the
Council and Mayor to enlist residents in those efforts, especially throughout 2024 when there will be many celebrations related to
the Union Station.

Mayor Comments
           Mayor Nadolski congratulated Chair Richey and Vice Chair White. He also thanked former Chair Choberka for her
service over the past year. He then addressed the comments regarding the Northern Utah Wildlife Rehabilitation Center
(NUWRC); during his campaign for Mayor, he communicated it was his goal to ensure success for both the NUWRC and the
Dinsoaur Park. The NUWRC agreed to a six-month lease contract extension, and they have done a great deal of positive work in
the community. He added that the Dinsoaur Park is also a very valuable asset to the Ogden community; the City has an agreement
with them to operate their facility on City land and their success is very important as well. Administration has been working to
help the NUWRC find a new location and he has visited personally with both the Dinsoaur Park and the NUWRC and he is
supportive of proceeding with termination of the lease as planned in order to avoid additional costs associated with delays of the
improvements being worked on by the Dinsoaur Park. However, he is willing to consider adjustments and support of City staff to
help the NUWRC to be prepared to move to their new location by the March 7, 2024, deadline. He stated he believes that this is
an opportunity for the community to come together to help both entities succeed, and he listed some things that could be done by
residents and contractors to accelerate the relocation of the Center. He would also like the Dinsoaur Park to unveil their plans for
the property in order to dispel the rumor that the NUWRC is being evicted in order to build another parking lot; he believes their
future plans are very exciting and will be supported by the community. He also commented on the feedback provided by Mr.
Wayne and noted that the City’s Public Services Director will be meeting with his counterpart at Marriott-Slaterville to work on a
solution for the standing water on 1200 West. He addressed Ms. Sanchez’s comments about water conservation and noted he
believes the County Commission was considering service delivery in the Ogden Valley, which is a much different matter than
what Ogden City considers due to infrastructure challenges in the Valley. He then thanked Mr. Pate for his comments about the
City’s history, noting Ogden turns 173 years old today; it was incorporated on February 6, 1851, and was the third city west of the
Missouri River to be incorporate, behind San Francisco and Salt Lake City. He concluded by reading an email he received from an
individual who visited Ogden City and found the residents to be very nice, the restaurants to be good, and the treatment of his wife
at a local hospital to be of high value. He stated he was so pleased to receive an email from someone who enjoyed visiting Ogden
City, especially when the City receives so much negative feedback. He stated he does not know the specifics of the experiences of
those who lease or own hangars at the Ogden Airport and he asked Mr. Carlson to reach out to him to discuss his situation further.
           Past Chair Choberka asked that City Administration research the details of the agreement with the purchaser of the
Brown Ice Cream building in order to provide an answer to Mr. Satow’s questions at a future date.
           Mayor Nadolski then briefly addressed Ms. Castillo’s comments about the group dwelling ordinance; it is his
understanding that the 2,500 measurement refers to lot sizes, not the dwelling size.
