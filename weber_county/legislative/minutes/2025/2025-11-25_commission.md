     2. Public Hearing to take input on the operating and capital budgets of Weber County for the 2026 calendar
        year.

            Steffani Ebert (County Comptroller): Our budget process started in August. Weber County is not proposing
            any tax increases for 2026. Most of the county's money comes from property taxes and sales taxes, but the
            county only keeps a small part (about 16-18%) of the total property tax collected. The General Fund (which
            pays for things like public safety and general services) spends the largest amount (69%) on employee pay
         and benefits. There is a near term challenge with the paramedic services fund, which is expected to have a
         $1.2 million shortage in 2026. County savings will be used to cover it for now but will need to review the
         issue next year. Capital project plans include fixing up existing buildings like the Weber Center and working
         on the expansion of the medical facility at the jail. The County will add two part-time staff in the County
         Attorney’s Office and an outreach coordinator at the animal shelter.

         Public Comment: None.
         Commissioner Harvey: If anyone has questions related to tonight’s budget presentation, please feel free to
         contact any of the Commissioners. I want to compliment our staff. While many of the surrounding counties
         have increases of 20-30%, our staff made necessary reductions to absorb the compensation increases. I am
         grateful for them. I appreciate our first responders. We will follow the results of the compensation study and
         remain fair with our compensation. Our Sheriff has been very supportive of the three Commissioners during
         this study.
         Chair Bolos: The added Animal Control position is paid out of a separate fund outside of the General Fund.
         The additional amount requested by the Sheriff was $700k but our HR Director felt strongly further
         adjustments should be made resulting in more than double the requested amount. To meet a portion of that
         increase, the Sheriff’s department came up with over $300k in savings. Instead of a tax increase, our
         Commissioners prioritized our employees over moving money over to Capital budgets as in prior years.


    3. Public hearing to take input on compensation increases of executive county officers included in the 2026
       Operating and Capital Budgets. Pursuant to UCA §17-66-401.

         Steffani Ebert (County Comptroller): State statute requires a public hearing for any increases to salaries for
         County elected and executive officials. Elected officials are proposed to receive a 3% pay increase. Other
         executive officers will receive increases based on results of the compensation study, which will be 3.5% for
         most and higher for anyone who’s current pay was found to be below market average.
