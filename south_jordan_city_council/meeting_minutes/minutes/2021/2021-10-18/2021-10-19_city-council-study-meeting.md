Planning Director Steven Schaefermeyer said regarding the rezone, it’s pretty straight forward
and the Planning Commission recommended approval unanimously.

City Attorney Ryan Loose explained the resolution that was added allowing the Mayor to sign an
agreement regarding the Glenmoor Golf Course. Earlier in the day he received a call from a
council member who suggested a larger gap period between when they exercise the given option
and when we would have to repurchase it; it’s highly unlikely that would happen. He contacted
the attorney for the owners of Glenmoor and was told that’s fine, instead of a five month
separation in time, it is now one year separation in time.

Council Member Brad Marlor said he was the one who suggested the larger gap. He has no
worries about problems in this case, but he has seen problems with other deals in the past and the
current time frame would only give us five months to react. His preference is that we have
enough time to find a buyer so it doesn’t hit the balance for the year.

Attorney Loose said Glenmoor extended this until mid-2023, they actually own the property they
want to use and are remediating it. Things are moving along; we know where, it’s more about
when and how things develop. We’d like to see them apply for the zoning change sooner rather
than later.

City Manager Gary Whatcott said Glenmoor has partnered with Daybreak and the property will
integrate into their street network.

         E. Presentation Item

                  E.1.       South Valley Chamber of Commerce update. (By South Valley
                             Chamber of Commerce President/CEO, Jay Francis)

President/CEO of South Valley Chamber of Commerce Jay Francis reviewed his prepared
presentation (Attachment A).

Council Member Marlor asked if the courses offered by the chamber were included in the regular
fees.

Mr. Francis said no, those are an additional fee.

Council Member Shelton asked which cities are members of this chamber.

Mr. Francis said the cities that have essentially said this is their “Chamber of Choice” are Sandy,
Draper and Riverton. They have two members from Tooele, memberships from all over the
county. In years past, when they were located in Sandy, 53% of their membership came from
outside those city boundaries.

Council Member Shelton asked how he sees those cities benefiting from being a part of the
South Valley Chamber.

Mr. Francis said they benefit through the programs and opportunities the chamber provides.
Their businesses are stronger, they are able to grow. The chamber also steps in and helps with
economic development, doing things like packaging or resources. He chaired EDC Utah for two
years so he has a good insight into that world. Just by making the businesses stronger and
opportunities to want to stay and grow, that makes the city better.

Council Member Marlor asked if there is any coordination going on between the local chambers.

Mr. Francis says they coordinate a lot with the Salt Lake Chamber, he is also a member of the
US Chamber and he attends when he can. The US Chamber has been a great partner. He has
reached out to the South Jordan Chamber and Brian Synan about doing some things together but
they haven’t organized anything yet. For public policy they work closely with Salt Lake,
specifically because they have a great Public Policy Department; there are about ten of them who
are members of the Utah Chamber Policy Coalition with the Salt Lake Chamber driving that.

Council Member Marlor asked how other cities, like Herriman and Bluffdale, are doing; do they
have their own chambers and are they active.

Mr. Francis said he wasn’t sure about city participation. Susan Schilling runs the chamber for
Bluffdale/Herriman, she used to run the chamber for Riverton before they made the decision to
change.

Council Member Harris asked if businesses tend to choose their local chamber versus a more
regional one, or do they join both.

Mr. Francis said they could do both, but the reality is if they are a small business they probably
can’t afford both. They probably have eight to ten that do both and are members of the South
Jordan Chamber and theirs.

Council Member Marlor said it’s public information if money is being contributed to the
chambers from a city, he asked if Mr. Francis knew of any cities making those types of
contributions currently to the South Valley Chamber.

Mr. Francis said Sandy, Draper and Riverton all contribute yearly.

         F. Staff Item

                  F.1.       Summerfest follow-up. (By Director of Administrative Services, Spencer
                             Kyle)

Director of Administrative Services Spencer Kyle introduced Associate Director of Recreation
Janell Payne who reviewed their prepared presentation (Attachment B).

Council Member Marlor asked if we have looked at whether any other cities are planning big
events for the August dates being proposed.

Associate Director Payne said she did look, and didn’t see any.

Mayor Ramsey asked about the Bluffdale fair dates.

Special Event Coordinator Natalie Domino said Bluffdale’s is usually the week before the
proposed August date; the Salt Lake County fair has been canceled.

Council Member Shelton asked about the week after the proposed June dates as a possibility.

Associate Director Payne said our carnival isn’t available and you run into some competing
events like Orem City’s fair.

Director Kyle said the three dates they are presenting are when the carnival is available. There
was one other date they were available, but it was a holiday weekend and that usually leads to
decreased attendance.

Council Member Shelton asked if school is out the same week as the June proposed dates.

Director Kyle said yes, this is the last week of school and we run into the conflicts with high
school graduation. Family night is usually the Thursday night before the last day of school; they
usually have higher numbers that night so it being a school night doesn’t seem to matter.

Council Member Harris asked which dates they would suggest.

Associate Director Payne recommended the June date.

Mayor Pro Tempore McGuire received a text from the mayor, she said school started August 16
this year for Jordan School District; she is supportive of August as her first choice. The first
weekend of summer is not her favorite, it’s the weekend of graduation and school ends.

Council Member Zander said we had huge school participation this year, she asked why we
haven’t had that in the past.

Manager Whatcott said the teachers usually don’t want to deal with it at the end of the school
year. Typically the band and other clubs end their participation a week or two before school
ends.

Council Member Zander asked if we keep the June dates, do we forego having the high school
kids participate like they did this year.

Coordinator Domino said two years ago they had the Bingham marching band, possibly
Herriman as well but she couldn’t remember.

Director Kyle said the issue is that it’s not easy to get the bands there.

Manager Whatcott said they struck a deal with Bingham that year to get them to participate, but
it’s not typically something they do. He asked what the participation would be like for the parade
and floats in August since they’ll be done with the parades for the 24th and lots of groups don’t
have anywhere to store their floats.

Director Kyle said we would probably see floats from other cities.

Coordinator Domino said she works with neighboring cities to get them to participate and they
agree to go to each other’s parades; stakes usually don’t participate in the Summerfest parade.

Director Kyle said the August dates would get the floats from other cities, but not necessarily
from other groups.

Council Member Zander said we need to decide what events are our priorities, is it participation
in the parade, the carnival, the chalk art, etc.

Director Kyle said we only have one option for the carnival right now and we are in a contract
with them. We could look at out of state carnivals or other options, those would be challenging
for this coming year, however we could look at other options long term.

Manager Whatcott said we do a revenue share with the current carnival we contract with, that
helps offset some of our costs.

Director Kyle said we don’t pay them, they end up paying us from the revenue.

Council Member Marlor said we can’t just look at one thing, we have to look at all the factors.
Regardless of the dates, participation by the community is the goal; we want to get as many
residents as possible to attend.

Director Kyle said they need direction soon, the contract with the carnival tentatively has the
June date but they can also work with us to move it to one of the other two dates. They don’t
need a decision tonight, but they need one soon.

Manager Whatcott said since he’s been here, this has been moved at least ten times, had the
name changed and been rebranded multiple times. It has been on many different dates in the past,
including a mid-August date; that date was changed afterwards because they have been rained
out multiple years in the past. If they want long term results, getting the community behind this,
they need to brand it with a date and keep the branding going to get residents because there isn’t
a rich tradition to it.

Mayor Pro Tempore McGuire said he thought there was a survey sent out after Summerfest to
get feedback, he asked if the dates were part of that survey.

Associate Director Payne said only the attendees were surveyed and they were positive about the
date in September, however the respondents were the ones who were already attending.

Council Member Harris asked, based on the previous attendance and dates, what would staff
recommend as the best date.

Manager Whatcott said the fall date is too hard internally, the August date is hard internally
because the park is being heavily used by other groups at that time that has to be adjusted and
moved around.

Associate Director of Parks Colby Hill mentioned it normally takes 4 weeks to get the turf back
after Summerfest in June and would be at least 6 weeks in August in the heat of summer.

Manager Whatcott said he thinks June is the easiest from a staff perspective, but it doesn’t have
to be there and he can understand why others would want to move it to another date for various
reasons.

Manager Whatcott said in the past, with the Recreation Center, they would leave it open until
2:00 a.m. and host a graduation party as part of Summerfest. If we kept the June date, maybe
there is a way we could incorporate Friday night as part of the high school graduation fun where
the carnival is just for Seniors.

Director Kyle suggested maybe a parade entry from the graduating class each year as a tradition.

Council Member Marlor said he believes if families know we are planning this at the same time
every year, as a tradition, they will plan for it as well.

Manager Whatcott said they thought they were making a good choice for Covid at the time, but
this was planned so far ahead of time that they couldn’t see the impact.

Director Kyle said with the June date being the weekend school gets out, people haven’t started
camps or vacations; this is the last weekend before people start leaving town and they think
they’ll get the best participation numbers at that time. The August date would be better than the
September date, but there are more competing activities for families that time of the summer.

Mission
The PPGAC is committed to informing and educating chamber members and local businesses regarding
relevant legislative and regulatory issues. The Committee’s fundamental role includes advocating the views of
the business community to our elected officials on public policy and legislation on the local, state and federal
level that might have an impact on the business community. The mission of the PPGAC is to cultivate positive
and production working relationships with elected officials to create a healthy business climate.
