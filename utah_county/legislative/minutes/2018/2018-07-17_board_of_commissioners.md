21.    DISCUSS AND APPROVE OR DENY, A TAX SALE PROTEST FILED BY ROBERT J POULSEN
       ON BEHALF OF JOHN WOLLENZIEN RELATED TO PARCEL SERIAL NO. 55:132:0003.
(Track A, 01:37:47)
Tax Administration Supervisor Burt Harvey and Deputy Attorney Paul Jones came forward. Burt explained this parcel
was sold to the highest bidder with a 90% interest only and Mr. Wollenzien owns the remaining 10% interest. Burt
stated the tax sale notices were sent to Glen Berg, who has since passed away. Burt then sent out a neighbor notice
along with a preferred bidder application to Mr. Wollenzien. The application was returned and reviewed by the Tax
Sale Committee; Mr. Wollenzein was not granted preferred bidder status.

John Wollenzien came forward with his attorney, Robert Poulsen. Mr. Poulsen described the property as being
surrounded by very steep cliffs, having excess water flow issues, and narrow access problems which would require
any buyer to encroach on Mr. Wollenzien’s property. He emphasized that the parcel is not a suitable building lot or
an economically viable unit. The buyer, Spence Hansen, stepped forward at this time. He stated that he is an engineer
and planned to build his new residence. He said the water problems were over-exaggerated and access to this parcel
UTAH COUNTY BOARD OF COMMISSIONERS
MINUTES – July 17, 2018
Page 9

could be found on the northeast corner where there is a right of way onto Scenic Drive. He offered to purchase Mr.
Wollenzien’s 10% interest.

The commissioners, Bryan Thompson, and Paul Jones discussed the criteria for preferred bidder status. Paul cited
County Code 21-5-8 to explain preferred bidder status in which the Board of Commissioners granted the level of
discretion to the Utah County Clerk/Auditor. Bryan further clarified that Mr. Wollenzien had options to acquire the
parcel without the status. Mr. Wollenzien attended the tax sale to bid on the parcel but dropped out before the winning
bid. He also had the opportunity to pay the taxes in advance and remove it from the tax sale. He could then try to
work out an agreement with the Bergs in purchasing the property. Commissioner Graves believed Mr. Wollenzien
should have been granted preferred bidder status. Commissioners Ivie and Lee agreed that Mr. Wollenzien had his
opportunities to acquire the parcel.
       Commissioner Lee made the motion to deny the tax sale protest as stated as Agenda Item No. 21 subject
       to written findings. The motion was seconded by Commissioner Graves and carried with the following
       vote:
                                                                                             AYE: Nathan Ivie
