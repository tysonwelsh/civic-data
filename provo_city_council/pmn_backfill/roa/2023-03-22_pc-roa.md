17.03.100 Prohibition Against Issuance of Municipal Permits and Administrative Approvals.

The City shall withhold business licenses; permits for kennels; anyor permits or approvals for any alteration, repair, or
construction pertaining to any existing or new structures or signs on the property;, or and any permits or approvals
pertaining to the use and development of the real property or the structure where a violation is located. Where a notice
of violation has been issued, the City shall not process any requests or applications, proceed with any review or approval
process, or grant an approval of any type in relation to the property, business, use, or structure with which the violation
is associated. The City shall withhold such permits and approvals until a notice of compliance has been issued by the
Director. The City shall not withhold permits or approvals when necessary to obtain a notice of compliance or to correct
serious health and safety violations.

STAFF PRESENTATION
The Staff Report to the Planning Commission provides details of the facts of the case and the Staff's analysis, conclusions,
and recommendations. Staff gave an overview of the proposal and specifics of the plan for the applicants business at 247
W Center Street.

 CITY DEPARTMENTAL ISSUES
 • Important issues raised by other departments – addressed in Staff Report to Planning Commission

 NEIGHBORHOOD MEETING DATE
 • A neighborhood meeting will be held on 03/30/2023.

 NEIGHBORHOOD AND PUBLIC COMMENT
 • The Neighborhood Chair was not present or did not address the Planning Commission during the hearing.

 CONCERNS RAISED BY PUBLIC
 Any comments received prior to completion of the Staff Report are addressed in the Staff Report to the Planning
 Commission. Key issues raised in written comments received subsequent to the Staff Report or public comment during
 the public hearing included the following: None.

 APPLICANT RESPONSE
 Key points addressed in the applicant's presentation to the Planning Commission included the following:
    • Shawn Homer spoke about the parking options that were looked at for “The Rise” event center, but only was able

                                                        Page 1 of 3
        to get verbal agreements. He also confirmed the parking count required based on occupancy of the building. He
        agreed that a garage that far away wouldn’t be the most useful option available and that there is a lot within 800
        feet to satisfy demand, which people would most likely use.
    •   Nathan Gagon expressed his opinion that the added distance may not be an issue for a lot of people but he would
        keep looking for closer parking, or create incentive for parking in the garage they would contract with. He went
        on to give an overview of the business and stated that there are four off-street spaces on the site. He noted the
        average occupancy is 60 people but are hoping for up to 300 people.

PLANNING COMMISSION DISCUSSION
Key points discussed by the Planning Commission included the following:
   • The Planning Commission confirmed that there are plenty of public off-street spaces nearby but aren’t available
       to be reserved for businesses.
   • The Commission asked about the requirement to have written approval from Section 14.34.320, the ability for
       businesses on Center Street to park under current standards with “grandfathered” right to lesser parking, and that
       there are a lot of off-street parking spaces available downtown.
   • Staff spoke with the Commission about the Conditional Use Permit process and regulations.
   • The Commission stated their desire to bring business downtown and help the economic vitality of Center Street
       and recognize there is plenty of parking available and struggle to find a solution.
   • Staff brought forward a possible solution of a TDM (Transportation Demand Management) program to reduce
       the parking requirement from Section 14.37.050, Provo City Code. The Planning Commission supported that idea
       and confirmed with the applicant if that is a possible solution. Staff offered changing the staff recommendation
       to continuing the item indefinitely to allow time for the applicant to work on a TDM associated with the
       Conditional Use Permit.

FINDINGS / BASIS OF PLANNING COMMISSION DETERMINATION
   • The Commission liked the idea of a TDM because it gives the City more authority to enforce the parking issue if
      it is not being managed effectively and that wouldn’t open it up to the entire downtown like the proposed text
      amendment would.
