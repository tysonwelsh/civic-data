Item 1: A presentation regarding UDOT University Avenue bridge construction (25-090)
00:00:58

Councilor Gary Garrett introduced the item and welcomed representatives from the Utah
Department of Transportation (UDOT). Andy Spencer, Project Manager, and Wyatt Woolley,
Communications Manager, presented on the planned replacement of the University Avenue
bridge.

Mr. Woolley explained that UDOT inspections in 2021 determined the existing, roughly
60-year-old structure had reached the end of its service life, necessitating full replacement.
Planning and design have been completed, a contractor is on board, and early site activity is
anticipated to begin in early October, with noticeable lane impacts expected later in October or
November. The project is expected to take approximately two years, with anticipated completion
in 2027. Construction will proceed in phases—west side first, then east side—maintaining one
travel lane in each direction during work. UDOT urged the public to avoid the work zone
whenever possible and stated that pedestrians and bicyclists will not be permitted on the bridge
during construction.

The presenters described the completed bridge configuration as two vehicle lanes in each
direction, six-foot bicycle lanes, and eight-foot sidewalks. Mechanically stabilized earth (MSE)
walls will eliminate open areas beneath the structure; parking or camping under the bridge will
no longer be possible. The bridge will reduce the number of spans to three (over the UTA
tracks/connector road, the main rail body, and 600 South). Lighting and fencing elements
identified as City betterments will enhance the appearance and safety of the facility. The new
profile will provide greater shoulder width and a gentler rise to improve user comfort.

Traffic operations in the bridge corridor (approximately 920 South to 400 South) will change to
improve safety. After construction, certain turning movements at 500 South will be removed.
Southbound traffic on University Avenue will be limited to right turns at 500 South; eastbound
traffic on 500 South will be limited to right turns onto University Avenue. Northbound right
turns from University Avenue to 500 South will be restricted due to conflicts with the adjacent
frontage-road access; a physical island and signage will discourage the maneuver while
preserving emergency access. To offset these changes, the left-turn movement at 400 South will
be restored. The small “teardrop” connection south of the UTA parking lot will be removed;
access to FrontRunner will be routed via 920 South. Portions of the pavement within the project
area will be reconstructed in concrete.

UDOT outlined recommended alternate routes during construction. To reach Center Street from
the south, motorists should remain on I-15 and exit at Center Street rather than using University
Avenue through the work zone; for north-/southbound city travel, Lakeview Parkway is an
alternate route, with minor intersection improvements (including a double-turn movement)
planned to increase capacity. UDOT emphasized that drivers familiar with local at-grade rail
crossings may continue to use city routes they know, but the state’s detours prioritize state
facilities and overhead crossings to reduce train-related delays.

Council Discussion: Councilor Becky Bogdin inquired about southbound lane transitions near
500 South and the frontage road removal. Mr. Spencer confirmed the southbound auxiliary lane
will transition to a bicycle lane/shoulder and that space reclaimed from the frontage road
accommodates bridge widening. Councilor Bogdin also asked about potential future widening at
920 South due to redirected access to FrontRunner; Mr. Spencer anticipated increased volumes at
920 South but noted that signal timing and phasing could be used to manage queues and that no
widening decisions have been made.

Councilor Travis Hoban asked about using 500 West as an alternate. Mr. Spencer indicated
UDOT promotes detours on state routes and overhead crossings; while local drivers may choose
city streets, official detour maps avoid unfamiliar at-grade crossings for safety and predictability.

Councilor Katrice MacKay commended UDOT’s public communication and outreach efforts.

Councilor George Handley requested information on pedestrian and bicycle detours. Mr. Spencer
stated pedestrians and cyclists will be signed away from the bridge work zone and directed to
signalized crossings, with routing coordinated with the City; suggested options included crossing
at 920 South or using routes via the mall/1200 South and then connecting north–south on 100
West to the new bridge area.

Item 2: An ordinance amending the zone map classification of real property, generally
located at 3200 W Bulldog Lane, from the OSPR and R1.20 zones to one-family residential
with performance development overlay (R1.6(PD)) zone. Lakeview North.
(PLRZ20250397) 00:26:20

Councilor Gary Garrett introduced the item. Bill Peperone, Development Services Director,
presented background on the project and was joined by Matt Meyer of Nilson Homes.

Director Peperone explained that from the outset, the City’s goals for this development were to
create single-family detached housing with approximately half of the units restricted for
workforce housing. Following annexation of the property (completed in November 2025), Nilson
Homes was selected through an RFP process due to their experience with mixed-income
developments and their proposal for exclusively detached homes. The subdivision will include
68 units, with 34 reserved for workforce housing. Those units will be prioritized for public

Director Peperone highlighted that the workforce and market units are integrated throughout the

vibration impacts during infrastructure construction. Public Works will install vibration monitors
along the eastern boundary to track potential impacts. Other public input has questioned whether

conflicts, noting that wetlands on-site have been preserved. A small pedestrian bridge is planned

Katrice MacKay confirmed that all wetlands on the site are preserved and that design around
them is preferable to off-site mitigation.

Councilor George Handley asked about the size and price of the market units compared to
workforce homes. Mr. Meyer explained that market units will be slightly larger (approximately
1,500–1,700 square feet) and priced in the $600,000 range, while workforce units are intended to
be priced in the high $400,000s to meet affordability targets (up to 120% of Area Median
Income). Workforce homes will be on land held by the nonprofit arm of the Provo Housing
Authority through a land trust, ensuring long-term affordability. Buyers will own the homes but
not the land, and resale will remain subject to trust provisions. Market rate homes will be fee
simple.

Item 3: A discussion regarding the Provo River setback and design standards (25-091)
00:59:58

Policy Analyst David Pyle presented background and sought Council direction on potential code
changes and design guidance for development along the Provo River corridor.

Pyle summarized prior individual meetings with Council members and identified goals to protect
and use the river as a natural amenity by encouraging development that actively integrates with
the river. Concepts discussed included orienting buildings toward the river, incorporating small
commercial uses such as restaurants near the corridor, and establishing design standards that
reflect the river’s natural setting and enhance visitor and resident experiences along the River
Trail.

Regarding setbacks, Pyle noted that a 100-foot setback from the Provo River has existed since
the 1980s and is currently located in Chapter 15.05. In 2021, guidelines were added allowing
encroachments into the 100-foot area under specified conditions, including a 40-foot
maintenance easement requested by Public Works to accommodate river maintenance equipment



Provo City Municipal Council Work Meeting – September 23, 2025                           Page 4 of 12
and access. Councilors sought clarification on how the 40-foot provision has functioned in
practice and why recent projects appeared closer than 100 feet.

City Attorney Brian Jones clarified that the code maintains a 100-foot standard with an exception
pathway. Under that exception, projects may be permitted within 100 feet following project plan
review if certain criteria are met (including access spacing, a minimum 40-foot maintenance
easement, and location conditions related to levees and mapped flood hazard areas). He noted the
2021 updates also increased the required freeboard above Base Flood Elevation from one to three
feet and that the discussion at that time centered on flood safety rather than environmental
preservation.

Council Discussion: Councilors expressed interest in reaffirming the 100-foot setback as the
baseline standard in ordinance, coupled with clearly defined, narrowly tailored criteria for any
exceptions. Several Councilors supported moving beyond advisory general-plan concepts toward
codified design expectations—potentially through a Provo River design corridor—to require
projects within a defined distance to engage the river (e.g., building orientation, trail
connectivity, and public-facing amenities). Councilor MacKay emphasized opportunities for
restaurants and small shops to activate the corridor when appropriately sited. Councilor Handley
supported establishing consistent, objective criteria while allowing limited, site-specific
flexibility where flood safety can be guaranteed and public amenities justify proximity. Chair
and Council comments also referenced comparable design approaches used in other locations as
examples of integrating a water feature within a project.

Councilor Rachel Whipple (via Zoom message) added that “facing the river” could include
buildings that front a street if they still embrace the river at the rear, such as by providing a
riverside patio or glass-enclosed dining area; she cautioned against designs that create a solid
wall along the river edge.

Next Steps: Development Services Director Bill Peperone advised coordinating with Public
Works Director Gordon Haight before recommending interim changes to the exception language,
given flood-protection considerations. He will assess the risk of encroachment approvals under
existing zoning while comprehensive design-corridor standards are developed. The Chair
indicated the item will return to a future work meeting for additional direction. No action was
taken.

Item 5: An ordinance amending Provo City Code regarding the area where accessory
dwelling units are permitted. (PLOTA20250238) 01:34:44

Planner Jessica Dahneke presented a request to amend the ADU eligibility map to include the

Ms. Dahneke explained that the text amendment would alter the ADU eligibility map by adding

special-case map additions. She noted staff generally supports ADUs as a housing tool identified
in the General Plan; however, staff recommended denial of this application to the Planning
Commission due to concerns with owner-occupancy compliance under 14.30.

Owner-Occupancy Context: Chapter 14.30 requires an “owner occupant,” defined as an
individual holding at least 50% title and intending to occupy the dwelling as a primary residence.
Two owners are on title (William and Christina Gibson). Documentation was submitted by Ms.
Gibson evidencing her intent to occupy as her primary residence; Mr. Gibson does not intend to
occupy. Ms. Dahneke cited subsection (D) of 14.30 addressing proof of primary residency and
referenced subsection (D)(2) regarding documents an owner may provide (e.g., no rental

income/tax deductions; not listed on leases or insurance). Staff expressed concern that the current
wording appears to function as an exception without being clearly framed as such.

Public/Commission Input: A July neighborhood meeting recorded 24 citizens were in favor and
5 were against. The Planning Commission recommended approval contrary to staff’s
recommendation, reasoning that tax documentation from Mr. Gibson could not yet exist due to
the short ownership period and therefore should not be required for compliance under
14.30(D)(ii).

Council Discussion: Councilor Becky Bogdin asked whether the property is currently rented;
Ms. Dahneke stated the applicants indicated to the Planning Commission that tenants are present

Item 10: A resolution approving the appropriation of $158,396 to add three full-time
firefighters for the airport for the fiscal year ending June 30, 2026 (25-094) 02:54:43

Airport Director Torgersen and Fire Chief Headman presented a resolution requesting the
appropriation of $158,396 to add three full-time firefighters dedicated to the airport. With Breeze
Airways’ continued expansion, the FAA will require Provo Airport to meet ARFF Index C
beginning January 1, which mandates the constant presence of two firefighting vehicles staffed
by two firefighters. Until now, coverage has been achieved by assigning three full-time Fire
Captains and supplementing them with cross-trained Airport Operations staff. This arrangement,
however, has created scheduling difficulties and strained the airport’s operations capacity.

The proposal calls for hiring three new full-time firefighters to staff the airport on the
department’s 48/96 schedule, one firefighter per shift. These new positions will work alongside
the Fire Captains, ensuring consistent and compliant ARFF coverage. Chief Headman explained
that firefighters will rotate on an annual basis to maintain proficiency in EMS and fire
operations, with a broader pool of about 18 trained firefighters available to provide relief
coverage. This arrangement reduces the reliance on overtime and alleviates staffing burdens that
have required airport operations employees to fill ARFF roles, effectively returning 2.5 full-time
equivalents back to their primary airport duties.

The total appropriation request is $158,396, which includes approximately $36,000 for personal
protective equipment, radios, uniforms, and training. Ongoing specialized training is expected to
cost around $16,000 annually. These costs are partially offset by reductions in overtime that are
already budgeted, and the remainder will come from the Airport Operations Fund. The airport
recorded a $1.1 million revenue surplus in FY25, along with an underspend of $65,000, and
remains self-sufficient.

In terms of facilities, the firefighters currently share space with airport operations in offices not
designed for 24-hour use. Construction of a new Snow Removal Equipment building is
underway, with expected completion in July or August of next year. Once complete, airport
operations and equipment will relocate, leaving the existing space to be remodeled into a
dedicated fire station.

During discussion, Council members confirmed that the three positions would be new hires, not
transfers, and inquired about the adequacy of training, accommodations, and ongoing costs.
Councilors expressed strong support for the proposal, noting its importance for safety,
compliance, and relieving the airport’s overextended staff. The resolution will return to the
Council for a vote in two weeks.

consultant Brent Potter (Blue Line Designs) on the draft Dog Park Master Plan and
recommended implementation strategy. Staff summarized community input (including a
statistically valid survey indicating approximately 58% of residents own a dog), key desired
amenities (fencing, shade, human/pet water access, and waste collection), and primary concerns
(aggressive dogs, inattentive owners, and maintenance). Guiding principles emphasized equitable
distribution consistent with the current level-of-service goal of one dog park per 30,000
residents, preference for city-owned land, cost awareness, and consistent safety/usability
standards.

Two short-term implementation paths were outlined. One emphasized delivering smaller,
lower-cost facilities with available funding—specifically Rock Canyon and the RAH/Exchange
