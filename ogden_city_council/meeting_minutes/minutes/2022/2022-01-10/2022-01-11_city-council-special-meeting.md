Proposed Ordinance 2022-1 establishing representative voting districts for
the Board of Education of the Ogden School District

A memo from Council staffregarding proposed voting districts for the Board of Education of the Ogden School District
came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Ogden School District is located completely within the boundaries
of Ogden City. Because of this, state law designates the City Council as the legislative body responsible for redistricting. School
district boundaries must be realigned this year to follow newly established precinct lines. The Council decided that it would be
best for the Ogden School Board to review precincts and provide an unofficial recommendation. This recommendation is
considered unofficial because the School Board did not vote on the map. The map should be adopted by January 11, 2022 in order
to accommodate the School Board candidate filing period.

Council Policy Analyst Watkins summarized the memo and provided the proposed map to orient the Council to the
voting district boundaries.

Chair Nadolski invited input from Ogden School District Business Administrator Zane Woolstenhulme. Mr.
Woolstenhulme thanked the Council for their consideration of the proposed voting district map for the District; he summarized the

Proposed Joint Resolution 2022-1 of the City Council and Mayor Caldwell
outlining a cooperative effort to plan and make decisions regarding the
construction/renovation of the Marshall White Community Center and
establishing a timeline to move the project forward

A memo from Council staff came before the Council to consider a joint resolution regarding the Marshall White Center
(MWC). The memo stated the joint resolution would outline a cooperative effort to plan, analyze options, and make decisions
regarding new construction or the renovation of the MWC.

Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
summarize the implications of the proposed Joint Resolution.

Chair Nadolski then introduced in writing proposed Joint Resolution 2022-1, entitled:

“A joint resolution of the Ogden City Council and Ogden City Mayor outlining a cooperative effort to plan,
analyze options, and make decisions regarding the construction or renovation of the Marshall N. White
Community Center and establishing a timeline for the completion of major tasks required to move the project
forward.”

Chair Nadolski then called for public input regarding the proposed resolution.

Suzanne Daley stated it is great for the public to have this document to review, but she has one concern and that relates to
the pool at the MWC; if the pool is going to remain at the MWC, the community will be led to believe that the MWC will be the
location for a premier recreation center in the community. However, the joint resolution does not clearly communicate that. She
noted the first ‘whereas’ clause in the document mentions a major project and keeping the MWC in its current location and she
hopes that means that Administration intends to build a $30 million facility in that location, or that they clearly communicate to
the community any different intentions.

David Timmerman echoed Ms. Daley’s concerns; the residents of Ogden have communicated their desire for a new
facility in the current MWC location. He is also concerned about the timeframe for the project as outlined in the joint resolution
and he suggested that the two-year timeline be reduced even by just six months. This project is very important to the community,
and he is grateful for the Council’s consideration of the matter this evening.

Chair Nadolski stated that everyone would like to see the project completed much quicker, but it will take time to be
communicative and collaborative with the community and to make sure that the project is done right the first time.

Heath Satow stated he appreciates the efforts of certain Council members in pushing this matter forward; some have
really done their job well. He personally knows someone who was kicked off the Planning Commission for expressing her
opinions about the MWC and he understands that has caused others to tread lightly. He noted the ultimate statement of the joint
resolution is that the City intends to make a major investment to enhance, renovate, and/or replace the existing facilities. He stated
the use of ‘and/or’ is critical and the document essentially communicates that the Administration intends to do something that
enhances the building. While this would be an improvement, it may give the Administration the ability to drag the issue out and
ultimately underperform. He sees no definition of what constitutes a major investment and there is no definition of the minimum
goal of enhancing the building. For example, the Administration could completely repaint the interior of the building in the year
2024 and claim they have met the goal of enhancing the facility. Unless the Council keeps pressure on Administration to exceed
the minimal goals outlined in the joint resolution, nothing meaningful will happen until the current Mayor leaves office in January
of 2024. The community is counting on the Council to stay on top of this matter.

Daniel Mathews thanked the Council for moving forward with an action regarding the MWC, but noted he shares some
of the concerns that have already been voiced regarding the joint resolution. He suggested the creation of a joint sub-committee
including members of the City Council and Administration, along with the State representative for this area, to focus on

Public Comments

Heath Satow referenced the discussion during tonight’s work session regarding proposed amendments to the Rules and
Norms specific to public displays and speech; he feels the comments made by Council member Choberka, Vice Chair Lopez, and
Chair Nadolski on the top were very reasonable, but he strongly disagreed with Council member Hyer’s comments about the
display of signs opposing an issue. The point of the act of showing solidarity is a legitimate form of protest when residents do not
feel they are getting the representation they deserve; he also believes it is effective. If people find speaking up around people who
are holding signs to be scary, they should use the option they have to call into a meeting. This issue is about the Council’s
discomfort, not the publics.

Jordan Silver stated he is a career pilot and regular user of the Ogden Airport for the past 18 years; he expressed gratitude
for an operational airport in the City of Ogden, but he has been saddened to see the Airport deteriorate rapidly from a user-friendly
place to conduct recreation business to an unpleasant, financially ruinous endeavor. He believes the current Airport management
and the City Attorney are trying to effect positive change in the name of progress at the Airport, but he feels they are doing so
with a secretive underhanded attitude that is accompanied by malice and greed. He has many complaints as follows: first, is the
class action lawsuit between ORAA and Ogden City regarding illegal actions of Airport management and Ogden city including
confiscation of private property, unequal enforcement of onerous and excessive hangar inspections, continuous failure to comply
with grant assurances required for continued receipt of federal funds, unreasonably short land leases to current hangar owners, and
unprecedent insurance requirements for aircraft and hangar owners. Second, efforts and massive tax dollar expenditures on
attempts to bring large commercial operations to the Ogden Airport are ill planned and implemented and are currently failing.
Third, rejection of proposed growth by uses like Foresight and the U.S. Forest Service, and others, would have brought revenue
and jobs to the Airport; seeking to evict and render assets worthless for companies like Sky Dive Ogden, is problematic. Fourth,
the Airport Advisory Board is consistently ignored by the City, has recently refused public comment at their meeting, and has a
member with a conflict of interest as he is a manager of the federally contracted airport control tower. Fifth, air traffic controllers

Taylor Knuth thanked the Council for their adoption of the joint resolution regarding the Marshall White Center. He then
discussed the Human Rights Coalition’s Municipal Equality Index Score; this was determined by conduction of a nationwide
survey to determine inclusivity of the LGBTQ+ community. Salt Lake City received a score of 100, the highest score possible, but
Ogden only received a score of 58. This is a result of the lack of non-discrimination laws preventing unfair treatment in the areas
of housing, employment, and public accommodations as well as the lack of insurance policies that would provide coverage for
domestic partners. The City does not have a LGBTQ+ liaison position in City Administration or the Police Department, which
also contributed to a low score. He mentioned other factors, such as youth bullying and lack of community support. He
encouraged the Council to consider this index score as a solid measure of success in future policy considerations.

Council member White inquired as to the individual that completed the survey. Mr. Knuth stated that the Coalition’s
website indicates that the survey was sent to the City’s Administrative Offices, so he imagines the City’s Diversity Coordinator
completed the survey.

Sebastian Benitez echoed the comments made by Mr. Satow about freedom of speech and the rights of individuals to
display signs communicating a message. He strongly believes in freedom and does not believe that anyone’s voice should be
restricted or silenced.

Betty Sawyer also echoed the sentiments expressed regarding the possibility that the Council will enact restrictions that
will limit freedom of speech and involvement in public discussions. There must be great care in passing policies, especially when
no problem exists. According to the discussion during tonight’s work session, there has only been one or two problems in the last
several years and it is problematic to create policy when no problem exists or when a problem is only created through rhetoric.
There are ways to address the issue of signage without enacting policies that are unproductive.

Angel Castillo praised Council staff for their efforts to increase transparency of Council actions through the creation of a
podcast and streaming meetings on Facebook. Ogden City is leading while Weber County is not; she is very disappointed that
Weber County does not stream their meetings whatsoever. She then stated that it has been so important to consider options for
allowing residents to participate in meetings during the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic. She indicated Weber State University’s
Stewart Stadium holds 17,500 spectators and there are currently 24,147 new active COVID cases in the State of Utah. She asked
the Council to encourage their constituents to be vaccinated and receive booster shots; this is the only way of getting back to
normal, which includes keeping kids in school and keeping businesses open.

Mayor Comments

Mayor Caldwell thanked the Council for their action regarding the Marshall White Center this evening; there are many
things yet to address and work through and he appreciates the willingness of the Council and the public to do that hard work. He
then responded to Ms. Castillo’s comments regarding the COVID-19 pandemic and indicated that the health and wellbeing is the
biggest priority for his Administration. The last two years have been very difficult, and it is necessary that everyone work
collectively to make the health of the community the most important priority.

Council member Comments

Council member White stated that when she first recommended the Council consider a resolution regarding the Marshall
White Center (MWC), she wanted to be sure that it clearly communicated that the project is important not just to a certain
community, but to the entire community. She wants to continue to move forward and feels that the joint resolution outlines some
important first steps. She also shares concerns about the use of some ambiguous terms included in the joint esolution and
acknowledged that it is not perfect, but it is a great first step in moving forward. She is hopeful that it will lead to something
positive coming to fruition.

Council member Choberka stated she appreciates the community engagement this evening and also appreciates the
efforts behind the adoption of the MWC joint resolution. She added there will be future open house events that the public can
participate in to provide feedback regarding the MWC project. She also encouraged everyone to participate in the Martin Luther
King, Jr. Day events mentioned by Ms. Sawyer.

Council member Hyer stated he wished to clarify that he is a huge proponent of free speech and his comments during the
work session were not intended to stifle free speech; rather, he wants to ensure everyone feels comfortable expressing themselves

their opinion, and he wants to see that stopped.
