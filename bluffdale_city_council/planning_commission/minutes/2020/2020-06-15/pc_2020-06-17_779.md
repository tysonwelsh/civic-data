7.     PUBLIC HEARING, CONSIDERATION, AND RECOMMENDATION on a City
       Initiated Text Amendment Application to Amend Title 11-8A-10 and 11-8D-10
       Regarding the Distance Between any Accessorv Building and any Dwelling from
       Twenty Feet (20') to Ten Feet (10') in the R-1-43 and R-1-87 Zones. (Application 2020-
       28). Staff Presenter, Ellen Oakman.

Associate City Planner, Ellen Oakman, presented the staff report and reviewed the cun-ent verbiage
in the City Code and the proposed text amendments. The proposal is to change the minimum
distance between a residence and an accessory building from 20 feet to 10 feet in the R-1-43 and R-
1-87 zones. She noted that the 10-foot minimum already exists in the R-1-10 zone. The Building
Code is also at 10 feet. The cmTent standard is old and the City Council feels it needs to be revised
to enhance the 1ights of property owners. Ms. Oakman next reviewed the City Zoning Map to show
where the impacted zones are located.

In response to Commissioner Walston's question regarding any potential advantage to the 20-foot
minimum, Ms. Oakman stated that in other cities the distance is even less than six feet. She
considered the current requirement of 20 feet to be excessive. Mr. Crowell interjected that the
previous thinking was likely that the larger the property in the zone, the bigger the distance between
homes and accessory buildings. Regulations have evolved over the years in tenns of setbacks,
coverage, and other requirements as trends have changed. There are safety concerns when the space
is less than 10 feet, but a 10-foot gap is adequate for mitigating safety hazards. Requirements vary
greatly from city to city. Ms. Oakman stated that the proposed text amendments will have no
bearing on setbacks from the property line and the distance between a home and an accessory
building.

Chair Cragun opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public hearing was
closed.

Johnny Loumis, Jr. moved to forward a positive recommendation to the City Council for Text
Amendment Application 2020-28 based on the findings presented in the staff report dated
June 2, 2020. Holly Brown seconded the motion. Vote on motion: Johnny Loumis, Jr.-Aye;
Holly Brown-Aye; Ulises Flynn-Aye; Stephen Walston-Aye; Debbie Cragun-Aye. The motion
passed unanimously.

Ms. Oakman reported that the R-1-10 zone currently has no permitted animal rights attached to it.
The City Code also lacks regulations regarding small animals. The text amendments seek to add
