       d. The Villas at Stone Creek Master Development Agreement and General Land Use Map
          Amendment
Kaylynn Nichols, a planner for Peterson Development, explained that the property for The Villas at
Stone Creek was located at 4977 West 7800 South, within the Stone Creek master planned
community. The applicant desired to amend the Future Land Use Map for approximately 30 acres
from Professional Office, Medium Density Residential, Neighborhood Commercial, and Parks and
Open Land designations to Medium Density Residential, Neighborhood Commercial, and Parks and
Open Land in an existing P-C(ZC) Zone. The Future Land Use Map amendment would facilitate
development of 116 senior housing units, construction of an assisted living facility, eight single-
family dwellings, and a one-acre commercial lot. She explained that in the many years the applicant
had been working and planning with the property, traffic movement had shifted, and the subject
location was no longer a viable place for retail commercial.

Ms. Nichols described the proposed senior housing units and assisted living facility. She said the
goal for the Stone Creek development was to provide a range of housing types. Ms. Nichols stated
landscaping would be HOA maintained, described planned parking, and explained that water would
be available, but would be limited to Phase One of the project for the first approximately 18 months.

Mindy Hill with Covington Senior Living said the proposed design was planned to enable and
facilitate aging-in-place. She spoke of jobs and education opportunities that would be created. Vice
Chair Green asked staff if a senior housing overlay needed to be applied to the property. Ray
McCandless responded the proposed use was permitted in the existing zone. He said staff did not
feel a senior housing overlay was necessary.

Responding to a question from Council Member Worthen, Chair Whitelock stated the four-plex units
were intended for sale. Council Member Worthen said she would like to see senior units available
for rent.

 8.    BUSINESS ITEMS
         c. Enterprise Funds Transfer
Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck showed a property tax rate history for West Jordan
from 2012 to the current fiscal year. Council Member Jacob commented that a year ago he suggested
holding the mil rate constant each year, and if that had been done, a property tax increase would
not be necessary. Ms. Steck showed a comparison of declared or certified tax rates for surrounding
cities. She showed property tax numbers for an average-value home in West Jordan.

Ms. Steck stated the property tax increase would need to be 3.9% if the Council approved the
proposed transfer of funds from Enterprise Funds to the General Fund. Without the proposed
transfer from the Solid Waste Fund, the property tax increase would need to be 5.82%, or the
Council would need to instruct staff to reduce the budget or use reserves. If the Council chose not to
approve any Enterprise Fund transfer, the property tax increase would need to be 19.58%, or the
Council would need to instruct staff to reduce the budget or use reserves to balance the FY 2023
Budget. Ms. Steck commented that the Council’s decision regarding the proposed Enterprise Fund
transfer would also impact the 5-year financial plan for the General Fund.

Vice Chair Green said he was not a fan of the Enterprise Fund transfer; however, the City needed
$2.5 million to fund part of the Budget. He said he was in favor of the compromise of eliminating the
Solid Waste Fund transfer in FY 2023, with a 5.82% property tax increase. Vice Chair Green said he
was not in favor of reducing the budget or using more reserves knowing the economy may be
heading into a recession.

Council Member Worthen said she was not willing to approve more than a 3.9% property tax
increase. She said she agreed with comments about the Solid Waste transfer, but did not want to
use additional reserves, and did not want to approve more than a 3.9% increase.
City Council Meeting Minutes – June 22, 2022                                                      Page 9



Council Member McConnehey said he would rather see more of a property tax increase to remove
the Enterprise Fund transfer and reduce utility fees. Council Member Jacob said he did not like the
utility transfer at all. He said he agreed with the 5.8% property tax increase to remove the Solid
Waste Fund transfer, but believed the Solid Waste fee should reduce as well.

Chair Whitelock said she believed the Council needed to follow through with the 10% property tax
increase the Council was told would be necessary to provide for public safety and provide
competitive compensation for employees. She said she believed the utility fund transfer had a very
clear path in which, each year, the Council put the transfer on an agenda and noticed a public
hearing, held a public hearing, and voted on the issue in a public meeting. She said she would be in
favor of listing the amount of the transfer on the monthly utility bill. Chair Whitelock said she agreed
with Council Member McConnehey that most tax-exempt entities used their own contractor for solid
waste, and said she would agree with not including the Solid Waste Fund in the transfer. She said
she believed the utility transfer made sense.

Council Member Pack said he was a proponent of small inflationary index increases, and would
ideally not want to take from one fund to pay another. However, the City had grown and services
were needed. He expressed support for the 5.9% property tax increase the Council had discussed.
