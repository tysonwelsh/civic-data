5.     Planning Commissioners HB-409 Training on Appeals and Variances. Staff Presenter,
       Todd Sheeran, City Attorney.

City Attorney, Todd Sheeran, provided the training, with the aid of a PowerPoint presentation. The
first topic involved Appeal s and Variances. Mr. Sheeran di scussed property rights that are accorded
to landowners. He explained that distinctions need to be made regarding what people should be
allowed to do on their property and what issues neighbors can legitimately complain about to rectify
or mitigate. Property ri ghts are a balancing act between " Regulation" and "No Regulation."
Mr. Sheeran presented a hypothetica l appli cation and examples were prov ided.

In order to consider what someone mi ght want to do on their property, the Land Use Authority must
first determine what is allowed in City Code. The key factor is the zoning of the subject property
and what is included in the Land Use Table. If a proposed use is not included on the Land Use
Table, the property owner can seek a text amendment through the City Council. Another option
was to request a zone change if there is a zone that would include the proposed use on the Land Use
Table. The final option was to seek a variance.

Using the Land Use Table for the R-1-43 zone of the subj ect property, Mr. Sheeran identified the
allowances provided in the zone. Some uses are Permitted or Conditional Uses. The number of
animals allowed is also included in Chapter 11 .160.280. Mr. Sheeran next defined what a
Conditional Use is and how Conditional Uses are approved.

If a zone change is requested, the Planning Commission and City Council are involved. If there is a
Conditional Use request, only the Planning Commission needs to be involved. A variance is
required when a proposed land use does not fall within the purview of the Land Use Table as a
Permitted or Conditional Use. Requests for variances must go directly to the Appeals and Variance
Hearing Officer ("A VHO"), who has a quasi-judicial function.

Mr. Sheeran next described the differences between Administrative and Legislative Acts. The
Planning Commission and , in some cases, staff can make decisions in the form of Administrative
Acts. Legislative Acts have a lot of latitude but they belong to the City Council, often with
preliminary vetting and recommendations from the Planning Commission. Administrative Acts
must be in compliance with current C ity Standards.

Mr. Sheeran presented a slide designati ng the appeal options for different decisions. Appeals for
Legislative Acts by the City Council go straight to the District Court. It is very difficult to get a
District Court to overturn a Legislative Act. Conditional Use Permits and Site Plans are
Administrative Acts . The appeal fo r an Administrative Act would go to the A VHO first. The
AVHO will review the documentation prepared by the Planning Commi ssion to detem1ine if there
is an appropriate amount of supporting evidence on the public record to support the decision. The
appeal of a dec ision by the AVHO goes directly to the District Court.

Mr. Sheeran noted that a variance is somewhat different from an appeal. Variances involve taking
action that is not allowed in the C ity Code because variances are deviations from the City Code. If
a variance is granted. it runs with the land. so the variance would be recorded with the County

Recorder' s Office. In order to obtain a variance, the following five criteria enumerated in Utah
Code Atmotated § 10-9a-702, Section l l-3-4(A)(l )( ii) must all be met:

       (i)     Literal enforcement of the ordinance would cause an unreasonable hardship for the
               applicant that is not necessary to carry out the general purpose of the land use
               ordinances;

       (ii)    There are special circumstances attached to the property that do not generally apply
               to other properties in the same zone;

       (iii)   Granting the variance is essential to the enjoyment of a substantial property right
               possessed by other property in the same zone;

       (iv)    The variance will not substantially affect the general plan and will not be contrary to
               the public interest; and

       (v)     The spirit of the land use ordinance is observed and substantial justice done.

Messrs. Crowell and Sheeran emphasized that all five criteria must be met in order to grant a
variance. The Bluffdale A VHO has not granted a single variance because he is a trained legal
authority. As noted previously, an appeal to the AVHO ' s decision woul d go to the District Court.
Mr. Sheeran gave exan1ples of issues he has dealt with in his career. Mr. Crowell clarified that
legislative decisions must not be made by the AVHO.

Mr. Sheeran noted that site plans and conditional use permits are administrative items that allow for
very little discretion. ff a proposal meets all of the City Code Standards, the Planning Conm1ission
must approve the site plan in spite of negative feedback that might come from the public. He also
cautioned against requiring more of the developer than is specified in City Code or that might be
considered an exaction, which is prohibited.

If an issue occurs that would require legislative action, the City Council would initiate that action,
