 2. DISCUSSION TOPICS
       a. Review of the Cottages at Parker Place rezone and amended Development Plan from
          Dec. 20, 2023
          Associate Planner Mark Forsythe informed the Council that the setback variance granted
          to the Cottages at Parker Place subdivision by the Board of Adjustment had expired. He
          requested feedback and direction from the Council on the possible extension of the
          variance, on-street parking, lot sizes, and detention basin agreements.

          Responding to a comment from Council Member Green, Mr. Forsythe noted that the
          applicant proposed setbacks that fell between City Code and the expired variance. Council
          Member Green stated the proposed development would be nonconforming because it
          could not comply with R-3-8. He was comfortable with the proposed Development Plan,
          but uncomfortable with the previously granted variance, and wanted to prevent similar
          situations in the future.

          Vice Chair Lamb pointed out the reason the subdivision was approved was because it
          went through the Planning Commission, City staff, and the City Council, noting that the
          Senior Housing Overlay did not work out. He emphasized the need for flexibility when
          plans did not work out. He supported the proposed setbacks, considering it an
          improvement over the previous plan.

          Council Member Whitelock commented that Liberty knew what they were purchasing
          when they purchased the subdivision with the Senior Housing Overlay. She agreed that
          the Council needed to work with the applicant and appreciated that Liberty had improved

       c. Discussion regarding Section 8-14-7 Grave Decorations to allow more flexibility to
          family members as to what can be placed on graves and the length of time items may
          remain
          Council Member Whitelock informed the Council about concerns from multiple residents
          regarding the placement and duration of items on graves. Both Council Member Whitelock
          and Council Member Bloom proposed an amendment to Section 8-14-7, allowing grave
          decorations from mid-October until the week after Easter to offer residents more
          flexibility.

          Vice Chair Lamb raised concerns about staff having contact information for everyone
          involved and inquired about the process for contacting family members regarding grave
          decorations. Council Member Bedore proposed including a requirement for a name and
          contact information on items placed in the cemetery.

          Council Member Green expressed comfort with the proposed timeframe but suggested
          expanding the types of allowed decorations while maintaining the established 18-inch
          height restriction.

          Parks Division Manager Dave Naylor expressed concern regarding the aesthetics of the
          cemetery with the proposed six-month period. He received opposite complaints before
          the current ordinance was in place, and suggested a balance was needed. Council Member
          Green wanted something that was easy for staff to administer.

          The Council and staff discussed possible timeframes. Mr. Naylor believed a one-month
          period for grave decorations during winter months would probably work. Responding to
          Council Member Lamb’s question about the fate of decorations collected by staff, it was
          reported they were typically discarded except for those deemed to have value.

          Mr. Naylor introduced the new Cemetery Sexton Craig Hudson. City Administrator Korban
          Lee asked if the Council would be willing to let the Sexton set the clean-up dates, taking
          the many factors involved into consideration. Mr. Hudson said there was a lot going on in
          the cemetery year-round, and suggested educating the public was important. He said he
          believed a strong written policy was important, with flexibility to consider individual
          situations.

          Chair Jacob suggested letting staff draft a policy for Council review. Council Member
          Whitelock expressed the opinion that the policy should include some definite “guard
          rails”, along with flexibility for the Sexton. Mayor Burton appreciated flexibility for the
          Sexton to help him be more effective in his job.

          Council Member Green mentioned the need for a discussion regarding expanding
          cemetery space in the near future.

       d. Discussion on the maximum amount of fines for code enforcement violations


Committee of the Whole Minutes            Wednesday, January 17, 2024                          Page 3
          Council Member Green shared that he and Council Member Whitelock had discussed the
          perception of code enforcement, debating whether it should focus on imposing
          substantial fines or achieving compliance. To prevent excessive fines, Green proposed
          capping fines and re-inspection fees at $750. Council Member Whitelock emphasized the
          need to consider the purpose of code enforcement and desired outcomes, highlighting the
          importance of maintaining the city's appeal for residents. While acknowledging the
          significance of code enforcement, she expressed a desire to avoid residents feeling that
          the city was excessive with fines.

          Code Enforcement Officer Brock Hudson outlined the code enforcement process, starting
          with a complaint and inspection. He mentioned the Mayor's introduction of a courtesy
          notice, although it wasn't officially written into the code. Mr. Hudson detailed the
          timelines for notices of violation, hearings for noncompliance, and the six-week period
          before fines were imposed. Council Member Bloom expressed the view that the existing
          fines appeared reasonable, given the chances for compliance in the process. Mr. Hudson
          noted the rarity of invoicing a $750 fine and expressed the opinion that fines were
          motivation for compliance.

          Council Member Green stated perception was important and did not want the City to
          appear draconian with fines. He would feel more comfortable if the maximum fine was
          $500 plus re-inspection fees. Mayor Burton commented that the purpose of the fine was
          to motivate and encourage compliance.

          Responding to a question from Council Member Bedore, Mr. Hudson had not perceived
          any change in compliance with the decrease in the maximum fine from $1,000 to $750.
          Mr. Hudson spoke of compassion shown by staff when individuals were willing to work
          with them on enforcement. He estimated his department had a 90% compliance rate, with
          a couple thousand cases per year.

          Council Member Green wanted to find a balance, but now felt it may not be worth
          adjusting what was in place. Council Member Bloom did not see an issue and could not
          see an improvement the Council could make at the moment. She advocated taking no
          action until Title 16 was updated.

          Chair Jacob agreed and noted the need to update the consolidated fee schedule to mirror
          fees outlined during the discussion. Mr. Hudson agreed.

       b. Discussion of Citywide Survey results
          Council Office Director Alan Anderson reminded Council about the 2023 Citywide Survey
          presentation in July of 2023. He sought Council support for the 2024 Citywide Survey
          included in the budget. He asked if they wanted to use the same survey questions, or add
          something new. Council Member Green noted the lack of discussion regarding use of the
          survey results. Mr. Anderson clarified that results were intended to for the annual budget.
          Council Member Green reiterated that no action had been taken with the 2023 survey
          results. Council Member Bloom noted that survey results would be used during
          discussions of the budget at the upcoming Council Retreat.
