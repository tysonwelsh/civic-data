The RTP has to have a process to amend the list, understanding this is a four year plan and things do change, under federal law there are air quality determinations, financial constraints, and duration of project changes. There were recommendations with the recent Federal Certification Review that suggested updating the RTP amendment process. The updated RTP process breaks the amendments into three levels. The process for the amendment goes first to the Interagency Consulting Team (ICT) to determine the level of amendment. The ICT comprises the MPOs within the state that have air quality requirements, UDOT, UTA, Federal Highway Administration, Federal Transit Administration, and the Department of Air Quality. Page | 4 Level 1 are Staff Amendments for exempt projects that are not regionally significant and when air quality conformity determination is not needed. If MAG staff and the Interagency Consultation Team (ICT) determine the project fits within level 1, the MPO Director, keeping the MPO and TAC informed, approves the amendment. Level 2 are Board Amendments for non-exempt projects that are not regionally significant and when a new air quality conformity determination is needed but a new regional emissions analysis is not needed. The amendment will be reviewed by MAG staff, ICT, sponsoring community representatives, and other interested parties. If the ICT determines that a new regional emissions analysis is not needed, the MPO Board and ICT could recommend a 30-day public comment period and the MPO Board approves the amendment. Level 3 are Full Amendments for exempt projects that are regionally significant and when a new air quality conformity determination and a regional emissions analysis is needed. Level 3 applies to projects involving changes to regionally significant projects like scope changes or when projects are added or removed from the RTP. The amendment will be reviewed by MAG staff, ICT, sponsoring community representatives, and other interested parties. MAG staff to declare, in writing, the request to FHWA, conduct a 30-day public comment period, and seek approval of the MPO Board. Mayor Kurt Ostler asked about an example of a project where the Level 1 may be applicable. Shawn Eliot stated the projects that would not come to the MPO Board are where maybe the lines on the map are drawn wrong, spelling errors, or changes that are miniscule. Household Travel Survey Overview Tim Hereth had the opportunity over the last year and a half to be a part of a survey of residents of Utah. The study cost approximately $1.7 million, which MAG paid a small portion of. This study goes to help form more realistic models for traﬃc. The study helps to understand who, where and why of travel. It was conducted through an application on a phone that would track the day to day gps of the phone. Trips can be quite complicated with walks, to dentist appointments, to running to the gas station. The study is conducted about every ten years; somewhat in line with the census data. There have been three studies completed within the state over the last thirty years. Tim Hereth stated the data showed daily trips had increased by about 11%-12% from about 10 years ago. Mayor Carolyn Lundberg asked if this is based on population or household. Tim Hereth stated it is based on the household. Mayor Matt Packard asked the quality of those that took the survey. Tim Hereth stated the sample was random throughout the state, but for areas that needed more data collection or that may be missing an age group, there were letters mailed out in hopes to balance those types of responses. LaNiece Davenport asked for clarification of what counts as a trip. Tim Hereth said each time you get in the car and drive from point A to B is one trip. Moving back from point B to A or moving from point B to C is considered a second trip. Page | 8 An interesting data point is a little over two trips a week are generated with online delivery services. About half of those trips replace one trip the household would have taken. Tim Hereth recommended the MPO Board take time to visit the www.unifiedplan.org website to do a deep dive into the data. An example of the data that can be broken down is that more people commute from Utah County to Salt Lake County than the combined travel from Davis and Weber County to Salt Lake County.
