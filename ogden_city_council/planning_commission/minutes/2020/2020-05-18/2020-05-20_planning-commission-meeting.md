    Commission will further discuss the proposed design standards for the CBD.

2. Discussion, Pending Ordinance: Apartments in Commercial Zones

    Mr. Montgomery stated the Council has adopted a temporary regulation to prohibit any
    new multi-family residential units in commercial zones, allowing a six-month time period
    for an ordinance to be adopted which might consider particular standards or areas where
    these are and are not appropriate usage of land. He stated while Ogden City has an
    abundance of commercially zoned property, there is concern that commercial areas
    turning into residential uses limits the future tax base for retail.

    He described different types of housing, ranging from detached single-family home,
    duplex, triplex, fourplex, townhome multi=plex, mid-rise and high-rise as typical. He
    suggested there may be particular elements in multi-family housing in terms of their form
    and function, the type of units, whether they be studio, one bedroom, two bedroom or even
    micro units or live/work space. He stated while home ownership is desirable, it is not
    always practical, indicating there is often a need for people to have the option to move due
    to career changes, education or other more income. He stated multi-family housing is
    often considered more affordable but can be a higher cost than ownership. A particular

developer may have the intent for ownership but due to the market, units might become
rentals. The City has no ability to control whether units are rental or owner-occupied, and
projects may be developed based on the desires of tenants, diversity and availability of
land. He further reviewed housing types stating some appear to be more successful over
time than others and wanted commissioners to consider what may affect a project’s
longevity such as access to units, building style or amenities. It was felt consideration
should be given to what we “want” to see developed rather than what we “need” and there
should be some foresight in planning choices.

Commission further discussed housing trends in terms of income levels, how to entice
developers, how retail can be mixed with multi-family, what can be done to make housing
more affordable, and attractive for potential residents, noting there is a difference between
low income and affordable housing. While Ogden has more low-income housing than
other cities in the county, it also has a higher poverty level, and much of the housing is
substandard and needs to be upgraded. It was felt while there is an abundance of low-
income housing in the City, affordable housing options are limited, particularly for young
families. Concern was expressed that existing infrastructure may determine the type of
housing, with Commissioners feeling multi-family should be in areas that are walkable
rather than vehicle oriented. Commission also noted one amenity often neglected in
multi-story projects is elevators.

Mr. Montgomery noted the East Central Community has up to 75% rental housing while
the desire is less than 50%. He reviewed projects which have recently been approved and
noted their locations. As he had suggested Commission members to drive by particular
construction project sites, Commission discussed each of these and whether they felt they
were or were not a good location for multi-family development.

Commissioner Schade stated as we review the ordinances, efforts should be made to level
the playing field, with the same standards, whether a project is in the R-4, R=-5 or
commercial zones. Mr. Montgomery compared taxes of two projects in commercial
zones, with one being multi-family residential and the other commercial. Discussion
continued whether areas where multi-family is more desirable might be rezoned as well as
identifying hat type of multi-family makes sense in a particular location. While some
projects have developed on parcels which have been long-time vacant, these may not
always be conducive to a typical apartment-living community with transit connections and
nearby retail or entertainment uses. Discussion continued relating to the increased
potential for work-at-home options, whether parking should be limited, and the potential for
growth of the surrounding community, relating to the use of parks and schools. Mr.
Montgomery asked Commissioners to be watching for good and bad examples to assist in
crafting ordinance language.

Commission further discussed the trends of the millennial generation, stating while it is
commonly assumed their desire is to downsize, many have indicated what they choose is
governed more by what they can afford rather than what they want. Apartment or
condominium living may be desirable for both young singles or families as well as the
aging population but if overpriced, there are no options available to them. Commission felt
the longevity of a project may depend on its proximity to schools, grocery store, shopping,
medical, library or entertainment.

Mr. Montgomery asked the Commission to think about commercially zoned areas where
multi-family should or should not be allowed as well as areas where they exist and whether
the location is appropriate. He also asked them to also consider whether a project would
keep its desirability for 10-20 years without looking shabby, and if there are ways to assure
required green space is maintained over time. He also asked them to be aware of
successful projects and consider what makes them different from those thar are not as
successful.

Discussion continued relating to community experiences, indicating high density housing
is typically a buffer between commercial and single-family residential, there are locations
where diversity of uses within the same community has worked, and that residents can
feel as isolated in a single-family home as in a multi-family facility, but it is the sense of
community that is important.. Commissioners felt certain amenities create better
interaction and invite neighbors to interact, and that the sense of community where one
feels welcome may retain residents as well as the type of structure. It was stated
standards should be clearly defined in the potential ordinance.

There being no additional business, the meeting was adjourned at 7:40 p.m.
