         RESOLUTION; AND RELATED MATTERS. Economic Development Consultant,
         Randy Sant, explained that the purpose of this resolution is to move forward in
         refinancing the existing bond with a lower interest rate that will result in an economic
         savings to the RDA and City of $150,000 to $200,000 a year. This resolution
         establishes the parameters that the agency must work within for the refinancing and
         provides an amendment to a pledge to secure the bonds with excise tax and tax
         increment. It designates officers who will have authority to approve the final terms and
         provisions of the bond, provides authority to place the bonds, and provides the notice
         of intent to issue the bonds.

         The resolution also appoints the designated officers to represent the Redevelopment
         Agency Board to make decisions on pricing, the transaction, interest rate, the terms,
         changes that need to be made to the documents, etc. That saves time to help meet the
         schedule to place the bonds by the first part of November. The designated officers will
         be the RDA chair or vice-chair, the executive director, and the finance director.

         This issuance requires that they cannot issue any new debt. In a normal bond issuance
         you would pay for the cost of issuance. In this case, they cannot issue that additional
         debt. So the cost of issuance would be the obligation of the RDA to pay at closing.
         They estimate that fee to be about $225,000 which will be taken from RDA funds.

         Jason Burningham, of Lewis, Young, Robertson & Burningham, Inc., reviewed the
         bond structure. They have determined a public offering would be the most
         advantageous which means the RDA would select an underwriting firm who would
         buy the bonds from the RDA and then distribute them to the public.

         Board Member Siwik asked if they are still securing the bond with the excise tax and
         are the same things that were pledged on the original bonds being pledge.

         Finance Director, Kyle Kershaw, answered in the affirmative, that the pledged revenue
         will be excise tax and tax increment.

         Board Member Siwik asked if it is up to them at some future point, if they find there is
         enough growth within the project area, to pull back the excise tax and use it elsewhere.

         Mr. Kershaw explained that the excise tax is pledged but with every annual budget the
         Board will be presented with how much and what type of funds will be available, and
         used, to make the debt service payment.

         Board Member deWolfe asked if there are any risks that come with doing a public
         offering versus a direct offering.

         Mr. Burningham explained that the cost of issuing bonds in a public offering will be a
         little bit more than issuing them under direct placement. They are not an additional risk
         but they are an additional cost in terms of issuing the bonds. But what they find is the
         interest rate differential between the public and private placement significantly
         overcomes that cost difference. The risk is it takes longer to get into the marketplace
         which is a risk of the interest rate they will get.

         Board Member deWolfe asked what budget line item the $225,000 would be coming
         out of, and does it put at risk any projects they have slated for this year.

         Mr. Sant said they anticipate using the proceeds from the sale of the D’Ambrosio
         property to cover this cost and it won’t impact any other items scheduled in the budget.
         He explained the pieces that make up the $225,000 fee.

         Director deWolfe moved to approve this resolution.
