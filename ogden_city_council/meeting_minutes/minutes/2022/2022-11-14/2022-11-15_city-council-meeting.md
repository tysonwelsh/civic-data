Proposed Ordinance 2022-47 amending the Zoning Map of Ogden City by
reclassifying property located at 305 Harrison Boulevard from R-1-8 (Single-
Family Residential) to R-2 (Two-Family Residential)

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed zone change for property at
305 Harrison Boulevard came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated William Sneddon has submitted a petition
to rezone the subject property from R-1-8 to R-2. The petition is meant to allow the development of single-family attached units
on the 2.46-acre property. The Planning Commission has recommended approval of the petition finding that the proposed R-2
zoning is consistent with the community plan.

Deputy Planning Manager Simpson summarized the memo and stated the former market site fronted on both 2™ Street
and Harrison Boulevard with a small convenience store. Since the time the market closed, several significant changes have taken
place on the property. The market building was removed from the site in the early 2000s and the property sat undeveloped until
2017 when the Leadership Learning Academy was constructed on the western half of the property. In addition, around the same
time, the intersection of 2nd Street and Harrison Boulevard was realigned to the north to line up with Sheridan Drive. The eastern
portion of the former Sherwood Market site remains undeveloped and is the subject of the petition. The Planning Commission
reviewed the proposal at its September 7, 2022 meeting and forwarded a recommendation of approval. The Commission’s
recommendation was based on the finding that the rezone will be consistent with the General Plan policies and consistent with the
Hillcrest/Bonneville Community Plan.

Chair Nadolski introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2022-47, entitled:

“An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Zoning Map of Ogden City, as adopted by Section 15-3-3 of
the Ogden Municipal Code, to reclassify as two-family residential zone (R-2) property heretofore classified as
single-family residential zone (R-1-8 and R-1-6), generally located at 305 Harrison Boulevard, and providing
that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final passage.”

A copy of the proposed ordinance was deposited with the City Recorder and ordered that the City Recorder have at least
one copy available for public inspection in her office during all business hours.

Chair Nadolski invited input from the applicant.

Mr. Sneddon stated he has considered several different options for the subject property, including commercial and
residential uses. He is interested in building a project that will compliment his existing business nearby and will improve the
subject property and the surrounding area. He stated he appreciates the Council’s consideration of his application.

Minutes of Regular Meeting of Council of Ogden City, Utah, November 15, 2022

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed amendments to the East
Central Community Plan and associated zoning amendments came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the
City’s Planning Division has worked for the last several years to develop updates to the East Central Community Plan with
associated zone text amendments and rezones applicable to the East Central area. The updates have been based on several recent
significant changes that have taken place within the East Central community planning area. This includes the development of the
Ogden Express (OGX) bus rapid transit system, the adoption of the Nine Rails Creative District Master Plan, and changes to
accessory dwelling units (ADUs) throughout the community. The proposed amendments include updates to the East Central
Community Plan itself to incorporate the transit system and development plans along the route, the incorporation of the Nine Rails
Creative District, and changes to ADUs throughout the East Central area. The associated zone text amendments and rezones
introduce the Transit Overlay (TO) and R-9 Nine Rails Creative District zones and subsequent rezones, the creation, definition
and standards for Cottage Lots and Live-Work units and other changes to support those and other related uses that would further
the goals of the transit system and creative district. The amendments also propose a significant change to ADUs by eliminating
previous restrictions in the community plan area as well as making minor changes to the height standards for ADUs.

Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
proposed amendments. Plan update objectives include planning for land uses near the Ogden Express Bus Rapid Transit (BRT)
line; incorporation the Nine Rails Creative District into the Plan; and considering allowing ADUs in the R-1-5 areas. He
summarized the public involvement process facilitated by Planning staff, including collaboration with the Diversity Commission,
soliciting input through the City website, postcards, a survey, and an open house event.

Mr. Brierley concluded the Planning Commission recommends amending the East Central Community Plan, Zoning
Ordinance, and Zoning Map to promote mass transit and incorporate the Ogden Express BRT, Implement the Nine Rails Creative
District Master Plan, and provide for ADUs; there are a few corrections to the proposed ordinances associated with the
amendments and those corrections have been submitted to Council staff.

Chair Nadolski invited Council Deputy Director Symes to read the corrections referenced by Mr. Brierley. Mr. Symes
cited a few typographical errors in two of the ordinance documents, indicating they are minor in nature and do not change the

noted she moved to Ogden from a “Navy town” that tried to implement ADU uses but did not do so effectively and the result was
negative. The Ogden City Planning team has done a great job at considering language and standards that would prevent that type
of outcome in Ogden and she is in favor of the current proposal.

Cullen Chapman stated he lives in District Four of the City and while development sounds doable and beneficial, there is
on major problem and that is the availability of off-street parking. He echoed the concerns expressed by Mr. Jones and stated there

D.S. Anderson stated that he does not live in the East Central Community Plan area, but he echoed the comments made
by Mr. Jones and Mr. Chapman regarding on-street parking and the increase in vehicles in residential neighborhoods. He
