     Mayor Lang informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular Council

     consider public comments regarding application Z-11-2022, filed by West Valley
     City, Requesting a Zone Change for Property Within the Lake Park Corporate Centre
     from M (Manufacturing) to BRP (Business/Research Park) on 384.8 Acres.

       Proposed Ordinance 23-04 related to the proposal to be considered by the City
       Council subsequent to the public hearing, was discussed as follows:

       ACTION: ORDINANCE NO. 23-04, AMEND THE ZONING MAP TO SHOW
       A CHANGE OF ZONE FOR PROPERTY LOCATED WITHIN THE LAKE
       PARK CORPORATE CENTRE FROM M (MANUFACTURING) TO BRP
       (BUSINESS/RESEARCH PARK)
       Steve Pastorik, CED Director, discussed proposed Ordinance 23-04 that would
       amend the Zoning Map to Show a Change of Zone for Property Located Within the
       Lake Park Corporate Centre from M (Manufacturing) to BRP (Business/Research
       Park).

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

          Staff is proposing this zone change for the following reasons:

                 The General Plan designation within the Lake Park Corporate Centre
                  for the areas outside of the golf course is Business Park.
                 A portion of the Lake Park Corporate Centre is already zoned BRP.

                 The Lake Park Corporate Centre is largely developed as an office park.
                 Suburban Land Reserve (SLR) is no longer involved in the governing
                  board of the Lake Park Corporate Centre.

                  partially zoned M to BRP.

B.     ACCEPT PUBLIC INPUT REGARDING APPLICATION ZT-5-2022,
       FILED BY WEST VALLEY CITY, REQUESTING TO AMEND SECTIONS
       7-6-301 AND 7-6-308 OF THE ZONING ORDINANCE BY REVISING THE
       USE RESTRICTIONS AND DESIGN STANDARDS FOR THE BRP
       (BUSINESS/RESEARCH PARK) ZONE
       Mayor Lang informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular Council
       Meeting scheduled January 24, 2023, in order for the City Council to hear and
       consider public comments regarding application ZT-5-2022, Filed by West Valley
       City, Requesting to Amend Sections 7-6-301 and 7-6-308 of the Zoning Ordinance by
       Revising the Use Restrictions and Design Standards for the BRP (Business/Research
       Park) Zone

       Proposed Ordinance 23-05 related to the proposal to be considered by the City
       Council subsequent to the public hearing, was discussed as follows:

       ACTION: ORDINANCE NO. 23-05, AMEND SECTIONS 7-6-301 AND 7-6-308
       OF THE WEST VALLEY CITY MUNICIPAL CODE TO AMEND CERTAIN
       REQUIRMENTS CONCERNING THE BUSINESS/RESEARCH PARK ZONE
       Steve Pastorik, CED Director, discussed proposed Ordinance 23-05 that would
       amend Sections 7-6-301 and 7-6-308 of the West Valley City Municipal Code to
       Amend Certain Requirements Concerning the Business/Research Park Zone

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

          Staff’s original proposal added the following standards to the BRP zone:
               limit the amount of warehousing to 60% of all buildings on a lot,
MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JANUARY 17, 2023
                       -4-


            limit the amount of light industrial to 60% of all buildings on a lot,
            prohibit overhead doors from facing high-image arterial streets, and
            require warehouses to meet the commercial design standards.

     After receiving input from Lake Park Project, LLC, a property owner within
     Lake Park Corporate Centre who expressed concerns about the proposed zone
     change from M (Manufacturing) to BRP, the Planning Commission
     recommended that the following land uses be allowed in the BRP zone:
          brew restaurant, brew restaurant and liquor retailer, and small brewer;
          fast food establishment with drive-up window; and
          indoor event center.

     Staff is forwarding the proposed amendments for two reasons. First, the two
     main areas of the City with BRP zoning – Lake Park Corporate Centre and
     Metro Business Park – are predominantly office uses. The proposed use
     restrictions will help prevent these areas from becoming predominantly
     industrial. Second, given the large amount of property zoned to allow
     industrial uses, there are plenty of opportunities for exclusively industrial use.
     Adding the proposed standards to the BRP zone will further differentiate it
     from other zones.

  Councilmember Whetstone asked if any prohibited uses proposed would affect the
  golf course. Steve replied no. Councilmember Harmon stated he would rather
  reduce the amount of warehousing and light industrial to 30% so that the look and
  feel of the area remains office. He noted that he wouldn’t want to see higher semi
  traffic in the area given the location of schools and round-a-bouts. Mayor Lang
  asked if there are any restrictions on warehousing in the manufacturing zone. Steve
  replied no. Mayor Lang indicated that she would not want to allow drive up
  windows for fast food in this area. She asked if a lot of warehouse uses have
  applied. Steve replied that he is unsure if any were turned down by the Board in the
  Lake Park area but the Council denied a biotechnical center that had manufacturing
  uses. Mayor Lang asked how the spine clinic is coming along. Steve replied that it
  is still stalled and in the subdivision process. Councilmember Fitisemanu asked
  how the 60% limitation on warehousing and industrial uses was determined. Steve
  replied that this was calculated when working with the developer who was
  proposing the biomedical complex. Councilmember Huynh stated that he believes
  the zoning should remain to allow more office. Councilmember Harmon stated that
  this change would allow more office to come in. Steve agreed and noted that this
  change is more restrictive than what is currently in place.

  The City Council will consider Ordinance 23-05 at the Regular Council Meeting

RESOLUTION 23-02: APPROVE THE PURCHASE OF EQUIPMENT FOR USE
BY THE PARKS AND RECREATION DEPARTMENT
Nancy Day, Parks and Recreation Director, presented proposed Resolution 23-02 that
would approve the Purchase of Equipment for Use by the Parks and Recreation Department

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       Golf course maintenance equipment at both courses is worn down and not
       functioning as it should. It no longer performs as designed without significant
       repair time and new parts. The current equipment has the equivalent of 200,000
       miles on it. Some of the failing parts have an 18-month replacement time or cannot
       be purchased at all. Some equipment is completely non-functional limiting what
       the staff has available to complete necessary tasks.

       Maintenance equipment pieces which are available in the current fiscal year
       include:

              John Deere HD300 Sprayer
              Toro Sand Pro 3040 with front plow
              Toro Sand Pro 3040
              Toro Groundsmaster 3300 72” mower
              Cushman 1200 Hauler Utility Carts
              Weidenmann deep time aerifier

       The total cost of this equipment (for both courses) comes to a total of $317,829.
       Suppliers are on the State Contract for purchasing.

Councilmember Harmon stated that he believes this type of equipment should be included
in the budget and that there should be a capital replacement reserve in place. Nancy
indicated that this was proposed as part of a program modification and noted there is no
particular line item within the budget for this equipment. Wayne indicated that the City has
approached the purchase of this equipment in different ways over the years, including
leasing. He stated that between operational funds and previous debt service for the golf
course, putting aside a capital replacement reserve hasn’t been an option. Wayne indicated
that this is something the City could be in the position to do now if a funding source could
be identified. Councilmember Harmon stated that he would like to see this occur for these
types of purchases. Councilmember Whetstone asked if capital funding is uncommon in
    MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JANUARY 17, 2023

municipalities. Wayne replied that it can be but depends on the municipality and its unique
needs. He indicated that West Valley City is fairly young and inherited a lot of needs and
experienced significant growth as well. Wayne provided a brief history on how previous
Council’s have directed funding. He noted that the upcoming budget looks healthy and this
is something that the Council can consider funding.

The City Council will consider Resolution 23-02 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled

RESOLUTION 23-03: AUTHORIZE THE PURCHASE OF NETTING AND
RELATED SERVICES FROM JUDGE NETTING MOUNTAIN WEST
Nancy Day, Parks and Recreation Director, presented proposed Resolution 23-03 that
would authorize the Purchase of Netting and Related Services from Judge Netting
Mountain West.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       Golf course netting at Stonebridge Golf Club exists for the safety of property and
       persons located directly adjacent to the golf course. The poles and netting are
       original. Maintenance staff have worked each year to repair them to prolong their
       life. The nets have outlived their effectiveness and can no longer be repaired to
       adequately aid in the safety of the residents and properties.

       Bids were provided by the following companies:

              Judge Netting Mountain West:          $90,021
              Net Connection:                       $101,178
              Coastal Netting Systems:              $96,543

       Judge Netting Mountain West was selected as the lowest responsible bidder.

Councilmember Harmon asked how long the City has owned Stonebridge. Wayne replied
about 25 years. Councilmember Harmons stated that the City could start reserving funds
for this replacement to occur in another 25 years. Wayne replied that this is something the
Council can consider as part of the upcoming budget.

The City Council will consider Resolution 23-03 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
January 24, 2023 at 6:30 P.M

EQUIPMENT FROM GARRETT PARKS AND PLAY FOR THE SUNSET HILLS

Nancy Day, Parks and Recreation Director, presented proposed Resolution 23-04 that
would authorize the Purchase of Playground Equipment from Garrett Parks and Play for
the Sunset Hills Park.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       As construction of Sunset Hills Park approaches, the Parks and Recreation
       Department is pre-ordering the site furnishings: playground equipment, pavilion,
       tables, benches, and exercise equipment for the park. By pre-ordering, the
       department is saving money. Garrett Parks and Play will be the supplier for the
       playgrounds, playground shade structures and impact material.

       Sunset Hills Park is located at 6420 South Oquirrh Mesa Drive. It is 3.09 acres of
       land that is currently a vacant lot. We are beginning the bidding process in January
       to build the park in 2023. It is a common practice of contractors to mark up the cost
       of equipment. The Parks and Recreation Department would like to purchase this
       equipment now to avoid the inflation that occurs each year and to avoid any mark-
       up the contractor might add. Garrett Parks and Play is a state approved contractor
       and thus it is not necessary to bid the project. They hold state contract MA2568 for
       playgrounds. They will be supplying and installing the playgrounds, playground
       shade structures, and impact materials.

Mayor Lang asked if the City is installing the equipment. Nancy replied that the contractor
will install. Mayor Lang asked if the installation is included in this cost. Nancy replied yes.

The City Council will consider Resolution 23-04 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
January 24, 2023 at 6:30 P.M

Steve Lehman, CED, presented application SMI-11-2022, Filed by Ken Milne, Requesting
Final Plat Approval for the Day Estates Subdivision Located at 3789 South 5200 Westnt.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       Ken Milne is requesting final subdivision approval for the Day Estates Subdivision.
       The property was recently rezoned from the A zone to the R-1-8 zone and has a
       development agreement to guide the project.

       The subdivision will consist of 7 lots on approximately 1.9 acres. This equates to
       an overall density of 3.5 units per acre. Lot sizes range from 8,003 square feet to
       17,134 square feet. The City Council granted an exception to the frontage
       requirement of those lots fronting 5200 West.

       Access to the subdivision will be gained from 5200 West. There are three flag lots
       within the subdivision. The flag on the north will gain access via a single stem
       while the other two flag lots will share a common access easement.

       The existing dwelling is anticipated to stay. The developer has expressed interest in fixing
       the old structure and has hinted about using some of the architectural features from this home
       for new homes within the subdivision.
