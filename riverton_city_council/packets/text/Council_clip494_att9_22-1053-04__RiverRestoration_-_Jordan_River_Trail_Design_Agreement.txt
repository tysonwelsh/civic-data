Dear Trace,
RiverRestoration appreciates the opportunity to offer our engineering services for the planning and design
of bank stabilization and river access project in Riverton City. We understand the City is looking to
stabilize the west bank of the Jordan River along the Jordan River Parkway north of Dr. O Roi Hardy
Park. Erosion is currently occurring just downstream of a Salt Lake County bank stabilization project
constructed in 2008/2009. The project will provide additional river access and a passive park area similar
to recent improvements at the Big Bend project in West Jordan, Utah.
Our services will include Project Management, Site Assessment and Survey, Conceptual Design,
Preliminary Design, Permitting, and Final Design. The city communicated that the project currently has a
budget of $120,000 for planning, design, and implementation. RiverRestoration will work with City staff
to develop a design that meets the financial constraints and aid you in developing grant applications for
this project from the Utah Office of Outdoor Recreation (due March 16, 2022). A detailed scope of work
can be found in Attachment A and an estimate of design and planning fees is provided in Attachment B.
Please contact me if you have questions or concerns. We are looking forward to working with you and the
rest of the project stakeholders involved in this project. Thanks again for the opportunity.

Attachment A – Scope of Work

Task 1 – Project Management and Coordination
The purpose of this task is to manage resources to generate deliverables, meet deadlines, and coordinate
with Riverton City (City) staff and other project partners. This task includes the following work activities:
    1.1 Attend project kick-off meeting with City staff and other project partners. Meeting will be held in
        person on the project site.
        Deliverables: None
    1.2 Conduct general coordination with the City staff, subconsultants, and project stakeholders.
        Deliverables: None
    1.3 Perform monthly tracking, reporting, and invoicing.
        Deliverables: Monthly status reports and invoices.
    1.4 Assist the City with grant materials for the Utah Office of Outdoor Recreation Grant.
        Deliverables: Preliminary design and cost estimate for the grant application.

Task 1 Assumptions:
    There will be no significant schedule delays. Extension of the project timeline may result in
       additional design and project management costs.
    City staff will determine kick-off meeting attendees and choose a date and time for the kickoff
       meeting.
    City staff will fill out and submit the grant application for the Utah Office of Outdoor Recreation.

Task 2 – Site Reconnaissance and Survey
The purpose of this task is to perform an existing data search and site investigation and gather necessary
survey data for the design of the proposed improvements. This task includes the following work activities:
    2.1 Review existing documents provided by City staff and project stakeholders.
        Deliverables: None
    2.2 Conduct site assessment visit.
        Deliverables: None
    2.3 Perform topographic/hydrographic survey of the project site.
        Deliverables: None
    2.4 Prepare base map in AutoCAD to document existing conditions.
        Deliverables: 11x17 PDF base map of existing conditions
    2.5 Perform aquatic resource delineation, identifying wetlands and ordinary high water within the
        project reach.
        Deliverables: Aquatic resource maps and areas for use in the permitting effort (Task 6).

Task 2 Assumptions:
    City staff or project stakeholders will provide the RiverRestoration team with all existing data
       that needs to be reviewed as a part of this task.
    The topographic survey will be augmented with available LiDAR, downloaded from the State of
       Utah’s GIS database.

Task 3 – Conceptual Alternatives
The purpose of this task is to develop a conceptual design alternative (30%) to present to City staff and
project stakeholders. The conceptual design will include the evaluation of design features and costs to
meet project goals and objectives within the identified budget. The conceptual design will allow the
stakeholders to select the project features and approach prior to moving into permitting and construction
documents. This task includes the following work activities:
    3.1 Use information collected from the data review, survey, input from City staff and project
         stakeholders to develop a conceptual design with selectable additional features. The concept design
         will be aligned with the project’s Goals and Objectives. Select design features for the project
         elements along with estimated costs developed in Task 3.3 will be used to prioritize Project Goals
         and Objectives within a limited budget, leading to a Preliminary Design.
         Deliverables: Conceptual Plans in PDF format.
    3.2 Develop 30% Construction cost estimate for the concept plan and selectable features.
         Deliverables: Conceptual level cost estimate the concept plan and selectable features in PDF
         format.
    3.3 Present alternatives at a Conceptual Design Review (CDR) meeting. The meeting may be held
         virtually.
         Deliverables: CDR Presentation materials and response to City and stakeholder comments.

Task 3 Assumptions:
    City staff and project stakeholders will select preferred options, if any, prior to moving to Task 4.
    This task does not include structural/geotechnical engineering, landscape architecture, or
       professional land surveying and assumes these services are not needed at the conceptual design
       phase.
    The RiverRestoration team and City staff will discuss available construction budget prior to
       beginning work on the conceptual design.
    It is assumed that stormwater infrastructure, including detention and conveyance, will not be
       required for this project. Grading the improvements to drain off into the Jordan River or the
       surrounding landscape shall be sufficient.
    It is assumed that flexible asphalt or rigid concrete paving is not required for this project. The
       addition of these surfaces would require a geotechnical investigation and associated scope
       increase.
    It is assumed that lighting is not required for this project. Any lighting or electrical-related
       improvements will require an additional scope.

stakeholders. The 60% design will be used in the permitting process. This task includes the following
work activities:
