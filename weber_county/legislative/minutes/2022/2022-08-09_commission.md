G. ACTION ITEMS:
     1. APPROVAL OF A SCOPE OF WORK AGREEMENT WITH THE DEPARTMENT OF
        WORKFORCE SERVICES REGARDING IMPLEMENTATION OF THE ICAN MODEL,
        DEVELOPING A COORDINATED SYSTEM WITHIN THE COMMUNITY TO ADDRESS THE
        NEEDS OF TANF ELIGIBLE FAMILIES.
            Chair Jenkins: This is the culmination of four years of work for Melissa and myself.
            Melissa Freigang (Executive Director, Weber Prosperity Center of Excellence): Nycole Tylka from the
            Department of Workforce Services (Assistant Director for DWS over TNAF programs) and two families who
            want to share their experiences with the program. I want to talk about the resilience and courage of these
            families. As you know the system is difficult to navigate. The partners that are here today help ensure there
            is no duplication of services or missing services; we average 27 agency partners surrounding our families
            helping them move forward. We measure families against themselves using our baseline measurement tools.
            Each family is assigned a resource integration coach upon entering the program. (Resource integration coaches
            recognized).
            Chair Jenkins: I had the chance the to sit and watch the resource integration coaches work and navigate through
            the system and it’s truly a labor of love for what they do. And from my heart I appreciate you.
            Mrs Freigang: I want to take a moment to have each of the attending partners introduce themselves. It’s really
            about the human relationships and social capital. This is more about a long term assistance to help change the
            cycle for the children. Average family income moves from $10,800 annually to $28,735 on average for
            families employed. In Weber County IGP statistics, there are zero post-secondary degrees. In our ICAN
            families there is 45% post secondary enrollment.
         Nycole Tylka (Assistant Director over TANF programs, Department of Workforce Services): TANF is a
         federally funded grant each state receives. The Department of Workforce Services (DWS) administers that
         grant for the state of Utah. As part of that grant, there is a required cash assistance program administered
         through DWS. Other TANF funds are allocated from legislative appropriations or the TANF spending plan.
         The Weber County ICAN program was funded in the last legislative session.

         Mrs. Freigang: $4.5M raised in the last three years for the ICAN program. I would love for our families to
         stand up and I invite the two that are comfortable speaking come forward to share their story. We are really
         focused on our employer partners now; and not just job placement but having them come to the table as
         partners. Northrop Grumman is getting kids in for tours onsite and getting them interested in careers. The
         magic in this program is the human-to-human, one-by-one connection that empowers.

         Nathan Fernandez: I have a lot to say about this program. I experienced divorce and a broken family two
         years ago. I am a suicide survivor. I existed on my friend’s couch for several months and had no ability to get
         myself treated for my problems. Through ICAN, I was able to understand how to apply for assistance I was
         absolutely needing; I was able to get on Medicaid; I was able to begin weekly therapy sessions for my serious
         issues neglected for many years; I had no food and they helped me get food through Catholic Community
         Services; when I explained I was existing on my friend’s couch and saw no way out, they explained about rent
         relief program was available; they helped me get into a new place. I was able to get physical therapy for
         injuries. I was able to get a bus pass. I was able to be employed for twice I had been employed for before; I
         never thought that was possible. I felt powerless in that time of struggle. If I didn’t have that help, I don’t
         know if I would be here and if I could provide a safe, stable household for my kids to visit. I just wanted to
         say thank you for being willing to fund a program and staffed by people who care.
         Liz Morrison: I have been part of ICAN program for the past two-three years. I can proudly say I am a recent
         BYU-I graduate with my bachelors degree and ready to start a job search. My oldest just graduated from high
         school and has a job at Costco and is on par to get his associate’s degree in a year. I am very grateful.
         Stephanie Winters: It’s been about a year I’ve been working with ICAN; I was referred by my Bishop. I am
         a single mother of twins boys, one with autism. I’m making $5 more an hour than before and not hurting my
         body doing housekeeping. It’s very rare to find a program like this and I really consider them my friends. This
         is an amazing program. You make things better by making yourself better and this program is the embodiment
         of that. My kids are going to Boys and Girls Club and they want to continue going after school starts. It’s a
         phenomenal program.
         Chair Jenkins: This program was started by Commissioner Jim Ebert. Ever since I got here, the push for this
         program was to see if we could implement this on a much larger level. The program has been able to help 40
         50 families at a time. I told Melissa before I left I wanted to get this program to a level that I could feel the
         difference. We have been to the state many times and they have never come through with funding, with it
         always being cut at the last minute. This funding will provide help four times over what could be done
         previously and serve hundreds of families and over 600 people. It’s magic to watch this process work for
         families to overcome barriers. The system is tougher than we think even with DWS doing what they can. For
         some it’s just not enough. Melissa is using TANF funds to do IGP work that is for all intents and purposes no
         longer funded by the state. This is the chance to scale this program to what we envision.
         Commissioner Froerer: I want to thank Melissa for her efforts. As you know four years ago, I was not the
         biggest cheerleader for this program as I am an outcome-based guy and I wanted to see the outcomes before
         we spend government dollars on a program. You have convinced me this is something we as a community
         need to raise each other through a hand up not a hand out. This is something that needs to continue. We hope
         this can be a model for the entire state. If some of the state legislators could be in the room today, I think we
         might change some viewpoints. You’ve changed mine.
         James Ebert (Boys and Girls Club): I have had an opportunity to spend some time with Melissa over the last
         couple of years. I just want to express my appreciation to Commissioner Jenkins and Commissioner Froerer.
         My congratulations to you on building this out program. I hope Commissioner Jenkins that in the next phase
         of your life there is some passion to continue this program. To do that, I think there needs to be a mindshift
         from intergenerational poverty to economic development. Maybe it’s time for us to have conversations that
         it’s not about a social service issue, it’s about a quality of life issue, it’s an economic development issue and
         capturing those to who we lost over time. Maybe we look at economic development under community
         development, which is more about quality of life.
         Chair Jenkins: This has never been a ‘hand out’ program but, as Commissioner Froerer said, it’s a ‘hand up’
         program. We want those participating in the program to exit it with successes.
