         The purpose of the work session was to review the agenda for the City Council meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.;
receive an update regarding the Nine Rails Art Plaza project; discuss CARES Act Grant funding; discuss Council business; and
hear Council comments.

Agenda Review
        Chair Choberka and members of Council staff briefly reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council
meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Nine Rails Art Plaza Update
          Chair Choberka disclosed that she has a personal connection to someone who may stand to benefit financially from the
Nine Rails Art Plaza project and she recused herself from participating in discussion of the matter.
          Representatives from the Community and Economic Development Department provided an update on the progress of the
Art Plaza planned for the comer of 25 th Street and Ogden Avenue (445 25 th Street). The staff have secured grant funding of more
than $3 .4 million for construction and on-going programming for the Plaza. Additional Recreation, Arts, Musemn, Parks (RAMP)
grant funds may come available in 2021. It is estimated that the total cost of acquisition and construction of the project will be
more than $4.3 million.
          Community and Economic Development Business Development Manager Meess used the aid of a PowerPoint
presentation to provide the Council with an update regarding the project. She first provided background information regarding the
project dating back to 2015. The latest action was taken in 2020 when the City contracted with Sasaki for plaza design and began
pursuing funding and programming partnerships. She presented an aerial image to orient the Council to the Nine Rails District
boundaries and the location of the Art Plaza.
          Jake McEntire, Union Creative Agency, discussed the community engagement process associated with the project; a
project website was created and 511 individuals have visited the site. A survey was conducted and there were 149 English and 5
Spanish responses. Additionally, a virtual question and answer session was held on Facebook and the reach from that event was
5,000 English and 2,700 Spanish participants. Participants were given the opportunity to provide feedback regarding their desired
uses for the space. An onsite event will take place this Thursday to solicit more feedback from participants regarding their visions
for the design of the plaza.
          Ashley Bowen then reported on the design process and principles; principles were identified by the Master Plan,
community input, and the vision of the Arts Advismy Committee for the project. There were also a few workshop meetings with
several stakeholders and this led to the development of three initial concepts that would organize the land and the components of

CARES Act Grant Funding
         Council member Hyer declared he is the owner of a small business that has applied for a Corona Virus Aid and
Economic Security (CARES) Act fund grant; he recused himself from discussion of this item.
         Council Policy Analyst Mabey explained the City Council is considering a proposed budget amendmentto recognize and
appropriate $5,598,675 in CARES Act funding. An interlocal agreement is also proposed for consideration between Ogden City
and Weber County to coordinate the distribution of funds for nonprofit and small business grants.
         Comptroller Stout explained the City received the frrst wave of CARES Act funding in June this year. The City has now
received a second wave of funding in the amount of$5,598,675 and City Administration recommends a budget amendment to
recognize and appropriate the additional funding. Administration proposes allocating $1,299,350 to COVID business grants and
$1,299,325 to COVID City related expenditures. She presented a spreadsheet identifying the amounts of CARES Act funding
being awarded to various Departments/Divisions in the City and the purpose of the expenditure. City Divisions have been
informed of the opportunity to request CARES Act funds through an approved application; they must provide justification ofhow
the expenditures will help the City prevent, prepare for, and respond to the impacts of the public health emergency. Ms. Stout
concluded the remaining $3,000,000 would be distributed to non-profits and small businesses according to the terms of the
recommended interlocal agreement with Weber County for the disbursement of CARES Act funding.
         Brief discussion among the Council and City staff centered on the types ofbusinesses in the City that are eligible to apply
for funding from the City and from the County.

Council Business
         Council member White reported the Utah League of Cities and Towns is trying their best to produce a quality annual
convention in the middle of this pandemic. They are proceeding with a virtual format and she encouraged Council members to
support the event and the League. They will also be providing mobile tours, some of which will be in Ogden.
         Council member White then offered a 'shout-out' to the crew of employees that responded to the damage caused by last
week's windstorm. She asked that her gratitude be passed along to the Public Services Department. She noted she heard from an
employee in South Ogden City who thanked Ogden for their support and response to the areas of their city that were hardest hit.
