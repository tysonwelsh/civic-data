31          1.1       Additional Discussion Items.
32
33   Chair Allen reported that Project ZMA-22-001 is a public hearing to receive public comments and
34   take possible action on a request from Adam Nash to rezone the property located at 3526 East Fort
35   Union Boulevard from Residential Single-Family (R-1-8) to Residential Multi-Family (R-2-8). This
36   request also includes a proposal to amend the land use designation of the property from “Residential
37   Office” to “Residential Medium-Density”.
38
39   Community and Economic Development Senior Planner, Andrew Hulka, stated there are two parts to
40   the request. The first is a land use map amendment and the second is a rezone. Staff determined that
41   it meets the intent of the General Plan and is suitable for the area. A property rendering was displayed.
42   The property is under one acre in size with a proposal to change the land use designation from
43   Residential Office to Residential Medium-Density. The proposed rezone was from Residential
44   Single-Family to Residential Multi-Family. Mr. Hulka reported that the lot standards are similar to
45   what is allowed in an R-1-8 zone. There is a smaller setback in the R-2-8 zone with a total of at least
46   15 feet between the two side yards with side setbacks of no less than five feet. The applicant is
47   contemplating a three-lot subdivision with R-2-8 zoning which, if approved, would allow two units
48   on each lot.

 2   Mr. Hulka explained that the City initiated a land use map change along the eastern portion of Fort
 3   Union Boulevard. The discussion included not implementing heavy commercial or intense
 4   development in the narrower sections of the road. Properties facing neighborhood streets were to
 5   remain as Low-Density Residential. Previous concerns included leaving the property as low density,
 6   which may lead to undesirable results and lead Staff to select options that would result in mixed-use
 7   development. Doing so would allow opportunities for redevelopment that fits the context of the
 8   neighborhood. Staff felt that the R-2-8 zone was appropriate and would be consistent with the area.
 9   He clarified that the current zoning is R-1-8 and only allows single-family residences where R-2-8 is
10   being proposed to allow for single or two-family dwellings.
11
12   Commissioner Ebbeler commented that the public comments received were consistent with the
13   potential height concerns. He understood that the R-1-8 and R-2-8 zoning ordinances were similar.
14   Mr. Hulka confirmed that the maximum height in both zones is 35 feet. Community and Economic
15   Development Director, Michael Johnson, clarified that the modified setbacks and additional height
16   granted are not options with the proposed property.
17
18   Commissioner Mills was hopeful that what is proposed will be utilized to implement the Fort Union

39          3.1     (Project ZMA-22-001) A Public Hearing to Receive Comments and Take Possible
40                  Action on a Request from Adam Nash to Rezone the Property Located at 3526
41                  East Fort Union Boulevard from Residential Single-Family (R-1-8) to Residential
42                  Multi-Family (R-2-8). This Request Also includes a Proposal to Amend the Land
43                  Use Designation of the Property from “Residential Office” to “Residential
44                  Medium-Density”.
45
46   Community and Development Senior Planner, Andrew Hulka, presented the Staff Report and stated
47   that the above request includes a proposal to amend the land use designation from Residential Office
48   to Residential Medium-Density. Residential Medium-Density corresponds to the R-1-6 zone with

     Cottonwood Heights Planning Commission Meeting – 03/02/2022                                           5
 1   smaller lot sizes and single-family areas with R-2-8 zoning. A property rendering was displayed. Mr.
 2   Hulka reported that the property currently includes R-1-8 zoning and a single-family home with
 3   accessory buildings. A Concept Plan was submitted that had yet to be reviewed by Staff. Mr. Hulka
 4   stated that all subdivisions with fewer than 10 lots require staff review and approval and are subject
 5   to all subdivision and Code standards.
 6
 7   Mr. Hulka reported that through 2019, the long-range plan for Fort Union Boulevard included mixed-
 8   use development. At that time, the City initiated a land use amendment to change the properties to
 9   either Low-Density Residential, Medium-Density Residential, or Residential Office Land Use. The
10   City desired to have redevelopment of the corridor without leading to a more intense use or
11   commercial development. Staff supported the applicant’s rezone to R-2-8 as this would allow for
12   residential uses only and be consistent with the immediately adjacent properties. Staff recommended
13   that the Planning Commission recommend approval to the City Council. It was confirmed that the
14   maximum height in the proposed R-2-8 zone is 35 feet, which is the same height restriction that exists
15   in single-family zones.
16
17   Commissioner Ebbeler asked how many lots were included in the request. Mr. Hulka stated that the
18   current property includes a single-family dwelling and two parcels owned by the same owner.
19
20   Commissioner Mills asked for clarification regarding the flag lot issue and preferred there not be a
21   flag lot included with the project. With regard to the Fort Union Master Plan, he believed there may
22   be interest in the lack of accessibility to the proposed area. Mr. Hulka reviewed the basic standards
23   for flag lots. Commissioner Mills did not believe it was likely and would allow the applicant to clarify
24   his intent. He confirmed that any new subdivision is required to provide frontage improvements that
25   include curb, gutter, sidewalk, park strips, street trees, and streetlights. He noted that as discussed
26   previously, Staff was working with the Engineering Department to create cross-sections to ensure
27   that a design is in place and to require improvements.
28
29   Associate City Planner, Samantha DeSeelhorst, stated that others have interested in the property and
30   proposed different configurations. They were not, however, able to make anything other than what
31   is proposed work. Staff discovered that when looking at the width there may be adequate gross
32   acreage to fit one extra lot but there would be no buildable area.
33
34   The applicant, Adam Nash, from Land Development, LLC commented that he was not seeking any
35   exceptions or variances and stated that the plan was to develop the property. He explained that the
36   property requires large enough pads to develop high-end properties. He noted that they do not intend
37   to include a flag lot. They were anticipating 211 feet for the initial profile to be built on that section
38   of Fort Union Boulevard. The project will also include curb, gutter, sidewalk, and a bike lane. He
39   reported that they developed twin homes at 3267 East Bengal Boulevard and what is proposed will
40   be a step up from those units. The existing home was built in the 1930s with two outbuildings that
41   were converted to apartments. He stated that they have no historical value.
