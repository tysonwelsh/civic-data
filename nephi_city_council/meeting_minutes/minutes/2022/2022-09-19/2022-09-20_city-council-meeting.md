Mayor Justin Seely opened the regular council meeting and welcomed all present.

PRAYER: Councilor Jeramie Callaway

PLEDGE OF ALLEGIANCE: Luke Flickinger, Owner Utah Landscape and Rock.
Mr. Flickinger gave a brief history of Utah Landscape and Rock and noted that their revenues have
doubled this past year. He said that he wanted to honor his dad who had suddenly passed away last
year and who had started the business 10 years ago. He has rebranded the business but had painted his
Dad’s sayings on each company truck.

CONSENT AGENDA APPROVED:

  Councilor Memmott moved to approve the consent agenda which included the approval of the
minutes for the regular council meeting 9-6-22, and a list of claims on the warrant register dated 9-20-
22. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

WELL-BEING SURVEY RESULTS DISCUSSED:

   Utah State University Professor Courtney Flint joined the meeting via Zoom. Ms. Flint discussed the
results from the 2022 Well-Being Survey. Nephi had 250 complete responses. She asked the council to
forward the link to the results to the citizens of Nephi. Overall 60% of the respondents gave a 4 to 5
rating for community wellbeing and 82% of respondents gave a 4 to 4 rating for persona wellbeing.

PUBLIC COMMENT:

   Tony Perides told the council that they are doing “a hell of a good job.” He said he owns and
manages the Haven Coffee House, and they are having a lot of success. They have seven employees and
are having many tourists visit their establishment. James Jenkins said that he is managing the social
media for the business.

HIVE MASTER OPERATING AGREEMENT APPROVED:

  City Administrator Seth Atkinson reviewed the most recent draft of the Hive master operating
agreement and exhibits. The Juab School Board has already approved the operating agreement. Staff
has spent numerous hours with the staff from the school district working on the agreement. Board and
City Council leadership have also met together and examined the agreement. The agreement has been

drafted by Kasey Wright, the City Attorney, who is also counsel for the school district. The city has had
outside counsel also review the agreement and their recommendations were incorporated.
   The very beginning of the agreement contains a preamble stating the following:

   “The Hive is the result of an unprecedented cooperative effort between private donors, JSD, and City.
This project would not have happened but for the participation of all three groups. The intent of these
three groups is to create a facility that will be the jewel of Juab County and benefit students, City
residents, and the local economy for decades to come. The Hive is the first facility in the State of Utah
that will be equally owned by a city and a school district. JSD and City recognize that they serve the same
children. The parties further recognize that each party is generally supported by the same taxpayers.
With such recognition, JSD and City enter this Agreement with a willingness to sacrifice their own
interests at times to benefit the children and residents of east Juab County.”

  This preamble provides a context for future generations and government leaders to encourage
continued cooperation in the operation of the Hive. The agreement establishes the 50/50 ownership
arrangement for the school district and city. It also sets forth operating rules and a master schedule is
attached as one of the agreement exhibits. A building replacement fund will be established to fund
future repairs and replacements to the structure. The schedule for the building replacement fund is
attached as an exhibit. Both entities’ insurance carriers have also reviewed the agreement and their
changes have been incorporated. It is anticipated that the spirit of cooperation will continue well in the
future, but the agreement also has a dispute resolution process.
  Councilor Parady moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the Hive agreement. Councilor Worwood
seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

REED RIDGE ESCROW AGREEMENT:

  Mr. Atkinson explained to the council that the developer of the recently approved Reed Ridge
Subdivision (6-lot subdivision north of Maverick) would like to record the plat prior to the installation of
the public infrastructure. The city code has a provision for this in Title 11.8.3 . According to this section
of code the developer must post 125% of the cost of the improvements into an escrow account and
enter into an escrow agreement that meets with the approval of the City Attorney. The City Attorney
has crafted the escrow agreement that has also been reviewed by staff. The cost of the improvements
has been calculated at $1,053,213.40.
   Councilor Memmott moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the Reed Ridge Escrow Agreement.
Councilor Callaway seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

OUT-OF-STATE TRAVEL APPROVED FOR CODE ENFORCEMENT OFFICER ANDREA HOLM:

 Councilor Worwood moved to approve the out-of-state travel for Code Enforcement Officer Andrea
Holm to attend a conference in Wendover this coming March. Councilor Parady seconded the motion.
The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

CITY ADMINISTRATOR AND CITY COUNCIL UPDATES:

   Mr. Atkinson reminded the council about the Juab County Transportation Master Plan open house on
September 28th at 6:00 to 7:00 p.m. in the Commission Chambers.
  Mr. Atkinson updated the council about the DWR land swap. He said that the yellow-book appraisal
was completed and that there was about a $20,000 difference in the value of the properties. The DWR
property was valued more than the Nephi City property.
  Councilor Callaway thanked everyone that helped with the employee summer party.
  Councilor Memmott reported that the Central Water Conservancy District is still working on the water
study. They are planning on drilling a test well in North Canyon. He would have more of an update on
the study near the first of the year.
  Mayor Seely showed some photos of the track being laid in the Hive recreational facility.
  Councilor Worwood reported that interviews for new library workers would be held during the week.

MEETING ADJOURNED:

  Councilor Worwood moved to adjourn regular council meeting and to convene into executive session
to discuss the character and competence of an individual. Councilor Parady seconded the motion. The
motion passed on a unanimous vote.
Meeting adjourned at 8:17 p.m.
