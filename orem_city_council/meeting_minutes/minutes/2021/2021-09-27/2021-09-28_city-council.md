PERSONAL APPEARANCES

Stephanie Visnaw thanked the City Council for continuing to make changes to the State Street
Districts. She has heard from several residents that developers and commercial property owners
have taken some liberties and are not being held accountable for all city ordinances. She feels
larger developers are controlling the meetings and are given preferential treatment and
communications with residents are not being heard. During the moratorium it is an opportunity
for Orem City to hold discussions with residents and small business owners not just developers.

Debbie Lamb stated she lives in the Hillcrest area and has some concerns with the University
Mall and the Woodbury properties. Woodbury continues to purchase property in their
neighborhood that are zoned residential and use that area as a staging lot for construction with
large trucks and equipment. She questioned why Woodbury does not have to abide by the laws
of the city. The traffic in their neighborhood is due to individuals trying to get to the mall or
Costco to avoid State Street, once they realize they cannot get to those locations they speed out
of the neighborhood.

Ethan Harris stated he is a resident of the Hillcrest neighborhood. Their homes are under
constant threat from all sides and feels it is from the Woodbury Corporation. Residential lots are
being illegally fenced off and used for construction storage and for commercial purposes.
Woodbury has ignored the city code and city zoning in these locations and is destroying the
value of the homes. There are rumors of Woodbury opening roads through their neighborhood
and Costco is talking about expanding which would illegally encroach into this area. If Orem
wants to continue to be known as Family City USA help us and our homes against those that
illegally encroach in to residential areas for commercial expansion.

Annette Olsen stated she has lived next to the University Mall for 45 years. They have a traffic
problem in their neighborhood with cars looking for an easier way to get to the mall and Costco
and additional high density housing will only make it worse. They have watched their
neighborhood get crowed out by the commercial around them. She urges the elected officials to
look at their stewardship and see if they are serving the developers or the residents who elected
them. Please support Orem’s residents and preserve our neighborhood.

John Barrick inquired as to the site plan application process, Development Services office and

       PUBLIC HEARING / RESOLUTION – Crandall Orchard Open Space Conservation
       Easement Resolution of Support

Mr. Taylor stated the proposal is related to four separate properties owned by Tim and Natalie
Crandall and utilized as orchards. Together they are 12.76 acres in size and located at 66 N 800
East St., 825 E Center St., and 101 S 800 East St. Two other properties used as part of the same
orchard operation are located at 31 and 75 S 800 East St and consistent of 4.09 acres. Although
they are owned by separate parties at this time, they will also be considered as part of the
easement proposal should the owner's consent to it in the future. Darren Hawkins has requested
this action of the Council on behalf of Tim and Natalie Crandall (all Orem residents). In order to

complete the property owner's application to Utah State's LeRay McAllister Critical Lands
Conservation Fund for partial funding of the proposed easement. The fund requires property
owners to have a "letter of support from the local government where the project is located." A
resolution seems to be the most practical way for the City Council to state that support.

Mrs. Lauret inquired as to who retains the funds from the easement and how long does this stay

is lost forever.

Quinn Mecham thanked his fellow residents regarding the Hillcrest neighborhood. He has had
the opportunity to be the neighborhood historian and found that orchards are a fundamental part
of the history of Orem. Orchards are a monument to the past and a quality of life. Orem is a
place that is tied to the land and nature and thanks city staff for their time and energy. He
concluded by urging the Council to support this effort.

Corey Ramich grew up down the street from this farm. He feels this is noble of the Crandall’s to

Jim Crandall stated this is something their family had been looking at doing for a long time. He
has watched his family for generations struggle and even at one time loose this property, later

Darren Hawkins wanted to thank the city staff in helping with this idea and the Council in their

       ORDINANCE – City Code Updates – Item pulled from Consent Items

Mr. Peterson stated it was his understanding this change is to clean up outdated information in
the city code. His concern was a change to some of the GRAMA policies in relation to the
Development Review Committee (DRC) information being availability through that request
process.

Mrs. Schriever reviewed the changes to the city code being proposed and discussed the changes
in 2-26-9 by stating in 2018 Senator Bramble proposed and these changes were approved that
not just written and recorded information of a closed meeting are protected but also the
transcript. These changes are to update and codify the city code with the state code that was not
changed prior. A closed meeting is not to be discussed and is protected under GRAMA. Mr.
Peterson inquired if the DRC fit under that language of a closed meeting. Mrs. Schriever stated
the DRC is not a public body and would fall under the State code amendment of 2018.

Mr. Clark indicated the DRC meeting is not a meeting with the developer. It is a meeting of
several staff members that review the proposal and ensure their plans meet code. Comments are
made and issues and concerns are redlined then returned to the applicant.

Further discussion continued regarding the DRC and what is a public record and what constitutes
protected records.

Mayor Brunst moved by Ordinance to approve the City Code Updates as presented. Mrs.
Lauret seconded the motion. Those voting aye: Richard F. Brunst, Tom Macdonald, Terry
Peterson, Debby Lauret, David Spencer, and Brent Sumner. The motion passed.
