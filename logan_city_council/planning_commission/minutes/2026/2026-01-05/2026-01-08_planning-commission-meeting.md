PC 26-001 Copperwood Townhomes 2 Resubmittal [Design Review & Subdivision] Jake
Thompson/Jacob & Lynette Sanchez, Lisa & Chase Carlisle, authorized agent/owner(s) are
requesting a design review and subdivision permit to subdivide .92-acres into 11 lots and construct
10 new two story townhome units at approximately 532 West 200 South in the Mixed Residential Low
(MR-12) Zone; TIN 02-314-9001, 02-036-0014; -0005; -0004; -0002, in the Woodruff Neighborhood.

Staff: Aimee Egbert, Logan City Planner reviewed the request for a design review and subdivision
permit to subdivide approximately .92-acres into 12 total parcels, including one lot containing an
existing single-family dwelling, ten townhome lots, and one common parcel. The proposal includes
the construction of 10 new two-story townhome units and several boundary line adjustments with
adjacent parcels to achieve compliance with the Land Development Code (LDC).

The LDC requires multi-family infill developments resulting in three to twenty units to provide a 37-
foot minimum private access road consisting of two 10-foot travel lanes, one 8-foot on-street parking
lane, curb and gutter, and a 4-foot sidewalk along one side, while maintaining a 2-foot setback from
property lines and an 8-foot setback from existing structures. New residential buildings must also
meet 125% of the minimum zoning setbacks. As proposed, the project meets the LDC setback
requirements.

The LDC allows on-street parking to be removed for up to 30% of the private access road to
accommodate site pinch points. The proposed 240-foot private access permits up to 72 feet without
on-street parking. The proposed pinch point represents approximately 26% of the private access
toad length and complies with this standard.

As proposed, the pinch point section of travel lanes is at 24 feet in width including curb and gutter.

The LDC requires 25 feet in width. There is also an intersection in the private access road near the
pinch point that was not included in the pinch-point calculation. The Commission needed to determine


whether the intersection should be included in the 30% pinch-point calculation and if the pinch point
travel lanes can be 24-feet wide.

As proposed, the project shows a 7-foot parking setback on the west side of the six-unit townhome
building adjacent to parking stalls. The LDC requires an 8-foot setback.

LDC 17.43.080 allows the Commission to approve adjustments to minimum design standards and
setbacks up to 10% from the base standard when the Commission determines the adjustment is
justified.

A. Egbert explained to the Commission that, based on comments received regarding the boundary
line adjustment to the east for the private access road, the following conditions should be added to
ensure the adjacent property continues to meet the required setbacks after the boundary line
adjustment.

1) The property to the east of the private access shall maintain a 14-foot setback from the home to
the west property line, consisting of a minimum 12-foot driveway and 2-foot setback.

2) The property to the east of the private access shall maintain a 2-foot setback from the west property
line and the rear parking stalls.

A. Egbert also explained that the Environmental Department is requiring an additional condition of
approval stating that, prior to building permit approval, the applicant shall submit a revised plan
demonstrating compliance with all solid waste access, grading, and approach requirements.

Additionally, condition number two needs to be updated to state that the subdivision will expire in two
years, not one year.

S. Doutre asked if the proposed parking along the private access road is required. A. Egbert clarified
that the parking along the access road is additional parking and that all required parking is provided
within each townhome’s attached garage.

Proponent: Jake Thompson stated that the townhomes will be owner occupied.

Public: Joshua Gardynik stated that intersections should not be included in pinch-point calculations,
noting that developers could otherwise add intersections to bypass pinch-point requirements. Mr.
Gardynik also stated that the property would need to be filled approximately five feet to meet grade,
requiring a retaining wall along the south property line.

Adam Mackelpray, civil engineer on the project, stated that the site will tie into the retention pond in
the southwest corner and that soil removal, rather than infill, may be required. Mr. Mackelpray also
stated that intersections are inefficient and add cost, and that developers would not use intersections
to manipulate pinch point calculations. Mr. Mackelpray explained that the proposed pinch point
consists of two 10-foot travel lanes and two 2-foot rolled curb and gutter on each side, for a total
width of 24 feet, and requested approval of a one-foot reduction. Mr. Mackelpray also requested a
reduction of the west parking setback from the six-unit townhome building from 8-feet to 7-feet.

Commission: Darren Farar, Logan City Engineer, stated that the LDC does not specify curb and
gutter dimensions and that Engineering would allow a 2-foot rolled curb and gutter.

S. Doutre stated that the intersection should not be included in the 30% pinch point calculation, as
intersections function opposite of a pinch point. The Commissioners agreed.


The Commission discussed the pinch point and determined that it should have a minimum width of
24 feet, consisting of two 10-foot travel lanes and two 2-foot curb and gutter sections.

Condition #5 was modified to allow a 7-foot setback.

The Commission added a new condition #6 requiring the property to the east maintain a 14-foot
setback from the existing home to the west property line and a minimum two-foot setback from the
driveway and existing rear parking stalls.

The Commission added a new condition number 8 under Environmental, as described by staff, and
amended condition #2 to reflect a two-year subdivision expiration period.
