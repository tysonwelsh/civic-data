2022. Meeting was a mobile tour of various multi-family projects.

   1. Residential Developments. The commission will tour residential developments in
      Ogden and surrounding areas to observe residential development standards.

       Commission toured various multi-family projects in Ogden City, West Haven, Clearfield
       and Layton and noted lack of amenities in some communities while others appeared to
       be more inviting for both residents and guests. They noted entryway design and
       locations and felt many had inadequate porches. They also felt balcony space should be
       designed to be usable for seating and not a space for bicycle storage. The location and
       placement of mailboxes is a concern when at entryways but appeared to work well when
       imbedded into the building. Commission noted the contrast of colors as well as building
       materials might be a factor contributing to the presentation of these structures to the
       surrounding neighborhood. One project was identified as not having enough color, and
       while there is lots of brick, the appearance of the buildings is undesirable. Units also are
       not easily identifiable.

       Parking also was discussed, indicating while the Ordinance allows reduced parking when
       near a public park, Commission felt there should be a requirement for the developer to
       contribute to the improvement of these parks. The concern of mixed-use projects also
       was discussed as the current market for retail space is difficult. Commission viewed
       some projects which was developed under the previous commercial standards, and
       noted electrical meters are visible, there is too much parking and no on-site amenities,
       with the project appearing to be unlivable. Patio and deck spaces are too small and
       appear to be used as storage. Other factors include width of sidewalks, mix of building
       materials, limited courtyards and stucco extending to the ground

       It was felt some cities lack regulations with units being spaced too close together, too
       many building materials, trash containers and utilities visible, small access driveways,
       and the size of garages and back-up space being too small to accommodate vehicles.
   Other items noted included drainage pipes visible on roof, open entryways, visible air
   conditioning units, often located on balcony space, lack of entry features with some
   having front doors facing interior open space or inside garage space. Commission felt it
   important that building materials are limited, as some have up to five different materials.
   Individual solid vinyl fencing between units creates a harsh appearance yet provides
   privacy and security. Some projects had a mix of single-family, twin homes and
   townhomes, creating a better community appearance. Connectivity to the outlying
   community as well as between units and amenities of the project is also desirable.

2. Motel conversions to multiple family. The commission will visit motels considered
   for conversion to multiple family development.

   Commission visited the vacant Day’s Inn site and discussed issues relating to the
   conversion of hotel/motels into multi-family housing. They noted there is a lack of space
   for the creation of amenities and that parking for these is typically one per unit. They also
   noted parking is in front of the building which is not allowed for mufti-family uses. While
   the number of motel units is unknown for this project, the petition to rezone the Motel 6
   property indicated there were 71 units and the applicant desired to create 71 senior
   housing studio apartments. It was noted the Zoning Ordinance limits multi-family to 64
   units in the C-3 zone and 36 units in the existing C-2 zone. It was noted while there may
   be a need for senior housing, the conversion of hotels/motels to long-term dwelling units
   often creates undesirable space and these become housing nightmares. Concern was
   expressed that these buildings need to have updated appearance, and not look like a
   cheap motel with lipstick. They noted there are typically limited facilities for food
   preparation and felt there is a need for kitchens to be created containing a full-size
   kitchen sink, range and refrigerator. Commission also felt on-site management is
   desirable, which then would meet the definition of a single-room occupancy, which are no
   longer allowed, but have limited standards. It also was suggested building design should
   accommodate interior entryways rather than being loaded from parking spaces as this
   type of units create policing issues. They further discussed both building code issues
   noting that long-term rentals have different standards than night-to-night tenants,
   suggesting the length of leases also might be regulated. It was also noted while there is
   an indoor pool, the construction of is covering does not meet current standards.
   Construction materials and design was also discussed. Commissioners felt it is
   important to have a mix of types of units, noting that a single studio apartment for each
   of the existing motel units is not desirable and would typically not meet density
   regulations of the current zone. Commission felt some standards need to be adopted in
   order to regulate the conversion of hotels and motels into housing units. It was
   suggested a pending ordinance might be created which would prohibit any applications
   for these to become vested while no standards or regulations exist. Commission asked
   how fast this could occur and when an Ordinance revision would become available for
   their review. Mr. Brierley indicated language could be drafted and a pending ordinance
   published within a week, and noted these are limited to a six-month time period, but could
   be shorter. Discussion continued relating to standards the Commission felt is
       appropriate for these, including a mix of number of bedrooms, definition of a kitchen and
       consideration of whether on-site management is necessary. Commission felt amenity
       packages are important and should include open space standards similar to those
       required for other multi-family projects in commercial zones. Building materials,
       architectural design and screening of utilities also are important considerations to be
       consistent with other multi-family regulations.
