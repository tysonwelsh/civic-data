Councilmember McDougal asked for more information about the location and design of the
towers. In response, Mayor Staggs said that all of this information was included in the packet
which was provided to each of the Councilmembers.

Mayor Staggs said the cost for this broadband option is $790,000 compared to a previously bid
project which was north of $1.3 million. The IT department has vetted this project and feels
comfortable with it.

Mr. Calvert said that TuVas did not indicate where the towers would be located but said that
some of the towers would be attached to City buildings. In the contract, it states that TuVas
must obtain a conditional use permit to build the towers and this would go through the Planning
Commission.

Mayor Staggs pointed out that the City already has 10 towers and additional towers would only
be built if they were to expand their services.

Mr. Calvert said that TuVas would provide 10 gigabytes of internet for $2,800 per month. This is
a better speed for a lower price than the City is currently paying. Mr. Calvert said this price will
be held for 10 years through the contract.

Councilmember McDougal asked about the budget for this project. Mr. Hicks said the budget
was $1.5 million. Councilmember McDougal asked about supply chain and increased costs due
to the supply chain. Mr. Calvert said that supply chain is always an issue, however, any existing
City contract has a fixed amount. Mr. Calvert said that TuVas has agreed to complete this
project by June 30 which means there is not a supply chain issue in this market.

Councilmember Pierucci asked if there is a concern with getting UDOT permits. Mr. Calvert
said there is no fiber in UDOT right-of-ways so there will not be an issue.

Councilmember McDougal asked about the reliability of TuVas. Mr. Calvert said this is the first
city project they have done but they have done other projects in rural areas. Mr. Hicks said the
technical equipment that will be used by TuVas is supported by IT and this equipment is used
commonly and is compatible and reputable.

Mr. Calvert said the City currently uses the same technology that TuVas will install between the
Old Dome and the rodeo grounds. This is not new technology.

Councilmember Buroker recommends that the Council go to TuVas’s website which has a lack
of information and no recommendations.

Councilmember McCay asked where the funds come from and if the money had to be used for
a fiber project. Mr. Hicks said they have budgeted ARPA funds for the broadband project.
ARPA money is currently budgeted for broadband, secondary water, and the Greenwell.

Councilmember Buroker asked what happens if ARPA funds are not used for the fiber project.
Mayor Staggs said that initially ARPA money only allowed for broadband, sewer or water
infrastructure. However, the City learned that for cities receiving less than $10 million, they
preferred that it was categorized as “all revenue replacement.” This opened it up to be able to
use ARPA funds for other things. Mayor Staggs said that the City had many water projects and
the broadband project so they decided to allocate ARPA funds to these projects.

          South Valley CTAA Presentation

Tyson Lybbert, Vice President Sales at Visit Salt Lake, said this legislation for the Convention
Tourism Assessment Area was passed last year and allowed for the formation of special
assessment districts where hoteliers can levy a fee that is passed onto the guest. The money
collected would be used to boost hotel occupancy and average daily rates. To get to this stage,
they had to go to the 77 hotels and get their approval. Only 5 hotels have voiced dissent. The
projected increase in hotel room revenue will be $3.6 million.

Scott George, Vice President of Woodbury Corporation, which owns 11 hotels in Salt Lake
County said he has been a part of many tourism initiatives. He believes there is a need to offset
the costs of groups that come into the valley. He said the CTAA is a great opportunity for this
community.

Clay Partain, Executive Director of the Sport Salt Lake, division under Visit Salt Lake, said he
has seen an increase in sports tourism. He works with various groups to bring more
tournaments and events to Salt Lake.

Jay Francis, South Valley Chamber, said the South Valley Chamber works on promoting
tourism and was able to have a big economic impact in 2022 by hosting and supporting events
in the south valley.

Councilmember Pierucci said he is pro-business and appreciates the value that this industry
provides to the community. He asked if Riverton’s vote would be meaningless because there
are no hotels in Riverton and whether the City could participate in the future. Mr. Lybbert said
the opt-in is for five years with a yearly renewal each year after. He would like to keep the south
valley together and said part of the funds could be used to attract a hotel to Riverton.

Councilmember Buroker asked clarifying questions about the CTAA.

Public Hearing(s)/Action Items

          Ordinance No. 23-08 – Commercial Uses Amendment, Amending Section 18.65,
          18.70, 18.75, 18.80, and 18.100, Removing Commercial Uses Language in
          Deference to the Adopted Table of Commercial Uses, Amendment Proposed by
          Riverton City

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained this was a public hearing for a
proposed Code Text Amendment to adjust Riverton City’s Commercial Uses in Title 18 – Land
Development Code. This proposal does not make substantial changes to current policy or
practice. The goal of this proposal is to make it easier for customers, city officials, and city staff
to identify land uses in the city’s commercial zones. To achieve this goal staff identified these
objectives:

          1- Place all commercial zones in one Table of Uses, and
          2- Eliminate references to commercial uses in each commercial zone description
          (18.65, 18.70, 18.75, 18.80, 18.85, and 18.100).

Mr. Lethbridge said that at the Planning Commission meeting on December 8, 2022,
commissioners recommended that City Council consolidate two table of uses by adding unique
uses from the PPC Table of Uses to the existing “Table of Commercial Uses”. City Council
approved this change on January 17, 2023. Commercial zones are not consistently described,
and some discrepancies have been found between zone descriptions and the Table of
Commercial Uses. Staff is proposing to eliminate references to commercial uses in each
commercial zone description and thus eliminate any discrepancies in the code by deferring to
the updated Table of Commercial Uses. This change would affect commercial zone descriptions
18.65, 18.70, 18.80, 18.85, and 18.100 by removing references to approved, conditional, and
prohibited uses. Thus, the Table of Commercial Uses would become the controlling legal
authority for all uses in commercial zones. This change is organizational and does not alter the
uses allowed or prohibited in each zone.
