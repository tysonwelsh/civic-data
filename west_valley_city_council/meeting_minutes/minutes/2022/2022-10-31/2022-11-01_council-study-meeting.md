       COMMUNICATIONS
       A.  PARKING ORDINANCE
           Eric Bunderson, City Attorney, provided two ordinances that would address on-
           street parking. He noted that this was requested by the Council. He stated that the
           last time this was discussed, the Council talked about a fine structure, motor homes,
           and commercial vehicles. Eric indicated that staff has written an affirmative defense
           into the ordinance for motorhomes which means that code enforcement officers and
           parking enforcement officers will have to check registration before writing the
           ticket and the person who gets the ticket can use this defense to get the ticket
           dismissed. He indicated that this should create a balance to the needs of the people
           who are actually living and recreating in West Valley City versus other problems
           that were discussed last time.

              Eric stated that staff is proposing to make the prohibitions for parking on the
              driveway the same as parking on the street. Eric provided pictures that show heavy
              commercial vehicles parking on streets that staff is finding difficult to ticket and
              hold up. He indicated that there is a prohibition on vehicles that are over a certain
              length and over a certain gross vehicle weight but, if the Council desires, staff can
              craft an ordinance that prohibit these types of heavy vehicles entirely based on gross
              weight. He noted that currently, the ordinance prohibits the gross weight of a
              vehicle above 18,000 pounds on the street and above 12,000 pounds on driveways.
              Eric explained how the gross vehicle weight rating can be looked up online. He
              stated that the way this proposed ordinance is written, personal heavy vehicles are
              still allowed so if a resident wanted to buy a big truck or convert a semi into an RV
              and park it on their driveway, they could. He noted that government vehicles, such
              as school buses and fire trucks, are also excluded.

       Mayor Lang asked why the ordinance would remove the 24-48 hour parking
       allowance. Wayne stated that non-residents park on City streets and live out of
       their motorhomes. He noted that these individuals have learned that they are able
       to move their motorhomes/RV’s a block and get away with it without receiving a
       citation. Mayor Lang asked if the appeal process is straight-forward. Eric replied
       yes. Councilmember Harmon asked how RV’s would be addressed in a rental
       situation. Councilmember Nordfelt stated that the registration would still likely be
       tied to the address but if not, the resident could provide proof of rental status and
       get the ticket dismissed. Councilmember Harmon expressed concern about heavy
       duty pickups. Councilmember Whetstone agreed and asked if a truck with a rack
       would be a concern. Eric replied that a F650-F750 with a large rack would likely
       not be allowed. Councilmember Christensen asked about Amazon delivery vans.
       Eric replied that these would likely be well within regulations and Mayor Lang
       noted that these vehicles would not likely be residential issues as they don’t
       typically stay with the employee. Mayor Lang stated that she has received
       complaints about F-150’s with a trailer (containing lawn mowers) parked on
       streets receiving tickets while lawns are being mowed. She asked how the weight
       of the trailer is determined. Eric replied that the trailer weight rating would have
       that information online or on the trailer itself. Councilmember Huynh stated that
       he believes concerns should be reported by residents.

       Mayor Lang stated that she is concerned about the section of code that requires
       residents to be actively loading and unloading RV’s on streets. She indicated that
       there may be multiple reasons why a resident would have their RV on the street
       including construction projects. Councilmember Christensen stated that he has
       had work done on his property and was able to use his neighbors parking and
       driveway. Councilmember Nordfelt stated that large trailers and RV’s on street
       are a safety concern for children. Mayor Lang requested that the ordinance state
       that residents can appeal for parking tickets of an RV/motorhome if a special
       circumstance applies (such as a construction). Councilmember Harmon agreed.

       Councilmember Christensen suggested that the fine structure be raised to reflect
       first offence to be equal to handicap violation’s. Mayor Lang and a majority of
       Council agreed the initial fine listed in code was sufficient.
