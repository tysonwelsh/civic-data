                                           PUBLIC COMMENTS
Benjamin resident Clark Caras noted the public comments made during the previous Commission meeting on June 2,
2015 regarding open pit mining in the West Mountain area affecting traffic. He stated the gravel pit trucks are still
driving at dangerous rates and asked what the next step in the correction process would be. Though the Utah County
Sheriff’s Office has increased their presence to monitor speeding, Mr. Caras believes the truck drivers are communicating
with each other by radio to avoid key areas. He has witnessed five incidents since the last meeting where trucks have
run through stop signs in the community. Mr. Caras questioned why the citizens were not given a chance to reject this
mining operation which negatively impacts youth recreation and farming; economic development is great unless it is
suddenly put upon you.

Jacky Caras has lived in Benjamin for 56 years, and can no longer open the West-facing windows of her home because
of noise from the gravel trucks. The screens in her brand new windows are being knocked from their frames as the trucks
rattle down the street. The gravel companies have the right to make a living, but it has caused a huge obstacle for the
residents.

Benjamin resident Collette Russell commented on the mining operation traffic during the last Commission meeting on
June 2, 2015, and she plans to continue attending the weekly sessions until the situation is resolved. Ms. Russell
explained how Benjamin is designed as a farming community, producing beef and vegetables for local grocery stores.
Her son was unable to get onto the road with his tractor for ten minutes while waiting for traffic. The semi-trucks are
zig-zagging on the roads, and Ms. Russell questions whether they have permission to be there. She is also concerned
that Native American artifacts and other geological and historical sites are being disturbed. Ms. Russell reiterated the
citizens should have had a voice in the location of the gravel pit, as rural communities are important. Commissioner
Graves noted that he is very familiar with Benjamin and its history, as he is related to the Stewart family by marriage.

Leslie Urmston lives on 7300 South in Benjamin. She thanked the Board for beginning their meeting with a prayer and
the Pledge of Allegiance. Ms. Urmston’s family relocated to Benjamin for the quiet farming community. While Ms.
Urmston loves America and appreciates enterprise, she questions whether this location was the only viable option for
a gravel pit. Her daughter has been riding horses on the main road since five years old, and it is much more dangerous
with these big trucks. Ms. Urmston asked if she could have voted to say she does not want trucks which stink of asphalt
and diesel coming down the road, and pointed out that fifty miles per hour is too fast for huge semi-trucks pulling trailers.

Orem resident Merlin Weekes came forward to address the Provo/Orem Bus Rapid Transit project. He collected more
than 800 signatures in opposition to the recently passed County Resolution for Subordinated Transportation Sales Tax
Revenue Bonds (April 21, 2015). Utah County is unnecessarily spending money and will devastate the businesses of
merchants on University Avenue. Mr. Weekes stated the petitioners could have collected enough signatures for the
referendum on the resolution had they been given three more days. He hopes the Board will take the time to think about
the long-term effects of this resolution, as there is a there is a lot of pressure to involve federal money from a government
steeped in debt. Mr. Weekes encouraged minimization and setting a conservative example, suggesting the county wait
ten or fifteen years until mass transit is truly needed if it even gets to that point before making commitments.

Cecylia Worthington lives on a small side road in Benjamin with a blind spot which can be dangerous for even two
passenger vehicles. Her sons raise show animals, and she would not let her 14-year-old to walk down the street recently.
That same day, Ms. Worthington saw a gravel truck barrel through the blind spot right where her son would have been
walking had she allowed it. She confirmed the truck operators are avoiding Sheriff patrols and continue to run stop signs,
posing a great danger to children on their bicycles and teen-aged drivers with less experience on the road. Ms.
Worthington is very worried that someone is going to get hurt.

Commissioner Lee mentioned Benjamin residents have been contacting him, and he has spoken with the Utah
Department of Transportation (UDOT) about facilitating a public meeting to address this issue. Commissioners Graves
and Ellertson agreed such a meeting would be welcome, and Commissioner Graves credited the Public Works
Department and the Sheriff’s Office for their assistance in the matter. Someone asked from the audience whether the
gravel pit operators would be present at this meeting, and the commissioners indicated they would definitely be invited.
The Board discussed with members of the audience potential places for a meeting.

Utah County Clerk/Auditor Bryan E. Thompson commented on County Resolution 2015-57 for the issuance of
Subordinated Transportation Sales Tax Revenue Bonds. A petition process was started, and came to a conclusion on
Friday, June 5, 2015 at 5:00 P.M. Four hundred petition packets were submitted with a total of 14,833 valid signatures.
For a successful referendum, 17,885 signatures were needed for the resolution to be placed on the November 2016
General Election. As a result of these numbers, the issue will not be referred to the ballot.
