1. Presentation of the Summit County Active Transportation Plan- Caroline 
Rodriguez, Regional Transportation Planning Director      
 
Director Putt explained this is a presentation by Transportation Director, Caroline 
Rodriguez.  She will explain what is happening in the County.  Ms. Rodriguez 
acknowledged and thanked those who have helped with the project.  She said the 
purpose of this visit is to familiarize the Commission with an overview of the planning 
processes that are utilized and give an update on upcoming projects.   
 
Ms. Rodriguez said this plan is intended to act as a jumping off point for the active 
transportation strategy.  This is the first time the County has created such a plan.  It will 
be reviewed and adopted by the County Council and then handed to the Utah

Department of Transportation.  This will keep UDOT in the loop of what Summit County 
is doing.  Additionally, Staff can refer to the plan.  A priority project list will be created.   
 
Ms. Rodriguez said the plan can be revisited as the need arises and the priorities can be 
adjusted as warranted.  The project team and steering committee developed guiding 
principles.  An analysis was conducted of existing plans.  Base maps were created of 
community resources.  Highlighted were the gaps, constraints, opportunity, and needs 
of the different communities.     
 
Summit County has 58 miles of different bike facilities, different than recreation.  Most 
of those are within 50 miles of Park City boundaries.  Heavy winter snow poses a 
challenge to making those facilities usable year-round.   
 
Ms. Rodriguez said that Summit County’s mode share program (defined as transit, 
biking, or walking) identifies these activities as lower than both the State and National 
averages.  The 2012 Utah Travel Survey had the same findings.  The majority of people 
are not commuting by bike in Summit County.  This data indicates there is great 
potential for change, depending on improved conditions for both biking and walking.   
 
Collision data showed there were approximately 9,000 collisions between January 2010 
and December 2017.  A little over 1% of the collisions involved a pedestrians or a bike.  
The time of most pedestrian collisions was at 2:00 p.m. and between 6:00-8:00 p.m.  
Bike collisions peaked between 2:00-4:00 p.m.  It tapered off at 8:00 p.m.  Around 93% 
of these accidents reported injuries.  There were two fatalities.  Both fatalities were a 
pedestrian/vehicle interaction.  Both were on Highway 224, within ¼ of a mile of each 
other.   
 
Ms. Rodriguez said the steering committee was made up of people from various 
agencies.  There were interviews of stakeholders and two public surveys were taken.

Neighborhood charrettes were conducted throughout Summit County.  As a result of 
this input, the committee came up with the proposed facilities.    
 
As a result of this work, 50 opportunities throughout Summit County were identified.

These can be categorized into 7 facility types.  There were some common themes

FINDINGS OF FACT 
1. The applicant is requesting an amendment to the Snyderville Basin 
Development Code to create a Neighborhood Mixed Use zoning district (NUM-I). 
2. The NMU-I zone is a concept that was initially created and presented to the 
Planning Commission by Staff in July of 2017. 
3. Because the process was not moving forward (due to other Staff priorities and 
responsibilities), the applicant took the NMU-I draft language, modified it, and 
applied to amend the Development Code.   
4. The Commission reviewed the proposal as a standalone document at work 
sessions on April 23, 2019 and June 11, 2019.   
5. The Planning Commission contemplated the creation and adoption of zoning 
tools to guide future development in these areas.  
6. The General Plan identifies several current and future mixed-use neighborhoods 
in the Snyderville Basin.   
7. The General Plan contemplates the creation and adoption of zoning tools to 
guide future development in these areas.  
 
C
ONCLUSIONS OF LAW 
1. The amendment is consistent with the goals, objectives, and policies of the 
General Plan.   
2. The amendment will not permit the use of land that is not consistent with the 
uses of the properties nearby.    
3. The amendment will not permit suitability of the properties affected by the 
proposed amendment for the uses to which they have been restricted. 
4. The amendment will not permit the removal of the then existing restriction 
which will unduly affect nearby property. 
5. The amendment will not grant special favors or circumstances solely for one 
property owner or developer. 
6. The amendment will promote the public health, safety and welfare, better than 
the existing regulations for which the amendment is intended to change.

engineering codes.  The applicant’s plan has a steep driveway.  A slope analysis shows 
that the driveway would be in a bowl-type area.  It is somewhat hidden from the 
roadway.  Planner Lewis said the end result is that there isn’t a good way to build on the
