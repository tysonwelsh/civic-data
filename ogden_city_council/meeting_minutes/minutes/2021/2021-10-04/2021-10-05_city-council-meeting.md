Water Conservation Proposal
           City Administration is proposing a program to incentivize property owners to remove turf and install water efficient
landscaping in park strips. Administrative staff are currently in the process of developing this program, and the purpose of this
brief is to provide a status update.
           Planner Corbridge used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to smmnarize Administration's proposed water conservation
program. The purpose of the program is to promote water-efficient landscaping and assure sensible use of the city's water
resources, enhance the visual enviromnent, allow for increase in population, while delaying the need for major infrastructure
improvements within Ogden City, and meet state water conservation goals. The program has developed responses to current State-
wide drought conditions as well as increased water waste. Utah's regional conservation goal is a 16 percent reduction by 2030
with 2015 usage as the baseline by which reductions will be measured. Ms. Corbridge discussed present water conditions, noting
the City's main water supply, Pineview Reservoir is cunently at 16 percent of capacity as of October 4, 2021. The City's code
already allows for water-efficient landscaping and some residents and businesses are already implementing new landscape plans.
However, the City's Code does not require water-efficient landscaping. She presented photos of several existing park strips
tl1roughout the City, after which she presented an illustration used as an example of an option for adjusting landscaping at a
single-family home on a quarter-acre lot, which could decrease the amount of water used by 130,000 gallons per year. Factors for
Council consideration include additional code modifications aimed at creating water efficient park strips; it is necessa1y to educate

Zone Text Amendment- Serving Alcohol on Sidewalks
          CuiTent zoning regulations allow restaurants to establish outdoor dining in certain parts of the sidewalk. However,
alcohol may only be served outdoors when the dining area is attached to the restaurant. In some cases, outdoor dining areas are not
attached to restaurants, and you have to cross the sidewalk to get to the table. Per Ogden zoning code, alcohol may not be served
to these detached dining areas. The petitioner is requesting to remove restrictive language and allow alcohol to be served in
detached outdoor dining settings.
          Deputy Planning Manager Brierley used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the petition; the City's
ordinance regarding serving alcohol on City streets reads as follows: "Access to the dining area directly from the sidewalk or other
portions of the public right of way is prohibited when alcohol beverages are to be served to patrons of the establishment within the
approved sidewalk use area. Under such circumstances, the permittee must control egress and ingress to the sidewalk use area
directly through the interior of the building." The State of Utah has rules that apply to all types of restaurants and the rules indicate
that a restaurant may have a patio that is either contiguous to the building or one that requires crossing public property to enter,
with the approval of the local government authority (city). He then noted the vision of the General Plan is to encourage food and
drinking establishments to provide outdoor seating on public right of ways. Responsive to the petition, the Planning Commission
and staff recommend that the City Council amend the Ogden City Code to allow restaurants to serve alcohol in sidewalk dining
areas not directly connected to the building.
          The Council briefly discussed the recommendation and offered support for including an item on a future meeting agenda
to act on the proposed text amendment.

          Proposed Honorary Street Name Ordinance
                     Council member Choberka has submitted a Council Initiative form for the development and consideration of an honoraty
          street name ordinance. The initiative came in response to the discussions from a petition to assign an honorary street name to a
          portion of 2nd Street. The initiative was submitted with the direction that an ordinance be drafted that would look at how and who
          is able to make requests for honorary street names and to work with the Diversity Commission to review and revise the ordinance
          to create equity and inclusion in the core of the process. Council staff has been working with Council member Choberka and
          members of the Diversity Cmmnission, in conjunction with legal staff, to develop a draft proposal aimed at meeting the direction
          given from the Council initiative. The purpose of the work session is to review the draft ordinance and to allow the Council to
          discuss the proposal, recommend changes, or provide any other direction for the continued development of the ordinance. The
          Council's initiative form was developed in 2018 as a way Council members could fonnally submit ideas and proposals. Once
          submitted, the proposal is to be reviewed by Council staff and discussed at a Council work session. Once reviewed by the Council,
          Council leadership is to provide direction to staff to proceed with further development of the policy or request a recmmnendation
          from the Planning Commission or other entity as desired by the Council.
                     Council Deputy Director Symes used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to smmnarize the Council initiative request
          and the recommendation from the Diversity Commission. The current City ordinance governing regular and honorary street name
          changes indicates such a name change can be initiated by a petition from a property owner so long as their property is adjacent to
          the street for which they are requesting the name change, or as a result of a recommendation from the City Engineer. The Planning
          Commission must review the petition or City Engineer recommendation and provide a recommendation to the City Council. The
          City Council holds a public hearing before voting on the proposed name change. The recmmnendation from the City Engineer is
          required to eliminate duplication, avoid confusion for emergency services, or for any other good cause; honorary names should
          not be duplications and should not cause confusion for emergency services. If the name change is done by petition, the petitioner
