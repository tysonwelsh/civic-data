¢ Discussion — 2016 Salaries for Elected Officials — Executive Buttars commented that
the proposed budget is shown with and without a salary adjustment for elected officials.
Buttars indicated that elected officials have pointed out that other county employees
know what their wage will be when hired, but qualify for increases. Buttars feels there
needs to be a long-term plan for how this is going to be addressed in the future.

Council member White reiterated the view that elected officials’ salaries should only be
reviewed for adjustment in the year prior to the election for that position. Attorney Swink
said that any time an individual is hired in any job, they may reasonably expect to have a
raise and elected officials should not be any different. Swink also remarked that there

are offices that have a salary compression problem — some employees are close to the
department head’s salary. Council member Erickson agreed it is reasonable for elected
officials to expect to be considered for salary adjustments each budget cycle.

Chairman Robison asked if there are any department heads making more than elected
officials. Director Wood responded there are three making more and three making less,
but are close to elected officials’ salaries. Wood added the proposed elected officials’
salary adjustments are not huge - $13,531.00.

Robison agreed with Council member White concerning elected officials’ salary
adjustments. Vice Chair Merrill mentioned that three elected officials have six-year
terms this election cycle and should be considered at the same time as those with four-
year terms. Robison agreed.

Chairman Robison noted that Cache County does not have a turnover problem with
elected officials and observed that Cache County really can't be compared to
Washington County, as has been alluded to in previous discussions, because Cache
has a lot of agricultural land, but Washington County has multiple second residences
that are taxed at 100%. Robison feels elected officials have had excellent salary
increases, averaging $2,000.00 a year, over the fifteen years she has been on the
Council.

Vice Chair Merrill said the Council would love to give raises and to not raise taxes and to
provide needed services, etc., and there is only so much money and that is the
challenge. Merrill said that the tax notice he saw from Utah County also lists the
percentages of the property tax that the different entities are receiving. For instance, it
listed the Nebo School District as receiving 74% and the county was somewhere around
6%. Merrill wonders if Cache County can do something similar on its tax notices.

Executive Buttars will come up with numbers with and without elected officials’ salary
increases when he presents the proposed budget at the next Council meeting for the
Council to consider when making salary decisions.

Dear Legislators: Nov. 19, 2015

Road construction and maintenance needs in Cache County are at least as great as in other locations in
Utah. In particular, as costs increase, it is very difficult for small communities to adequately fund their
transportation infrastructure needs. Therefore, many local leaders were enthusiastic about the
provisions of 2015’s HB 362 which will increase revenues from the gas tax and also the general concept
of a local option transportation sales tax.

During the last election cycle, the Cache County Council decided not to put the local option sales tax on
the ballot and we supported the Council in that decision. Although the additional funding is badly
needed, we could not support the provision of the bill that required that 40% of the revenue to be
allocated to our local transit district. The Cache Valley Transit District is an excellent organization that
serves many of our communities and provides an important transportation option to our residents. But
we believe that the needs of the Transit District are not as great as the needs for improved roads. As
Cache County continues to grow at a rapid rate, we have pressing needs to build, improve, and maintain
our county roads, but the funding base is grossly inadequate. What we need is a funding source that
can be used for those critical needs.

We are aware that Representative Jack Draxler is proposing legislation that will amend HB 362 to give
counties of the third class greater flexibility in allocating the transportation sales tax. Specifically, the
change would allow County Councils or Commissions the authority to allocate between 10% and 40% of
the revenue generated to transit, with the remainder going to cities and to the county.

We strongly support Representative Draxler’s proposal because it will allow the revenues to be used to
meet our most critical transportation needs. Further, we trust our Cache County Council to make a
prudent decision on the specific allocation of the transportation sales tax funds.

We ask for your support in amending HB 362 during the coming legislative session.

2016 Executive Budget Proposal — Financial Highlights

General Property Tax — Assumes an increase of $452,510 or 4.19% over the certified
tax rate and is based on the Schedule of Forecasted Property Taxes for 2016
included in the proposed budget. The proposed tax rate is 0.001828. A primary
residential home owner with a value of $201,181 is estimated to have an increase of
$8.74 per year or $0.73 per month.

County Option Sales Tax — Assumes an increase of $195,758 or 4.3% in Sale Tax
based on the average collections for the last five years and the projected collections
for 2015.

Personal Property Tax — Assumes an increase of $5,000 or 0.6% and is based on 2015
projected collections.

Condition of Probation — Based on a projected revenue increase of $90,000 or 21.4%
from the Department of Corrections.

Contract with the Utah Department of Corrections — Increases by $725,500 or 68.1%
and assumes an average of 75 inmates per day: 50 inmates in a rehabilitation
program reimbursed at $68.43 per inmate per day and 25 inmates not in
rehabilitation program at $59.47 per inmate per day.

Contract on Federal Inmates ~ Increases by 25,000 or 3.9% and assumes an average
of 34 inmates per day.

County Assessing & Collecting —- Continues the current tax rate with new growth
revenue of 7.6% and is based on the Schedule of Forecasted Property Taxes for
2016.

Municipal Services Property Tax — Implements a new tax on real property in the
unincorporated area of the County to help offset the cost of municipal-type services
already provided. The impact of the tax on the average primary residence of
$248,992 is $33.55 per year or $2.80 per month.

Local (Unincorporated Area of the County) Sales Tax — Estimates an increase of
$40,606 or 5.0% based on the average collections for the last five years and the
projected collections for 2015.
