           1. 2      Mayor' s Report


         Mayor Kristie Overson reported on the recent Trick or Treat event held at Centennial Plaza.
She said there were far more attendees than had been anticipated and she was anxious to hear
what the Parks and Recreation Committee decided to do in the future.


The Arts Council recently hosted the Taylorsville/ SLCC Symphony in presenting a Halloween
concert. There was also an art show held at the Centennial Room in the MidValley Performing
Arts Center.


The Taylorsville Exchange Club recently observed the National Exchange Day of Service by
providing underwear, socks and hats to both Fremont Elementary and Taylorsville Elementary
Schools. She enjoyed joining the Exchange Club as they delivered these necessities to the
schools. Club members had also donated their time to The Haunted Woods in Murray during the
previous week. The Murray Exchange Club would reciprocate at a future Taylorsville event.

The      previous     week   was   also   Red   Ribbon   week,   so she and Chief Cottam had visited

The city had already run out of the free leaf bags that were distributed each year to city residents.

Speaking of parks, they had now been winterized for the season which meant that park restrooms

Chief Cottam invited Officer Michael Fullwood to step forward and be sworn. The oath of office
was administered by the city recorder, after which Officer Fullwood was congratulated by city
administration and the City Council.

         The Chief explained that Officers Teynor and Lutz were members of the National Guard
and had recently presented the City with a Patriotic Employer award in appreciation of their
constant support.




Chief Cottam then asked Donny Gasu and Brandy Stephens to join him at the podium while he
explained that Sherry Durfee had been named as the Police Employee of the Month for July
2023. He explained that TVPD had the best civilian staff in the state and Ms. Durfee was an
integral part of that staff. She had a great deal of experience which made Mr. Gasu' s and Ms.
Stephens' jobs easier.


C. J. Moore had been named the Police Employee of the Month for June 2023. Officer Moore and
K-9 Turbo stepped forward to be recognized for winning several awards, specifically the top
Narcotics Detection Team in Utah. Chief Cottam stated that being a K-9 handler was a 24/ 7 job
and that Officer Moore was phenomenal at it.


     Chief Cottam then presented his report to the Council, focusing on data from July through
September. He provided statistical data on criminal activity in each of the five- council districts,
while providing similar data from 2021 and 2022 as a comparison. He briefly reviewed maps
which showed the location of traffic accidents that had occurred during the 3rd quarter of 2023
and also showed the locations where traffic citations had been issued. A bar chart was used to
illustrate the number of citations issued as opposed to warnings, and Chief Cottam also provided
data regarding the types of citations issued. He then gave an overview of the activity level of the
police department' s civilian staff regarding the processing of GRAMA requests, expungements,
case screenings for district and justice courts and victim advocate services. Finally, Chief Cottam
provided information regarding officer response times to various call types.

The Chief explained that when speaking with school kids, he suggested they wave at police
officers when they saw them. He reminded everyone that the officers are human beings who
appreciated a greeting, were entitled to take a periodic break as call volume allowed and could
sometimes    be found doing paperwork in their patrol vehicles which served as their" offices." He
also explained that if an officer activated his/her lights and sirens to get through an intersection,
it was often because the call they were on didn' t warrant lights and sirens for the whole drive but
it was still important for the officer to arrive as soon as possible. In some instances it was best
not to arrive on certain situations with a siren   blaring, but again— it was important to get to many
scenes as quickly as they could.

5.       PLANNING MATTERS


         5. 1        Discussion of" Introduction" Chapter of General Plan— Mark McGrath


 e Long-Range Planner Mark McGrath explained that the population of Salt Lake County was
expected to increase by almost 500,000 people by the year 2060. Utah County was expected to
increase even more. Therefore, it was critical to have an updated, well-thought-out general plan
to guide land use in Taylorsville. He explained that the General Plan would be separated into
nine separate chapters, with Chapter 8 having been adopted in 2022. Each chapter would
summarize existing conditions, followed by the objectives, strategies and recommendations for a
community vision. He then briefly summarized chapters one and four for the councilmembers.
