Washington County Land Use Authority Meeting
                                 June 09, 2020

The regular meeting of the Washington County Planning Commission was conducted via electronic
means through Zoom on Tuesday, June 09, 2020 from the Commission Chambers in the Washington
County Administration Building, 197 East Tabernacle, St. George, Utah. Chairman Kevin Jones
opened the meeting at 1:30 p.m. He led the Pledge of Allegiance and explained how the meeting would
be conducted.

Commissioners present: Dan Briggs (Alternate acting as a voting member), Shayne Durrant, Slade
Hughes, Mark Owen on Zoom, Reed Scow (Alternate), Doug Solstad, and Jon Walter.

Excused: Brandon Anderson

Staff present: Scott Messel, Community Development Director; Natalie Nelson, Deputy County
Attorney; Doreen Bowers-Irons, Administrative Assistant; Cheyenne Bentley, Deputy Clerk at host
location; Todd Edwards, County Engineer; Kurt Gardner, County Building Official; Kyle Layton,
Building Inspector.


1. FINAL SUBDIVISION PLAT APPROVAL. Review and approve the final subdivision plat to
amend and extend the Diamond Valley Acre Phase 2 Subdivision Plat. The Parcels are DVA-2-A-83-
DA and 7326-A-6-DA. Parcel DVA-2-A-83-DA. The parcels are located at 1383 W. Diamond Valley
Dr. in Diamond Valley. The applicant is Justin Wanlass.

Mr. Messel provided a power point presentation that included a copy of the plat and a map of the area.
He reported that Cory Morgan had been working with county staff on possible options to subdivide his
two parcels located east of the Diamond Valley Elementary School in Diamond Valley. Unfortunately,
Cory passed away. His son in-law, Justin Wanlass is moving forward with the 3-lot subdivision plans.
Parcel DVA-2-A-83-DA, which is Lot 83 of Diamond Valley Acres Phase 2 Subdivision. There is an
existing single dwelling on Lot 83. Lot 83 is approximately 1 acre in size. Access to the existing single
family dwelling on Parcel 7236-A-6-DA is from a driveway across Lot 83. The proposed subdivision
will divide Parcel 7236-A-6-DA into 2 flag lots. County code permits two flag lots on one flag stem
access. The two flag lots identified as Lots 83A and 83B are each approximately 6 acres in size. The
preliminary plat and zone change were approved in April 2020. The zone change brought the proposed
3-lot subdivision into zoning compliance. Lot 83 was rezoned from RE-40 (Residential Estate 40,000
square foot minimum lot size) zone to RE-20 (Residential Estate 20,000 square foot minimum lot size)
zone. The primary purpose for the zone change was to meet the street frontage requirement. Lots 83A
and 83B meet the zoning standards of the existing A-5 (Agricultural 5 acre minimum lot size) zone. As

2. PUBLIC HEARING/ORDINANCE AMENDMENTS. Review, discuss, and hold a public
hearing for amendments to the Washington County Ordinances: Title 10, Chapter 4: Definitions; Title
10, Chapter 7: Agricultural; and Title 10, Chapter 6, Articles A and B: Open Space.

Mr. Messel explained some of the amendments to the Ordinances, Title 10, Chapter 4: Definitions;
Title 10, Chapter 7: Agricultural; and Title 10, Chapter 6, Articles A and B: Open Space. These
amendments had been discussed in previous meetings. He pointed out one of the changes was to the
Agricultural zone to add agritourisim in the agricultural zones. He cited the ordinance.

The commission and staff reviewed the amendments. Some clarification questions on the proposed
changes were asked and answered. In addition, some clerical corrections were made. The discussion
included moving forward with conditional uses and how to address them.

Chairman Jones opened the public hearing.

No one was in attendance who wished to speak.

Chairman Jones closed the public hearing.

MOTION:           Commissioner Hughes MOVED to recommend that the County Commission adopt
                  the amendments to the Washington County Ordinances: Title 10, Chapter 4:
                  Definitions; Title 10, Chapter 7: Agricultural; and Title 10, Chapter 6, Articles A
                  and B: Open Space, with a change to shooting range tactical definition to any legal
                  entity instead of listing them all out and correcting the clerical changes that were
                  addressed.

                  Commissioner Walter asked for a short discussion. He pointed out that the idea
                  was brought up as to whether this fits in a different zone. He stated he had two
                  thoughts. One, could it be a better fit in a different zone. Two, if it is in this zone,

6.    COMMISSION & STAFF REPORTS. General reporting on various topics.

Commissioner Durrant stated there have been emails going back and forth regarding the upcoming
conditional use permit application for the Kolob project on whether we should open it up for public
comments or not. He believed that as a planning commission they all needed to be on the same page.
He understood that code does not require public comment, and he was not averse to having public
comments but was adverse to the 99% of the comments that are “not in my backyard”. He would like
to see a more standard set on public comments and input during public hearings. All the clapping,
cheering, and applause should not be allowed to take place. That is why he was opposed to opening
the meeting up for public comment because emotions rule. He asked of everyone’s feelings on the
matter.

Commissioner Briggs stated he spoke to a planning and zoning director of a city who said it best.
“Every person has the right to speak to what they want to speak to but they don’t have a right to go to
the meeting and do it. They can use other mediums to make their voice heard. They have the right to
speak and we have the right to listen, it just does not have to be in a meeting where it is not a public
hearing”.

Commissioner Durrant concurred. He noted he reads all of the comments received but takes offense to
allegations of impropriety, innuendos and all the slander. We come to these meetings to help make the
county a better place and this should not be an arena for that type of behavior.

Chairman Jones pointed out he was the one who generated the email. We are all aware that this is a
very contentious issue. We have read the large numbers of emails both pro and con and the concerns
have been noted. The idea he had of opening up the meeting for a limited time to cover any items that
we are not comfortable with. If someone has more questions regarding fire or water, then we could
have someone from those jurisdictions come and address them. The idea was to keep it limited and as
chair, he would not allow that type of behavior in any public meeting. Four commissioners responded
that they were not in favor of opening the meeting to comments and he sent out a follow-up email
communicating that they would not open the meeting up for comments. Commissioner Durrant
