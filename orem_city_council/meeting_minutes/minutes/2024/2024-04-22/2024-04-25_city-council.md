UTILITIES

Mr. Peay discussed the north border of Orem and the interconnections in vaults that have
the ability to exchange water if needed. Currently, the cities are working on a maintenance
agreement, where the cities will partner on the maintenance of 2000 North in Orem (200 South
in Lindon). Mr. Lambson applauded the coordination between Lindon and Orem during the
runoff concerns. Mr. Garrido spoke of the shared sewer connections between:Lindon and Orem.
He spoke of the process that is used to reconcile sewer from Orem into this shared connection.
He spoke about stormwater and there is a connection to Orem from Lindon’s main ditch. Mr.
Garrido continued to speak about the Sewer Treatment Plant. Lindon City owns about 12.5% of
the facility. They participate in all the capital improvement related to that facility.

Ms. Millett asked about the irrigation system and how it affects Orem. Mr. Garrido
explained that Lindon City is one of the bigger customers for the Provo Bench water. They are
about 75-80% of the water in the canal. To protect Lindon’s interest, they need to police the

water that is about 6 miles of open ditch. In the past years, Lindon has spent money to fix the
canal. There were concerns that a landslide could eliminate the canal. As part of this project,
Lindon has the opportunity to tap into the Murdock Canal. Instead of having 6 miles to police,
now they will have about 2,000 feet of pipe to get a quick connection to their secondary water

concern that there will not be enough water volume to push the water to current users in Orem.

Lundburg spoke about how cities have been subsidizing the maintenance of the canal and that it
would cost millions to properly pipe it. Mr. Broderick explained that there are two components.
The first part is the canal or ditch company that all shareholders participate in the cost. The
laterals off the ditch are the shareholders responsibility to take care of. As the cities grow, less
people are using the canal system so it becomes very expensive for fewer users.

Mr. Garrido bought up the sewer treatment plant and the costs needed to keep it
functioning. Mr. Bybee explained that about a year and half ago, Orem adopted a new capital
facilities plan. It includes $160 million worth of estimated improvement upgrades for the
treatment plant over the next fifty plus years. In the short term, the first phase is about $60
million worth of projects. The next phase is to find ways to finance the project. This includes an
increase in our sewer utility fee and grants. This is an issue for many of the sewer plants in Utah.
Mr. Peay gave information on the water reuse project.

EDUCATION ,

Ms. Millett informed the group of a meeting with the Alpine School District (ASD) board
she attended yesterday. She offered to send the link of the meeting to this group. ASD has
reviewed the results from the MGT study. They are currently studying the results and on Tuesday
they should be voting on what they are going to submit to the county for the ballot. In the
meeting they were discussing the 6 options and the need to narrow it down to two options. Mr.
Bybee confirmed that Orem has not hired anyone to research the school district split but that he
has had conversations with Laura Lewis about it. Mayor Lundberg said that Lehi is doing a
formal study. Lehi’s decision could cause a shift in how cities may align. The school district
recommendations could do the same.

Ms. Millett expressed concerns she has heard from residents and she believes that Orem
voters are open to a split that is not Orem only. Mayor Young would like to see Orem be
proactive in what they think is best for their families. Mr. Hooley discussed the things he has
heard from Lindon residents. He would like to see discussion between the different city leaders.
Ms. Millet explained that an interlocal only allows those cities to vote, where the district vote
means everyone in the district votes on the split. Mr. Earl clarified that if there is an interlocal
agreement then the cities in the agreement just need a majority vote by district, not each
individual city. The school district initiated split is only approved if the majority of voters in the
proposed school district boundaries approve, as well as the majority of the existing school

FIRE/EMS

Mayor Lundberg spoke about a presentation that Chief Sanderson gave to their City
Council. Chief Sanderson summarized what he presented. He spoke of the high level of service
Orem fire is offering. They are even being called into neighboring cities to assist due to their
specialities. He discussed with Lindon the contract with Orem and the fees associated with that
contract. The city managers of Orem and Lindon will be meeting to discuss the contract and any
concerns with it. Vineyard is putting in a small fire house in their community that will only
enhance the department's abilities as a whole. Mayor Lundberg appreciated that Chief Sanderson
was willing to come explain the costs for Lindon to cover their fire and paramedic needs.

TRANSPORTATION

Mayor Young brought up the boundary area on 2000 N (Orem) or 200 S (Lindon) east of
State Street. There is a planned development in that area. One issue Orem is concerned about is
street side parking in that area. Lindon’s side of that street does allow for street parking. Mayor

Lundberg expressed interest in the project. She brought up the concern for her residents about the
increased traffic on this road due to the temple being built in the area. The concerns include the
Murdock Canal Trail and further east there is a recommendation to have a four way stop instead
of a two way stop.

Mayor Lundberg brought up the widening project of 1600 North by UDOT. Mr. Clarke
said that UDOT is in the process of purchasing right of way. They are hoping to start the project
next spring. They are about 30% designed but it depends on right of way purchases. UDOT has
assured the City of Orem that they will find the funding for the project.

ADJOURN

Mr. Macdonald moved to adjourn this meeting, seconded by Ms. Millett. Those voting yes:
David Young, LaNae Millett, David Spencer, Jeff Lambson, Tom Macdonald, Chris Killpack,
and Jenn Gale. The motion passed.

PASSED and APPROVED this 14" day of May 2024.
