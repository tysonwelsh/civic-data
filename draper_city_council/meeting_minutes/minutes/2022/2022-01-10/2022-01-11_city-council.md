Study Session

1.0    Oath of Office for Fire Department

1.1    Fire Chief Clint Smith stated that he was proud to present new members of the Fire
       Department that evening, and commented that both of the new members of the
       department were experienced paramedics. He noted that he had worked with Jeff
       Smith when he had worked for Unified Fire Association (UFA), and said that he had gone
       through school to become a licensed PA in addition to his work as a firefighter. Fire
       Chief Smith then stated that the other new member, Carlos Lopez, had been in the fire
       service for about seven years, and had chosen to leave a nearby city’s fire service to
       come work for Draper. He felt that both men embodied the core values of the Draper
       Fire Department, and he thought they were a good fit.

1.2    The oath of office was administered to Jeff Smith and Carlos Lopez by City Recorder
       Laura Oscarson. A photograph was taken of Fire Chief Clint Smith, Carlos Lopez, and
       Jeff Smith.

2.0    Discussion: Parks and Recreation Capital Improvement Projects

2.1    Parks and Recreation Director Rhett Ogden detailed the improvements that had been
       made to the Jordan River Rotary Park, namely the expansion of the parking lot and the
       creation of a new one. He stated they had updated the entrance, and improved the trail
       access from the entrance. He then shared an aerial view of the trail system and
       explained that they had made improvements to the trails and added connectors to the
       trail system. He noted that his favorite trail was the Forget-Me-Not trail, which had
       been completed over the previous autumn. He added that all of the loop trails had been
       completed as well. He recalled that the Council had previously requested for the

      Recreation department had emphasized the development of hiking-only loop trails and
      updated the parking lots to have restrooms when possible.

      Mr. Ogden added that the signage had been updated as well, to show that the trails
      were only for hiking, and not horseback riding or biking. He said that they were
      currently working on the interpretive signs for the trails, which would include
      information about the geology, biology, and watersheds in the area.

      Mr. Ogden said that they had just received bids for the replacement of some park
      playgrounds. He noted their budget for this was about $250,000, and those
      playgrounds would be installed by the coming summer. He added they had also ordered
      an “all abilities” playground, and it was an all-inclusive playground designed for people
      with disabilities in mind. He said that playground was also expected to be installed by
      the summer. He went over some details of the features of the playground.

      Mr. Ogden said that they would crack seal, slurry seal, and spot repair the pavement on
      paved trails as well. He said that the project would be ongoing beginning in the spring
      and would go through the summer, and was anticipated to be finished by the end of the
      summer. He also noted that they would be reviewing the Parks and Trails Master Plan,
      which had last been updated in 2018. He said that they would be taking public input,
      and a draft of the updates would be completed in the spring.

2.2   Mr. Ogden then went over projects that the Parks and Recreation Department wanted to
      undertake that required Council approval. He discussed the need to establish
      sustainable trail routes at the summit of Potato Hill and said that it currently was not
      safe since there were abandoned routes that were in a state of disrepair. He noted that
      this did not require any funding, but they did need permission before they could start on
      the projects. Mayor Walker and the council members gave support to the Parks and
      Recreation Department to update the trail routes.

      Mr. Ogden then requested permission to utilize an existing railroad bridge to create a
      crossing for the Porter Rockwell trail, located at 1300 East. He said they could add a
      button that would allow for hikers to cross the road, and this would cost about $75,000.
      He noted that this would also require Utah Transit Authority (UTA) approval, and would
      be subject to their requirements. He acknowledged that this might become political.
      Councilmember F. Lowry thought it made sense to add a crossing from a safety
      standpoint.

      Mr. Ogden requested that they update the West Bluff and Deer Ridge Area. He wanted
      to grade the parking lot, create a new off-leash dog area, create about three miles of
      foot-only trails, and add a disc golf area. He said that they had already secured funding
      for these projects from the Primitive Trails Funds, and so they would not need additional
      funding. He anticipated this would take about two years to complete.

2.3   Councilmember Tasha Lowery thought it was important that they add a restroom,
      especially if there were kid-friendly trails. Mr. Ogden explained that there was space for
      a restroom, but they would need additional funding to install a restroom.

3.0   Council/Manager Reports

3.1   Councilmember Cal Roberts reported that he had received an email from the Traverse
      Ridge Special Service District (TRSSD) members, who had told him that they projected
      that their cash reserves would be depleted by the end of the year. He said that the City
      needed to play an administrative role for them to find solutions to that problem.

      City Manager David Dobbins explained that they could limit or restrict completely the
      administrative control that TRSSD had, and they could turn all responsibility over to the
      City Council. He said that the TRSSD Board had indicated that they wanted to turn
      control over to the City. He also anticipated that this would become a very politicized
      issue. Councilmember Cal Roberts added that they would need a tax increase, and
      asked how that would be handled. Mr. Dobbins replied that the City would determine
      what the new tax rate would be, and then they would have to hold a public meeting to
      discuss the increase in a public forum.

      Councilmember Tasha Lowery thought that the TRSSD Board needed to acknowledge
      that there was a huge problem before the City took over administrative control.
      Councilmember Cal Roberts pointed out that if they ran out of cash and were unable to
      pay for snowplows, that would cause a huge problem for the City, and they would need
      to get ahead of the issue.

      Councilmember Tasha Lowery thought that since the TRSSD Board was aware of the
      deficit, they needed to be honest about that with their constituents so that nothing
      came as a huge surprise when the City took over the administration of the board.

      Mr. Dobbins noted that they have not submitted a budget for the last two years, and
      were non-compliant with the State in terms of budget. He said that the Council would
      not want to own the TRSSD budget.

      Councilmember Cal Roberts proposed that a meeting be held with some members of the
      City Council, members of the TRSSD Board, and members of the finance staff where
      they could work out the details of how the transfer would work and get into the specifics
      of what the TRSSD Board needed. The Councilmembers discussed that the TRSSD
      Board knew what needed to be done, and they simply needed to act on it. The Council
      members agreed that they did not want to be the ones that were responsible for the tax
      increase, and thought that TRSSD Board should be the ones to increase the tax.

      Councilmember Tasha Lowery suggested that they help the TRSSD Board by sending a
      staff liaison, but said that she did not want to take on responsibility for the entire board.
      Councilmember Mike Green agreed that they could allow TRSSD Board to utilize the City
      Staff, but pointed out that TRSSD Board had not shown that they were capable of
      making changes promptly. He said that he did not want to be involved with this either,
      or thought that giving them access to the City Staff would be a good idea, rather than
      take over administrative function completely. Mr. Dobbins agreed that TRSSD Board
      needed to hold a meeting, and said that they could pressure them to have a meeting.



3.2   Councilmember Mike Green reported that a constituent at SunCrest had complained
      about speeding on Traverse Mountain Road and a fire hydrant being blocked. He
      acknowledged that speeding was a big problem on that road, and said that it should be
      easy to go investigate the fire hydrant complaint and determine if it was blocked.

      Mayor Walker ended the Study Session and moved to the Business Meeting at 7:00 PM.
      The Council returned to the Council/Manager Reports at the end of the Business
      Session.

3.3   Public Works Director Scott Cooley informed the Council that the Traffic Committee was
      in the process of changing the parking notices in the Senior Band area. He explained
      that currently, the signs indicated that parking was only allowed on one side of the
      street at a time, but which side of the street allowed parking changed daily, and this
      was confusing to residents and difficult to enforce. He said that they would update the
      parking notices so that parking was permanently limited to one side of the street at all
      times.

      Mr. Cooley then discussed that the Traffic Committee had received a lot of comments
      and complaints about the crosswalk at Bangerter Highway and Southfork Drive. He
      stated that the crosswalk went over five lanes of traffic, and many people had requested
      that a pedestrian signal be installed. He said that a traffic signal was not currently
      required, but the Traffic Committee would post signs that directed pedestrians to cross
      the street at a bridge that was 750 feet away from the current crosswalk. He added
      that pedestrians would still be allowed to cross the street where the crosswalk currently
      was, and he discussed that unmarked crosswalks were shown to be safer for

5.0   Public Hearing: Ordinances #1521 and #1522, ordinances of Draper City
      amending the text of the Land Use and Development Code and amending a
      Master Area Plan per the Land Use and Development Code of the Draper City
      Municipal Code relating to the Vista 600 West Master Area Plan TSD Subzone
      Designations Map.

5.1   Planning Manager Jennifer Jastremsky indicated the area on a map and said that the
      area had a mix of residential, commercial, and retail zones. She said that the applicant

                                                                                   Page 5 of 7
      wanted to amend the concept plan to increase the residential use, change the retail use
      to not allow hotels, and eliminate the office space. She explained that the applicant did
      not want to amend the subzone, but only wanted to switch out the concept plan within
      the Code. She added that the existing concept plan had 250 residential units and
      15,000 square feet of office space, and the applicant wished to add another 55
      residential units and eliminate the office space. The applicant also wanted to include a
      clubhouse, maintenance shed, and other features to the residential area and wanted
      clarity about what kinds of architectural features would be required for those structures.
      Ms. Jastremsky indicated on the map where the proposed entrance points for the
      development would go. She reported that the Planning Commission had reviewed this
      issue in December, and had recommended approval.



5.2   Councilmember F. Lowry asked how many apartment buildings would be included with
      the new plan, and Ms. Jastremsky explained that the number of units would increase
      from 250 to 305.

5.3   Howard Cooke introduced himself as a representative of the applicant, Gardner
      Company, and thanked Ms. Jastremsky for her presentation. He explained that initially,
      they had wanted the space to be used for office use, but due to the COVID-19 pandemic
      the need for office space had greatly decreased, and so they had shifted their focus to
      retail. He reported that they had gotten a good response from retailers so far, and he
      wanted to work with the City to find tenants that everyone was excited about. He
      thought that the area was underserved for retail, and thought that it would be a good
      addition to the area to have service-oriented retail stores.

5.4   Mayor Walker opened the public hearing. No one came forward so Mayor Walker closed
      the public hearing.

5.5   Ms. Jastremsky noted that the overall property was 15.24 acres. Councilmember Mike
      Green commented that he welcomed more retail space, but was unsure that they
      wanted residential units in that area.

7.0   Action Item: Ordinance #1523, an ordinance amending sections 3-5-020, 3-5-
      030, and 3-5-050 of the Draper City Municipal Code regarding subordinate
      bodies and public comment at meetings of subordinate bodies.

7.1    City Manager David Dobbins explained that the ordinance stated that all of the
      subordinate bodies in Draper would no longer include a public comment section as a
      part of their meetings. He further explained the reasoning behind this change was that
      all of the committees were subordinate bodies to the City Council and that any

                                                                                    Page 6 of 7
      comments or complaints about the committees should be brought to the attention of the
      City Council. He added that this also prevented the committees from being blamed for
      things that were out of their control. He said it would direct the public to make their
      comments to the City Council, rather than going through a subordinate body first.

7.2   Councilmember Green moved to approve Ordinance #1523. Councilmember
      T. Lowery seconded the motion.



7.3   A roll call vote was taken with Councilmembers Green, Lowery, Lowry,
      Roberts, and Vawdrey, voting in favor. The motion passed unanimously.
