      Steve Pastorik, CED Director, discussed proposed Ordinance 24-02 that would

      amend Titles 7 and 9 of the West Valley Municipal Code to Update Certain
      Requirements Concerning Subdivisions.

      Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
      information as follows:

             City staff is proposing an ordinance change to amend the Zoning (Title 7)
             and Condominium (Title 9) Ordinances to comply with State code changes
             from HB 406 and SB 174. Both HB 406 and SB 174 were passed by the
             Utah Legislature in the 2023 General Session of the Utah Legislature and
             later signed by the governor. Copies of these bills are included with this
             issue paper. Among other changes, the bills prohibit cities from requiring
             an applicant to post an improvement completion bond for landscaping on
             private property (HB 406) and prohibit the legislative body of a
             municipality from reviewing subdivision applications for single-family
             dwellings, two-family dwellings, or townhomes (SB 174).

             Instead of having a separate subdivision process for subdivision
             applications for single-family dwellings, two-family dwellings, or
             townhomes, staff is proposing that the subdivision review process be
             amended to not require the approval of the City Council for any
             subdivision, especially since these decisions are administrative.

      Councilmember Huynh asked when this would become effective. Steve replied
      that the law takes effect February 1st but noted that if this ordinance were
      approved by the Council, it would take effect next Tuesday. Councilmember
      Fitisemanu clarified that this wouldn’t preclude the Council from inquiring, it
      would just eliminate the Council from voting on subdivision applications. Mayor
      Lang stated that the rezone portion of the application is important. She asked
      when engineering is conducted. Steve replied that engineering is done as part of
      the subdivision. He noted that engineering can’t be required at the zoning phase of
      the application. Steve stated that if the Council is concerned about a particular
      requirement in the subdivision code, this can be changed. Councilmember
      Harmon added that the Council can also require certain things as part of a
      Development Agreement.

      The City Council will consider Ordinance 24-02 at the Regular Council Meeting
      scheduled January 9, 2024 at 6:30 P.M.

 ACTION: ORDINANCE NO. 24-03, AMEND SECTION 7-13-704 OF THE
 WEST VALLEY CITY MUNICIPAL CODE TO AMEND CERTAIN
 REQUIREMENTS CONCERNING ACCESS FOR LOTS OR PARCELS IN
 COMMERCIAL COMPLEXES
 Steve Pastorik, CED Director, discussed proposed Ordinance 24-03 that would
 Amend Section 7-13-704 of the West Valley City Municipal Code to Amend Certain
 Requirements Concerning Access for Lots or Parcels in Commercial Complexes.

 Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
 information as follows:

        In July of last year, the City Council adopted Ordinance 22-29, which
        prohibited private streets in new subdivisions except for subdivisions
        within the M (Manufacturing) zone. At the time, the Council had
        expressed concerns about the financial burden private streets place on the
        residents of planned unit developments (PUDs) with private streets.
        However, as currently written, the ordinance prohibits the creation of pad
        sites within commercial developments.

        The proposed ordinance would allow for the creation of new pad sites
        within a commercial complex. For reference, the term commercial
        complex is defined in the ordinance as: “two or more Commercial Uses on
        a single Lot or adjacent Lots which are dependent upon each other to meet
        minimum standards for parking, vehicular circulation, or Landscaping or
        which are approved as elements in an overall Site Plan under a
        Conditional Use or Subdivision application”. Pad sites within a larger
        commercial development would be part of a commercial complex.

              Examples of existing commercial developments where vehicles must drive
              over another parcel to access pad sites include the Valley Fair Mall, the
              Walmarts along 5600 West at 3100 S and 6200 S, Winco at 2572 S 5600
              W, and the Kohl’s anchored center at 3031 S 5600 W.

       Councilmember Harmon asked if this change would just apply to commercial pad
       sites. Steve replied yes. Mayor Lang asked if more road base is required. Steve

RESOLUTION 24-04: AUTHORIZE THE PURCHASE OF A MARSHMASTER
AMPHIBIOUS VEHICLE
Dan Johnson, Public Works Director, presented proposed Resolution 24-04 that would
authorize the Purchase of a Marshmaster Amphibious Vehicle.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:
     MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JANUARY 2, 2024
                            -9-


       The Public Works Department maintains over 60 acres of wetland or pond areas.
       A common problem in wetlands is the growth of Phragmite Australis, an invasive
       species that chokes out natural vegetation, plugs up ponds, creates camping areas
       and is a fire hazard.

       The Public Works Department currently uses an amphibious ATV to smash and
       spray phragmites, which is a hunting ATV and was not designed for the rigors of
       phragmite maintenance.

       There are narrow time windows in the summertime when phragmites need to be
       addressed. The Public Works Department recommends the purchase of a larger
       amphibious vehicle to be more efficient in the efforts to control phragmites. The
       City also has responsibility over several other water bodies throughout the City that
       have maintenance needs other than phragmites, and this vehicle could be used in
       those situations as well.

       The Marsh Master is a heavy-duty hydraulically driven amphibious tracked vehicle
       that has the power to drive through thick vegetation smashing it down breaking the
       stalks so they can be sprayed with a herbicide to limit re-growth. This vehicle has
       the capability to carry the herbicide enabling the treatment of the area at the same
       time increasing productivity. It also has the capability to traverse through any of
       our pond systems to remove algae growth and other maintenance needs.

Councilmember Harmon asked if this will require a budget opening. Dan replied no.
Councilmember Fitisemanu asked if this will impact the number of employees needed to
maintain the wetlands. Dan replied that it will still require 2 employees but would decrease
the amount of time needed. Councilmember Whetstone asked if there is a way to contract
this duty out for a lower price tag. Dan replied that he doesn’t know of any other City that
has this equipment and the State is unwilling to assist due to the amount of wetland area
they maintain. He noted that this would be a 20-year piece of equipment. Councilmember
Harmon asked if staff could lease out the equipment for the months needed. Dan replied
that he would look into this. Mayor Lang asked if the equipment would be stored inside.
Dan replied yes. Mayor Lang asked if there is a preventative time that staff can spray to
prevent phragmite. Dan replied that he would look into this.

The City Council will consider Resolution 24-04 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
January 9, 2024 at 6:30 P.M
