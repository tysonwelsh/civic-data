123   3.3) Public hearing and recommendation of Gardner Company’s request for review of the
124   Skye Area Plan Amendment, creating some design changes to the General Nonresidential
125   Design Requirements in the Mixed-Use areas.
126
127   Jacob Curtis presented the item. This amendment would add two design exceptions. The first is
128   for drive aisles, which would allow drive-thrus adjacent to Center Street, Traverse
129   Terrace, 500 W, and Timpanogos Highway. This would minimize the number of drive-thru
130   applications that come before the Planning Commission. The second exception is for the glass
131   location on buildings. They are requesting the flexibility to not provide 20% glass on the façade
132   facing the public street. They would prefer the glazing to be on the front or side elevation if it
133   faces the private street. The 20% glass requirement would still be met. Both exceptions are due
134   to slope issues in the area.
135
136   Commissioner Kunze asked where the 20% glass standard came from. Curtis said it was likely
137   from the city design standards. He explained that staff is looking into where the figure is coming
138   from.
139
140   Mark Murdock was present, representing Gardner Company, along with his colleagues Dave
141   Denison and Chase. They are trying to push pedestrian traffic off of Center Street. He showed
142   how backs of buildings are difficult to see due to the grading, thus the desire to move the glazing
143   to other sides of the building.
144

165   3.4) Public hearing and recommendation of the Downtown Revitalization Plan and State
166   Street Station Area Plan including parts of Main Street and State Street.
167
168   Jacob Curtis presented the item. He said that the engineering department would like to table the
169   item for a later meeting but would still like to hold public hearing. This plan is a guiding document,
170   not a regulatory one. It establishes a vision for downtown Lehi. Curtis turned the time over to the
171   co-applicant.
172
173   Sam Taylor, the consultant with Landmark Design, was present online. He gave a brief overview
174   of the plan. It is an intentionally visionary plan that looks 20+ years into the future. The goal is to
175   implement it through incremental change so that adjustments can be made as development occurs.
176   As the plan is an overview, many details and specifics are not present. Taylor shared the eight
177   guiding principles of the plan which were chosen based on public input. He discussed the different
178   districts that the plan focused on, the main ones being the Mills, the Downtown Core, and the
179   Station Area.
180
181   The goals for the downtown core include making Main Street more walkable and organizing more
182   public open spaces to encourage foot traffic. Taylor emphasizes that the city will need to
183   compromise and cooperate with potential and current business owners in order to encourage
184   development. The goal for the Mills district is to encourage financial investment and development
185   through allowing mixed-use zoning. This would also help walkability. Additionally, Taylor
186   recommends loosening design standards to encourage more development in these already
187   developed areas.
188
189   Commissioner Kunze acknowledged that there will be change in Lehi regardless of how people
190   feel about it, and she appreciates having a plan to ensure the change is good for the city. She asked
191   how the Mills district would be connected to the Downtown Core. Taylor explained that the plan

      Lehi City Planning Commission                     4                                    October 23, 2025
192   was to make more pedestrian infrastructure down Main Street, but that much of the area between
193   the districts would likely stay the same in the current iteration of the plan.
194
195   The goals for the Station Area district are a little more difficult, as they need to be coordinated
196   with UDOT and UTA. The city is currently unsure what method of transportation will be
197   implemented. Taylor said that in the meantime increasing transportation connectivity, expanding
198   utilities, and creating street safety improvements for pedestrians are goals. Commissioner Lockhart
199   asked if the plan still hinges on Union Pacific being willing to move their railroad turnaround. That
200   is still the case. Commissioner Lockhart asked if there was a backup plan for the area. Taylor said
201   that it would be difficult to get development to happen at all in the area if the turnaround is not
202   removed, as there will be issues with getting emergency access to the area.
203
204   Some changes have occurred since the plan was last presented in a joint work session with the
205   Lehi City Council. The commercial overlay on 100 E has been removed due to concerns from
206   Commissioners and Councilors. A massing graphic has been added to the Mills District section.
207   The possibility of Light Rail Transit (LRT) has been added to the Station Area District section.
208   Language encouraging homeownership and language encouraging coordination, analysis, and
209   monitoring of transportation improvements has been added. Additionally, reallocation of
210   residential unit caps in the Station Area and Mills Districts is now the recommendation rather than
211   removal.
212
213   The draft has been put up for the public to review, and responses are being incorporated. Most
214   respondents feel that the plan is meeting most of its stated goals. There are concerns over
215   inadequate reduction of traffic and improvements of parking, as well as concerns over context-
216   sensitive housing. Respondents are very supportive of open spaces, parks, and community
217   amenities and gathering places.
218
219   Commissioner Kunze asked if public input was still open and how it is being advertised so
220   residents are aware of it. It is still open. Jacob Curtis explained that fliers have been handed out to
221   businesses, posts have been made on Facebook and on Engage Lehi. Business owners have also
222   been emailed. Commissioner Kunze worried about overwhelming respondents with a document
223   over 100 pages and asked if it would be possible to make a condensed version for public awareness.
224
225   The next steps for the plan involve incorporating public feedback and the remaining feedback from
226   DRC.
227
228   Jacob Curtis mentioned that tabling the item tonight will allow for another few weeks to get as
229   many people involved in the feedback process as possible. Commissioner Jones said that he needs
230   more feedback before he feels comfortable hearing the proposal.
231
232   Commissioner Lockhart opened and closed the public comment period and noted that there would
233   be an additional public hearing on November 13th.
234
235   Commissioner Kunze shared that she thinks that people are worried about massive changes but
236   explained that the plan is far-reaching and meant to be incrementally implemented.
