                                            SUBDIVISION


 3.       Public Hearing and Consideration of a Subdivision Plat Amendment,
          Amending the "Cousin' s Corner" Subdivision at Approximately 1891, 1909,
          1927, 1943, 1956         and   1960 W Jordan Canal Road, Taylorsville, Utah.
           5S22 / SUB- 000236- 2022) Jim Spung, Senior Planner




      Senior Planner Jim Spung explained that applicant Van Nguyen ( TYC Investments,
LLC) was requesting Planning Commission review and approval to amend the Cousins
Corner subdivision to create seven conforming residential lots. The subdivision area
included approximately 125,009 square feet (2. 87 acres) of real property located at 1891;
1909; 1927; 1943; 1956; and 1960 W. Jordan Canal Road in Taylorsville, Utah. Approval
of the subdivision amendment would bring five existing parcels into conformance with the
R- 1- 10 zoning district and create one new R- 1- 10 lot. This amendment would also convert
two existing parcels to Taylorsville City right-of-way, which were already owned by the
city and used for Jordan Canal Road ( see Exhibit F). This amended plat would replace
the original Cousins Corner subdivision plat in its entirety. The existing Cousins Corner
plat was recorded with Salt Lake County on December 14, 2009 ( see Exhibit E). This plat

created four lots with access via a new private lane off Jordan Canal Road. When this
plat was recorded, Jordan Canal Road was aligned adjacent to the canal and terminated
at 6200 South. A short time later, UDOT acquired several properties in the area ( including
those within the Cousins Corner subdivision) to expand the 6200 S. right-of-way. Jordan
Canal Road was also re- aligned as part of that project. After the UDOT project was
complete, the property not used in the right-of-way expansion was sold at auction. UDOT
is exempt from municipal platting procedures, so the original Cousins Corner subdivision
was never amended, and the resulting parcels created and sold by UDOT do not conform
to the R- 1- 10 zoning district standards. This subdivision amendment is an attempt to bring
the area into conformity with current zoning regulations.

The subject property was in the Single- Family Residential ( R- 1- 10)
                                                                     zoning district and
was made up of nine parcels. The property currently had one single-family residence and
one accessory building at 1956 W. 6200 S., owned by David Goodwin. These structures

were proposed to remain as- is. There were also five undeveloped parcels, two parcels
owned by the city used for Jordan Canal Road right-of-way, and one remnant parcel
contiguous with the single- family home that was owned by UDOT ( see Exhibit C). The

frontage along Jordan Canal Road included an existing park strip and 4- foot sidewalk —
no changes were proposed to the right- of-way cross- section.     Surrounding zoning
designations were as follows:


North: Single- family R- 1- 40 and R- 1- 20
South: Single- family R- 1- 10

The     proposed     subdivision      complied    with   the    minimum     lot   area   and   dimensional
requirements. Building setbacks for the R- 1- 10 zoning district could be found in Section
13. 20. 020 of the Land Development Code ( LDC). Proposed Lot 1 was a corner lot and
would be subject to the corner lot building setbacks identified in the referenced section.
There was an existing irrigation canal that ran along the eastern boundary of the proposed
plat, adjacent to proposed Lot 7. In accordance with Section 13. 28. 110, development
adjacent to a canal is subject to the following:

A.    Any parcel being subdivided or developed that is adjacent to or has within its
boundaries a canal right-of-way may be required to provide along such right-of-way a
fence as determined by the Director. As an alternative to fencing the canal and with the
review and approval of the Director, the developer may pipe the canal. If the canal is
piped,   the developer must obtain written permission from the canal company and
construct the pipe according to the canal company's requirements and specifications.
B. All fences bordering canals shall be installed as part of the improvements for the
subdivision or other development. No occupancy permit, whether temporary or final,
shall be granted until all required fencing is installed in the subdivision or development.

The proposed improvement plan included installation of a 6- foot-tall vinyl fence along the
eastern    property line     of    Lot 7,   adjacent   to the   canal.   Per Section     13. 28. 110( B),   no

occupancy permit, whether temporary or final, shall be granted until all required fencing
was installed in the subdivision or development. Additionally, Section 13. 28. 090 required
the installation of a wall along the southern boundary of the subdivision ( adjacent to 6200
S). The proposed improvement plans included the installation of a new 6- foot masonry
wall along the southern property lines of Lots 1 through 7, with the westernmost part of
the fence stepping down in height to accommodate the clear vision triangle for the
intersection of 6200 S. and Jordan Canal Road. The proposed plat amendment had been
reviewed by city departments and review agencies and generally complied with city
standards; however, there was one outstanding comment that needed to be addressed
prior to final approval and recording. There is a small remnant parcel ( 827. 64 square feet)
on the northern portion of proposed Lot 3 that was currently owned by UDOT. This parcel
was likely an unintended remnant that resulted from the acquisition of property and re-
alignment of Jordan Canal Road. Prior to final approval, the applicant must coordinate
with UDOT to reconcile the ownership of this property. Option 1 was for UDOT to quit-
claim the property to David Goodwin ( owner of proposed Lot 3 1956 W Jordan Canal
Road). Option 2 was UDOT retained ownership of that property and an access easement
in favor of Lot 3 be established. Option 2 would require UDOT to sign the plat as an owner.
Any future development on the site was subject to compliance with adopted zoning,
subdivision,      engineering, and other applicable standards. Appropriate development
permits must      be issued prior to construction activity on the site.

Chair Wilkey invited the applicant to address the Commission.

Van Nguyen introduced himself and stated he was available to address any questions
they had.

Chair Wilkey opened the public hearing.

City resident Paula Nelson expressed concern about the Goodwin family who owned Lot
No. 3. She was concerned that they were losing their property. She was assured that was
not the case. She then asked for details about the homes that would be built on the lots.
Chair Wilkey explained that was undetermined since there was not yet an application to
develop the land. Ms. Nelson concluded her comments by expressing her concerns about
traffic on a nearby cul- de- sac and how it could potentially affect the safety of children in
the area.


There was no one else who expressed a desire to speak, so the Chair closed the public
hearing.
