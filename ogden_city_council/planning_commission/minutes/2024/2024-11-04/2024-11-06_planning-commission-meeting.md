6. Conditional use permit Rooftop cell antennae. 2427 Jefferson Ave
        Petitioner Jared White was requesting approval to install some new cellular antennas. The
area sets in a densely residential zone which leaves very few structures tall enough, and the zoning
code doesn't allow for monopoles in most of the sections they would need covered. The building at
this address is one of the few buildings that does have enough height for them to create the coverage
that they need without having to build a new tower in the area.
        Planner Jennifer Patrick stated the property is 2.1 acres and is located on the southwest
corner of 24th Street and Jefferson Avenue. It is a six-story apartment building that was built around
1975. The antennas on the west side of the rooftop will be set back from the edge of the building 1O
to 11 feet. The structure is called The Sled and weighted down with concrete blocks. The additional
antenna equipment that will be attached to the penthouse facing east will be painted the same color
as the building to better blend in and won"t be visible from the street. In the Ogden Municipal Code, 15-
7-2 there are considerations or criteria for conditional use permit, and that is, the use is not
detrimental to persons or property, the use is consistent with the general plan, particularly the
Community Plan, the use is compatible with the character of the site and adjacent properties, the
design is compatible with the site and the surrounding area, and the use is compliant with the
regulations and conditions of the zone. The C-9 zone requires a conditional use permit for new cell

7. Somerset Villas at Chester, 218 Chester St
       a. Final group dwelling
                Petitioner John Clayton expressed his concern with having the connectivity of this
          subdivision for all the residents and going to the amenities area being in the back space of
          the homes as suggested by Planning instead of in the front as in his proposed plan. They
          feel that having it in the back would be adding a lot of concrete and be taking away what
          little grass area they will have for families to enjoy as well as taking away from the
          community.
                Assistant Planning Director Joe Simpson showed slides and said what they're looking at
          is 29 townhome units and one single family home plotted for individual ownership. In
          response to having the sidewalk connectivity to amenities for the residents, a requirement
          from the code in the group dwelling, is that four-foot sidewalks be provided from the
          entrances in the development to the main gathering areas or amenity areas, and those must
          be from the front entrances. The garage sides of the buildings can't function as the front
          main entrance of the project as they had proposed, because there is setback separation of
          the projection of the garages from the main walls of the buildings. So, staff does not
          recommend approval of that part of the plan as it goes against code. However, staff has
          drawn up a report that could help minimize the look of the needed concrete sidewalks by
          incorporating some landscaping in the staff recommended site plan. Also, the only place it
          would make sense to put an entry feature is on the single family lot, so staff is
          recommending in order to meet that code requirement that that be the location for the sign
          for Somerset Villas, and that that area be included with the plat for the HOA to maintain as
          part of the development, so that single family wouldn't be obligated with that and have an
          easement for it. There is code requirement that 50% of the units must have at least 100
          square foot patios but those patios can't extend more than six feet into the building
          separation requirements, so staff recommends taking those buildings that could work,
          where they could get those 15 needed units to meet the patio requirement with the
          enclosure.
          (more slides shown for plans and example) So, in closing, given that the conditions laid out
          by staff are met, they recommend approval.

8. PRUD amendment, Sunset Ridge, Harrison Blvd & Shadow Valley Dr
        Assistant Planning Director Joe Simpson reminded everyone they"re looking at amending a final
approved PRUD, which is a planned residential unit development project for Sunset Ridge, and really
the only thing that they"re amending is the common areas. So, to do this, the commission needs to
consider that there have been changes that warrant amendments to the approved PRUD plans, and that
the PRUD standards are being met, providing enhanced building designs, active open space,
landscaping, fencing and so forth. Why a developer chooses. to use a PRUD is they're able to get an
increase of the density in the development in exchange for providing active, open spaces and amenities
throughout the development. Overall, the site is over ten and a half acres and partially developed.
Phases l and 2 have been mostly developed, phase 3 has been started and phase 4 is yet to be built.
They need to make some changes before 3 can be completed and 4 can be started, which suggestions
are being outlined here. The PRUD project is broken up into 4 phases, but all the phases are
interconnected, it's all one development. One note is that sidewalks are typically required on both sides
of public streets, but the PRUD code allows those to be reduced, providing trails or pathways through
the common space that's maintained by the HOA. The project also includes, benches, pavilions, a tot
lot and a clubhouse, and those are outlined here on the map and the placement (slides shown). It's over

9. Engineering standards update
        Ogden City Engineer Taylor Neilsen began that the engineering standards have multiple
components for public improvement. They have amendments to their APWA drawing, that's American
Public Works Association. They put together a set of standards that help them control their contracts
and a lot of construction that happens within the public right of way, and they have also adopted their
own standard drawings. Then they adopted a storm water design manual, something that helps guide
them through a lot of flood control and other related items (slides, examples and pictures shown).
They've had some standards since about 2008. It used to be that they just tried to have references to
ordinances and other items, which they still do, to aid them in construction and development, especially
within the right of way. But their standards make sure they are as consistent as possible with the
ordinances, to ensure that they don"t have any conflicting problems or issues. When developers come
in, they can ensure that they"re getting the product that they want and that they end up with. They
consider climate, materials, components, fixtures traffic studies, effects on the environment etc. and
compare numbers and how things have worked with other cities.
        Planning Director Barton Brierley said these zoning and subdivision ordinances and engineering
standards all affect how our community is developed, what the city is like, and how it will be in the
future. That's why any project comes to commission for review. And Planning wanted to make sure that
the engineering standards and the zoning and subdivision ordinances all work together. They spent
quite a bit of time going over everything for Taylor and his team to consider so that by working together
the plan would be what was in the best interest of the city. So, staff was very pleased with the proposal
before the commission, and they recommend that the commission find that it's consistent with the
zoning subdivision ordinance and recommend the City Council adopt the standards.
