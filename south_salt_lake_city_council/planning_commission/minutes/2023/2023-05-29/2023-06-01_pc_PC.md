STAFF BUSINESS – INFORMATION ITEMS

       •   Code Update

Deputy Community Development Director, Eliza Ungricht reported that the Code Update was
referenced during the Work Meeting. Some of the updates were City-initiated and some were
applicant-initiated. At the next Planning Commission Meeting the intention was to share draft
language with Commissioners for review and discussion. It would possible for the Planning
Commission to forward a recommendation to the City Council at that time. Most of the code updates
had to do with landscaping, parking, and drive approaches. There would also be some clarifications
made to the frontage requirements for curb cuts. Currently, that language was not very clear. As
part of the update, there would also be some discretion given to the City Engineer to allow for
different spacing of curb cuts. For that to occur, there needed to be a traffic study or circulation plan
provided that would justify why the spacing was needed. Certain requirements need to be met, such
as safety, circulation, clear views, and so on. This would provide a little bit more flexibility for
developments but ensure that there were safety considerations.

Ms. Ungricht explained that there were also Code updates related to parking. Currently, single-
family homes are required to have at least one stall in an enclosed garage. However, an applicant
wants to convert his garage into more living space. If the requirements were changed, it would allow
for more livable space but may also result in more vehicles being parked on the street. Other updates
included an amendment to the parking stall depths and drive-out widths as well as additional
language for drive-thrus. For the latter, the current requirement is five stalls from behind the order
box for stacking. The amendments would ensure that additional stalls could be required by the City
if a Traffic Study indicated that more stacking stalls were necessary.

The City was also looking at allowing parking reductions of up to 20%. This is currently allowed
in the Downtown and Transit Oriented Development (“TOD”) District, but the City was looking at
changing some of the stipulations and allowing it in other areas as well. For example, within a one-
quarter mile radius from the TRAX Station. There would need to be a Parking Study to make sure
projects are not being under-parked. Ms. Ungricht reported that other potential Code updates
included some clarifications. This included language related to deliveries for food services. That
type of service differs from appliance deliveries and loading areas for moving.

Ms. Ungricht reported that the City is also looking at changing the landscape requirements in parking
lots. There is currently a landscape island required for every seven stalls. That makes it difficult for
developers, business owners, and property owners to configure their parking lots. The update will

not require landscape islands every seven stalls but there would still be a requirement to have 5% of
the parking lot landscaped. The amendment would allow property owners to choose where the
landscaping will be placed. As part of that, the landscape buffers on the frontage would be increased

development. With that being said, if there was a large parking lot with more than 60 stalls,
landscape islands would be required. She clarified that the requirement would not be every seven

Ms. Ungricht reported that an applicant-initiated amendment was related to the setbacks in the
Downtown District. It is currently five feet for front, side, and rear setbacks. The petition was to
lower those and it was recommended that on the front there be a zero-foot setback with the sides and
rear remaining at five feet. That would create a more urban and walkable area. Where the building
has to be placed in relation to the property line would also be amended for consistency. Staff wanted
to make sure that the sidewalks are wide enough. In the downtown, the minimum was a 10-foot
sidewalk. It was necessary for there to be enough width for pedestrians to walk. Commissioner
Carter expressed support for the 10-foot sidewalks and felt that the width added safety.

Ms. Ungricht continued to review potential Code updates. There would be composite fencing added
for commercial buildings. Currently, if a commercial building abuts a residential use it is required
that a CMU wall or a pre-cast cement wall be built as a buffer. The composite fencing is a bit more
affordable, durable, and still looks nice. This would give developers and business owners a little bit
more flexibility. Another update related to the setbacks for large flex buildings. For flex buildings,
it was recommended that the window percentage be lowered. It is currently 40% but if the building
is pushed back 200 feet from the right-of-way, there is not necessarily the need to have all of those
windows. The recommendation was that it be lowered to 15%. Staff was comfortable with that
recommendation. With that change, there was a desire for more upgraded materials on the front of
the building and more architectural relief to assist with the visual breakup. Ms. Ungricht shared
information about the Final Code Update. A table would be added that included all of the setbacks
for the different zones in the City.

The Code Updates were to be formally presented to the Planning Commission at the next meeting.
Ms. Ungricht looked forward to sharing draft language with the Commissioners at that time.

Action Item

Applicant:     Aardvark Solutions, Inc., Jensen Dobbs, Representative

Mr. Rodriguez presented the Staff Report and stated that the above item relates to a Conditional Use
Permit to establish a childcare center. The property is located at 3200 South 700 East and is in the
Commercial Corridor Land Use District. Childcare centers are a Conditional Use in the zone. A
two-story commercial building that had traditionally been used for offices and professional services
currently exists on the site. The purpose of the application was to allow for St. John’s Community
Child Development Center to expand its existing non-profit childcare program. The building has
two occupiable floors and one subterranean story devoted to parking. Parking is accessed from the
north. Images of the existing conditions were shared with the Commission.

If the applicant was granted the requested Conditional Use Permit, the intention was to pursue
entitlement for interior renovations of the building as well as some exterior site improvements. After
the renovations, 15 parking stalls would be located in the basement. With those 15 parking stalls,
there would be a maximum of 75 children allowed at the property at any given time. Mr. Rodriguez
shared the proposed Site Plan. There were minimal changes proposed for the exterior but the
changes included the addition of a playground on the west side of the property. On the east side of
the property, there would be a ramp added for Americans with Disabilities Act (“ADA”)
accessibility. There would also be some reconfiguration of the fencing on the site. As for the
changes proposed to the interior, this included reformatting the existing floorplan to accommodate
six separate classrooms, adequate provisions for a fire suppression system, and ADA accessibility
enhancements to include an elevator.

In terms of the General Plan considerations, Staff found that the application would continue to
welcome new residents and businesses into South Salt Lake. It would also maximize South Salt
Lake's social and economic equity through value-based decision-making. Staff found that the use
was highly compatible with the subject property. The property would also serve as an area of
transition to the R-1 zoning that was to the immediate west of the property and to the larger retail
area that was to the immediate south. As a result, Staff recommended approval of the Conditional
Use Permit with the proposed Conditions of Approval. The recommendation was based on the
Findings of Fact and Conclusions of Law outlined in the Staff Report.
