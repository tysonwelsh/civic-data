    5. PRUD AMENDMENT TO AMEND HOUSE TYPES FOR SINGLE FAMILY LOTS WITHIN THE VILLAGE AT WOLF
       CREEK DEVELOPMENT, AND TO OBTAIN APPROVAL FOR SHORT TERM RENTALS ON 20 LOTS (CUP2021-06)
       Steve Burton, of the County Planning Division, stated that this Planned Residential Unit Development was just south
       of the Wolf Creek Resort in the FR-3 Zone where 35 single-family lots were platted. Blue Rock Builders, the
       applicant, was present. The request was to amend the conditional use permit, specifically the house types (approved
       in the late 1990s-early 2000s) on 20 lots. In addition, the applicant would like to amend the architecture/color, the
       building height to 45 feet, and the ability to have short-term rentals which are listed as a conditional use.
       Mr. Burton said that the current ordinance allows a PRUD developer to obtain an overall approval for short term
       rentals. Staff presented this proposal to the Ogden Valley Planning Commission with a recommendation for
       approval. On March 23, 2021 the Planning Commission unanimously recommended approval of the Mountain
       Rustic and Mountain Craftsman home designs with earth tone colors, and not white, and recommended denial of: a
       40-foot height limit; of the Mountain Modern design (which has never been allowed); and the request for short term
       rentals. Their recommendation was based on the following findings: the recommendation for denial of the 40-foot
       height limit was based on the fact that the area homes have an average height of 35 feet; the denial of the mountain
       modern house type and white as a house color was that it did not fit in with the surrounding area house types; and
       the denial of short term rentals was based on the fact that the Planning Commission felt the development’s private
       roads were too narrow and would cause safety issues. After the Planning Commission’s recommendation, staff
       considered their concerns and recommended application approval and that the following were reasonable conditions:
       1) Average building height cannot exceed 35 feet, 2) A notice must be recorded to each of the lots in this amendment,
       indicating which changes were made as part of this approval and must be prepared and approved by the Planning
       Division prior to receiving the conditional use permit amendment, 3) That the color white is not allowed as a primary
       house color, 4) That the Mountain Modern house type is not allowed, 5) That ‘no parking’ signs be placed along the
       private street, in front of each unit included as part of this application, and 6) That 2 on-site parking spaces be
       provided per unit, either through a dwelling’s garage or driveway. If a dwelling does not have sufficient driveway
       area, the 2-car garage shall be used as the two parking spaces.
       During previous review, Commissioner Jenkins had believed that there should be “no parking” signs, that the
       asphalt was wide enough, but if they were going to rent they needed the spaces on the property for the renters,
       thus the need for two parking spaces, and that boats, snowmobiles, trailers, etc., could not be on the road. He did
       not necessarily like denying the color white. Mr. Burton said that staff tried to include those conditions based on
       the Planning Commission’s considerations. He had reviewed those with the developers beforehand and they were
       agreeable to them. The Planning Commission felt that the color white was too bright, that it did not fit in.

         Christopher Crockett, Deputy County Attorney, addressed Commissioner Froerer’s question regarding HB0409
         stating that conditional use permit considerations had to be based on objective standards, and it would have to
         relate to a provision in the county’s ordinance governing conditionals uses in order to maintain objectivity. Mr.
         Burton stated that a consideration of the conditional use permit was to look at how development would tie-in to
         the surrounding area. Commissioner Froerer said that the Wolf Creek area was currently zoned for short term
         rentals, that he didn’t feel the Planning Commission should chime in on public safety issues, and that the fire
         department had no issues with this item. Mr. Burton agreed; he had spoken with the Fire Marshall last week who
         indicated having no issues as long as there were “no parking” signs. Mr. Crockett read from Utah Code §17-27a-
         506(2)(a) which stated that a land use authority shall approve a conditional use if reasonable conditions are
         proposed, or can be imposed, to mitigate the reasonably anticipated detrimental effects of the proposed use in
         accordance with applicable standards. In this case the county consulted with the fire department, there will be
         “no parking” signs, and the county will rely on its code enforcement.
         Commissioner Froerer moved to accept staff’s recommendations 1-6 listed above for approval of the PRUD
         amendment for house types for single family lots within the Village at Wolf Creek Development, and approval
         for short term rentals on 20 lots; Commissioner Jenkins seconded.
