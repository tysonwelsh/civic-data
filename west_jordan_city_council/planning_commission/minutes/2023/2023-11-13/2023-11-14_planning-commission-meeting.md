4. Highlands South Lot 5 Pad B; 7862 South 5600 West; Preliminary Site Plan and
Conditional Use Permit for Restaurant, Fast Food; SC-2 Zone; Peterson Development
Co., LLC/Barrett Peterson (applicant) [#31298, 31335; parcel 20-35-226-012]

Jeff Seaman, Peterson Development, explained that this is a retail pad in front of Home Depot on Lot 5

that faces 5600 West. He pointed out the drive aisles and access points. He said there has been a lot of

interest in this area, but he did not know which tenant was going into this building.

Tayler Jensen gave an overview of the item for a preliminary site plan and conditional use permit for

fast food, with a drive through for a two-tenant building. The existing access drives to the north and

west were planned to come in with Lot 3, so any approvals tonight will be conditioned on either Lot 3

building the accesses or the accesses be shown on Lot 5 construction plans. All lot and bulk standards

are met. The Design Review Committee reviewed the elevations and recommended approval.

Based on the analysis contained in the staff report, staff recommended that the Planning Commission

approve the Preliminary Site Plan and the Conditional Use Permit for a Restaurant, Fast Food

(General) for Highlands South Lot 5 Pad B, a two (2) tenant commercial building located on

approximately 1.174 acres at 7862 South 5600 West, with the conditions and requirements of

approval listed in the staff report.

Conditions of Approval

Staff recommended the access to Lot 5 be addressed in a condition to either show the access on the

construction plans for Lot 5, or that they are constructed with Lot 3 prior to construction of Lot 5.

If the Planning Commission disagrees with the Staff's analysis and/or identifies any reasonably

anticipated detrimental effects of this use, reasonable conditions should be identified, which, if

imposed, would reasonably relate to, and substantially mitigate each of the reasonably anticipated

detrimental effects of the use, and which would comply with existing law. Such information should

be clearly included in any motion made by and subsequent decision of the Planning Commission

relative to the Application. Any Planning Commission decision will supersede any conflicting analysis

or opinions of the staff stated in the report or otherwise presented to the Planning Commission.

Commissioner Richardson was concerned with the added traffic this will present on 7800 South. She

asked for an update on when the road will be expanded. She also wondered if there were plans for a

signal on 7800 South.

Mayor Burton gave an update on the plans to widen 7800 South in this area, which will be five lanes

from the roundabout to U-111. The project will start next spring with the sewer replacement and then

the road widening. He offered to provide a copy of the plan.

Commission Chair Allen opened the public hearing.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

Commissioner Richardson said she would vote against the project because 7800 South needs to be

expanded before additional development is approved in the area.

5. Carroh Way Retail; 2877 West 7800 South; Preliminary Site Plan and Administrative

Conditional Use Permit for Restaurant, Fast Food; SC-2 Zone; Iron Horse Real
Estate/Tate Baxter (applicant) [#30728, 30729; 21-33-127-100]

Tate Baxter, Iron Horse Real Estate, said they have been working with staff on the proposal to develop

a retail pad for some time. It is a two-tenant building, with each having a drive-through lane. He

explained traffic circulation. He provided the following answers to questions from the Commission.

There was not a location with this particular layout that he could think of, but this new style is being

developed. There will be clear directional signage, and the lanes will be striped and there will be no

hard barrier to separate them. He provided an additional explanation on how the traffic will circulate
and merge at the exit.

The Commission mentioned some concerns with the potential vehicle conflicts with the two lanes,

especially when customers are first getting used to the layout. Regarding the question about access

from 7800 South, Mr. Baxter stated that UDOT and West Jordan would not even allow an exit slip lane
access. This was because the distance from Carroh Way to a new access would not meet the code for
spacing.

our Master Transportation Plan and UDOT regulations. This commercial pad is part of the Cottages at

Applecross Subdivision and access has always been intended to come off of Carroh Way rather than

7800 South. He explained the driveways, which can be confusing at first. Tenant one’s drive lane has

six stacking spaces, which meets the requirement. Tenant two has a longer stacking area and pick up is

on the north side of the building. Ten stalls are required and 16 are shown in addition to the stacking
lanes, which are also considered parking. The building is quite small and only one tenant has an inside

Commissioner Allen asked if it were true that it is not the city’s responsibility to enact or enforce
CC&Rs of the HOA.

Duncan Murray said the city does not get involved with enforcing CC&R’s unless they conflict with
city regulations. He reminded the commission that approval of the site plan is based on compliance
with code provisions. A conditional use permit is a little different as the Commission should identify
any possible detrimental effects and ways to find mitigating conditions. If there are no conditions that
adequately address the concerns, then there are occasions to deny based on the intensity of the use. For
example, there has been a lot of testimony tonight regarding Criteria C. On C3 the site circulation
potentially might not work. The public comment seems to be focused more on what traffic will do on
Carroh Way combined with the residential traffic and not with stacking on the site. C4 talks about
access to public streets and highways and whether the residential traffic has the ability get to the main
street. C6 asks will this development unreasonably interfere with the lawful use of properties within
the vicinity or does the testimony show it will slow down the flow even more. C8 deals with public
health, safety, and welfare and the ideas presented are that maybe people will take risks to get past the
impasse and if that would cause more accidents. If the Commission identifies detrimental effects, then
what conditions could mitigate them. They could consider if the site layout could change, maybe there
is more professional information needed, and what are the detrimental effects and what are the
appropriate conditions, but the decision should be based on the criteria.
