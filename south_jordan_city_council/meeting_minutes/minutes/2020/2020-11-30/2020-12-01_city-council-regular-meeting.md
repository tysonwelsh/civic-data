         E. Mayor and Council Reports

Council Member McGuire said last night’s Light The Night event was great and very organized.
There was a great turnout. Mayor Ramsey concurred. The turnout was amazing. The residents
loved it and appreciated it.

Council Member Shelton said he met with the leadership of the Western Growth Coalition. They
had a meeting that included representatives from Wasatch Front Regional Council and UDOT to
help them with the Resolution where they are seeking funding for transportation projects on the
west side of Salt Lake County. They will also have UTA review that Resolution. That Resolution
will likely be brought to the City Council for a vote the first meeting in January.

Council Member Harris said he and Council Member Marlor judged the city gingerbread house
contest. He said there was a lot of time and effort from the residents to put those together.

Council Member Zander said the Light the Night event was well done by staff. It was great to
see the City Council in person. She said she and her husband attended an event called
Thanksgiving Hero’s and they delivered meals to families in need. She expressed appreciation to
people in the community that give back and organize such events.

Council Member Marlor arrived at this point in the meeting.

Council Member Marlor said they did not have any Architectural Review Committee meetings
the last two weeks. He said he and Council Member Harris judged the gingerbread houses last
night. It was an enjoyable event and he is amazed at the professionalism and fun people had
putting those together.

Mayor Ramsey said she attends a lot of meetings regularly. She said she was asked to speak to
some students at BYU during the Women in Politics event. She said the Daybreak Community
Association had their annual meeting. She said Chief Carr did a video for that; she did a video
for that as well. She reported on the Council of Governments meeting. In January, her term as
chair should be up. She said the southwest Mayors met on the visioning project. She said she
appreciates the effort and unity going into that project. They are trying to determine the best plan
for options out here. They anticipate having a good initial report before the legislative session
starts. She said they hope to move the needle once the legislature sees what the needs are in this
area. She said she is almost finished with her project for the Cox transition team. She said that
has taken a lot of time, but it was a privilege to visit with so many people.

         H. Public Hearing: Ordinance 2020-15, amending Chapter 10.20.010 of the South
            Jordan Municipal City Code regarding parking on South Jordan City roads during
            snowstorms. (By Public Works Director, Jason Rasmussen)

Public Works Director Rasmussen reviewed the background information on this item. He
reviewed a prepared presentation (Attachment A).

Mayor Ramsey said making the Ordinance easier to remember and easier to enforce will be
helpful.

Mayor Ramsey opened the public hearing. There were no comments. She closed the public
hearing.

Council Member Zander clarified that during a snow event, people can park on the even
numbered side of the street. Mr. Rasmussen said yes, and he noted that the even numbered side
of the street is the south or west side of each road. Council Member Zander said on Pale Moon
Drive, the even side is a north side and there is no parking allowed that side. Mr. Rasmussen said
if the even numbered side is a no parking zone, it is okay to park on the other side of the road.
Council Member Zander clarified that the no parking zone trumps the snow ordinance in that
case. Mr. Rasmussen said yes.

Council Member Zander said she feels the Ordinance is well written and they have thought
through the concerns.

Council Member Marlor said this is well written. He asked when the survey was done, did
people understand or did they ask a lot of questions? Mr. Rasmussen said one main takeaway

         I. Public Hearing: Resolution R2020-54, amending the 2019-2020 Annual Action Plan
            for CDBG-CV funds. (By CDBG Coordinator and Assistant to the City Manager,
            Katie Olson)

CDBG Coordinator and Assistant to the City Manager Katie Olsen reviewed a prepared
presentation (Attachment B).

Mayor Ramsey opened the public hearing.

Ms. Olson read a statement from Peggy Daniels from South Valley Sanctuary (Attachment C).

Mayor Ramsey closed the public hearing.

Council Member Zander said she loves hearing about the groups that support those in need. She
thanked staff for being thoughtful, thorough, and professional in making this selection.

Council Member Zander said in the survey with the residents, one thing that was exposed is how
many residents don’t know where to go for help. That was alarming to her. There is an increase
in residents using those services. What can the city do to get that information to the residents?
Do they put it on the website or social media? What is the plan? Ms. Olson concurred that the
numbers were alarming. She said the CDBG website lists the providers at the top. They will also
be putting an article in the FOCUS. She said they will be adding more social campaigns and
spotlighting their services. They will also be giving information to the service center.

Council Member Zander recommended they get information out there on social media. Ms. Van
Cleave said there are some difficulties with CDBG items. Sometimes it is hard to be in
communication with low to moderate income individuals. They are not necessarily following the
city’s social media. They also may not be getting the mailers. They are finding it is harder in
high density housing. They have some ideas to work through that. Right now, they are working
to use multiple channels.

Council Member Zander recommended they do something in the month of December. It will be a
hard month for some people.

CM Whatcott said it is also the responsibility of the organizations to promote themselves. He
said they are happy to help. He understands these organizations are not trying to spend their
resources on advertising. Mayor Ramsey said they also run lean, in terms of staff.

aware. Often times, the police and fire are in contact with the individuals needing care because of
the service they provide. That may be more effective than social media. Police Chief Carr also
recommended that they educate the victim advocates.

Council Member Shelton recommended they put together a card that has the contact information
for the different services that they can hand out. Mayor Ramsey concurred.

Mayor Ramsey recommended that they put that card in the Welcome to South Jordan packets
done by the Chamber of Commerce. She also recommended it be listed on a community
resources page on the city’s website. Mr. Preece said he would work on that.

Council Member Zander said Daybreak holds socials for new residents. If the City came up with
a card, it could be handed out at that event.

Council Member Marlor made a motion to approve Resolution R2020-54. Council Member
Zander seconded the motion. Roll call vote. The vote was unanimous in favor.

South Jordan City Council,



South Valley Sanctuary would like to thank Katie Olsen and the CDBG Committee for recommending us
for funding. We would like to also thank the South Jordan City Council for its continued support.

South Valley Sanctuary has remained opened and continues to provide essential services for survivors of
domestic violence during this pandemic. The COVID-19 pandemic presents a unique circumstance where
the many jobs that are not typically considered hazardous have suddenly become very dangerous for
the workers. This includes many of the positions at South Valley Sanctuary where staff are at high risk of
exposure to the virus while at work.

During 2020, South Valley Sanctuary has seen a 25% increase in the number of South Jordan residents
that have accessed our crisis shelter plus a 150% increase in the number of South Jordan residents that
we have placed in hotels. We have seen a 25% increase in the number residents that have received
homeless services. Stay-at-home orders, intended to protect the public and prevent the widespread of
COVID-19, left many domestic violence victims trapped with their abusers. Domestic Violence survivors
face the difficult choice between staying with or returning to their abusers or becoming homeless
because they can’t find or afford safe housing. Women experiencing domestic violence are often left
homeless despite their ability to escape threatening situations.

The funds that South Jordan provides will help to cover funding gaps created by COVID-19.

Thank you for making domestic violence survivors a funding priority.

Sincerely,

Peggy Daniel

Grants Administrator
