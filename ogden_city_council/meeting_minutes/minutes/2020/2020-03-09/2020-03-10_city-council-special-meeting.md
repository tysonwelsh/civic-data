          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed amendments to the Fiscal
Year (FY) 2020-2024 Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the proposed
amendment is to incorporate a new project into the current plan: Airport AR093 - N01th East Fire Flow Improvements. This
project is intended to improve water facilities at the Ogden Hinckley Airport to ensure adequate fire protection for future
development. Prospective developments near the Airpmt are anticipated to result in up to 150,000 square feet in new facilities
within the Northeast Commercial Aerospace Project Area. This includes Phase 1 of the Borsight project- a 30,000 square foot
hangar and office facility that are anticipated to expand local employment opportunities.
         An additional memo from the Comptroller regarding the proposed budget amendment associated with the CIP
amendment came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the proposal is to transfer $250,000 from airport parking

improvement funds for airport water systems engineering and design to ensure adequate water flow for fire suppression needs.
The project scope is encompassed in CIP project Airport AR093 - North East Fire Flow Improvements; this CIP project is

          Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo regarding the proposed CIP amendment, noting the Planning
Commission considered the matter in December 2019 and January 2020 and voted unanimously to recommend approval of the
incorporation of the project in the CIP based upon the following findings:
               • Project is consistent with the City's General Plan;
               • Protects the safety and integrity of the airpmt; and
               • Allows improvement and expansion of the airport.
          Comptroller Stout summarized the memo regarding the proposed budget amendment. She noted the funding will take
place within the CIP fund as money is being transfened from the project intended to facilitate parking improvements at the
Airport. The funding will provide for the design of the fire flow infrastructure at the airport as outlined in the CIP project scope.
The action will increase the total City budget by $250,000. She presented the proposed project scope as well as an aerial
photograph orienting the Council to the proposed location of the water tank.
          Council member White asked if the $250,000 project for parking improvements at the Airport being eliminated as a
result of the proposed CIP amendment or if it is simply being delayed for funding in future years. Ms. Stout replied that will be
determined upon completion of the master plan for the Airport which will have capital recommendations for the Airport. Council
member White then asked why Airport CIP projects are funded through the City's General Fund. Ms. Stout responded that is
because the Airport cmTently cannot sustain operational costs. The City Council has the authority to support transfers from the
General Fund to an enterprise fund. Council member White asked why the funding is not coming from the City's utility enterprise
funds given the project is related to fire flow improvements. Ms. Stout answered the project will only provide fire flow for one
specific area. Enterprise funds are an optional funding source, but Administration is also evaluating the funding associated with
CIP project CD086, which includes funding for infill and development projects that may not be fully utilized. City Attorney
Williams added that the tank is not a water delivery system in the sense that it is not providing water services to enterprise
customers; therefore, it is not necessary for the project to be funded through the City's Water Utility Division.
          Council member Nadolski asked if approval of the cunent CIP and budget amendment proposals will make it impossible
to use water enterprise funds as a future funding source. Mr. Williams replied that if the tank were fashioned for multi-use, it may
be possible to utilize water funds. If the tank is used for water storage at the Airport that will benefit the entire water system, the
use of enterprise funds could be considered. He added that is yet to be determined. The primary function of the tank is
aeronautical at this point. Council Policy Analyst Mabey added that the $250,000 budget amendment is intended to fund the study
and design for the tank project. Future budget considerations will relate to the actual construction of the tank. Ms. Stout stated that
is conect.
          Council member Nadolski expressed concern about the fact that the CIP project that is being defunded had been carefully
considered during a project prioritization process and budget development process. The new project, AR093, has not received that
same level of attention. Ms. Stout stated that is conect; Administration felt that due to the implications of the project, it was
appropriate to consider it outside of the normal prioritization and budgeting processes.

Mayor Comments
         Mayor Caldwell thanked everyone for their comments. He is very concerned about diversity issues in the City and much
progress has been made by way of creating a Diversity Commission and a position of Diversity Affairs Officer for the City.
Sometimes it can be difficult to engage people to participate and he hopes that the efforts of the City will make it possible to better
address those difficulties. However, the Marshall White Center is a priority in the City. A one-year CIP funding recommendation
of$90,000 is not reflective of the fmancial contribution the City has made to the Center in the last few years. He stated the CIP is
not the budget and there will be continued discussions about other funding opportunities for the Center. He thanked so many who
have spoke tonight that have done great work for the City and stated he respects the input of all residents and he is committed to
work hard to address their concerns.

Council member Comments
          Council member White said it is important for residents to understand that the CIP is not the foundation of the City's
budget; rather, it is a mechanism to provide the community with detailed plans about long term capital expenditure needs. Those
expenditures are for City public infrastructure, such as streets, transportation, and utility projects; however, the City can pursue
other funding mechanisms outside the CIP Plan. The City has anywhere from $75 to $80 million in CIP projects and it is
impossible to fund all those projects at one time. That is why Administration works to prioritize projects and program them into a
five-year plan.
          Council member Nadolski stated that he does not want discussion about City processes to be interpreted as dismissal of
the concerns of the residents about their perceived community needs. The CIP is not the foundation for the budget, but it is true
that the Plan includes just $90,000 for the Center when everyone knows that the actual need is millions of dollars. It is necessary
for City leaders to be mindful about investing in things that people care deeply about. The reason that people are emotional about
the Marshall White Center is that they have been asking for answers and the City has failed to put a plan in place that
communicates to them the City's planned investment and project time lines. He does not want to label people as emotional because
it can be interpreted as dismissive of their feelings. He added that he does not fault the community for not clearly understanding
the difference between the CIP and the budget or for understanding the process the City follows to develop plans. He is very
frustrated that the City cannot accelerate certain processes to provide the community and the Council with answers about the
Marshall White Center. He wants to be transparent and provide actual fmancial data about what it will cost to provide the things
many residents have requested. He concluded that the concept oflocating a YMCA in Ogden has been discussed, but there are no
clear answers about whether that will come to fruition.
