 14.           Ordinance 2021-46 amending Provo City Code regarding Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs) and their
               development standards. Citywide Application. (PLOTA20210345) (3:23:09)

         Motion:           An implied motion to adopt Ordinance 2021-46, as currently constituted, has been made
                           by council rule.

John Magness, Council Analyst, provided update to ordinance from previous meeting.

Brian Jones read from HB82. He explained the provisions written in the bill and emphasized that cities may prohibit
ADU’s in a certain percentage of the city in primary residential areas. He also asked the council if they thought it
would be helpful to view a presentation that was made during the work meeting prior to taking public comment.

Councilor Shipley responded that the presentation would not be helpful.

Councilor Hoban added that the decision was made in the work meeting that a citywide application will not be
sought. He said they needed to determine where they will be allowed/disallowed and what the occupancy will be.

Councilor Fillmore asked Brian if there were any laws that would prohibit the city to require all ADU’s to be owner
occupied.

Brian responded that there was not. He also talked about off street parking requirements.

Councilor Harding said it’s important to keep in mind the tools that the city has including the state law that
provides increased penalties for individuals who violate these ordinances.

Chair Sewell opened public comment.

Linda Magelby of Provo urged council to prohibit ADUs to the full 67%. She stated she hopes the council will be fair
when determining where the ADUs will be allowed.

Robert Todd of Provo suggested that council allow neighborhoods to help make decision. He said he felt it was
important particularly because individuals in each neighborhood chose that neighborhood for a reason.

Paul Payson of Provo discussed why his family chose to move to Provo from Colorado instead of Orem.

David Wright of Provo addressed fears that some residents had regarding traffic and parking.

Grant Pace of Provo discussed why his family chose Provo to live. He said his family had reservations when
selecting Provo until they found an HOA which prohibited ADU in their neighborhood. He urged Council to focus
less on percentage and more on preferences of residences.

Austin Taylor of Provo expressed his support of a citywide application of ADUs due to housing shortages.

Celest Kennard of Provo asked Council to be cognoscente of infrastructure of neighborhoods. She expressed her

David Magelby of Provo read part of letter from Legislator Thurston and emphasized that the state is not requiring

Dillon Sindrich of Provo said he wants to stay in Provo after graduating from BYU. He said he is in full support of a
citywide ADU allowance due to housing shortage. He stated it would provide more affordable housing in Provo and
would make it easier for younger individuals and families to move to city. He also mentioned it would increase tax
revenue if ADU’s were allowed.

Cathy Giles of Provo expressed her biggest concern would be parking. She said there would need to be a way to
enforce and penalize individuals who violate parking policies.

Linda Crandall of Provo stated there are a lot of apartments and townhomes being built and said the single-family
homes in her neighborhood is being outnumbered them. She also said Provo has been notorious for not enforcing
rental laws and thinks it would become a worse problem if ADUs were allowed.

Peter Christensen, representing the Utah Central Association of Realtors, recommended citywide application of
ADUs. He stated that one of the ways the city can help with affordable housing is to allow ADUs. He said if applied
citywide, the whole city would not participate and stated it would be harder to manage if it was only applied to
certain areas.

Alan Swenson of Provo said his neighborhood was not designed for ADUs. He said there needs to be recognition of
what limitations there are in each neighborhood.

Austin Harvey of Provo voiced his support of wider adoption of ADUs.

David Harman, real estate broker in Utah County, said he lives in a neighborhood with a few ADUs and does not
see the problem. He stated ADUs help younger people qualify to buy a home and that many residents count on the
income to pay mortgage. He voiced the value of neighborhood is not going to go down but rather ADUs allow for
value to go up. He also reminded that many homes won’t qualify for allowance of ADU due to not having available
parking.

Michal Middleton of Provo mentioned he purchased a home in a single-family neighborhood for a reason and does
not want to live anywhere else. He urged the council to study each neighborhood and decide where ADUs would
be best.

Cash Allred of Provo voiced feedback to concerns people have.

James Spears of Provo said residents have different needs and wants when purchasing a home and an expectation
of what the neighborhood would be like. He said not allowing neighborhoods to help decide would be unfair to the
homeowners. He also asked that there be more enforcement in the city.
