                                  PLANNING COMMISSION MINUTES
                                         Meeting of November 18, 2021
                            Logan City Council Chambers (290 N 100 W Logan, UT 84321) www.loganutah.org

Minutes of the meeting for the Logan City Planning Commission convened in regular session on
Thursday, November 18, 2021, Chair Dickinson called the meeting to order at 5:30 p.m.

Commissioners Present: Regina Dickinson, Sandi Goodlander, Roylan Croshaw, Ken Heare, David
Lewis, Jessica Lucero, Jordy Guth

Commissioners Excused:

Staff Present: Russ Holley, Mike DeSimone, Tanya Rice, Ben Anderson, Mohamud Abdullahi,
Kymber Housley, Tom Dickinson, Jeannie Simmonds (City Council)

Commissioner Croshaw moved to approve the minutes from the October 28, 2021 meeting as
submitted. Motion seconded by Commissioner Guth. The motion was approved unanimously.

PUBLIC HEARING

 PC 21-059 Brickyard -Continued from the October 28, 2021 meeting. [Design Review
 Permit] Jake Thompson/Direct Homes Inc, authorized agent/owner, request a design review
 permit to build a new 37,789 SF building, with 30 residential units, 2 commercial units, and
 underground parking structure, located on .8 acres at approximately 50 West 800 North in
 the Mixed Use (MU) zone; TIN 05-048-0018. (Bridger Neighborhood)

STAFF: T. Rice reviewed the request for a design review permit to construct a 1,500 SF single-
story commercial building and a 10,080 SF, 4-story residential apartment building with 24, 3-
bedroom units on a .80-acre parcel located at approximately 800 North 50 West that was rezoned
from Commercial to Mixed Use in March 2021. Between the two buildings, this project meets the
required building frontage, setbacks, lot coverage, and connectivity.
The plan shows a total of 53 parking stalls (surface and covered). However, parking lot
specifications and landscaping requirements will eliminate four (4) stalls thereby reducing the
proposed number of 53 down to 49 parking stalls. The proponent is required to address this issue
by either reducing the number of units, reducing the number of bedrooms, or reconfiguring the
parking to meet the minimum parking requirements. This is addressed in condition # 3. 36 bike
stallsare required for this project in the MU zone and is addressed in condition # 5.
A 5-foot perimeter landscape border needs to be added to the parking lot along 50 West. Two of
the parking isles do not have the required landscaped area at the end. The landscaping is
addressed in condition # 7.
Commissioner Guth asked about the approval of their revised landscape/parking plan. Staff
explained that staff reviews landscaping plans and will review the parking plan for code compliance
at the time of building permit application.
Commissioner Dickinson asked for clarification on condition number three where it says parking
allowance granted by staff. T Rice said that parking approvals are granted at the building permit
phase, based on the final design.


M. DeSimone said if there would have been an alternate parking plan submitted the committee
would then have some discretion. There was a brief discussion that the last part of condition three,
parking allowance granted by staff, should be removed.

PROPONENT: Joe Beck, Architect, said that they will be able to meet the conditions without
drastically changing their proposed plan.

PUBLIC: Kevin Forth talked about the concern with the added use of our natural resources from
this project, specifically water, and how it should be considered when approving these multi-unit
complexes.

Todd Peterson asked if there would be roadside parking along 50 West and if this apartment will
be high-end units or affordable housing? M. DeSimone said that the Planning Commission’s
decision is not based on those items.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Croshaw asked about the retaining wall that runs along the
northeast side of the property. Joe Beck, the project architect, said that the retaining wall is on the
neighboring property, they are not planning on impacting the wall. If the wall is dilapidated, they
will negotiate repairs with the neighboring property owner. There could also be room for some
landscaping in front of the retaining wall.

Commissioner Dickinson asked about the parking along 50 West and if the curb will be painted
red. Tom Dickinson, City Engineer, said that they will need to determine if the road is wide enough
for parking. Kymber Housley, City Attorney, said that once the property is developed the impacts
can be assessed, and then it can be determined if a red curb is necessary.

Commissioner Guth asked about continuing the project to come back with their updated parking
and landscaping plan. M. DeSimone said that the planning commission doesn’t usually see the
landscaping plan. Staff will approve the updated plan or determine if the change is significant
enough to return to Planning Commission.

Commissioner Goodlander stated that this is a beautiful building and she is comfortable approving
the project with the existing conditions.

Commissioner Lucero mentioned adding a condition that includes landscaping around the retaining
wall. M. Desimone said that the landscaping is already a condition.

 PC 21-064 Cache County Food Pantry -Continued from the October 28, 2021 meeting.
 [Design Review Permit] Courtney Wallace/Cache Community Food Pantry, Inc., authorized
 agent/owner, request a design review permit to build a new 9240 SF building to the west of
 their existing building to use for additional food storage. The project is located at
 approximately 359 South Main Street in the Commercial (COM) zone; TIN 02-052-0029; -
 0001. (Ellis Neighborhood)

STAFF: R. Holley reviewed the Food Pantry’s updated plan for their new 9,240 SF warehouse
building for additional food storage and a clean room for food preparation. The project is located
west of the existing food pantry building at 359 South Main street. Two existing sheds are
proposed for demolition as well as a single-family home on 400 South that will be demolished to
create the space for the new driveway and parking.
The updated plan provides an extra 10-foot buffer between the new driveway and the home to the
west and includes a 6-foot chain link fence with privacy slats. Staff feels like the chain link fence
should be upgraded to a more solid fence material. There is also a 34-foot wide Xeriscape buffer
area next to the approach on 400 South, that will help screen the parking lot and the new building.
The survey done on the church parking lot when service was in session determined that there
were a total of 45-50 cars parked in the main shared parking lot and back where the new
warehouse is proposed. The Food Pantry serves 165 families a week, in shifts of 33 families per
shift, and 10 employees. Based on these numbers, staff feels that the 49 proposed parking stalls
are sufficient, and recommends approval of the parking plan.
All the concerns brought up by the public comments from the last meeting have been addressed
in the conditions.
Commissioner Croshaw asked where the retention pond is located and how the shared parking
would work if the food pantry moved. R. Holley said that the retention pond has to be behind the
project and screened or located underground with landscaping on top. If the food pantry was sold
the easements would run with the property.
Commissioner Dickinson asked if condition #18 could be removed because it repeats condition #4
requiring a fence.
Commissioner Guth asked if the tree is removed in the park strip on 400 South, will be replaced.
R. Holley said that condition 10a states that a tree will need to be planted in that park strip.

PROPONENT: Justin Campbell, Architect on the project, stated that this new proposal is an
appropriate plan for this commercial property next to a single-family home. He asked if a solid
fence is required by the code or if it is just a recommendation. R. Holley stated that when a
parking lot abuts a residential property, there is a buffering requirement that includes a solid
feature. What is not clear in the code is if a chain-link fence with slats is considered solid. Justin
Campbell stated that a chain link with added slats is more of an opaque fence than transparent,
plus with the vegetation that will be planted, he feels this meets the intent of the code.

PUBLIC: Kevin Forth, who lives next to the project, said that the efforts that have been made to
address their concerns are more than fair.

John Belles, Pastor of the Alpine Church, said that the additional parking will be a blessing and will
lessen the parking along the street.


Ruth Forth, the neighbor to the west, said she is still concerned about the location of their
dumpsters. She asked what type of xeriscaping they are planning to put in along the driveway
buffer, will it grow tall enough to block the view of the new building. She said they measured the
width of 400 South and it is 36’ wide from curb to curb, and wonders if that will be wide enough for
a semi to turn into the driveway. There will be a total of 12 trees removed for this project, will they
be replacing all 12 trees.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Dickinson stated that there is a requirement for a landscaping plan,

There was discussion about the fence requirement and if a chain-link fence with slats should be
considered a solid fence.

Croshaw asked about condition # 11 stating that dumpsters need to be screened. R. Holley said

 PC 21-066 Ethan’s Honest Automotive Service and Repair Zone Change [Zone Change]
 Jason Thompson/Miller Real Estate Enterprises LLC., authorized agent/owner, request a
 zone change of a 2-acre lot from Commercial (COM) to Commercial Services (CS) to build a
 new 8,000-11,000 SF automotive service and repair shop. The project is located at 175
 West 1950 North in the Commercial (COM) zone; TIN 04-232-4275. (Bridger Neighborhood)


STAFF: T. Rice reviewed the request for a zone change from commercial to commercial services
on a 2-acre vacant lot located at 175 West 1950 North for an automotive repair shop. The rezone
is being requested because some of the services the auto repair shop offers are not permitted in
the commercial zone, such as long-term storage for extended days of automotive services. The
surrounding zones are residential to the west and commercial to the south and the east. To the
north of this property is North Logan City. The future land use map shows this area as commercial.
The commercial zone is intended for retail services near mass transit, hospitality services, and
same-day services. The commercial services zone is intended to fill a need between commercial
and industrial land uses, it allows for extended days of service and a wider range of repairs and
services. The difference between the proposed project and the auto dealership directly to the east
is that the dealership's focus is primarily on auto sales, it is oriented on Main Street, it is on a much
larger lot, and doesn’t abut residential properties.

Commissioner Lucero asked what the zone is directly to the north, in North Logan. M. DeSimone
said he believes it is commercial then residential zoning further north.

PROPONENT: No comments from the proponent.

PUBLIC: No public comments were made.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Croshaw said that this would be considered spot zoning and that
does not make sense. Lucero said she agreed, and she is not in favor of the rezone.


MOTION: Commissioner Lucero moved to forward a recommendation of denial to the Municipal
Council on a zone change for PC 21-066 with the findings for denial as listed below.
Commissioner Goodlander seconded the motion.

RECOMMENDED FINDINGS FOR DENIAL
1. The Logan City FLUP identifies the area as COM and is not intended for industrial uses.
2. The CS Zoning district allows a wide range of land uses that could be considered incompatible
   with residential land uses.
3. This land is better suited for commercial uses as it is adjacent to commercial and residential
   zones.
4. There is no other CS zoning in the area.
5. Changing the zone from COM to CS is inconsistent with previous planning commission
   decisions in this area.
