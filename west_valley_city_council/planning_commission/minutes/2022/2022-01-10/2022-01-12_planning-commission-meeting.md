ZT-7-2021
West Valley City
A zone text change to define and establish parking standards for townhomes

At the request of the City Council, City staff is proposing an ordinance amendment to define and
establish parking standards for townhomes. As both the Planning Commission and City Council
have reviewed proposals for townhome developments, parking is often discussed and addressed
by development agreement. In all recent development agreements for townhome projects, the
amount of parking required has exceeded the current ordinance requirement of 2 spaces per unit.
For this reason, the Council asked that the ordinance be updated to reflect the higher standards.

The Council’s desire for more parking for townhome developments is driven by several factors.
First, with recent, significant home price appreciation, some families that would have purchased
a single unit dwelling are now moving to a townhome as a more affordable option and single unit
dwellings typically have more parking available. Second, many of the townhomes that have
recently been constructed are just as large if not larger than single unit dwellings. Third,
homeowners often use their garage as storage and park their personal vehicles in the driveway.
Fourth, townhome developments usually incorporate private streets that, unlike public streets,
generally do not allow on-street parking or, in some cases, allow only limited on-street parking.

The proposed ordinance defines a townhome as “a Dwelling Unit in a row of at least three such
units in which each unit has its own front and rear access to the outside, no unit is located over
another unit, and each unit is separated from any other unit by one or more vertical walls.” In
addition to defining the term townhome, the proposed ordinance adds the following standards:

      requires single unit dwellings, townhomes, and twin homes to provide a driveway with
       minimum dimensions of 18 feet by 18 feet,
      requires single unit dwellings, townhomes, and twin homes that are not adjacent to a
       public street with no more than two lanes to provide an additional 0.75 spaces per unit,
      sets a minimum interior dimension of 20 feet by 20 feet for townhome garages, and
      prohibits things like stairs and water heaters from encroaching into the 20 feet by 20 feet
       garage space.

Single unit dwellings and twin homes were added to the first two standards because it is possible
to have single unit dwelling and twin home garages accessed by an alley similar to townhomes
and single unit dwelling and twin home developments can also be served by private streets.

Staff Alternatives:

   2. Continuance.

   3. Denial.

SA-8-2021
Lake Park Place Subdivision – Lot 1 Amended
4099 West Lake Park Boulevard
M & BRP Zones
2 Lots
12.8 Acres

BACKGROUND:

Gary Christiansen is requesting approval to amend lot 1 in the Lake Park Place Subdivision. The
proposed amendment will create an additional building lot within the boundary of what is known
as lot 1. The subdivision consists of 2 lots on 12.8 acres, is zoned BRP (Business/Research Park)
and M (Manufacturing) and is located at approximately 4099 West Lake Park Boulevard.

ISSUES:

The proposed subdivision is being submitted to divide the existing lot 1 into 2 lots. The original
parcel was created in the early days of Lake Park by a meets and bounds subdivision. In 2015, the
parcel was subdivided to create two lots in the Lake Park Place Subdivision. The Desert First
Federal Credit Union built on what is known as lot 2 of that subdivision.

The proposal for this application is to subdivide lot 1 to create an additional building lot. At the
present time, lot 1 is vacant. Lot 1A will be approximately 5.5 acres in size while lot 1B will be
approximately 7.3 acres.
Access to the property will be gained from Lake Park Boulevard and from Parkway Boulevard.
Although at this time, it is only proposed that access come off of Lake Park Boulevard. The
property owner has requested that the City evaluate consideration for full access on Lake Park
Boulevard. This would necessitate the removal and modification to a portion of the existing
median. After review and discussion from the Engineering Division, this request will be granted.

The two lots will share a common access point and will also share in cross access and parking
easements. The original plat referenced a Master Declaration of Easements, Covenants, and
Restrictions that was recorded in February 1996. While looking through this document, the
question of these kinds of easements would be between property owners and is not necessarily
established in the Master Document. The property owner has the option to create these additional
easements by plat or by document. Given that we do not know who will occupy these lots, the
document option is probably the best method.

STAFF ALTERNATIVES:

1.     Approve the Lake Park Place Lot 1 Amended Subdivision subject to a resolution of staff
       and agency comments.

2.     Continue the application in order for the developer to address the Planning Commissions
       concerns.

              would most likely do the cross-access and cross-parking easement in a separate
              document. Mr. Rindlisbacher said that it is his understanding that a cross-access
              easement already exists with the credit union, giving the applicant access to
              Parkway Blvd.

C-22-2021
Valley Fair Mall
3601 S. Constitution Blvd.
C-2 zone, 43.83 acres

The applicant, Vestar, representing the Valley Fair Mall, is requesting conditional use approval
for outdoor events at 3601 S. Constitution Blvd. The zoning for this property is C-2, General
Commercial and the General Plan designation is General Commercial. The surrounding zone is
C-2 and R-4-8.5 to the north, City Center (CC) and R-1-8 to the west, C-2, RM and R-1-8 to the
south, and C-2 and I-215 to the east.

In November 2020 the West Valley City Municipal Code section 7-7-110 was amended to allow
for Outdoor Event Centers as a conditional use in conjunction with a Regional Shopping Mall.
This was also done in coordination with a Business Stabilization and Improvement Grant from
the WVC RDA for plaza/Town Center improvements. This proposal was specifically targeted to
provide “entertainment shopping” which encourages people to come, linger and shop.

The next step in the process is the conditional use application. The submittal outlines the typical
types of events the Mall would coordinate in an effort to achieve the goal and fulfill the
requirements of the matching grant program. Events that are coordinated by outside entities, i.e
car sales, firework stands or carnivals, would still follow the Temporary Use/Special Event
process as outlined in the City Code (7-2-112)

The outdoor events are planned for two specific areas on site. The first is the plaza (Town
Center). This area was specifically designed to host events so it was constructed with a stage,
splash pad and electrical. A sound system was not installed so equipment and speakers are
brought in by the event performers themselves. That equipment will all be located around the
stage and directed towards the mall (east) so the sound is contained within the Town Center area
and in accordance with the Salt Lake Health Department Noise Ordinance. The second location
is the parking lot adjacent to JCPenney which is used on a more limited basis as outlined in the
schedule. These locations are targeted to minimize the disruption of traffic and noise to the
adjacent properties.

All of the events would follow typical mall hours (10:00 a.m. – 9:00 p.m). This also ensures
restroom access inside of the mall for anyone attending events. If additional equipment is
brought to the site that requires approval from the Building or Fire Departments, the Mall
Marketing representatives are required to coordinate that review. Also, if business licenses are
required for any outside vendors that are brought to the site that will coordinated with the Mall
Marketing representatives.


Staff Alternatives


Approval of outdoor events subject to any issues raised at the public hearing as well as the
following conditions:

   1. The outdoor events schedule shall be consistent with what was submitted in Exhibit A. If
      the intensity of programming increases, then it shall be re-evaluated by the Planning
      Commission.

   2. The outdoor events shall only occur in the Town Center and JCPenney lot as outlined in
      Exhibit B.

   3. The outdoor events are subject to the Salt Lake Health Department Noise Regulations.

   4. No external sound equipment shall be used in the JCPenney lot events and all sound
      equipment in the Town Center shall be oriented to the east towards the Valley Fair Mall.

   5. All outdoor events shall occur during typical mall hours (10:00 a.m. – 9:00 p.m.) and
      interior restroom access shall be provided for all events.

   6. Must meet requirements of all affected departments and agencies, including but not
      limited to Building, Fire and Business Licensing.

   7. This use is subject to review upon valid complaint.

C-23-2021
Ken Garff Pole Signs
4770 S 5600 W
M Zone (36.53 Acres)

The applicant, St. Clair Signs, is requesting conditional use approval for two pole signs on the
Ken Garff Automotive Group truck processing center property. The overall site is 36.53 acres
and is zoned M, Manufacturing. The West Valley City General Plan designates this area as light
manufacturing. The surrounding zoning is M to the north, west, and south. The properties to the
east of 5600 West are in Kearns and include an industrial property and single unit dwellings.
Other surrounding land uses include The Ridge Golf Course to the south and west, a
power substation to the west and self-storage and a convenience store to the north.

There are two existing monument-style signs on the property. The applicant is proposing to
remove the existing signs and construct two identical multi-tenant signs. The signs will be 11’
tall and include approximately 114 sq. ft. of sign area. A concrete base will be incorporated on
both signs. The signs are designed more like a monument-style sign, but they are too large to
conform with the City’s monument sign standards, which allows for multi-tenant signs up to 7’
tall and 60 sq. ft. in area.

The property is over 10 acres, which means that pole signage is allowed. The parcel also has
two frontages, and the code allows one pole sign per frontage. The proposed signs will be well
within the code allowance for pole signs, which is 200 sq. ft. and 35’ in height. No additional
pole signage will be allowed on the property.

Title 11 requires that all pole signs be incorporated in a landscaped area that is at least equal to
twice the area of the sign. The proposed signs require 456 sq. ft. of landscaping. The existing
landscaped area around the signs exceeds the minimum requirement, but it is all currently lawn
in that area. Staff recommends that the landscaping in front of the signs be enhanced with
additional live plant material.

Staff Alternatives:

Approval, subject to the resolution of any concerns raised at the public hearing, as well as
the following conditions:

     1. The signs shall be constructed per the approved sign plan.
     2. No additional pole signs are allowed on the property.
     3. The landscaping shall be enhanced in front of the proposed signage by adding
        additional live plant material such as shrubs, perennials, and/or ornamental grasses.
        A landscape plan for this area shall be provided to staff before a building permit is
        issued.
     4. All requirements set forth in Title 11 of the West Valley City Municipal Code
        shall be met.
     5. A building permit shall be obtained for the signs.

Continuance, for reasons determined at the hearing.


Discussion:    Brock Anderson presented the application. He stated that the applicant had

               being considered two separate pole signs because of their placement. Brock said
               that these two identical pole signs would be considered as two separate pole signs,
               and that both are allowed because there are two street frontages.

               Neal Knight, the applicant, said that they would submit a landscape plan to be
               approved before any work on the landscaping is done.

C-24-2021
Moreno Office Building
7011/7041/7055 W Gates
Ave. M Zone (1.31 Acres)

The applicant, Tisha Moreno, is requesting conditional use approval for an office building and
storage area at 7011/7041/7055 W Gates Ave. The parcels are zoned manufacturing (M). The
properties to the north are zoned manufacturing, while all other adjacent properties are zoned
agriculture. The West Valley City General Plan designates all properties along Gates Avenue
as light manufacturing, and all properties to the south as large lot residential. There are towing
and impound yards to the north, a vacant lot to the east, and single unit dwellings to the west
and south.

The applicant operates a construction company, Southwest Exteriors, that specializes in
residential and commercial siding projects. The applicant received conditional use approval
for an office and outside storage area in June of last year (C-13-2021), but then purchased an
additional parcel to the east and successfully re-zoned that property from A to M. With the
extra parcel the applicant has a new proposal and is looking to get conditional use approval
for this concept. The proposal is for a 12,000 sq. ft. two-story office building. The building
will accommodate the office-related portion of the applicant’s growing business, and the
remainder of the building will be leased to other tenants.

The proposal includes three phases. To begin Phase 1, the garage attached to the existing
office building will be removed to allow for enough room to construct the two-story office
building in the center of the site. Construction of the parking lot and installation of the
landscaping on the south and west sides of the building will also begin in Phase 1. Once the
office building is completed Phase 2 will begin, which means the existing office building will
be demolished and the parking and landscaping will be finished in that area. There is an
existing home on the east edge of the site that will not be removed until Phase 3. When the
Moreno’s purchased that parcel they agreed to let the homeowner continue to live in the home,
and the home will not be removed until he vacates the property. Since the entire parking lot
will not be completed until the third phase of the project, the building will not be able to be
fully occupied because there will not be enough parking on the site. The parking for the
project will be evaluated as Tenant Improvement permits are obtained. There is a development
agreement that was recorded when the property was rezoned that outlines requirements and
timing for some of the improvements.
All the provisions of this agreement shall be met.

In the applicant’s original proposal last year there was going to be an outside storage yard for
equipment and supplies. At the time this application was made they were proposing this
storage area to be along the south side of the site, but there were not a lot of details regarding
screening or surfacing provided. Since then, the applicant has indicated that the equipment
and materials will be enclosed in a building. Details on that building have not been provided,
but it will need
to meet all setback and height requirements for the M Zone. If there is any outside storage on
the property, it needs to comply with the requirements in 7-7-119 and meet applicable setback
requirements. The proposed parking lot shall be for customer and employee parking only.
Construction vehicles or equipment cannot be kept in the parking lot.

The new office building will be subject to all the commercial design standards outlined in 7-
11- 300P. The building exterior will primarily be stucco and stone, with accents of fiber
cement or wood siding. Since there is agricultural zoning to the east, west and south, the
building height shall comply with the setback requirements outlined in 7-6-307(2). There is no
height restriction for the structure at the 20’ setback from the north property line since the
building will meet all the commercial design requirements and there are no residential uses to
the north.

The M Zone requires a minimum of 5% landscaping on the site. All landscaping requirements
of Title 7 shall be met. The required parking will be determined by the square footages of the
uses within the two-story building. Parking areas are allowed to have a 10’ setback from the
east and west property lines per the Development Agreement, but there must be a 20’ setback
from the south property line. All other applicable landscaping and screening requirements for
the parking areas shall apply as outlined in 7-9. The east, west, and south sides of the property
shall be screened by a 6’ masonry wall as outlined in 7-6-303(5). The site lighting shall be
installed so it does not negatively impact the adjacent residential uses as described in 7-9-113.
All signage on the property shall meet the requirements set forth in Title 11 of the WVC
Municipal Code.
