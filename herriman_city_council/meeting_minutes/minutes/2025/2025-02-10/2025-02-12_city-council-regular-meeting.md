        2.1. January 2025 City Status Report – Trevor Ram, Management Analyst
      Management Analyst Trevor Ram presented the January 2025 City Status Report and year-
      end summary. He noted updated median sale price numbers and affordable housing unit
      counts from the realtor group, which had increased since Q2 2024.

      Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn commended the Economic Development team for bringing in
      25 new businesses. Councilmember Steven Shields expressed concern that the definition of
      the "affordable housing unit" metric was meaningless without context. City Manager
      Cherpeski explained the numbers received by the realtor group was based on 30% of gross
      monthly income for housing expenses.

      After debate, the Council decided to remove the affordable housing metric from the report
      due to concerns about its clarity and usefulness. Councilmember Teddy Hodges requested
      adding percentages for different housing types to provide more context.

        2.2. Mid Biennial Budget Review and Discussion – Nathan Cherpeski, City Manager; Kyle
             Maurer, Director of Finance and Administrative Services; Trevor Ram, Management
             Analyst
      City Manager Nathan led a mid-biennial budget review and discussion, emphasizing the
      importance of understanding the City's current financial landscape. He provided context on
      revenues and expenses across multiple funds, including the general fund, public safety fund,
      and parks/recreation fund. City Manager Cherpeski highlighted that property taxes and sales
      taxes were the main revenue sources, noting that sales tax collections had doubled since
      2020, a trend which reflected both population growth and increased commercial activity.

      City Manager Cherpeski presented charts depicting historical inflation rates, to highlight how
      the figures impact city finances. He explained the challenge of maintaining purchasing power
      and expressed the city's strategic need to keep up with inflation, while balancing growth. The
      Council debated on various strategies for adjusting property tax rates over time to maintain
      a stable fiscal environment that would accommodate both inflation and growth pressures.

      City Manager Cherpeski outlined several future budget challenges which included addressing
      the need for increased staffing, managing facility costs for police and fire departments, and
February 12, 2025 City Council Meeting Minutes                                        Page 3 of 9


      developing infrastructure to support new growth. He made it clear that a delicate balance
      must be struck between maintaining expected service levels and managing available
      revenues.

      The Council discussed the positive and negative aspects of different financing approaches.
      Councilmember Henderson proposed an innovative approach for managing the city's
      finances, suggesting the allocation of a portion of future sales tax growth to help fund
      increases in public safety costs.

      City Manager Cherpeski noted he would schedule a separate meeting to review the Capital
      Improvement Plan (CIP) in more detail, given its critical role in managing long-term
      infrastructure investments. He also mentioned that the city might need to contemplate the
      inclusion of a Truth in Taxation hearing as part of its budget calendar submission to the
      county. This would only be necessary if the Council anticipated increasing tax rates. The
      Council reached a consensus to include the possibility of Truth in Taxation in their budget
      calendar, even if they ultimately decide not to pursue an increase.


        2.3. Community Service Unit update – Brent Adamson, Herriman Police Department
              Commander
      HPD Commander Brent Adamson provided an update on the Community Service Unit. He
      explained they had reorganized and rebranded the division, combining animal services and
      code enforcement under one unit called Community Services. This would allow for more
      efficient staffing and cross-training between the two areas.

      Commander Adamson discussed the new administrative law judge process for code
      enforcement cases. While it has been effective in gaining compliance, the costs have
      exceeded the initial budget. He requested an additional $5,000 to continue hearings through
      the end of the fiscal year.

      The council debated the costs versus benefits of the administrative law judge approach. They
      acknowledged it was achieving compliance but expressed concern about the ongoing
      expenses. Commander Adamson noted they were considering moving more types of
      violations like animal services and parking to the administrative process in the future.

      Councilmember Hodges moved to temporarily recess the City Council work session at 6:52
      p.m. Councilmember Shields seconded the motion, and all voted aye.

        2.5. Legislative Update
      City Attorney Todd Sheeran provided an update on relevant bills in the state legislature. Key
      bills discussed included:
           • HB 81 on fluoride amendments, which would prohibit adding fluoride to public water
               systems.
           • HB 433 on agritourism building amendments, which raised some concerns about
               exemptions from building codes.
           • HB 355 on critical infrastructure material amendments, which would override local
               zoning for mining activities.
           • HB 368 on local land use amendments, a large omnibus bill with many provisions.
      The council also discussed bills related to artificial intelligence use in law enforcement
      reports and quotas for police activities. They debated the pros and cons of various proposals.

      9. Discussion and Action Items
          9.1. Consideration of Traffic Impact Fees for New Commercial Development – Bryce
               Terry, City Engineer
      City Engineer Bryce Terry presented information on traffic impact fees for a proposed Beto’s
      Mexican restaurant. He explained the City’s process for calculating impact fees based on
      estimated daily trips generated. For this project, a site-specific traffic study resulted in a
      recommended fee of $98,767.

      City Engineer Terry relayed the applicant request for the Council to consider a lower fee,
      noting it was significantly higher than nearby businesses had paid previously. The Council
      debated balancing fair application of fees with economic development goals. The Council
      agreed to have a broader discussion about the impact fee policies at a future meeting, and if
      it was determined the fees would be reduced, a refund could be considered.

          9.2. Consideration to approve an independent consultant agreement for a Flood
                Hazard Mitigation Study – Jonathan Bowers, Public Utilities Engineering Manager
      Public Utilities Engineering Manager Jonathan Bowers presented an agreement with Bowen
      Collins & Associates to conduct a comprehensive flood hazard mitigation study. The study is
      set at a total cost of $399,997.05 and was notably supported by a FEMA grant, covering 75%
      of the expense.

      Manager Bowers explained the study would position Herriman to better prepare for and
      mitigate flood risks through advanced modeling and planning efforts. The Utah Department
      of Emergency Management would serve as intermediaries for the grant funds, earmarked
      under specific conditions requiring adherence to strict scopes of work.

      Manager Bowers also discussed possible alternatives to contracting Bowen Collins &
      Associates, including the potential for soliciting bids from other consultants. However, he
      highlighted Bowen, Collins & Associates' prior involvement in drafting the grant application
      and their familiarity with Herriman's current stormwater infrastructure.

      Councilmember Hodges moved to approve an agreement with Bown, Collins and Associates
      for a total of $399,000 ($99,750 from Herriman City local funds and $299,250 from a FEMA
      Grant) to conduct a Flood Hazard Mitigation Study. Councilmember Henderson seconded
      the motion.

          9.3. Review and consider a Resolution adopting a procurement policy for the City of
               Herriman – Kyle Maurer, Director of Finance and Administrative Services
      Finance Director Kyle Maurer presented a newly developed procurement policy for the city,
      intended to replace the policy currently contained in city code with a more detailed
      document. This comprehensive policy implemented changes such as updated spending
      thresholds for quotes and bids, defined new roles like Purchasing Officer and Purchasing
      Agent, introduced detailed processes for various types of purchases, broadened the list of
      exceptions to competitive bidding, and established a formal protest process for vendors. The
      council expressed their appreciation for the in-depth nature of the policy.

      Councilmember Ohrn moved to approve Resolution R06-2025 adopting a procurement
      policy. Councilmember Henderson second the motion.
