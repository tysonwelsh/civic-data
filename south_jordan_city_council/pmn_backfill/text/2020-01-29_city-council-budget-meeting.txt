                               SOUTH JORDAN CITY
                     CITY COUNCIL SPECIAL BUDGET MEETING #1

                                          January 29, 2020

Present:       Mayor Dawn Ramsey, Council Member Patrick Harris, Council Member Brad
               Marlor, Council Member Jason McGuire, Council Member Don Shelton, Council
               Member Tamara Zander, CM Gary Whatcott, CFO Sunil Naidu, Strategic
               Services Director Don Tingey, ACM Dustin Lewis, City Attorney Ryan Loose,
               HR Director Teresa Cook, City Council Secretary MaryAnn Dean

Mayor Ramsey welcomed everyone present. She excused Council Member McGuire who will be
here shortly.

   A. Meal

The City Council had a brief dinner break.

   B. Invocation: By IT Director, Jon Day

IT Director Day offered the invocation.

   C. 2020-21 City Council Budget Session #1

CM Whatcott introduced Kyrene Gibb from Y2 Analytics. He said staff has spent a lot of time
looking at alternatives for the budget. He said they have a plan to balance the budget, without
raising taxes and they can hire the 11 needed personnel for public safety.

Kyrene Gibb, Y2 Analytics, reviewed the recent resident survey that was conducted. She said the
residents prefer to cut services and expenditures rather than see a tax increase. She said only 26
percent support a tax increase.

Council Member Marlor asked what services are they willing to cut? Ms. Gibb said public safety
and street maintenance are the residents’ highest priority. The lower priority is communications,
community events and programs, and recreation. There was also indication that the services most
favored to be cut is new resident orientation, the South Jordan parade and float, tennis, holiday
lights, and street fence maintenance.

Council Member Harris asked if there were any patterns between the Council Districts? Ms.
Gibb said there is a lot of consistency in terms of priority.

They talked about the timing of the survey relative to opinions regarding increase in property
taxes. Ms. Gibb said she feels if the survey was done at a different time, the 3 to 1 ratio
supporting a reduction in services vs increase in taxes would hold.

Ms. Gibb said if anything in the survey could have been done different, it is that the open ended
feedback could have been guided better.

Council Member Shelton said the residents are educated and well informed. It was noted that
some residents wanted further education on certain issues. Ms. Gibb said in the survey process,
the purpose is to gather information, not disseminate information. CM Whatcott said he feels
many residents were misinformed, based on their comments regarding South Jordan having high
property taxes. He said they should maybe provide a graph where South Jordan sits in the
spectrum of taxes to other communities in Salt Lake County. Many residents said South Jordan
has the highest taxes in the state and it is untrue. Ms. Gibb said she does not feel a graph would
have changed the sentiment regarding taxes.

It was noted that Holladay city is in the process of trying to distribute information about the low
property tax burden. They are in a dire capital needs situation, and it is requiring a lengthy
messaging campaign.

The City Council discussed the upcoming budget.

CFO Naidu said he appreciated meeting with Mayor Ramsey and the City Council individually.
He said if they do not change any service levels, they will be able to manage until the growth
revenue meets with the current level of service. It will require the use of some savings.

Mr. Naidu said they need to staff Fire Station 64 and they need some police personnel. He said in
2019, the revenues were lower than they thought because of the loss of Kennecott revenues. He
said they have to adjust for the loss of those revenues. He said the gap between the revenues and
expenditures this year is covered by savings. He said it is currently hard to fill some positions
because of the low unemployment rate. He said vacant positions might be one area to create
some savings. Another way to create savings is for people to hold off attending training.

CM Whatcott said they did have some savings in operation changes. The last time the city asked
for new money was in 2007. He said operations run flat. Most of the growth is wage inflation.

They discussed the difficulty in all businesses right now finding and keeping employees. CM
Whatcott said it is difficult in public works because they can make more in the private sector in
construction.

They discussed the problem in public safety. Not only are they down employees, but training for
those positions is several months to a year. CM Whatcott said that is problematic in all
government agencies.

Council Member McGuire arrived at this time.

CFO Naidu said the savings will cover the shortfall in 2020. In 2021, they will still be a little
short, but the revenues will increase over time.

CFO Naidu reviewed what the general fund covers: operating expenses, debt service, and CIP
projects. He reviewed funding mechanisms for capital projects; savings, $1.8 million for
infrastructure improvements, impact fees, grants, class c funds, and the ¼ of a ¼ gas tax.

CM Whatcott said they cash fund their capital projects and that is not typical.

CFO Naidu said the proposal to close the gap between the revenues and expenditures in 2021 is
to eliminate the $1.8 million. He said the ¼ of the ¼ (gas tax) takes its place. It was noted that
the inclusion of the $1.8 million was a policy that the City Council asked for, it was not done by
Ordinance. Part of the $1.8 million is used for capital maintenance.

Council Member Marlor said he is concerned about using the ¼ of the ¼ gas tax. He is not sure
they can count on that money. City Attorney Loose said the state is bonding against it. It is very
stable. There is a limitation on how the gas tax can be spent.

They discussed the possibility of a future downturn in the economy. CFO Naidu said they are not
going to see a large sales tax revenue bump. They will always keep their expenditures in line
with their revenues. They are proposing to use some savings for capital projects, but they are not
relying on that money. The solution in a downturn is to not add additional projects.

CM Whatcott said this proposal allows them to maintain what they are currently doing, including
capital projects. They will be able to hire staff on the front end of Fire Station 64 without adding
new revenue to the pot. He said the problem is that if they want to maintain the level of service
with the continued growth, at some point, they will need to hire additional employees and as of
right now, there is no additional money to do that. He said the demand of services, and the
associated cost, will outpace their ability to fund it. The main component is the employee salary
and salary inflation.

In the current plan, they are not adding additional money to the rainy day funding, but they are
not taking any money away from it.

CFO Naidu said this plan will not affect the city’s bond rating.

Council Member Marlor asked if any of the bonds are coming due? CFO Naidu said there are a
couple of bonds that will be paid off in a few years.

CM Whatcott noted that property taxes collected from the city pays for the police department
and half of the fire department.

Council Member Harris asked if they annex property into the city in the future, will they have to
raise taxes? CM Whatcott said it depends on the arrangement; he feels it would have to be a self-
funding situation. Council Member Marlor said they need to review impact fees more regularly.
It was noted that other community’s police departments are funded with a special service district
assessed separately on the property tax. It is not a fair comparison to compare city to city.
Herriman pays 3 times in property tax if you add the city tax with the public safety taxes.

Mayor Ramsey noted that staff is excellent in getting grants to help with funding.

It was noted that South Jordan pays less taxes for higher value homes than Riverton and
Herriman. They reviewed the city taxes on home values (Attachment A).

It was further noted that other cities have some serious infrastructure costs.

CM Whatcott said they have published the city tax graph in the FOCUS. Council Member
Marlor said they should include that in the survey to educate residents. City Attorney Loose said
Sandy and Draper have received millions in funds; that is why their taxes are low.

They discussed how the changes would affect the Fire Department.

Fire Chief Dawson said they are looking at 11 new FTE for public safety. The plan is to stagger
hiring employees overtime.

CM Whatcott said they anticipate the fire station will be ready in October or November, but staff
will start in January. It can take up to 3-4 years to get a fire fighter fully functioning
independently.

Fire Chief Dawson said there are times where there are no ambulances available in the city. He
said one more medic unit will get them where they need for a number of years. They are
budgeting $300,000 for a new ambulance. They are hoping that it will come in closer to
$250,000.

CM Whatcott said the plan isn’t optimal, but it’s in a comfortable range from a risk perspective.

Council Member Harris said the budget plan makes sense for this year. He said they should
evaluate certain things that they can tighten up on without cutting employees.

Council Member Zander said she appreciates the creativity of staff for this solution, and she is
comfortable with the current direction.

Council Member McGuire said he is comfortable with the plan for a year. He said he is
concerned about the future of the city and how lean they run. He is worried about burning out
employees and making sure they have funds to provide services to the residents.

Council Member Marlor said he is also comfortable with the plan. He said he would like to see a
list of the CIP projects, and he would like to know what the unfunded projects are. He asked that
city meetings not be scheduled when they have other meetings and activities planned that day.

Council Member Shelton expressed concern about taking $1.8 million out because the CIP list
had significant maintenance items on it. CM Whatcott said the Class C funds and some savings
can be used for that. They will show all CIP projects and make that determination. He said the
majority of the residents don’t want a tax increase, but the reality of decreasing services would
be difficult. He said they will have to figure out how to increase their revenues at some point.

Council Member Shelton said he would like to make sure their legislative representatives
understand how the loss of the Kennecott revenue has effected them. It was noted that the bill
said there would be no local government fiscal impact.

City Attorney Loose said he feels Rio Tinto is the one who knew clearly. The players at the
Legislature were not fully aware of the impact of point of sale.

Council Member Marlor asked why would they reduce corporate tax? City Attorney Loose said
the argument is if they go after the corporations, it will take jobs and businesses away.

The City Council determined to have the next budget meeting on February 25th at 5:30 p.m.

ADJOURNMENT

Council Member Marlor made a motion to adjourn. Council Member Shelton seconded the
motion. The vote was unanimous in favor.

The January 29, 2020 City Council budget meeting adjourned at 7:22 p.m.
