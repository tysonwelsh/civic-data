Mayor Lang opened the Public Hearing.

Randy Moore, the applicant, stated that this is a high-quality low density proposed
development. He detailed the number of parking stalls, public streets, quality and
size of the units, etc. Mr. Moore indicated that he met with residents and adjusted
his concept to meet their requests and address their concerns. Mr. Moore stated that
there is an easement that allows Dominion Energy to access their site. He indicated
that vinyl fencing will be placed next to residential and a concrete masonry wall
will face 5600 West. Mr. Moore stated that he has been working with UDOT on
traffic and will continue to do so. He explained that this is a uniquely shaped parcel
that is surrounded by various uses. He stated that this could be developed with a far
heavier use but he feels a high quality, low density townhome community would
be a great buffer for the various uses in the area. He noted that townhomes are a
lifestyle choice for people who don’t want a yard to maintain.

Pam Bryson stated that that she lives in the neighborhood north of this proposal.
She indicated that she has been involved in the process, even when a different
developer proposed a multi-family community at this location 2 years ago, and has
felt that her voice has been heard through the process by both staff and the
developer. She indicated that she likes how this concept has been modified and
could see herself or her family living in the proposed development. Ms. Bryson
stated that she feels this proposal is more conducive to the neighborhood. She
indicated that there is still some concern regarding traffic and additional space
between units and the existing residential neighborhood. Ms. Bryson asked the
Council to ensure the wording in the agreement protects the rights of the residents
and there are no loopholes for changes that would negatively impact them.

Darrell Curtis stated that he is concerned about traffic with this proposal. He
indicated that this will add more vehicles to 5600 West. Mr. Curtis stated that he
feels the notice should go to more residents than just those within 300’. He indicated
he understands the housing crisis but these units would not save anyone money and
can be turned into rental units down the line.

Mayor Lang closed the Public Hearing.

The City Council previously held a public hearing regarding proposed Ordinance
22-17 that would amend the Zoning Map to Show a Change of Zone for Property
Located at 4590 South 5600 West from A (Agriculture, Minimum Lot Size 1/2 Acre)
to RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling Residential) and R-1-10 (Single Unit Dwelling
Residential, Minimum Lot Size 10,000 Square Feet).

Councilmember Huynh thanked all those who spoke. He indicated that he agrees this
is a unique location and the cost of land has increased, as well as the demand for this
type of housing. Councilmember Huynh stated that he would prefer to see single family
homes in this area and is concerned about additional traffic as well as the long-term
viability of this type of project.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Lang there were no further questions from members of the
City Council, and she called for a motion.

       Councilmember Huynh moved to deny Ordinance 22-17.

       Councilmember Harmon seconded the motion.

Councilmember Nordfelt stated that he agrees this is a challenging location and
indicated that he feels there is a need for townhomes and this appears to be an
appropriate place for that type of use. He indicated that the developer has worked
with the City and residents to come up with a good product that will be a great
addition to the City. Councilmember Fitisemanu agreed and indicated that this
proposal has taken into consideration all the issues the previous applicant had
proposed and created a better product for the City and residents. Mayor Lang stated
that she doesn’t like high density and she feels there are better uses for this parcel.
Councilmember Harmon stated that he feels the RE zone would be a better fit at
this location.

ORDINANCE 22-20: AMEND SECTIONS 1-2-102 AND 1-2-107 OF THE WEST
VALLEY CITY MUNICIPAL CODE TO UPDATE CERTAIN PARKS AND
RECREATION FEES
Wayne Pyle, City Manager, discussed proposed Ordinance 22-20 that would amend
Sections 1-2-102 and 1-2-107 of the West Valley City Municipal Code to Update Certain Parks
and Recreation Fees.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:
      MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING MAY 10, 2022
                            -8-

       The Parks and Recreation Department has a variety of fees for services that may
       require small changes at times based upon costs associated with providing these
       services and programs. Department staff work to efficiently monitor these fees and
       determine if changes need to be made. Due to the quantity of these type of fees, a
       change was approved which authorized the City Manager to review and approve
       certain fees for community and recreational services based on several conditions.
       This is found in the Consolidated Fee Schedule under section 1-2-107 (7) entitled
       Community and Recreational Services Fees.

       The Parks and Recreation Department would like to include several additional fees
       within this section. These include:
           Family Fitness Center:
                   o Childcare Fees
                   o Kidz Kamp Program Fees
                   o Kindergarten Kamp Fees
                   o Afterschool Kamp Fees
           Golf Courses:
                   o Green Fees
                   o Cart Fees
                   o Club Rental Fees
                   o Driving Range Ball Bucket Fees
                   o Tournament Fees
                   o Player Pass Fees
           UCCC:
                   o Rental Fees

Councilmember Huynh stated the he believes the City Council should still review these
types of changes.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Lang there were no further questions from members of the City
Council, and she called for a motion.

       Councilmember Huynh moved to deny Ordinance 22-20.

       Councilmember Harmon seconded the motion.

Councilmember Nordfelt stated that this change gives staff more flexibility to respond as
costs change and evolve. He indicated that these are small adjustments, and he doesn’t
believe staff should have to go through a longer process to get them changed as needed.
Councilmember Christensen agreed and noted that he feels these types of minor fees are
administrative decisions, not legislative ones. Councilmember Harmon stated that he
doesn’t feel this is a big process for staff to go through and noted that elected members of
