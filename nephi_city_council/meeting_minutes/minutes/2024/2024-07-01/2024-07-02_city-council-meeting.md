PARKING CODE ORDINANCE 07-02-2024 ADOPTED:

     City Administrator Seth Atkinson reviewed with the council that as Nephi City has begun to develop
with more commercial businesses that are proposing drive-through aisles to sell products and services,
such as fast-food restaurants, staff have been revisiting the code to ensure that appropriate traffic
requirements are being expected of developers. Two items have been discussed as possibly needing
updating in the city parking code.
   It has been noted that as drive-through aisles have been proposed initially for several developments
the plans have shown a drive-through that does not have any contiguous drive aisle lane attached for
customers to enter/leave the drive-through lane for proper traffic flow patterns, and some of them have
been for quite long distances. To clarify for developers the expectation of both a drive-through aisle and
a contiguous drive aisle lane for ingress/egress staff is proposing some altered code language.
   Several developments have been recently proposed that wish to provide for the circulation of a
project in a one-way drive aisle lane approach, but Nephi City’s current code requires a minimum of
twenty-five (25) feet of drive aisle whether for one-way or two-way traffic, giving no discount for a one-
way aisle. Staff is revisiting this requirement to allow reduced drive aisle width requirements for one-
way drive aisles by proposing some altered code language.
  Mr. Atkinson also stated that some additional definitions were needed to clarify the new changes to
the parking code, as well as allowing staff to state, with more clarity, specific locations within a site plan
for a drive-through business when referencing these locations in site plan notations, and requests for
site plan modifications.
   The Planning Commission had recommended not going lower than 18 feet for a drive aisle and staff
was recommending 16 feet. After some discussion, the council advised staff to keep with the Planning
Commission’s recommendation of not lower that 18 feet.
   Ordinance 07-02-2024 was presented that made changes to 10-16-5 of the Nephi City Code.
Councilor Shari Cowan moved to adopt Ordinance 07-02-2024. Councilor Skip Worwood seconded the
motion. The motion passed on the following roll call vote:

COMBINED USE-1 AND COMBINED USE-2 ZONES DISCUSSED:

   Mr. Atkinson explained to the Council that in 2022, during the time when the city’s General Plan was
being updated, the Planning Commission and City Council held discussions on changes to the Combined
Use zones. As the city has considered ways to zone for future land development along the Main Street,
there may be a way to promote best practices for commercial areas, residential/commercial mixes, and
transportation routes.
  City staff had discussed the concept of commercial pods or areas and then providing multiple
transportation routes to these pods. In this way, the city can avoid using Main Street as the sole
transportation corridor to access commercial services. Many communities that began as smaller
communities, like Nephi, end up zoning their entire Main Street area as commercial. As more and more
commercial businesses locate to the area of Main Street, traffic patterns begin to be congested during
the day as commuters and customers traverse Main Street to get to their commercial
destinations. Examples include Logan, Heber City, and Spanish Fork.
   By grouping together commercial entities into “pods” and then having multiple traffic routes to
access these pods, it disperses the traffic patterns and avoids congestion. Surrounding these
commercial pod areas would be zones of commercial/residential mixed-use development, or Combined
Use. The CU-I zone would consist of more commercial options with some residential. The CU-II zone
would consist of more residential options and some commercial. In this way, the zones can promote a
transition from a heavy commercial area to a light commercial area. The changes made to the CU-I and
CU-II zones a couple of years ago reflect this philosophy. Placement of these zones has been slated as a
future discussion of the Planning Commission and the City Council. Mr. Atkinson showed a concept map
of this idea to the council for future consideration.
    Councilor Parady asked about establishing architectural standards. City Planner Shauna Talbot said
that at one time Cedar City was able to get some grant funds to add cohesive architectural element to
their Mian Street.

GSBS SELECTED AS THE POLICE STATION ARCHITECT:

  Mr. Atkinson updated the Council that the city has recently held a Request for Qualifications process
to select an architect/engineer to design the new police station. The city received 6 submissions and
held interviews with two firms. Participants on the selection committee were the City Administrator,
Chief Morgan, Capt. Bert Wright, Lieutenant Jace Peterson, Tiffiny Lund, Lieutenant Corey Houskeeper
from the Highway Patrol, and Brittney Elmer from the Utah State Department of Public Safety.
  After interviews were held, the unanimous decision from the committee was to select GSBS as the
architects for the Police Station. Some of their experience includes the design for the Heber City Police
Station, the Murray Police Station/City Hall, and the Salt Lake City Police Station. They have also
designed police stations for smaller cities in Texas. Due to their extensive experience with police
stations and their desire to work with our unique project, they were a standout company in a field with
many qualified firms. The selection committee recommended moving forward with GSBS, getting a
contract in place, and then working with them to come up with process details for the design work.
   Councilor Callaway moved to go forward with the committee’s recommendation of GSBS as the
architects for the police station. Councilor Cowan seconded the motion. Councilor Travis Worwood
stated that he would abstain from voting due to a conflict of interest. The motion passed with all other
councilors voting yes.

DBT TRANSPORATION SERVICES APPROVED FOR THE AWOS REPLACEMENT PROJECT:

   Mr. Atkinson updated the Council that the city had recently sent out a request for proposals (RFP) for
qualified companies to replace the airport’s outdated Automated Weather Observing System
(AWOS). The RFP was sent to four companies and the city received one bid. The one responsive bid is
the company who is currently maintaining the city’s AWOS system, and the bid OF $121,949.87 was
lower than the engineer’s estimate. Mr. Atkinson said that the City’s engineer felt that since most of the
companies eligible to bid on the project had confirmed the receipt of the RFP, that the city could accept
the single bid.
  DBT Transportation Services is the responsive bidder and staff and the engineer working with the city,
JUB, recommend awarding the bid to DBT.
   Councilor Travis Worwood moved to award the AWOS replacement project contract to DBT
Transportation Services and authorize the Mayor to sign the application for BIL funding. Councilor Skip
Worwood seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.
