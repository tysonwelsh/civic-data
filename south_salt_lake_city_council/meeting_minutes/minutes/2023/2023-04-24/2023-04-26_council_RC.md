          Mayor Wood gave a flood update. She showed the slips of paper that were passed
          out to the residents closest to Millcreek. Staff delivered them in English and Spanish
          to around 300 homes East of State Street, and to around 50 homes/businesses West

          in Give Pulse for volunteers to be notified when the City needs help delivering
          sandbags to critical locations. There are also three ring cameras that have been
          installed for Stormwater staff to monitor the City’s at-risk locations.

      6. INFORMATION.

              a. Flood Preparation Update
                 Stormwater Division Manager, Corby Talbot, said that the City has 25,000
                 sandbags filled up and ready to deploy. There are 10,000 staged throughout
                 eight or nine areas in City right now, most of them being on the East side.
                 The latest update from Salt Lake County Flood Control shows about 40+
                 inches of water equivalent in the snowpack. He said that the staff has a plan
                 in place for the East side and West side. With warm temperatures coming in
                 the next weeks, they are ready to jump to action and welcome all the help.

                     Council Member Williams had a question from a resident who was asking if
                     they must schedule a time to get sandbags.

                     Mr. Talbot said that they can call connect line at 801-464-6757 where staff
                     will confirm their address, to see if it’s in an area of threat that they have
                     established, and if approved, will set a time for them to go to Public Works
                     to get bags.

                     Mayor Wood said that when City Hall was closed on April 14th due to the
                     HVAC system being updated, her staff went to the EOC (Emergency
                     Operations Center) and went over a couple of scenarios. They have
                     sandbagged wells and have done anything precautionary that they can do
                     ahead of time.

    2. A Resolution of the South Salt Lake City Council Renaming the City Owned Building
       Located at 2530 South 500 East from the Columbus Community Center to the South
       Salt Lake Community Center.
       Council Chair Bynum gave a quick history of this matter. The South Salt Lake Youth City
       Council did a presentation and received a lot of comments from residents that were
       polarized. The City then began to research survey tools to be able to get feedback from
       residents which time to acquire and then build a good survey base. The majority of the
       residents said that they supported the name change with the next largest group saying
       that they didn’t care. The City Council now feels comfortable moving forward on this
       matter and has come up with a neutral name for the center which, she feels, will be a
       better way for the City to make itself known among Salt Lake Valley cities.

   3. An Ordinance of the South Salt Lake City Council Amending Section 3.11.030 of the
      South Salt Lake Municipal Code to Modify Fees Relating to Business Licensing in South
      Salt Lake City.
      Community & Economic Development Director, Jonathan Weidenhamer, went over the
      proposed Ordinance, a copy of which is attached and incorporated by this reference.

       Per State guidelines, the Business Licensing department is only allowed to charge for the
       cost of doing business. The Zions Bank study quantified how much the City charges in
       comparison to other cities.

       Since the last meeting, the South Salt Lake Business Chamber emailed their group,
       which has 400+ contacts, with what each business type would be charged. There was no
       response.

       The proposed fees would generate $209,000 in revenue and Mr. Weidenhamer
       emphasized that these are costs that the City has already been subsidizing with other
       parts of the City budget. He also said that these fees have not been updated since 2011.

       The additional study they did since the last meeting shows that it’ll be 414 of the 2,350
       businesses (which is under 20%) that will be producing $189,000 of the estimated
       revenue. These businesses are mainly fast-food restaurants, auto related businesses,
       banks, convenience stores, etc.

       An alternative that he brought for the smaller businesses that would fall into the ‘mom
       and pop shops’ category, mainly restaurants and booth rentals, is for delayed
       implementation. Instead of applying the full fee in the first year, it can be split between
       two years. For home occupation licensed businesses, he feels that the $32 increase is so
       nominal and wouldn’t be considered devastating.

       Council Member Pinkney thanked him and the staff for their work and feels that by
       doing so, the City and Council will be able to know that they did their due diligence in
       this matter.

       Council Member Williams made a motion to approve the Ordinance as it was presented
       with the delayed implementation for restaurants and booth rentals.
