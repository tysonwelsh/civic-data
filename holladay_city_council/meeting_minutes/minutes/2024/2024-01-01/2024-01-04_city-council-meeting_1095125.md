Swearing-In Ceremony – 6:00 p.m.
Mayor Dahle opened the meeting and recognized those in attendance. The colors were presented
by the Unified Police Department (“UPD”) Honor Guard. The Pledge of Allegiance was led by
Council Member Quinn’s grandson, Ira Wilson.

Former Council Member Lynn Pace spoke on government. He extended congratulations to those
who put their names forth and ran for office. Mr. Pace ran for office four times and served 14½
years. The City was incorporated in 1999 and he took office in 2004 and left in 2018. He
remarked that those who serve are in the business of building a community and a place we all
want to call home. This is most magnificent at the local level by electing friends and neighbors
who have our interests in mind and take care of what residents care about most.

Mr. Pace reported that in City government, they are building the community. When he
graduated from high school in 1979, the Cottonwood Mall was a thriving enterprise and the
Village Center was a drive-in. There were so many children that the school district built Howard
R. Driggs Elementary School a few blocks away from Holladay Elementary. At the time,
Holladay was part of the unincorporated County and there was no leadership speaking on behalf
of the community. When he was a high school senior, Mr. Pace took a job at Burton Lumber for
$3 per hour. He lived in Holladay his entire life and after school and marriage, he purchased his
parent’s home.

 Mr. Pace acknowledged the changes that have taken place over the years. Holladay Elementary
eventually closed due to a lack of students. The City purchased the building, converted it into
City Hall, and acquired the property to the west. The Fire Station was relocated to where the 10-
story building once was. Since the City’s incorporation, they have had local leaders look out for
the residents. Going forward, the community will continue to change. The challenge for local
leaders is to build a vision for the future. He hoped future generations will recognize the City of
Holladay as valuable of a place and community as he did.

Mr. Pace shared the following lessons learned:
       1.     We are building the community of Holladay for our children. Let’s make sure it
              is the Holladay we want them to have.

       2.      When you serve as an elected Council Member, it is not about you but is a team
               effort. We lead for a season because of the trust.

Mr. Pace reported that he has served under nine elected mayors with very different personalities.
All were given a torch to carry for a season. He was honored by the trust and to have been able

VII. City Manager Report – Gina Chamness
City Manager, Gina Chamness reported that the 2024 Legislative Session began on January 16,
2024. At the Legislative Pre-Session Briefing, Dave Spatafore addressed emails his office will
be sending to the Legislative group each week. There will be emails to Staff and any Council
Members who want to be part of that conversation. She cautioned that there might be a lot of
email communication, so it was possible to send out only the summary email.

Council Member Gray asked if the summary emails would go out each week. This was
confirmed. Council Member Durham asked if it would be helpful to involve more Council
Members. Ms. Chamness noted that if there is an issue where Council feedback is needed, it
would be brought to the City Council weekly. The Council Members wished to receive the
summary email rather than individual emails.

Ms. Chamness noted that there had been brief discussions about Happy Healthy Holladay earlier
in the meeting. There was an opportunity to apply for grant funding that would provide funding
for a two-year position. There was no clarity about whether that would be a part-time or a full-
time position but it would be designed to focus on prevention in the community rather than
mitigation. Generally, the focus will be on areas of mental, emotional, and physical health in the
community. She questioned whether the City Council was supportive of trying to obtain grant
funding for that position. Council Member Durham asked if there would need to be ongoing
funding for that position in the future. Assistant City Manager, Holly Smith, explained that it
would be at the discretion of the Council. However, the intention was that after the grant
funding ran out, the position would continue to be funded by the City.

Ms. Smith explained that the Council will need to consider whether there is a desire to continue
funding the Happy Healthy Holladay position afterward. Council Member Fotheringham noted
that there is a similar Mental Health Counselor in the Unified Police Department (“UPD”)
budget. Ms. Chamness clarified that the UPD position is more of a direct service, while the
Happy Healthy Holladay position is more public health-focused and will look at different
prevention policies.

Council Member Durham referenced the Happy Healthy Holladay data that was presented
previously. Substance abuse and mental health issues are important to address in the community.
As a result, he was tentatively in favor of applying for the grant. That being said, he thought it
would be worthwhile to know more about the specifics of the grant and the position. Council
Member Fotheringham believed it would be appropriate to implement the position and if there
are measurable results, that would determine whether or not the position was funded by the City
in the future. Council Member Brewer understood the potential significance of the position but
wanted some clear examples of what the position would entail. Council Member Quinn
wondered how much of a commitment needed to be made. Ms. Smith explained that the desire
was for the position to continue after the grant ended, but if the City did not believe it was
warranted, there was no requirement to continue.

Council Member Brewer asked about the grant requirements. He wanted to know if the position
needs to be specifically opioid focused. Ms. Smith believed it was broader in nature but
something that needs to be addressed in the community is opioid addiction. The main challenges
facing the community are opioid addiction, depression, anxiety, and suicide. Ms. Smith
reiterated that Happy Healthy Holladay is currently working on a strategic plan. If the position
moves forward, that person will work to execute the plan and create programs where there are
existing gaps. Council Member Durham agreed with Council Member Fotheringham that the
grant makes it possible to try out the position. Mayor Dahle thought it was appropriate to apply
for the grant as long as success can be measured. It was clarified that there will not necessarily
be a long-term commitment. It sounded like the Council wanted to see the measures of success
and then make a decision about the position.

VIII. Council Reports & District Issues
Council Member Brewer reported that he received a lot of phone calls about the one-half-acre lot
on the corner of Sycamore and Murray Holladay. Mayor Dahle reported that the public hearing
was to take place on January 18, 2024 and assumes there will be a lot of public comment. There
will then be a discussion during the Work Session with the item on the February 1 agenda for a
vote, depending on how complicated it is.

Council Member Durham reported that a few residents gave him positive feedback about the
holiday decorations in the plaza. As for Happy Healthy Holladay, the Strategic Plan work was
underway. The next step was a meeting with community members to have focus group
conversations to better identify needs in the community. It was clear that substance abuse and
mental health are important issues but there was a desire to understand other issues that face the
community as well. Council Member Durham shared an update on the Tree Committee.
Something the Committee was trying to do was boost meeting attendance and increase the
overall level of involvement. There had been some questions about the structure of the
Committee and what was dictated by ordinance. Additionally, there had been conversations
about more outreach for the Tree Committee. Council Member Durham suggested regular social
media announcements for meetings.

Council Member Fotheringham informed those present that Kathy Murphy from the Arts
Council passed away unexpectedly. She was the person most closely associated with the
Summer Concert Series and the concert events. Ms. Murphy was an incredibly important part of
the Arts Council. He expressed his appreciation to her and for the significant contributions she
made to the City. Council Member Fotheringham reported that he attended the UFA Benefits
and Compensation Meeting at the UFA headquarters. He discussed a presentation that had been
made and the speech that had been given about UFA and the community and thought the
message should be shared at the City Council level.

Council Member Quinn discussed the Community Renewable Energy Agency Board. The
Board decided to propose to Rocky Mountain Power that a date be set for April 15, 2024, for the
Public Service Commission. The hope was that if a date was provided, progress could be made.
