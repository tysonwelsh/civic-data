       (Administrative Action).
       On the request of Brent Anderson, representing Loveland Living Planet Aquarium for a
       Plat Amendment on 12.99 acres of property located at 12047 South Lone Peak Parkway.
       Application SUBD-045-2023. Staff contact: Jennifer Jastremsky, (801) 576-6328,

Planning Manager, Jennifer Jastremsky, presented the Staff Report and stated that last year the
Commission reviewed a Site Plan for a new Science Learning Center to the south of the existing
Aquarium Building. It is in the Commercial Special District Land Use Designation and within the
Living Planet Aquarium Commercial Special District zoning designation. The existing subdivision
plat for the properties is two parcels. The approved site plan for the proposed Science Learning
Center was displayed showing where new building will be located and where it will be connected
to the existing Aquarium. It will have a new entrance where one can go north to the aquarium or
south to the Science Learning Center. On the existing plat, a property line goes straight through
the proposed entrance. The proposal is to adjust the property line so that the entire aquarium
building is on one piece of property.

Instead of going straight across, the property line will go 10 feet out from the existing Aquarium
Building all the way around and connect the property to the south. The reason for the 10-foot
extension is due to the Building Code, which requires a building that is closer than 10 feet to a

4.     Training: Open and Public Meetings Acts and Ethics.

City Attorney, Mike Barker, presented the annual mandated training on Open Meetings and Ethics.
The primary issue with City Councils is often closed meetings. The Planning Commission will
generally not go into a closed session. He congratulated the Planning Commission and staff for
following the law. The Open and Public Meetings Act applies to the Planning Commission
because they meet the definition of a public body and the requirement in the City Code that the
Planning Commission conducts its business subject to the Open Meetings Act. The bylaws state
that the Commission must comply with the Open and Public Meetings Act. Chair Adams asked if
the Planning Commission has ever held an Emergency Meeting. Mr. Barker was not aware of one.

Mr. Barker reported that every December, the Annual Meeting Schedule is published for the
following year as required. All meetings are properly noticed and open to the public. The Bylaws
specify that the Planning Commission should not discuss anything on the agenda or future agendas
during the dinner meeting. Past meetings are open to discussion unless an issue is under appeal.
State law requires that the agenda be published at least 24 hours before the meeting. It is generally
published on Fridays to provide ample time to review the materials provided. A meeting cannot
be held without a quorum, which requires three Planning Commission Members. The Chair can
be part of that quorum and vote if needed. Voting procedures were discussed. Mr. Barker reported

that meetings are recorded, prepared, and approved within the proper timeframe. In response to a
question raised, Mr. Barker stated that if a quorum is not present the meeting would be canceled
and re-noticed.

The Municipal Officers and Employees Ethics Act is found in Utah State Code Title 10 and applies
to all elected officials, appointed officers, and City employees. The Planning Commission falls
within the category of Appointed Officers. The Ethics Disclosure was revised to meet the
requirements of the Act. Some of the questions are phrased differently and there are fewer
disclosures on the form. There are also bullet points that specify the action to be taken if there is
a conflict of interest. The form is designed to comply with the City Code and the Act.

Mr. Barker next discussed conflicts and identified illegal acts for which there are criminal penalties
such as using any information acquired for personal gain or to secure special privileges for oneself
or others. For example, a Commission Member might be privy to information that is shared with
a friend or family member for financial or other gain. There is also a prohibition against seeking
a gift or benefit of substantial value for oneself or others meant to influence or reward them for
their actions taken. There are exceptions such as accepting an occasional non-pecuniary gift of
less than $50. The Commission is free to accept all publicly presented awards for public service.
One can also obtain a bona fide loan in the ordinary course of business.

Mr. Barker reported that a Planning Commission Member cannot agree to receive compensation
for assisting a person or business in any transaction involving the City unless it is disclosed. If a
Planning Commission Member were to receive compensation to assist someone with a business
transaction with the City they would be required to file a sworn statement with the Mayor at least
10 days before reaching that agreement or before compensation is received, whichever is earlier.
The disclosure must also take place in an open meeting immediately before discussion of the
transaction. Unless the business that is to benefit attends a public meeting, disclosure is not
required. He stated that there is not much case law on the Act and violations are rare.

Mr. Barker identified transactions that need to be disclosed such as a Business License or Liquor
License that involve the City if the Planning Commission Member’s interest exceeds $2,000. If a
Planning Commission Member owns a business that does business with the City, they would need
to make a public disclosure to the Planning Commission. If a Planning Commission Member has
a personal interest or investment that creates a conflict between their personal interest and public
duties it must be disclosed in a public meeting.

Mr. Barker reported that there are criminal penalties associated with violations of using public
office for personal benefit and receiving compensation for assistance in a transaction involving the
City. If the Commission has any ethics questions, they should be discussed. Penalties range from
second-degree felonies to Class B misdemeanors, depending on the value of the benefit received.
Violations result in removal from office. If disclosure requirements are violated, the Act mandates
dismissal from office. The City may also rescind or void any contract pursuant to a transaction
that is entered into and not disclosed. City Code requires recusal if it is a legislative action and the
Commission Member has an interest in the subject matter of the action. Mr. Barker stated that
ethics issues establish a threshold and the bar is often set higher to prevent violations.
