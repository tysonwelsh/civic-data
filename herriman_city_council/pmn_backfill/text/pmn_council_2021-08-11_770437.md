2. Administrative Reports
   2.1. Public Works Facility Building Update - Justun Edwards, Public Works Director and Cody
        Stromberg, Deputy Police Chief
Public Works Director Justun Edwards noted there were major changes to this project. He then introduced
Scott Henrickson with GSBS Architects, Brian Jacobson also with GSBS Architects, and Johnny Hollingshead
with Layton Construction. He directed all questions to them. Director Edwards then went on to report the
work staff had done since their last meeting and ask for feedback. He presented an overview of the site which
was positioned off Rose Crest Road and 129000 South.

Deputy Police Chief Cody Stromberg presented the concept of evaluating a potential public safety building.
He said the ultimate task was to evaluate staffing, programming, and spatial needs at full build out. He noted
this would amount to 120 sworn officers needed. In assessing feasibility, Deputy Chief Stromberg said they
attempted to do a combination of public safety units. He pointed out both the past and current plans for the
public works facility. The current one he presented would have 51,000 square feet with reduced shop and
office space.

Councilmember Steve Shields asked how the updated square footage would affect the current City Hall facility.
Deputy Chief Stromberg replied that this was still being assessed and discussed. He stated they would look at
future phasing to accommodate any needs that may arise. Deputy Police Chief Stromberg then went over the
building footprint which was effectively and functionally the same. He noted the main difference was the
converted shelf space and accommodations, which he said were presented in their packets. He also noted they
added an emergency operations center. Director Edwards then discussed cost estimates, and said the updated
changes brought the budget to $29 million which was a budgetary increase. He included a cost breakdown in
the material given to the Council.

Director Edwards pointed out that another task for this project was finding alternatives. He stressed the
thought they put into these alternatives but noted they were still just concept designs. He highlighted one of
the alternatives they worked on which was a plan to utilize the Herriman City Community Center currently
housing the Herriman branch of Salt Lake Community College along with the Butterfield Park addition. He
noted they wanted to build more buildings in this area, including some additional features such as animal
services. He acknowledged this would affect the current facility, and went through the map of the area which
highlighted the fact they could only really work in the northern area. He said the cost for these changes would
be around $6 million total, which included inflation estimates. Councilmember Shields asked if there was any
August 11, 2021 – Herriman City Council Minutes                                               Page 3 of 17
data for the delayed response due to the lack of facilities and if this was yielding any actual public safety issues.
Deputy Police Chief Stromberg acknowledged that there were some risks to public safety. Director Edwards
stressed the benefits of these proposed changes outweighed any cons.


Director Edwards discussed cost estimates for building modifications from contractors. He said estimates
rolled out to roughly $3 million retrofit the building. Deputy Police Chief Stromberg added having the extra
storage space would be a huge help. City Manager Nathan Cherpeski brought up the hurdle of mitigating the
parking impact, as they would have to strike a deal with the neighboring LDS church to curb costs.

building should they leave the building at any point. Councilmember Shields stated the proposal could not

would fully accommodated all needs. Ultimately, they decided to step back and evaluate the City’s options
further.


    2.2. Deer Hunting and Mitigation Program Regulations Update - Justun Edwards, Public Works
         Director
Director Edwards reviewed two items. The first item regarded hunting within City limits and the second item
related to the City’s deer mitigation plan. First, he went through the dates for hunting season which would run
from August 21st through November 30th; which would include the regular season and extended archery hunts.
He noted in the past, the City had been good about placing signs at trail access points with rules and contact
information for DWR. He added there was increased patrolling and communication from the Herriman Police
Department on the matter. Director Edwards noted most people were understanding about the restrictions,
and most only called to ask questions about city policy.

Director Edwards then reviewed the deer mitigation program. He said deer mitigation efforts started in 2014
for public safety reasons as there were 160 reported auto accidents involving deer. Moreover, Director
Edwards acknowledged citizens had been concerned with excess deer in their yards. With new equipment, he
noted they have had an easy time trapping deer, but new regulations did not allow relocating deer due to the
spread of disease. He added the deer trapping dates were being restricted from August 1st through October
31st despite the most successful period for this going from mid-October through the end of December. He
felt with these modifications, they should work with the DWR as they will not be able to trap as many deer.

hunting presence which would help as well.

    2.4. Discussion of a draft amendment to modify Flag Lot regulations in City Code - Clinton
         Spencer, Planning Manager
Planning Manager Clinton Spencer expressed an interest in working with the staff and Planning Commission
to address issues and look for ways to implement flag lots within certain areas of the community. He noted
there was a need within the City to accommodate these lots. He stated current ordinance was approved in
April of 2017. He went on to present some benefits and negative aspects of flag lots. He listed the positive
aspects as follows: they improved property taxes, increased population which increased revenue, added to
private property values, and discouraged demolition. The cons included not providing traditional forms of
development, could change historical/agricultural use of private property, and it could change the nature of a
subdivision and affect privacy. He then went through the map and detailed areas with potential safety concerns.

    9.7. Discussion and consideration of an ordinance to amend the Herriman Land Development
         Code to modify the multi-family design standards in residential and mixed-use zones (File
         No. Z2021-052) - Clinton Spencer, Planning Manager
Planning Manager Spencer began the discussion. He said this ordinance addressed concerns over a last meeting
on modifying multi-family design standards in residential and mixed-use zones. He noted the goal was to
better meet standards in place and present new opportunities. He stated the proposal did not explicitly dictate
designs but does give references so buildings adhere to quality standards. He noted the standards for
consideration included whether or not this was harmonious with the surrounding areas. He said it boiled down
mostly to building types and how they affected the design characteristics in the City. Planning Manager Spencer
said building types being looked at include garden types, stacked units, townhomes, and apartments.

Councilmember Ohrn worried about eliminating other possibilities by narrowing down the building materials
to 40-percent brick or stone. She questioned the purpose of this ordinance because it would have to fit the
40-percent rule even for those who do not live in more dense areas. She also said they would have to see a
real benefit to the City if they were to deviate from the standard. Councilmember Henderson agreed.

Councilmember Shields said he would love to see more architectural diversity in buildings, but he did not

    9.9. Discussion and consideration of a resolution approving a Master Development
         Agreement policy - Chase Andrizzi, City Attorney
City Attorney Andrizzi led the discussion on the consideration of a resolution on creating policy on MDAs
processing along with the requirements within development agreements. He noted the process was created
based on State law, but also what the council might want based on previous discussions. He said they had an
application created by the Planning Commission but no formal procedure. City Attorney Andrizzi said the
new plan would have him look at drafting a development agreement, go on to the Planning Commission, and
ultimately be presented to the City Council to address additional terms, and determine whether they will
approve or deny the application. He clarified the Planning Commission suggested hearing things before any
decisions were made, but Councilmember Ohrn was hesitant about this being part of the process.

Councilmember Shields said he understood the effort to meet a timeline using multiple tracks, though in his
mind they had established a framework in getting to this point that should be repeated with future MDAs. He
said this included more of a focus on reviewing the MDA.

Councilmember Smith said they should stick to a default policy that applied to working groups in this process.
City Manager Cherpeski asked if there was a size requirement for MDAs and if the Council wanted to do this.
City Attorney Andrizzi reviewed three non-negotiables: 1) the MDA identify what the maximum number of
units are in the project, 2) what the actual density of the project was, 3) a prohibition of any land added under
the development agreement, 4) a loss of units when a church or school was developed in any zone, 5) this zone
density was total units divided by maximum density, 6) certain zones could not be developed outside of
preordained purposes.

Councilmember Henderson said because some developers do not like commercial development, he felt they
needed more caveats and amendments in their plan. Councilmember Shields thought this may be a good point
as standards needed to be more concrete. Councilmember Henderson asked if the general term was “church
and school” or if they need to be more specific. Planning Manager Spencer said this would be more defined
in the agreement.
