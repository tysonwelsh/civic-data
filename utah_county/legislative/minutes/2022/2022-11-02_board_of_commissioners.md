Canvass & Certification

(a) Before the board of canvassers convenes, the election officer shall: (i) count
the ballots; (ii) prepare a certified summary of: (A) all ballots counted; and (B) all
ballots not counted, with an explanation regarding the reason the ballots were not
counted; and (iii) make available to the board of canvassers for inspection, all
ballots, registers, BOOKS, and forms related to the election. (b) The board of
canvassers shall canvass the election returns by publicly: (i) reviewing the
summary reports prepared by the election officer and any ballots, registers, Books,
or forms requested by the board of canvassers; and (ii) certifying the votes cast:
(A) each person voted for; and (B) for and against each ballot proposition voted
upon at the election.

Definitions

(a) "Ballot" means the storage medium, including a paper, mechanical, or
electronic storage medium, that records an individual voter's vote.

(b) “Ballot" does not include a record to tally multiple votes.

(9) "Canvass" means the review of election returns and the official declaration of
election results by the board of canvassers.

(26) "Election returns" includes the pollbook, the military and overseas absentee
voter registration and voting certificates, one of the tally sheets, any unprocessed
ballots, all counted ballots, all excess ballots, all unused ballots, all spoiled ballots,
the ballot disposition form, and the total votes cast form.

Post Election Retention of materials

provisional ballots cast.

(c) preserve all other official election returns for at least 22 months after an

GRAMA & Public Access (63G-2-201)

(2) A record is public unless otherwise expressly provided by statute. (3) The

following records are not public: (a) a record that is private, controlled, or protected
under Sections 63G-2-302, 63G-2-303, 63G-2-304, and 63G-2-305; and (b) a
record to which access is restricted pursuant to court rule, another state statute,
federal statute, or federal regulation, including records for which access is
governed or restricted as a condition of participation in a state or federal program
or for receiving state or federal funds.

“Access to “Ballots” & “Election Returns” are currently governed by election code
via the disposition section which prescribes retention, preservation, and
destruction.

Proposed post-election analysis

e Depending on the level of detail requested of election returns, the proposed
analysis appears to require access to the detailed ballot cast records (data
export of the “cast vote record(s)” aka “CVR". This is not specifically defined
by code; but, seems most similar to “ballots” or “election returns”

e Ballots and election returns are not currently public documents per Utah code
and therefore are not disclosable to the public at any time.

e What about an internal review (vs public disclosure)?

Timing:

1) post canvass, the election returns are sealed “without opening or
examining”--thus, an analysis of the detailed record is prohibited

2) during canvass? The elections office & board of canvassers can likely
review the CVR during the canvass period

Legislative questions

e Current election code includes many outdated terms that date back to the mid
1990's.

e Legislature should review election code comprehensively to update
terminology to reflect current systems and technology

e Legislature should make clear what types of results and data from elections
are public or protected and what items can be reviewed or analyzed.

e Colorado: releases all “cast vote records” after the election

e Policy considerations

Constitutionally protected privacy vs transparency
Summary results vs more detailed ballot-by-ballot results
