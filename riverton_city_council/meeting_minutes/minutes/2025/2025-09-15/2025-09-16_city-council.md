         12600 South CRA Discussion

Mayor Staggs reminded the Council that during the January strategic planning meeting, they
discussed the skate park’s deteriorating condition. A contractor had inspected the skate park
and advised that it could not be repaired and would require a full tear-out and replacement, as it
was at or near the end of its useful life. He noted that a community survey had been conducted,
and asked Josh Lee to report.

Josh Lee, Communications Director, presented results from two surveys conducted by the
Communications Department—one online and one in person at the skate park. The first
question addressed skate park usage, showing that a significant majority of residents or their
family members either never or rarely used it in the past year. The second question addressed
the $1–1.5 million estimated cost to replace and relocate the skate park, with most respondents
not supporting the replacement or relocation. About 17% of respondents selected “other,”
largely expressing opposition to relocation and suggesting alternative improvements. Among
the 28% of respondents who supported replacing and relocating the skate park, 54% preferred
Centennial Park or the new regional park east of Mountain View Village, while 8% preferred
keeping it at the current location or suggested other alternatives.. When asked how they would
prioritize spending the $1 million estimated cost, a majority favored other amenities, like bike
paths or pickleball courts, over a new skate park.

Mayor Staggs explained that in 2022, the Council had designated the half-acre skate park
parcel, along with surrounding property along 12600 South, as part of a community
reinvestment area. At that time, the decision allowed for potential redevelopment if the skate
park needed replacement. Additionally, a May boundary realignment addressed a state law
preventing restaurants serving alcohol from being within 300 feet of a community space,
ensuring future restaurant development could proceed legally. He emphasized that this
boundary adjustment did not automatically open the skate park for development and clarified
that misinformation had circulated about that.

Councilmember McDougal, drawing on personal experience speaking with youth, he noted that
the skate park’s appeal is short-lived, which raises questions about cost-effectiveness. He
suggested that the funds proposed for the skate park might reach more youth if allocated to
other programs with high current demand, such as softball, where modest investments like
additional lighting could accommodate more participants and provide longer-term benefits.

Councilmember McCay expressed concern about prioritizing the skate park over other

Discussion/Action Items

         Ordinance No. 25-21 – RM-6 Amendment, Proposed Updates to the RM-6 Zone to
         include provisions for using private roads in specific circumstances and clarify
         Development Standards

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, reviewed an ordinance regarding the RM-6
zoning district, which is primarily multi-family but has been adjusted over time to function more
like single-family detached housing. The ordinance mainly addresses private lane use, but a
key issue was the rear yard setback. Currently, the standard is 15 feet, or 25 feet if the project
is adjacent to single-family zoning. The Planning Commission recommended standardizing it
at 15 feet for all projects, but the Council expressed concern.

The revised ordinance (Exhibit A) proposes keeping the 25-foot setback when adjacent to
single-family zoning, with the option to modify it through an SD designation during rezone
approval. Option B would standardize it at 15 feet but still allow changes via SD designation.
The staff recommendation is to adopt Option A (25 feet), reasoning that it is generally easier to
reduce a setback later than to increase it.

Public Hearing(s)/Action Items

         Ordinance No. 25-22 – Amending Riverton City Code Sections
         18(65,70,75,80,85,95,100 & 215), Commercial Dumpster Enclosures Code

Mr. Lethbridge reviewed an update to the commercial architectural standards concerning
dumpster enclosures. During previous code updates, the language regarding dumpster
enclosures was accidentally removed. This ordinance reinstates that language and consolidates
the different versions that existed across commercial zones. No new standards are being
created; it simply ensures that dumpster enclosures meet architectural requirements, including
height and screening standards. The Planning Commission forwarded the item with a
recommendation for approval.
