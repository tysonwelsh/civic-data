11            Discussion on City Property used to Access Private Property (re: Glen & Kimberly
12            Johnson) – Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director and Ryan Carter,
13            City Attorney
14
15   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that the Council will be focusing on
16   one particular piece of property where the homeowners have requested access to private
17   property owned by Riverton City. However, he said that there would be ramifications for other
18   properties in the City based on the decision tonight. The property in question is on Lovers Lane
19   and owned by Glen and Kimberly Johnson. The private property they are requesting access to
20   is the City’s open space property. Mr. Lethbridge explained that the Council needed to
21   determine if there was a process by which someone could apply or make a proposal to use
22   public property as a temporary access point. He displayed a couple of examples of previous
23   requests to access City property. Up to this point, the City has said at a staff level that public
24   property is not available for private use or access.
25
26   Councilmember Stewart said that he would like to require a bond to use this public land to cover
27   repairs.
28
29   David Brickey, City Manager, said the bonding process is very timely and not always successful
30   in getting payment. He suggested some other means for obtaining cash value and said that
31   money would be returned to the applicant if there was no damage done to the public property.
32   He believes there is more compliance if the City has cash in hand.
33
34   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, explained that if the City were doing a road cut permit, they would
35   require a fee at $0.25 per square foot, an insurance policy against third party liability, and a
36   bond of some kind. He said that calculating a bond is easier when talking about a road cut
37   permit than any other type of landscape because the cost is fixed. He believes that a cash bond
38   and an insurance policy should be required.
39
40   Councilmember Wells commented that homeowners need to be able to get to the back of their
41   house to landscape. He believes that the only thing affected in this particular access to public
42   property is weeds. The property owners are willing to pay a bond and replace any damage that
43   is done.
44
45   Councilmember McDougal said that he is against granting use of City property. He does not
46   want to put more burden on staff. When contractors are using public property, it shuts down
47   public lands and the trails are no longer usable because of damage. The City should not bear
48   the cost and have city workers tracking it and following up on it. He understands that
49   landscaping without the use of the public land will cost the homeowners more but he thinks this
50   is a cost that the homeowner should bear, not the City.
