STAFF: R. Holley reviewed the request for a Subdivision and Design Review Permit for the Altura
project on the south end of town at approximately 830 West 2071 South and wraps around the Strata
project from the same applicant and developer. This project is proposing 494 dwelling units on
approximately 29 acres that historically have been used for agriculture purposes. The MR-20 zone
allows for 20 units per acre, as proposed this project is just under 17 units per acre. The main issue
in the proposal is the street layout.


                                    Green shows the proposed streets


Multiple Land Development Code sections address street and block layouts to create well-
connected neighborhoods and efficient transportation systems for all modes of travel. Logan City
has a history of street layout that forms connected grid patterns and square-shaped blocks. Areas
of town, depending on topography, sensitive lands, or other similar barriers have permitted
deviations or modifications from the formal grid network. For approximately the last 20 years,
Logan City codes have reinforced the requirement for creating well-connected street and block grid
patterns. Limited modifications may be approved by the hearing body but must be rooted in unique
site-specific constraints.
       LDC 17.30.190 Future Street and Block Master Plans
       C. Future Street and Block Plan.
       1. All developments over five acres must include within their development proposal a
       plan illustrating how the subject property could be divided into Blocks or Mini-blocks (depending on
       the zone or overlay zone).

       §17.30.170 Connectivity Standards
       A. All streets must connect to other streets, forming a grid street pattern that extends
       Logan’s historic street grid. Mini-blocks are the preferred road configuration (See Figure
       17.30.170.A.1), except where physical conditions of the site or abutting properties preclude street
       connections. Wherever the street layout cannot conform exactly to the grid pattern due to topographic
       constraints, pre-existing development patterns, or the presence of critical areas, a modified grid which
       provides a close match shall be achieved.


Based on the standard block size this project should create three new blocks. With the proposed
north/south and east/west streets in the center of the development, one block is created on the south
side of the project site, but the two upper blocks are not bound or enclosed. When projects create
interior blocks, the developer is responsible for the construction of the entire street width. When
projects create exterior or perimeter blocks, the developer is responsible for half of the street width
(centerline). When the adjacent property subsequently develops, the other half of the street will be
installed, and the full streetscape will be completed. Considering the code language, staff finds no
reason or unique constraint as to why this proposed project cannot follow block layout standards.
Staff has requested a new design layout that complies with the Land Development Code, but the
applicant has chosen not to redesign and is looking to the Planning Commission for direction. As
conditioned with a re-submittal of the site plan showing code-compliant block patterns for the
Planning Commission to review and approve, the project meets the requirements in the Land
Development Code.
Staff has been working closely with the developer trying to comply with these code sections but feels
that they are not quite there yet.
Building frontage, for the most part, complies with the 60% minimum but a couple of areas on the
north side of the project are well below the standard. By simply rotating a building’s orientation from
the side façade facing the street to the front-facing, the building frontage percentages could increase


significantly. As conditioned with frontage compliance, the project complies with the requirements of
the Land Development Code.
For 494 dwelling units, 988 total parking stalls would be required. The project is proposed with 1056
total parking stalls (452 garaged & 604 surface) meeting the minimum stall requirements. As
conditioned with bike racks, the project complies with the Logan City parking code requirements.
The proposed project provides reasonable direct pedestrian circulation for the townhome structures
as most have front doors adjacent to the street. The 24-plex buildings orient inward towards the
parking lot and in some locations lack direct pedestrian connections to the adjacent street. As
conditioned with more direct pedestrian connections for the 24-plex buildings, the project meets the
requirements in the Land Development Code.
R. Holley stated that buildings should be oriented towards the adjacent street or common courtyard
with front facades and front doors for visual and walkability reasons. In certain cases, for practical
reasons or unique site constraints, buildings can orient away from the street, but façade
considerations (four-sided architecture) and walkability should create inviting architecture and
maintain direct walkability. As conditioned with the Planning Commission determining the impractical
nature of the design and whether buildings should be oriented toward the street, the project meets
the requirements in the Land Development Code.

 PC 22-020 10th West and 2nd South Project - Rezone [Zone Change] Michael Jewell/Triiio
 QOZB II, LLC, authorized agent/owner, request a rezone of 31.39 acres from Commercial
 Services (CS) and Commercial (COM) to Mixed Use (MU) to create a multi-faceted development
 that will include townhomes, apartments, commercial, and retail space, located at approximately
 1000 West 200 South; TIN 05-064-0013; -0028, 06-002-0003. (Ellis Neighborhood)


STAFF: R. Holley reviewed the request for a rezone of approximately 31 acres (three parcels) from
Commercial & Commercial Services to Mixed Use with the intent to develop a future mixed-use
project. All three properties are currently vacant and have historically been used for agricultural
purposes. The Future Land Use Plan shows this area as Mixed Use. The MU designation in the
General Plan is described as having high concentrations of commercial and residential uses.
Structures would typically be multi-story with projects laid out in a compact fashion that allows
residents to work, play, and live all within a walkable center.

The surrounding area currently has a wide range of commercial and industrial uses such as
restaurants, offices, light manufacturing, and storage. The Land Development Code (LDC) allows
for up to 30 residential units per acre of land in the MU zone. It allows for a “horizontal” mixed-use
pattern, which does not require every building to contain ground floor commercial and upper-story


residential but does specifically require a minimum amount of commercial square footage. For 31
acres, a minimum of 7,500 SF of commercial space shall be provided. The MU zoning district permits
a wide range of commercial uses that include, but are not limited to, restaurants, offices, retail,
hospitality, and personal commercial services. The current zoning designation of COM & CS does
not allow horizontal mixed-use buildings or projects.

Given the proximity to existing single-family residential areas to the south and the light manufacturing
areas to the northeast, a mixed-use project may act as a transitional area or buffer between
incompatible land use areas. This location is adjacent to Logan City’s gridded core, it offers better
mobility and transportation choices for future residents and puts less development pressure on the
rural and agricultural lands farther out into the valley.

When a commission or council contemplates a rezone, they need to evaluate the range of uses
permitted in that zone make sure they are comfortable with all options.

Commissioner Lucero asked if this area would be in the Ellis Elementary boundary. R. Holley said
he believes this area is in the Ellis Elementary boundary. It is in the Mt Logan Middle School, and
Logan High School Boundary.

Commissioner Lucero asked where the access to 1000 West would be. R. Holley said that UDOT
will allow an intersection every block (660 ft) onto 1000 West, so we can anticipate a stop sign
intersection onto 1000 West at Center Street and 130 South (to align with the existing 130 South
