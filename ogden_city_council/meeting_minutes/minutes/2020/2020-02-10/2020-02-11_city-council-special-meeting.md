League of Women Voters
          A memo from Council staff regarding recognition of the League of Women Voters for 100 years of encouraging
informed and active participation in government came before the Council for consideration. Council member White read the
certificate ofrecognition issued by the Ogden City Council and Mayor Caldwell and presented Terri McCulloch, President of the
Weber League of Women Voters, with the certificate.
          Ms. McCulloch thanked the Mayor and Council for this recognition. She noted the National League of Women Voters
was created 100 years ago and the Weber County branch was created in 1963. Some of the current members were part of the
initial membership of the League, including herself, and they appreciate the support they receive from Ogden City. The League is
a non-partisan, non-profit organization that advocates for voter education.

Proposed Ordinance 2020-4 amending the Ogden Municipal Code by
amending subsection 4-1 C-8.C to revise the minimum criteria for art grant
funding
         A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed arts ordinance amendments
came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the Council is asked to consider an ordinance amendment to no
longer require non-profit status for an organization to receive city arts grants for project suppmt. This proposed change does not
modify the non-profit requirement for general suppmt arts grants.
         Arts Coordinator Buckley summarized the memo and stated City ordinance currently provides that two types of arts
grants can be awarded to support and promote arts development in Ogden.
              1. General Support Grants: These grants are designed to provide general suppmt for organizations who have a
                  significant focus on arts programming. These grants are only available to non-profit organizations who have
                  their principal place of business in Ogden City.                                   •

              2.   Project Suppmt Grants: These grants may be awarded to individuals or organizations. Cunently, an
                   organization receiving a project support grant must either be a 501( c)(3) nonprofit corporation or have a Utah
                   nonprofit charter. Special Arts Grants also fit within this category, per Ogden Code Ann. § 4-1 C-8(D-2) and are
                   considered separately from the regular grant cycle.
          All awarded arts grants require City Council approval and the completion of a study showing a benefit to the City, per
Utah Code § 10-8-2. Recipients of any aits grants are required to enter into an agreement with the City to use grant monies for the
specific purpose that the grant has been awarded. The City must receive equal value in return for any grant expenditure, whether
measured in terms of tangible or intangible benefits. Administration proposes the Council consider an ordinance amendment to no
longer require non-profit status for an organization to receive City arts grants for project supp mt. This proposed change does not
modify the non-profit requirement for general suppmt aits grants.
           Council member Stephens asked if the intent of the ordinance amendments is to entice more local artists to apply for arts
grants. Ms. Buckley answered yes and indicated that each year the City notices an increase in the diversity of the group of
applicants for project support grants. She added that other agencies have reached out to Ogden City for information on the grant
program as they would like to pattern their own aits program after it.
           Council member Nadolski asked if there is any opportunity to adjust the grant award criteria to give those that have
secured non-profit status a higher score while still allowing those without non-profit status to apply for a grant. Ms. Buckley
replied that project support grants are already available to both 50l(c)(3) entities and non 501(c)(3) entities. General support
grants are typically awarded to more established programs or entities. The only way she can see to provide a higher score to non-
profit entities would be to increase the amount of grant funding available. Council member Nadolski stated he understands the
time, effort, and money that it can take to secure non-profit status and he wants to reward artists that have taken on that endeavor.
Ms. Buckley said she understands that point of view and noted that if the City is not willing to foster smaller organizations that
may not have 501 (c )(3) status, they may not grow into the larger organizations that produce treasured programming that benefits
the community.
           Chair Choberka asked for an example of a business entity that is not a non-profit organization that has received funding
in the past. Ms. Buckley cited a grant application from Wasatch Coffee Roasting Company to create their originality art gallery.
They provide arts events throughout the year, such as poetry readings and music for First Friday Arts Strolls. They also
continually repaint their alley and it has become a destination point for other artists in the community.
           Chair Choberka introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2020-4, entitled:
                   "An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Ogden Municipal Code by amending Subsection 4-lC-8.C
                   to revise the minimum criteria for arts grant funding; and by providing that this ordinance shall become
                   effective immediately upon posting after final passage."
           A copy of the proposed ordinance was deposited with the Deputy City Recorder and ordered that the City Recorder have
at least one copy available for public inspection in her office during all business hours.
           Chair Choberka then called for public input regarding the proposed ordinance.
           Alicia Washington stated she is heavily involved in the arts in Ogden. The conversation tonight has been interesting and
she noted the shift from the distinction between for-profit and non-profit entities to individual and business applicants. People
have spoken much more passionately about the artistic work done by an individual versus artistic work done by a business.
Businesses seem to apply for grants and then bring in one person to work on the arts endeavor. Conh·ast this with the individual
who creates a greater impact on the community at large and pursues programming that is more inclusive and diverse. She
suggested the wording of the ordinance be examined to clarify that a business entity should not be able to apply for project grants.
Those grants should be reserved for individual applicants.
          Ms. Buckley responded to Ms. Washington's comment, stating the City has had several individuals apply for grant funds,
but if the ordinance were amended to deny small organizations the ability to apply for project grant funds, that would hinder those
organizations and they may not grow into the larger organizations the City desires.
           Chair Choberka clarified she believes Ms. Washington was suggesting that the individual who will be responsible for art
programming at a business be the applicant rather than the business itself. Ms. Buckley stated she interpreted Ms. Washington's
comments to mean she felt that only individuals should be allowed to apply for grant funding. She wants to be sure because there
are many local organizations that apply for grant funds. Some of those are limited liability corporations, but they are truly small
business organizations. This led to discussion about various business classifications based upon their income level, after which
Chief Deputy City Attorney Brown noted that the ordinance is intended to provide support for smaller business organizations that
do not have other opportunities to secure grant funding in the community. Ms. Buckley added that the application vetting process
is very intense.
           Council member Nadolski asked if the A1ts Advisory Committee voted to support this ordinance amendment. Ms.
Buckley replied this is not an item that requires a vote of that body, but they are aware of it. Council member Nadolski stated that
he would have liked to hear advice from the Committee. He also noted that he hopes the focus of project grants is on the quality of
projects being promoted, along with their passion and purpose.
           Council member Lopez asked if a business entity applicant must provide financial documentation to provide their need
for the grant funding. Ms. Buckley answered yes and noted that applicants have been denied in the past because their fmancial
documentation did not prove there was a need for public mt funding.

          Council Policy Analyst Mabey mentioned it is important to clarify that the proposed ordinance amendment is a minor
text adjustment intended to specify that business entities can apply for project support grants. She stated the context of the          _J
discussion was good but recommended to focus the discussion on the change currently being considered.
          Council member Lopez asked ifthere are any safeguards in the grant application review process to balance the number of
grants that are given to private businesses versus individuals. Ms. Buckley answered there has not been a need for such a
safeguard in the past because there is a good mix of applicants each grant cycle. She noted project support grants are truly for arts
projects and the applicants for those grants are usually individuals or arts entities.
          There was general discussion regarding the potential implications of the proposed text amendment. Council member
White asked whether the city itself can apply for these grants. Ms. Buckley indicated that there would be concerns with conflicts
of interest and city arts projects have other funding available.
