Wayne Pyle, Chief Executive Officer, presented proposed Resolution 20-09 that would
extend a loan to the Housing Authority of West Valley City and authorize the execution of
a promissory note.

Written documentation previously provided to the Redevelopment Agency included
information as follows:

       The Housing Authority recently acquired property located at 1739 West Lake Park
       Drive and 1755 West Lake Park Drive. These properties were purchased by the
       Housing Authority for purposes of redevelopment in the area that would have
       potentially been financially structured similar to the former Harvey Street Project.
       As the development prospects in the area are evolving and the utilization of private
       sector Opportunity Zone funding becomes a matter of timing and as the
       redevelopment timelines are adjusting it has become necessary for the Housing
       Authority to create a mechanism by which they can transfer these properties to the
       RDA when it is appropriate and necessary for deal structure purposes so that the
       redevelopment can proceed while also leaving open the option to utilize the funding
       mechanisms available from both the Housing side and the RDA side in the
       redevelopment. As originally envisioned, the project funds would be put together
       and proposed utilizing methods very similar to the Harvey Street Development
       which incorporated federal, state and local dollars as well as nonprofit and private
       sector dollars. Because there are private sector developers interested in investing
       opportunity zone funding and because those dollars are, by federal law, required to
       be invested by the end of the year 2020 and further because working through a
       HUD development process with require a substantial amount of time, (potentially
       years) staff would like the flexibility of having these properties in the ownership
       that is most advantageous so that development deals can be brought forward in a
       more swift manner potentially utilizing more private dollars rather than federal
       housing or RDA dollars. This arrangement provides a broader ability for the RDA
       to push to market rate non housing based deals even in light of the very significant
       costs of purchasing the ground in the area. While the staff works to put together
       various deal options to bring to the City Council and the Redevelopment Agency
       Board, having the greatest flexibility and ability to leverage private sector dollars
       will lead to a better overall proposal.


       The RDA has been studying the Redwood corridor and the evolution that has
       occurred over the last decade. The Redwood Corridor is now prime for repurposing
       and redevelopment with growing demands for mixed use spaces. The opportunity
       to acquire these parcels of land became available and the Housing Authority
       purchased them as a hold and sell for redevelopment purposes scenario. Shortly

       after these purchases were made, Covid-19 caused an interesting pause in the real
       estate market which has increased the pressure on developers to utilize their
       opportunity zone dollars in a much shorter timeframe. “Placing” these dollars in
       an area does not ensure development or development rights and any developer with
       which the staff would recommend partnering would be brought before the City
       Council for initial partnership approval and then many times after for all
       appropriate development approvals. This project is reminiscent of the early days
       of Fairbourne Station where maximum flexibility in deal making capability and
       maximum assistance from the City Council will be necessary if redevelopment is
       to occur. This action provides the RDA with the ability to include these properties
       in the overall discussion in a way that makes sense to prospective developers and
       in a way that allows for the potential removal of federal funding strings that make
       development significantly more difficult and untimely.
