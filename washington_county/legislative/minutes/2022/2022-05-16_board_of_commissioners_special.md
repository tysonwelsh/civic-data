WASHINGTON COUNTY COMMISSION
WORK MEETING MINUTES
MAY 16, 2022

The Work Meeting of the Board of the Washington County Commission was called to
order by Chairman Iverson at 3:00 p.m. on May 16, 2022, in the Washington County
Administration Building Commission Chambers, 197 East Tabernacle, St. George, Utah.
Those in attendance: Chairman Victor Iverson, Commissioner Gil Almquist,
Commissioner Adam Snow, Deputy County Attorney Victoria Hales, County
Administrator Nicholle Felshaw, Community Development Director Scott Messel,
Convention & Tourism Director Kevin Lewis, Public Works Director Ron Whitehead, and
Deputy Clerk Cheyenne Bentley.

Others in Attendance: Matt Rhodes, UDOT; Brittany McMichael and other Convention &
Tourism staff; Alisha Low and other Community Development staff.

REGULAR AGENDA ITEMS FOR CONSIDERATION:

DISCUSSION OF SPECIAL EVENTS IN THE COUNTY, THE SPECIAL EVENT
PERMITTING PROCESS, AND THE PROVISIONS IN THE COUNTY’S

SPECIAL EVENTS ORDINANCE, TITLE 3 OF THE WASHINGTON COUNTY
CODE

Community Development Director Scott Messel explained that events with over 75 people
must apply for a special event permit with the County Office in order to hold an event.
Detailed plans of the event must be submitted and include health and safety factors like
porta potties, and routes.

Tourism Director Kevin Lewis suggested that policies should be put into placed based on
community and event size.

Events are categorized based upon the following risk factors: High-risk events include
bicycle races, marathons, triathlons and Ironman. Medium risk events include running and
sporting events, and film production. Low risk events include weddings, symphony, and
art festivals.

Deputy County Attorney Victoria Hales stated that special events code sections could be
drafted and/or changed for the size and risk factors of the events. Potential changes could
include fee increases, changing participation thresholds, and stronger consequences for
non-compliance.

It was also suggested that there should be a higher fee for road use events, increased fee
for expedited permits, allowance for multi-year permitting, and an event calendar of all
planned events for the year. Fees should be more in line with other municipalities and
entities such as UDOT. Multi-year permitting and other code changes could streamline
special event permitting, and make it easier for repeat events and applicants.
