BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY
This application is a request for approval of a Development Agreement Amendment for
approximately 21 acres located on the south side of Kimballs Ln, at approximately 632 E
Kimballs Ln (Exhibit C & D). The property is currently zoned RM2. The applicant is
requesting the City review and adopt design standards and a concept site plan for the
property.

The application includes six properties. Two of the properties contain no structures and
have been used for agricultural purposes. The other four contain single family homes built
in 1947, 1952 (two of the homes), and 2005.

The City Council approved a Development Agreement, Zoning Map Amendment, and Land



Kimballs Junction                                                App. # 2025-0293-DA
Development Agreement Amendment Request           1
Use Map Amendment on June 17, 2025 for five of the six properties. These applications
allow for the property to be built with multi-family residential at a density of 25-27 dwelling
units per acre. The Development Agreement also called for 30 work force housing units to
be built on the property and that the applicant would work with the City on a concept plan
to bring back in an amendment. On October 21, 2025 the City Council approved a Zoning
Map Amendment and Land Use Map Amendment to the property located at 625 E 11900 S
to the RM2 zone and Residential High-Density land use designation. The current application
will formally add the property into the boundaries of the Development Agreement.

The City Council approved the Kimballs Station Area Plan on September 2, 2025. This
master plan looks at overall development within a ½ mile radius of the Kimballs Lane TRAX
Station. The plan called for multi-family housing on the subject property. The
recommendations for the Kimballs Lane Station have been included as Exhibit H of this
report.

200(C) and (D). This section depicts the required materials for such requests as:

        C.   Procedure: All development agreements, upon proper execution, shall be
             recorded with the respective County Recorder's Office, shall run with the land,
             and shall be binding on all successors in the ownership of the affected
             property. A development agreement shall contain, at a minimum, the
             following:
              1. A legal description of the land subject to the development agreement.

                  development standards and the provision of public facilities.

Staff has reviewed the Development Agreement Amendment proposal. The Engineering
Division has some additions that they would like included in the Development Agreement
Amendment. Public improvements, such as sidewalk, park strip, curb and gutter, are
required on both Kimballs Lane (11800 South) and 11900 South, except as modified by the
Development Agreement Amendment. Where this project is effectively cutting off the
potential extension of the public portion of 11900 South to connect to a public street in the
future, a public cul-de-sac is required at the end of the public portion of the street to
provide a turn-around that meets city standards. The cul-de-sac provides the required turn-
around space for garbage trucks, snow plows, and emergency vehicles, as well as any
public traffic using 11900 South. The concept site plan currently shows a hammer head
turn around at the end of 11900 South, which may be impeded by the proposed collapsible
bollards. Emergency services would be able to remove the collapsible bollards to turn
around, but garbage trucks, snow plows, and the public would not.

2A: Unified Pattern of Development
The Kimballs Station development will adhere to a cohesive set of site design
standards intended to promote high-quality architecture, pedestrian connectivity,
and a vibrant, livable community environment consistent with Draper City’s Station
Area Plan.

Site Organizations and Layout

The site is designed around a connected street and trail network that prioritizes
pedestrian movement and access to transit. The layout emphasizes walkability and
permeability, with direct connections to the Kimballs Lane Trax Station, the East
Jordan Canal Trail, and internal open space amenities.


Architectural Character and Building Placement

Architecture will reflect a high-end, contemporary design aesthetic utilizing durable,
quality materials that complement the surrounding urban fabric. Architecture will be
consistent with the images shown in these Design Standards. Variations in rooflines,
façades, and setbacks will be employed to create visual interest and avoid
monotony.

Open Space and Landscaping

Landscaping will be integral to the project’s character, with the East Jordan Canal
serving as a central open space feature. A continuous, landscaped pedestrian trail
will run along the west side of the canal. Additional trails link the pocket parks to
the clubhouse and pool area. Planting palettes will incorporate native and drought-
tolerant species to enhance sustainability and reduce long-term maintenance needs.

Access, Circulation, and Parking

Internal streets will vary in size according to their level of traffic use. All internal
streets with be privately owned and maintained by the Kimballs Station
Homeowners Association. Roadway and alleyway widths are described in Sections
2B and 2C below.

                                                        2B: Setbacks & Parking Standards

                                                        The Kimballs Station community is designed to balance density and livability within the context of
                                                        Draper City’s Station Area Plan, which envisions a cohesive redevelopment pattern within a ½-mile
                                                        radius of the Kimballs Lane TRAX Station. Setbacks and building separations in this project
                                                        contemplate the future build-out of the entire Station Area Plan, ensuring that this community
                                                        integrates seamlessly as adjacent properties within the Station Area Plan are redeveloped in the
                                                        future.

                                                        Building Setbacks and Separation

                                                        Residential buildings will generally be spaced a minimum of 10 feet apart with setbacks designed
                                                        to enhance the streetscape and create usable outdoor areas. Where possible, building placement
                                                        will reinforce a defined street edge, with garages and driveways positioned to reduce visual
                                                        dominance along public roadways such as Kimballs Lane and 11900 South.
                                                        Buildings will be placed a minimum of 10 feet from the boundary lines of adjacent residential
                                                        properties within the Station Area Plan. Additional landscaping elements such as trees, berms,
                                                        swales, or shrubs will be used in areas directly adjacent to existing residential homes to buffer the
                                                        two uses.

                                                        Driveways and Parking Design / On-Street Parking

                                                        Driveways serving individual units will maintain a minimum depth of 18 feet to allow for vehicle
                                                        parking. Parking throughout the site will be designed to ensure convenience and efficiency.
                                                        Perpendicular parking stalls will be 9 feet x 18 feet, and parallel parking stalls will be 9’ x 20’.
                                                        Townhome parking requirements are 4 spaces/unit (2 garage spaces, 2 driveway spaces).
                                                        Condominium parking requirements are 2 spaces/unit (1 garage or covered parking space plus 1
                                                        driveway or covered parking space). Additional guest parking will be spread throughout the site.
                                                        Current Draper City code requires each unit to provide 2.25 parking spaces (2 per unit + 1 guest
                                                        stall per 4 units). Applying this matrix, Kimball’s Station is required to provide 1,260 parking stalls
                                                        through a combination of garage, street, and guest off-street parking. This site plan proposes a
                                                        total of 1,375 parking spaces.
                                                        On-street parking will be limited to one side of the street to maintain safe travel lanes and
                                                        accommodate emergency vehicle access. No parking will be allowed in private alleyways.

                                                        Garage and Façade Relationship

                                                        Garages will be designed to be subservient to front porches and building façades, reducing their
                                                        visual prominence and supporting an attractive, pedestrian-oriented streetscape. Architectural
                                                        treatments, varied setbacks, and porch features will be employed to ensure garage doors do not
                                                        dominate the public view.

                                                        Roadway and Alleyway Width Standards

                                                        All primary and secondary roadways will accommodate two-way traffic, on-street parking (one
                                                        side), emergency access, and pedestrian safety within a compact, walkable street network. These
                                                        roadways will be at least 27’ wide from front of curb to front of curb. All alleyways will be 20’
                                                        wide, accommodate two-way traffic, prohibit parking on both sides, provide access to garages and
                                                        support safe, low-speed shared use between vehicles and pedestrians.

2D: Building Heights and Massing
The Kimballs Station development is designed to establish a high-quality, urban
residential environment that aligns with the vision and density objectives of Draper
City’s Station Area Plan.

Height and Urban Form

Building heights within Kimballs Station will reflect a fully realized Station Area
buildout, promoting an urban residential character consistent with the City’s long-
range plans for this district. The project will feature a mix of 3- and 4-story buildings
that create a strong street presence, reinforce a pedestrian-oriented scale, and frame
public spaces and internal streets with visually engaging façades.

Building Height Standards

Building heights will vary by product type and location to create architectural diversity
and interest throughout the community. 4-story condominium buildings shall be a
maximum of 60 feet in height, measured from the average adjacent finished grade to
the average pitch of the roof. 3-story townhomes and condominium buildings shall
be a maximum of 50 feet in height, measured from the average adjacent finished
grade to the average pitch of the roof.

Building Articulation

Building forms will be broken up using horizontal and vertical articulation, balconies,
varied rooflines, and changes in materials and colors. These elements will ensure
visual richness and maintain a comfortable human scale despite the higher density
consistent with the images shown on this page.

Roof Forms, Architectural Elements, and Covered Parking Structures

Rooflines will vary across building clusters to enhance visual interest while
maintaining a cohesive overall aesthetic. Pitched, flat, or parapet roofs may be
utilized as appropriate to each architectural style. Building entries and corner
elements will receive additional design emphasis through articulation, glazing, and
materials that enhance the project’s urban character and contribute to a unified
neighborhood identity.

                                                      3A: Open Space Framework
                                                      The Kimballs Station community plan provides functional open space in the form of
                                                      trails, clubhouse and pool/hot tub area, playgrounds, gathering and seating areas, dog

                                                      density rights. Open spaces will not only provide recreational opportunities and visual
                                                      relief within a dense urban setting but will also serve as essential components of the
                                                      project’s stormwater management, pedestrian circulation, and neighborhood identity.

                                                      The East Jordan Canal is integrated into the project’s design as a public-facing amenity
                                                      with a walking trail on the west side of the canal and enhanced native plantings. This
                                                      trail will extend from Kimballs Lane on the north to 11900 South on the southern end.

                                                      Pedestrian pathways connect all parts of the community with the goal of Trax ridership
                                                      in mind. Trails are designed for accessibility, safety, and comfort. Trails connect key
                                                      nodes such as pocket parks, open lawn areas, the clubhouse and pool area, and
                                                      seating areas. A crosswalk connecting the project to the Kimballs Lane Trax Station will
                                                      further align with Draper’s vision for increased use of public transit.

                                                      Landscaping Design and Materials

                                                      Landscaping throughout Kimballs Station will establish a unified aesthetic that
                                                      complements the architecture and reinforces the urban character of the community. A

                                                      ensure sustainability, and minimize long-term maintenance needs. Street trees,
                                                      plantings, and buffer zones will be carefully arranged to define edges, provide shade,

                                                      open spaces remain attractive, functional, and safe over time. All common space
                                                      landscaping will be owned and maintained by the Kimballs Station Homeowner’s

3B: Site Amenities

Clubhouse Area

Kimballs Station includes a very robust amenities package. A Clubhouse, hot tub, and pool area are planned to

privacy, and aesthetic appeal.

Design and Integration

The clubhouse building materials, rooflines, and detailing will complement the surrounding townhome and condominium
architecture, reinforcing a cohesive community identity.

parapets, and cornice details may be used to provide shadow and articulation, reinforcing
architectural character and continuity.

Hi Jennifer,

I received the notice regarding the upcoming hearing for the proposed development at 632 Kimballs Lane. As
a Draper resident living within approximately one-tenth of a mile of the proposed site, I have serious concerns
about the potential traffic impacts associated with this project.

Based on the information provided, the development appears to include roughly 500 housing units. If each
household averages two vehicles, this could add over 1,000 additional cars to an area that already
experiences significant congestion.

Currently, 900 East already has heavy traffic. I do not believe 900 East, which functions as a one-way street
with nearby housing and businesses, could safely accommodate this additional volume. Kimballs Lane is also a
one-way road and serves as an access route to both Lone Peak Hospital and Juan Diego High School. Traffic
during school hours is already extremely congested. In fact, we have had vehicles strike our fence along this
road due to speeding and congestion.

Additionally, the trailhead and TRAX station in this area attract many pedestrians, including families and
students. Many people walk along these roads, yet sections of the south side of the street do not have
continuous sidewalks, creating further safety concerns.

For these reasons, I strongly believe a comprehensive traffic and pedestrian safety study should be
conducted before approving a development of this scale. While I do not oppose development in the area, the
number of units currently proposed and the surrounding road layout raise significant safety concerns for
residents, students, pedestrians, and emergency access.

Thank you for your consideration/understanding to our neighborhood concerns.

       infrastructure along both 700 East and Kimballs Lane does not appear to be designed to accommodate
       this level of increased traffic. In addition to resident vehicles, the city should also consider the volume of

       many families, students, and community members walking through the area daily. Increased vehicle
       traffic could create additional safety concerns for pedestrians.

       Kimballs Lane has long been a quiet and peaceful residential area. Introducing this number of housing
       units into such a small footprint could significantly alter the character of the neighborhood and the
       reasons many residents chose to live here.

       While I understand the need for thoughtful growth, the number of units currently proposed—combined

       emergency access.
