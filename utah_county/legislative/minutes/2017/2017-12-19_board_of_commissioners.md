8.     ADOPT (OR DENY) AN ORDINANCE TO AMEND THE UTAH COUNTY LAND USE
       ORDINANCE, SECTION 6-3, RELATED TO THE WATER SUPPLY REQUIREMENTS FOR
       PLANNED SUBDIVISIONS
(Track A, 08:50)
Brandon Larsen of the Utah County Community Development Office explained an applicant is working on subdivision
application for four lots. The applicant is proposing that irrigation water requirements be reduced for five-acre lots
on steep grades which are 35% unsuitable for cultivation and crops. Commissioner Lee commented on the staff
recommendations, and Mr. Larsen confirmed people would need to apply for the exception in each instance; the
applicant should be responsible for producing evidence on those conditions. Commissioner Ivie asked about the long-
term consequences of reducing requirements since irrigation water recharges the aquifer reserves. Mr. Larsen noted
that was not considered in the review and suggested that information be added as part of the required engineering
study.

Commissioner Lee added comments about the differences between ground water and aquifer recharge areas. Brandon
Larsen noted the ordinance could be updated to consider those ideas. Commissioner Ivie said he would feel more
comfortable with ‘covering the bases’ that way to avoid unintended consequences. Commissioner Lee mentioned this
particular property runs off into the lake and would not affect reserves. Commissioner Ivie replied the ordinance
would apply to all of Utah County – they need to think about Santaquin and Hobble Creek and other potential impacts.
Mr. Larsen volunteered to revise the proposed ordinance to include these issues and return for discussion in two weeks.

            Commissioner Ivie made the motion to continue Regular Agenda Item No. 8
            regarding an ordinance to amend Utah County Land Use Ordinance Section 6-3,
            related to the water supply requirements for planned subdivisions, for two weeks

11.   APPROVE AND AUTHORIZE THE COMMISSION CHAIR TO EXECUTE THE CO-
      SPONSORSHIP OF THE UTAH COUNTY ARTS BOARD FOR 2018
(Track A, 16:05)
Rochelle Wise, President of Utah County Art Board, outlined the purpose and organization of the Art Board. She
submitted for the record copies of the Art Board schedule and a construction proposal (copies of which can be found
attached to these minutes). Ms. Wise talked about the use of art gallery space in the Utah County Health and Justice
Building, mainly kept running by ten to fifteen volunteers donating hundreds of hours each year. She noted the
accomplishments in the last year of twelve shows, creating and maintaining a website, and compiling a source book.
The source book is an incredible opportunity to showcase professional local artists and advertise the art gallery. The
Art Board plans on raising $2,000 this year to distribute the book to local galleries and various businesses.

Rochelle Wise requested an increase in funds from the county, from the current $2,400 per year or $200 each month
up to $3,600 per year or $300 each month. She explained the Art Board is a small organization and all of the money
is allotted back into the community for art supplies, musicians, art judges, awards, advertising, and website renewal
costs – any increase will directly serve the community. Ms. Wise also spoke of goals for 2019, including the creation
of two display cases for small sculptures, jewelry, and other three-dimensional art. The commissioners discussed
procurement rules with Utah County Civil Division Chief Deputy Attorney David Shawcroft, and fundraising goals
with Ms. Wise. Ms. Wise summarized the Board may be able to help get sponsors and Commissioner Ivie asked her
to contact his Administrative Assistant. Ms. Wise also volunteered to return with additional information.

17.    ADOPT A RESOLUTION APPROVING THE 2018 BUDGETS FOR THE GENERAL FUND AND
       OTHER BUDGETARY FUNDS OF UTAH COUNTY, UTAH (Continued from the December 5 and
       December 12, 2017 Commission meetings)
(Track A, 27:30)
Commissioner Lee summarized the proposed budget for 2018 is $86,246,114, with revenue estimated at $78,769,229.
He noted those figures have been updated by $6,700,000 since a discussion last week regarding employee salaries.
Both commissioners stated they are comfortable with the $6,700,000 amount and the productive collaborative efforts
with Department Heads and Elected Officials to determine the use of those funds. Utah County Clerk/Auditor Bryan
E. Thompson commented on the ranking process between offices and noted some departments can use un-spent
monies from this year for final purchases in 2017. Utah County Sheriff James O. Tracy and Danene Jackson of the
Utah County Clerk/Auditor’s Office confirmed the $300,000 carry-forward funds for body scanners, a fire truck, and
kitchen equipment are included in the current schedule for 2018. Ms. Jackson mentioned re-appropriation may be
needed before the end of the year. Mr. Thompson explained the departments have been asked to identify any purchases
which will not be received by the end of this year, as required by county principles, to carry forward into next year.
Commissioner Lee pointed out the county is ‘tightening up’ the process and used employee benefits as an example.
He explained past practice was to estimate for family plan insurance prices if actual selections were not available; this
usually led to inflated figures. It is important to have actual rates to accurately maneuver the budget.

(Track A, 35:25)
Commissioner Ivie thanked the Department Heads and Elected Officials for coming together and considering overall
fiscal restraints. He noted their input and cooperation has been phenomenal and is indicative of the spirit of Utah
County. Commissioner Ivie acknowledged there are concerns regarding employee compensation and staffing
demands but the right steps are being taken and efforts will continue throughout the year. Commissioner Lee stated
he fully echoes Commissioner Ivie’s comments, adding his hopes that meetings can be expanded to give more
individuals an understanding of the whole budget process; this will help the county work together. Utah County
Attorney Jeffrey R. Buhman agreed with the commissioners, noting it was beneficial to coordinate with other
departments and see their priorities. Mr. Buhman called this the best budget process he has seen in his eleven years
as County Attorney.

Utah County Sheriff James O. Tracy added his commendations for the transparent budget process this year. Referring
to decisions made by previous commissioners, Sheriff Tracy also pointed out the budget ‘can has been kicked down
the road’ and it cannot be kicked any further. Critical services for the county must be considered, as well as a way to
fund them.
