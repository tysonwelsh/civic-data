1. Discussion regarding a request to convert the existing structure (commonly 
known as the Pace-Archibald Store or General Store) into a coffee shop through 
the Preservation of Historically Significant Structures provision of the Snyderville 
Basin Development Code.  As part of the proposal, the applicant is requesting to 
improve an existing shed into a restroom; 4459 S.R. 224; Parcel PP-108-111; 
Grady Kohler, applicant– Amir Caus, County Planner     
 
Due to the early completion of the site visit, the meeting began at 4:45.  Recording and 
recording keeping began shortly before 5:00.

A discussion ensued about the historical significance of the structure.  Is it truly 
historical and should it be designated as such?  The majority of the Commission 
indicated they believe that it should be designated as such.   
 
Chair Peck asked about replacing the roof to protect the interior wood.  Can the original 
roof be replicated?  Mr. Kohler said it is unknown what materials the original roof was 
made of.  He reported that Roger Roper, of the Utah Historical Society, said the roof may 
have been made of metal.   
 
Chair Peck asked Mr. Kohler what materials they are considering for the roof.  Mr. 
Kohler said the architect came up with a design to preserve the inside of the building, 
while obtaining the R-value required by the Building Department.  This design 
incorporates a pre-engineered metal roof system with built in insulation panels.  Chair 
Peck asked if solar panels could be placed at another location and not on the roof of the 
building.  Mr. Kohler said that according to Mr. Roper solar panels are permitted on 
historic buildings because they are considered a removable aspect.   
 
Kirk Huffaker, an architectural historian, was in attendance at Staff’s request.  He has 
been involved with the historical preservation of structures for over 20 years.  He 
agreed that solar panels are considered temporary and are not only permitted, but 
encouraged.  He added that historical structures can be modernized.   
 
Commissioner Stevens said she believes the proposed half-walls surrounding the 
building detracts from the structure.  Mr. Kohler said it was the architect that proposed 
having concrete pony walls.  He has a vision of preserving the history of the building 
while modernizing it at the same time.  The pony walls are intended to help with safety, 
aesthetics, and durability.

Chair Peck asked if a different element could be used.  It could be something that 
reflects the materials found in the structure.  Commissioner Stevens said the wall hides 
the historic pieces of the building they are trying to show off.   
 
Commissioner Cooke said he has concerns about the proposed sliding glass doors and 
the additional windows.  He would like to see the chimney preserved.  He thinks the 
space around the structure is as important as the structure itself.  The pony wall and 
some of the exterior details betrays the historical nature of the structure.  More period 
appropriate materials should be used.  
 
Commissioner Cooke asked Attorney Thomas about the restricted use covenant.  If the

the applicant would have to come back to the Commission for that to be allowed.  It 
would be a change of use.   
 
Commissioner Fine questioned if some of the proposed changes decrease the historic 
significance.  He is concerned about losing the essence of what this building was.  He 
wants to see the wood preserved.  The glass door takes away from the subdued lighting 
that would be natural to the building.  Mr. Huffaker said the Commission doesn’t want 
to allow changes that would remove the building from being eligible for the national 
historic register.  He said there are a lot of gray areas that are open to interpretation.

outdoor lighting.  He was uncertain if the Commission wanted amortization to remain 
or be taken out of the amendment.  He requested further instruction.  He also asked if 
there is any language found in the ordinance that the Commission would like to amend.

The public hearing was opened.  Mr. Yager repeated he is concerned about the new structure

Commissioner Dickey said he was unable to attend the meeting where the amortization 
policy was discussed.  He asked if there is an estimated cost to the residents for

amortization time period.  He noted these were smaller towns.   
 
Chair Peck asked the Commission if they believe seven years is an acceptable time 
period.  She was skeptical when she first heard this policy, but has come around.  
Commissioner Stevens said the community should be made aware of this well before 
the seven years are up.   
 
Commissioner Harte said he thinks Summit County should lead by example.  He is for 
amortization.  It will be important to get the word out to the public.  Commissioner Fine 
asked why seven years was chosen.  Could it be six?  Could it be five?   
 
Commissioner Cooke asked about the soffit lighting.  Some fixtures will not be easy to 
retrofit, such as architectural lights.  Director Putt said he doesn’t think that will be a 
problem.  Soffit lighting can be turned off.  He added there is a lot of remodeling in the

Commissioner Dickey asked if they should make the time period less than seven years.

1. Discussion regarding an amendment to the Kimball Junction Neighborhood 
Planning Area Plan contained in the Snyderville Basin General Plan- Patrick Putt, 
Community Development Director  
 
Director Putt said specific amendments are being proposed to the Snyderville Basin 
General Plan.  This meeting is the beginning of the public process.  Hopefully, the 
amendments better articulate the future of Kimball Junction as seen through the eyes of 
Staff, the Planning Commission, and the public.  Kimball Junction is often the focus when 
discussing the Snyderville Basin.  The General Plan states that site-specific detailed 
neighborhood plans are to be developed.  
 
Director Putt outlined what the following:

Director Putt said the hope is that the amendment will outline the vision for Kimball 
Junction during the next 25-30 years.  He explained that whenever the Code is 
amended, in a way that impacts property rights, there has to be a legitimate public 
purpose having a positive impact to the public good.      
 
Director Putt said it is obvious there are transportation issues; however, the 
amendment is not a Transportation Master Plan.  It will augment the Snyderville Basin 
Master Transportation plan.  At the most fundamental level, the amendment is an 
explanation of the neighborhood, the existing problems, opportunities, and the 
direction they want to take.  
 
Director Putt said the County Council established a committee to work on developing a 
new vision for the Kimball Junction area.  This committee has gone by different names.  
The current neighborhood plan is approximately ½ of a page.  He listed some of the 
committee members and thanked those who were in attendance.   
 
Director Putt displayed a slide of the area that this study included.  The plan took into 
account the Utah Olympic Park (UOP), even though it is a stand-alone neighborhood.  It 
was realized that the UOP and Kimball Junction impact each other.   
 
Director Putt described the discussion and review process that took place during the 
committee’s meetings.  They were able to narrow down the discussion items to a “Top
