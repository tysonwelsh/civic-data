A.     Redistricting Options
City Recorder Tangee Sloan explained the deadline for redistricting was May 10, 2022. Assistant
City Attorney David Quealy explained applicable State and City Code and said the goal of staff with
proposed redistricting maps was to be consistent with past practice and have a total population
deviation of less than 4%. He described the method used to reach deviation calculations (using 2020
Census data) and explained other redistricting principles. The ideal district population was
calculated to be 29,240, with four Council districts.

Vice Chair Green asked how future annexation would affect the proposed districts. Mr. Quealy
responded that speculating on populations based on future actions, even with an agreement in
place, would not result in a legally viable map. Responding to a question from Council Member
Worthen, Mr. Quealy said new district maps would be in effect for the next election cycle. Vice Chair
Green asked how often the City was permitted to redistrict. Mr. Quealy said he was not aware of
anything that dictated when redistricting was permissible but stated that solid population data was
required.

Mr. Quealy presented three legally viable redistricting options for Council consideration: a
quadrant-based district map similar to what was currently in place with changes based on Census
data; a horizontal district map with lines running east to west; and a vertical district map with lines
running north to south. Mr. Quealy commented that lines on the vertical district map were quite
straight along major corridors. Responding to a question from Council Member McConnehey, staff
said the number of households in the four districts on the proposed vertical district map were:

A majority of the Council indicated they were in favor of eliminating the horizontal district map
option. Council Member Jacob said he wanted to make the decision based on demographics and
issues. He commented that District 1 on the proposed vertical map faced very different issues from
District 4 on the proposed vertical map. Council Member Jacob said he would prefer to align the
areas that had a lot in common. Council Member Worthen said she believed both the quadrant map
option and the vertical map option would accomplish that goal.

Council Member Bloom commented that proposed vertical District 4 would grow to be significantly
larger than the other districts by the time redistricting occurred again. She suggested the proposed
quadrant map would divide the growth up over two districts. Council Member McConnehey said he
liked the idea of spreading future growth over two districts rather than one. Council Member Bloom
asked if it would be possible to align the proposed districts in a legally viable way that would
accommodate some of the anticipated growth.

Council Member Bloom said she would prefer the proposed quadrant map unless the proposed
vertical map could be adjusted to assume future growth. Staff responded there was not a lot of room
for change. Chair Whitelock expressed concern that the proposed vertical map would continue the
perceived east-side-versus-west-side component. She said she agreed having similar demographics
and similar concerns within districts was important. Council Member Pack said he believed the
proposed quadrant district map made the most sense. Council Member Jacob commented that the
proposed quadrant map would still have an east side and a west side component. Vice Chair Green
responded that the four quadrants met in the middle. Council Member McConnehey said the
districts in the proposed quadrant map felt more compact.

Council Members McConnehey, Green, Pack, and Whitelock expressed a preference for the proposed
quadrant district map. Staff said the next step was to schedule the issue as a business item on a
future meeting agenda. Vice Chair Green suggested scheduling a public hearing for input on both
the quadrant and the vertical maps. Chair Whitelock said she agreed and requested help from the
Mayor’s Office in promoting the public hearing.

B.      City Code Review Processes and Procedures
Council Office Director Alan Anderson said staff were prepared to put together a process and
procedure for a systematic review of City Code. Chair Whitelock said she viewed City Code as one
of the Council’s primary responsibilities. City Administrator Korban Lee commented that staff
would be involved in the review process. Chair Whitelock said she wanted the Council to have first
review, and to then forward direction to staff. Vice Chair Green said the Council would be sensitive
to the fact that review of City Code would take time and would involve many stakeholders.

C.      Crossing Guards
Vice Chair Green expressed concern that when schools went virtual during the pandemic, City
crossing guards were essentially told not to show up, and that they would not get paid. To assist
with this issue, he drafted a policy that would provide an opportunity for crossing guards to be
assigned some type of work by the City in the event of the School District switched to virtual learing,

so crossing guards would still receive a paycheck. He further explained that crossing guards had a
tough job, and it was tough to recruit and keep them. He wanted to send a clear message that the
City appreciated the crossing guards and cared about them.

Council Member Jacob pointed out that money had already been budgeted for their salaries and felt
the City should pay the crossing guards if the School District switched to virtual school.

Council Member Pack expressed appreciation for crossing guards and all volunteers. He then asked
about liability and the feasibility of staff being able to provide oversight in directing alternative
work and payment. He spoke of setting a precedent, and wondered what happened with Parks
Department employees if it rained for a week and they were not able to do the work they were
tasked to do. Council Member McConnehey said he agreed with the proposal in concept but wanted
more detail.

Vice Chair Green pointed out the proposed policy only covered the actions of a non-City jurisdiction.
Council Member Worthen said she agreed with the policy covering situations outside of the City’s
control and believed the crossing guards should get paid without other work being assigned.
Council Member Bloom agreed that crossing guards should get paid.

City Attorney Rob Wall was confident he and David Quealy would be able to help Vice Chair Green
ensure the policy was legally defensible. Police Chief Ken Wallentine reflected that last year the
Police Department made alternative work available to crossing guards if they wanted to work when
the School District switched to virtual learning. He reported that only one crossing guard took
advantage of that opportunity. Chief Wallentine was not sure if the demand was there for indoor
work, however, he was certain they would accept payment without work. He commented that the
crossing guard budget was already overspent and that school principals had more control than he
did over crossing guard hours. He mentioned that he had officers assigned to schools who also did
not have work when the school went virtual. He personally did not believe in paying people for not
doing work. The Chief explained that police officers often filled in when crossing guards called in
sick, and he believed his department had been sensitive to the situation. He reported of an email
sent to crossing guards during the most recent virtual school situation offering alternative work if
desired, and only one employee had taken advantage in the past.

Council Member Pack said he was in favor of looking at options to facilitate alternative work, but
not in favor of enabling getting paid for not working. Council Member Jacob said in general he did
not agree with paying people for not working, but said the situation was government imposed, and
gave the example of unemployment benefits.

Council Member Worthen did not view the situation as paying people for not working. She said she
viewed it as payment in circumstances out of their control. She further added that she did not think
crossing guards needed to come to City Hall for alternative work to get paid if the School District
switched to virtual school.

Vice Chair Green wanted code in place to take individual personalities out of the situation. City
Administrator Korban Lee said he loved where the Council’s heart was, however, it was clear, from
Chief Wallentine’s comments, that the City had already been operating in the way Vice Chair Green
was recommending with a list of alternative options for those who wanted to continue to work and
receive pay. Mr. Lee had concerns with putting the practice into code, explaining that he felt it would
be operational overreach taking away supervisory discretion and flexibility.
