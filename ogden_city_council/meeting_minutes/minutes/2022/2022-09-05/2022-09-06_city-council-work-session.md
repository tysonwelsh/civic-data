Proposed Zone Text Amendment to Regulations for Metal Accessory
Buildings

Planning Director Brierley explained Mike Miller has submitted a petition to allow non-architectural metal sided
accessory buildings over 400 square feet on lots smaller than one-half acre. The current ordinance allows the case-by-case review
and approval of accessory buildings larger than 400 square feet if they are sided with architectural metal or non-architectural
metals on lots larger than one-half acre. The ordinance does not allow an accessory building over 400 square feet made with non-
architectural metals on lots smaller than one-half acre. Mr. Miller applied for a permit to replace a wooden sided one-car garage
with a larger two-car, metal sided garage and was denied based on the current ordinance. Mr. Miller has filed a petition to amend
the ordinance to allow the type of garage he is requesting. Mr. Brierley discussed historical consideration of similar proposed text
amendments, after which he communicated the Planning Commission reviewed the proposal at its June 1, 2022 meeting and
forwarded a recommendation of approval. The Commission’s recommendation was based on the finding that the amendment is
consistent with the General Plan of creating design guidelines that protect the integrity of neighborhoods.

There was brief discussion among the Council and Mr. Brierley regarding opportunities for locating accessory buildings
in the side-yard if a property owner does not have a sizeable backyard; architectural standards/styles of approve metal building
materials; and the approval process for a metal accessory building.

Proposed Rezone of Property at 480 7" Street

Deputy Planning Manager Simpson explained petitioners Mark Koehler, Ken Crockett, and Mike Bastian have filed a
petition to rezone property at 480 7" Street. The property is the site of a church that is no longer being used. The petitioners are
seeking to develop single-family homes on the site. The petitioners have indicated that the R-1-5 zoning, with its smaller setbacks
and lot size requirements, allow for a more efficient layout for the new subdivision that would eliminate remnant parcels or
excessively larger parcels. The subject area is addressed in the Hillcrest/Bonneville Community Plan, adopted in 2004. Vision
Strategy 6.A. discusses the support for and the preservation of the single-family nature of the area:

“Support and preserve the existing single-family nature of the area through appropriate zoning classifications
(about five units per acre). Multi-family should be limited to existing developed area within the community.
New multi-family should be directed near to and along 12th Street and Washington Boulevard under the
guidance of a corridor plan.”

In addition to the community plan support, the General Plan’s land use policies lay out best practices when considering
rezoning property. The Planning Commission report provides further detail on how the proposal meets the applicable policies.
However, in summary, the planning staff outline how this proposal meets three goals including: creating a definite edge to a
development and buffering between uses, reflecting the prevailing character of the district, and avoiding having zoning boundaries
cut across individual lots or developments. The R-1-5 and R-1-6 zones have similar purposes as smaller lot single-family
residential zoning classifications. The main difference between the two zones is minimum lot size, 5,000 square feet for the R- 1-5
and 6,000 square feet for the R-1-6, and slightly different setback requirements. The Planning Commission report details the
differences and provides an analysis of surrounding lots. The R-1-5 zoning would not be inconsistent with many of the existing
lots in the area. The Planning Commission reviewed the proposal at its July 6, 2022 meeting and forwarded a recommendation of
approval. The Planning Commission’s recommendation was made with the finding that the proposed rezone will provide a
definite edge to development and buffering between types of uses, will reflect the prevailing character of the overall district, will
not cut across lots or developments, and that it is supported in the Hillcrest/Bonneville Community Plan.

Discussion among the Council and Mr. Simpson centered on the history of the subject property, which was first acquired
by the LDS Church 140 years ago. The construction of the existing homes around the church building occurred shortly after.

Council member White asked if the building lots in the proposed project will be large enough to accommodate accessory
dwelling units (ADUs). Mr. Simpson answered yes; the lots would be large enough to provide for attached or detached ADUs.

Proposed Nonprofit Grant Program - $1.3 Million American Rescue Plan Act
(ARPA) Funding

Management Services Director Brown explained City Administration is proposing the creation of a grant program for
nonprofit entities funded with $1.3 million of ARPA funds. The minimum grant under the program would be $25,000 and must be
for essential capital improvement projects. Preference will be given to those organizations that provide youth or family services.
The grant program eligibility criteria are as follows:

e Must be a nonprofit 501(c)(3) with a physical location in Ogden.
Must be in operation as of the date of grant application.
Must be free of any city, state, or federal tax liens or judgments.
Must supply documentation to support application.
Must have a minimum annual revenue of $250,000.
e Funds must be obligated by December 31, 2024, and spent by December 31, 2026.
The City will evaluate the following criteria when selecting grant recipients:
e Type of services provided with preference given to youth and family support services.
Description of essential capital improvement project to be funded.
Identified total project costs and other funding sources leveraging the project.
Collaboration with other community-based agencies or city departments.
Geographic distribution of services.
e Organizational sustainability.

Applications will be submitted to the Comptroller’s Office. All applications will be reviewed by the City’s loan
committee consisting of the following individuals: Mark Johnson, Sara Meess, Jonnalyne Walker, Buck Froerer, Brandon Cooper,
and Korahle Jensen. The loan committee will determine which nonprofits receive grant funding. The Fiscal Year 2022-2023
Budget adopted by the Council on August 2, 2022, included an appropriation of $1.3 million of ARPA funds for a nonprofit grant
program. No additional budget action is needed.

Discussion among the Council, Council staff, and City Administration centered on general program guidelines, such as
whether an administrative fee for the application program will be charged; minimum and maximum grant awards; and the manner
in which unawarded money will be used/appropriated.

Council Business

Ogden Wildfire: Chief Administrative Officer Johnson provided the Council with an update on the status of the wildfire
on the bench above Rainbow Gardens; the residences are secured, and the fire is mostly surrounded. The fire is relatively small, at
five to seven acres in size, but there is a lot of fuel on the property that can burn easily. Twenty-five homes have been evacuated
and a local church in the area will accommodate the up to 80 residents who may not have another place to go. There have been no
injuries and the Fire Department is using water from Beus Pond to fight the fire.

Boundary Adjustment with North Ogden City: Council member Hyer reported he has had some conversations with
North Ogden Mayor Berube regarding the past decisions made regarding the proposed boundary adjustment to allow a
development to occur in North Ogden City. He stated that North Ogden’s leadership is happy to consider a 50-50 revenue split for
the property upon the development being completed, but there are other issues to be considered, such as harmonious development
standards, appropriate setbacks, and access to amenities that will benefit both communities. He asked if the Council would like to
reconsider the decision that was made. The Council discussed the matter with Planning staffand Mr. Johnson, with a focus on the
options available to them for reconsidering the boundary adjustment petition. Three members of the Council ultimately concluded
they are willing to reconsider the boundary adjustment, so long as the applicant and North Ogden can provide assurances that the
development will take into consideration Ogden City’s development standards in an effort to protect Ogden residents from
negative unintended consequences of the project. They indicated they feel that North Ogden planning staff and the applicant
should do the leg work to resurrect the application. Council member Hyer stated he is willing to communicate the Council’s
desires to the North Ogden Mayor.

Short Term Rental Properties: Council member White stated she has been participating on a sub-committee created by
the Utah League of Cities and Towns to consider legislation for that would aid cities in monitoring, enforcing, and licensing short-
term rental properties.

Community Renewable Energy Program: The Council briefly discussed the status of the City’s participation in the
program created by the Community Renewable Energy Act (CREA) and next steps. Council Executive Director Eller-Smith stated
that the next decision point for the Council is by the end of this calendar year; ifthe City opts in, it would be necessary to develop
planning documents to be submitted to the Public Service Commission. That body would then determine a participation rate for all
entities who have opted into the program, after which the Council would have the option of adopting or rejecting an ordinance that
would solidify the City’s participation in the program. Council member White asked when the City will hire a sustainability
employee that can assist the Council in navigating this program. Mr. Johnson stated that he hopes to advertise the position within
the next 30 days; other positions had a higher priority and Human Resources has been working to fill them. Council member
Choberka stated she believes the City’s Sustainability Committee would help Administration develop an appropriate job
description for the position. She added she would like to hold a fact-finding work session regarding the CREA. The Council
discussed the proposal and concluded by expressing a willingness to participate in such a meeting in the near future. Council
member Richey stated it would be important for the Council hear information from individuals or groups on all sides of the issue.
Council member Choberka agreed; she acknowledged the Act and program are very complex and it is important for the Council to
have all pertinent information to inform their decision. Chair Nadolski concluded that his biggest struggle has been the fact that
the Council does not have enough information about the costs and impacts of the program; until he has clear information about the
potential cost for City residents, he is uncomfortable moving forward.
