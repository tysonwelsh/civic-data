5.    Cazier Excavating Office; 5548 West Bagley Park Road; Conditional Use Permits for: 1.
      Auto operations and fleet vehicle maintenance facilities; 2. Fuel, oil and heating oil
      distribution and storage facilities in the Drinking Water Source Protection Overlay Zone;
      and, 3. Outside storage and operations; M-1 Zone; Bertoldi Architects/Ray Bertoldi
      (applicant) [#19842; parcel 26-01-354-002]

Scott Benson, with Cazier Excavating, said the property was subdivided a year ago. The business
operations are moving from Draper. The building will house eight offices, conference room, etc. and a
three-bay shop for maintenance. They have 50 employees. They are running nine dump trucks right
now, leaving once in the morning and returning in the afternoon. Most employees go straight to the job
site, so primarily only the office staff is at the building.

Kent Shelton asked for details on the fuel storage.

Scott Benson explained the dual containment system for the 12,000-gallon aboveground tanks. There
will be three pumps; diesel, dyed diesel, and regular fuel.

Ammon Allen asked if they hold meetings requiring all employees to be on-site at the same time and,
if so, is there sufficient parking for 50 employees.

Scott Benson said yes, there is plenty of parking.

Lisa Elgin explained the three conditional use permits. All fleet maintenance and repair will take place
inside the building, except for the truck wash area. The water from the truck wash has to be disposed
of in the sewer rather than into the storm drain. Fuel storage has a dual contained tank, so as long as
those tank types are used, exterior containment isn’t required. Thirdly, all outside storage and
operations will be screened with a precast concrete wall on the east and north sides and a chain-link
fence with slats on the west side. Elevations for the fence that runs from the front of the back building
to the subdivision wall need to be submitted. The gate has to meet code and be a solid material.

8.    Text Amendment – West Foothill District; Amend the 2009 City Code Adding Article K
      West Foothill District to Title 13 Chapter 6; City-wide applicability; City of West Jordan
      [#19973]

Larry Gardner turned the time over to Ivory Development, who was the genesis of the amendment.

Chris Gamvroulas, president of Ivory Development, said they have been working with staff for several
months. They have 565 acres, which have been owned by the Lamar Jones family since 1949. He
described the current conditions of the property and surrounding area. The city master plan shows part
industrial and part business research park. They don’t know quite how this will develop out, but the
amendment will create guidelines so there is predictability when they talk to potential businesses. They
would like to move the property out of agricultural zoning but give flexibility to users that will be
major employers and to be able to respond to the market. They want to be proactive in addressing
some uses such as commercial/retail and hospitality. By knowing that those are approved uses will
give them confidence to work with potential businesses.

Bryon Prince, vice president of Ivory Development, said the project fulfills the long-term general plan
for sustained commercial use and securing the scale for a large regional employment center. It provides
versatility and flexibility for economic development opportunities. The vision is for a landscape berm
along U-111. The uses would be inclusive of those allowed in the M-1 and BR-P, with some ancillary
uses that provide a mix of industrial, retail, office, commercial, and higher density residential. There
will be a master development plan and design guideline document attached to any property in the West
Foothill Zone. It will guide cohesive landscaping, fencing, signage, wayfinding, architecture, lights,
etc. GOED and edcUtah have been looking for large-scale sites, which has guided this ordinance. They
Planning & Zoning Commission Meeting Minutes
January 21, 2020
Page 8

want to capture some demand for research park but would include uses that go along with research
park like commercial, hotels, banks, and other things that will be sustainable with the critical mass of
daytime traffic. They envision some higher density housing that will help with the impact on
transportation and the environment. That would be capped at 10% of the overall site for workforce
housing to those who work in the business park. The more meaningfully point of the ordinance is that
any property within the West Foothill zone would have design guidelines. He gave examples of
landscaping that would be maintained by the HOA. Pedestrian connectivity throughout the business
park is envisioned from land use to land use with trails and place making.

Larry Gardner said 10% of the 600 acres could potentially be multi-family/workforce housing. This
will be a job creation center with a minimum of 30 and maximum of 60 units per acre within the
business park setting. Hopefully the majority of the people will be working within near proximity. He
pointed out that the landscape plan in this area will be based on water conservation principles from
Jordan Valley Water Conservation District, so there may or may not be sod.

Bryon Prince said Jordan Valley Water Conservation District put out a letter with standards for
outdoor space, which aligns with their vision. There are attractive ways to provide buffers and
landscaping without using Kentucky bluegrass. Lake Park is a good example with an attractive entry,
cohesive look and feel, and proximity to residential. The details of the landscape vision would be in the
design guidelines and will encourage creativity.

Larry Gardner said the best thing about this type of ordinance is to provide a consistent look and feel,
unlike what the current industrial ordinance requires. He showed a business park type in Overland
Park, Kansas. They incorporate different amenities, a mix of uses, and housing within the business
park. Retail tends to be on the hard corners with small grocery and food establishments. Buildings
aren’t necessarily tall, because they have the ability to spread out. The parks have a consistent
architectural theme, so you know that you are in the park. He felt that this ordinance will allow for
something to be proud of.

Staff recommended that the Planning Commission forward a positive recommendation to the City
Council to amend the 2009 City Code adding Article K West Foothill District to Title 13 Chapter 6.

Chris Gamvroulas said they have an opportunity in West Jordan to develop in a consistent manner and
not as piecemeal zoning and infrastructure. They want to take a more holistic approach. They have
been researching the requirements for utilities and looking ahead to preserve area to make sure they
can accommodate different types of users. The diagonal road is a challenge for some industrial users,
and there is 200 feet of fall that will need retaining, etc., but if they can plan the area then they can
respond to economic development opportunities. The Jones family has entrusted them as development
partners because they are looking at the project holistically, and they are looking to benefit all parties,
including the city.

Bryon Prince explained that they have been working on the utility analysis and involving West
Jordan’s consultants and staff to understand the infrastructure and utility requirements. The very first
questions they get from GOED deal with land use and infrastructure. This park will secure flexibility
on the land uses and it will have a cohesive master plan to accommodate the infrastructure needs.

Matt Quinney asked if there are building height restrictions.

Matt Quinney asked how likely it will be that they will get to the potential 3600 units of multifamily.

Matt Quinney said he went to the Olympia Hills meeting last week. The talk was about live/play/work

level, so they may have more income. People will have opportunities to live there if they choose to.

Matt Quinney thought this would make a great research hub for Utah State.
