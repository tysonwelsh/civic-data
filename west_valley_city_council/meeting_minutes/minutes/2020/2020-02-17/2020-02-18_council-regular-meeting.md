B.   ACCEPT PUBLIC INPUT REGARDING APPLICATION ZT-16-2019,
     FILED BY APC TOWERS, REQUESTING A ZONE TEXT CHANGE TO
     AMEND SECTION 7-7-406 OF THE WEST VALLEY MUNICIPAL
     CODE, PROVISIONS APPLICABLE TO MONOPOLE TOWERS
     Mayor Bigelow informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular
     Council Meeting scheduled February 18, 2020, in order for the City Council to
     hear and consider public comments regarding application ZT-16-2019, filed by
     APC Towers, requesting a Zone Text Change to amend section 7-7-406 of the West
     Valley Municipal Code, provisions applicable to monopole towers.

     Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
     information as follows:

            Currently the maximum height for a monopole tower in any zone is 60’ and
            the maximum width, including the antennas, is 24 inches. If the tower is
            stealth, there is some flexibility in some of the standard requirements.
            Stealth is defined as follows:

                   7-7-402 Stealth. A communications antenna, facility and/or
                   Structure that is completely disguised as another object or otherwise
                   concealed from view thereby concealing the intended Use and
                   appearance of the facility and where the dimensions of the facility
                   reasonably approximate the dimensions of the object they are being
                   disguised as and the location of the Stealth facility is in concert with
                   its surroundings. Examples of Stealth facilities include, but are not
                   limited to, trees, synthetic rocks, flag poles, light pole standards, or
                   architectural elements such as dormers, steeples, and chimneys.
                   Final determination regarding Stealth poles shall be made by the
                   Community and Economic Development Staff based on these
                   standards. All antennas and associated equipment not camouflaged
                   in a manner to be completely disguised as another object or
                   otherwise be concealed from view by concealing the intended Use
                   and appearance of the facility are not Stealth.

            However, APC Towers feels that in some instances a stealth application
MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING – FEBRUARY 18, 2020
                        -5-

           may be difficult to achieve and would look more out of place than just the
           tower itself. They also stated that if the allowable tower height and width
           were increased it would provide more opportunities for colocation which
           could reduce the number of tower sites needed. Therefore, they are
           proposing to amend the monopole tower standards to allow for a 100’ tower
           with a full antenna array (36” width) for properties located within the M
           Zone and are also designated as Heavy Manufacturing in the General Plan.

    Mayor Bigelow opened the Public Hearing.

    Nephi Garcia, representing APC Towers, stated that the majority of Codes across
    the nation encourage co-location, including West Valley City’s ordinances. Mr.
    Garcia detailed his experience with monopoles, City codes, and co-locations. He
    indicated that he feels the Code should be different for manufacturing zones. Mr.
    Garcia stated that phones operate more complicated now and it’s important that
    monopoles are able to accommodate the various carriers in the area. Mayor Bigelow
    asked why a stealth unit wouldn’t accommodate as many carriers. Mr. Garcia stated
    that a pine tree would stand out and a palm tree or water tower can only
    accommodate two carriers.

    Mayor Bigelow closed the Public Hearing.

    information as follows:

           The applicant is proposing to rezone a portion of this property to
           accommodate a restaurant tenant that wishes to operate a commissary
           from this location. The existing auto sales/service use would also like to
           have some space for outside storage. These two uses are only allowed in

A.   ACCEPT PUBLIC INPUT REGARDING APPLICATION ZT-15-2020,
     FILED BY WEST VALLEY CITY, REQUESTING A ZONE TEXT
     CHANGE TO CREATE THE RESIDENTIAL SUSTAINABILITY (RS)
     ZONE
     Mayor Bigelow informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular
     Council Meeting scheduled February 11, 2020, in order for the City Council to
     hear and consider public comments regarding application ZT-15-2020, filed by
     West Valley City, requesting a Zone Text Change to create the Residential
     Sustainability (RS) Zone. He indicated that this Public Meeting was held but this
     application was continued by the City Council.

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

              At the request of the City Council, City staff has prepared a zone text
              change application to create the Residential Sustainability (RS) zone. The
              purpose of this zone is to encourage single unit dwelling development that
              generates its own power, is highly energy efficient, conserves water and is
MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING – FEBRUARY 18, 2020
                        -11-

         long lasting. No property is proposed to be rezoned to the RS zone. This
         ordinance simply creates the RS zone in the Zoning Ordinance. Anyone
         requesting the RS zone would be voluntarily agreeing to follow the
         standards outlined in the zone. As an incentive to utilize the zone, 8,000
         sq. ft. lots are allowed.

         After reviewing the original proposal from staff, the Planning Commission
         recommended some changes that are outlined in the table below:

                Standard                 Original Ordinance        PC Recommendations
         Maximum Zoning District               5 acres                 No maximum
         Size
         Energy Efficiency                 Calls out certain          HERS Score of 30
                                        features like insulation
                                          and tankless water
                                                heaters
         Type of Solar Panels              Tesla Solar Roof             Not specified
         Type of Electric Car            Tesla Wall Connector         Level 2 (240-volt)
         Charger

         Since the initial direction for this ordinance came from the Council, the
         proposed ordinance does not incorporate the Planning Commission’s
         recommendations.

         After suggesting changes at the February 11, 2020 City Council Meeting,
         staff proposed the following modifications:

               The Annual Fuel Utilization Efficiency (AFUE) rating for gas
                furnaces was increased from 95% to 96%.

               Options for water heating were expanded to include hybrid water
                heaters and other types of water heaters with a Uniform Energy
                Rating of at least 0.92.

               Kitchen faucets were added to the list of plumbing fixtures that are
                required to meet WaterSense standards.

               The maximum slope for lawn areas was decreased from 33% to
                25%.

                   Overhead sprinklers were limited to lawn areas. A pressure
                    regulator, filter and flush-end assembly were added as required
                    elements of a drip system.

                   Allowed materials for paths were added along with a requirement

    Mayor Bigelow noted that while a Public Hearing was already held, the Council would
    still like to make Public Comment available if members of the public still wished to
    speak.

    Mayor Bigelow opened the Public Hearing.

    Danny Gutierrez requested a change to the Tesla Roof requirement. He indicated
    this roof is more expensive but is not as efficient. Mr. Gutierrez expressed concern
    that it would be difficult to market this type of roof and stated that it is important to
    make a development sustainable but also affordable.

    Upon inquiry by Mayor Bigelow, Steve Pastorik, CED, stated that Tesla Roof is
    more expensive. Steve indicated that Tesla Roof’s are tiles and not solar panels. He
    briefly explained the difference. Steve explained how the proposed ordinance is
    currently written to state “Tesla Roof or equivalent” and indicated that this would
    limit the roof type to tile. The Mayor and Council discussed tile roofs versus panel
    in both aesthetic sensibility and efficiency. They also discussed roof pitch and
    sunlight. Councilmember Buhler stated that the ordinance is new and may need
    modifications and changes down the line but it’s important to start somewhere.
    Councilmember Nordfelt stated that Development Agreements allow for
    modifications. He indicated he doesn’t want the ordinance to be too narrow and
    indicated that providing for solar panels may be a good allowance. Councilmember
    Lang stated that she feels it would be better to hold developers to a higher standard,
    particularly since the Council has conceded in lowering many other standards for
    this zone. Mayor Bigelow agreed and noted that energy efficiency is important, but
    it is still important to have some type of standard.

    Danny Gutierrez indicated that Rocky Mountain Power has talked about decreasing
    rebate allowances in January 2021. He indicated that tile roofs are not proved and
    are very expensive. He stated that he doesn’t believe he can build a sustainable
    community at an effective price with this requirement.
