CONSENT AGENDA APPROVED:

NO PUBLIC COMMENT

GREATER NEPHI CHAMBER OF COMMERCE UPDATE:

   Executive Director of the Greater Nephi Chamber of Commerce, Abby Ivory updated the council on
the Chamber’s membership, projects, meetings, and goals. Ms. Ivory reported that the membership had
reached 100 members. She presented a Welcome to Nephi card that show places to shop, lodge, eat
and see while visiting Nephi. She reported on conferences she had attended, and she said that she
meets with Utah County chambers representatives monthly. She displayed an upcoming community
guide magazine that will be ready in the fall. The Chamber plans on starting an “Athlete of the Week” to
increase the Chamber’s exposure on social media. Central Valley Medical Center will be the sponsor of
the Athlete of the Week. The council expressed their appreciation for all the work that Ms. Ivory has put
into the Chamber being successful.

KIMBERLIE FULTON AND STEPHANIE HALL RATIFIED AS LIBRARY BOARD MEMBERS:

  Library Director Ruth Bonzo read the backgrounds of Kimberli Fulton and Stephanie Hall and
presented them to the council for ratification as new members of the Library Board. Ms. Hall will be the
Library Board Chairman. Councilor Parady moved to ratify the two new members of the Library Board.
Councilor Worwood seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

DEER ACRES PLAT C SUBDIVISION FINAL PLAT APPROVED:

  Mr. Atkinson updated the council that the Planning Commission had recommended final approval for
Phase C of the Deer Acres Subdivision. Deer Acres Phase C is a six-lot subdivision that is located at
approximately 800 to 900 N and 1100 E and includes dividing off around five (5) acres out of the 67.75
acres available in the total parcel for this third phase of the subdivision. The subdivision is in the R-1
residential zone with a minimum of .25 acres per lot, and 80 feet of frontage required. Verification that
each lot will have the required forty (40) pounds water pressure at the water meter that the state
requires has been the biggest concern with this subdivision. All other final plat requirements have been
met, and edits made to the plat based on the DRC notes. The City has verified with Sunrise Engineering
that the state mandated 40 lbs of pressure will be met at each meter in the lots of the subdivision.
   Councilor Callaway moved to approve the final plat for Deer Acres Subdivision Plat C. Councilor
Parady seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

ZONE CHANGE FOR DIRECTRIX DEVELOPMENT - ORDINANCE 06-20-2023-A ADOPTED:

   Mr. Atkinson explained that Several years ago, the city entered into an agreement with Directrix
Corporation (Mad Dog Knives) to sell a city-owned property for the purpose of a business expansion for
Mad Dog Knives. The parcel was a triangle piece adjacent to I-15 and Sheep Lane.. The parcel was
originally annexed as part of the Biglow Annexation and was zoned Highway Commercial 1 (HC-1).
Based on the activities that have been conducted at their current location on Main Street, the zoning for
the business would be better suited to Light Industrial (ID-3) and light industrial is not currently allowed
use in the HC-1 zone. The Planning Commission has reviewed this rezoning request and is
recommending it to the City Council.
   Councilor Ostler moved to adopt Ordinance 06-20-203-A that changes the zone of the property
owned by Directrix from HC-1 to ID-3 zone. Councilor Worwood seconded the motion. The motion
passed unanimously.

RESOLUTION 06-06-2023 APPROVAL OF INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT WITH JUAB COUNTY FOR THE
ADMINISTRATION OF THE 2023 MUNICIPAL ELECTIONS ADOPTED:

   Mr. Atkinson reported that Governor Cox had issued a proclamation requiring that all municipalities
use county services to run the municipal election. This is, in part, due to the early retirement of
Representative Chris Stewart and the need for a special election. The dates of municipal primary and
regular elections have changed in accordance with the special election. In order for the county to
administer the City’s 2023 elections the adoption of Resolution 06-06-2023 and the approval of a
memorandum of understanding between the county and the city is necessary.
  Councilor Parady moved to adopt Resolution 06-06-2023 and to authorize the the Mayor to sign the
MOU with the county. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion.

CERTIFIED PROPERTY TAX RATE ADOPTED:

JUAB COUNTY/NEPHI CITY JUSTICE COURT AGREEMENT APPROVED:

   In June of 2021, Nephi City entered into an agreement with Juab County that defined the sharing of
justice court costs including the justice court judge, court clerks, and facilities. That agreement ends
June 30, 2023. The City Recorder, City Attorney, Chief of Police, and City Administrator met with county
officials at the end of February to discuss drafting a new agreement. The new agreement states that the
court clerk will be an employee of the County and Nephi City will pay 56% of her total compensation and
pay $32,000 for the justice court judge with yearly increases to both payments. Nephi City will also pay

TREES DECLARED SURPLUS PROPERTY:

    Mr. Atkinson updated the council that there are several trees in the Vine Bluff Cemetery and in
Pioneer Park that are at their end of life. These particular trees may be in demand due to their wood
quality. The demand may be such that it would exceed the $1,000 limit in the surplus policy for the City
Administrator to declare them surplus property and to elicit bids for the trees. Mr. Mark Simmons, who
is a woodworker, commented that the trees are not that valuable because the removal of the trees
would be dangerous and difficult. Mr. Atkinson recommended that the proceeds from any surplus sale
be allocated to the cemeteries.
    Councilor Ostler moved to declare the trees surplus property. Councilor Callaway seconded the
motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

UTAH MUNICIPAL POWER AGENCY AGREEMENT APPROVED:

  Mr. Atkinson reviewed a lease agreement with UMPA that is for land near the south substation
where UMPA is in the process of constructing a power generation station. Nephi City is a member of the
UMPA and will benefit from the power generation station. Councilor Callaway moved to authorize the
Mayor to sign the lease agreement with UMPA. Councilor Worwood seconded the motion. The motion
passed on a unanimous vote.
