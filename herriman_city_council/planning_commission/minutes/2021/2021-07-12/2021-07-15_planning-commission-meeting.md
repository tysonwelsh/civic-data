agreement. He informed the Commission elements of the draft Olympia Master Development Agreement

part of the report from the County and impacts and costs were being reviewed. Commissioners requested

administrator were explained. The Community Development Director would make decisions based on input
from other directors. If discrepancies warranted further discussion items may be referred to Council for a
decision. Commissioners articulated concerns with lot sizes, designated pedestrian crossings and street widths.

Planning Manager Clint Spencer reviewed the request for an approximately 6,400 square feet retail building with
four potential tenants including one restaurant, two retail and one other flex space in the Anthem Commercial
development. The proposal met the fifteen percent landscaping requirement and included 44 parking spaces.
The setback was reduced fifteen feet along Herriman Main Street which included a two-foot berm. This was the
first application under the new Engineering department procedures, which moved the full Engineering review
from after the Planning Commission meeting to before Commission approval. All engineering concerns have
been addressed. The consideration of issues for conditional use items included harmony with the surrounding
development, suitability, aesthetic impact, service issues, and economic impact and all were met. The Anthem
Commercial Group had approved the architecture plans. Staff recommended both the colors and material of
the dumpster enclosure match the main building. Commissioners requested traffic flow with the two drive-thru
windows.

engineering process worked well. He explained the traffic engineer completed a full traffic report and he
identified stacking locations for the drive thru windows. Signage would clearly identify each entrance. Attractive
architectural features were added to the back of the building, which faced Main Street. The plat would be
recorded prior to building permit approval. This would be constructed in conjunction with item 3.2, the dentist
office which will leave all the parking available for the restaurant in the evenings.

Commissioner Sickles would like to see block sizes limited to around 400 square feet to make it more walkable.

Lead project consultant for the developer Eric Longvardt approached the podium and expressed the opinion a
typical block size was 660 feet. He explained multiple factors, such as the existence of trails, made any block
size walkable. Much of road planning lined up with existing roads and longer block lengths were more conducive

City Planner Maloy reviewed sample flag lot regulations and described specific requests. The current ordinance
increased standards and restrictions and did not distinguish between minor and major collector roads. The
benefits of flag lots were improved revenues, increased population, increased private property value, and
preservation of existing structures. Proponents thought flag lots discouraged property accumulation and
subdivision development, changed historic use of private property, potential privacy impacts, and increased
vehicular traffic. He then went through the map and detailed areas with potential safety concerns. The draft
amendments addressed the following issues, clarified the intent and language of existing City Code, allowed a
flag lot on a minor collector street with additional conditions, increased width of a flag lot private driveway to
accommodate utilities and landscaping from 20 feet to 25 feet, allowed more than one flag lot in a subdivision,
and allowed Planning Commission modification of fence standards to avoid conflicts, such as double fencing.
Commissioners expressed it should be dependent upon the specific situation, with proper setbacks, egress, and
distance from intersections.

Chair Berbert opened the public hearing.

No comments were offered.

Chair Berbert closed the public hearing.

10-29-8: FLAG LOTS
A lot in an agricultural or residential zone that does not have required frontage on a public street, and which has
    two (2) distinct parts: the flag, which contains buildable area and is located behind another lot; and the staff,
    which connects the flag to the street, provides the only street frontage for the lot, and contains less than the
    minimum lot frontage requirement for the zone.
Standards:
    1. The staff portion of a flag lot shall front on and be contiguous to a dedicated local public street or minor
        collector when located at least fifty feet (50’) from any street intersection and a minimum of five feet (5')
        from an adjacent driveway. Flag lots shall not front on a major collector or any arterial street.
    2. The minimum width of the staff portion of a flag lot shall be twenty-five feet (2025') to accommodate
        the required drivable surface, drive approach, utilities, fencing, and landscaping as needed. On properties
        where the length of the staff is greater than one hundred fifty feet (150'), the width of the staff shall be
        no less than twenty-five feet (25') of drivable surface unless otherwise may be increased if recommended
        by the City Engineer and Fire Marshal and approved by the Planning Commission.
    3. The maximum length of the staff portion of a flag lot shall be two hundred twenty feet (220') unless
        otherwise approved by the Planning Commission upon recommendation of the unified fire authority.
    4. The flag lot shall have a hard-surfaced driveway from a public street to the required parking area for the
        flag lot. Adequate provisions shall be made for drainage of the driveway such as curb and gutter,
        landscaped bermings, or swales, and fencing.
    5. A flag lot shall not be created as part of a subdivision where there are more than two (2) lots in the
        subdivision or from an illegally divided lot or parcel.
    6. A flag lot shall not gain access via an easement on an adjacent property.
    7. No building or construction, except for driveways, shall be allowed on the staff portion of a flag lot
        unless the minimum width thereof is the same or greater than the minimum width for a lot as allowed
        by the zone in which the flag lot is located (excluding entrance features and streetlights).
    8. The front side of the flag portion yard of a flag lot shall be deemed to be that side nearest to the dedicated
        public street upon which the staff portion fronts, unless otherwise determined by staff on a case-by-case
        basis the Planning Commission.
    9. The staff portion of a flag lot shall be deemed to end, and the flag portion of the lot shall be deemed to
        commence, at the extension of the front lot line.
    10. The square footage of a flag lot shall be at least one-half (1/2) acre. The square footage located in the
        flag portion of the lot, which shall be exclusive of the square footage located in the staff portion of the
        lot, shall be at least one-third (1/3) acre.
    11. The front, side, and rear yard requirements of the flag portion of a flag lot shall be the same as required
        by the zone in which the lot is located.
    12. Only one single family dwelling shall be allowed on a flag lot.
    13. The approved building envelope of a flag lot shall be illustrated upon the final subdivision plat.
    14. A six-foot (6') solid vinyl privacy fence shall be installed on all sides of a flag lot, including along both
        sides of the staff portion of the lot, subject only to the requirements of section 10-21-3 of this title
        regarding front yard fencing. Planning Commission may remove or modify this standard to avoid double
        fencing, preserve mature vegetation, or maintain clear views for public safety.
    15. All applicable street improvements, including curb, gutter, and sidewalk, shall be installed along the front
        of both the flag lot and the lot or parcel from which it is being subdivided.
