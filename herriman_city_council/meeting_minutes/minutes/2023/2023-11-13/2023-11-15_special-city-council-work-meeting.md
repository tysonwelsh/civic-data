               SPECIAL CITY COUNCIL MINUTES

                          Wednesday, November 15, 2023
                           Approved January 10, 2024

The following are the minutes of the City Council meeting of the Herriman City Council. The
meeting was held on Wednesday, November 15, 2023, at 5:30 p.m. in the Herriman City
Council Chambers, 5355 West Herriman Main Street, Herriman, Utah. Adequate notice of
this meeting, as required by law, was posted in the City Hall, on the City’s website, and
delivered to members of the Council, media, and interested citizens.

Presiding: Mayor Lorin Palmer

Councilmembers Present: Jared Henderson, Teddy Hodges, Sherrie Ohrn

Staff Present: City Manager Nathan Cherpeski, Assistant City Manager Wendy Thomas, City
Recorder Jackie Nostrom, City Attorney Todd Sheeran, Communications Manager Jonathan
LaFollette, City Planner Michael Maloy, Deputy Police Chief Cody Stromberg, Community
Development Director Blake Thomas, City Engineer Bryce Terry, and Public Works Director
Justun Edwards.

Councilmembers Excused: Steven Shields

5:30 PM – SPECIAL WORK MEETING: (Fort Herriman Conference Room)

1. Council Business
Mayor Palmer called the meeting to order at 5:34 p.m. and excused Councilmember Steve
Shields from the meeting.

2. Administrative Reports
   2.1. Discussion regarding Panorama, a proposed 350 acre planned development by
        Development Associates, Inc. – Blake Thomas, Community Development Director

      Community Development Director Blake Thomas presented the proposed Panorama
      development which covered approximately 640 acres, including various types of units,
      commercial spaces, and open areas. The discussion delved into the current general plan for
      the site, zoning considerations, and proposed land uses. The Council emphasized the need
      for a refined plan from the developer, specifying what stays and what needs to change. Issues
      regarding publicly-owned parcels, potential property swaps, and surplus processes were also
      discussed.

      The Council considered the need to amend the Interlocal Agreement with Salt Lake County
      due to the proposed changes in housing density within the Community Development and
      Renewal Agency project area. Director Thomas turned the discussion to the open space
      requirements and suggested it would be better to consolidate the open space for usability
      purposes or to consider paying a fee-in-lieu option for certain park developments to be
      utilized in regional parks.

      Director Thomas observed the proposed Public Infrastructure District (PID) and highlighted
      the implications for funding public infrastructure, such as parks. Councilmember Shields
      stated there needed to be further discussion on how to prioritize projects while considering
      the impact of the PID funding and the limitations of impact fees. The Council asked about
      potential conflicts between collecting impact fees and implementing a PID, which lead to the
      discussion of how to balance the financial aspects of public infrastructure development.

      Director Thomas continued with highlighting the three land use agreements that would need
      to be either stated and/or amended: Rose Crest Development Agreement, Wasatch
      Development Agreement, and untitled land. The Council discussed potential trades with
      Wasatch and presented a proposed master plan encompassing 185 acres. The proposal would
      include a rezone, consideration of implementing a PID, along with trail connectivity. Mayor
      Palmer inquired about the elevation change and if it would support the necessary open space
      amenities for the community. The Council discussed materials which could be used to install
      the trails while considering maintenance costs along with what would be preferred.

      Director Thomas presented the road development and commercial areas within the proposed
      plan along with their respective zoning change proposal. He asked if the Council had any
      feedback to provide to the developer as they looked to have a collaborative effort for the
      development.

      The Council expressed appreciation for the level of detail provided in the presentation and
      voiced interest in further discussions and collaboration of the proposed master planned
      development. The Council also expressed concerns about the hillside slopes, deviations from
      overlay layouts, and the potential impact on open space. Mayor Palmer emphasized the need
      for a detailed comparison of existing and proposed plans, considering factors like lot sizes,
      land uses, and commercial areas.

      A cross-section exhibit was presented, illustrating road layouts and standards, prompting
      discussions on the width of roads, turn lanes, and considerations for bike lanes and parking.
      The conversation also delved into the design of sidewalks, park strips, and the challenges of
      maintaining such features. Concerns were raised about the cost of ongoing maintenance,
      especially related to water conservation guidelines.

      Director Thomas presented the comprehensive development plan for the area, which
      received positive feedback in terms of its conceptual aspects from the Council as they
      expressed interest in moving forward but emphasized the need for detailed comparisons and
      analyses before making decisions. The Council acknowledged the complexity of the
      negotiation process, especially regarding the development agreement. The Council relayed
      support of the project based on the conceptual renderings presented and highlighted the
      necessity of the detailed analyses and comparisons prior to progressing further. The
      developer was tasked to focus on the General Plan amendments and zoning considerations
      for subsequent review by the Planning Commission.

      Director Thomas relayed the interest of the applicant in developing a challenging piece of
      land in the ravine. The Council aimed for a cohesive approach with the developer and focused
      on understanding the implications of changes to the project. The development team relayed
      concerns about potential delays due to the holiday season. The Council stated communication
      was important and suggested the information could be disseminated prior to scheduled
      Council meetings.

      The Council identified topics for future meetings, including land use, cross sections,
      engineering components, design guidelines, and amendments to existing Master
      Development Agreements. It was noted the project would be considered by the Planning
      Commission and scheduled for subsequent City Council meetings.

      3. 7:30 PM – Adjournment
      Councilmember Henderson moved to close the City Council work meeting at 7:23 p.m.
      Councilmember Ohrn seconded the motion and all voted aye.

      I, Jackie Nostrom, City Recorder for Herriman City, hereby certify that the foregoing minutes
      represent a true, accurate and complete record of the meeting held on November 15, 2023. This
      document constitutes the official minutes for the special Herriman City Council Meeting.
