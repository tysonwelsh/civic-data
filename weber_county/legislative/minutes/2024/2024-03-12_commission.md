   Todd is the new assistant director of Culture, Parks, and Recreation. We have warning of a natural event that
   will occur this coming Thursday. Todd will share what we have heard.

      Todd Ferrario (Asst Director, Culture, Parks, and Recreation): I was contacted by Emergency Services about the
      potential of a downwind event possible this Thursday and Friday. Ten years ago we had a similar event that
      blew down many structures and caused significant damage. Roads has a huge job in these events to remove trees
      off of roads. We will look out for the parks and assist where we can.

      Chair Harvey: Our structures here in Weber County are usually very well built and sound. We are hearing this
      could be a ten year high for winds anticipated to be around 90 mph. This could be a ten year high for a wind
      storm.

F. PRESENTATION:
   1. 2023 Salvation Army Ogden Core Report.
      Captain Jacob Bratton: I wanted to start with our mission statement:

                  The Salvation Army, an international movement, is an evangelical part of the universal Christian Church.
                  Its message is based on the Bible. Its ministry is motivated by the love of God. Its mission is to preach
                  the gospel of Jesus Christ and to meet human needs in His name without discrimination.

            Since the Salvation Army landed in 1897, our motto of Soup, Soap, and Salvation has endured the test of
            time. We have never required anyone to listen to a message to be saved or any other spiritual requirement.
            We simply serve because we are given the opportunity to serve and in doing so hope that someone may ask
            why we do what we do. The Salvation Army has been serving in Ogden for 137 years. We provide social
            services ranging from food insecurity services such as pantry and food vouchers, transitional housing,
            addiction treatment programs, youth programs, a thrift store, and many more while holding to core values
            such as serving suffering humanity. In Ogden, Monday through Friday from 8 to 10am we run a breakfast
            and to-go lunch serving about 100 meals a day. Monday through Friday from 10am to 1pm we operate our
            food pantry and rental and utility assistance programs serving about 60 families per day. These Pantry boxes
            include everything from canned goods to fresh produce, meats, dairy, hygiene, pet food, diapers for both
            adult and children, and baby wipes. We also work at providing specialty boxes for those with dietary
            restrictions such as vegetarians and gluten-free diets. Last year we served 29,000 meals to the community in
            Weber County. We gave out about 15,000 food boxes which equates to about 367,000 pounds of food given
            out. Over 20,000 different individuals were served that came through our doors at 2615 Grant Avenue in
            some way shape or form last year. Our Angel Tree Program helps low-income families provide Christmas
            for their children as well as we now accept seniors that are experiencing low-income difficulties. Last year
            over 2,000 individuals were served by this program. We do this with the help of amazing Partnerships
            within the community Just Serve, Weber Human Services, DWS, Easter Seals, ALCAP, the police
            department, CCS, and The United Way, to name a few. We are preparing to break ground and build a 52
            unit, double occupancy, low income senior housing facility called Silver Crest. We look forward to the
            potential revitalization of the rest of the property at 2615 Grant Ave.
         Tim Kendall, Board Chairman: I want to thank the Commission today. I have served on the Board for 15
         years and currently serve as the Chair. I think it is important to report on the impact the Salvation Army
         makes that doesn’t get a lot of media attention or notoriety. Captain Bratton and his family were moved here
         from their last station in Hawaii one year ago. They hit the ground running and have done a fabulous job.
         They have the energy and everything they need to be successful here. Silver Crest is a really big deal. We
         expect to break ground in mid-April. This is going to be a place where our vulnerable elderly to live in safe,
         secure facility.

         Commissioner Froerer: You mentioned Silver Crest. Is that going to be on the 26th and Grant site?

         Captain Bratton: Yes. The address will be 2655 Grant; it will be between the chapel and the apartment
         complex.

         Commissioner Froerer: That is a needed asset in downtown Ogden, especially at that location.

         Commissioner Bolos: Thank you for what you do in our community and for sharing this report with us.
