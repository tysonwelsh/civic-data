       A.     REZONE, “CARDENAS REZONE” A REZONE OF 2.62-ACRES LOCATED
              NEAR 1768 WEST 13400 SOUTH. THE CURRENT ZONING IS RR-22, THE
              REQUESTED ZONING IS R-4. APPLICANT- JUAN CARDENAS WITH DEL
              SOL PROPERTIES.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich presented the Staff Report and stated that the subject properties
include several properties with frontage on 13400 South and/or Redwood Road. The
applicant is Juan Cardenas, but there are several property owners involved who have all
signed Affidavits of Ownership and have acknowledged the application. The current zoning
is RR-22 and the requested zoning is R-4. Both zones are single-family detached zones.
They differ in lot size, and RR-22 includes animal rights for lots larger than one-half acre.

Mr. Prestwich displayed the current zoning map and the General Plan and explained that
the General Plan designates the area as medium-density residential with minimum 10,000
square-foot lots. Most of the properties on the east side of the subject property are one-half
acre and larger. The properties on the west side of Redwood Road are medium-density with
minimum 10,000 square-foot lots.

When the City was initially incorporated, the zoning throughout the open spaces was
designated as RR-22. Many areas have been rezoned but what remains is typically RR-22
unless it has been rezoned since the beginning. The setbacks, lot coverage percentage,
and lot frontage are the same for all single-family residential zones. The lot widths and sizes
are slightly smaller for R-4 with 10,000 square feet and 80 feet of frontage. The biggest
difference would be that there are no animal rights with R-4.

Mr. Carter stated that several comments referred to spot zoning and explained that the
concept of spot zoning is that if there is a small portion of land with different zoning than the
surrounding properties, it is automatically a bad thing. For many years, spot zoning was
considered negative and cities often would impose regulations limiting the ability for spot
zoning. In 2008, the Utah Legislature ended the concept of spot zoning. They created a
statute that states:

       “Neither the size of a zoning district nor the number of landowners within a district
       may be used as evidence of the illegality of a zoning district or the invalidity of a
       municipal decision related to that sort of thing.”

In doing so, the Legislature eliminated the concept of spot zoning; however, one issue that
is considered for zone changes indeed is if the proposed zone change would fit well with the

       B.     HOME OCCUPATION, “CAMERY’S DAYCARE” AN IN-HOME DAYCARE
              TO BE LOCATED AT 4847 WEST VICTORINE STREET. APPLICANT-
              CAMERY JACOBSEN.

Mr. Prestwich explained that as a home occupation, this is an administrative action item in
contrast to the previous item which was legislative. The final decision will be made by the
Planning Commission. The item was noticed to the neighboring property owners in
accordance with City Code. The proposed home occupation is located on the west side of
the City in the Monarch Meadows Development within the townhome project. Camery’s
Daycare is located at 4847 West Victorine Street in the Villas at Monarch Meadows,
Building #9, Unit #3. The applicant is requesting a home occupation for a daycare. She is
proposing up to 10 children per day and up to three cars per day, which would be equivalent
to four to six trips. Pick-ups and drop-offs will be staggered throughout the day, so crowding
will not be an issue. Parents and customers will park in the driveway of the home or the
neighborhood guest parking. Children must be walked to the door. Normal business hours
are proposed.

The Conditional Use is based on the fact that the applicant would like more than eight
children per day and it is in a townhome development. Mr. Prestwich displayed an aerial
view of the property, including the backyard and the parking plan. There is a main floor with
a basement and upper level and a total of approximately 1,500 square feet. The main level
and the basement, along with the backyard, will be the areas of operation. The State
mandates that there be a fenced outdoor area for children to play with the Conditional Use.

Mr. Prestwich stated that this is the first step in the process for this application. From here,
the applicant will have to file for a State Business License, which will not be issued until the
City has approved the zoning. The City will then issue a Business License based on the
State's approval. Even if the Planning Commission were to approve the application for 10
children, the State may have a different Code and only approve for eight, for example. The
Homeowners Association (HOA”) could also override the Planning Commission approval
and limit the number of children to less than 10. Included in the packet was a list of Home
Occupations that are allowed and the Fixed Standards that correspond to them.
Qualifications and Conditions are also included. The Planning Commission can adjust the
conditions as deemed necessary. A few concerns were raised regarding the supervision of
the children, parking, and traffic.
