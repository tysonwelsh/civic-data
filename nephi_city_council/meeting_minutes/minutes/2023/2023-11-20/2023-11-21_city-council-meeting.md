FY 2023 AUDIT REPORT PRESENTED BY JON HADERLIE FROM LARSON AND COMPANY:

   Mr. Jon Haderlie, representing Larson and Company presented to the Council and Mayor the certified
annual audit report for the fiscal year ending June 30, 2023. He reported that the auditors were offering
a “clean” opinion. This means that the city received an unqualified or unmodified audit opinion, and the
audit team believes the financial statements are free from material misstatement. The audit report
stated that there were no internal controls or state compliance findings. Mr. Haderlie thanked Finance
Director/Recorder Lisa Brough, City Administrator Seth Atkinson, and staff for the smooth audit process.

CONSENT AGENDA APPROVED:

  Councilor Memmott moved to approve the consent agenda which included the approval of the
minutes for the regular council meeting 11-7-23, work session minutes of 11-14-23 and a list of claims
on the warrant register dated 11-21-23. Councilor Callaway seconded the motion. The motion passed
on a unanimous vote.

NO PUBLIC COMMENT

RESOLUTION 11-21-2023 RECERTIFICATION OF THE JUSTICE COURT ADOPTED:

   Ms. Brough updated the council that per state statute, the Nephi City Justice Court is required to be
recertified at the end of each four-year term. The court’s term will expire on January 31, 2024. The
Justice Courts Standards Committee and the Utah Judicial Council will recertify the court by reviewing a
letter of opinion from the City Attorney Kasey Wright and a questionnaire filled out by Justice Court
Judge Barbara Finlinson.
   Councilor Worwood moved to adopt Resolution 11-21-2023: A RESOLUTION REQUESTING THE
RECERTIFICATION OF THE NEPHI CITY JUSTICE COURT. Councilor Memmott seconded the motion. The
motion carried unanimously.

MASTER FEE RESOLUTION 11-21-2023- INCREASING DISPATCH FEE ADOPTED:

   City Administrator Seth Atkinson reviewed with the council that in 2017 the Council adopted a fee on
utility bills to cover the costs of dispatch services provided through Central Utah 911. At that time a

$2.50/ month fee was instituted to cover operational costs for Central Utah 911 services and a capital
cost that Nephi City would need to pay to Central Utah 911 for their facilities. This capital cost of
$48,754 will need to be paid to Central Utah 911 on or before year 9 of our membership in their
service. The city had been contributing to an account in the capital projects fund from the utility
dispatch fee and had saved enough to pay the capital fee. Last year the city needed to use these funds
to pay for radios needed for dispatch services to work due to some funding changes that occurred with
Homeland Security funds.
  The city will now need to save for this capital fee again. In the adopted budget, the staff assumed that
a $0.25 increase would be added to the current public safety/dispatch fee to build up the capital fee
again as well as cover increases in operating costs from Central Utah 911. Staff has proposed this $0.25
fee increase in the attached master fee resolution and will change the fee from $2.50/month to
$2.75/month.
    Councilor Memmott asked if there will ever be any other capital outlay fee that the city will need to
pay. Mr. Atkinson answered that it is a one-time buy-in. Central Utah 911 has allowed the city to save
overtime.
    Councilor Callaway moved to adopt Resolution 11-21-2023-A. Councilor Worwood seconded the
motion. The motion carried on a unanimous vote.

WHITE MOUNTAIN OPERATING AWARDED BID FOR WORWOOD WELL REDRILLING:

   Mr. Atkinson presented the summary of bids for the Worwood Well redrilling project. There were
four companies that submitted bids. The lowest bidder was White Mountain Operating with a bid of
$499,476.00. Mr. Atkinson said that this company had recently finished a well in Moroni and have
worked with Sunrise Engineering and had good references. Mr. Atkinson stated that the city staff is still
doing some soil testing before they proceed with the project.
  Councilor Worwood moved to award the bid to White Mountain Operating. Councilor Callaway
seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.

CITY COUNCIL AND CITY ADMINISTRATIVE UPDATES:

          Mr. Atkinson updated the council that the Well-Being Survey administered by Utah State
          University is scheduled for March and April 2024. UDOT has requested to add some questions
          about transportation to the survey. Atkinson stated that the city can add questions to the
          survey. USU is asking for a $500.00 donation for the survey. The council agreed to donating
          and participating in the survey again.
         Mr. Atkinson reported that the city is halfway through the PAR tax period. He asked the
          council to start getting some perceptions from the public of how they feel about the PAR tax
          and how it was used and if it benefited the community.
         Mr. Atkinson answered a question of the council from an earlier meeting about the storm
          drain easements that UDOT is negotiating with property owners on the south end of the city.
          The property owners wanted to connect to the pipe that drains into the big pond. Because of
          federal regulations the property owners cannot connect into our pipe but the agreements say
          that they can convey their water to the pond, but the property owners would be responsible
          in obtaining easements necessary to convey the water to the pond.
         Callaway gave an update on the Christmas lighting event. He stated that Haven House would
          be providing hot chocolate and there would be hayrides and a movie at the event.
         Seely reminded everyone to support local businesses this holiday season. He said that the
          local businesses support the community in so many ways throughout the year.
         Mayor Seely reported that Representative Burgess Owens had recently visited the new
          slaughterhouse being built on Hwy 132 and he was impressed that the private sector has
          worked so well with the city. Owens stopped at the Branded Barber and was impressed with
          their efforts to support veterans. Owen also informed the Mayor that he is still working on the
          grant for the police station.

Noting no need for an executive session, the regular session adjourned.
