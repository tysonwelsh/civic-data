2. PUBLIC COMMENT
Mr. Jim Vesoca  invited the Council to attend the ribbon cutting event for the ninja 
agility course at 5:00 PM tonight at the West View Park in West Valley City.     
Mr. David Quinn shared a postcard he received from the American Association 
of Retired Persons (AARP), informing seniors that their property taxes could be 
deferred until after death.  Shortly after receiving the notice from AARP, he 
received his tax notice from the County, stating he owed $ 3,000.  He attended a 
hearing to discuss a reduction, and other taxpayers who were there for the same 
reason were present.  Mr. Quinn was upset by the lack of privacy and felt that if the 
County could afford to defer his taxes, it ought to be willing to reduce them 
instead. 
Council Member Theodore  stated Mr. Quinn could speak with Grace Hanley, 
Exempt Secretary, Treasurer ’s Office, who was present during the meeting.  She 
took a picture of the AARP notice for her records.  
Council Member Winder Newton  stated the deferral program was something 
passed by the State Legislature, and it allowed qualifying taxpayers to defer their 
taxes while paying a small amount of interest.  In the meantime, the State would help 
provide the deferred revenue to the County.  She told Mr. Quinn the Utah Office of 
State Treasurer could provide him with more information about the program and let 
him know if he qualified.

Magna City at the following approximate addresses:
 
· 2905 South 9150 West (commonly known as the “Magna Neighborhood

Park”) 
· 8802 West 2600 South (commonly known as the “Magna Copper Park 
Dog Area”) 
· 9094 West Magna Main Street (commonly known as “Mantle Park”)
WHEREAS, these parcels are more specifically described in the quitclaim 
deeds contained as part of the two interlocal cooperation agreements 
attached hereto as Attachments “A” and “B.”
WHEREAS, the County desires to formally transfer and convey the Magna 
Neighborhood Park and the Magna Copper Park Dog Area to Magna City, 
who desires to formally take and receive the same, for the purposes and on 
the terms and conditions set forth in the interlocal cooperation agreement and 
quitclaim deeds attached hereto as Attachment “A.”
WHEREAS, the County also desires to formally transfer and convey Mantle 
Park to the Community Reinvestment Agency of Magna, who desires to 
formally take and receive the same, for the purposes and on the terms and 
conditions set forth in the interlocal cooperation agreement and quitclaim 
deed attached hereto as Attachment “B.”
WHEREAS, pursuant to Utah Code § 17-50-312, a public hearing was held 
on April 29, 2025, at the County Government Center to allow an opportunity 
for public comment on the proposed dispositions.
AND WHEREAS, it has been determined that the best interests of the 
County and the general public will be served by transferring ownership of the 
three parcels and by executing the two interlocal cooperation agreements and 
the quitclaim deeds attached to this Resolution.
RESOLUTION
NOW, THEREFORE, IT IS HEREBY RESOLVED, by the Salt Lake 
County Council that the transfer of the three parcels of real property 
described herein is approved; and the Mayor is hereby authorized to execute 
the two interlocal cooperation agreements and the three quitclaim deeds in 
substantially the same form as those attached hereto in Attachments “A” and 
“B,” which by this reference are made a part of this Resolution.

12.1 Proclamation Honoring Arlyn Bradshaw for His Years of 
Service on the Salt Lake County Council
25-2919
Attachments: Staff Report
Arlyn Bradshaw Proclamation
Sponsors: SLCo Council. 
(Approx. 2:25PM, 5 Min.) 
Informational
Mr. Isaac Higham , Council Office Director, Council Office, read the 
proclamation:  
WHEREAS, Arlyn Bradshaw came to Salt Lake County as staff and aide to 
the Chair to then Councilman Joe Hatch; and 
WHEREAS, Arlyn decided to continue his career in public service by 
running for the vacant County Council District 1 seat in 2010 following 
Councilman Hatch ’s retirement, winning that election and subsequently being 
elected to office a total of four times; and 
WHEREAS, during his time serving on the Council, Arlyn served as the 
Chair of the Salt Lake County Redevelopment Agency for over 12 
Page 11 of 14
County Council Meeting Minutes May 20, 2025
consecutive years; and 
WHEREAS, Arlyn served on many boards during his Council tenure 
including but not limited to: the Visit Salt Lake Executive Board, Utah 
Performing Arts Center Board, Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Services 
Area Board, Animal Services Advisory Board, Utah State Homeless Services 
Board, Salt Lake County Board of Health, Utah State Transparency 
Advisory Board, Salt Lake City Airport Advisory Board, and a very brief 
one month appointment to the Inland Port Authority Board; and 
WHEREAS, Arlyn served on the National Board of Directors for the 
National Association of Counties representing Utah. Although his party 
affiliation was different than most of the county officials across Utah, he was 
well respected by both Republicans and Democrats. His Idaho roots 
definitely helped him relate to some of the rural commissioners; and 
WHEREAS, Arlyn was elected by his colleagues to serve as Minority Leader 
of the Democratic Caucus seven times during his Council service; and 
WHEREAS, Arlyn was known as the Council ’s steady hand, making the 
motion to approve the complex weekly consent agenda -thereby undoubtedly 
setting a record for most approved motions by a Council member that will 
likely never be broken; and 
WHEREAS, Arlyn ’s Council tenure included passionate work on animal 
welfare and Salt Lake County ’s No -Kill mission, improving air quality, 
preserving open space and the natural beauty of our Wasatch canyons, 
economic development, tourism promotion, mental health services, criminal 
justice reform, active transportation, and tireless advocacy for Salt Lake 
County’s employees; and 
WHEREAS, Arlyn was known as a collaborative, collegial, and friendly 
Council member among his colleagues -and for being a good sport 
participating in their inside jokes like Steve Debry commonly singing “You 
Can’t Always Get What You Want” to his “good friend Arlyn” and playing a 
holiday piano duet with Aimee Winder Newton; and 
WHEREAS, Arlyn is a die -hard fan of the University of Utah athletics teams 
and used that to find common ground with his Council colleagues of all 
political stripes; and

WHEREAS, Arlyn had a well -earned reputation as a straight -shooter not 
playing political games and for being dependable and reliable -especially when 
it came to reliably needing to ask his staff for a pen at the start of every 
Council meeting; and 
WHEREAS, Arlyn ’s intelligence and wisdom often helped focus Council 
deliberations, and his insights, explanations, and institutional knowledge were 
relied upon by Councilmembers from both parties; and 
WHEREAS, Arlyn was willing to make difficult decisions and had the 
courage to make bold changes that he knew would benefit the county long 
term; and 
WHEREAS, Arlyn will be dearly missed by the constituents he represented,

service, and by his County Council colleagues. 
NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that the Salt Lake County Council

Salt Lake County Council and wishes him well as he fulfills his new duties as 
Associate Deputy Mayor and looks forward to grilling him with hard -hitting 
questions as he begins his new life on “the other side of the table” during 
Council meetings.

video as a tribute to Arlyn Bradshaw ’s service as a Council member, which

Mr. Arlyn Bradshaw , Associate Deputy Mayor of Finance and 
Administration, stated he was grateful Joe Hatch took a chance on him all 
those years ago and hired him as an administrative assistant.  Eventually, he 
was able to run for Mr. Hatch’s seat.  He expressed an appreciation for all 
the other colleagues who had moved on to other things.  He also expressed 
an appreciation for the council form of government because despite all the 
division that seems to occur at the federal level and even the state level, 
Council members rarely see each other as political enemies.  He stated he had 
come to love county government because local government is in the “quality 
of life” business.
