Blaine Gehring said staff has been approached several times in the past few months about
converting existing multi-family developments into condominiums. He said condominium
conversions is the process of dividing real estate held under one title into individually owned
units that share common elements, such as exterior walls, a lobby, or recreational facilities.
Almost any type of property can be converted to condominium ownership: an apartment
building or attached housing (duplex, townhouse). A condominium development may be
very large or may be as small as eight units. This proposed ordinance provides some
important things to consider in light of the current housing affordability problems in Utah.
By converting existing rental properties to condominiums, ownership opportunities are
opened up. This creates more affordable housing units for ownership rather than the new

Millcreek City Council Meeting Minutes 8 April 2019 Page 5 of 11

townhomes being built in today’s high cost market. Older units can now be brought up to
current code standards making them safer, and in some cases, more energy efficient.

Gehring said in addition to the condominium conversion requirements, the ordinance
establishes rules for new condominium projects. These would be subject to the same
standards as PUDs when it comes to minimum size and development standards. The
ordinance amendment was presented to all of the community councils in February and all of
the councils recommended approval. The Planning Commission heard this application twice
and recommended approval of the ordinance amendment as presented.

Mayor Silvestrini asked if the Planning Commission discussed the item at their March
meeting. Gehring said they did.

1.2 Public Comment

Aaron Walker, 1068 Granite Mill Court, said Mike Winder referred to residents as the
“blight people” during a meeting in March and said that they had conspiracy theories.
Walker said he took offense to the terminology used, especially since Winder worked on
getting the term removed from state statute. He expressed concern about a future blight
study under a different name; impediment to development study. He also expressed
concern about the City bringing in apartment complexes as the only source of revenue.
Mayor Silvestrini said he sees the residents as valued residents and not as “blight
people.” He said Millcreek amended the blight study map and did not approve it based on
the public input provided. He said Millcreek is not bringing in developers. The city center
master plan is driven by residents asking for a city center. He said the development was
occurring anyway, so the Council tried to get ahead of it by planning for it. He said the
City responds to applications received and is trying to shape that process to the benefit of
the residents of Millcreek. He said the Council is trying to listen to the residents and
make Millcreek better by planning. Mayor Silvestrini said Millcreek did not advocate for
the State legislation, but it does good things. He said Millcreek was not trying to blight
anyone’s property, but to see if an area needed extra development help. He said the
legislation eliminates the Taxing Entity Committee, which nobody knew about, and will
instead use an interlocal agreement process which will be heard by the Community
Reinvestment Agency and City Council. Mike Winder said he did not recall using the
term, “blight people” but if he did, he apologized. He said it was not meant to be
disrespectful but if it was used, it was meant as short-hand for people who are concerned
about the blight study and process. He said he thought the Granite Mill Court neighbors
were fantastic and are a tremendous asset to Millcreek.

4.1 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution 19-07, Approving the Execution of
an Interlocal Cooperation Agreement with the City of Holladay for the Design and
Construction of 3900 S.
Mayor Silvestrini said Millcreek was able to obtain grant funding in the amount of $8.7
million to reconstruct 3900 S. from 2300 E. to I-215. He said that stretch of road is one of
the worst in the City and 3900 S. is the only east-west connector from the canyons to the
west side of the valley without connection to I-15. Bike lanes will be added to make it
easier to use for bicyclists. He said there is no sidewalk in Holladay from 2700 E. to I-
215 and no sidewalk in Millcreek from 2700 E. to 2300 E. The plan is to install sidewalks
in those sections, add bike lanes, have an east travel lane, west travel lane, and a center
turn lane. There will be an open house on this matter for members of the public to look at
preliminary plans and to give comment. Millcreek collaborated with Holladay to obtain
the grants so there needs to be an agreement with Holladay to construct the road.
