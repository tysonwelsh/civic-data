CONSENT CALENDAR:
     a. Consider approval of a Purchase and Sale Agreement between the City of
        St. George and The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints for
        property located at 85 South 400 East.

          BACKGROUND and RECOMMENDATION: This item is for the purchase of a former
          The Church of Jesus Christ chapel on the corner of 100 South and 400 East for a
          new Fire Station to replace the current station on 1000 East. Staff recommends
          approval.

      b. Consider approval of a Real Property transfer and Road Cost Sharing
         Agreement between the City of St. George and The Dixie Foundation.

          BACKGROUND and RECOMMENDATION: The City of St. George and the Dixie
          Foundation currently own (as tenants-in-common) approximately 12.61 acres of
          land located near the hospital facilities, which was granted to them jointly by the
          Foremaster family. The Foundation now seeks to monetize a 1.89-acre parcel
          known as Lot 4 and is asking the City to quitclaim their interest in that parcel to
          the Foundation. The City is seeking reimbursement from the Foundation for a
          portion of the extension of 1450 East to its connection with 900 South. This
          agreement sets forth the terms and conditions of the property exchange and road
          reimbursement. Staff recommends approval.

      c. Consider approval of a Purchase Agreement with Big T Recreation to
         purchase nine (9) shade structures for the tennis courts at Tonaquint
         Park.

          BACKGROUND and RECOMMENDATION: This agreement is for replacement shade
          structures for the tennis courts at Tonaquint Park. These structures are being
          purchased through State Contract ID MA3408. Staff recommends approval in the
          amount of $53,500.

      d. Consider approval of a professional services agreement renewal with

          BACKGROUND and RECOMMENDATION: This professional services agreement with

           the booster pump occupies was not included in the final plat of the subdivision

           an adjacent and subsequent phase of the development. Until

      j.   Consider approval of a quitclaim deed quitclaiming 50% of City ownership
           of a portion of 3000 East to Washington County.

           BACKGROUND and RECOMMENDATION: The Council of Governments (COG) is
           made up of local elected officials and is managed by the Washington County
           Commission. The COG manages funds appropriated by the State of Utah to
           purchase right-of-way for major future roads within the city. When funds are
           approved by the COG to purchase right-of-way, their rules require that the
           ownership be placed 50% in Washington County's name and 50% in the City until
           construction is complete. The simplest way to accomplish this is for the city to
           purchase the property in the city's name using appropriated COG funds, and then
           quitclaim 50% interest back to Washington County. Staff recommends approval.

      k. Consider approval of a Memorandum of Understanding between the City of
         St. George and G-Six Investments, LLC (the Glendenning family)
         regarding property located at 76 Tabernacle Street.

           BACKGROUND and RECOMMENDATION: G-Six Investments, LLC is seeking to
           remodel and rehabilitate a building next to the City-owned Electric Theater on
           Tabernacle Street. In so doing, repairs and changes to the City's building(s)
           regarding eave overhangs and water drainage are required. G-Six has agreed to
           make those repairs at their cost. G-Six also seeks to bury the current overhead
           powerlines to that building and seeks to work with the Energy Services department
           to contribute to the costs associated with burying those lines. In addition, the
           Theater was expanded across a pedestrian easement granted to the former owner
           and the City has agreed to relocate that easement on the west side of the Theater
           building. The applicant has also asked for a temporary construction easement on
           City owned property while they remodel. Staff recommends approval.

      l.   Consider approval of a hangar lease with the Division of Facilities
           Construction and Management and Department of Public Safety.

           BACKGROUND and RECOMMENDATION: The State, through the Department of
           Public Safety and the Division of Facilities Construction and Management, desires
           to lease hangar space to house and operate a helicopter to be used for emergency
           transport. Because this is a benefit to the City, the agreement requires only a
           nominal rent. In addition to the lease, the State desires to construct improvements
           to the hangar to include office space. The City is agreeing to hire and pay the
           contractor to be reimbursed by the State in the amount of $198,500. Staff
           recommends approval subject to review and approval of the agreement by the
           Legal Department.

AMEND CITY CODE/ORDINANCE:
    Consider approval of an ordinance amending Section 10-13E-4 of City Code
    for designating a landmark site and to amend the allowed conditional uses
    for landmarks. The proposed amendments would extend the required age of
    the structure to 100 years old and remove the option to use the landmark site
    as a short-term rental. Reference No. 2022-ZRA-001

      BACKGROUND and RECOMMENDATION: The landmark criterion was first established in
      2006. At that time, the structure was required to be a minimum of 50 years old. In
      2019 that qualification was changed to being built before 1970. This current proposal
      changes the age of the structure to be at least 100 years old. The last time a change
      was made to the conditional use portion of this chapter was in 2006 when the second,
      third, and fourth conditions were created. This proposal will remove the option to use
      the landmark site as a short-term rental. At their meeting held on April 12, 2022, the
      Planning Commission held a public hearing and recommended approval.

      The developer will not be required to pave the roadway at the time of this
      development for the following reasons: 1) The south side of the roadway would not be
      constructed with this development as the developer is only required to install 25 feet
      of pavement, curb and gutter on the north side of the roadway. Paving the roadway
      would increase the amount of runoff that drains to the properties to the west and
      south as this roadway will slope to the west and this would have a negative effect on
      these properties; 2) The property to the south is lower in elevation by approximately 8

      support the roadway improvements; 3) The developer will pay the City the cost of the
      asphalt pavement for which the developer is not installing so that these funds can be
      used in the future when property to the south or west develops and extends the
      roadway; 4) There are two existing homes that currently use the location of the
      future roadway to access their lots to the west; 5) By not paving this roadway until
      future development occurs to the west and south, the cost to the city to maintain this
      access to the two existing homes will be minimal compared to maintaining a paved
      surface.
