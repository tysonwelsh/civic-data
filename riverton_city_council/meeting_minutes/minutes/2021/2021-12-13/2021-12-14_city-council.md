     Bangerter Environmental Study Presentation - Brian Allen, UDOT Project Manager

Brian Allen, UDOT Project Manager, presented information pertaining to the project at 13400

However, the City does need to commit to which project they prefer and costs above $99 million
would be split half and half.

         EntryPoint Networks Discussion – Bruce Patterson

Mayor Staggs explained that EntryPoint Networks had been awarded the RFP to install the
backbone fiber network in the City and introduced Bruce Patterson who presented on behalf of
EntryPoint Networks.

Bruce Patterson said the initial contract was divided into phases. Phase One revolved around
design and costs to enable City Council to move forward and make decisions. He explained
that they had designed the path, contacted UDOT about sharing assets and met with staff. As
part of the design process, he said they created a city wide design of what it would look like to
provide fiber to every address.

Mr. Patterson explained that the market is very volatile for supplies and labor. Mayor Staggs
commented that the first quote for fiber cable and ducts was $0.40 per foot and is now at $0.75
to $0.80 per foot. Mr. Patterson said that supplies are getting very scarce and it is hard to get
labor lined up.

Mr. Patterson presented a google earth map showing the backbone fiber network plan. The two
slides he presented are included in the packet. The backbone fiber network would go down
12600 South and make use of existing UDOT conduit which is a great savings to the City, $20
per foot. The backbone fiber network ties all of City assets together. There would be a savings
between the sports complex and the Public Works building because the City installed an empty
conduit when they built the road.

Mr. Patterson explained that 90% of the costs are for fiber duct and cable. He explained each
of the line items in the project summary page. He said this a private network and the speeds
should be exponentially better than what the City currently has.

Councilmember Stewart said that one of the goals for this project was to have a connection in
every district and his district is completely cutoff. Mr. Patterson explained that this is a

Public Hearing

         Ordinance No. 21-32 – Amendment to the “Standards and Specifications Plan
         Manual” amending and adopting a standards for Narrow Trench Excavation –
         Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that the City maintains a Manual of
Standards and Specifications which has details, installation instructions and designs for public
infrastructure improvements. The subdivision and site plans that the Commission reviews are
required to meet these Standards. When utility work is done in City streets, the work must be
permitted and inspected by the City according to the Standards. The standards are adopted by
City Council and are part of City Ordinance.

Mr. Lethbridge said that this proposed change to the standards formalizes City practices for
digging and repairing narrow trenches, these narrow trenches are used by utility providers and
are often used in city streets or in the parkstrips.

Mayor Staggs opened the Public Hearing and called for public comments; there being none, he
closed the Public Hearing.

Councilmember Stewart MOVED that the Riverton City Council adopt Ordinance No. 21-32
– Amendment to the “Standards and Specifications Plan Manual” amending and
adopting a standards for Narrow Trench Excavation. Councilmember Wells SECONDED
the motion. Mayor Staggs called for discussion on the motion. There being none, he called for
a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows: McCay-yes, Stewart-yes, McDougal-yes, and Wells-
yes. The motion passed unanimously.
