e Voter registration database;

Fiscal Year 2022-2023 Budget Amendment — Merci Boxcar

Comptroller Stout explained the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2022-2023 budget amendment totals $175,000; it includes
recognition of a donation from the Union Station Foundation ($75,000) and a transfer from the General Fund ($100,000) to the
Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) Project RM0024-Merci Car Restoration. The transfer from the General Fund consists of General
Fund Expense Contingency ($82,000) and Union Station Expense Contingency ($18,000). She then read the following statement
from the CIP project brief: “The Museum at Union Station owns a historic wooden 40 foot by 8-foot boxcar, which was Utah’s car
in the historic 1949 Merci Train. The car honors Utah’s contributions to France following World War II. The car came to Ogden
in 2002, where it was given a volunteer-led restoration. Since this time, the boxcar has experienced the effects of weathering and
sun damage. The Museums are committed to the long-term strategic preservation of this boxcar following a two-prong approach
outlined by the State Historic Preservation Office. First, we plan to hire a railcar historic preservation company to do a
professional restoration of the boxcar, including the replacement of rotted wood, paint restoration and repair, and restoration of the
original roof. Second, we plan to build a structural cover for the car to protect it from future damage from water and weather. This
strategic approach will stabilize and preserve the car for decades.”

Council member White asked if the boxcar will be moved to a different location. Arts, Culture and Events Division
Manager McBride stated the boxcar will be moved to the south side of the Union Station, where there is a circular path that
spectators can use to view the boxcar. Council member White asked if it will be open and accessible to the public. Ms. McBride
stated it is part of the museum collection; people will need to buy a ticket to be granted entry to the boxcar. Discussion then
centered on the process of selecting a restoration company to work on the boxcar.

Proposed Zone Text Amendment — R-2S Two-family and Small Lot
Residential Zone
Planning Manager Brierley explained that, as a result of an Ogden City Council initiative, the Planning Commission was :
asked to make arecommendation on the property at 3841 Jackson Avenue, and whether it should be rezoned from R-3 to R-2; the
property is the former Grandview Elementary School property, which was declared as surplus by the Ogden School District Board
on September 15, 2022. The City received a great deal of public input on the Grandview property. Nearly all sentiment was to
keep the property as open space, although a potential development on the property was subject to the R-3 zoning standards.
Council members expressed concern with the R-3 zoning and what would be allowed to be developed under those regulations,
with a lack of clear height limits being a specific area of concern. In response, Administration directed Planning staffto prepare a
recommendation to the Planning Commission and City Council to amend the zoning standards for the R-3 zone to provide a
reasonable height limit, which ultimately was approved by the Council on April 4, 2023. Despite the amendments to the R-3
zoning standards regarding height limits, Council member Hyer has indicated he would still like to pursue a rezone of the
Grandview property. In evaluating the housing needs of the community, the Commission considered that, if the incomes of future
) households match current levels, about two thirds of those future residents will be able to afford to live in midrise or high-rise
apartments. Additionally, about 15 to 20 percent would be able to afford to live in “missing middle” housing types such as
townhomes, duplexes, multiplexes, and small cottages, while less than a quarter of future residents would be able to live ina
detached single-family home. Because most of Ogden is built out, much of the vacant single-family land is in sensitive, protected
or hard-to-develop lands, and there are very little options for expansion. Arguably, it will be very difficult for Ogden to
accommodate even half of the need for single-family and ownership housing. The surrounding area is mixed development with
offices, multi-plexes, a park, a church, and single-family developments. The Commission also considered the site’s proximity to
Weber State University, McKay-Dee hospital, and the OGX line, and found this location to be suitable for housing more dense
than typical single-family development. Consequently, the Commission is recommending a new zone, R-2S, which would allow
townhomes (platted so units can be individually owned), duplexes, and small-lot single-family homes. The zone would not allow :
mid-rise apartment buildings. This proposal is only for the creation of a new zone, the potential zone change of the Jackson
Avenue property would be considered at a later date.
Council member Hyer asked how the Planning Commission was convinced to recommend a zone change to a zone that is
not currently included in the City’s land use code. Mr. Brierley stated the Planning Commission is charged with considering the
future needs of the community and providing recommendations based upon those needs. He noted the Planning Commission
recommended creation of the zone and then a subsequent action to change the zoning of the Grandview property. Council Deputy
Director Symes stated that both actions can be taken in the same meeting, but they should be noticed individually on a meeting
agenda. Council member Hyer stated he would like to reach out to the Ogden School District Board to determine if they are
comfortable with the recommended zoning of the Grandview property before taking action on the matter. Mr. Symes stated he will
communicate with the Board/District staff regarding the matter.
There was then discussion of the demand for the housing types that would be allowed in the R-2S zone and the amount of
land still available in the City for this type of zone and housing product.

‘ non-owner-occupied residential vacation rental. The current owners (Snell & Wilmer) want to sell the units and advertise them as
_ potential vacation rentals. The text would be amended to allow a historic building with eight or fewer units to be considered as
non-owner-occupied residential vacation rentals for the purpose of determining compliance with the block spacing requirements,
if it meets the following criteria:
bp
e The building is registered on the Ogden City Register of Historic Resources.
e The residential units were condominiumized prior to January 1, 2023. ro]
e The building is located along the Ogden Express (OGX) Bus-Rapid Transit route.

The City Council reviewed the proposal during the May 2, 2023 work session. Council members expressed a desire to
assist the petitioner in their goals and requested further information on the item in order to potentially find a beneficial
compromise. Mr. Brierley noted the Planning Commission reviewed the proposal and unanimously recommended denial to City
Council. The recommendation was based largely on the zoning ordinance amendment not being consistent with the General Plan
policies. The East Central Community Plan states that transit-oriented development includes higher density residential
development, commercial and business services within walking distance of the stops. According to the Commission, there is a
need for housing along the BRT route, and amending the ordinance would remove housing options in the area. He added that the
proposed ordinance amendment is provided by the Petitioner; Planning Staff did not draftnor include an administrative ordinance
proposal due to the Planning Commission issuing a recommendation of denial. At a future City Council Meeting, with no
ordinance to adopt/not adopt, Council’s action would be to either uphold the Planning Commission’s recommendation of denial,
or direct that an ordinance be drafted for future consideration.
