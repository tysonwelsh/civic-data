Proposed Ordinance 2025-6 rezoning property located at 150 32nd Street
from C-2 (Community Commercial) to R-4/CO (Multiple-family Residential
with Conditional Overlay)
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed rezone of 150 32nd Street
came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated David Anderson has submitted a petition to rezone one acre of
property from C-2 (Community Commercial) to R-4 (Multiple-family Residential). The petitioner requests to establish zoning on
the property to allow development of single-family rowhouses. On November 6, 2024, the Planning Commission considered the
proposal and recommended approval of the rezone to R4, with a Conditional Overlay (CO) and development agreement to require
the residential units to be platted to allow individual ownership. On January 21, 2025, the City Council reviewed the proposal
during a work session.
          Planning Director Brierley summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
implications of the proposed zone change. The subject property is vacant and is on the same commercial block as Big O Tires,
Dunk Lee Auto Sales, Penske Truck Rental, a small warehouse/storage building and a Rocky Mountain Power utility substation.
The property abuts the R-1-5 zone to the south, where there are several single-family residential developments with homes built
primarily in the early 1900s. The C-2 zone is a community commercial zone typically established in areas having access to major
streets and a low likelihood of conflict with other uses and should be an edge to residential development. The commercial zoning
in this area (C-2 and C-3) has been established primarily along Wall Avenue from 28 th to 36th Streets. There is also a stretch of C-
3 zoning along 31st Street, connecting the commercial corridor along Wall Avenue to the commercial corridor along Washington
Boulevard. In November of 2024 the Council approved a text amendment that prohibited new residential dwelling units in the C-
2/CP-2 and C-3/CP-3 zones. When the council was considering this text amendment there were several reasons brought up that
supported the change. One reason was the limited number of vacant commercial properties in the City, and that allowing
residential within the city’s main commercial zones could drive out needed commercial space. Another reason was because these
commercial zones are vehicle oriented and are not conducive to creating walkable communities. The R-4 zone is a multiple-family
residential zone that allows for single-family, two-family, and multiple-family dwellings. The R-4 zoning designation would
potentially allow for 25 units on the subject property. The applicant has proposed single-family rowhouses for the development
type. The subject property is part of the Jefferson Community plan. The land-use map from the Jefferson Community plan
indicates that the M-1 (Limited Manufacturing) and C-2 zoning are desirable zoning designations for the subject property. The
staff report to the Planning Commission explains that although not supported by the community plan’s land-use map, rezoning the
property to R-4/CO is consistent with certain vision strategies of the community plan including the desire for more
homeownership opportunities. The Planning Division has explained that approving the rezone with a conditional overlay will
make the property more likely for development, and needed public improvements like sidewalks would be installed sooner. The
Planning Division has also explained that the R-4 zoning can help to provide an appropriate transition and buffer between the
existing R-1-5 zoning and the commercial zones in this neighborhood. On November 6, 2024, the Planning Commission
considered the request and recommended that the Council approve the proposal to rezone to R-4/CO. The Planning Commissioner
who voted no explained that they felt the project does not meet the General Plan, any future homes developed at the site would not
be buffered from the adjacent commercial developments, the future development would be an isolated neighborhood, the
community amenities of the Jefferson Community are not reachable by foot, and the area is unsafe for the play of children. There
were no public comments about the proposal during the Planning Commission meeting. For a copy of the presentation in its
entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Acting Chair Hyer invited input from the applicant. Mr. Anderson indicated that Mr. Brierley explained his application
well; he added that he purchased the property from his grandfather five years ago after he had owned it for 50 years. He was a
commercial developer in the community, but found the property was not a viable site for commercial use after trying to attract
commercial uses to the site unsuccessfully for years. He feels that his application complies with the Jefferson Community Plan,
which identifies a need for more single-family housing in the Plan area.
          Acting Chair Hyer introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2025-6, entitled:
                    “An ordinance of Ogden City Utah, amending the zoning map of Ogden City, as adopted by Section 15-3-3 of
                    the Ogden Municipal Code, to reclassify as Multiple-Family Residential Zone/Conditional Overlay (R-4/CO)
                    property heretofore classified as Community Commercial Zone (C-2), generally located 150 East 32nd Street
                    and providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final passage.”
          Acting Chair Hyer then called for public input regarding the proposal.
          Travis Pate stated that he is concerned about the dynamic of this use on a ‘main street’. He also desires some connectivity
between the proposed development and adjacent uses and neighborhoods. He also asked the Council to consider a mechanism that
would ensure individual ownership of the units to prevent one buyer from purchasing all units. He feels some of the community
plans in the City have been disregarded by developers from outside of Ogden City and the Council needs to consider ways to
prevent that from happening.
          Linda Sanchez stated about a year ago the community was asking for fewer high-rise housing developments and more
individual, single-family homes. This project is exactly what the City has been asking for and it would be ‘crazy’ for the City
Council to reject it.
          No other persons came forward to be heard.
          Council member Myers asked Mr. Brierley to discuss the City’s ability to mitigate issues that may arise as a result of
commercial and residential uses abutting one another. Mr. Brierley stated there are setback standards and design standards, but no
specific requirement for the developer of the residential project to implement noise mitigation amenities. The Council discussed
whether it is possible to add language to the development agreement or as part of the conditional overlay zoning that would
require some additional buffering; they also discussed options for requiring individual ownership of the units at the site. Mr.
Brierley stated that individual ownership may eventually be difficult to enforce, but language can be included in the development
agreement to encourage individual ownership of the units.
          Council member Myers stated it would be helpful for him to hear from the applicant in response to some of the public
input that was offered. Mr. Anderson stated that he feels his proposed project is a step forward for the property and the
neighborhood; the Council can choose to do nothing, which will result in the site being vacant, but it is his intention to develop a
project that will be most viable as a for-sale product for individual homeowners. However, there is nothing that would technically
prohibit a commercial entity from purchasing all of the units.
          Acting Chair Hyer asked Mr. Anderson to address his ideas for mitigating noise pollution from adjacent uses as that may
be a concern of future residents of the project. Mr. Anderson stated that he had not thought of that issue before tonight but
understands it may be an issue. Residents will be responsible for deciding if they are comfortable living in units that are near
commercial uses. Council member Blair agreed and stated he feels this scenario is different than what is most commonly seen in
the City in that the commercial uses are already established, and residential uses are coming in now; anyone looking to live in one
of the units on this property will have a clear understanding of the surrounding uses. Acting Chair Hyer agreed, but indicated that
will not ensure that future residents may complain about noise generated by adjacent businesses. Mr. Anderson stated he does not
see any issue with planting trees or building a wall, but he would need to evaluate the market viability of those types of
improvements.
          Brief continued discussion among the Council, staff, and Mr. Anderson centered on options such as creating a
homeowner’s association (HOA) that could enforce rules and regulations that would require at least a certain percentage of home
ownership rather than rental units. Mr. Anderson stated he understands the ideas behind those types of tools and will explore them.
          ON A MOTION BY COUNCIL MEMBER RICHEY AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER MYERS,
ORDINANCE 2025-6 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – COUNCIL
MEMBERS BLAIR, CHOBERKA, MYERS, RICHEY, AND VICE CHAIR HYER. VOTING NO – COUNCIL
MEMBER GRAF. Chair White was not present when this vote was taken.
          Acting Chair Hyer stated his first reaction to this application was that it is contrary to the ordinance passed by the
Council recently that prohibits multi-family housing projects on commercially zoned parcels; however, he understands that this is
a generational property for Mr. Anderson’s family and he believes strongly in personal property rights and that property owners
should be allowed to do what they desire with their property. His opinion was changed by the input provided by the applicant.
          Council member Graf stated he is a champion of homeownership and housing in Ogden; the reason he voted in
opposition to the application is that the subject property will be the only residential parcel and will be surrounded entirely by
commercial uses. He is concerned that the livability of the residential development may be difficult in that environment.
