Point of the Mountain Transit Study Update and Local Preferred Alternative Approval – Patti Garver, UTA Patti Garver, UTA gave an overview of the Point of the Mountain (POM) Transit Study. The purpose of the project is to study high-capacity transit connection between southern Salt Lake County and northern Utah County with connections to existing and emerging development areas. The process to update agencies and begin environmental analysis began January 2021 and will continue until Fall 2022, or longer. Benefits and similarities of Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) and Light Rail Transit (LRT) were discussed. The Locally Preferred Alternative (LPA) recommended by the Steering Committee is a common ground segment from Draper FrontRunner to Lehi (Traverse Mountain) that will cover 8 miles, with up to 7 station. Two of the stations are scheduled to be located at the POM with an option to extend to include two additional stations and transit way. To implement the common ground segment, an LPA adoption is needed by project stakeholders. Next steps are to present the LPA to City Council meetings, adoption by the MPO and UTA Board of Trustees, begin the environmental study, and secure additional funds for the study with TTIF, UTA funds, STBG funds, and legislative appropriation. Mayor Mann moved that the Point of the Mountain Transit Study Locally Preferred Alternative be approved with UTA and the Consultant Team considering the southern terminus at 2100 North area to capture the Lehi TOD planned at 2100 North. Mayor Johnson seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. TIP Amendment: FrontRunner Commuter Rail Strategic Enhancements - Bob Allen, MAG As a result of the Utah 2021 Legislative session, $100 M of one-time funding will come through the TTIF program and $200M of transportation bond funding was approved for strategic enhancements of the UTA FrontRunner Commuter Rail. MPO Regional Planning Committee April 1, 2021 Page 3 As part of the total request to improve the Commuter Rail reliability , enhance speed, frequency, and capacity, UTA has identified three necessary components: ● Double-tracking in strategic sections ● Station Improvements ● Additional Vehicles By double -tracking at strategic locations of the Commuter Rail along with station improvements and the purchase of additional vehicles, FrontRunner will increase peak hour frequency to 15 minutes at key stations between Ogden and Provo, reduce travel time on express trains, and increase seating capacity on peak hour trains from 8 cars to 14. The total estimated project cost is $350M. This project is new to the UTA Capital Plan and requires early action to be amended into the TIP to enable potential federal funding for the remaining funds to make these FrontRunner Commuter Rail Strategic Enhancements. MAG Staff and TAC recommend the TIP be modified to add Frontrunner Commuter Rail Strategic Enhancements project. Mayor Stout moved that the Frontrunner Commuter Rail Strategic Enhancements project be added to the TIP as presented. Mayor Mann seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. UVX Free Fare Continuation via 4th Quarter Cent Sales Tax - Andrew Jackson, MAG Andrew Jackson gave an overview of the adopted 2021Utah County Budget, explaining how the 4th Quarter Cent Sales Tax (4th Quarter) will be allocated and the process of how the estimated $11M will be spent by UTA. The CMAQ annual subsidy of $600k expires August 2021. If a replacement funding source is not found, UTA will need to charge fare to ride UVX. Andrew Jackson stated that by keeping UVX fast, free, and frequent, ridership will remain high and it will help keep congestion levels down. Andrew Jackson is proposing to use the existing revenue source from the 4 th Quarter to continue keeping UVX a no fare service. If the 4th Quarter is enacted, UTA will receive approximately $11M per year. Utah County and UTA have an agreement on how the funding will be spent. Trustee Acerson reported that UTA is going through the process to determine the actual operating and maintenance costs for UVX. He recommended that Mayors become familiar with tonight’s discussion and weigh into how it pertains to their city and potential congestion as these discussions will be helpful for MAG and UTA in the process.
