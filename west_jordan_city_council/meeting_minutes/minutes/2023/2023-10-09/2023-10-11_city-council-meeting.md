 3. SPECIAL RECOGNITION
       a. Special recognition of Tangee Sloan as 2023 Recorder of the Year for the Utah
          Municipal Clerk Association
           Mayor Dirk Burton recognized Tangee Sloan for being named 2023 Recorder of the Year
           by the Utah Municipal Clerk Association, and for her years of service with West Jordan.
           Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck recognized Ms. Steck for her exemplary
           positive attitude, mentorship of the customer service team, process improvement, and
           passport services. Ms. Sloan thanked her family, City Administration, the City Council, and
           her staff for their support. City Council Members expressed appreciation to Ms. Sloan and
           took the opportunity to shake hands and take photographs.

 4. PUBLIC COMMENT
 Chair McConnehey opened the public comment period at 6:13 pm
 Citizen Comments:
 Brett Seegmiller, West Jordan resident, spoke against the 9000 South expansion. He said the City’s
 Master Plan had been changed several times and asked the Council to hold off and come up with
 a different solution.
 Ty Henderson, West Jordan resident, said he was representing 187 voices regarding the 9000

 South expansion project. He believed alternatives were available, and said he created a petition to
 put a hold on development and hear resident concerns.
 Nicki George, West Jordan resident, spoke against the 9000 South expansion as planned. She
 hoped as the Council considered how to create needed access; they would take precautions to
 make it safe. Ms. George suggested pedestrian controlled light signals were needed.
 Laurie Sheen said she appreciated the responses to her texts and phone calls from the Council. She
 said she understood construction on 9000 South going west was scheduled to begin soon and
 asked the Council to make finding new solutions a priority.
 Jackie Gale was sharing comments of neighbors unable to attend. She said the only reason given
 that she could find that New Bingham Highway could not be the main traffic corridor was that it
 was “in the plans” for 9000 South. She said it was difficult to find answers to questions, and wanted
 to know: the timeline for realignment, the impact on school safe walking routes, what the
 requirements were for getting a light at New Bingham Highway and 6400 West, what roads were
 West Jordan City and UDOT responsible for, how could residents work with consultants through
 the environmental needs process, and could a crosswalk be installed at 6250 West 9000 South for
 pedestrian traffic.
 Logan Gregory, West Jordan resident, believed there was a problem with communication when
 addressing the Council during the meeting; there was actually no “communication”. He asked if
 points of concern were being recorded. Chair McConnehey explained that every meeting was
 recorded, and minutes were prepared. Mr. Gregory felt that streetlights and traffic congestion
 were concerning and suggested there needed to be a lot of dialogue with residents regarding 6400
 West and U111. He expressed the opinion that decisions should not be taken lightly.
 Jake McNinch, West Jordan resident, said he was representing 342 signatures on a petition raising
 concerns with a Peterson Homes development south of 8200 South. He said studies had shown
 high density development could decrease property value, increase crime rates, put a strain on
 local resources, overburden schools, produce less revenue for local government, and create
 parking problems. Mr. McNinch believed it was crucial to maintain quality of life in the community
 and urged the Council to reconsider the proposal with all the points he listed and add the issue to
 a future agenda.
 Ryan Wilkinson, West Jordan resident, expressed the opinion that the City Council did not care
 about residents’ concerns. He noted that he had been complaining about noise from the airport
 for over a year, and the Council had done nothing to stop the noise. Mr. Wilkinson requested
 responses to his emails.
 Laura Shin, West Jordan resident, questioned whether the Council had seen her roadway between
 9000 South and New Bingham, and asked Council Members to have meaningful one-on-one
 dialogues with residents.
 Chair McConnehey said a Council discussion of the Transportation Master Plan was tentatively on
 the agenda for October 18, 2023.
 Chair McConnehey closed public comments at 6:37 pm

 6. PUBLIC HEARINGS
       a. Resolution No. 23-059 creating the Wood Ranch Public Infrastructure District (PID)
          and authorizing the governing document and interlocal agreement
          Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck recognized members of Third Cadence,
          Randy Larsen with GilmoreBell, and Brennen Brown with D.A. Davidson attending in
          support of the Wood Ranch Public Infrastructure District (PID); they were available to
          answer questions. Ms. Steck confirmed administrative compliance for the creation of an
          infrastructure district and described evaluation criteria. She expressed the opinion that
          creating the PID would be a good thing for the City.

           approximately 50 similar PIDs approved across the state. Chair McConnehey commented

       b. Ordinance No. 23-24 amending City Code Section 13-2-3 (Zoning Regulations), 14-2-
          2 adding a definition of “Material Change”, and clarifying Section 14-2-3 (Subdivision
          Regulations) that stormwater impact fees are due with subdivision approval
          City Planner/Zoning Administrator Larry Gardner oriented the Council with Senate Bill
          174, signed into law in 2022. He proposed an amendment to City Code adding a definition
          for “material change” to Sections 13-2-3 and 14-2-2, and recommended clarifying Section
          14-2-3 that stormwater impact fees were due with subdivision approval. With the
          proposed amendment, a material change would be determined by a review team
          consisting of City planners, engineers, real property administrators, fire officials, and
          other City employees.

           Chair McConnehey opened a public hearing at 7:18 pm
           Citizen Comments:
           None
           Chair McConnehey closed the public hearing at 7:18 pm
