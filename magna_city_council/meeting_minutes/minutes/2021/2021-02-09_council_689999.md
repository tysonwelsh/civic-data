                                             Staff Meeting

Electronic Board Meeting Determination

                 Greg Schulz read the following statement regarding the electronic meeting
determination:

               Consistent with provisions of the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act, specifically
Utah Code Ann. § 52-4-207(4), and acting in my capacity as the Mayor of the Magna Metro
Township (“Magna”), I hereby make the following written determinations in support of my decision
to hold and convene electronic meetings of the Magna Metro Township Council without a physical
anchor location:

1.     Conducting Council Meetings with an anchor location that is physically accessible for
members of the public to attend in person presents a substantial risk to the health and safety of
those who may be present at the anchor location.

2.     This determination is based upon the following:

       a.     Pursuant to the Public Health Orders issued by the State of Utah and Salt Lake
       County, the Magna Metro Township remains under a state of public health emergency
       related to the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, and significant person-to-person
       transmission of the virus continues to occur in Magna; and

       b.        All other facts noted in the August 7, 2020 Letter of Determination are still in effect.

                These written determinations shall be read into the minutes at the beginning of the
Council meeting held in Magna on January 12, 2021, and future Council meetings as appropriate.
Council staff are instructed to include in the public notice of each Council meeting the information
on how a member of the public may view and make comments during the Council’s Electronic
meetings.

              This written determination is effective until midnight on February 28, 2021, and
may be re-issued by future written determinations of the Mayor at that or any other appropriate
time.

Public Hearing

            THIS BEING THE TIME heretofore set for a public hearing to receive public
comment regarding the following requests:

Application #REZ2020-000100 – Ronald Larsen requesting approval of a zone change from R-1-
6 (Single-Family Residential) to R-2-6.5 (Multi-Family Residential) on 0.5 acres of property,
located at 7637 West 3100 South.

              Mr. Jim McNulty, Planning Manager, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District
(GSLMSD), reviewed the application stating adjacent properties in the immediate vicinity are in
the R-1-6 and R-2-6.5 multi-family residential zones. The current general plan shows it as an
area for potential growth and change.         The GSLMSD and the Planning Commission
recommended approval. He believed the applicant wanted to subdivide the property and build
two twin home units.

               Council Member Peel, seconded by Council Member Hull, moved to open the
public hearing for public comment. The motion passed unanimously.

                 Council Member Prokopis asked if the neighboring properties were properly
notified.

               Mr. McNulty stated yes, they were. One adjacent resident spoke in favor of the
rezone at the Planning Commission meeting.

               Council Member Peel, seconded by Council Member Prokopis, moved to close the
public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

               Council Member Peel moved to approve Application #REZ200-000100 with the
zoning condition set by the Planning Commission.

                 Mayor Peay asked what the zoning condition was.

              Paul Ashton stated the condition was that the applicant comply with the ordinance
and other requirements.

              Greg Schulz stated the condition was that the applicant work with the GSLMSD
Planning Division staff and outside review agencies to meet the policies for ordinances they
administer. The motion passed unanimously.

                 Council Member Prokopis asked if these units were going to be owner occupied
or rentals.

                 Mr. McNulty stated the GSLMSD was told they would be owner occupied, but that
can change.

Special Orders/Recognition

            Mayor Peay stated the Magna Metro Township is losing its chief of police. Chief
Jason Ackerman is moving to another job.

               Chief Jason Ackerman stated this change started as a temporary assignment,
but the Merit Commission has voted to convert it from a civilian position to a permanent sworn
position. When the Sheriff posted the opening, he was the only one who put in for it. It is not
                                                  5
DATE           TUESDAY                             FEBRUARY                              9, 2021

official yet; it will be going before the Unified Police Department (UPD) Board next Thursday. He
has enjoyed the opportunity of working in Magna and will miss many people. He also commended
Lt. Zach Van Emmerik, Interim Chief, for picking up the slack here.

              Council Member Peel asked if Chief Ackerman could announce the position yet.

              Chief Ackerman stated it is looking like the position will be the chief over Human
Resources, but decisions are still being made. This position will play into UPD’s reorganization,
due to Taylorsville’s departure from the UPD.

              Council Member Prokopis stated Chief Ackerman is well-qualified for the
position. The UPD will benefit greatly from having him in that position.

             Mayor Peay stated there will be a committee to look for a new chief. If any Council
Members get a call to sit on that committee, and they are interested, he recommended they get
involved.

Reports from Community Stakeholders

Magna in Motion

                Keri Duckworth stated she met Thursday with other members of healthy cities for
an event Salt Lake County organized. At the meeting, members discussed how they could
combine efforts to coordinate events or share activities. Also, Magna in Motion just wrapped up
its first week of its Healthy Heart challenge. There were 28 participants online. The winner of
that first week was announced last night. The second week’s challenge is called a Happy Heart
is a Healthy Heart. She also challenged everyone to give someone a compliment within the next
couple of days.

              Council Member Hull asked how people could join in the Healthy Heart challenge.

               Ms. Duckworth stated the challenge is on Magna in Motion’s Facebook page.
There is also a daily challenge posted on the front page.

Magna Chamber of Commerce

              Sara VanRoosendaal stated the Magna Chamber of Commerce’s website is
currently down, so info@magna chamber.org is not sending or receiving. However, its social
media sites and phone number are still active, and someone can email it at magna chamber of
commerce@gmail.com.

She announced that the Magna Kennecott Senior Center is doing its drive-through food bank this
Thursday from 10:30 a.m. to 11:30 a.m., and the parking lot will be open at 8:30 a.m. Also, the
                                              6

Pirate Swim Club is planning to use the Cypress High School pool again, since the wall has been
fixed and some rental restrictions with Granite School District have been negotiated. The
restrictions include a COVID test before being allowed to play or rent. That means every swimmer
wanting to practice or swim in the facility with the Pirate Swim Club will have to test every two
weeks. Since that COVID test is upwards of $10 per test, it will strain the families of some of the
members, so the Magna Chamber is seeking sponsors for the swimmers or the club. A
sponsorship letter request is going to be sent out in the next day or two from the Magna Chamber’s
Gmail account.

              Greg Schulz stated he would be talking with the County’s Economic Development
Division tomorrow and could ask about getting Coronavirus, Aid, Relief, and Economic Security
(CARES) Act funds for this.

             Ms. VanRoosendaal stated the Pirate Swim Club is a 501(c)7, so has not qualified
for CARES Act funds.

Magna United / Magna Community that Cares

                Kellen Schalter stated the coalition just finished its resource assessment and will
present that to the Council sometime in March. She also updated the Council on the vape
detectors stating that last week, she had a walk-through at the Matheson Junior High School with
a potential contractor. The Granite School District will be getting her a quote on that this week,
and once she gets it, she will compare it to the grant funding the coalition received. It will then
take one to three weeks to get the vape detectors into the school.

Reports from other Municipal Service Providers

Unified Police Department (UPD)

               Lt. Zach Van Emmerik, Interim Chief, delivered a presentation on the Fourth
Quarter Report from October to December 2020 for the Magna Precinct, including staffing, calls
for service, general offenses, calls per officer, response times, and October through December
case maps. He also reviewed the Magna Precinct Detective report regarding detective statistics;
the Violent Crime Unit report, i.e. death investigations, robberies, felony assaults, kidnappings
and attempts; the Special Victim report, i.e. child sex abuse, adult sex abuse, missing persons,
runaway juvenile cases, and ungovernable juveniles; the Special Operations (SWAT) report and
high risk operations; the Special Operations (K9) report regarding K9 deployments; and the
juvenile crime statistics from October to December.
