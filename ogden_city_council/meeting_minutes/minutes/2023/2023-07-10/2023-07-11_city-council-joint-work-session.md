Proposed Amendments Regarding Camping and Related Activities on
Public Property

The Public Services Department has requested an amendment to Section 7-2-15 of the City Code by amending the
definition of camping and prohibiting the storage of personal property for more than 15 minutes on public property.

Parks and Cemetery Manager Stewart explained that as mandated by recent State legislation, in order to receive
Homeless Shelter Cities Mitigation Restricted Account funds (Utah Code 35A-16-403(2)(g)), cities must enforce an ordinance
that prohibits camping. Camping prohibitions are common in cities where unauthorized camping leads to public spaces being
rendered unavailable for their intended use. These affected areas cause unsanitary conditions, health risks to campers and the ,
surrounding community, environmental damage, fire hazards, criminal activity, and conflict with residents and businesses. The
proposed amendments create a more specific definition of camping and will be easier to enforce. Amendments also clarify that the
camping ordinance will not be enforced when the City’s shelters are full. Additionally, storing personal property is a similar
problem that often accompanies camping, and the proposed ordinance gives officers an additional tool to combat the problem.
Storing personal property in public areas obstructs pedestrian and vehicular traffic and negatively affects the aesthetics of public
spaces.

Chair Choberka inquired as to the amount of Homeless Shelter Cities Mitigation Restricted Account funding the City
receives. Management Services Director Brown answered $1.7 million. There was also a discussion about the manner in which the
funding can be used in the City.

Discussion centered upon communication between the City and local homeless shelters regarding the proposed ordinance
amendments and the efforts of the City’s homeless advocates to address the matter of camping on public property.

The Council concluded they would like additional discussion of the proposed ordinance amendment and enforcement
procedures before scheduling action on the matter.

Proposed Amendments to Notification Requirements

Based on recent State law changes, cities are no longer required to provide public notice using the newspaper for land use
applications. Instead, State law generally requires posting notices on the city’s website, on the Utah Public Notice website, posting
in at least three public places, and, if applicable, mailing to affected entities as required by both State and City code. As such, the
proposed amendments remove the newspaper noticing requirements for subdivision code section changes, street and alley
vacations, zoning ordinance changes and rezones, general plan amendments, and designations to the local historic register. The
Council extended consideration of the proposed amendments at the March 21, 2023 meeting. Council members voted to extend
consideration with the direction that information regarding noticing costs and newspaper subscribership and circulation be
presented.

Planning Manager Brierley used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the State law changes; the proposed
ordinance amendment addresses notices for the following types of applications:

If the proposed amendment is approved, the requirement to publish notices in the newspaper will be replaced with a ( .)
requirement to post the notice on the State Public Notice Website. During the March 21, 2023 meeting, the Council asked
questions about newspaper circulation in the area and staff found that subscription to the paper has decreased by over 30,000 in
the last 30 years; current circulation is just 22,000. Since the Council tabled the proposed ordinance on March 21, Planning staff
has conducted their own survey to determine how people who attended Planning Commission meetings had learned of certain
agenda items; they found that just one person out of the 40 polled learned of an application through the newspaper, but they had
read a headline and article about the application, not the actual legal notice. The Council also asked about the cost associated with
newspaper noticing; each notice has a cost of approximately $200, for an annual average cost of $2,400. Mr. Brierley concluded
the Planning Commission has recommended that the Council adopt the proposed ordinance amendments to replace the
requirement for posting notices in the newspaper with a requirement to post it on the State Public Notice Website.

The Council thanked Mr. Brierley for the research he conducted; they also discussed the way to replicate the historic
record of notices that is found when notices are posted in the newspaper. They discussed opportunities for establishing noticing
procedures in the City that would help residents to know how often or which days of the week certain notices are posted or
communicating with residents about the ability to subscribe to certain public bodies on the State Public Notice Website.
Proposed Amendment to Mayor's Compensation Beginning January 1, 2024

Council Executive Director Eller-Smith used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss proposed amendments to the
ordinance regulating the Mayor’s compensation. At the direction of the Council, the Council Office contracted with Evergreen
Solutions to perform a benchmarking study for Ogden City’s elected officials and the Chief Administrative Officer position.
Beginning in January 2022, Evergreen surveyed peer cities similar in size and form of government. Evergreen used this data to
develop recommended wages and stipends for Council members, the Mayor, and the Chief Administrative Officer. The results of
the Evergreen study were presented to the Council; Evergreen recommended the Mayor’s annual salary be $136,440, based upon
the results of the survey regarding Mayor compensation. The peer average compensation was $124,673.96 per year. The Bureau
of Labor Statistics calculates that inflation between July 1, 2021 (the year the peer cities established their respective mayoral
salaries) and May 31, 2023 (the most recent number available as of July 5, 2023), the inflation rate was 10.29 percent. Applying
that inflation rate to the benchmark is an option for determining the 2024 Mayoral salary. This would put the salary at
$139,000.00 (rounded). Council staff has prepared a draft ordinance for consideration by the Council, but ifno changes are made
to the Mayor’s salary by January, the compensation will default to the current compensation rate. (
