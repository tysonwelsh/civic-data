5.     Presentation on Active Transportation Plan Update.               Jeremy Searle (WCG),
       Presenter.


Tim Watkins from the Wasatch Front Regional Council ("WFRC") stated that the WFRC is
pleased to sponsor the project through its Transportation Land Use Connection program. The
WFRC's role is to announce the program, invite applications, and facilitate the procurement
selection process. He believes the project is moving at a good pace and in a thoughtful manner.

Jeremy Searle from Wall Consultant Group("WCG") presented the Active Transportation Plan
Update. He reported that WCG:


       Reviewed previous studies;
       Identified key destinations in the existing Active Transportation Network;
       Conducted a safety analysis;
       Performed public outreach;
       Collected data; and

       Studied future networking projects and potential funding sources.

Previous studies include the 2020 Bluffdale City Transportation Master Plan; the ongoing update
to the Parks, Trails, Recreation, and Open Space Plan; WFRC's 2023 Regional Transportation
Plan and 2024 Comprehensive Safety Action Plan; the ongoing Salt Lake County Active
Transportation Implementation Plan; and the Utah Department of Transportation's Utah Trail
Network. Key destinations include the Jordan River Trail, schools, commercial areas, and parks.
The existing Active Transportation Network of bicycle lanes and trails was identified, as well as
current gaps in the network.


Mr. Searle reported that safety analysis is an important part ofactive transportation because people
need to feel safe and comfortable when walking or cycling. WCG researched accident and incident
statistics to determine trends and make mitigation recommendations such as grade-separated
crossings, additional sidewalks, High-Intensity Activated crosswalK("HAWK")signals, etc.

Public outreach efforts included a public survey map,Healthy Bluffdale Coalition walking audits,
a stakeholder meeting,and social media outreach. Mr.Searle noted that they also obtained detailed
information from the public engagement survey that was conducted for the Parks, Trails,
Recreation, and Open Space Plan update. WCG staff visited trails to monitor their condition and
provide suggestions for improvement. A stakeholder meeting was held approximately one month
previously to gather ideas and identify concerns, and a second meeting would be held to present
the proposed plan. The stakeholder group includes members ofthe Planning Commission and City
Council, Staff, residents, and representatives from UDOT,WFRC,and the Jordan School District.
Social media outreach was also conducted.


Count data for the Jordan River and Mountain View Trails was reviewed. On average, 1,500
people per day use the Jordan River Trail. In response to a question from Commissioner Kraupp,
Mr. Searle reported that boxes on the Jordan River Trail count trail users. The Bluffdale counter
is broken,so City-specific data was not available.

The draft Active Transportation Plan was reviewed next. Future on-street facilities are primarily
standard and buffered bicycle lanes, as well as shoulder bikeways or shared roadways on low-
volume roads. Recommended spot improvements include rectangular rapid-flashing beacons,
HAWK signals,signage, and high-visibility or raised crosswalks. Some grade-separated crossings
are recommended in areas of Redwood Road.


Chair Cragun lives near the trail crossing on 14600 South, which is a high-traffic road. At certain
times of the year, vegetation blocks the signal and drivers cannot see that it is flashing until it is
too late to stop. Mr. Searle commented that it is a challenging crossing that has been identified in
the Plan as a future grade-separated crossing. There are plans to widen 14600 South, and he
believes that will provide the opportunity to create a tunnel for the trail.

In response to a question from Chair Cragun, Assistant City Engineer, Addison Mitton reported
that as Staff notices issues with vegetation, they submit a work order to the Public Works
Department.

       • Amend Assorted Sections of the Bluffdale City Land Use Regulations to Update

Associate City Planner, Ellen Oakman presented the StaffReport and stated that the City-initiated
Text Amendment will create new residential zones and amend lot size standards. The matter was

first discussed in a February 12, 2025, City Council Work Session on the General Plan.
Subsequent discussions took place on February 26 and March 7, 2025. The City Council
concluded that they supported the creation of additional single-family residential zones, the
proposed Text Amendment would support housing plan development efforts, and the community
had an interest in property rights and subdividing.

Key elements of the Text Amendment include:

   • It was not a rezone of any property, but rather the creation oftwo new zones in City Code.
   • It preserves existing animal rights.
   • The proposed amendments provide greater flexibility and property rights for property
       owners who may want to subdivide.
   • Changes to the Land Use Table include updates to indicate that the proposed R2 Zone is
       similar to the current RAl and the new R3 is similar to the current R-1-10 Zone, which
       will be renamed R4. The Land Use Standards and setback requirements were also updated.
   • Surrounding cities have one-half and one-third acre zones.
   •   There will be new zone titles.

   • Single-family residential zone sections will be consolidated into one section of the
       Residential Chapter ofthe City Code.
   • There is a relationship to the General Plan.

The proposed Ordinance would create two new zones, consolidate single-family residential zones,
create new zone lot standards based on feedback from the City Council and community research,
and update other sections of Code to be consistent with the new residential zone titles.

The new zones specified in the Text Amendment are as follows:

R-1-87, with a minimum lot size of87,120 square feet(two acres), will be renamed RA2.
R-1-43, with a minimum lot size of43,560 square feet(one acre), will be renamed RAl.
R2 is a new proposed zone with a minimum lot size of20,000 square feet(one-half acre).
R3 is a new proposed zone with a minimum lot size of 13,000 square feet(one-third acre).
R-1-10, with a minimum lot size of 10,000 square feet(one-quarter acre), will be renamed R4.

Ms. Oakman reported that most surrounding cities have one-halfand one-third acre zones, as well
as overlay zones,to provide density bonuses. She then reviewed the proposed changes as follows:

11.80.010, Smgle-Family Residential Zones includes the new R2 and R3 zones, as well as minor
updates to setbacks in the R4 Zone.

11.160.280, Keeping of Animals preserves animal rights in the RAl and RA2 zones and

research of similar zones in nearby communities aided in determining requirements like setbacks,

Chair Cragun understood Mr. Crowell and stated that a modification to the General Plan was
recommended to potentially define density. In response to a question from Chair Cragun,
Mr. Crowell reported that Low-Density Residential is up to four units per acre. Very Low-Density

Ms. Tubbs presented the Staff Report and stated that the City-initiated Text Amendment was the
result of discussions at a City Council Work Session where staff was provided with direction to
execute minor changes to City Code to accommodate anticipated businesses in the City and
incorporate feedback received from community members. She reviewed the proposed changes as
follows:


11.350.020 Table ofPermitted Conditional and Accessory Uses for Nonresidential Zones only
permitted tattoo establishments in the Heavy Commercial Zone. Changes would allow them in
the General Commercial, Heavy Commercial, Regional Commercial, Light Industrial, and Heavy
Industrial Zones.


In response to a question from Chair Cragun, Ms. Tubbs reported that tattoo establishments were
not prohibited with cause. Rather,no one had applied to open one in other zones. The City Council
indicated that business owners had expressed interest in bringing their business to Bluffdale and
asked for more location options.

Ms. Tubbs reported that an automotive services specialist had expressed interest in moving their
business to Bluffdale. However, the City did not want to encourage all automotive services on
Redwood Road, especially in the Destination Retail Zone, as they want to prohibit overnight
vehicle storage and junkyards. The following Land Use Designation would be added to allow
minor, retail-focused automotive services including tire or brake services, windshield repair or
replacement, etc.


AUTOMOTIVE SERVICE, MINOR: A primarily sales-oriented establishment with a
showroom and associated automotive maintenance services. Use includes tire sales and service,
brake service, battery service, and windshield tinting, repair, and replacement. Use does not
include body repair services, paint services, or automotive sales. No outside storage or overnight
storage of vehicles is permitted.

Mr. Crowell reported that the City has had an offer from a nationally-owned tire service center for
a portion of its property in the Destination Retail Zone, and the text change would be required to
allow that business to operate. Ms. Tubbs indicated that the zone is located at the intersection of
Bangerter Highway and Redwood Road, and minor automotive services would only be allowed in
that specific area.

Ms. Tubbs reported that the original Bringhurst Station Development Agreement designated two
commercial areas. Area A was designated for retail-focused businesses, but the City Council
directed Staffto designate a portion ofit as Area B to provide flexibility in the remaining frontage
along Redwood Road for potential non-retail businesses. The applicant indicated that they are
having difficulty filling the spaces as this location does not have a traffic light is inadequate
visibility.


Chair Cragun opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public hearing
was closed.


Commissioner Flynn expressed approval for the changes and noted that retail spaces are currently
struggling to find tenants.
