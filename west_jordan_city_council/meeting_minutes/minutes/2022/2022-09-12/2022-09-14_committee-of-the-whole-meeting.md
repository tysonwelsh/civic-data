 2. DISCUSSION TOPICS
         a. Water and sewer rate study presentation
            Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck presented an updated Water and Sewer
            Rate Study. The Council had asked Ms. Steck if it would be feasible for the City to
            implement a flat rate for sewer similar to other agencies, and a flat rate for water that
            would include basic culinary water use and tiered landscape water use.

            Administrative Services Director Steck presented a “basic water” option for residential
            water rates that would include up to 5,000 gallons of water at a monthly rate of $28.50,
            with tiered rates for additional usage. The basic water rate option would result in
            approximately $3.00 savings per month in winter months, and a slight savings in higher-
            use months for the average user. She explained that 64% of water customers would
            benefit from this option. Winter water bills would be consistent and predictable for most
            users, basic “culinary” use would be included in the flat rate, and usage in excess of the
            “culinary” water could be messaged as “landscape” or “extra” water. On the other hand,
            36% of users would not benefit from the proposed change. Customers who used less than
            5,000 gallons per month would be paying for water they did not use, and gallons
            consumed between 5,001 and 7,000 would increase in cost by 58%. She also pointed out
            that rate structures had changed twice in less than five years.

          Vice Chair Green supposed most customers who regularly only used 1,000 – 3,000 gallons
          of water per month were elderly, fixed-income customers. He asked if there was a way to
          fix the rate so that low-end water users had a stable, fixed monthly rate for the first 2,000
          gallons. Ms. Steck adjusted the numbers in her spreadsheet and showed that a base rate
          of $22.00 per month for up to 2,000 gallons would be revenue neutral for the City.

          Council Member Worthen pointed out that the current rate structure had a base of $20.40
          per month, plus $2.30 per 1,000 gallons used from 0-7,000 gallons. She did not
          understand why the Council was looking at changing the rate structure again considering
          the two recent changes and did not want to make someone pay for something they did
          not use.

          Responding to a request from Vice Chair Green, Ms. Steck calculated that a base rate of
          $20.00 instead of $20.40, with the $0.40 shifted to tiers 2 and 3, would remain revenue
          neutral for the City.

          Council Member McConnehey was in favor of leaving the rate structure as it was, with
          customers paying for what they used on top of the base rate. Council Member Worthen
          agreed. Council Member Jacob felt the current structure provided a good balance.

          Vice Chair Green was fine with the rate structure but preferred to leave the base rate and
          tier one rate fairly low for the customers using the least amount. Council Member Pack
          was in favor of making the base rate and tier one rate as low as possible, particularly for
          those on fixed incomes. He wondered why the rate was being addressed again when it
          had already been adjusted twice in under five years.

          Council Member McConnehey agreed with Vice Chair Green that it was beneficial to have
          the conversation. Ms. Steck shared background of the current water rate structure noting
          that the current water rates were the same as those adopted by the Council in 2020, with
          a 2% inflation adjustment.

          Vice Chair Green was concerned with the $0.40 on the base rate. Council Member Jacob
          did not think $0.40 made a big difference, even for someone on a fixed income. Vice Chair
          Green suggested that with three Council Members in favor of leaving the rate as it was,
          the Council move on to the discussion of sewer rate options.

          Ms. Steck explained how sewer volume was calculated and stated the average monthly
          sewer bill was $33.57 ($22.44 base rate plus $2.09 per 1,000 gallons). She presented a
          “basic sewer” rate option of $33.57 per customer per month. Changing to the basic sewer
          rate option would benefit 43% of customers. Fifty-seven percent of customers would not
          benefit from the change. Vice Chair Green expressed the opinion that staff could be doing
          something far more important than recalculating sewer bills for 23,000 customers each
          year. Ms. Steck agreed that auditing the calculations did take time every year; however,
          she was not happy with how high the basic sewer rate would need to be and suggested

       b. Jordan Valley Water Conservation District Board Member Discussion
          Council Office Director Alan R. Anderson informed the Council of a letter from Lyle
          Summers indicating his desire to resign from the Jordan Valley Water Conservation
          District (JVWCD) Board. Mr. Summers indicated he could serve until the Council found a
          replacement. The JVWCD Board was a multi-county district, with members nominated by
          municipal legislative bodies and appointed by the Governor by consent of the Utah State
          Senate. West Jordan was to nominate two individuals for consideration by the Governor
          (one appointment).

          Council Member Jacob expressed interest in serving on the JVWCD Board if the Council
          chose to nominate a Council Member. Mr. Anderson explained the JVWCD Board meeting
          schedule. Council Member Worthen was in favor of opening the nomination to citizens,
          staff, and Council Members. Vice Chair Green felt that the JVWCD Board Member
          representing West Jordan needed to be an elected official because they would be
          accountable to constituents. Vice Chair Green expressed interest in serving on the JVWCD
          Board. Council Members Worthen and Jacob expressed interest in nominating Mayor
          Burton. Mayor Burton indicated willingness.

          Council Member McConnehey wanted to make sure West Jordan was represented by the
          right person, whether that person was a Council Member, a member of staff, or a member
          of the community. He said he appreciated what Mr. Summers had done on behalf of West
          Jordan. Council Member Worthen agreed.

          Responding to a question from the Council, Utilities Manager Greg Davenport, who had
          currently been attending JVWCD Board meetings, commented that the Board made policy
          decisions, reviewed the financial situation of the District, and approved contracts on
          behalf of the District. Council Member McConnehey expressed the opinion that there
          needed to be a tight connection between the Council and the City representative on the
          JVWCD Board to avoid policy surprises. Mr. Davenport suggested it would also be helpful
          to know a bit about water. Council Member Jacob responded that information could be
          learned and suggested the City’s representative needed to be someone accountable to the
          citizens.

          Council Member Worthen and Pack suggested reaching out to the community if time
          allowed to see if there was interest to be on the board and Council Member McConnehey
