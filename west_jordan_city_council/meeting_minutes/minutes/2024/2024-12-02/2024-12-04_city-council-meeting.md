 6. PUBLIC HEARINGS
       a. Ordinance No. 24-55 amending the General Plan Future Land Use Map for 6.23 acres
          at 6676 and 6686 S Redwood Road, adding 'Potential Residential Overlay District
          (ROD)' designation
          City Planner/Zoning Administrator Larry Gardner presented proposed amendments to
          the General Plan Future Land Use Map for 6.23 acres at 6676 and 6686 South Redwood
          Road, to add a ‘Potential Residential Overlay District’ designation. Mr. Gardner explained
          the amendment would bring the City into compliance with Strategy F of the Moderate
          Income Housing Report sent to the State.

           Council Member Green suggested the State would be more likely to accept the designation
           without the word ‘potential.’ Mr. Gardner said he agreed with the suggestion, and
           expressed the opinion that ROD was a wonderful tool for the location. Chair Jacob said he
           agreed with the suggestion to remove the word ‘potential.’

           Chair Jacob opened a public hearing at 7:12 pm.

           Comments:
           Chris McConnehey, West Jordan resident, said he believed the area would be a good place
           to try the zone, and expressed hope that a developer would be willing to follow the zone.
           He expressed support.

 7. BUSINESS ITEMS
       a. Ordinance No. 24-47 a Master Development Agreement/Plan for Copper Meadows
          (76.21 acres at 8200 S. Stokesley Dr.) and Ordinance No. 24-48 amending the General
          Plan Land Use Map to Low-Density Residential and Rezone to Planned Community
          Zone (PC)
          Chair Jacob said the two proposed ordinances were reviewed and postponed by the
          Council at the previous Council meeting. Council Member Green said his motion to
          postpone at the last meeting was to clear up a few questions he had. He said his questions
          regarding the detention pond and open space had been answered. He said he had
          wondered if the developer would be amenable to moving some of the buildings around,
          and wanted clarification regarding the gravel pit. Council Member Green said he found a
          discrepancy with some of the acreage while researching the gravel pit, which had since
          been resolved. Council Member Green said he had met with and pushed Peterson
          Development since the last meeting. He showed an image depicting landscaping and
          usability of the detention pond that he proposed to be included in the Copper Meadows
          MDP as Amendment 1.

           He presented the following language as Amendment 2:
                Peterson Development will seed areas designated as natural open space with
                appropriate seeding mixture determined in consultation with the Utah
                Conservation Division or other appropriate state agency.

           Council Member Green proposed an amendment to design standards as Amendment 3:
                As development proceeds, Peterson Development agrees to determine the
                feasibility of changing the design of town homes currently depicted to look
                more like single family homes (estate or mansion homes). Any changes in
                design standards in the current town home configuration to look more like
                single family homes is considered a minor deviation in the MDP.

           Council Member Green said his issues with the project would be resolved with the three
           proposed amendments. Council Member Whitelock said it was important to her to listen
           to residents. She said she would motion to postpone because she did not feel the Council
           had had enough time for open discussion with the residents. Council Member Whitelock
           said she felt the three proposed amendments were great, but she would like to work out
           additional changes with Peterson Development.

           Council Member Whitelock moved to POSTPONE Ordinance No. 24-47 a Master
           Development Agreement/Plan for Copper Meadows (76.21 acres at 8200
           S. Stokesley Dr.) and Ordinance No. 24-48 amending the General Plan Land Use Map
           to Low-Density Residential and Rezone to Planned Community Zone (PC) for
           Copper Meadows (76.21 acres at 8200 S Stokesley Dr).

           The motion failed for lack of a second.

           Council Member Green emphasized that his motion at the previous meeting to postpone
           was made because he had questions, and the questions had been cleared up. He said in


City Council Meeting Minutes            Wednesday, December 04, 2024                          Page 6
           the four public hearings held, he had heard nothing but “no” from residents, with no
           solutions offered. Council Member Green said he had received no response to his offer
           two months ago to meet with residents to discuss concerns. He stated the existing zone
           was no longer used in City Code and needed to change no matter what. He said he had
           heard residents say they wanted R-1-8 on the property, which would allow 380 units in
           the project, and pointed out Peterson Development was proposing 318 units. Council
           Member Green said the land would be developed whether or not the neighboring
           residents wanted it. He said the reduction in density to 4.2 units per acre was a direct
           result of listening to the residents and spoke of the importance of proposing meaningful
           solutions.

           Council Member Green said there was no demonstrable proof that town homes would
           bring down existing property values. He said the annexation issue already cleared the
           Planning Commission stage. He said the rail line mentioned was outside West Jordan City.
           Council Member Green spoke of the difficulty of balancing competing interests. He said
           the Legislature was looking at cities and the way they rezoned and said he did not want
           to paint a target on West Jordan by voting the project down. He suggested the residents
           may like alternative plans less than the proposed plan. He pointed out Jones Ranch to the
           south was authorized for up to 10 units per acre. Council Member Green expressed
           willingness to sponsor an RCA to modify the MDP based on valid input.

           Council Member Shelton said the State Legislature was considering by-right zoning,
           which would take away power from cities to zone property and control what was built.
           He said he was not a fan of the proposed cluster housing, but pointed out the proposed
           density was lower than in most neighborhoods in the city. Council Member Shelton said
           if the Council voted down the proposal, it would give the State Legislature a large arrow
           in their quiver for passing by-right zoning. With by-right zoning, developers would be
           able to develop 7-8 units per acre without permission from the City. Council Member
           Shelton said there were several elements of the proposed development he did not like,
           but he would vote in favor out of a desire to do what was best for the city.

           Vice Chair Lamb said he did not want to postpone a decision again because it had already
           been discussed for over a year. He spoke of what the Legislature could do if the Council
           voted against the proposal. Vice Chair Lamb said the developer planned to put the nicer,
           more expensive units near the Sycamores neighborhood, and spoke of the need for more
           affordable homes in the city. Vice Chair Lamb said he believed the end result could be
           worse if the Council voted against the proposal that evening.

           Council Member Bedore said he believed the neighborhood meeting that had been
           planned for the next evening would have provided false hope. He said he suspected
           Peterson Development would not have attended the neighborhood meeting. Council
           Member Bedore emphasized that residents did not own him just because they voted for
           him or contributed to his campaign. He agreed the Council worked for the residents, and
           said the Council did the work and took the time to research and understand all sides of
           every issue that most residents did not want to do. He said the only things that owned his
           vote were his conscience and the Constitution. Council Member Bedore said he did not
           want a target on the city, and said he was there to do what he believed was best for the

       c. Resolution No. 24-041 amending City Council Policies and Procedures
          Policy Analyst & Public Liaison Warren Hallmark explained proposed changes to
          formatting and language of Council Policies and Procedures.

           Council Member Whitelock said she did not want to remove the Chair’s ability to allow
           five minutes for public comment for those representing a group. Chair Jacob commented
           that occasionally someone would ask for more time and say they were representing a
           group, and then everyone else in the group also took the time to speak. The idea behind
           allowing additional time for a spokesperson was that the spokesperson would speak for
           the whole group. Council Member Green said he would be comfortable with allowing five
           minutes for a spokesperson to speak if those they were representing had previously
           signed something indicating approval for the spokesperson to speak for them.
           Council Member Shelton agreed with Council Member Whitelock. Council Member
           Whitelock said she was not in favor of limiting resident input. She said she agreed that
           sometimes advantage was taken and the allowance was abused, but said she would rather
           give the time than take it away. Council Member Bedore expressed agreement.

           MOTION: Council Member Whitelock moved to APPROVE Resolution No. 24-041
                   amending City Council Policies and Procedures, with the exception of
                   deleting the five minutes for a group, which would remain.
                   Council Member Shelton seconded the motion.
