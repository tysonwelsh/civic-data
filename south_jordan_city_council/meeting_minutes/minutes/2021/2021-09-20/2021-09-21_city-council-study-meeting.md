       A. Welcome, Roll Call and Introduction: By Mayor Dawn R. Ramsey

Mayor Ramsey welcomed everyone present and introduced the meeting, she excused Council
Member Patrick Harris who was absent.

       B. Invocation: By Council Member Tamara Zander

Council Member Tamara Zander offered the invocation.

         C. Mayor and Council Coordination
Council Member Don Shelton said he had a call from a resident, expressing concern about the bike
skills track at the Bingham Creek Park because it is paved; she is concerned that cyclists might not
use it because of the pavement.

Council Member Tamara Zander said that today she, City Engineer Brad Klavano, Deputy City
Engineer Jeremy Nielson, City Attorney Ryan Loose and City Manager Gary Whatcott had a
meeting with some residents who attended the last council meeting to further discuss their concerns
over a traffic light. They have been emailing her for weeks and Mr. Nielson has done a great job
of relaying information to them, however they wanted a personal meeting with Council Member
Zander. She appreciated staff attending the meeting and sharing the details of the situation with
the residents, they are proceeding with the traffic light as planned. Two of the residents that came
weren’t happy, but they were congenial and respectful.

Mr. Whatcott said that Mr. Nielson had a third party look at it using UDOT standards, which are
more restrictive in some areas, and it still met two warrants.

Council Member Zander, in preparation for this meeting with the residents, called the school
district’s safety engineer, the principal at the school, spoke with Mr. Klavano and Mr. Nielson,
and read through the minutes of the last council meeting. She thinks they feel heard, but she feels
that if the city were to not install that traffic light there would be another uprising.

Mayor Ramsey pointed out that we had no residents that live across the street show up at the
meeting to speak against the light or send their complaints beforehand.

Council Member Brad Marlor said they finally have resolution to 2200 West construction. It was
a tough road to fix and there was a lot involved, but it really looks beautiful.

Mr. Whatcott said there is only one more section of 1055 West that doesn’t have curb, gutter and
sidewalk on it; once 1055 West is done, almost everything east of Bangerter will have curb, gutter
and sidewalk.

Council Member Marlor said this project was started back in 2000, where they did every little
piece they could afford, and he is so thankful for the grant to 2200 West as that covered most of
it.

Mr. Whatcott said that work on 2700 West in front of the Wheadon property is scheduled for
probably Spring 2023.

Mr. Klavano said Associate Engineer Deven Serr was really the one responsible for 2200 West.

Mr. Whatcott said compared to Draper and other communities we have worked hard to get all these
streets done.

Mr. Klavano said they are working with contractors to get the streetlights in November and the
rest of the landscaping will be done next spring.

Council Member Zander asked if it was a possibility, after 10600 South is done, to have a ribbon
cutting. Keep it blocked off for a short period and allow residents in the middle of the intersection
for a ceremony.

Mr. Klavano will speak with UDOT about a possible ribbon cutting for the 10600 South opening.

         E. Presentation Item:

                  E.1.       City Pavement Preservation update. (By Associate Engineer, Deven Serr
                             and Associate Director of Public Works, Joey Collins)

Associate Director of Public Works Joey Collins reviewed prepared presentation (Attachment
A).

Council Member Zander asked if other cities do these same types of evaluations.

Mr. Collins said he believes many other cities do it as well.

Associate Engineer Deven Serr continued the review of prepared presentation (Attachment A).

Council Member Shelton asked how long roads typically will last.

Mr. Serr said a neighborhood asphalt road can last up to 50 years, however a main asphalt road
like 10600 South would need asphalt in 20 years or less; it just depends on volume of traffic and
varies with weather and other factors.

Council Member Zander asked what the best weather conditions for the basic repairs are.

Mr. Serr said the hotter the better. Salt Lake City is an example of what happens when the roads
are not well maintained throughout the years.

Council Member Marlor commented on Fiber companies doing installation, where they lay it in
a 2 inch area next to the curb; he asked what issues/problems this could cause in terms of
deterioration.

Mr. Klavano said he’ll be bringing a city standard to the council at the next meeting for repairs.
He met with a lot of other cities, Sandy City developed a new standard that he is going to modify
a little bit and adopt. Any time you cut the road it’s not optimal, but they are trying to do the best
they can to protect the city roads. He said he was just down in San Antonio, TX and they just got
Google Fiber; every morning he’d go out and walk the neighborhood to see what their streets
looked like. His staff has been visiting other cities in the area with Google Fiber to see what was
done right and wrong so we can develop a better standard. There will be a bunch of water line

when they come in and fix the road it’s all done and doesn’t have to be torn up afterwards.

Council Member Zander asked if we have a standard in place, will Google Fiber have to stick to
that standard.

Mr. Klavano said it depends, if it’s too restrictive they won’t do the work. The key is inspections,
he is planning to charge a fee that’s high enough to hire two contract employees to watch them

         F. Discussion Item:

                   F.1.      11400 S. Residential Development, approximately located at 1472 West
                             11400 South. (By applicants, Jake and Amanda Ackerman)

Jake Ackerman reviewed his prepared presentation (Attachment B).

Council Member Marlor asked about garages on the townhomes.

Mr. Ackerman said there are no driveways on the townhomes, but they do have two car garages.

Council Member Zander asked who will manage the park, will it be open to the general public or
gated off as resident only.

Mr. Ackerman said the parks will be gated off, the amenities will be a part of an HOA.

Council Member Zander expressed appreciation that he listened and made serious changes based
on the council’s feedback, she is pleased with what he has brought back to them. She feels that
keeping this as a traffic light is important and wishes they could go back to the Ivory Project and
be more thoughtful. She is okay with the 8.4 density as she can see there is space with the canal
that he is not getting credit for, so the overall feel will be less dense.

Council Member McGuire asked about vinyl fencing marked around canal, if they will be
keeping that with the proposed trail along the canal. He appreciates Mr. Ackerman coming back
with a much better plan based on their suggestions.

Mr. Ackerman said there have been discussions about possibly a split rail fence for security
purposes, on the side that doesn’t have a trail they thought about possibly a split rail with a chain
link fence.

Strategic Services Director Don Tingey suggested he check out Rushton Meadows, they have a
split rail fence with metal behind it to keep kids safe.

Council Member Marlor appreciated their creativity and willingness to listen, also that there are
affordable housing units. He feels strongly that, in the future, they should strongly encourage all
projects to have some affordable housing.

Council Member Shelton likes it and appreciates his responsiveness to the council’s input.

Planning Director Steven Schaefermeyer asked the council how critical the canal piece is to the
overall project, along with what their expectations are.

Mayor Ramsey said they are expecting what has been presented today.

City Manager Whatcott said the canal paths are usually paved at 10 feet wide so city vehicles can
drive on them. If the city is maintaining the canal trail they would need an easement or to have
the trail piece dedicated to the city.

Mayor Ramsey said if there is a problem with the canal company and it looks like it will undo
what has been presented, she and the council would like to know. She is impressed with Mr.
Ackerman’s efforts and the way they have put this together. She feels the council is in favor of
this new plan.

Council Member Zander motioned to adjourn the Study Meeting. Council Member
McGuire seconded the motion, vote was unanimous in favor; Council Member Harris was
absent from the vote.
