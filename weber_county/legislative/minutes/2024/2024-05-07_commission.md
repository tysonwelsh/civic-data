D. THOUGHT OF THE DAY: Commissioner Froerer: As we approach Mother’s Day this Sunday, I want to take this
   opportunity to help everyone understand, as one who has lost their mother, the importance of mothers in our
   society. The driving force for a lot of us has been the inspiration, the guidance, and the education received form
   our mothers. It’s hard to describe the significance of what they contribute to each and every one of our lives.
   There are a couple of quotes that reflect my feelings. The first, “A mother is she who can take the place of all
   others but whose place no one else can take.” The second quote is, “Happiness is seeing your mother smile.” I
   remember times my mom smiled and times she didn’t smile at me that much. But when she did smile at me, it
   was always a great feeling. If she was here today, I would tell her, “I want to wish you a very happy Mother’s
   Day and let you know how much I appreciate all that you do and have done for us. Thank you for being an
   amazing mom. Your love and support have actually been my guiding light through all my struggles. I am and
   will be forever grateful for everything you have done in my life.”

      Commissioner Bolos: I feel the same about my mother and I miss her. But I also have the great honor of being a
      mother and that is one of the greatest blessings I have ever had, to influence children and watch them grow and
      develop into the kind of people you want them to be.
E. PUBLIC COMMENT:
   1. Carolyn Brierley, former Ogden City Special Event Coordinator: I am here today to give background on the
      Ogden Marathon coming up on May 18th. In September of 2001, Mayor Godfrey called me and said he wanted
      to have a marathon. At that time we needed permits from the Forest Service, BLM, Highway Patrol, all the
      departments in Weber County, and of, course, Ogden City. I was not worried because I knew we would never
      get permission to run down Ogden Canyon. The final permit came in the third week of January. I told the
      Mayor all permits are in so let’s plan for next year. He said, “No, I want to do it Cinco de Mayo this year.” I
      said, “Mayor, that’s only four and a half months away.” He said, “I don’t care. Bigger things than this have
      been pulled off in less time. Just do it.” So with a $5k budget, Brian Green and I pulled off a marathon relay
      that first year; each relay team was required to provide one volunteer. There were 620 runners in that first
      marathon. The largest marathon we had was 11k runners, including all races. We have a huge festival on the
      line and a VIP tent called the Lounge on the Line where the very best breakfast is served at 7am and the very
      best lunch is served at 11am. I am here to invite the Commissioners to give a VIP pass to whoever they would
      like to come see the awesome things that take place when the runners cross the finish line. This marathon has
      a huge economic impact to the county and the city. And it is rated as one of the top ten marathons in the nation.
      We have had participants from every state and 10 foreign countries.
            Chair Harvey: All three of us have participated in this event in one year or another. And next year we will
            have all three of us competing as a team.
            Commissioner Bolos: Is that a promise?
            Chair Harvey: It is for me. What about you, Commissioner Froerer?
            Commissioner Froerer: Absolutely!
            Chair Harvey: Commissioner Froerer will be running the first 20 miles, Sharon will take the next few, and I’ll
            have enough wind for the last 100 feet.
