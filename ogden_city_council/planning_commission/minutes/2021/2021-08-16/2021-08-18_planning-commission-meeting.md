1. Amended Conditional Use Permit, to allow parking lot addition at the Lantern House,
   approximately 269 W. 33rd Street. (Attachment 1)

    Mr. Will VanderStappen stated this had been the anticipated location of a donation building
    that has not been constructed, and has become an area for overflow parking. He noted many
    of the homeless people do own cars, and that there are events within the facility which
    attract others. As it has not been improved, they have found it is not legal to be used for
    parking. This request is to construct a 20,000 square foot parking lot, providing 51 parking
    spaces, including lighting, landscaping and storm water drainage.

    Commissioner Safsten asked about how the parking lot can be designed to prevent loitering.
    Mr. VanderStappen responded their proposal is for cobble around much of the landscaping
    as well as large boulders.

    Mr. Montgomery stated while the main use as a shelter had received a conditional use
    permit, this accessory parking lot will amend that certificate by replacing the donation center
    with a surface parking lot, and consideration by the Commission is to determine whether the


                                                     1
   proposed parking lot will create new impacts to the surrounding area. He stated the plan
   shows a unique landscaping design including cobble and groundcover with boulders in the
   parkstrip along Pacific Avenue. He noted the initial landscape plan had included trees on the
   northwest side which looks like they had grown for a time period but are now dead, stating a
   condition of approval should be for these trees to be replaced. He also suggested the
   applicant be cognizant of the type of trees within the parking lot and that a design theme be
   extended around the parking lot area. He felt the proposed parking lot would create less of
   an impact to surrounding uses than the donation center which had previously been proposed
   as the donation center would likely generate more traffic. Other surrounding uses are the
   railyard and the regional detention basin, and the use as a parking lot would not create any
   negative impacts to these uses. Staff recommendation is for approval subject to the dead
   trees along the northwest portion of the property being replaced, boulders being placed along
   Pacific Avenue, trees within the parking lot be a hardy urban tree and all staff and
   departmental requirements be satisfied.

   MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Schade to approve the conditional use
           permit based on the findings the proposed parking lot, with amendments is not
           detrimental to other persons or property, is consistent with the General Plan, is
           compatible in use and design with other surrounding uses and is in compliance
           with all other regulations and requirements. Approval is subject to the following
           conditions:
           1. Provide additional boulders in the front of the parking lot to create a unified
              landscape theme.
           2. Replace dead trees along the northwest portion of the property that have died.
           3. Revise tree type in parking lot to a hardier urban tree.
           4. Satisfy all staff and department requirements.
           Motion was seconded by Commissioner Safsten and passed unanimously with
           Commissioners Safsten, Sandau, Schade, Southwick, Stoker and Graf voting aye.

2. CBD Site Plan Review, for building addition at 2242 Washington Boulevard. (Attachment 2)

  Mr. Dan Schmeling stated this building is currently vacant and the intent is to renovate it into
  retail space on the ground level and two apartments on the upper level including a terrace
  space. A two-car garage in the rear alley also is intended to be replaced as it is dilapidated.
  He stated the new garage will be on the same building footprint, and due to its proximity to the
  property line, the garage door space remains open. The finish material for the garage is to be
  perforated metal with the same material to extend around the second floor terrace on the rear.
  He stated the building had been covered with rock and stucco which has now been removed,
  and due to the condition of the underlying brick the intent is for the brick to be painted a dark
  gray color. He reviewed other details of the building façade, stating the intent is to create a
  balcony and to redesign the entry on the main floor as well as painting the sign panel at the
  top of the building.

  Mr. Thaine Fischer stated as owner of this building as well as the two adjacent buildings to the
  south, he I working with Rocky Power in an effort to obtain utility easements s well as

upgrading and burying existing power lines. A meter box is intended to be installed of the
building which will be acceptable if an easement is able to be created.
Mr. Stratford asked whether encroachment permits will be obtained for the balconies which
extend over the right-of-way. Mr. Fischer indicated he had understood these would be
obtained by UDOT as Washington Boulevard is a state road. Mr. Stratford responded while the
State does own the right-of-way, the sidewalk is the responsibility of Ogden City and an
encroachment permit will be required for balconies, but are typically not required for awnings
which exist on the adjacent building. Mr. Fischer clarified the decks would be a minimum of
12’ above the sidewalk.

Mr. Montgomery stated the Planning Commission is charged to review both the building
material compatibility and architectural lines. He stated perforated metal was a typical
building material on the back of buildings at the time this building was constructed, and also
exists on adjacent buildings. While the storefront design is consistent with other buildings on
Washington Boulevard, the separation between the windows should be more consistent with
the historic appearance of the building, suggesting a thicker divide between windows be
created. He then reviewed the building color proposed, stating while he understands the
reasoning for choosing a dark color to mask the various colors of brick and mortar which had
been damaged by the stucco material, other buildings in the downtown are more earth-toned
and Staff recommends the color be modified to better match the reds and browns and fit with
the downtown, suggesting a taupe color would keep both some gray and brown hues, He
stated the building is on the property line, and the existing garage extends onto property
owned by Rocky Mountain Power. The building is required to be within the property lines
owned by the applicant, and either additional property must be obtained or the size of the
garage reduced. Staff recommendation is for approval subject to the following conditions:
1. A lot line adjustment be approved by Ogden City in order to keep the garage within
   the property lines;
2. The storefront windows be modified to include thick mullions around the transom
   windows;
3. Paint color be changed from light gray to a dark gray, charcoal or dark taupe color
   for the brick and
4. All staff departmental recommendations be satisfied.

Mr. Fischer informed the Commission he will be meeting with Rocky Mountain Power soon in
order to change the property lines as recommended by Staff. He felt the lot adjustment could
be completed within 30 days.

            1. A lot line adjustment be approved by Ogden City so the garage will be
