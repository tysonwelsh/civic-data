     A. CONSIDER A PROPOSED TEXT AMENDMENT RELATED TO THE
                  REGULATION OF INTERNAL ACCESSORY DWELLING UNITS IN
                  CHAPTER 1.01 AND CHAPTER 17 OF THE MIDVALE MUNICIPAL
              CODE
Nate Rockwood said as part of the 2021 legislative session the State approved HB- 82
which requires cities to allow for internal accessory dwelling units (IADU) in areas zoned
as primarily residential. The actions required by the legislation are to take effect on
October 1,2021. Staff has prepared the relevant sections of code to include the uses of
lnternal Accessory Dwelling Units (IADU) and External Accessory Dwelling Units (EADU)
in zones that allow detached single family use.

The new legislation does provide some discretion in implementation and application by
individual jurisdictions. Some key points for discussion are as follows:
Summary Requirements:
.       An internal ADU must exist inside the footprint of the home, be a detached
       dwelling, and be the primary dwelling of the owner of record.
.      An internal ADU must comply with health, building, and fire codes, and have rental
       agreements for at least 30 days.
A City May Not:

                                                     5
Proceedings of City Council Meeting
September 21, 2021

        Except as specified in state code, a city may not regulate the total internal ADU
       size, lot size, or frontage.
A City May:
       A city may require a business license, that the dwelling exterior appearance not be
       altered, and one on-site parking spot.
        A city may prohibit the installation of a separate utility meter for an internal ADU.
        A city may decline an internal ADU in a mobile home, for a dwelling with a failing
       septic tank, or on a lot of 6,000 square feet or less.
       Most cities and towns shall allow internal ADUs in 75% of the area zoned primarily
       residential.
       Cities and towns may use short-term rental websites to ensure that internal ADUs
       are not advertised or rented as vacation rentals.
       Cities and towns may impose a lien against the owner that, after a period to cure
       compliance issues, has a daily fine of up to $1 ,000.

Staff is recommending including the majority of the regulations allowed on IADU including
requiring a business license, including one on-site parking, no IADU on lots 6,000 sq. ft.
or less, no separate meters, or ADU in mobile homes. Staff is not recommending that the
City restrict any zones with the 75% threshold allowed by state code. Staff recommends
that the City reserves that allowance for a future time when a specific zone or area may
experience issues with lADUs.

Staff also recommends that a single detached unit may have both a single IADU and a
EADU if the property can meet all requirements of each unit independently of each other,
including all parking standards.

Pursuant to the text amendment process described in 917-3-1 of the Midvale City
Municipal Code, Stafi presented a draft text amendment to the Planning Commission for
recommendation. Following a public hearing on September 15, 2021, the Planning
Commission recommended the Council adopt the proposed changes.

STAFF RECOMMENDATION:
Staff recommended the City Council hold a public hearing and approve Ordinance No.
2021-0-19, adopting text amendments related to the regulation of lnternal Accessory

Scope of project: To conduct a study that will determine if trails along the two canals in
Midvale are feasible with the current property ownership/use and, if not, what steps the
City should take/consider in the alternative.

Methods of Disbursement: Funds will be deposited in one lump sum payment no later

       A.      DISCUSSION ITEM ON OPIOID LITIGATION SETTLEMENT
Lisa Garner said in July of this year, a nationwide settlement agreement was reached
between several states, including Utah, against the three largest pharmaceutical
distributors-McKesson, Cardinal Health, and AmerisourceBergen-and manufacturer
Janssen Pharmaceuticals, lnc., and its parent company Johnson & Johnson. These
settlements could potentially provide substantial funds to states and subdivisions for
abatement of the opioid epidemic across the country and will impose transformative
changes in the way the defendants conduct their business. Utah has agreed to participate
in these settlements and has also pursued claims in bankruptcy proceedings for Purdue
Pharma and Mallinckrodt. ln the settlement agreements, the State of Utah and the political
subdivisions will all receive more funds if more subdivisions join. lf Utah enters both
settlement agreements with no subdivision participation, it will recover approximately

                                             10
Proceedings of City Council Meeting
September 21, 2021

$151 million dollars over 18 years. lf Utah enters both settlement agreements with full
subdivisron participation, Utah and the political subdivisions will recover approximately
$270 million over 18 years. Utah should also recover about $72 million over 9 years for
the bankruptcy cases of Purdue and Mallinckrodt. Most, if not all, of the money will be
required to be used to address the opioid epidemic.

The purpose of this discussion is to get direction from the Council as to Midvale's
participation in the settlement agreements and distribution of the proceeds. lf Council
would like to pursue joining the State in the settlement agreements, staff will need to
participate in meetings regarding the distribution of settlement proceeds, review allocation
agreements, and identify qualifying expenditures within Midvale City. The allocation
amounts and criteria is not known at this time, but it is anticipated that funds could be
available by the summer of 2022.

The Council agreed to proceed
