ZONE TEXT CHANGE APPLICATIONS
ZT-6-2021  Petition by WEST VALLEY CITY requesting a zone text change to allow inoperable
           vehicle parking for an automobile service use under certain conditions. (Staff – Steve
           Pastorik at 801-963-3545 or steve.pastorik@wvc-ut.gov)

Discussion:    Steve Pastorik explained that currently any outside parking over twenty-four hours is
               considered outside storage, which is not allowed in a commercial zone. He said that there
               have been many requests, mainly from automotive businesses, to have the option of
               leaving cars awaiting parts outside longer than twenty-four hours. Steve stated that this
               proposal would allow cars to be parked outside over twenty-four hours under certain
               conditions that are outlined in the staff report.

               Commissioner Winters stated that the conditions do not state how long they will be
               allowed to keep these cars outside, and he asked Steve if this was intentional. Steve said
               they did leave that out intentionally. He stated staff is not intending to have the cars
               parked indefinitely, and it would be challenging to track the number of days they are
               parked outside. Steve explained that the current ordinance has allowed Code Enforcement
               to look at the businesses that store a large number of cars to see which cars have been
               parked there for quite some time. However, this proposal addresses the companies with
               just a few cars that need to remain outside until the parts arrive.

               Commissioner Woodruff feels this proposal could allow some situations to get out of
               hand. He asked if there is a car parked behind a building on blocks or missing tires, how
               would that be handled? Steve said that within the conditions it is stated that there can be
               no visible damage to the vehicle. So, the parts they are waiting for have to do with
               damage under the hood. He said we could be more specific if the Commission feels it is
               needed. Commissioner Woodruff noted that it is hard to know when to stop adding
               specifications, and maybe we need to see what happens with the proposed conditions
               first.

               Commissioner Lovato feels that the examples Steve presented showed the cars being well
               screened behind the buildings, and his concern is someone parking these cars in a more
               visible location. Jody said this proposal intends to allow a couple of vehicles to be parked
               over 24 hours, not to allow for a storage yard. She explained that these cars can not take
               up any required parking stalls, encouraging quick rotation of inventory.

               Commissioner Winters suggested that it says “adjacent street” in the condition, making
               the street plural encompassing the entire surrounding area.


ZONE CHANGE APPLICATIONS
Z-7-2021  Petition by MANDI MCKAHAN requesting a zone change from C-1 (Neighborhood
          Commercial) to R-1-7 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 7,000 sq. ft.).
          The property is located at 4048 South 4800 West on 0.18 acres. (Staff – Steve Pastorik
          at 801-963-3545 or steve.pastorik@wvc-ut.gov)

Discussion:    Steve Pastorik explained that when this proposal was initially submitted, the applicant
               was showing the subject property measuring 8,000 sq. ft. and was proposing a zone
               change to R-1-8. It was then determined to be shy of 8,000 sq. ft. and the application was
               removed from last week's agenda. He stated that this proposal was re-noticed, requesting
               a zone change to R-1-7 with a minimum lot size of 7,000 sq. ft.

               Steve presented a bit of history regarding the subject location. He explained how the
               subject location, consisting of two parcels and the 0.32-acre parcel to the south, was once
               combined and zoned C-1. Sometime in the '80s, the parcels were separated, retaining the
               C-1 zoning. Steve said the separate lots must not have been created legitimately where
               the minimum lot size for a commercial zone is 20,000 sq. ft. He noted that typically,
               given the parcels were not separated by the City, there would not be an option to rezone
               this parcel and develop the property. These parcels now have different owners, so it
               would be tough to put them back together, leaving them unusable. Steve stated that the
               applicant is requesting the zone change to build a home on the subject property. He said
               that staff feels this is a good option and will fit well the surrounding residential area,
               where the other option would be to look for an exception for a commercial business to
               operate on a 7,000 sq. ft. lot. Steve said that staff is suggesting a development agreement
               that will include granting an exception on the width of the lot, dedicating ten ft. right-of-
               way to the City, and provisions that the home to be built on the property meet the
               previous standards for an older lot.

               Chair Meaders asked if Steve had any neighbors reach out to him with concerns regarding
               this proposal. Steve said there was a gentleman that attended last week's study meeting
               who seemed interested. However, he has not received any calls or emails.

               Commissioner Lovato asked why the two parcels in question had been split, and Steve
               said the .12 acre parcel was separated in the 80's and the .07 acre lot sometime after that.
               He explained that rezoning the two properties together or separately would still leave the
               acreage well under 20,000 sq. ft. The applicant is proposing to rezone both parcels.

               Commissioner Winters asked if the housing standards outlined in his packet are the
               criteria to be added to the development agreement. Steve said that is correct and
               explained that the Utah Legislation passed a law eliminating the housing standards minus
               a few exceptions during the last legislative session. Therefore, they are to be outlined in
               the development agreement.


GENERAL PLAN/ZONE CHANGE APPLICATIONS
GPZ-14-2021 Petition by BRAD REYNOLD CONSTRUCTION requesting a General Plan change
            from General Commercial to Medium Density Residential (7 to 12 units/acre) and a zone
            change from C-2 (General Commercial) to RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling Residential).
            The property is located at 3554 South 3200 West on 3.2 acres. (Staff – Steve Pastorik at
            801-963-3545 or steve.pastorik@wvc-ut.gov)

Discussion:    Steve Pastorik stated that the subject property is eligible to apply for the RM zone
               because it is located within a half-mile of a light rail station. He explained the applicant is
               proposing to build a 37 unit townhome development, and each alley-loaded unit would
               have a patio and a 750 sq. ft. finished basement. Steve pointed out a stub street located in
               the southwest corner of the lot with only a portion exposed. He stated that there had been
               communication with Public Works about a way to connect this stub road.

               Steve explained that the ordinance states that sixty percent of the building can be made of
               fiber cement or stucco. He said the applicant is proposing approximately seventy percent
               fiber cement siding. Steve stated they could use different variations and colors of the
               fiber cement and ask for an exception. As an alternative, and could add stucco or add
               more brick or stone to meet the requirement.

               Steve stated that the applicant proposes 3.1 stalls/unit as their parking ratio, and other
               townhome developments have a higher percentage. He said one staff suggestion is to
               remove one unit to accommodate more parking stalls. Chair Meaders agrees with the
               need for more parking. Commissioner Winters suggested removing some trees to free up
               parking space. Steve said they are meeting the fifty percent landscaping requirement, so
               if they did remove trees, they would need to add landscaping somewhere else on the
               property.

               Commissioner Lovato is concerned about future parking issues if they build a nineteen-
               unit development without driveways. Commissioner McEwen agrees. Commissioner
               Winters asked if there was a parking requirement in the code. Steve said that there is a
               requirement of two parking spaces per unit, and if you look at the townhome
               requirements specifically, a two-car garage is required. Therefore, the applicant is
               meeting the parking requirement. Steve noted that the City has been pushing for more
               parking, and there have been conversations about changing the code.

               Commissioner Lovato said part of the issue could be that the garages are too small and
              asked if there is a requirement for the garage size. Steve said there is a size requirement
              for garages regarding single-family homes but not for a townhome development. Steve
              said that judging from this discussion, the Commission would be interested in looking at
              the parking requirements for townhomes. The Commission said they would.

              Commissioner McEwen asked if this proposal has the same applicant as the proposal for
