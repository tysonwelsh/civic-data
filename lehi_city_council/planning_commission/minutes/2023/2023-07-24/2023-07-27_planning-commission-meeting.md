2.2.) Public hearing and consideration of Anna Black’s request for conditional use approval
of the East Main Street Retail Center Conversion Site Plan with a drive-thru located in an
existing building at 1325 East Main Street, changing the existing auto repair business to a
retail store.
Applicant Anna and Cody Black were present and available for questions. Mr. Black discussed
details about the property, including parking and drive-thru considerations for a new development,
and changes to the building's size and facade.

Chairperson Newall opened the public hearing.
Chairperson Newall closed the public hearing.

The Commissioners discuss concerns about a new business proposing a drive-thru without a
comprehensive traffic study, only a trip generation study. They highlight a history of crashes and
argue for the importance of a full traffic study to assess potential impacts on traffic flow. The
Commissioners expressed the need for more information, site plans, and engineering solutions to
address the potential traffic issues before approving the project. Commissioners did express that
they did think that despite the concerns they do think it’s workable and a joint solution could be
achieved.

Motion: Commissioner Carlson moved to table the application for 30 days to allow applicant time
to provide a comprehensive study and to prepare a site plan that presents a solution to the traffic
issues that have been experienced and incurring for the westerly building. We grant the applicant
to extend to a later day if they so choose to bring a solution for Anna Black’s request for conditional
use approval of the East Main Street Retail Center Conversion Site Plan with a drive-thru located
in an existing building at 1325 East Main Street, changing the existing auto repair business to a
retail store; including the DRC comments; with the findings as listed in the staff report. Also with
the existing findings that for traffic is substantial and has existing issues and that it could be more
problematic adding 38 more cars to the existing conditions is a concern and it’s not possible to
make a decision with the site plan and other items mentioned above and that it justifies the
commission to add these conditions. The motion was seconded by Commissioner Bunker.

Vote: Commissioner Newall, yes; Commissioner Bunker, yes; Commissioner Everett, yes;
Commissioner Carlson, yes; Commissioner Eyre, yes. The motion passed unanimously.

2.4) Public hearing and recommendation of Lehi City’s request for review of an amendment
to the Lehi City Development Code Chapter 4, Amendments, changing when General Plan
amendments are allowed to be processed.

Chairperson Newall opened the public hearing.

Chairperson Newall closed the public hearing.

2.9) Public hearing and recommendation of Lehi City’s request for review of an amendment
to the Lehi City Development Code Chapter 37, Design Standards, adding a height limit of
3 stories in the Historic Downtown Design District.

Staff recommended revisiting Lehi City's development code, Chapter 37, in response to a request.
The focus was on adding stories to historic downtown design districts to maintain a suitable scale.
Proposed changes included non-residential use on the first floor without vehicle parking and
limiting building height to three stories, aligning with the goal of preserving the historic scale. The
past omission of these elements from the code was not intentional, and the proposed restrictions
were consistent with the current landscape in Lehi City's historic district.

Chairperson Newall opened the public hearing.
Chairperson Newall closed the public hearing.

Some commissioners expressed dislike for the attempt to scale historical stories, emphasizing the
human scale. Concerns were raised about limitations based on historical technology, and opposed
restricting the potential for taller, vibrant buildings. The discussion involved the city council's
request and addressed the height issue, expressing the need for architectural considerations rather
than a strict story limit.

Motion: Commissioner Everett moved to recommend approval to Lehi City’s request for review
of an amendment to the Lehi City Development Code Chapter 37, Design Standards, adding a
height limit of 3 stories in the Historic Downtown Design District; including the DRC comments;
with the findings as listed in the staff report. The motion was seconded by Commissioner Eyre.

Vote: Commissioner Newall, yes; Commissioner Bunker, yes; Commissioner Everett, yes;
Commissioner Carlson, no; Commissioner Eyre, no. The motion passed with 3-2 vote.

2.10) Public hearing and recommendation of Lehi City’s request for review of an amendment
to the Lehi City Development Code Chapter 38, Transit Oriented Development, adding a
requirement for for-sale housing as a part of the residential mix.

The amendment, prompted by City Council, aimed to diversify housing by mandating at least 10%
of TOD housing units for sale (e.g., condos or townhomes). The commission discussed the
breakdown of residential and retail spaces, considering the goal of housing diversity. While there
was a brief consideration of owner occupancy, it was deemed challenging to enforce. The emphasis
was on introducing housing variety rather than imposing stringent ownership requirements.

Chairperson Newall opened the public hearing.
Chairperson Newall closed the public hearing.

The Commissioners concerns arose about an abundance of rental properties in developments. The
commission discussed promoting owner-occupied townhomes and condos, suggesting a mandate
for at least 10% of housing for sale in Transit-Oriented Development (TOD). Enforceability and
potential disincentives for developers were noted.

Motion: Commissioner Everett moved to send a positive recommendation for Lehi City’s request
for review of an amendment to the Lehi City Development Code Chapter 38, Transit Oriented
Development, adding a requirement for for-sale housing as a part of the residential mix; including
the DRC comments; with the findings as listed in the staff report. The motion was seconded by
Commissioner Carlson.

Vote: Commissioner Newall, yes; Commissioner Bunker, yes; Commissioner Everett, yes;
Commissioner Carlson, yes; Commissioner Eyre, yes. The motion passed unanimously.
