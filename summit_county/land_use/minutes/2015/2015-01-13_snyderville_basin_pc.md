2. Public input session for a Development Agreement  Amendment regarding ridgelines 
at the Promontory Development  – Amir Caus, County Planner 
 
County Planner Amir Caus presented the staff report and explained that the Promontory 
development agreement was approved in 2001 and when it was approved, it referred to 
ridgelines where no development was to be seen from certain vantage points.  However, 
Staff recently found that the development agreement did not specify those vantage points, 
and the applicant initiated an amendment to the agreement to codify the specific vantage 
points.  Planner Caus explained that those vantage points have historically been used by 
the County to evaluate ridgelines and sensitive areas.  He noted that Promontory lies in 
the Eastern Summit County Planning District, but it was originally reviewed by the

3. Public hearing regarding amendments to the Gener al Plan Phase II 
 – Jennifer 
Strader, County Planner     
 
Community Development Director Patrick Putt introduced Staff and provided 
background on the proposed General Plan amendments.  He explained that the General 
Plan was last reviewed and revised in about 2002 to see if it was consistent with the 
community values and the direction they wanted to go as a whole and in individual 
neighborhoods.  He explained that the General Plan is an advisory document and is meant 
to offer suggestions, direction, and explanation as to how the community will evolve.  It 
is a written and illustrated strategy for dealing with the Snyderville Basin’s assets, 
characteristics, and opportunities.  It explains the vision, goals, and policies for a number 
of issues and is a roadmap to create the community they want and a guide to future 
decision making.  State law also requires that communities adopt a general plan and 
revise it regularly if the community will be involved in land use regulations. 
 
Director Putt explained that the Planning Commission and Staff have worked on updating 
the General Plan for several years.  They completed the first phase of the update a year 
ago.  It focused on the character of the neighborhoods in the Snyderville Basin and laid 
the foundation for Phase II, which deals with what will happen in the future.  A public

Director Putt discussed three things they are trying to achieve with the update to the 
General Plan.  One is a strategy that involves no new development entitlements beyond 
what exists right now through existing zoning or approved projects that are yet to be 
built.  He emphasized that no development rights will be taken away, but they will not 
create new or more entitlements.  Second, they want to work together to identify and 
direct growth to where it can be best accommodated, not simply fill in the remaining 
open spaces.  They want their community to be known by the quality and livability of 
their neighborhoods, not by impressive highway systems, and they believe there is value 
in the open space between communities.  Third, they want to explore developing policies 
and codes to help them transfer or move approved existing development into more 
suitable locations to achieve more open space and better community design.  He 
presented a drawing of the Snyderville Basin and explained that it was the first thing 
Staff reviewed in starting the process.  He indicated the road system and explained that 
they want to turn it into a broader network.  He also indicated the “green halo” of open 
area around the Basin.  He emphasized that no decision will be made this evening, and 
they are here to listen to the community tonight.  Another public hearing will be held on 
January 27, and it is possible that the Planning Commission could forward a 
recommendation at that meeting.  At that point, there will be another public education 
and public hearing process with the County Council, which will make final modifications 
and approve the document.  After that, changes will be made to the Development Code 
based on what is adopted in the General Plan.
