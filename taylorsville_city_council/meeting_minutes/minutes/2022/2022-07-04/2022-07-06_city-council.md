1.      WELCOME, INTRODUCTION AND PRELIMINARY MATTERS

Chair Barbieri called the meeting to order at 6:29 p.m. and welcomed those in attendance. A roll
call was conducted wherein the Chair and Councilmembers Burgess and Knudsen were present.

        1.1      Opening Ceremonies – Pledge/Reverence – Councilmember Knudsen (Opening
                 Ceremonies for August 3, 2022 to be arranged by Chair Barbieri

Councilmember Knudsen directed the Pledge of Allegiance before sharing the following George
Washington quote: “Happiness depends more upon the internal frame of a person’s own mind
than on the externals in the world.” He pointed out that although there might be much to complain
about, each of us could only control so much. It was helpful to focus on one’s own sphere of

        1.2      Mayor’s Report

      Mayor Kristie Overson reported that along with other mayors and police chiefs across the
valley, she and Chief Cottam had participated in an event designed to decrease the growing
problem of speeding motorists. It involved creating promotional videos about speeding and she
appreciated Sandy City Mayor Monica Zoltanski for initiating the project. Mayor Overson hoped
to see the video soon.

In a partnership with the Granite School District, the Mayor had participated in creating another
promotional video welcoming the 8,800 students back to the 13 schools in Taylorsville (8800
young residents attended those schools). The video would be used that fall as students returned to
school for the 2022-2023 school year.

Speaking of promotional videos, several were now available for viewing on the city’s website.
They promoted not only the City but businesses within Taylorsville as well.

Taylorsville’s annual cleanup event had been very successful two weeks before. Many people had
volunteered their time such as the Youth Council, the Taylorsville High School football team, city
employees and the Green Committee. She felt that Councilmember Burgess and the committee did
a tremendous job on the project.

The 26th Annual Taylorsville Dayzz had recently taken place, and this was the first year that TVPD
had taken the lead regarding event safety. She gave a shout-out to the Taylorsville Dayzz
Committee, Chair Jim Dunnigan, and TVPD Sgt. Jake Palmer. She said the parade had been very

       3.2       Taylorsville Municipal Justice Court Report – Judge Chris Bown

              Hon. Chris Bown explained that he had now been on the bench for a year and still
        felt he had the best staff around. He also spoke highly of the prosecutors and public
        defenders. Judge Bown pointed out that police could make his life difficult as a judge but
        that did not happen here. He hoped he was making an impact on the lives of those that
        appeared before him. He wanted them to feel heard.

        Utah’s Administrative Office of the Courts had issued a renewed mandate for masks to be
        worn in courts within Salt Lake County due to rising COVID-19 numbers. They could
        also now only have ten defendants in the courtroom at a time and some types of cases were
        moving back to virtual appearances.

        June 2022 Statistics
    • 1,053 traffic cases filed
    • 816 traffic cases adjudicated
    • 110 criminal cases filed
    • 130 criminal cases adjudicated
    • 25 small claims cases filed
    • 19 small claims cases adjudicated
On-line dispute resolution had reduced the number of small claims filings, although he expected
the numbers to rise when the statute increased the dollar amount threshold to be heard in justice
courts to $15,000
    • 3 appeals had been filed during June

The benchmark for adjudicating criminal cases was 180 days. That benchmark had been reached
in 94% of those cases in June.

The benchmark for small claims cases was 270 days and that had been reached for 100% of those
cases in June.

The benchmark for traffic cases (including DUIs) was 90 days. This was challenging in DUI cases
due to the necessity of lab testing. Still the benchmark had been reached in 93% of the June cases.

Reckless Driving had recently become a Class A misdemeanor so that charge would no longer be
prosecuted in the justice court but would instead go to District Court. Additionally, any speeding
citation issued for going more than 25 mph over the speed limit could now be prosecuted as a Class
A Reckless Driving.

Domestic Violence cases set for trial could now be transferred to district court if either involved
party requested it. That law had taken effect on July 1st. DUI cases that were second offenses were
now Class A misdemeanors so they too would now be prosecuted in District Court.

Automatic expungements would now be handled by the Administrative Office of Courts, making
it a less expensive and quicker process for the defendants.

Some Kearns High students visited the court on a public defender day. He had also gone to
Taylorsville High School and was currently mentoring a University of Utah Law Student.

7.      OTHER MATTERS

        7.1      Jordan Valley Water Conservancy: Property Tax Increase Discussion
                  – John Taylor

      City Administrator John Taylor also served on the Board of Trustees at the JVWCD and took
a moment to introduce Mark Stratford to the council. He served as the District’s attorney. Mr.
Taylor explained that JVWCD provided water to cities within Salt Lake County and to wholesalers
like Taylorsville-Bennion Improvement District. Their budget was $150,000,000 per year and
they brought in about $88,000,000. Their revenue sources were very similar to those of a
municipality—property taxes, water rates and impact fees. The District would need to increase
property tax by 7.8%.

Mr. Taylor then turned the time over to Mr. Stratford. He had worked with Ogden City for 14
years and felt an affinity for municipal law. This was an important opportunity for the District
which was accountable to local legislative bodies such as the city council.

All the city’s capital projects needed water. The District’s tax rate was increasing by 7.8% which
would raise approximately $1.7 million for the District. It would also help support certain projects
such as expanding the water treatment plant as well as tanks, wells and additional infrastructure.
For an average home in Taylorsville, it would result in an additional $10 per year. Their public
hearing was scheduled for August 10, 2022 at 6:00 p.m. at the JVWCD office.
