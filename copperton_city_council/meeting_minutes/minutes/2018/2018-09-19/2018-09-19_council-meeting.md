               Michele Nicoletti spoke under Citizen Comments regarding the horse therapy
non-profit. She owns a cheese business at the end of the circle and is concerned about traffic,
parking and flies. She also asked why the utilities are not coming from a nearby Kennecott

              Cheryl Carrigan spoke under Citizen Comments stating she knows the people
who own the horses. The non-profit is called Unbridled Hope. It is a horse rescue operation that
uses the horses for children with disabilities and wounded veterans for therapy purposes. It is
not open to anyone.

                Mayor Clayton stated the organization is located on Salt Lake County land and is
not subject to the Copperton Council’s authority. However, Copperton could have some input.
He is also concerned about odor and flies and has reached out to West Jordan code enforcement

the road in terms of leasing the land to the non-profit, and they are willing to see what they can
do to address concerns.

                 Mayor Clayton proposed a town hall meeting to discuss the issue and possible
solutions. It is Rio Tinto’s land. This could be beneficial to the town if it can be done in the right
way.

                Council Member Pazell stated the impression they received is that the stables
will be downhill and out of sight, but that does not appear to be the case.

               Mayor Clayton stated he will work on holding the meeting in the next two weeks.

Unified Police Department Tax

               Council Member Pazell stated he has requested a review of the tax proposal from
the Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA).

               Scott Carver, SLVLESA Administrator, gave an overview of the SLVLESA tax.
The only purpose of SLVLESA is to raise taxes to pay the law enforcement bill, which pays for
the wages of the officers, buildings, adding new officers, and keeping fund balance healthy.

            Council Member Bailey asked what was Copperton’s percentage of the vote on
the SLVLESA board.

                Council Member Pazell stated it is one vote. Although he approves of the way
Scott Carver is handling things, he does not approve of the way SLVLESA is structured. For
example, by operating on a calendar year, SLVLESA has to pay $150,000 in interest payments.
UPD’s management is a mess, which is why Riverton and Herriman have decided to leave. It is
not wise to increase taxes when the money management has been poor.

              David Osoro asked what Copperton residents pay to SLVLESA.

              Council Member Bailey stated that would be on each homeowner’s tax bill.

              Joyce Sauter stated she had no problem in increase for police officers, but does
have a problem with an increase for buildings. Buildings should be taken care of by the cities.

              The Council reviewed the following Resolution approving the levying of property

Planning and Zoning

               Mr. Curtis Woodward stated the General Plan is a great guiding documents, but
under state law it is an advisory document. The General Plan cannot trump zoning. All zoning
is based on the ordinances adopted by the Council.

The Council has considered the first draft of a Historic Preservation Code. The priority is mostly
about making sure that new construction is compatible with the existing housing stock in
Copperton rather than trying to control existing homes. The Planning Commission will have a
discussion about the more vague aspects of the ordinances. An ordinance would have to have
standards that are measurable and enforceable.

             Vern Winkler, Planning Commission, stated he is concerned about requiring
new houses to following rules but letter existing homes do whatever they want. Compatibility
sounds easy but there are many different styles of homes.
