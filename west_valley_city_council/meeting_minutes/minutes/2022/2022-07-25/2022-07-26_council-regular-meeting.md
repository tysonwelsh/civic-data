A.    ACCEPT PUBLIC INPUT REGARDING APPLICATION ZT-3-2022,
      FILED BY WEST VALLEY CITY, REQUESTING AN AMENDMENT TO
      CHANGE THE ZONING ORDINANCE (TITLE 7) AND THE
      CONDOMINIUM ORDINANCE (TITLE 9) TO PROHIBIT PRIVATE
      STREETS, ALLOW PARKING SPACES FOR TOWNHOMES AND
      CONDOMINIUMS ATTACHED TO THE SIDE OF EACH OTHER TO
      BACK ONTO A STREET, AND CREATE TWO NEW STREET
      CLASSIFICATIONS FOR USE IN PLANNED UNIT DEVELOPMENTS
      Mayor Lang informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular Council
      Meeting scheduled July 26, 2022, in order for the City Council to hear and consider
      public comments regarding application ZT-3-2022, Filed by West Valley City,
      Requesting an Amendment to Change the Zoning Ordinance (Title 7) and the
      Condominium Ordinance (Title 9) to Prohibit Private Streets, Allow Parking Spaces
      for Townhomes and Condominiums Attached to the Side of Each Other to Back Onto
      a Street, and Create Two New Street Classifications for Use in Planned Unit
      Developments.

      Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
      information as follows:


             The City Council has expressed concerns about the financial burden private
             streets place on the residents of planned unit developments (PUDs) with
             private streets and directed staff to prepare an ordinance revision that would
             prohibit private streets. This ordinance is in response to that direction.

      Mayor Lang opened the Public Hearing.

      Pete Travish asked if there is a grandfather clause or existing developments. Mayor
      Lang stated that these proposed changes would only apply to new developments
      moving forward.

      Christopher Clifford stated that he has been in and around West Valley City for
      nearly 32 years. He stated he has developed in the Decker Lake area and elsewhere
      throughout the City. Mr. Clifford stated that he is supportive of the recommendation
      passed froward from the Planning Commission. He indicated that there is no
      fundamental difference between attached units and detached units in a PUD and
      removing the ability to provide private streets in certain scenarios would be like
      designing a car and taking the wheels off. He stated that its important to consider
      remaining housing stock options when facing an affordability crisis. Mr. Clifford
      stated that to not provide an option for private streets for detached housing will
      significantly impact development moving forward. He noted that it’s
      discriminatory and may put the city in a Legal situation.

 Russell Moore stated he has applications that have been approved and are in the
 process and questioned whether these will be impacted. Steve Pastorik, CED,
 replied no and indicated that these are vested with a development agreement. Mr.
 Moore stated that he recently brought an application before the City Council that
 he adjusted to having all public streets but the Council still denied it. He indicated
 the as a developer, he likes the idea of public streets because he isn’t responsible
 for maintaining them. He noted, however, that removing the option of private
 streets entirely creates difficulty in design and can create other unforeseen
 problems. He stated that smaller properties will not be able to develop and gated
 communities will not be an option. Mr. Moore stated that some residents like
 HOA’s because they know who to complain to if there are maintenance or road
 issues.

 Robert Farsworth stated by removing the option of private streets, the City will find
 it almost impossible to develop certain infill properties. He indicated that one of the
 nicest PUD’s in the City would not be approved if this ordinance were in place. Mr.
 Farsworth stated that there are many good product types, including those in the RS
 Zone, that will need private streets. He noted that gated communities will not be an
 option in West Valley City.

 Sam Work stated the he owns a long narrow property in the City that would be
 impacted by this proposal. However, Mr. Work stated that he supports this change.
 He stated that the financial upkeep of private streets often because too difficult and
 communities fall into disrepair which causes issued in neighborhoods.

 Matt Watson stated a blanket prohibition of private streets would impact the City
 negatively. He stated that there would be little flexibility which creates difficulty in
 developing unique properties. Mr. Watson stated that private streets are narrower
 but still exceed fire standards and also help prevent people from speeding through
 neighborhoods. He indicated that he understands the concern about burdening
 residents for the cost of upkeep but suggested other options like reserve studies.
 Mr. Watson stated that housing demand is high and is currently unaffordable and
 restricting the type of homes that can be built further contribute to the problem.

 Justin Baer stated that he represents a company who owns property in West Valley
 City. He provided 3 exhibits and 3 properties that would be difficult, if not
 impossible, to develop if this application were approved. Mr. Baer detailed the
 issues developers would face.

 Mayor Lang closed the Public Hearing.

B.    ACCEPT PUBLIC COMMENT REGARDING APPLICATION SV-2-2022,
      FILED BY NORTHRUP GRUMMAN, REQUESTING A VACATION OF A
      PERPETUAL EASEMENT AND RIGHT-OF-WAY LOCATED AT
      APPROXIMATELY 7000 WEST 5400 SOUTH
      Mayor Lang informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular Council
      Meeting scheduled July 26, 2022, in order for the City Council to hear and consider
      public comments regarding application SV-2-2022, Filed by Northrup Grumman,
      Requesting a Vacation of a Perpetual Easement and Right-of-Way Located at
      Approximately 7000 West 5400 South.

      Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
      information as follows:

             Katherine Pasker, representing Northrop Grumman Innovation systems Inc.
             is requesting a vacation for a perpetual easement and right-of-way dedicated
             to Salt Lake County in 1932. Subsequent to West Valley City’s
             incorporation in 1980, this easement and right-of-way was transferred to the
             City. The purpose for the vacation is to clean up title records.

             According to County records, Arnold and Areva Petersen dedicated 33-feet
             of their property to Salt Lake County for a future public highway. This deed
             was recorded in November 1932. The highway was obviously not built,
             and the property was subsequently developed by Hexcel.

             Although the unimproved street will be vacated by ordinance, the ordinance
             will have no force or effect of any franchise rights of any public utilities,
             nor would they be impaired thereby. The ordinance will also protect any
             holders of existing franchises, water drainage pipelines, or other such uses
             as they may presently exist under, over or upon said property or as are or
             may be shown on Salt Lake County records.

      Mayor Lang opened the Public Hearing.

      Catherine Pasker stated she is representing Northrup Grumman. She introduced
      Lyndon Young of Northrop Grumman as well. Ms. Pasker indicated she is here to
      answer any questions.

      Mayor Lang closed the Public Hearing.

      Requesting Final Plat Approval for the 4100 South Commons Office Condominiums

      Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
      information as follows:

             Brook Hintze, representing Back Development, is requesting consideration
             to amend the 4100 Commons Office Condominium Plat. This plat was
             recorded with the office of the Salt Lake County Recorder in December

        2021. The property is located at 3745 West 4100 South.

        The purpose for the original condominium plat was to re-purpose the old
        Granger Medical site by converting the interior space for individual
        condominiums. The original plat divided interior spaces into smaller units
        which allow businesses the opportunity and affordability to own a piece of
        a much larger, nicer facility for business operations. According to the
        applicant during the original approvals, “It creates synergy within the

        parking requirements within City ordinances will continue to be satisfied.
        The applicant will be required to update and revise the declaration of
        condominium which will be recorded with the amended plat.

RESOLUTION 22-122: APPROVE TWO BETTERMENT AGREEMENTS
BETWEEN         THE      CITY     AND     THE     UTAH       DEPARTMENT            OF
TRANSPORTATION FOR CERTAIN ADDITIONAL IMPROVEMENTS ON AND
AROUND MOUNTAIN VIEW CORRIDOR
Wayne Pyle, City Manager, presented proposed Resolution 22-122 that would approve Two
Betterment Agreements Between the City and the Utah Department of Transportation for
Certain Additional Improvements On and Around Mountain View Corridor.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       Betterment Agreement No. 2 includes a $900,000 credit from UDOT project funds
       designated to aesthetic improvements, which was applied toward City betterments.
       Total cost to the City for this betterment agreement is $584,375.08. Funds for these
       betterments are available from a previous budget year, a budget opening is not
       required.

       Betterments in this agreement include:
           Lighting & landscaping on 3500 South, 5600 West, Cilma Drive and 3575
              South Parking Lots
           Lighting on Masters Drive, Marsha Drive, 2400 South, South Frontage
              Road and 3100 South
           Miscellaneous site furnishings on the trail
           Speed humps on Cilma Drive
           A credit from UDOT for City-furnished street lights

       Betterment Agreement No. 3 includes:
           5600 West stamped concrete parkstrip
           3500 South property landscape restoration for power burial improvements
           2400 South roadway improvements to accommodate the City’s new road
              project (Beagley Lane extension - $263,751 from Beagley project funds)
