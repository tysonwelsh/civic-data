Bonds

   • 2000B - $14,000,000 – 4.3% to 5.6% interest rates

Secondary Water Fees

   • Secondary monthly fees based on lot size (FY 2001)

         • 1/4 - 1/3 acre = $16.50 (FY 2018 $27.93; current fee = $22.93)
         • 1/3 – 1/2 acre = $19.50 (FY 2018 $33.68; current fee = $28.68)

Council Members asked questions regarding the necessity of the secondary water metering
project and Water Director Duane Green explained that water capacity was available and the
system was carrying the water but water pressure and the draw on the system was the issue of
concern. He said that with the implementation of water meters, residents would become aware
of their water usage and implement water conservation practices.

City Attorney Ryan Carter explained the State Legislature requirements for secondary water
metering. He said that water metering was an effective tool to encourage water conservation.

Questions from the Council Members were then addressed by Mr. Hicks regarding the water
bonds and refinancing procedures.

Council Member McCay asked if in the areas of Riverton that were still going to be developed,
did the agreement require that water shares per acre be given to the City. Ryan Carter said that,
generally speaking, any time you have new development coming on line they are required to
dedicate shares to go hand in hand with the development of their project, three acre feet for one

were drawn from and he said a proposed ordinance would be before the Council very soon that

secondary water users should be monitoring their use.

Council Member Stewart spoke of the background of REDIIF and explained that REDIIF was
created a number of years ago by the City Council as an idea to invest in future infrastructure.
Mr. Hicks explained that the title of REDIIF was the Riverton Economic Development
Infrastructure and Investment Fund. He said management’s intention at the time was for the City
Council to have a way to generate more revenue; they were not charging franchise fees, but
legally had the right to do that as a City. The Council then agreed to start charging franchise
fees to generate some revenue, as long as it is for economic development and infrastructure,
such as the 2007 road project.

Council Member Stewart then asked why the sanitation fund should be its own enterprise fund.

Kevin Hicks explained that any revenue stream that was funded by the people that were using it
qualified to be an enterprise fund; however it was not required or mandated.

10. Citizen Comment

Mayor Staggs called for public comments.

Emily Britton, resident of Midas Crossing Subdivision, spoke of a flood plain that had been
designated in her neighborhood and said she and her neighbors had not been made aware of
an existing flood plain at any time during the purchase of their homes until they received a letter
from the City. She said she and her neighbors were advocating for a study on the land and a
solution for their neighborhood. She said the neighborhood needed assurance of increased
safety in regards to flooding of the creek and requested options of what could be done to make
their homes safe. She recommended that, for the safety of their neighborhood, funding for a
study regarding the flood plain be included in the budget.

Mark Newman encouraged the purchase and development of property located east of the Main
City Park. He said he would like to expand the park in honor of his mother and he was willing to
sacrifice his project for the City and asked that the City Council reconsider his offer and find the
funding for the property and project. He said he was willing to donate $200,000 worth of work on
the project. He said if the City does not expand the park, he will develop the property with
housing units.

There being no further comments, Mayor Staggs closed the Public Comment period.

Council Member McDougal expressed his concern regarding the Sanitation Fund and said that
the Enterprise Funds needed to be addressed, how they were currently being funded, and look
ways to make them financially soluble for the future. Mayor Staggs said that those discussions
had taken place in Strategic Planning and the Council had decided to keep the rates where they
were for the 2020-2021 Budget Year.

Lengthy discussion was held regarding the Sanitation Fund and Kevin Hicks explained that the
auditor’s recommendation was to treat it like an enterprise fund and charge the fees to cover the
service; however, if the Council chose not to do that, it should not be called an enterprise fund.
He suggested that in the future the resident’s fees at least pay for the cost of the totes. Council
Member Wells agreed to exploring options but not this year amid the Covid-19 pandemic.
Council Member McDougal said “this can has been kicked down the road by previous
administrations and the longer the can is kicked down the road the fee is going to be larger”. He
said the longer it was delayed the worse it was going to be for the citizens.

Discussion was then held regarding REDIIF and Kevin Hicks said that REDIIF was a capital
improvements fund and it needed to be kept as long as there were plans for its use. Council
Member Buroker said the Council needed to do the right thing for the citizens and to make sure
they were making the improvements that make the citizens lives better.

Discussions continued regarding Fund Balance amounts and what would be best for the City.
Kevin Hicks suggested that the Fund Balance be held at six-month income, which would be a
stable source of income. Also, that amount would not impact the bond rating, it would satisfy the
requirements of the auditors, and it would address the future needs of sanitation. It was
determined that further discussion would be held at the Strategic Planning Meeting in January.
