OPENING CEREMONY:
 Shelly Ortiz, Director of the Utah State University Center for Community provided the
opening ceremony and Marcus Jenkins, President of the Black Student Union led the
audience in the pledge of allegiance.
Ms. Ortiz stated that the center highlights the mission and commitment of USU. The
purpose of the center is to cultivate goodwill and greater understanding on campus/off
campus and the surrounding communities. The center offers education opportunities and
unique exchanges. The community is invited to the Juneteenth Community BBQ at
Bridger Park on June 19 in celebration of the State Holiday.
Mr. Jenkins introduced the Black Student Union Council. Juneteenth is a powerful
symbol of resilience, culture, and ongoing struggle for justice. It is a day to remember
Black History, educate peers, and mobilize strength. On June 19, 1865, Federal troops
arrived in Galveston, Texas to end slavery two years after President Abraham Lincoln
signed the Emancipation Proclamation. The community is invited to this year's
celebration as neighbors and as a community.

UNIT CITATION AWARD

On May 15, 2024, members of the Logan City Police Department’s Investigations
Division assisted Detective Micah Vehrs who was working an Internet Crimes Against
Children Sex Enticement case. The suspect, a 33-year-old male, planned to meet at
Denzel Stewart Nature Park with what he thought was a 13-year-old girl to engage in
sexual activity. A detailed plan was put together to apprehend the suspect.

The suspect, when contacted at the park, refused to comply with lawful orders to
surrender despite several minutes of negotiation. Team members contained the suspect
and kept innocent parties from the area. Negotiations did succeed in coaxing the suspect
from his vehicle, but he continued to be argumentative and unwilling to follow
commands.

Two officers moved around to the back of the suspects’ vehicle and attempted to deploy a
Taser to safely apprehend the suspect, however the device mal-functioned. The suspect
spun around to face them and immediately started reaching for his waistband. Det Vehrs
rapidly moved towards the suspect, grabbed him, and took him to the ground while other
team members moved in to assist. The suspect resisted and continued to reach for his
waistband. Officers yelled, “He’s reaching! He’s reaching!” Despite valiant efforts by
the apprehension team, the suspect was able to remove a handgun from his waistband.
Officers saw that and yelled, “Gun! Gun!” Det. Vehrs while still fighting the suspect,
recognized the immediate danger to officers, and unholstered his weapon, pointing it
directly at the suspect’s chest. Almost simultaneously Detective Vehrs and the suspect
discharged their firearms. The suspect’s discharge fortunately hit the ground directly in
front of Detective Crotts who was on his knees wrestling with the suspect. Shrapnel from
that round hit Detective Crotts in both of his legs requiring medical attention at Logan
Regional Hospital.

Despite suffering a mortal gunshot wound to the chest, the suspect, in his last seconds of
life, attempted to point his gun at officers. Agent Whitlock maneuvered quickly to help
get the gun out of the suspects hands.

Team members began immediate lifesaving efforts on the suspect and an ambulance was
quickly summoned. Despite desperate efforts to save this man’s life, he was pronounced
dead at the scene.

These good Peace Officers demonstrated great courage and selflessness in their actions
that day. Without thought for own safety, they placed themselves in harm’s way with an
armed assailant and kept our community and each other safe from multiple dangers posed
by this man. This unit of officers didn’t want this result to happen, but they were prepared
and upheld their oath to protect and serve and did so valiantly even as they worked
feverishly and heroically to try and save his life.

Councilmember A. Anderson inquired when the maker space will come available.

Michael Sauers, Library Director answered that the aim is early July.

Councilmember Johnson asked if the fees associated with the makerspace are for
commercial use, the meeting room is still free for non-profit or community space.

Mr. Sauers confirmed that this is the case, the fees are waived for non-profits or as a
community space.

The proposed resolution will be an action item and public hearing at the July 15, 2025
Council meeting.

No further workshop items were presented.

OTHER CONSIDERATIONS:

Councilmember Johnson requested confirmation the City is still on track for July 15 for
the solar power feasibility study.

Mayor Daines confirmed that the timeline is still on track.

Councilmember Johnson expressed concerns about the bike lane on 500 North being used
as a parking lot.

Mayor Daines asked if warning tickets have begun to be issued.

Police Chief Jeff Simmons responded that tickets have not yet been issued. Normally,
parking enforcement is not available on Sunday.

The Council discussed possible avenues including further enforcement and education.

Mayor Daines discussed the possibility of changing the time of the parade earlier due to
the heat.

Councilmember López requested training or informing staff in the event of immigration
entering the city premises. He encouraged the discussion regarding the library and
recreation center, so staff are aware of what they can and cannot do.

ADJOURNED. There being no further business to come before the Council, the Logan
Municipal Council adjourned to a meeting of the Logan Redevelopment Agency at 6:45.

p.m.



Esli Morales, Deputy City Recorder
