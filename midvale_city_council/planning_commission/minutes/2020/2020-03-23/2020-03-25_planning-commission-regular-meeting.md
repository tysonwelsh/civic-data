   1. CUP-21-36-477-029; SPECTRUM AUTO REPAIR; 8490 S STATE ST;
      CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT FOR VEHICLE REPAIR USE; STATE STREET
      COMMERCIAL ZONE; SPECTRUM REAL ESTATE, LLC (APPLICANT)

Ms. Jana Ward present that the applicant, Spectrum Real Estate, LLC, is requesting a conditional
use permit approval to establish and operate a vehicle repair business in an existing commercial
building located at 8490 S State Street. The building contains two tenant spaces with the
applicant occupying the entire ground level. The mezzanine is a separate leasable space
approximately 1,600 ft² in size. The property is located in the State Street (SSC) zone.

The applicant proposes to occupy a 6,000 ft² tenant space with three bays for auto repair
including cosmetic, mechanical, structural and paint work. There will be three employees and
hours of operation are proposed to be 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM, Monday through Friday.

The subject property is adjacent to other State Street Commercial (SCC) zoned properties on all
sides.

ORDINANCE CONSIDERATIONS:

The State Street Commercial (SCC) zone allows vehicle repair uses under a conditional use
permit, provided the Planning Commission concludes that the application fully mitigates all
identified adverse impacts and complies with the following general standards:

     1. The application complies with all applicable provisions of this title, State and Federal
        Law;
     2. The structures associated with the use are compatible with surrounding structures in
        terms of use, scale, mass and circulation;
     3. The use is not detrimental to the public health, safety and welfare;
     4. The use is consistent with the Midvale City General Plan, as amended;
     5. Traffic conditions are not adversely affected by the proposed use including the
        existence of or need for dedicated turn lanes, pedestrian access, and capacity of the
        existing streets;
     6. Sufficient utility capacity;
     7. Sufficient emergency vehicle access;
     8. Location and design of off-street parking as well as compliance with off-street parking
        standards provided for in §17-7-13.8;
     9. Fencing, screening, and landscaping to separate the use from adjoining uses and
        mitigate the potential for conflict in uses;

     10. Compatibility of the proposed mass, bulk, design, orientation, and location of the
         structures on the site; including compatibility with buildings on adjoining lots and to
         the street;
     11. Exterior lighting that complies with the lighting standards of the zone and is designed
         to minimize conflict and light trespass with surrounding uses; and
     12. Within and adjoining the site, impacts on the aquifer, slope retention, and flood
        potential have been fully mitigated and the proposed structure is appropriate to the
        topography of the site.

Staff has not identified any apparent conflicts with provisions of the zoning ordinance or state
and federal law. The business will be conducted within the existing development and tenant
space. No modifications to the building or site are proposed to be made. If any future changes to
the site or building are proposed, another review will be required. To comply with other
requirements of the Midvale City Municipal Code, a building permit for tenant improvements
may be required and a business license will need to be obtained. Staff recommends including
these two items as conditions of approval.

No apparent concerns regarding public health, safety, or welfare have been identified.

For vehicle repair uses, the minimum parking requirement is 1 space per employee and 4 spaces
per service bay, resulting in a minimum of 15 parking stalls required. The applicant intends to
have 3 employees and operate with the existing 3 service bays. The site plan shows 30 parking
stalls designated to this tenant space. The mezzanine tenant space has 8 designated stalls directly
east of the building that are called out on the site plan.

The proposed use is adjacent to other commercially zoned properties, and no concerns regarding
compatibility with surrounding structures; public health, safety, and welfare; traffic; utility
capacity; emergency vehicle access; conflicts with adjacent uses; or impacts to the aquifer,
slopes, flood potential, or topography have been identified.

STAFF RECOMMENDATIONS:

Based on compliance with the requirements of the Midvale City Municipal Code demonstrated
in the application or addressed by the inclusion of conditions of approval, Planning Staff
recommends approving the conditional use permit for a vehicle repair at 8490 S State Street with
the following conditions of approval.

   1. The applicant shall obtain a building permit for any improvements or modifications made
      to the existing building and lot.
   2. The exterior lighting on the building and site shall be updated to comply with current
      ordinance requirements.
   3. The applicant shall obtain and maintain a Midvale City Business License prior to
      operation of the proposed business at this location.
   4. All signage on the property shall comply with the requirements of the SCC zone and sign
      permits shall be obtained prior to installation of such signage.
   5. All vehicles related to the business shall be parked in the stalls associated with the tenant
      space as designated on the attached site plan or within the building.
   6. The parking spaces shall be striped as shown on the submitted site plan.

   2. TXT-20-01; TEXT AMENDMENT TO ADD “SPECIAL SERVICE DISTRICT
      FOR WATER AND SEWER” AS A PUBLIC FACILITY IN THE PUBLIC
      FACILITIES OVERLAY ZONE; CITY STAFF (APPLICANT)

Ms. Lesley Burns presented that Midvale City Staff is proposing a text amendment to add
“Special Service District for Water and Sewer as defined by Utah Code” as a type of public
facility in the Public Facilities Overlay Zone. The purpose of the Public Facilities Overlay Zone
is to “provide for locations and appropriate standards for a range of public uses conducted by
governmental and specified agencies. These uses are operated for the benefit of the community
and provide basic services and essential support services for the community. Because of the
nature of these uses, they are generally not identified with any specific zone district, having the
need to locate in areas that best serve the community. The overlay zone allows the city council,
through its legislative discretion, to provide for areas in which to locate public facilities to best
support the community in a cost effective and efficient manner. It is the intent of this overlay to
protect neighboring private uses from incompatible public uses and ensure the impacts
associated with the public uses are appropriately mitigated.” Utah Code defines a special
service district as “a limited purpose local government entity” and provides the parameters under
which it must operate. Special Service Districts for water and sewer that serve Midvale include:
Midvalley Improvement District, Sandy Suburban Improvement District, and South Valley
Water Reclamation. The proposed text amendment will allow these entities the ability to use the
Public Facilities Overlay Zone for new development within existing or new operating sites
within Midvale under the legislative discretion of the City.

Staff has prepared a text amendment for the Planning Commission’s consideration as included in
Attachment A.

STAFF RECOMMENDATION:

Based on special service districts for water and sewer providing an integral service to Midvale
City, similar to services provided by the City itself, and, therefore, aligning with the stated
purpose of the Public Facilities Overlay Zone, Staff recommends the Planning Commission
forwards a positive recommendation to the City Council to adopt Attachment A.

   3. REZ/SPR-22-31-104-009; MIDVALLEY IMPROVEMENT DISTRICT
      FACILITY; 150-160 E 8000 S; REZONE TO ADD PUBLIC FACILITIES
      OVERLAY TO EXISTING TOD ZONE AND SITE PLAN MODIFICATIONS TO
      ACCOMMODATE LARGE VEHICLES; MIDVALLEY IMPROVEMENT
      DISTRICT (APPLICANT)

Ms. Jana Ward presented that the Midvalley Improvement District (MID) is a special service
district that was organized for the purpose of constructing and maintaining the area’s sewer
system. The purpose of the district is to provide public sanitary sewer collection and conveyance
services to the residents within boundaries of the district. Its main operations facility is located at
150-160 E 7800 S. MID is proposing to make some improvements to this facility. These
improvements are intended to create safer access from 7800 S and a better circulation pattern for
the District’s large vehicles and the public’s RV’s and trailers when utilizing the RV dump
station on site. This proposal does not expand the current 1.5-acre land area of the MID’s
existing facility but relocates and expands a carport structure so it is within 5 feet of the east
property line, redesigns the access and circulation through the site by widening the west
driveway access from 26 to 40 feet, and makes the 3 access points off of 7800 S one-way. The
MID property is currently zoned Transit Oriented Development (TOD). The existing use and

PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION MARCH 25TH, 2020
development of the property predates the current zoning ordinance and is considered non-
conforming under the TOD zone. It is not like other TOD type developments and is more
characteristic of the specific public facility that it is. Because of this and the specific community
function this facility serves, the request is being made to rezone this property to include the
Public Facilities Overlay and to approve the proposed site plan allowing the following changes to
the existing site which will allow the facility to operate more effectively and in a safer fashion:

   •   Construction of a relocated carport with a 5-foot setback from the east property line. The
       TOD Zone requires a 15-foot landscaped buffer because the east property line is adjacent
       to a single-family residential zone.
   •   Allowing the existing two-foot retaining wall with 6-foot chain link fence along the east
       property line to remain. The TOD Zone requires a 7-foot high screening fence adjacent to
       a single-family residential zone.
   •   Widening the functioning west driveway on 7800 S from 26 feet to 40 feet and making
       the driveways one-way. The TOD Zone limits one-way drive widths to 20 feet.

In order to consider a rezone of the property to include the Public Facilities Overlay, a site plan
is required to be reviewed in conjunction with the proposed rezone application. The intent of the
Public Facilities Overlay is to allow specific public facilities to be located and developed to best
support the community in a cost effective and efficient manner, while ensuring the impacts
associated with the public facility are appropriately mitigated. The required findings for this
type of proposal include the following:

   A. There is a demonstrated need for the public facility within the community at large and it
      is not contrary to the public interest.
   B. The public facility is consistent with the goals and policies of the general plan and
      applicable ordinances of the city where feasible.
   C. The public facility is located, planned and developed in such a manner that it is not
      inconsistent with the health, safety or general welfare of persons residing or working in
      the city. This includes, but is not limited to the following:
          1. The generation of noise, noxious or offensive emissions, or other nuisances which
              may be injurious or detrimental to the surrounding area.
          2. The availability of public services to support the public facility, including utilities;
              vehicular, pedestrian and public transit systems; police; fire; education; and social
              and health services.
          3. The adequacy of landscaping, screening and buffering, building setbacks, parking,
              open space, or other development characteristics necessary to mitigate the impact
              of the public facility on neighboring properties.
          4. Proposed public facilities which exceed the bulk, height, density, or other use
              standards in the underlying zone must demonstrate such variances are essential to
              the function or operation of such facility. The development standards of the
              underlying zone and adjacent development shall be utilized where feasible.

This property has functioned as MID’s operation facility for several years. The use of the
property is not proposed to change from what is existing today. The existing access and vehicular
circulation on the site, however, are inadequate for the large service trucks entering the property
as well as the public bringing in RV’s and trailers to utilize the RV dump station. Trucks and
RV’s/trailers are forced into oncoming traffic to make the right turn into the facility because the

PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION MARCH 25TH, 2020
existing accesses are too narrow. In addition, the existing carport structures are continually being
damaged from RV’s/trailers running into the structures because of the tight space and no clear
direction when entering the site. By moving the carport structures and creating clear one-way
accesses as proposed, more area is provided for the public to maneuver RV’s and trailers within
the property. This proposal provides MID with an opportunity to create a safer environment for
MID’s employees and the public. The information provided by MID explains the existing issues
in more detail.

The City Engineer has reviewed and is supportive of the proposed widened access provided the
westernmost curb cut is eliminated and replaced with high back curb as part of the site
improvements. Staff believes any potential impact to existing single family residential properties
to the east with the proposed reduced landscape buffer and allowing the existing chain link fence
to remain is eliminated by the 65-foot wide canal which is located between the MID east
property line and the existing single family residential lots.

The items the Planning Commission must specifically discuss and determine if they are
warranted and do not negatively affect the surrounding area include the following:

   1. The reduced landscaped buffer, including the retention of the existing 6-foot high chain
      link fence, adjacent to the single-family residential zone along the east property line.
   2. The widened access drive along 7800 South to accommodate large vehicles.
