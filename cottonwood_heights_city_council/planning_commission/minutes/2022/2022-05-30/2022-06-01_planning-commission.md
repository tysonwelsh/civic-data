36   Senior City Planner, Andrew Hulka, presented the Staff Report and stated that the above matter
37   was before the Planning Commission previously with an original request for a rezone from R-1-8
38   to R-2-8. The matter was forwarded to the City Council with a recommendation of approval. The
39   application was heard by the City Council at its April 2022 Business Meeting where it was

44   displayed. The applicant was still proposing twin homes, which require additional buffering
45   between the proposed and existing neighbors. Staff recommended approval based on the findings
46   set forth in the staff report.

13                          Hearing to Receive Comments and Take Possible Action on a Proposed
14                          Ordinance Update Relating to the Sensitive Lands Evaluation and
15                          Development Standards (“SLEDS”) Ordinance (Section 19.72 of the
16                          Zoning Ordinance).
17
18   Ms. DeSeelhorst presented the Staff Report and stated that staff received feedback from the Federal
19   Emergency Management Agency (“FEMA”) and the U.S. Forest Service. The general sentiment
20   was that the Ordinance is consistent with their process and provided minor language updates.
21   Verbiage changes included recognition of waterways being reviewed in addition to the ecosystems
22   on adjacent and larger components to the Riparian Protection Area. She clarified that the zone
23   runs from the top of bank on both sides of a waterway spanning horizontally. If there is a conflict
24   between City regulations or other jurisdictions, the most restrictive shall apply. The proposed
25   language provided clarification that buffer zones are measured from both sides of the waterway.
26
27   Ms. DeSeelhorst reported that the Forest Service has focused its Riparian Protection Plans on
28   course, woody debris. The debris is most often pieces of felled trees measuring greater than three
29   inches in diameter and greater than three feet in length that occur in waterways. The felled trees
30   are important for riparian health, provide soil function, habitat forest generation, and are a critical
31   component to the function of these areas. The Forest Service recommended the course, woody
32   debris not be removed unless it is a critical hazard to human health or safety. A Table of Uses was
33   displayed. FEMA recommended the specification that development be located in the Glossary
34   and include, but not be limited to the above items.
35
36   Commissioner Mills suggested language be added to clarify that structures within the 0 to 100-
37   foot range be limited in size to less than 10,000 square feet. Ms. DeSeelhorst recommended the
38   Commission provide direction to Staff as they review what may be an appropriate threshold.
39
40   Commissioner Ebbeler believed that the size issue could be resolved by considering the impervious
41   surface rather than the square footage of the structure. Staff noted that there are impervious surface
42   maximums in the Sensitive Lands Ordinance.
43
44   Mr. Hulka provided a consolidated comprehensive list to the Ordinance. Public comments were
45   received requesting a larger, interactive map, discussions with fault hazard experts regarding
46   setbacks, and a summary of powers and duties of individual City groups. He stated that a major

45   Senior City Planner, Andrew Hulka, presented the Staff Report and displayed a property rendering.
46   The property is current Residential with several existing structures. The original request was heard

 1   by the Planning Commission in March 2022 who forwarded a positive recommendation to the City
 2   Council who denied the request in April 2022. Concerns were discussed. The applicant has now
 3   returned with a slightly new proposal that does not include a change to the land use designation.
 4   The current land use is Residential Office and was considered in 2019 by the previous City
 5   Council.

 7   A Concept Plan was presented. Mr. Hulka stated that the Concept Plan reflects a conceptual three-
 8   lot layout that conforms to the Residential Office setbacks, which require a 25-foot side yard
 9   setback when the property is adjacent to a residential zone with a 30-foot rear yard setback.
10   Residential uses, including single and two-family dwellings and home occupations, are permitted.
11   Mr. Hulka confirmed that no commercial or office uses will be permitted in the proposed zone.
12   The request was determined to be consistent with the current General Plan land use designation.
13   Staff recommended approval.
14
15   The applicant, Adam Nash, reported that the City went through an exhaustive Master Plan process
16   in 2019. The subject property was identified as being zoned RO with the understanding that there
17   are currently 15 similar parcels located along Fort Union Boulevard. Mr. Nash reported that twin
18   homes are historically less expensive than single-family residential and will contribute to the
19   affordability index.
20
21   Associate Senior Planner, Samantha DeSeelhorst clarified that due to the audio platform being
22   used, additional public comment would be addressed at the next City Council Meeting where there
23   will be an opportunity for public comment.

27   Cynthia Blair gave her address as 7183 Macintosh Lane, which is west of the subject property.
28   Approximately 90 signatures were obtained from those opposed to the previous request and who
29   appreciated the increased setback included in the current request. The neighbors were
30   overwhelmingly opposed to the zone change due to the increase in density, traffic, and noise.
31   Ms. Blair encouraged the omission to deny the request.
32
33   Robbie McFarland, a Reindeer Drive resident, lives around the corner from the subject property.
34   She preferred single-family zoning and believed that any potential development will become
35   rentals or Airbnbs. She encouraged the Planning Commission to consider the concerns of the
36   residents versus the desires of the developer.
37
38   Mary Ellen Johnson reported that she has resided on Macintosh Lane for 36 years. She found it
39   disheartening that the request has returned for consideration. She expressed concern with twin
40   homes becoming short-term rentals and believed that the lower pricing will not bring in residents.
41   She encouraged the Commission to recommend denial of the request.
42
43   There were no further public comments. The public hearing was closed.

35          3.2     Project ZTA-19-001 – Samantha DeSeelhorst and Andrew Hulka, Overview.
36                  A Public Hearing to Receive Comments and Take Possible Action on a
37                  Proposed Ordinance Update Relating to the Sensitive Lands Evaluation and
38                  Development Standards (“SLEDS”) Ordinance (Section 19.72 of the Zoning
39                  Ordinance.
40
41   Ms. DeSeelhorst presented the Staff Report and stated that the Riparian Guidelines are listed in
42   the existing Sensitive Lands Evaluation and Development Standards (“SLEDS”) Ordinance.
43   Ultimately, the chapter referenced does not exist. The discrepancy was addressed as part of the
44   overall SLEDS update. A visual rendering depicting the regulation process was displayed. The
45   update included information received from the Federal Emergency Management Agency
46   (“FEMA”) and the U.S. Forest Service, both with vast experience in regulating areas around



     Cottonwood Heights Planning Commission Meeting – 06/01/2022                                       6
 1   waterways. The changes recommended were minor in nature and support the direction of the City
 2   ordinance. One clarification of note called out certain pieces of felled trees, defined as course,
 3   woody debris, which should be left in the waterway to benefit the habitat. Threshold expansion
 4   was reviewed. Ms. DeSeelhorst confirmed that the debris is not to be removed unless it poses a
 5   significant hazard to human health or safety.
 6
 7   Commissioner Bevan recognized the ecological aspect of the debris remaining but stated that the
 8   unremoved debris was responsible for flooding homes in St. George that were washed into the
 9   river. He believed there would be increased liability to the City should it be required that the debris
10   remain. He suggested that it be reconsidered. The proposed amended language was discussed.
11   Ms. DeSeelhorst appreciated the concerns expressed and confirmed that Staff will continue to
12   engage in discussions with experts in riparian urban areas.
13
14   Chair Mills supported further discussion and gathering of information and standards from
15   surrounding municipalities.
16
17   Commissioner Wilde stated that when considering incorporating Forest Service regulations by
18   reference, he suggested also considering the same Forest Service whose burn policy resulted in
19   burning half of the northern half of Arizona. Ms. DeSeelhorst remarked that the City is not
20   adopting any reference verbatim but is merely trying to learn based on expertise and the lands they
21   regulate.
22
23   Mr. Hulka reported that the City’s Sensitive Lands Ordinance designates certain areas sensitive to
24   specific geologic or geotechnical hazards. Staff made comprehensive updates to the Ordinance
25   and has received an abundance of feedback. The new maps more accurately depict fault zones
26   while using lidar detection to identify risk. The update was reviewed by the City’s Geology
27   Consultant as well as City Staff who have consulted with representatives of the Utah Geological
28   Society (“UGS”) and local university professors. He stated that a major point of discussion at the
29   previous meeting included whether a 15-foot fault minimum setback was appropriate versus the
30   20-foot setback listed in the current standards. The consensus was to leave the 20-foot setback
31   as-is.
32
33   The Purpose section was updated and condensed while retaining language regarding natural
34   beauty, ecological integrity, access to mountain areas, and natural drainage channels in addition to
35   important purpose statements.
36
37   Staff clarified that the language on the Study Area Maps was updated. The maps allow the City
38   to refer to additional hazard data provided by the State UGS map, several soil classifications, and
39   where other hazards may be present.
40

42   Development Review Committee (“DRC”), and the Director. Staff recommended the Planning
43   Commission determine that the DRC is no longer required. The proposed changes were reviewed.
44   The Architectural Review Committee (“ARC”) will be solely responsible for architectural design
45   requirements. The responsibilities of the Director were described. It was noted that the Director
46   determines what can be counted as a natural slope east of Wasatch Boulevard. Appeals will be

 9   Commissioner Anderson asked for clarification between the definitions of City, Staff, and the

21   definition more narrowly states that it includes departmental representatives. Clean-up items were

 4   Will McCarvill gave his address as 3607 Golden Hills Avenue and stated that revising SLEDS
 5   technical standards to reflect updated knowledge on how best to analyze and evaluate danger is
 6   important to protect lives. He believed that the revision should be continued and commended the
 7   efforts of Staff. Mr. McCarvill explained that the SLEDS ordinance is focused on managing
 8   development on land that is dangerous to build on. The roles and responsibilities should not be
 9   revised as the current process was designed to provide several levels of oversight and are in place
