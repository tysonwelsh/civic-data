Septic Tank Maintenance Ordinance
Council Member Bowen stated the Council needs to study an ordinance that
requires septic tank maintenance.
David Church stated the Council can pass an ordinance that says anyone with a
septic system in the canyon has to maintain it within a predetermined timeframe and then a policy
on how to check on the results.
Mayor Smolka stated he has been working on this for years and thinks several
ordinances will be needed to make it work. First, an ordinance needs to be passed that requires
testing at the point of sale.
Council Member Hook stated he would like Mr. Church to look at what other
municipalities are doing about septic tank maintenance. He would also like to move carefully,
starting out with a more collaborative and educational process.
Mr. Church stated Utah cities do not have any type of policy or ordinance after the
septic tank is installed. The Council should have a process with the water company where the
homeowner needs to show proof of inspection of the septic tank before they can turn the water
on.
Mayor Smolka stated an ordinance will take several months to create something
that is not too heavy handed, and that the Council agrees with.

Council Member Bowen stated Camp Kostopulos built a resident cabin with a
septic system within two miles from their culinary well and a couple of hundred yards from the
stream. If Camp Kostopulos wants to keep its septic tank, it will need to install a holding tank,
which will cost $2,000 - $4,000 a year to pump it. Salt Lake City has taken a position against
Camp Kostopulos’ septic tank installation. They are enforcing a court order or federal regulation
that prohibits septic systems within two miles from Salt Lake City’s culinary well.
David Church stated every culinary well head will have a protection zone that is
approved by the DEQ. Permitted uses are determined based on how far from the well head the
structure is. Salt Lake City has some input to protect its well source.
Council Member Bowen stated these regulations could force the shut down of a
lot of homes. This needs to be a high priority, if Salt Lake City will not meet with the Council then
a legal complaint will need to be filed.
Mayor Smolka stated this issue gets into federal law.
