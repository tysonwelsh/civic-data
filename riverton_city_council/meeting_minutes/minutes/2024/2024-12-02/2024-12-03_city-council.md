       Speed Dips vs. Speed Bumps Discussion

Cary Necaise, Public Works Director, provided an update on the City’s current traffic calming
policy, specifically regarding speed bumps, speed humps, and dips. It was noted that in 2018,
the City Council passed an ordinance prohibiting the installation of speed bumps, humps, and
tables. Since then, staff have declined requests for these features based on the ordinance but
still conduct speed studies in response to complaints. Most studies show that the 85th percentile
speeds—the speed at which 85% of drivers travel—are within acceptable limits, suggesting that
perceptions of speeding often differ from actual behavior.

Mr. Necaise presented a detailed comparison of traffic calming options:

Speed Bumps:

Advantages: Can be placed almost anywhere on the road and constructed using asphalt or
concrete. Concrete is preferred for its durability during future road maintenance.

Disadvantages: Removed during road overlays due to milling, issues with visibility and lighting
at night, and potential for vehicle damage. They also disrupt snow plow operations and are less
favorable for emergency vehicles.

Speed Humps/Dips:

Advantages: Cause less wear on vehicles, better accommodate snow plows and emergency
vehicles, and remain in place during road maintenance. Recent installations on Shaggy Peak
and Lampton roads are under evaluation to measure their effectiveness.

Disadvantages: Require careful placement due to road grading and cost more than speed
bumps. Minor water pooling may occur if deeper dips are constructed.

Mr. Necaise explained that the City has an internal traffic committee, which evaluates traffic
assessments, crash data, and other factors to determine appropriate traffic calming measures.

Councilmember McCay asked about the cost difference. In response, Mr. Necaise explained
that the cost difference between speed dips versus speed bumps largely depends on the
materials used. When comparing asphalt dips to asphalt bumps, the costs are nearly equal.
However, if both are constructed in concrete, speed bumps are approximately $2,000 to $3,000
more expensive than dips due to their larger size (14 feet compared to 4 feet). Regarding the
analysis for installing stop signs, he explained that in the evaluation process, they consider
factors such as speed studies, traffic counts, crash data, proximity to other intersections, and
whether stop signs are necessary for traffic control or safety.

         Off-Leash Dog Park Proposal

Ryan Carter, City Attorney, explained that a resident had requested that Margaret Park be
designated as a dog park, either entirely or during specific hours. The resident's request
stemmed from concerns about their dog being considered "at large" while off-leash in public
areas. He clarified that, under current laws, any off-leash dog in public spaces is considered at
large, even during activities like fetch.

Mr. Carter discussed the logistical and legal considerations of converting Margaret Park into a
dog park. He emphasized the importance of fencing to ensure safety and outlined the minimal
fencing modifications required to enclose the park. However, he noted that creating a
designated dog park area would necessitate regulations to ensure non-dog-owning members of
the public avoid the park during dog park hours for safety reasons. He also reminded the
Council about a previous plan to convert a detention pond near 3600 West and 11800 South
into a dog park, which was ultimately deprioritized in favor of other amenities. He suggested
revisiting this plan as an alternative to modifying Margaret Park. Finally, he recommended
gathering feedback from residents near Margaret Park to assess broader community interest
and potential concerns.

Councilmember Pierucci expressed support for exploring the idea of a dog park in the City,
noting its potential value given the changing dynamics of the community. He emphasized that

Margaret Park need not be the only option and suggested staff identify multiple potential
locations or consider parks with designated off-leash hours.

Councilmember McDougal expressed a preference to table the discussion for now, suggesting
that further exploration of a dog park should occur within the context of the City's broader
strategic planning. However, he supported incorporating a dog park into the design of new
parks in areas with greater housing density, as this could reduce costs and align with strategic
planning.

Councilmember McCay reflected on previous discussions regarding TRCC funding for a dog
park, noting that additional requirements, such as the inclusion of bathrooms, had increased the
costs at the time, leading the City to prioritize another project. She expressed familiarity with
Margaret Park, emphasizing its potential as a cost-effective option for a dog park due to its
existing fencing, which would require only minimal additions. However, she agreed with waiting
to discuss the matter within the context of broader strategic priorities.

Councilmember Haymond suggested that a dog park near the west side or closer to Mountain
View Village might better serve the community.

Kevin Hicks, City Manager, raised concerns about the potential expectations and associated
costs of developing a dog park. He noted that residents who desire a dog park often expect
more than a simple fenced area—they may want restrooms, separate sections for large and
small dogs, and other amenities to enhance usability and safety. He recommended that any
future discussions about a dog park should include clarity about the desired standard and scope
of amenities to ensure alignment with the City’s strategic planning and budget priorities.

Rebekkah Allen, Community Engagement Coordinator for Salt Lake City, addressed the Council

homelessness. The purpose of the count is to locate unsheltered individuals, gather data to
connect them with services, inform policymaking, measure progress toward ending
homelessness, and identify gaps in the local homelessness assistance system. Additionally, the
count helps increase public awareness, attract resources, and create reliable homelessness
estimates for Utah. She encouraged donations of hats, scarves, gloves, and socks for
distribution during the PIT Count.

         Ordinance No. 24-27 – Roberts Rezone, rezone of approximately 0.91-acres
         located at 4191 West 13400 South from R-1 (Residential 1-acre min) to C-R
         (Regional Commercial)

Tim Prestwich, City Planner, presented the Roberts Rezone, following a recommendation for
approval by the Planning Commission in a 7-0 vote. The property in question, currently zoned
R1, is the last remaining parcel in the area that has not been rezoned to regional commercial.
Neighboring properties have already been rezoned, some as far back as 2008, and the area
has been designated for commercial use in the general plan since at least 2001. The rezone
aligns with the surrounding properties and is being pursued as part of a plan involving
developers. The rezone request aims to fit in with the area's zoning, which includes regional
commercial, and the proposed rezone to regional commercial reflects the area’s existing land
use plan.

         Resolution No. 24-101 – Amendments to Fee Schedule

Nick Geer, Administrative Services Director, expressed appreciation for the staff and for Mayor
Staggs’s work in updating the fee schedule. Two key points were emphasized: first, the
inclusion of a false alarm fee, which was already in the code but had not been listed in the fee
schedule, to increase transparency; and second, removal of dog licensing fees due to upcoming
changes in animal control services. Other updates involved consolidating and removing fees
that were either no longer charged or relevant.

Councilmembers and staff discussed changes to water fees, transitioning from a flat-rate
discount to a percentage-based discount for certain groups, such as veterans and seniors. The
main reason for this change was to make the discount structure more transparent and
consistent with the County's existing practices. However, it was acknowledged that this change
could result in a reduced discount for about 50 seniors currently benefiting from a flat-rate
discount. They discussed the need to ensure that those affected by the reduction are informed
about the changes and given the opportunity for hardship considerations. Staff was tasked with
reaching out to the 50 affected residents to communicate the changes and the potential for a
hardship waiver through the County's process.

Additionally, metering for secondary water use was discussed, with the hope that residents will
be more aware of their water consumption and reduce usage, thereby alleviating some strain on
the City's infrastructure.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Public Hearing.

Councilmember McCay MOVED that the City Council approve Resolution No. 24-101 –
Amendments to Fee Schedule. Councilmember Haymond SECONDED the motion. Mayor
Staggs called for discussion on the motion. Mayor Staggs called for a roll-call vote. The vote
was as follows: Haymond-yes, McCay-yes, McDougal-yes and Pierucci-yes. The motion
passed unanimously.


         Resolution No. 24-102 – Budget Amendment

Mr. Geer explained that the proposed budget amendments included several key items:

Carryovers: These involve projects that extend beyond a single fiscal year. If funds allocated for
a project aren't fully spent by the end of the fiscal year, the remaining budget is carried over to
the next year to complete the project. The reason for carryovers is that some projects,
particularly construction-related, can’t always be completed by June 30 due to scheduling
constraints.

New Animal Shelter: The Council is being asked to amend the current year's budget to allocate
an additional $700,000 for the design and construction of a new animal control facility.

Water Van Replacement: A water van was involved in an accident (which wasn't the City's
fault), and the City received an insurance payout. The budget amendment is to cover the cost of
replacing the vehicle, with no additional funding required beyond the insurance payout.

Additional Vehicle Requests: Two vehicles are requested to be purchased early. These vehicles
were planned for the next fiscal year, but due to favorable market conditions, purchasing them

now at a reduced cost is seen as a better option.

Councilmember McDougal expressed strong support for the proposed budget amendments,
acknowledging that many of the amendments are related to carryovers for projects that are
already underway. He emphasized that securing bids early is important to avoid delays and
inflationary cost increases, particularly for the animal control facility, which has a hard deadline
that needs to be met.

Councilmember Pierucci expressed concern about the proposed $36,000 per animal cost for the
new animal shelter, questioning whether the City has thoroughly explored all options. He felt the
decision to move forward was being made under pressure and might be a short-term solution
that does not justify such a significant investment.

Mayor Staggs explained that alternative clinics were explored, but other facilities were not
interested in the model or had similar limitations regarding euthanization and interactions with
the public. He compared this situation to City-run services, like garbage collection, where the
City must step in due to lack of interest from private businesses.

Mayor Staggs also discussed the long-term costs of the shelter, suggesting that while the cost
per animal might seem high, it would be more manageable when considered over the facility's
lifespan. He acknowledged that, despite his reluctance to spend money on such a service, the
shelter would provide better control and care for animals, especially in cases where animals
were aggressive or violating ordinances.

Councilmember McDougal emphasized that the animal shelter issue was primarily a public
safety concern. He acknowledged the high cost but argued that the City's responsibility to
ensure the safety of residents outweighed the expense. He further highlighted the rising
construction costs and suggested that delaying the project would only result in higher expenses
in the future.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Public Hearing.
