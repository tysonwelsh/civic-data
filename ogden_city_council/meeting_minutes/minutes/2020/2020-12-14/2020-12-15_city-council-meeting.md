     Council Business
               Report on Lantern House Homeless Encampment Cleanup: Mayor Caldwell stated that he has sent out a great deal of
     infonnation about this event and he is happy to answer any questions about the matter. This event was not handled any differently
     than similar cleanup events in the past in terms of notification for businesses, property owners, and those staying at the
     encampment. However, in this instance, the director of the homeless shelter was extremely grateful to the City for the effort. The
     homeless shelter is currently at about 50 percent of its maximum capacity due to fear about the safety of the environment due to
     COVID-19. This resulted in many homeless individuals deciding to camp on the prope1ty outside of the shelter which has created
     great concern for businesses and residents living in that area. The City received many phone calls from people who were
     concerned about the lack ofresponse from the Ogden Police Department (OPD) and Administration. OPD located a camera near
     the encampment and captured evidence of assaults and other problematic behavior that should not be acceptable in the
     community. They decided to proceed with breaking up the encampment to increase safety for the homeless individuals as well as
     others living or operating a business in the area. The City is interested in creating a safe environment and all people who stood to
     be impacted by the cleanup efforts were adequately notified. The purpose of the Lantern House facility is to provide resources to
     those in compromised positions and this cleanup event resulted in people seeking those resources and staying at the facility .
./             Chair Choberka stated that those who reached out to her about this issue wondered about the timing of the cleanup event.
     They also asked about the City's relationship with the Weber-Morgan Health Department and their involvement in the event.
     Mayor Caldwell addressed the timing of the event and noted the City has been actively cleaning the area for the last 18 months.
     Crews were sent to the area three times per week to clean the area, but that was reduced to once a week after the onset of the

COVID-19 pandemic. The cleanup is not a new thing, but what was new is the increase in illegal activities in the area due to the
growth of the encampment. The City's stance in response to lawlessness has not changed and OPD had no choice but to respond
and deter that type of activity.
            Chair Choberka restated her question about the City's relationship with the Health Department. She noted she has heard
the City's response to the encampment was due to a recommendation from the Health Department. Police Chief Watt stated the
Health Deparhnent did contact OPD after they had received complaints about the situation in the area and perfonned their own
inspection. Based upon that inspection, they recommended that the City locate portable toilets near the encampment and increase
effmis to clean the area. The City contacted the Lantern House and was told that they were allowing homeless individuals to use
their restroom facilities under controlled circumstances, but many people were choosing not to utilize that service and were
choosing to, instead, use the land outside the Lantern House. Additionally, there has been an increase in trespassing, assault,
robberies, and vandalism at private properties around the Lantern House. OPD was trying to take a 'hands off approach relative
to the homeless population in the area, but that ultimately resulted in an increase in illegal activity. When the City visited the
encampment, it did so jointly with representatives of other service providers. A 48-hour notification was given to those living in
the encampment that they would need to move from the area. Some accepted the offer of support and service from the City, but
many rejected it and moved on from the area. The cleanup of the site was extensive, and he feels the steps the City took were in
the best interest of the entire community. Chief Administrative Officer Johnson added that the crews who were in the area
performing the work were personally thanked by many residents and business owners as they have been suffering because of the
increase in illegal activity in the area.
            Chair Choberka asked that City Administration provide the Council with information regarding the policies and
ordinances that were followed to carry out this cleanup effort. Chief Watt stated that a measured approach is employed before the
City takes any action that relates to an individual's constitutional rights. OPD has a homeless advocate who tries to contact each
individual in the area to offer services available, but he reiterated the most recent cleanup was not a new occurrence in the area.
During the three days leading up to the sweep, officers and the homeless advocate were at the location talking to individuals and
offering services. There were approximately 70 to 80 individuals living in the encampment and 3 0 of them chose to move into the
Lantern House. The rest chose not to utilize that resource and have moved on to other areas. The City continues to locate and
monitor them as well as offering them 'walk kits' to give them support during the time they are living on the streets.
            Council member Hyer asked if any personal property was seized during the cleanup. Chief Watt answered no. He said the
City only cleaned up and removed those items that were left behind. Individuals were given sufficient time to pack up their items
and take them with them and only those items left on the site after everyone had dispersed were removed. Mayor Caldwell added
that is the same practice the City has employed in all past sweeps of the property.
            Council member Lopez stated that he understands the City cannot force people to accept or seek help but asked if there
are any new programs on the horizon that could be offered to those who refuse the services currently available to them. Chief Watt
replied that the City's homeless advocate works closely with Weber Human Services to identify those individuals who are
suffering from severe mental health conditions and for which intervention is appropriate. Additionally, many homeless individuals
have addiction issues and sometimes the best fonn of intervention is legal action because the individuals are forced to stop their
substance abuse and seek help. Consistency is key in terms of providing support. If a homeless individual is offered assistance
repeatedly, many eventually accept that help and change their situation. This problem is not unique to Ogden and OPD is regularly
monitoring programs that have been employed in other areas and achieved success.
            Council member White asked if homeless individuals can legally camp within a public right-of-way. Chief Watt
answered no and indicated that state law regulates roadways and park sh·ips. The City has been somewhat lenient in enforcing this
ordinance in the past, but it is illegal for any person to camp on public property, which includes roadsides. Mayor Caldwell
indicated Ogden City Code Section 7-2-15 specifies "it is unlawful for any person to camp, lodge, sleep, cook, make a frre or pitch
a tent. .. or any other type of ground cover or shelter, or place sleeping bags, bedding or any other type of camping or sleeping
equipment upon any portion of a public way, as defined herein, and it is unlawful for any person using or benefiting from the use
of any of the foregoing shelter or camping or sleeping equipment to fail to remove the same from any such street for more than
five (5) minutes after being requested to do so by any police officer or citizen." The City has avoided being punitive in enforcing
this ordinance, but in this instance, it was necessary for the City to respond in order to restore a feeling of safety to the
neighborhood surrounding the Lantern House.
            There was general discussion centering on resources available from state government to address homelessness issues
occurring in the community and to fund services and support that is offered to homeless individuals. Mayor Caldwell noted that
the Lantern House relies heavily upon community support and private donations and he encouraged the Council to get the word
out to their constituents about how important that support really is.
            Council member White asked if the City reviewed the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) guidelines for
encampments. She noted that CDC guidelines suggest that encampments be left intact and asked if those guidelines were weighed
in consideration of the actions that were ultimately taken. Chief Watt replied that the City is aware of those guidelines but noted
that the environment was so unhealthy and that was considered to outweigh the intent of the CDC guidelines to leave an
encampment in place. He stated Administration ultimately decided that it was in the best interest of the entire community,
including those living in the encampment, to break it up. He noted there were over 70 people living in very close quaiiers, starting

    Fiscal Year 2021 Budget Amendment- 26 th Street Reconstruction
              Ms. Stout explained City Administration has proposed a budget amendment to allocate $4,886,600 of funding, largely
    from WACOG. This funding is for 26 th Street reconstruction and improvements from Wall Avenue to Washington Boulevard,
    along with improvements north of 26 th Street on Grant Avenue. She noted that City Engineering has estimated they will need to
    allocate an additional $835,000 to fund the entire project. This action would result in a net adjustment of the FY2021 Ogden City
    budget by $5,723,550.
              Mr. Anderson briefly reviewed the project brief for CIP project EN097. The project is to reconstrnct 26 th Street from
    Wall A venue to east of Washington Boulevard including improvements north of26t11 on Grant Ave. The road needs repair and this
    will allow for increased use. The project will also reconstruct the pavement section to be able to handle the traffic the streets
    currently experience including the bus related traffic. Improvements also include sidewalk and landscape with the addition of bike
    lanes, street lighting, and stonn drain facilities. The City approached WACOG for funding in the amount of $4,886,600 in
    FY2016. A request for $62,600 in corridor preservation funds was fully funded and programmed for the year FY2017. A request
    for $4,824,000 in sales tax (constrnction) funds was funded at 25%, due to limited sales tax funds, and the project was therefore
    awarded $1,206,000 in sales tax funds and programmed for the year 2021. WACOG has funded up to $3,987,050 for construction
    related costs for the year 2021. The utility projects for this item have been included within their respective CIP master plan list of
    WU015 and SA009. The Water Utility cost will be $350,000 and the Sewer Utility cost will be $783,000.
              General discussion among the Council and Mr. Anderson centered on the relationship between this project and other
    future redevelopment or improvement projects planned for the immediate area.
              Ms. Mabey indicated that a public hearing and potential action on the proposed budget amendment can be scheduled for
    January 19, 2021 ifthere are no objections from the Council. No objections were voiced.

    Ogden Police Department License Plate Reader
              The OPD has requested an oppmtunity to review with the Council its use oflicense plate reader (LPR) technology and
    current considerations to expand use of this technology. OPD has used LPR technology since 2008, beginning with a mobile LPR
    from a police vehicle. The technology has since grown, and its use has expanded in communities of various sizes throughout the
)   United States. OPD has researched technological advances and vendors over the past two years and identified a prefeffed vendor
    to implement a fixed LPR system for Ogden.
              Deputy Police Chief Young made a presentation to provide additional information regarding LPR technology and the
    benefits the technology can provide to a department. LPRs are a great crime fighting tool in individual neighborhoods and

communities as a whole. OPD issued a request for proposals for an LPR program and awarded the contract to Flock Safety.
Initially 20 LPR cameras will be installed inside the Project Safe Neighborhoods Zone using funding from Federal Project Safe
Neighborhoods (PSN) and Justice Assistance Grant (JAG) programs. This funding is ongoing for four-years. OPD is testing
another project that will allow them to add LPR technology to existing cameras. The Depaiiment will utilize LPR technology for
the following:
              • Amber ale1is
              •    Stolen vehicle alerts
              • Wanted fugitive and person ale1is
              • Hit and run accident investigations
              • Fraud and forgery investigations
              • Theft investigations
              • Investigation leads on all types of crimes
              • Traffic studies
          LPR will not be utilized in relation to a private person's residence or non-criminal investigations. OPD has worked with
the City Attorney's Office to understand any legal considerations related to use ofLPR. Data collected by the LPR is a protected
record under the State of Utah Govermnent Records Access and Management Act (GRAMA). Data may not be used or shared
other than for lawful purposes of LPR data. Data may not be preserved more than nine months without a preservation request,
disclosure order or criminal warrant. Data may be used in the aggregate for planning and statistical purposes if specific plate data
is not preserved or disclosed. OPD Policy number 65 ensures compliance with Utah State Law and the area Tactical Analysis
Center will manage all access and distribution ofLPR data. Data will only be used to improve public safety, conduct criminal
investigations, ensure compliance with local, state, and federal laws, and requests for data must be approved at two levels. There
will be an annual audit ofLPR data and usage to ensure compliance with the State Law and Policy. LPR is used nationwide and
LPRs are already in use in Utah, with a mobile solution in Ogden since 2009. He concluded his presentation by relaying success
stories associated with the use ofLPR devices and technology.
          Discussion among the Council and Deputy Chief Young centered on opportunities for criminals to subvert LPR
technology, the mobility ofLPR cameras, any requirement to publicly advertise the usage ofLPR technology, costs associated
with long-tenn storage ofLPR data, and the potential need to increase staffing levels in association with the introduction ofLPR
technology in the OPD.
