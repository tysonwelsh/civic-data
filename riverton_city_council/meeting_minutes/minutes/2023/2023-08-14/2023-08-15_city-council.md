       Emergency Management Committee Presentation

Jason Jones, Emergency Manager, explained that the Emergency Management Committee had
planned to participate in the public safety night similar to the previous year's, but unfavorable
weather conditions caused this event to be cancelled. They are now planning to be involved in
the block parties organized by Crimewatch to educate residents about emergency Management.

Mr. Jones said the committee has been meeting monthly on the third Thursday of each month at
7 pm in City Hall. Attendance was consistent, and the participants were energetic and
enthusiastic.

Mr. Jones said that in the spring he made preparations for potential flooding including
collaborating with Public Works and having drone assessments of dry creek beds. Proactive
measures, like distributing fliers to flood-prone areas, were taken with the help of the Riverton
Youth Council. Mr. Jones praised the communications department's effective dissemination of

Mr. Jones said that he had procured first aid, CPR, and AED training equipment for Riverton
employees and made sure the City adopted an Emergency Operations Plan. He discussed the
use of ham radios during the Riverton half marathon, noting their effectiveness without
encountering any problems. He said that he and Aubrey Pollino, Risk Manager, had made

Councilmember McCay also asked about CERT training. Mr. Jones said that there was not
much response to the in person training. While the goal was to have 30 people sign up, only

Discussion Items

         Town Days Discussion

Sheril Garn, Events and Operations Director, presented an overview of the recent Town Days
event. She expressed gratitude to her team for successfully executing Town Days despite
challenges such as employee absences due to maternity leave and family medical situations.

Ms. Garn said there are 23 different events associated with Town Days, with 14 of them being
free of charge. She further highlighted the high attendance levels across various events,
showcasing the significant community engagement. Specific events, such as the rodeo, car
show, parade, miniature golf, and various games and activities, were discussed, along with
estimated attendance figures. Notably, the parade's logistical challenges were mentioned,
emphasizing the need for careful planning and coordination.

Ms. Garn also mentioned sponsorships, volunteer contributions, and various aspects of the
event's management, such as flag ceremonies, workouts, and breakfast. The impressive
number of participants in activities like the country mile run and various games illustrated the
community's enthusiasm.

Ms. Garn concluded by acknowledging the City's role in making Town Days an accessible and
enjoyable experience for residents regardless of their budget.

Josh Lee, Communications Director, presented a summary of the survey results related to Town
Days. He said the survey was a success, with a positive response rate. He discussed adding a
third date for the rodeo, with roughly 50% of respondents expressing support, 20% opposing the
idea, and approximately 30% remaining indifferent. He said the overall sentiment conveyed by
the survey results was that the community had a positive outlook on Town Days.

Mayor Staggs said he thought there was some support for adding a third day to the rodeo. Ms.
Garn said she had discussions with Circle J Rodeo, who expressed their willingness to
accommodate a third day. Circle J Rodeo believed they could provide the necessary cowboys
for the extended event.

Ms. Garn conveyed that the cost increase to the City for this third day would not be a full third of
the overall rodeo cost. While there would be an added expense for an extra day of hosting the
rodeo stock, the overall payout for the event would remain consistent.

Ms. Garn conveyed the significance of the carnival and its reputation as one of the best in the
western United States. She said the carnival is highly sought after and in high demand. She
emphasized the challenges of securing the same carnival company in subsequent years if a
break is taken from hosting it. She shared her personal trust in the carnival company which was
based on positive experiences and interactions with the company.

Councilmember McDougal emphasized the importance of taking into account the well-being and
capacity of the City's staff when deciding to add additional events to Town Days. He expressed
gratitude for the efforts made thus far and encouraged a balanced approach that takes into
consideration the capacity of the City's dedicated employees.

Councilmember Buroker asked if they had considered doing the carnival the same week as the
rodeo. In response, Ms. Garn said changing the date of the carnival would be contingent on the
carnival company’s availability for the event. However, she explained that the carnival typically
requires a substantial amount of parking space, which could pose a problem considering the
needs of the rodeo attendees.

Councilmember Wells shared his thoughts on potentially relocating the Carnival to a different
park or location. He conveyed the sense of excitement that the Carnival brings, even for those
who may not participate in the rides themselves. He expressed appreciation for the inclusive
and dynamic environment created by having everything in one place.

Mayor Staggs shared his perspective on the Carnival and its role in the Town Days event. He
mentioned that there had been a previous experiment with moving the Carnival away from its
current location, and he was a strong advocate for bringing it back to its central spot. He
mentioned security and safety as areas of concern surrounding the carnival. He conveyed his
intention to address security concerns in next year's budget by allocating additional resources
for police presence and overtime.

Councilmember McCay proposed the idea of moving the Carnival to either the weekend before
or after the main Town Days event. She referred to feedback from the survey, where many
respondents expressed their enjoyment of the Carnival and urged the City not to eliminate it.
By scheduling the Carnival on a different weekend but still within the same general timeframe,
the City could potentially manage crowd sizes more effectively and ease parking challenges.

Ms. Garn responded by saying that the Town Days rodeo is currently aligned with the Browns'
schedule for the Fourth of July. It was noted that if the rodeo were to be moved to the weekend
before Town Days, it would require using the same grass area for two consecutive weeks,
which could impact the grass's recovery time. She expressed willingness to discuss the matter
with the Browns and explore the feasibility of such a change. She said that ultimately the
decision about the rodeo schedule was deferred to the councilmembers.

         Riverton Police Services and Recruitment Discussion

Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, introduced a proposal related to enhancing
employee benefits and engagement. He noted that Chief Barfuss’s team had taken the lead in
formulating the plan. Mr. Hicks explained that the proposal consisted of two main components.
The first component focused on employee benefits and retention. Recently, a new trend had
emerged among other agencies, where they allowed employees to retain their years of service
from various agencies when calculating vacation or leave time. This approach aimed to motivate
employees and attract them to the agency. The proposal recommended that the City adopt a
similar approach by allowing both police personnel and civilian staff to use their full years of
service from all URS governmental entities when calculating leave time. For law enforcement
personnel, it was specified that only their law enforcement experience would be considered.

Mr. Hicks emphasized that this proposal did not entail changes in the leave levels or amounts
offered by the City, but rather a modification to the way years of service were calculated. If the
Council approved, an amendment to the policy manual would be prepared to include this
provision.

Councilmembers and Mayor Staggs asked clarifying questions about the proposed policy
change.

Ryan Carter, Acting City Manager, expressed his cautious optimism about the proposal and its
potential impact on recruitment efforts. He conveyed that while he believed in the intention of
the staff to enhance the City's market position, he acknowledged the possibility of external
factors, such as economic changes, impacting the situation in the future. He acknowledged the
importance of maintaining a competitive edge in terms of attracting talent and pointed out the
dynamic nature of the job market.

City Manager Report

Mr. Carter shared several updates during the meeting:

Feeding Wildlife Ordinance: He informed the Council about receiving complaints from the
community regarding residents feeding wildlife, particularly birds, in a way that could attract
pests and rodents. He mentioned that a zoning code ordinance is being drafted to address this
issue by regulating how animals like birds can be fed, with a focus on above-ground feeders in
the backyard. A more detailed presentation will be provided once the ordinance has been
through the planning commission.

Response to Landslide Incident: He expressed gratitude to the Unified Fire Authority and the
Public Works Department, led by Councilmember Buroker, for their swift response to an incident
involving a landslide on Tithing Hill. He acknowledged the coordinated efforts to manage the
situation and praised the response teams' efficiency.

All Abilities Park Update: He provided an update on the progress of the All Abilities Park project
in Riverton City. He mentioned that a purchased park amenity designed to accommodate
children with mobility issues might cause a delay due to concerns about its potential impact on
individuals with other disabilities. He emphasized the City's commitment to creating an inclusive
environment and noted that further study is needed to ensure the park is truly accessible to
everyone.

Mr. Carter also introduced Mr. Craig Giles as the next city manager for Riverton City. He
highlighted Mr. Giles' extensive experience in public service and expressed excitement about
his upcoming role in Riverton City.

Riverton Police Department Presentation – Chief Blair Barfuss

Chief Barfuss explained the comparisons of staffing changes over previous years, with a shift in
rank titles to deputy chiefs and organizational restructuring to align with responsibilities. The
incorporation of animal services and code enforcement into the police department was
highlighted. Graphs depicting a five-year trend in calls for service demonstrated a consistent
rise followed by a significant reduction in December 2022. It was noted that proactive calls,
including traffic stops and citizen encounters, are also included in the calls for service statistics.

Chief Barfuss said the data indicated a pattern of increased calls for service in the first year,
followed by either stabilization or decrease in subsequent years. Similar trends were observed
in reported offenses. It was suggested that the initial year of the department's formation saw
heightened proactive policing, but concerns arising in 2020 regarding police perception,
proactive policing, and government support led to a decline in officers' proactive efforts.

Chief Barfuss highlighted several achievements, including the establishment of core values
("Riverton PD pride"), departmental restructuring for clarity, the release of law enforcement
policy manuals, and the reinstatement of the motors unit to address traffic enforcement needs.
A peer support group and a chaplain program were introduced, and efforts to enhance the major
accident and negotiations teams were undertaken.

Chief Barfuss discussed future departmental goals, with a primary focus on achieving DEP
accreditation through the Utah Chiefs of Police Association, potentially within the next few
months. Community policing efforts were to be intensified through district assignments of
officers to councilmembers, addressing specific community concerns. The evaluation of staffing,
improved officer recruitment and development, conducting salary surveys, and transparency in
communication were emphasized. Looking ahead, areas of high risk management were
identified, including the implementation of professional standards review boards for
transparency and addressing complaints. The resumption of animal control services and
proactive code enforcement with community resolutions were also highlighted.
Councilmember Wells asked if the police department tracks reportable accidents within the City
each year. In response, Chief Barfuss explained that data tracking and analysis are integral to
their operations. He said the police department monitors daily activities, including the specific
type and duration of each officer's involvement in calls. Additionally, he said they closely heed
citizen concerns and complaints, particularly regarding matters like crosswalks near school
zones.
