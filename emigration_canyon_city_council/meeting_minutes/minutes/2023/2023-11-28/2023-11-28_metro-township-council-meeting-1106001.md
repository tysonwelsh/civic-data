WHEREAS, Section 3.42.040(A)(3) states that “[u]pon review and approval of fees by the

Restroom Budget
David Richardson, Capitol Hill Construction, stated the project has some cost
overruns. The biggest change that was encountered was the slope behind the outhouse. The
original plan was to excavate the dirt going back a few feet and creating a concrete retaining wall.
What actually happened was the excavator ended up chasing the hillside. There were choices
made to make the project fit in the environment and stabilize the slope at the same time which
created a lot of unanticipated work. A few things that the Council requested also increased the
costs. There were cost overruns in other areas due to the increased time. Because the project
took longer than originally anticipated there were added labor costs.
There was a good contingency in the budget; however, it is still a bit over, largely because of
excavation costs that happened early on. The township still owes $14,000 and the overrun costs
are about $19,000. He is asking for $33,000 in payment. The true additional costs were closer to
$40,000.
Mayor Smolka stated he is concerned about the excavation and the way it started
and ended. There are 12 feet of retaining wall now and it extends a long way to the left. It does
not appear to be much of a reason to go that far over.
Mr. Richardson stated the excavator went back that far from the building to allow
space for the building to breathe. The dirt was removed and hauled off. The structure was
gradually built up and will remain for many years.
Council Member Harris asked if the $19,000 overage was because of the
excavation and retaining walls and if the wall was the first thing done in the project.
Mr. Richardson stated the overage was $25,000; however, other areas had
savings. The excavation was early on in the project, and it was assumed that the costs could be
absorbed in the contingency. As the project moved forward, there were other issues that came
up. The cider shingles quadrupled and there was a little bit extra on the mason costs.
Council Member Harris asked if Mr. Richardson considered coming to the
Council to update it on the costs. These costs come as a surprise.
Mr. Richardson stated he thought the costs could be absorbed.
Council Member Brems asked what the reason for the extended time was.

Mr. Richarson stated a lot of the delay was scheduling subcontractors then winter
weather was a problem.
Deputy Mayor Hawkes asked if the footing was part of the original bid.
Mr. Richardson stated no, it was not in the original drawings.
Council Member Pinon stated this project was a design-build. When the project
was awarded all the design components would be the responsibility of the team that did the work.
He met with Mr. Richardson and reviewed why the retaining wall incurred extra costs. They looked
at the accounting behind the scenes of how the project was executed. He does not like to see a
company go into the red, even though it was responsible for the design. These were unforeseen
conditions that caused a challenge to the budgets that were presented. The Council needs to find
a way to work with the firm to resolve the contract and maintain a future relationship.
Council Member Brems asked what the budget for the retaining wall was.
Mr. Richardson stated $14,300 was budgeted for excavation and $8,750 for the
retaining wall but almost $40,000 was spent.
Council Member Harris stated there was a 20 percent contingency, but it was
used and then some. She thinks that the firm bears some of the responsibility. Over the last two
years, the Council has never been informed of the costs. She proposes that the firm takes 20
percent off of what it is asking from the Council.
Council Member Brems stated he thinks that is fair.
Polly McLean stated the Council has a few options; it can amend the budget to
allow for the extra amount to be paid. The Council can refuse to pay the extra amount and pay
the contracted amount, or it can do something in between.
Mayor Smolka stated he thinks 20 percent is a good option. He asked Mr.
Richardson what he was prepared to offer the Council.
Mr. Richardson stated he can justify 20 percent less to his partners. That would
put the overage costs at about $15,000.
Mayor Smolka, seconded by Council Member Harris, moved to settle the contract
at the additional cost of $15,000 above the contracted amount. The motion passed unanimously
with a roll call vote showing Council Member Harris voting “Aye,” Council Member Brems voting
“Aye,” Council Member Pinon voting “Aye,” Deputy Mayor Hawkes voting “Aye,” and Mayor
Smolka voting “Aye.”
