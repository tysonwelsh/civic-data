Reports from other Municipal Service Providers

Unified Police Department (UPD)

                 Chief Brent Atkinson stated last week Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service
Area approved a new officer allocation for Magna. In addition, he would like to recognize some
UPD officers for the outstanding work they do. One case in particular embodies what these fine
men and women do on a daily basis that goes unnoticed. On Sunday, February 3, UPD patrol
officers were dispatched to a report of a domestic situation. A male individual called UPD and
said his female roommate was fighting with her boyfriend the night before. The male left the
residence and when he returned, his roommate was gone. When UPD officers arrived, they
determined the scene was suspicious and believed the female did not leave on her own free will.
The officers started good old-fashioned police work and were able to track down the address that
the suspect was at. Officers made contact with the suspect which turned into a violent situation.
It took a lot of effort to subdue the suspect. After the suspect was in custody, officers continued
to investigate and found the female in the basement. Looking at similar cases, the officers likely
saved the female’s life. It was a joint effort with patrol officers, a victim advocate, precinct
detectives, and violent crime detectives. The following individuals were recognized:

       Officer Madison Mahoskey
       Officer Taylor Santoro
       Officer Sean Williams
       Sergeant Levi Hughes

Unified Fire Authority (UFA)

                 Council Member Prokopis stated UFA promoted Jay Torgersen as the new
division Chief Officer over the medical division. Mr. Torgersen has 22 years’ experience and 15
years’ as a flight medic. In 1992 the wildland fire fighting team was created with 12 employees
and an old retired school bus painted red for transportation. This summer as many as 80
individuals will be hired for the wildland fire fighting team. This is a valuable resource to UFA and
the entire west, it is a regional asset. Half of the wildland fire fighting team is gone for the summer
months and the other half is stationed here to protect lands locally. For the first time in history
UFA has hired an outside person, Lyle Jennings, to manage the wildland division. Mr. Jennings
has 18 years with the Forest Service, Bureau of Land Management (BLM), and wildland fire
fighting team management experience. Mr. Jennings was sworn in a couple of months ago and
is getting the team ready for the summer. UFA hired 27 new recruits that are in the middle of the
16 weeks of training. UFA is working on trying to improve the public safety pension plan that was
severely cut. Vacancy’s are hard to fill, and UFA is looking to find new ways to attract quality
applicants. The barn fire on the White property was human caused; possibly from people
squatting in the barn.

Salt Lake County Planning and Development Services Division

                Council Member Prokopis stated he received a call with concerns about the Salt
Lake County Planning and Development Services Division. The individual had a simple ½ acre
lot that he wanted to subdivide into ¼ acre lots. He went to Salt Lake County Planning and
Development Services Division and an employee at the counter provided him with a ridiculous list
of expectation, charged him over $3,000, and gave him a timeline of four to six months. The
same individual did the same thing in Salt Lake City proper and he said Salt Lake County charged
him three times what Salt Lake City charged. In Salt Lake City he spent about a ½ hour in the
office and received a list of expectations. Everything else was handled by email in six weeks.
Unless issues like this are fixed this person will not be back in Magna. A simple subdivide should
not take four to six months, it seems excessive. Magna will lose developers.

              Council Member Peel stated this is not the only time this issue has been brought
up, something needs to be done and quickly.

                Max Johnson, Planner, Salt Lake County Planning and Development Services
Division, stated he will follow-up and report back.

2019 Consolidated Fee Schedule

The Council reviewed the following Resolution amending the 2019 consolidated fee schedule to
include fees for special events and park rentals:

RESOLUTION NO. 19-02-01                                                 Date: February 26, 2019

       A RESOLUTION OF THE MAGNA METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL AMENDING
       THE 2019 CONSOLIDATED FEE SCHEDULE TO INCLUDE FEES FOR
       SPECIAL EVENTS AND PARK RENTALS

                                           RECITALS

   A. The Magna Metro Township (“Magna”) is a municipality pursuant to Utah Code §§ 10-2a-
      401 et seq.

   B. The Magna Metro Township Council (“Council”) is the municipal legislative body for Magna
      pursuant to Utah Code § 10-3b-501.

   C. On December 18, 2018 as a part of the 2019 budget approval process, the Council
      approved the 2019 Magna Consolidated Fee Schedule (the “Fee Schedule”).

                                                4
DATE           TUESDAY                             FEBRUARY                             26, 2019


   D. In a review of the fee schedule in late January, it was found the fees for park rentals and
      special events had not been posted to the fee schedule that had been adopted.

   E. The attached exhibit (Exhibit A) reflects the special events and park rental fees proposed
      for addition to the fee schedule.

   F. Both fees allow Magna to offset some of the regulatory and use impact costs of special
      events and certain park activities.

                                        RESOLUTION

        THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED, the Council hereby approves the proposed fees for
special events and park rental, hereinafter known as Exhibit A, to the 2019 Magna Consolidated
Fee schedule; and be it further;

       RESOLVED, the effective date for these fees be as of January 1, 2019.

     APPROVED AND ADOPTED in Magna, Salt Lake County, Utah this 26th day of
