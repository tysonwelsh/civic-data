Amemo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed Union Station Campus
Master Plan Development Framework came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated as a follow-up to the MAKE
\ Ogden Plan and the downtown MAKE Ogden zoning ordinance rewrite and rezones, the Administration is proposing the Council
» adopt the Ogden Union Station Campus Master Plan Development Framework (Union Station Framework Plan) to help guide
development of the Union Station campus. As part of the MAKE Ogden downtown zoning ordinances adopted in 2023, language
in the zoning code allowed more strict zoning regulations for an area of the downtown ifthe area had an adopted master plan. The
Union Station Framework Plan would allow more localized and specific standards to be applied to development of the Union
Station campus beyond the base standards in the zoning ordinance.
Senior Planner Rypien summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the Union
Station Framework Plan and Campus Master Plan; he presented several images to orient the Council to the boundary of the
Framework Plan area, which is broken into three main areas: south parcel of 4.2 acres, Union Station campus of 8.4 acres, and
north parcel of 17 acres, for a total of 29.6 acres. The Ogden Union Station Campus Master Plan is built upon four pillars that are
crucial to the future integration of the campus into the larger community. Each of these pillars has been considered as equal to and :
supportive of the others as this plan has been developed. The pillars include:
e Economic Sustainability ;
o The future of the Ogden Union Station campus must contribute to the long-term economic vitality of
Ogden through a curated collection of employers, residents, entertainment and goods and services.
e Transportation
o The Ogden Union Station campus will continue to position itself as a multi-modal hub of
transportation and transit services for the region.
e = History and Culture
© The Ogden Union Station campus will celebrate the rich and storied history that has provided the
foundation for the Ogden of today and the future.
e =©. Education
o The Ogden Union Station campus will integrate opportunities for life-long learning through
partnerships with educational institutions and the local creative maker class.
Mr. Rypian noted development of the Union Station Campus fulfills the vision for Episode One from Make Ogden- to
reinforce Ogden’s beloved civic attributes and reignite investment in the downtown core. He indicated Planning staff would like
_ the Council to consider the four following factors:
} e Implementing Make Ogden - The Union Station Framework implements the Make Ogden — Downtown Ogden
: Master Plan.
e Governed by the Master Plan - The recently approved Make Ogden Downtown Implementation Ordinance
references the Union Station Framework Plan and states the area should be governed by the master plan.
a
* Protection of the Union Station and Laundry Building— The Framework Plan encourages public and private
reinvestment to existing buildings and improvements to the surrounding properties. f )
* Revitalizing Downtown — The reinvestment in Union Station and the properties north and south of the Union
Station will help revitalize downtown and be a catalyst to reinvestment in other surrounding areas.

Administration recommends that the City Council adopt the proposed Ogden Union Station Campus Master Plan
Development Framework based on it implementing the goals and objectives of the Downtown Ogden Master Plan.

Discussion between the Council and Mr. Rypien centered on the connections between the Union Station Campus and 25"
Street, as well as surrounding areas of downtown Ogden; and the timing of the phasing or each ‘episode’ of the MAKE Ogden
Plan.

Chair Choberka stated the Council has heard from residents who are concerned that the City could potentially be ‘giving’
the Union Station campus to private developers for their use and redevelopment and that this transaction could occur without
citizen input. She asked Mr. Rypien if that is the case. Mr. Rypien stated that is not the case; he has read the entire Framework
Plan and there is no mention of ‘giving’ any portion of the campus to a developer for private use. It is his understanding that the
intent of this Plan is to improve Union Station and the surrounding area for public purposes.

Chair Choberka then called for public input regarding the proposed Union Station Campus Master Plan Development

Proposed Zone Text Amendment— Use and Development Standards in M-1
and M-2 Zones
Planning Manager Brierley explained Administration is proposing several amendments to the allowable uses and
development standards of certain uses in the M-1 and M-2 zones. The amendments would do the following:
e Prohibit new junk and salvage yards, rebuildable car lots, recycling collection centers, and limit the area
vehicles could be stored in new auto repair shops.
e Limit new entertainment and fitness facilities, except motor vehicle related uses.
e Allow manufactured home sales displays and RV storage lots with offices.
e Limit new personal storage facilities.
¢ Modify setbacks, height, and lot coverage standards.
e Establish design standards for new industrial buildings.
3 e Establish landscape buffer standards between industrial and residential uses.

i The proposal focuses almost entirely on the M-1 (Limited Manufacturing) and M-2 (Manufacturing and Industrial) zones
and would not generally be applicable to the City’s other manufacturing zones, which include the MRD and DDR zones at BDO,
the OCIP zone in west Ogden, or the BEI and BEH zones within the Trackline Economic Development Project Area in west
Ogden. Administration posted a notice of pending ordinance on April 20, 2023 which limited the new applications for most
nn ______________
permitted and conditional uses in the M-1 and M-2 zones, prohibited new junk and salvage yards in the M-2 zone and prohibited
new recycling and collection centers and towing and impound lots in the M-1 and M-2 zones. The pending ordinance restrictions | }
expire on October 16, 2023.
Mr. Brierley used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to identify the locations of existing M-1 and M-2 zoned properties
in the community and discussed the benefits of the zones; he also provided a comparison of property tax value of uses currently
allowed in the M-1 and M-2 zones as well as the typical number of employees per acre of the properties. Junk or salvage yards
have a low taxable value and limited employment but have significant environmental impacts. The amendments would prohibit
any new salvage yards but will allow the existing salvage yards to remain. Additionally, new personal storage facilities would also
no longer be allowed in the M-1 and M-2 zones; existing facilities can remain and can also be expanded in a minor fashion, or if
population growth creates an increased demand for the use. He then discussed customer service uses, including restaurants, live
adult entertainment, drinking establishments, retail sales use, building material stores, churches, service stations, and athletic
clubs, noting the proposal is to limit recreation facilities to motor related, prohibit athletic clubs, and gyms, and prohibit adult live
entertainment in the M-2 zone. Additionally, changes are being proposed to development standards, including:
e Remove impervious coverage standard. ,
e Eliminate rear yard setbacks in the M-1 zone.
e Maximum height in M-2 zone = 90 feet.
e Buffers.
e Building durability/materials/architecture.
e Landscaping.
The Council and Mr. Brierley engaged in discussion regarding the most likely uses of properties in the M-1 and M-2
zones and the Council provided feedback expressing concern about placing limitations on property owners relative to the types of
users they can lease their property to. This led to discussion of the appropriate role of the City in regulating these types of matters
in the community and the Council suggested that the broad proposal be broken into smaller pieces and for the City to solicit input
from other stakeholders before proceeding with any significant changes.
Chair Choberka concluded there does not appear to be enough support for the proposed changes to move the item
forward to a meeting; however, she acknowledged the notice of pending ordinance will expire in the middle of October.
Community and Economic Development Director Cooper stated that Administration is not asking the City Council to abandon the
community need for storage unit and salvage yard uses, but to elevate the standards for the uses. The group discussed
opportunities for continued discussion of the proposed ordinance amendments in smaller pieces in order to be prepared to take
action on specific matters, like the storage unit and salvage yard amendments, prior to October 16th. They concluded to place the
item on the next few work session agendas for continued discussion and review.
