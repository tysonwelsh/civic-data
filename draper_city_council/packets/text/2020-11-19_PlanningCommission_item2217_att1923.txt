BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY
This application is a request for approval of a Site Plan and Plat Amendment for approximately 2.16 acres
located at approximately 839 East Pioneer Rd (Exhibit B). The property is currently zoned TC. The
applicant is requesting that a Site Plan be approved for a parking lot addition at the Draper Cove
development. Additionally the applicant is requesting that a Plat Amendment be approved to remove two
of the commercial units on the approved plat to allow for interior access to the new parking lot area and to
amend the common area on the plat to include the new parking lot.

Draper Cove Phase 1 obtained approval in 2019 for a Site Plan and a Subdivision Plat. It is currently
under construction. Phase 2 obtained Site Plan approval on July 9, 2020 and a Plat Amendment on
October 8, 2020. There is one residential home on the property which was built in 1951.


ANALYSIS

General Plan and Zoning. The Land Use Map of the General Plan calls for the Town Center land use
designation for the subject property (Exhibit C). This category is characterized as follows:

The property has been assigned the TC zoning classification (Exhibit D). According to Draper City
Municipal Code (DCMC) Section 9-8-020 the purpose of the TC zone is to “is to provide a method for
implementing special provisions found within the general plan and the Draper City conservation area
master plan regarding the establishment and promotion of a historic core area of the community
characterized by stringent design standards for buildings, public spaces, site design and landscaping with
a harmonious mix of commercial uses, limited high density residential uses and civic areas”. The TC
zone abuts the property on the south, east, and a portion of the west side of the subject property. To the
north is the CN (Neighborhood Commercial) zone and to the west is the RA2 (Residential Agricultural,
20,000 square foot lot minim) and RM1 (Multi-family Residential, up to 8 dwelling units per acre
maximum) zones.

Site Plan Layout. The applicant has purchased the parcel to the west of the existing Phase 1 site and will
be placing a parking lot on that parcel. In addition, the applicant has proposed to eliminate the two
northwest units within Phase 1 in order to create a drive aisle connecting the Phase 1 parking lot to the
new parking lot to the west. A secondary access will be provided to the new parking lot from the
property to the north. The property to the north is an office building and the applicant has obtained a cross
access agreement to utilize their existing access to 800 East. There is not any shared parking associated
with that agreement. The TC zone requires parking lots to be located to the side or rear of a building. The
new parking lot addition will not be to the rear or side of any building in Phase 1. There is a parcel to the
south of the parking lot addition that has been designated as a future building site within the site plan. It is
the intent of the applicant to develop that parcel as Phase 4 at a later date. The property owner has
provided an owners affidavit allowing the property to be part of the site plan as a future phase. The

prescribed in the TC zone. (Exhibit E)

Landscaping and Lot Coverage. The site shows an overall landscape amount of 17,360 square feet. This
equals 18.4% of the site. The TC zone requires at least 15% overall landscaping. The buildings will cover
26,621 square feet of property. This will create a lot coverage amount of 28.35% of the property. The TC
zone allows a total of 50% of a property to be covered in buildings. Landscaping will center around
property lines and between parking rows. The TC zone requires trees with a caliper over 4-inches that
have been certified by an arborist as healthy to be preserved or replaced on a caliper per caliper basis. The
applicant has obtained an arborist review of the existing trees on site. The arborist has determined that
there are two 12-inch caliper Honey Locust trees on the property that will need to be replaced on a caliper
per caliper basis. The applicant is providing 12 two-inch caliper trees to account for the removal of the
two Honey Locus trees. This is in addition to the requirement of 43 new trees based on the landscape area
square footage. (Exhibit F)

Parking. As the applicant has begun selling units within Phase 1, it has become apparent that the
breakdown of office uses on the second floor exceeds that originally anticipated. The original approvals
for Phase 1 assumed the second story of all units would be utilized as storage space. The applicant is
finding that about 30% of the 2nd story is actually being utilized for office. The parking lot addition will
provide needed parking spaces to ensure that the second story can be utilized for office uses. The required
parking for the site is 131, with a min/max range of 118-144 stalls. The applicant is providing 126 on-site
stalls and 17 on-street stalls. The TC one does not allow on-street parking to count toward the minimum
parking requirement. (Exhibit E)

Lighting. The applicant is proposing four new light poles within the parking lot addition area. The lights
will match those approved in Phase 1 and feature a pendant type light that will match the buildings
architecturally.

Fencing. The applicant will be providing a 6-foot tall concrete or masonry wall along the south property
line adjacent to the existing home as required in the TC zone. No other fencing is proposed.

Subdivision Plat. The proposed plat amendment will remove two units from Building A, leaving a total of
22 commercial units. It will also extend the boundary of the common area to include the new parking lot
area. The common area will go from 1.147 acres to 1.54 acres in size. This area will include all parking,
landscaping, drive aisles, and pedestrian walkways. An owners association has been set up for the
maintenance of the common area. The approved subdivision plat divides the commercial units into
condominiums This means each business owner will own the space the business is located in. The owners
associations ensure condo owners have a mechanism for property maintenance. (Exhibit G)

          The Planning Commission may approve the vacation or amendment of a plat by signing an
          amended plat showing the vacation or amendment if the Land Use Authority finds that there is
          good cause for the vacation or amendment; and no public street, right-of-way, or easement has
          been vacated or amended.
