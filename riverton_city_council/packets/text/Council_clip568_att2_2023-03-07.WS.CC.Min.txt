 1   Fund 40, REDIIF. Mr. Hicks said there was $600,000 to pay down the 2012 refunding. He has
 2   already sent a letter to the trustee and everything will be processed on June 1, 2023. He said
 3   that Fund 40 and Fund 45 will be used for the Bangerter Highway betterment at 13400 South.

 6   $500,000.

 8   Mayor Staggs asked about the revenue line showing franchise fees, gas and power as static.
 9   Mr. Hicks said these fees have stayed the same for the past 10 years and he believes this
10   number is a safe and conservative estimate.
11
12   Mr. Hicks pointed out rows 63-64 which would use REDIIF to help out with culinary and
13   secondary legacy projects.
14
15   Councilmember Buroker would like to include an item for trails in the budget. She believes the
16   County is interested in betterments on the Rose Creek and Midas Creek trails and would like to
17   collaborate with them to fund some of these projects. In response, Councilmember McDougal
18   asked if the Council needed to pick which trails to fund. There are funds in Fund 45 going
19   towards trails.
20
21   Mayor Staggs said that Fund 45 included line items for both Midas Creek and Rose Creek.
22   Mayor Staggs asked the Council to table this issue for further discussion.
23
24   In fund 41, Mayor Staggs said there is a 10 acre park and a 16 acre park for Western
25   Commercial District which is budgeted in 2025 and will be up to the Council to prioritize these
26   items. Councilmember Pierucci said the BMX park may be something the Council wants to
27   budget sooner than later. Councilmember Buroker said there may be a grant the City could
28   apply for to fund the BMX park.
29
30   Mr. Hicks explained the City is due for another impact fees study. The parks impact fee fund
31   was historically not project driven. Other impact fees are driven by project and money can only
32   be spent if it is on the capital facilities plan. He recommended adding more onto this plan and
33   mentioned that staff will be starting the process within the next month to update the plans
34
35   Mayor Staggs said there are a lot of legacy projects that are not on the capital facilities plan so
36   they would need to come from a different fund as they are not impact fee eligible. Mayor Staggs
37   believes the City is in good shape with the Stormwater capital projects fund. He said they can
38   tap into this fund as well as HB 244 which goes 15 years with $700,000 per year for road
39   projects.
40
41   In fund 44, Mr. Hicks said the $1.6 million reimbursement to Edge Homes for 13800 South is a
42   guess and it may be too high or too low. The Welby Jacob box culvert has been the same
43   number for the past couple of years and is unknown when this will happen. Mayor Staggs
44   suggested changing this amount to $500,000.
45
46   Mr. Hicks said when projects are pulled from the long range plan and added to the budget, staff
47   does its best to update the dollar amounts with current estimates.
48
49   Mayor Staggs said they should start the filtering project at the end of March. Councilmember
50   McDougal asked if they could fill the canals earlier this year. Mr. Necaise said that typically they
51   allow Utah Lake to fill up first before they will start to fill the canals. Stacie Olson also clarified
     Page | 3 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1   that they will not be filling the canals early because they do not want to encourage more water
 2   use.
 3
 4   Mayor Staggs explained that the City is planning to hire a Project Management Professional
 5   (PMP) because of the escalating costs of projects and the need to keep projects moving forward
 6   on time and on budget. Councilmembers agreed with the need for this position.
 7
 8   Councilmember Buroker asked if there was a budget item for Park Avenue. Mr. Hicks said that
 9   HB 244 (fund 47) would be used for Park Avenue for fiscal year 25-26. Councilmember Buroker
10   asked about Laurel Chase. Mr. Necaise said they are still designing this to see what the costs
11   will be.
12
13   Councilmember Pierucci asked what the process was for when a left hand turn is needed. Mr.
14   Necaise said that a traffic study is needed to see if changes are warranted.
15
16   Fund 45. Mr. Hicks said they have added a few things to this fund. For example, they have
17   highlighted deficiencies in street lighting and proposed $100,000 each year starting the year
18   after next. There are other projects that have been added but do not have numbers. They have
19   proposals for GPS street lighting and smart street lighting. There is also a contract obligation
20   with Salt Lake County to upgrade the flooring at the senior center every 12 years.
21
22   Mayor Staggs asked if there would be a desire to take over the senior center from the County.
23   Councilmember Buroker said she does not believe the City could program the senior center as
24   well as the County does. Sheril Garn, Director of Events and Operations, said she agrees that
25   she would not want to take over the senior center. She would rather the County take full control
26   of the senior center. Mayor Staggs said he would have a conversation with Mayor Wilson about
27   taking the senior center back.
28
29   Ms. Garn said the high level flooring cost is estimated at $145,000. She said the furniture is
30   also 12 years old and may need to be replaced.
31
32   Mayor Staggs said the Midas Creek trail and Rose Creek have $250,000 each for the current
33   year. The Jordan River expense to shore up the river came in much higher than expected.
34   Councilmember Buroker said this project will need to be done now or later.
35
36   Councilmember McDougal said that residents are interested in trails being enhanced. He would
37   like to invest in trails and finish existing trail projects. He would like to look at the budget next
38   year once the City determines how much the current trail projects will cost.
39
40   Councilmembers agreed to concentrate on current trail projects on Midas Creek, Rose Creek
41   and along the Jordan River. Councilmembers asked to set aside a work session dedicated to
42   the trail system and outline a trail plan and outline where enhancements are needed.
43
44   Fund 46, Stormwater capital projects. Mr. Hicks said these are legacy projects that are not
45   eligible for the impact fee. Mayor Staggs asked when all the legacy projects are complete, then
46   could that the City get rid of the utility fee. In response, Mr. Hicks said in the next few years, the
47   City could possibly get rid of the stormwater utility fee and raise the sanitation fee.
48
49   Fund 47. Mayor Staggs explained this fund includes the $700,000 which the City will receive
50   for 15 years from HB 244. This year the projects include Majestic Rise, 1830 widening and
51   Trestle Lane.

 1   Discussion/Action Items

 3            Resolution No. 23-19 – Objecting to Implementation of “Environmental, Social and
 4            Governance” Policies in the Finance Sector
 5
 6   Mayor Staggs asked Kevin Hicks to discuss the information about how bond funding is
 7   provided. Mayor Staggs said there is a Senate concurrent Resolution Number 9 which has
 8   objected to the ESG ratings and political ideology.
 9
10   Mr. Hicks presented information about bond funding using the ESG policies. This information is
11   included in the packet.
12
13   Mayor Staggs explained that ESG policies will prioritize a political score over the free market.
14   He believes the City’s resolution is a first step in pushing back on this policy and lends support
15   to state leaders in their decisions. The City will work with financial agencies that do not focus on
16   political ideology.
17
18   Councilmembers expressed support for this resolution.
19
20   Councilmember Pierucci MOVED that the City Council approve Resolution No. 23-19 –
21   Objecting to Implementation of “Environmental, Social and Governance” Policies in the
22   Finance Sector. Councilmember McCay SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for
23   discussion on the motion. Mayor Staggs called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows:
24   Buroker-yes, McCay-yes, McDougal-yes, and Pierucci-yes. The motion passed unanimously.
