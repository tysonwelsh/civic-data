residence and barn are located on one lot.

To help the public understand the ridgelining rules, Attorney Thomas gave some 
general background.  The Snyderville Basin has had ridgelining ordinances much longer 
than 15 years.  He said during this meeting the topic of the “glass house” has come up.  
When the application for the “glass house” came to the County, the position was that it 
ridgelined and would not be permitted.  This went into litigation.  The court ruled the 
County was not permitted to enforce the ridgeline ordinance.  As a result of this finding, 
it was recognized that there may be unique circumstances for lots of record if there is 
no other place to build a house.  
 
Attorney Thomas said currently there is a two-step consideration process: 
1. The house has to be located in the Ridgeline Overlay zone.     
2. In the event that a legally defined parcel or the access to a lot of record consists 
of critical lands, and if there are no permissible locations to build on the parcel, 
the property owner will be able to develop in the most sensitive manner for both 
access and structure location.   
Attorney Thomas said these are the steps that are required to get someone into the 
exception process.  At that point, the applicant is required to locate the structure on the 
best spot possible.
