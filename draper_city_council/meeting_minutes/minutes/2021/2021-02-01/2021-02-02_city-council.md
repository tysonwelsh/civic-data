1.0       Report: 2021 Transportation Impact Fees - Fred Philpot

1.1       Fred Philpot presented on the Impact Fee Facilities Plan (IFFP) and the Impact Fee Analysis
          (IFA), required documents based on the Utah Code when updating impact fee studies and
          assessing new impact fees. The process surrounding impact fees was defined by statute.
          This process entailed (1) noticing of intent, which the City had already done; (2)
          preparation and certification of IFFP and IFA documents; followed by (3) presentation of
          findings and a public hearing process. He explained that they were right in the middle of
          that process through the current presentation during a work session to allow the Council
          a review of the findings and analysis to provide input before moving into the public
          hearing phase. At that time noticing for public hearing would occur, the Council would
          hold a public hearing, and then would have the option to adopt, modify or reject the
          impact fees. Any modification could be lower than what was proposed. However, the
          City could not adopt a higher than the maximum allowable impact fee. Mr. Philpot
          explained that if an increase to impact fees occurred, there was a 90-day wait period
          before the increase could be assessed. The statute states, “any change to the impact
          fee,” however, typically by practice, if there is a decrease it becomes effective
          immediately.

          Mr. Philpot said that every IFFP and IFA typically contains four main components. First,
          the service area and demand of existing facilities and capacities are assessed. The City’s
          Level of Service (LOS) is evaluated and needed revenue sources to the fund capital
          improvement plan are identified. In the plan discussed, it was proposed from multiple
          service areas to a single City-wide service area. Excess capacity was reviewed in Draper’s
          existing inventory, resulting in the inclusion of $57 million in assets. All of those assets
          were not growth eligible as about $8 million was applied to new growth as buy-in or
          excess capacity. The Level of Service was defined as LOS D. Roadway systems were
          typically graded on a scale A-F with most entities falling in the C-D range, or congestion
          when sizing facilities. He said that trying to get above that becomes very costly and might
          not be achievable. For IFFP, it was recommended that Draper maintain a LOS D.

          In looking at future revenues to fund the Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) it was assumed
          that impact fee revenues would continue to be a source of revenue as well as General
          Fund money to help fund system improvements as well as non-growth-related
          improvements. No new debt was assumed in the analysis. If there was death utilization,
          it could be included in the analysis.

      Mr. Philpot noted that Parametrix was the engineer contracted to complete the IFFP.
      They identified some projects and isolated three main projects that were Impact Fee
      eligible and needed to be completed in the next ten years: Roadway 13400 South from
      Fort Street to 1300 East, roadway Vista Station Boulevard from Frontrunner Boulevard to
      12300 South, and roadway 12200 South from 300 East to 700 East.

      He explained that impact fee documents typically focus on a ten-year planning time
      frame, even though master plans and capital improvement plans often look beyond that
      time frame. The reason that is done is state statute requires that impact fees be
      expended within six years of when they were collected. As a result, the standard of
      practice is to look at a ten-year window.

      The three projects identified would result in a total of $18,075,497 in construction fees,
      $6,573,172 of which were impact fee eligible. Anticipated growth was matched with
      increase impact fees to estimate the amount of the proposed projects that would be
      impact fee eligible. He said that there might be additional costs beyond the ten-year time
      frame that might be impact fee eligible.

      This led to the proportionate share analysis, or impact fee analysis. Mr. Philpot noted
      some figures on a table: the $8 million of buy-in, $6 million for future facilities, and
      $18,000 in professional expenses such as an IFFP and IFA update. The trips served are
      analyzed, roughly 60,000 in the next ten years, to produce a cost per trip of nearly $250.
      The land-use type, from single-family to non-residential properties, is then looked at to
      find the adjusted trips per land-use type. These adjusted trips are used to calculate a cost
      per trip based on land use type. As a result, there would be an increase in impact fees for
      single-family residential for both the Valley (area A) and Traverse Mountain (area B). The
      same calculation was used for the other land-use types. The decrease in impact fees
      varied by land-use type because of the average daily trips. Although these values varied,
      the only area that would experience an increase was hotels in the valley area.

      Mr. Philpot outlined the next steps. As he explained before, the Council had the
      opportunity to provide input before noticing for an impact fee public hearing. Then the
      public hearing would be held where input from the public was solicited. Then, the Council
      could adopt, modify, or reject the proposed impact fees with the 90-day wait period if
      impact fees were increased.

       There would be an application process required for these sites along with a provided site
       plan showing the proposed layout of the site. A method for traffic circulation must also
       be provided. The layout may not impede public safety or emergency vehicle access.

       appointment only. Clinics would operate in a drive-through style, with limited walk-up

       International Fire Code requirements. Clinics would have to get all required licensing. To
