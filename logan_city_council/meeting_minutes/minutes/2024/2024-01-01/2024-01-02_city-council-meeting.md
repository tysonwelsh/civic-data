At the December 5, 2023 Council meeting, Light & Power Director Mark Montgomery
addressed the Council regarding the proposed resolution. The resolution is to certify that
the City is selling energy for a qualified use. An assurance by Logan City that the power
sold under this agreement is a “Qualified Use.”

Logan City is a member of Utah Associated Municipal Power Systems (“UAMPS”) and
is a participant in the Payson Power Project. UAMPS proposed to enter into a
Commodity Supply Contract (a prepaid contract) with Southeast Energy Authority to
purchase a portion of the natural gas necessary for the operation of the project at a price
that reflects a discount from the current contract price of natural gas for the project being
paid by UAMPS, and that the savings from such discount (roughly 8%) will be applied
by UAMPS for the benefit of the participants in the project based on their respective
entitlement shares for the next thirty years.

Vice Chair A. Anderson asked for more information regarding a hydro penstock.

Mr. Montgomery explained a hydro penstock is a large tube that feeds water to the
generators. An eight-foot steel pipe was damaged by a drunk driver driving off-road
which is the reason for the repair.

Mr. Anderson stated regarding the proposed $1,400,000, additional funding for the
Second Dam sediment removal project is being shifted to cover the cost. Should the
Council not approve the resolution, the project will continue and stop at whatever phase
once the budgeted amount has been reached.

Councilmember Simmonds requested confirmation regarding the amount and the project
that had a prior bid, and the reason for the change.

Mr. Montgomery confirmed the project had a prior bid amount. The biometric survey
was completed prior to the extremely high run-off year. At the time, the estimate was
14,000 cubic yards of sediment removal. Another biometric survey was completed, and
                                                                                 10 | Page
                 Logan Municipal Council Minutes ~ Logan, Utah ~ January 2, 2024
an additional 20,000 cubic yards needs to be removed for a total of 35,000 cubic yards.
The amount doubled after this past year of extreme runoff. The company is already on
site and ready to start. However, if only 14,000 cubic yards are removed, the amount
would not be enough to utilize Second Dam.

Vice Chair A. Anderson asked once the sediment was removed if that would bring power
generation capacity back up.

Mr. Montgomery confirmed that is correct. The reservoir will be appropriately restored.
The reservoir functions to trap sediment which it does very well. The problem is that the
pond is so full of sediment it is going into the generators. The generators are being
destroyed, currently, one generator is completely disassembled and has been shipped out
to be fixed.

Councilmember Simmonds remarked that despite the $1,400,000 that is still under the
other highly priced bid.

Mr. Montgomery confirmed that was the case as the other bid was for nearly $6 million
for only 14,000 cubic yards.

Councilmember Johnson asked what the longevity of sediment removal is and with what
frequency sediment removal needs to occur.

Mr. Montgomery answered sediment removal occurs in small phases, never in full
sediment removal. Part of the permitting is to create a procedure for sediment removal for
federal energy regulations, the intent is for sediment removal to occur more frequently on
a five-year basis. However, heavy run-off years like the past year are the ones that impact
us the most. A crucial impact is the fact that Third Dam is also full. Third Dam is not
trapping any sediment rather it is passing it all downstream to Second Dam. The third
Dam is a bigger reservoir, and hopefully will also be done in the near future. This is all
dependent on available funding.

Chairman López inquired who completed the initial assessment.

Mr. Montgomery answered that the initial assessment was completed by an engineering
firm. The company removing sediment also performed its own assessment using the geo
points set up by the original engineering firm. Upon completion of their assessment, the
company discovered there was an extra 20,000 cubic yards of sediment. The original
engineering firm, Franson Engineers, did another survey to confirm the additional
sediment found and confirmed that was indeed the case.

Mr. Anderson stated that the sediment removal will drop the reserves of the electric fund
below the targeted threshold by 1.4 million more. It will take longer for the electric fund
to recover. We should be fine to proceed and there are reserves in other funds. There is
also a pending loan from the electric fund to the library that will partially be paid back
sooner than anticipated.

REZONE – Consideration of a proposed rezone of approximately 0.13 acres of
property located at 181 North 200 East from Traditional Neighborhood Residential
(NR-6) to Town Center 2 (TC-2) Little Bloomsbury Inn and Gallery – Ordinance
24-01 – Russ Holley, Planner (1:01:25)

Planner Russ Holley addressed the Council regarding the proposed rezone.

RECOMMENDATION
Staff recommended that the Planning Commission forward a recommendation of
approval to the Municipal Council for a rezone of approximately 0.13 acres of property
located at 181 North 200 East (TIN# 06-066-0023) from Traditional Neighborhood
Residential (NR-6) to Town Center 2 (TC-2).

Land use adjoining the subject property
 North:     NR-6: Residential Uses             East:      NR-6: Religious Uses
 South:     NR-6: Residential Uses             West:      TC-2: Commercial Uses

PROJECT
The proponent is requesting to rezone the 0.13-acre property at 181 North 200 East from
NR-6 to TC-2. The applicant is planning a future small boutique hotel and gallery for the
property. If the rezone is successful, a subsequent Conditional Use Permit will be
requested to legally establish those commercial uses. The property is currently developed
with a single-family home, built in 1909, and a detached garage in the rear yard. An
open-ditch irrigation canal traverses the property flowing northward and essentially
bisects the parcel between the rear of the home and the garage.

GENERAL PLAN
The Future Land Use Plan (FLUP), adopted in 2008, identifies this property as Town
Center (TC). The General Plan, a nonregulatory visioning plan, describes TC areas as
being a hub of the City and Valley and reflects a vibrant downtown, with a variety of
commercial, mixed-use, and institutionally uses and activities. The TC designation
covers land from 200 South to 500 North and staggers between 100 East and 200 East
along the eastern side of Downtown Logan. The TC designation staggers between 100
and 150 West along the west side of Downtown.

Land Development Code (LDC) - Zoning
The TC-2, which encourages dense, walkable commercial and residential development in
the downtown area with unique and interesting building designs. This zone allows a
density of up of to 30 units per acre along with a mixture of commercial uses. The City
went through a deliberate and robust process in 2018 to redefine the Town Center zoning
districts which culminated in the adoption of Ordinance Number 18-07. This year-long
zoning process established TC-2 in the outer-ring areas of downtown as a way to buffer
or transition the residential neighborhoods further east and west of Downtown. The goal

                                                                                12 | Page
             Logan Municipal Council Minutes ~ Logan, Utah ~ January 2, 2024
of the zoning strategy adopted in 2018 was to further implement the visions expressed in
the General Plan through a methodical transition of uses and structure.

SUMMARY
Given the FLUP designation of TC and the current commercial land uses on the block,
the request to up-zone this parcel to TC-2 would be a predictable and expected zoning
change. With the LDS Temple located to the east of the block with extensive setbacks,
TC-2 development and land uses on the subject property and the remaining 200 East
block face would be compatible and pose minimal impacts to the residential areas further
east. Being a core development proposal, infrastructure and utilities can be more
efficiently utilized as compared to outer fringe areas (sprawl) that excessively burden the
transportation and infrastructure networks with challenging logistics, higher vehicle miles
driven, higher emissions, and lengthier utility lines.

AGENCY AND CITY DEPARTMENT COMMENTS
No comments have been received.

PUBLIC COMMENTS
Notices were mailed to property owners within 300 feet of the subject property. As of the
time of this report no comments have been received.

PUBLIC NOTIFICATION
Legal notices were published in the Herald Journal on 12/2/23 and the Utah Public
Meeting website on 12/4/23/23. Public notices were mailed to all property owners within
300 feet of the project site on 11/27/23.

RECOMMENDED FINDINGS OF APPROVAL
The Planning Commission bases its decision on the following findings supported in the
administrative record for this project:
   1. The surrounding zoning to the west is TC-2 and a rezone of this property will
       result in a contiguous zoning pattern on the block.
   2. The TC-2 zone is appropriate because it aligns with the adopted FLUP and fits
       into the existing land use patterns of the block.
   3. TC-2 zoning, and land uses are in close proximity to transportation choices,
       downtown commercial goods and services and employment centers.
   4. The majority of the surrounding existing land uses to the west are commercial in
       nature.
   5. The Conditional Use Permit process and LDC development standards will ensure
       a quality project that will be compatible with the surrounding neighborhood.

PROJECT
The proponent is requesting to rezone the 4.43-acre property from IP to AP and build a
70,275 SF self-storage (commercial storage units) facility. The AP zone is the only zone
that permits self-storage land uses, the applicant is planning a five-building, 503-unit,
70,275 SF self-storage facility. The recently annexed vacant property along 1000 West is
currently on the westernmost edge of the city.

BACKGROUND
On October 14, 2021, the Logan City Planning Commission (PC# 21-050) conditionally
approved the Infinite Storage (previous project name) entitling up to 900 commercial
storage units on the 4.43-acre parcel. As per the Logan City Land Development Code
(LDC) 17.51.010 and listed on the Design Review and Conditional Use Permit, the
approval expired one year from that date. (October 14, 2022). On March 1, 2022, the Logan
City Council adopted a code amendment ordinance prohibiting new commercial storage
units in the IP zone. The applicant submitted for a building permit on May 23, 2023, nearly
six months after the expiration date. The applicant cited informal correspondence with
water and sewer City personnel as the reason for the delay. On July 21, 2023, Walter Keane
submitted an appeal application to the Logan City Land Use Appeals Board. The Land Use
Appeals Board upheld staff’s denial of the project and determined the project to be expired.
The Appeal is now going through the District Court process with the hope to overturn the
Land Use Appeals Board decision.

GENERAL PLAN
The Future Land Use Plan (FLUP), adopted in 2008, identifies this property as Industrial
Park (IP). The General Plan, a nonregulatory visioning plan, describes IP areas as being
for large employment and production centers. Residential is not appropriate and site
development standards will emphasize large setbacks and ample landscaping. The IP
designation is generally located in the northwest quadrant of the city.

The Airport (AP) FLUP designation is specifically intended to promote the development
and enhancement of the Airport. The AP designation is also intended for commercial uses
typically associated with Airports, such as hotels, restaurants, and similar. The AP
designation should continue to foster the relationship with Utah State University and their
aviation instructional efforts.

Land Development Code (LDC) - Zoning
The IP zoning district no longer permits or conditional permits “self-storage” or
commercial storage units. In the spring of 2022, the adoption occurred with the debate
centered on commercial storage units being too land consumptive for most zoning districts
and the land use provides very few jobs and employment opportunities as compared to
other IP land uses. Currently, the AP zone is the only district that conditionally permits
commercial storage units in the City. Site development standards include ample setbacks
and landscaping to help mitigate and buffer the typically stark building designs and layouts.

Setbacks
The Land Development Code (LDC) requirements for setbacks in the AP zone are as
follows (as measured from property lines):
Front:                      10’
Side:                       20’
Rear:                       10’
Parking (front/rear)        20’/15’

The following setbacks are proposed for the building (as measured from the exterior
property lines of the project site, at closest points):
Front (west):                   45’
Side (north):                   40’
Side (south):                   58’
Rear (east):                    60
Parking (front):                30’

As proposed, the project meets minimum setback requirements of the LDC.

the AP zone. The total project site is approximately 4.43 acres in size and with a 70,275

Building Design
The LDC 17.12.060 requires high-quality and aesthetically pleasing building design in
industrial areas of town. Minimum articulation and material mixes are required for better
variety and interesting developments. Blank or featureless walls are limited to no more
than 40-foot lengths.
The proposed storage buildings are approximately 240’ wide and vary in depth at 40’-110’.
The proposed material for the storage units is concrete masonry units. As per commercial
storage design requirements, the buildings are oriented so that the storage unit bays do not
face the surrounding street. The front building is internally accessed, with the remaining
rear buildings accessed from exterior garage-style doors. The front elevation is proposed
with alternative metal façade materials rather than the AP required 40% transparency. As
conditioned, with enhanced landscape screening and the Planning Commission
determining the approval of the alternative materials, the project meets the requirements of
the LDC.

Parking Stall Requirements
The LDC does not prescribe parking ratios for this land use and suggests an alternative
parking plan based on the project details. Parking for exterior storage units occurs directly
adjacent to the unit in the 34’ wide drive aisle. Parking for this land use is temporary in
nature, as users either load and unload items and quickly exit the property. For the front
interior storage units, nine (9) stalls are shown on the submitted site plan. Based on the
project layout and unique land use, staff considers the proposed parking areas adequate to
mitigate overflow parking onto adjacent public streets.

Useable Outdoor Space and Landscaping
The LDC 17.10.070 requires 10% landscaping and 10% useable outdoor space in the AP
zone. The LDC 17.32 generally describes useable outdoor space as outdoor areas that are
designed for the benefit and enjoyment of the residents and their guests. Useable outdoor
space may include public gathering areas, patios, decks, walkways, plaza’s, active
recreational areas, and natural open space areas. The project site for this proposal is
approximately 192,000 SF and would require 38,400 SF of both landscaping and outdoor
space. The submitted concept landscaping plan shows approximately 40,000 SF of area for
landscaping and open space meeting the LDC requirement as submitted.

Lighting
The LDC 17.30.090 requires adequate lighting that adds aesthetic quality and improves
safety while mitigating unnecessary glare, sky glow and light trespass. The LDC limits
freestanding pole height to 32 feet and luminaire fixtures on buildings and canopies to be
concealed source, down-cast and shielded from neighboring properties. Light
measurements are required to range between 0.5 – 4.0-foot candles, so areas are sufficiently
safe, but not excessively bright. As submitted, no exterior lighting has been shown. As
conditioned, the project meets the requirements of the LDC.

SUMMARY
Given the FLUP designation of IP and the 2022 code amendment prohibiting new
commercial storage units in the IP zone because of their land consumption rates and lack
of larger employment opportunities, staff recommends following the FLUP and denying
the rezone request. It is unfortunate that the applicant let the 2021 entitlement lapse and
expire, but that is how the Logan City land use regulation and zoning is established.
Typically, land use authorities establish expiration dates to prevent old and outdated
surprises from popping up unexpectantly. The nearest AP zoned parcel is currently over
2,000 feet away and would create a small island of AP zoning that is not contiguous with
the actual Airport.

Mr. Holley provided a background of the commercial storage unit project. In October of
2021, the property was approved for a commercial storage unit project. The project is 4.3
acres. The permits issued by the Planning Commission and the City have expiration dates.
There is a one-year window for an applicant to file for a building permit or subdivision
permit. There is also an opportunity to get an extension letter which would give the
applicant an extra year.

In October 2022, this particular permit expired. In March of 2022, the City approved a new
code amendment that no longer allowed storage units in the IP zone, storage units are only
allowed in the Airport zone. Storage units are land-consumptive and typically only employ
one person. It is not an optimum occupation that produces jobs.

The applicant was grandfathered as the permit had not yet expired, and the City would
honor the permit. In October when the permit expired the applicant met with Mike
Desimone, Community Development Director & Tanya Rice, Planner. The applicant was
having issues with his contractor. Mr. DeSimone wrote an extension letter until
December 2022. The applicant had a three-month extension to submit a building permit,
the applicant did not submit an application until May of 2023.

The applicant did correspond with the Public Works Department in January and February
of 2023. However, the extension letter was very clear that the applicant must submit a
building permit application no later than December 2022. The applicant submitted the
permit in May of 2023, and the application was consequently rejected. In July of 2023,
the applicant’s attorney filed an appeal. The appeal went to the Land Use Appeal Board
in September of 2023, and upheld staff’s decision of denial. The applicant’s attorney
appealed to the District Court and in December of 2023 the judge also upheld City’s staff
decision of denial. The applicant has since appealed to the Utah Supreme Court with the
case currently pending.

All the appeals hence afterward have to deal with the expired permit. As a different
avenue altogether, the applicant has proposed the rezone in order to complete the project.
One of the arguments being made is that this is a “taking” (Taking is when the
government encroaches upon or occupies private property for public use), but that is not
the case as the IP zone is still a valuable zone where business and commercial usage is
allowed as well. The economic value of the property has not been robbed.
