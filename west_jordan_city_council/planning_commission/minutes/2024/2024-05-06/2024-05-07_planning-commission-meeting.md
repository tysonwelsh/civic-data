2. Fallen Angel Tattoos; 6908 South Redwood Road; Conditional Use Permit for Tattoo and

Body Engraving Services; SC-2 (Redwood Overlay) Zone; Fallen Angel Tattoos/Terrina
Carrillo (applicant) [#32519; parcel 21-22-381-032]

Terrina Carrillo, applicant, said they have been operating in Salt Lake City for 12 years. They have all

female artists and are best known for their work with breast cancer survivors and trauma survivors. She

clarified that their services do not include body engraving. They recently were recognized

internationally. They hold an annual fundraiser for Salt Lake County Animal Shelter, and they would

love to do something similar for West Jordan. This location gives them room to grow and has more

than enough parking, so traffic and parking will not be a problem. There are 3-6 clients at a time

staying about three hours. There are no effects on public safety services or surrounding businesses.

They do not require additional services or resources from the city, and there are no detrimental effects

on the health or welfare of the public. They have never had violations with the health department.

Hours of operation are about 10 a.m. to 7 p.m. They are all moms and like to go home to spend time

with their families. Music in the facility is soft, the same as in retail environments, and they do not

generate odors or fumes.

Emily Gonzalez asked about the amount of traffic that is generated in association with their events and

if that would affect parking.

Terrina Carrillo said the parking lot will handle the demand. Even with their business on State Street

that has very little parking, it did not create any problems. She explained that they have volunteers with

clipboards who go through the line and schedule a time and then the client comes back. She has a lease

at the other location for another year, and at that time they will decide if that location will close.

3. Terraine West Bench Grange Building; 7174 W Hidden Hills Way; Preliminary Site Plan;

PCH Zone; Third Cadence LLC/Gary Langston (applicant) [#31545; parcel 20-27-101-
001-4003)

Gary Langston, applicant, said this is the second commercial building within the Terraine West Bench

(Wood Ranch) development. It is a one-story, 3500 square foot building that matches the Bistro

building to the west. Materials include concrete, metal, and wood with a modern motif. This

community/event center will have offices for the HOA and one large space for about 150 people that

can be divided into smaller rooms. The residents can use the space for classes, receptions, etc. and

there is an outdoor area as well.

Ammon Allen asked Mr. Langston to explain how much parking is available within a reasonable

distance, including ADA parking.

Gary Langston pointed out the highlighted area on the site plan, which includes four ADA stalls to

service both buildings. Street parking is available within 250 feet and should be enough to

accommodate what is needed. An important part of the community is encouraging people to walk.

4. Copper Rim Preliminary Development Plan; Approximately 7516 South Copper Rim
Drive; Recommendation to the City Council for a Major Variation to the Preliminary
Master Development Plan and Master Development Agreement affecting portions of
the undeveloped areas of Copper Rim Planned Community by (1) removing the
requirement for 204 age-restricted senior housing units in Phase 9 and replacing it with
single-family residential “cottage lots”; (2) revising roads and private alleys; (3)
reconfigure phase boundaries, relocate lots, and other revisions; CW Land Co.
(applicant) [#29564]
Matt Dean, CW Land Co., addressed eight points regarding the proposed Preliminary Development
Plan update. 1) Their organization conducted several studies over the last 1’ years and found that age-

restricted housing is not feasible for this area. Construction costs are high and there is a lack of
financing opportunities for a development with a very shallow pool of potential renters or buyers. In
order to avoid a low-quality product that would fail, they propose that the 204 age-restricted units be
replaced with cottage homes to a wider range of buyers who want a quality single-family detached
product. 2) The overall density would be reduced by almost 17% to 731 units in the overall Copper
Rim master plan for an overall density of 3.5 units per acre. 3) The reduction in units frees up 150
ERC’s that will now be available for other projects in the city. 4) With the reconfiguration, there is a
slight increase in dedicated open space to 18.3%, which exceeds the 15% requirement. 5) Adjustments
in the phase line boundary and minor shifts in density within Phases 4 through 9 will now allow for
two points of access for emergency services and looping for water and sewer facilities. He explained
where some minor densities were shifted in order to accommodate the correct lot widths. 6) The
amended layout will help to preserve and stabilize the natural dry wash. 7) The amendment cleans up
inconsistencies from the original 2018 preliminary development plan, such as the preserve lot widths.
8) The original layout of the exterior loop road in Phase 9 is unfeasible and has safety and traffic
congestion issues. Moving the connection to the east will align with the existing intersection to the
north. It will also prevent the intersection from being too close to the Mountain View Corridor
overpass when 7000 South is widened. The original plan would have required massive cuts, fills, and
extensive retaining walls due to steep slopes in Phase 9. They felt that for these reasons the proposed
preliminary development plan changes are positive and an enhanced land plan will benefit the current
and future residents of the Copper Rim community and West Jordan as a whole.

Ammon Allen asked if something has changed since 2018 regarding the senior housing product or if it
was just an oversight.

Matt Dean said he was not on the project in 2018, but in their analysis in the past 1 4 years it shows
the rental rate that can be supported on this site is up to $72 per square foot per month, and this product
could not be built for that cost. So, they are removing the density and propose something that will get
built.

Ray McCandless added that staff likes the modifications in Phase 6 that now have pedestrian
connectivity where it was disjointed before. Also, the new lot layout on the west side of Phase 6 is
improved. There have been a lot of iterations and meetings to get to this point, and staff felt that they
have a good plan. He clarified that these amendments only affect the areas north of the roundabout.
Staff recommended that the Planning Commission forward a favorable recommendation to the City
Council to amend the Copper Rim Preliminary Development Plan as requested by the applicant and
update the Master Development Agreement to reflect the proposed changes. Following review by the
Planning Commission, the Master Development Agreement will be updated to reflect recommended
changes and forwarded to the City Council for approval.

George Winn asked if staff knew what else would be coming on the commercial parcels in addition to
Furniture Row.

Ray McCandless said the first phase is the only one with an approval, but a commercial subdivision
plat for future phases will come to the Commission. That subdivision plat will show access and lot
layout and how it is integrated with the overall development plan for the Interchange Overlay Zone
area.

Scott Langford said that all commercial site plans will come to the Planning Commission. The
Economic Development department is actively looking to program additional pad sites in that area.
Tom Hollingsworth asked if there will be a requirement for an additional playground or open space in
conjunction with the cottage lots.

Ray McCandless said the amenity package has not changed with this amendment. There are three tot
lots within the development.

Ammon Allen asked if there will be bridges over Mountain View Corridor at 7000 South and 7400
South.

Todd Johnson said yes. Once the road on the west side is completed with development then UDOT
will complete the bridge.

George Winn pointed out that the crossing at 7400 South will lead to Maple Hills Park, which is not
too far away from this development.

George Winn opened the public hearing.

Ryan Wilkenson, speaking remotely, said the change felt like a bait-and-switch. He wondered why it
took six years to identify that senior housing did not make sense for the project or if it was always
known. His main concern is with east/west traffic. It was a problem 20 years ago, and this development
adds 700 houses that he will have to compete with in traffic. He expressed his concern for lack of
citations regarding the left lane travel law. He was opposed.

Nils, speaking remotely, said he owns a townhouse to the north and with the winds that are in the area,
he said the project could generate a lot of dust. He asked if there was a mitigation plan.

Todd Johnson explained the SWPPP process and purposes that are required for every project. He said
the project may bring a little bit of dust but there should be controls in place to mitigate that and after
construction, the amount of dust will be drastically reduced.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

Jay Thomas said the first caller mentioned a bait-and-switch, which it could be, but he felt that the
change was for the betterment of the area and the community. With fewer homes and less density,
travel will be reduced.

George Winn asked if anyone wanted to address economic viability and traffic concerns.

Ray McCandless said the project paves the way for better east/west connections by setting the stage for
the bridges on 7000 South and 7400 South.

Duncan Murray said typically the Commission does not look at economic viability in these situations,
but they should focus on the code and regulation requirements.
