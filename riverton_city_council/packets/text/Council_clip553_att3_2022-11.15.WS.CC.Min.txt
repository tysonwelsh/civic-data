43   rough draft of the Little Library policy to the Council so it can be adopted at the next City Council
44   meeting.

47   was created for STEM materials only.

26   Mayor Staggs met with Casey Saxton earlier in the day and went through the schedule of
27   events for the month. On November 17, the Council will meet with legislators at the Old Dome
28   Meeting Hall. Mayor Staggs will communicate the top priorities including pushing for
29   transportation equity and the completion of Mountain View Corridor.
30
31   Another item that will be discussed is the intersection at 13400 South and Bangerter. In order to
32   have the intersection fully submerged, UDOT will require the City to pay $4 million. He said
33   some property owners have loosely committed to contributing money for this project. He would
34   also like to work with Senator Dan McCay and the lobbyists to utilize quarter-for-quarter monies.
35   This would leave about $500,000 to $1 million the City would contribute, most likely from RDIF
36   funds. He said the County also has $20 million for transportation choice money and $2 million
37   needs to be allocated. He would like to work with the lobbyist to secure some of this money for
38   this intersection.
39
40   Councilmember Buroker asked what the plan was for intersection at 2700 West and Bangerter.
41   Mayor Staggs said it would be the standard overpass and would not require any money from the
42   City. Mayor Staggs said that there would be large sound walls installed.
43
44   Councilmember McCay said she would like both intersections to be modified. Cary Necaise,
45   Public Works Director, said UDOT is already moving forward with the plans to have 13400
46   South fully submerged and 2700 West standard overpass. They have already done
47   environmental studies for both of these projects.
48

 5   Councilmember McCay would also like to express the importance of connectivity of roads.

 7   Mayor Staggs said he thought that Councilmembers and property owners expressed support for
 8   13400 South to be fully submerged. UDOT did not require South Jordan to pay anything extra
 9   for the intersection at 11400 South to be fully submerged. The intersection at 10400 South cost
10   $6 million in betterment fees and South Jordan was able to get quarter-for-quarter monies to
11   cover this cost. The intersection at 9800 South will also be fully submerged and all three
12   intersections have been largely paid for by other sources, not the city itself.
13
14   He said the original betterment cost for 13400 South to be fully submerged was $20 million.
15   UDOT agreed to share this cost and said they would pay $10 million and Riverton would be
16   responsible for the other $10 million. Now, the fee has gone to $4 million and the City will hold
17   them firm to these figures. He would like to have the Councilmembers all on the same page
18   because UDOT is well into the design on both of these intersections.
19
20   Councilmember Stewart asked if the letter regarding the land exchange was factored into the $4
21   million that UDOT is requiring for the betterment. He also expressed support for 13400 South
22   being fully submerged because line of sight is key to business.
23
24   Mayor Staggs agreed and said that Gary Whatcott from South Jordan feels the same way. He
25   believes there is also a psychological aspect when the intersection is not submerged, causing
26   the residents on the east and west to feel separated.

28   Mayor Staggs explained he communicated in writing to UDOT that the City gave them 21 acres
29   of property years ago and that is how the betterment was lowered from $10 million to $4 million.

 1   Public Hearings

 3            Ordinance No. 22-24 – Funaro Rezone and General Plan Amendment, Proposed
 4            Amendment of the General Plan to Medium Density Residential and the Zoning
 5            from RR-22 to R-4 for 0.5 acres at 1794 West 11800 South
 6
 7   Mr. Lethbridge explained this was a public hearing for a rezone application brought by the
 8   applicant for 0.5 acres at 1794 West 11800 South. The subject property is a residential ½ acre
 9   lot with about 125 feet of frontage on 11800 South at 1794 West. Neighboring properties to the
10   east and west are all zoned RR-22 but the property sizes range from 0.3 to 0.4 acre lots, less
11   than the 0.5-acres required in the RR-22 Zone. The property to the north is a 1.4-acre lot
12   zoned RR-22. Properties to the south across 11800 South are zoned in a mix of zones
13   including one lot of RR-22, and several lots of R-4 and RM-8.
14
15   Mr. Lethbridge said the property is currently zoned “RR-22” (Residential ½ acre). The applicant
16   is requesting this rezone to R-4 (Residential ¼ acre). There is a small home on the west front of
17   the lot, and the lot has two accesses onto 11800 South. The lot size is approximately 21,780
18   square feet and would be appropriate for a private lane and a subdivision. The final result would
19   be two lots of 11,000 square feet and a private lane.
20
21   Councilmembers asked clarifying questions of staff and the applicant.
22
23   Mayor Staggs called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Public Hearing.
24

26   – Funaro Rezone and General Plan Amendment, Proposed Amendment of the General
27   Plan to Medium Density Residential and the Zoning from RR-22 to R-4 for 0.5 acres at
28   1794 West 11800 South. Councilmember Wells SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs
29   called for discussion on the motion. There being none, he called for a roll-call vote. The vote
30   was as follows: Buroker-yes, McCay-yes, McDougal-no vote (technical difficulties), Stewart-yes
