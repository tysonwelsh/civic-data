PRESENTATION: DEPUTY MITTON & SCOUT/DIXIE _ELK’S CHARITABLE
FOUNDATION

Sheriff Brooksby discussed the circumstances surrounding Deputy Mitton being responsible for
Scout, a chocolate lab trained specifically for cadaver and human tracking. The Dixie Elk’s
Charitable Foundation donated $15,000, which covered all the costs to purchase and train Scout.
Scout recently finished the cadaver portion of training, which makes him the only cadaver certified
dog in the state of Utah. Scout will undergo further tracking training. Scout and Deputy Mitton
train together once a week, with initial certification being approximately 500 hours.

CONSIDERATION OF ORDINANCE NO. 2023-1245-O, AN ORDINANCE CREATING
THE SCENIC BYWAY OVERLAY ZONE SBOZ) ALONG STATE ROUTE 9, THE
DESIGNATED ZION SCENIC _BYWAY AND ADOPTING SBOZ ZONING
REGULATIONS AS TITLE 10 CHAPTER 30 IN WASHINGTON COUNTY, UTAH

Mr. Messel stated that Washington County is one of the fastest growing regions in the country,
which comes along with development pressure. Proposals have been made in different areas of the
County, specifically along SR-9, to build hotels, water parks, ferris wheels, and alpine slides,
which may not be the most appropriate items to build along the corridor. It is recommended to
adopt an overlay zone in order to keep the SR-9 corridor as a scenic byway while encouraging
appropriate economic development and growth.

MOTION: Motion by Commissioner Iverson to Adopt Ordinance No. 2023-1245-O, An
Ordinance Creating the Scenic Byway Overlay Zone (SBOZ) Along State Rout
9, the Designated Zion Scenic Byway, and Adopting SBOZ Zoning Regulations
as Title 10 Chapter 30 in Washington County, Utah with Lines 191, 192, 193,
428 and 429 Stricken and Inserting the Language Based on a Traffic
Engineering Study to Lines 190-192. Motion seconded by Commissioner
Almquist and carried by unanimous vote, with Commissioners Snow,
Almquist and Iverson voting aye.
