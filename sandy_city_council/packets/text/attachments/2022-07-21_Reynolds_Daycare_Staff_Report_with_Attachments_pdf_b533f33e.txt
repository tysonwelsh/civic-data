DESCRIPTION OF REQUEST
The applicant, Kathryn Reynolds, is requesting a Conditional Use Permit to allow for a Category
II daycare which permits up to16 children at one time or a maximum of 18 children per day on
the property located at 10734 South Vermer Way. (See Exhibit #1 – Application Materials). Any
child daycare desiring to operate with more than eight children per day is required to obtain a
Conditional Use Permit for a Category II home occupation from the Planning Commission.

The property is on a 0.46-acre (20,037 square feet) lot with a 3,422 square foot single-family
home. The applicant is proposing to operate Monday through Friday from 7:30 AM to 7:30 PM.
The applicant operates both a half day and full day daycare to stagger the number of children in
her care.

The applicant is currently the only full-time employee, and her husband and older children will
aide with childcare. The State of Utah determines the required ratios for employees to children
based on the age of the child. The applicant is required to obtain a business license through the
state as part of the business licensing process and is aware that she may need to add additional
employees depending on the number and age of children in her care.

The property is located south of 10600 S. on the west side of Vermer Way. It is part of the
Transwest Village Subdivision in the R -1-10 zone. Properties to the north, east, and west are
single family homes zoned R-1-10. The parcel to the south is a horse boarding facility zoned R-

ANALYSIS
Section 21-11-05(F)(3) of the Sandy City Development Code states:
(3) Child Day Care. The following items indicate maximum limits that may be granted by the
    Planning Commission when a child day care is expected to exceed eight at one time:
    a. A maximum of 16 children is permitted at any one time.
    b. A maximum of 18 children is permitted per day.
    c. These numbers shall include the licensee’s and any employee’s children if they are under
       six years of age under the care of the licensee at the time of the home occupation is
        conducted.
    d. A maximum of 24 vehicular stops per day for child drop off to pick up is permitted.

CONCERNS
Staff has concerns regarding the proposed traffic flow pattern as well as the number of proposed
staff. The property is located at the end of a dead-end road which would require daycare patrons
to drop off and pick up by pulling into the driveway and then backing out. The concern with this
is that the Transportation Engineer does not allow backing maneuvers and has proposed the
following alternatives:

   1. That the patrons drop children off on the corner of Lee Lane and Vermer Way. The
      applicant or the parents of the children would then walk the kids to the daycare. This
      location is in the public right of way on a public street, however it is in front of another
      property owners home. The current property owner at 114 E. Lee Lane, Susan Robinson,
      has agreed to this arrangement.
   2. That the applicant installs a circular driveway on her property to allow kids to be dropped
      off without having to do any backing maneuvers on the applicant’s property or making a
      U-turn at the end of the street.

With the first proposed traffic flow plan, staff is concerned with implementing a plan that
requires the use of a space that is not on the applicant’s property. This proposed traffic plan is the
preferred method of traffic flow by the applicant and by some of the adjacent property owners
who want to prevent additional traffic on the dead-end road. Staff is partial to the circular
driveway proposal as it wouldn’t tie any traffic to neighboring parcels and it would keep the
business entirely on the applicant’s property.

Staff’s second concern is regarding the proposed number of employees. The state of Utah
determines the exact ratios of children to caregiver and the applicant is required to meet all of the
state ratio requirements as part of both the Conditional Use Permit and the Business License. Part
of this requirement is that the applicants own children count towards the total children permitted
under a childcare license. Section 21-11-5(9)(a) of the land development states that any of the
licensee’s own children under the age of six are counted towards the total number of children
permitted on her childcare license. The applicant has a number of children which means that she
needs to carefully monitor her ratios of children to caregiver. The applicant is aware that she may
need to hire additional staff depending on the age and number of additional children.

STAFF RECOMMENDATION
Staff recommends that the Planning Commission approve a Conditional Use Permit for Kathryn
Reynolds for the property located at 10734 S. Vermer Way to operate a Category II Home
Occupation as described in the application materials based on the following findings and subject
to the following conditions:

Findings

   1. The proposed use meets the intent of the home occupation section of the Sandy City Land
      Development Code.
   2. The applicant is aware of and is willing to comply with all regulations applicable to this
      type of home occupation.

    6. That the applicant meet all state business licensing requirements for a residential daycare.

        The Conditional Use Permit is being requested for our Home Occupational Business which is In
Home Childcare licensed with the State of Utah’s Childcare licensing. Currently Sandy City will allow only
upto 8 children with a childcare license and without the Conditional use Permit. I currently have 7 of my
own children, with 1 on the way. My family alone would take up all the allotted spaces Sandy City has
allowed and defeat the purpose of getting licensed in childcare. We are asking for the Conditional Use
Permit to increase that allotted amount to 16 children in care at the same time. Note: Childcare
licensing will only allow upto 12 children at any given time if there is only 1 provider on site, in the event
I have another provider, that number can go upto 16 children in the future.

         The hours of operation will fluctuate depending on the needs of the clients, but will be no
earlier than 7:30 am and no later than 7:30 pm. Some clients may need only about 4 hrs. of care, while
others may need upto 9. Childcare will not exceed 9 hrs. on any given day. Children expected to be in
care will be between the ages of 6 months to 13 years old.

         In Home Childcare that is Licensed with the State of Utah should have little to no impact on the
character of the existing home, excepting alterations that will make the home more accessible for the
use of living, specifically living with children. Some alterations will be such as but is not limited to:
putting up fences 4ft high or higher, installing furnace/AC for more efficient regulation of utilities,
possible remodel of rooms in the home for better use, installing playground equipment in the yard.

        In home childcare is beneficial for a community. It allows children in the same family to stay
together and help foster good relationships within the family, whereas in childcare centers children are
separated into different age groups. In home childcare is more affordable than childcare centers helping
families better meet the financial demands of living. Having someone home in the neighborhood during
regular work hours deters burglary and can act as part of a neighborhood watch. These are a few of the
reasons in home childcare is good for the community.
