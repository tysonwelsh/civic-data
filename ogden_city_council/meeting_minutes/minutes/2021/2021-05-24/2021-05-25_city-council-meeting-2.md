                  A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department came before the Council to consider a resolution
        approving an honorary street name designation. The memo stated Anna Keogh submitted a petition to assign an honorary street
        name for a portion of 2nd Street between the Union Pacific railroad tracks and Washington Boulevard. The initially proposed
        honorary street name was "Bingham Fort Lane" in honor of Bingham Fort, which was located in that area. The proposal has since
        been amended to include the name of Chief Little Soldier. The new proposal would assign the honorary name as "Bingham Ft-
        ChiefLittle Soldier Ln." The proposal was reviewed by the Planning Commission and a recommendation of approval for a shorter
        distance was forwarded to the Council. As recommended by the Planning Commission, the honorary street name would apply to
        the portion of 2nd Street between Century Drive and Wall Avenue, which the Planning Cmmnission found to align with the fort's
        historic location more closely. Upon initial review by the City Council, the Council requested that the proposal be reviewed by the
        City's Diversity Commission. This review was completed, and the Diversity Commission forwarded a recommendation of denial
        to the Council. The Diversity Commission's recommendation was based on the belief that the proposal did not accurately
        represent and honor the perspectives of all the peoples of the area at the time. At the Council's February 2, 2021 work session,
        Council members discussed the proposal and the recmmnendation from the Diversity Commission. Council members expressed
        opposition to the proposal as presented. The petitioner requested additional time to develop an alternative proposal that would help
        address concerns expressed by the Diversity Commission. The alternative proposal was reviewed at the April27, 2021 work
        session meeting. The proposal has been amended from the original submittal of"Bingham Fort Lane" to "Bingham Ft-ChiefLittle
        Soldier Ln." The amended name is the cunent proposal; however, if approved, the "Ln" on the end will most likely not appear on
  \     the actual signage due to the limitations of spacing on the signs themselves.
_)                Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and stated used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound
        on the petition. He briefly reviewed the history of honorary street name designation actions at various locations throughout the
        City, after which he briefly discussed the evolution of the subject petition as detailed in the staff memo above. The intent of the
        petition was to honor the original name of this segment of 2nd Street; Bingham Fmt was established around 1850 near the

           Council member Choberka referenced the information in Mr. Montgomery's presentation regarding actions taken in 1889
to implement significant street name changes; she asked for details about that event. Mr. Montgomery stated that Mayor Keisel
was elected in that time frame, and he implemented street name changes that he referred to as the "Americanization of Ogden";
many street names were assigned past Presidents' names and numbered streets were renumbered extending to 2nd Street on the
north end and 25th Street on the south end.
           Council Deputy Director Symes then used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the Council's consideration of
the petition. The Council received the initial presentation about the honorary street name proposal at their November 10, 2020
work session; they directed Council staff to send a letter to the Diversity Commission requesting that they review the proposal and
provide a recommendation. The Diversity Commission discussed the proposal and engaged with members of the public and other
individuals who have unique perspectives on the proposal, including Darren Parry and James Singer. The Diversity Commission
voted on December 9, 2020 to recommend denial of the petition. A letter from the Diversity Commission providing background
on the recommendation was forwarded to the. Council on December 21, 2020. The Commission's letter was accompanied by a
letter from Mr. Singer providing additional context and background on the concerns expressed by the Commission. The Council
reviewed the Diversity Commission's recommendation at a work session on February 2, 2021 and, at that time, was not
comfortable with the proposal as it was presented. The Council allowed the petitioner additional time to work with the community
to see if an alternative proposal could be developed to help address the concerns expressed. The petitioner worked with Mr. Parry
and others to develop an alternative name; the alternative name proposed is: "Bingham Ft-ChiefLittle Soldier Ln". Additional
information about Chief Little Soldier was submitted by the petitioner. If approved, the name change would apply to 2nd Street
between Century Drive and Wall Avenue. The Council reviewed the alternative name at the April27, 2021 work session and felt
that the proposal could be moved to the next step in the process, including a public hearing and fmal consideration. The Council
has received comments both in support of and in opposition to the proposal and those comments have been forwarded to the entire
body. He then presented an image ofhow new street signs would look if the name change is approved, reiterating that the word
'Ln' will most likely be removed to provide needed space for the rest of the street name.

               Jeremy Shinoda, Ogden Diversity Commission, stated that on the surface, the proposal seems 'clean', but when looking
     further into the history of the area, the matter becomes more complex. The addition of 'Chief Little Soldier' to 'Bingham Fort'
     does not ameliorate the controversy and complexity of the issue; the storytelling has been one sided and on behalf of those that
     have been disenfranchised, it is safe to err on the side of being mindful of the problematic symbolism the current naming proposal
     represents. He takes issue with the continued use of what many consider a pejorative tenn for Native Americans in the letters that
     have been written as it shows a lack of cultural awareness. It is possible to be reminded of and aware of the history of the area
     without an honorary street name; he referenced current honorary street names, such as Martin Luther King, Jr. and Caesar Chavez,
     which have set a very high bar for receiving an honorary street name. Honorary means to 'bestow honor upon' and he asked that
     the Council not bestow honor on a name that is steeped in controversy. Through a planning lens, it is clear that the name is
     appropriate. However, through a diversity lens, it is possible to see something quite different. What was lacking up to now was to
     view the proposal through a different lens, but now that the Council has been equipped with this new viewpoint, they getto decide
     and they should take into account the letters provided by Mr. Gibony and Mr. Singer. The Council's consideration should include
     the input of all parties, not just those in favor of the honorary street name. He concluded he hopes the Council decides against
     approving the name change.
                Tammy Creager thanked Mr. Shinoda for his input, but noted she is a proponent ofthe name change and she encouraged
     the Council to vote to support it. She knows that part of what is occurring throughout the nation includes opposition to honorary
     road names, but part of the beauty of America is that many have arrived at their current location by following different paths;
     some descended from indigenous people and others from enslaved people. Some descended from immigrants across the world;
     she is half Chinese and the railroad that was built by the Chinese people is significant to her. Even though she experienced a
     typical Caucasian upbringing in Idaho where she was raised by a rancher and a full-Chinese mother, she identifies as a full
     American and she feels that those opposing the recognition of the cultural richness of the area are causing a significant part of the
     history of the area to be lost. Street names reflect the cumulative, commemorative decisions of municipalities and can be used as
     indicators for social and cultural uniqueness, which is particularly useful given the scarcity of cultural data at the local level. If the
     City does not continue to recognize the value of what has happened in the past, in addition to the value of what is occurring now,
     much is lost. She noted she and her husband have plans to improve the area and commemorate the history of the area; they will
     create a Bingham Fort cormnunity garden that can be used for generations to come while drawing attention to the history of the
