               H.1. MYSTIC SPRINGS EDUCATIONAL WETLAND CENTER SITE
               PLAN
               Address: 947 W. Shields Lane
               File No.: PLSPR202300174
               Applicant: Ana Paz, South Jordan City

Planner Damir Drozdek reviewed background information from the Staff Report.

Commissioner Trevor Darby asked about anticipated uses for the amphitheater.

Planner Drozdek responded there will not be anything like concerts, more for educational
purposes.

Commissioner Nathan Gedge asked about the area designated I-F on the map, and whether that
was industrial.

Planner Drozdek responded that yes, it is marked as industrial.

Commissioner Gedge noted there is a lot of dirt moving on the parcel, and asked if there are any
concerns with multiple projects developing at the same time.

Planner Drozdek said they have no concerns.

City Planner Greg Schindler said the property owners do have a grading permit, but there is no
application or other indication they are developing.

Commissioner Steve Catmull asked if there will be biking amenities like bike racks to lock them
up.

Planner Drozdek said they actually could stay on their bikes throughout this area, and that he was
unsure of those specifics.

Chair Hollist opened the public hearing for comments.

Brian Butters (Resident) – we own the property in the L-shape of the west side of this property.
My only concern is whether there will be any walkways along the private property/residential
area.

Chair Hollist closed the public hearing.

Planner Drozdek said the closest trails are about 130 feet from the resident’s homes on their
property, and the rules require those trails to be at least 100 feet away. He also noted that the
audience in the amphitheater is facing west, the presenter will be facing east.

Planner Schindler added that the area will be closed at night, so there will be no events at that
time.

Associate Director of Public Works Colby Hill discussed the original design, noting that they
moved the trails further from the homes, and that the pavilion is as far away as possible. There
are no plans for projected or amplified music, it is a learning center. There are no seats, it is a
graded hill that will have some grass.

The commission and staff discussed access for larger vehicles like school busses, and there were
no concerns from staff regarding that kind of access.

Commissioner Bevans asked about availability for funding of all three phases.

Director Hill responded there is funding for Phase 1, and much of the funding has come from the
TRCC grant from Salt Lake County.

Commissioner Gedge asked about parking and traffic in the area when busy.

Deputy City Engineer Jeremy Nielson responded that there is sufficient capacity for groups
coming in and out.

Commissioner Darby motioned to approve File No. PLSPR202300174, Allowing for
construction of a Wetland Educational Center at the address above. Chair Hollist seconded
the motion.

Yes – Commissioner Darby
Yes – Chair Hollist
Yes – Commissioner Gedge
Yes – Commissioner Catmull
Yes – Commissioner Bevans

Motion passes 5-0, unanimous in favor.

**Minutes from 9-26-2023 PC Meeting regarding the item below**

       I.      LEGISLATIVE PUBLIC HEARINGS

               I.1. RUSHTON BIKE PARK REZONE, REZONE FROM
               AGRICULTURAL (A-1) TO PARK OPEN SPACE (OS-P) SUBDISTRICT
               Address: 11050 S. Bangerter Hwy.
               File No.: PLZBA202300163
               Applicant: South Jordan City

Chair Hollist motioned for a short recess while staff worked through IT issues.
Commissioner Gedge seconded the motion; vote was unanimous in favor.

Chair Hollist motioned to reconvene the meeting once the issues were fixed. Commissioner
Bevans seconded the motion; vote was unanimous in favor.

Planner Andrew McDonald reviewed background information from the Staff Report.

Commissioner Nathan Gedge asked about the need and reason for a Conditional Use Permit in
this open space area for recreational use, and whether this could be improved in the future by
removing the need for the permit.

Planner McDonald responded that the zone is relatively new.

Chair Michele Hollist asked why this application doesn’t fall under the recent ordinance change
waiving certain requirements when the city is applying to itself for projects.

Planner McDonald responded that this doesn’t apply because it is a rezone.

Chair Hollist asked about potential restrictions on reserving that pavilion or facility.

Deputy City Engineer Jeremy Nielson responded that this is a smaller “pocket park,” which was
previously an HOA park that was turned over to the city; he doesn’t believe the space is
available for reservations. It’s always possible that a group could be interested in reserving the
park for an event or competition, but it was intentionally engineered not to draw things like big
competitions and avoid cars on the road. It is being engineered to be more of an amenity for the
local neighborhood, users on the Welby Canal Trail, etc.

Commissioner Trevor Darby asked about any legal liability for the city in terms of injuries at
these kinds of parks.

Assistant City Attorney Greg Simonsen noted that, aside from the fact that there would be hoops
to jump through because the city is a governmental entity, the city can be liable for negligence
like any other property owner.

Chair Hollist opened the public hearing for comments.
Kenneth Duquett (Resident) – I live two houses down from Highpoint park, and on a side note
Highpoint Park Pavilion is not reservable. It is used by the individuals in the cul-de-sac quite a
bit, but I have tried to reserve it for a birthday party and you can’t. It seems like I went to the
wrong meeting, I should have gone to the one before, but I was curious about what the parking
would be. Finding out that it’s not supposed to bring a lot of car traffic sounds great, as my kids
play in that cul-de-sac. Is there a way to see what the second phase is planning on right now?

Chair Hollist closed the public hearing.

Engineer Nielson noted that, regarding a Phase 2, there is only funding for the currently
presented Phase 1. Potentially in the future there is an opportunity to add a jump line, and it’s
shown on the images in the Staff Report for the potential Phase 2. The city is trying to be very
sensitive to this community, and at this point in time there is no plan to add that jump line or
money set aside for that.

Commissioner Laurel Bevans asked about the anticipated time for completion and details on the
construction.

Engineer Nielson responded it shouldn’t take more than a few months, with a completion
possibly in June.

Commissioner Catmull asked who the target area of the zone is in terms of location, and how
they will address pedestrian safety in the area as roads and traffic change in the future.

Engineer Nielson responded that is the reason for this being close to the Welby Canal Trail,
encouraging the use of that fairly new trail and taking advantage of the connectivity it offers. The
goal is to create connections between the local parks and amenities for those traveling through
the trails and to these destinations.

Commissioner Gedge motioned to forward a positive recommendation to the City Council
on File No. PLZBA202300163, Rezone from Agricultural (A-1) to Park Open Space (OS-P)
Subdistrict, based on the Staff Report and discussion this evening. Chair Hollist seconded
the motion.

Roll Call Vote
Yes – Commissioner Gedge
Yes – Chair Hollist
Yes – Commissioner Catmull
Yes – Commissioner Darby
