Mayor Staggs explained that Councilmember McCay wanted to discuss a community mini
library within the community.

Councilmember McCay introduced her friend, Pam Van Wagoner, who is involved in educating
children in the community and explained that she had invited her to present tonight.

Pam Van Wagoner said that she is the Salt Lake County 4-H Stem and Outreach Coordinator.
She wanted to invite Riverton City to participate in and support little free libraries called “Stem
Spots” which will have Stem books and resources available for residents. The Stem Action
Center will provide the literature and books and Salt Lake County 4-H has budgeted to maintain
the libraries. They are planning on three mini libraries including one in Magna, one in Kearns
and she would like to have one in Riverton.

Mayor Staggs asked if Ms. Van Wagoner is asking for just one location in Riverton and she said
yes.

Ms. Van Wagoner explained that each mini library will have a plaque on the front with the little
stem spot logo and on the side they will include the names of donors.

Councilmember Buroker commented that she had a mini library installed at the Jordan River
Parkway and suggested the City could use the same person to build the new Stem Spot. She
thinks the best place to put a mini library is where people walk and doesn’t think it would be as
productive if people drive and pick up materials. She commented that she is amazed at how
many books go in and come out of this mini library and would be willing to donate materials for
the new mini library.

Councilmember Stewart commented that he also likes the mini library idea and suggested that it
be placed in a central location in the City or suggested having two locations, one central and
one out west.

Councilmember Wells stated that the fire station on 2700 West may be a good location to have
the mini library. Councilmember McDougal agreed with Councilmember Wells about the fire
station and also said that a good place for a second one would be on the trail in Councilmember
Stewart’s district.

Councilmember McCay was also supportive of the idea but wanted to make sure the location
chosen would be safe so that it does not get vandalized or that the supplies taken and not used.

Mayor Staggs said that he believes all of the Councilmembers are supportive of the idea and
thanked Ms. Van Wagoner for coming.

         Ordinance No. 21-27 – Amendments to Section 18.150.120(8) Monument Signs, to
         allow signage incorporated into certain types of fencing to qualify as monument
         signs, amendment proposed by Riverton City

Mr. Lethbridge said this is a proposed amendment to Riverton City Sign Ordinance, specifically
to the section regulating monument signage and is proposed by Riverton City. A request was
made by a business in the City who had enclosed their site in a precast masonry fence to
replace the existing monument, now enclosed within the fencing, with a sign attached to one of
the fence panels. Riverton City Code section 18.150.040 defines a monument sign as a
“freestanding” sign, and there is not specific language in the code that can be interpreted to
allow for signage attached to or cast into a fence. In reviewing potential amendments to sign
ordinance, the Council indicated their willingness to consider allowing use of a fence panel in a
precast fence as a monument sign, and instructed staff to prepare an amendment for
consideration.

         Ordinance No. 21-28 – Amending Section 18.45, RM-6, amending the RM-6 zone to
         individual lot development only, amendments proposed by Riverton City

Mr. Lethbridge said that Riverton City currently has in its code the RM-6 Zone, which allows for
multifamily development at a maximum density of six units per acre. The code allows for both
attached units within commonly held open space, as is more typical with multi-family, or
subdivision of property into individual lots and that in its application of this Zone, Riverton City
has repeatedly only approved it with a Specific Development designation limiting development
to individual lots. Staff is proposing the zone itself be amended to only allow development
utilizing individual lots. This would still allow for both attached and detached units, as allowed
individual lots are specified for both and the standards for individual dwelling lots are already
contained in this section, and so the amendment is primarily deletion of the sections for
common open space developments.

Councilmember Stewart asked for clarification on this ordinance. He said he thought this
ordinance would make RM-6 a single-family zone.

Mr. Lethbridge explained that the language in place in the RM-6 zone is focused on individual
lots. He said that the property on 3600 West 12800 South which was recently rezoned to RM-6
utilized the individual lot standards and still allowed for detached and duplex units. He
explained that the ordinance did not adequately address how duplex units could be utilized on
individual lots. He said from the staff’s perspective they wanted to remove all the language that
expanded beyond the individual lot designation.

Councilmember McDougal said that several projects had a special designation put on them for
single family lots but with the new zoning they could have duplex units throughout. He asked if
the projects already approved could be changed. Mr. Lethbridge said that because there was
an SD designation put on those projects, they cannot be changed.

Councilmember McDougal asked if the Council wanted to approve an RM-6 zone and not allow
for duplexes then they would need to add an SD designation to accomplish that. Mr. Lethbridge
explained that because there are some current developments under the RM-6 that include
duplex units then this language would be appropriate to keep those projects moving forward.

Councilmember McDougal stated that his position is that the City should be leaning toward
single family detached units and the exception should be duplexes. Mr. Lethbridge asked if they

would want the code to state that duplexes could only be allowed under an SD designation.
Councilmember McDougal said he would like RM-6 zoning to be single family detached units
