MINUTES OF THE COTTONWOOD HEIGHTS CITY COUNCIL LEGISLATIVE
WORK SESSION HELD THURSDAY, JANUARY 28, 2021, AT 9:00 A.M. THE

GOVERNOR’S EXECUTIVE ORDER DATED 18 MARCH 2020

1.      Welcome and Determination – Mayor Michael Peterson.

Mayor Mike Peterson called the meeting to order at 9:00 a.m. and welcomed those listening. He
read in its entirety the declaration giving the Council the authority to hold the meeting via Zoom,
pursuant to Utah Code Annotated §52-4-207(4).

2.      Legislative Update and Discussion with City Lobbyists Mr. Greg Curtis, Mr. Brian
        Allen, and Ms. Chantel Nate.

City Lobbyist, Brian Allen, presented a Legislative update and stated that the larger issues being
tracked related to the Billboard Senate Bill that was recently pulled from the agenda. He confirmed
that they will continue to work toward finding a middle ground. He reported that SB-111
protecting records and zoning violations may not become a Bill as there is already a provision in
the Code that addresses the issue.

HB-98 that pertains to building inspections was next discussed. Mr. Allen believed this Bill was
problematic for cities with respect to the inspection and permitting process and confirmed that he
would meet with Paul Ray for further discussion. He reported that the Property Tax Bill was held
by the Committee with challenges. The Bill was never a windfall or replacement for lost revenue.
He believed Senator Cullimore was sympathetic to businesspeople and cities with regard to how
the revenue is reported. They were also watching multiple law enforcement Bills and were
confident that they are being handled correctly.

City Lobbyist, Greg Curtis, reviewed SB-244 regarding transportation and stated that there is an
ongoing revenue stream of approximately $11 million, $2 million of which was intended to go to
Salt Lake County as part of registration. Due to an error, the funds were stranded in a restrictive
fund. He explained that there is a processing Code that states that if the Legislature does not do
anything, it can be given to the County. He believed that the south and west side senators were
not inclined to return it to the County. Bonding was discussed. Mr. Curtis stated that this is a sales

tax fund and as a percentage of the County population, Cottonwood Heights officials would need
to determine if they would rather have one-time monies, which he believed would not be enough,
or approximately $270,000 per year for the next 15 years. It was clarified that the funding cannot
be used on stormwater. He expressed interest in the yearly dedicated revenue over a 15-year
period. Mr. Curtis agreed to pass along the administration’s request and believed that the City of
Cottonwood Heights would potentially receive close to $300,000 per year. If the Bill is successful,
it would essentially deplete the funds for a period of time.

Zoo Arts and Parks (“ZAP”) funding was next discussed. Mayor Peterson reported that Senator
Fillmore’s Bill, SB-88, was voted on by the Board with a 7-to-2 vote in opposition. Mr. Allen
stated that Salt Lake County ZAP funding is distributed in tiers where other counties are allowed
to keep the funding within their municipalities.

In response to a question raised regarding canyon transportation, Mr. Allen confirmed that
allocated funds will be processed through the Utah Department of Transportation (“UDOT”).
Mayor Peterson stated that UDOT has accepted Cottonwood Heights’ Wasatch Boulevard Master
Plan and committed verbally that it would not be a typical highway piece but include enhancements
that such as pedestrian sensitive areas, medians, and landscaping. In conversations with UDOT
officials, Mayor Peterson was informed that their standard was that no more than 1% can go toward
enhancements along that right-of-way and stretch of road. He expressed concern that any
remaining cost would fall back on the municipality.

City Manager, Tim Tingey, understood that UDOT refers to the less than 1% as aesthetic
improvements. Clarification of language was discussed. Mr. Curtis agreed that a portion of the
agreement will need to be worked through aggressively with UDOT. Council Member Bracken
believed the reference to 1% was taken from discussions in prior years and encouraged
clarification.

Open space was next discussed. Mr. Curtis stated that the Governor has included funding for
parks, trails, and outdoor recreation in his budget. The legislature is also supportive of that
funding, although he was unsure of the total amount.

3.      Adjourn.

MOTION: Council Member Bracken moved to adjourn. The motion was seconded by Council
Member Petersen. The motion passed with the unanimous consent of the Council.

The Work Session adjourned at 9:35 p.m.
