 1   In response to Council Member Buroker regarding Resolution No. 20-32, Interim City Manager

 3   within its municipal territory that did not have access to secondary water for irrigation purposes.

13   Riverton’s existing distribution line located at approximately 11600 South 1300 West. He said
14   the Interlocal Agreement that established the relationship between South Jordan and Riverton
15   had expired and needed to be renewed.

28        16.a        Ordinance No. 20-10 - Rezoning properties located near 4185 West 12600
29                    South from PCC (Planned Commercial Center) to C-R (Commercial Regional),
30                    John Linton Representing Holiday Oil, Applicant
31
32   Development Services Direct Jason Lethbridge explained that the public hearing was for a
33   Rezone of approximately two-acres located near 4185 West 12600 South. He said the property
34   was located at the southwest corner of 12600 South and the new 4150 west street, which was
35   currently under construction. He said the property was currently zoned Planned Commercial
36   Center (PCC) and the current General Plan designation was Regional Commercial/Office.
37   Nearby zoning includes PCC to the south and east, RM-14 SD to the west and C-R to the north.
38   He said the current PCC Zone allowed a large variety of commercial uses, including
39   convenience stores with gas stations. He said the requested Commercial Regional (C-R) zone
40   also allowed for convenience stores with gas stations. He said the two zones were very similar
41   in architectural and site plan standards, and both zones would require a fence to be constructed
42   along the shared line with the housing development to the west.
43
44   Mr. Lethbridge explained that the key difference between the two zones was the minimum lot
45   size for a standalone site. He said the PCC Zone required 5-acres, and the C-R zone required
46   2-acres. A site less than the minimum lot size could be developed in either zone if it were part of
47   a larger master site plan, in this instance Holiday Oil would like to begin development sooner
48   than the remaining property. There is also a significantly larger setback requirement in the PCC
49   Zone, as that zone contemplated much larger scale buildings and development. The setback
50   itself in the PCC was set at 40 feet, which was the same as the Commercial Regional Zone, but
51   then in a separate section of the PCC ordinance, Section 18.100.070(3), indicates that “No
     Page | 6 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     April 21, 2020



 1   buildings shall be allowed within 100 feet of a non-compatible zone”. He further explained that,
 2   while the intent of the proposed rezone was not to circumvent any setback requirements, the
 3   additional 60 foot separation requirement was, in staff’s perspective, excessive for the subject
 4   parcel. He said the Commercial Regional Zone was the more appropriate development standard
 5   for a commercial parcel of this size, and for development such as the proposed Holiday Oil.
 6
 7   Mr. Lethbridge said the on March 12, 2020 the Planning Commission held a public hearing on
 8   the rezone application and the Planning Commission voted to recommend approval of the
 9   application.
10

14   opposition of the proposed zone change that were in attendance at the Planning Commission
15   Meeting. However, he said he met with those residents following the meeting and he addressed
16   their concerns.

 5        17.b        Resolution No. 20-31 - To Initiate the Process by which the Riverton City
 6                    Council may Consider Creation of the Riverton Fire Service Area to Fund First
 7                    Responder Services within Riverton City
 8
 9   Interim City Manager Ryan Carter explained that Riverton City has initiated the process to
10   withdraw from the Unified Fire Service Area, which historically has served as the means by
11   which Riverton Residents have paid for First Responder Services provided by the Unified Fire
12   Authority. He said that, while withdrawal remains pending, the time was right to have a
13   discussion about whether the Council desires to create a new service area, to serve as a
14   substitute entity that would levy property taxes to pay for First Responder Services. Assuming
15   the Council desires to proceed, Staff provided a brief summary of steps that Riverton City must
16   take to create a new service area, which staff has informally referred to as the Riverton Fire
17   Service Area.
18
19   Mr. Carter said the proposed resolution would be brought before the Council in the meeting on
20   May 5, 2020. If the resolution is adopted by the Riverton City Council, the following future steps
21   would occur:
22
23        •    Riverton City will post a notice of a public hearing, once a week for 4 consecutive weeks,
24             in a newspaper of general circulation in Riverton City and publish notice on the Utah
25             Public Notice Website of a public hearing which will occur on the date of June 16, 2020.
26             [See: Utah Code Ann. §17B-1-211]
27        •    Riverton City will conduct a public hearing on the date of June 16, 2020, to receive
28             public input on: whether the requested service is needed in the area of the proposed
29             local district; whether the service should be provided by the county of municipality of the
30             proposed local district; and all other matters relating to the proposed service area. [See:
31             Utah Code Ann. §17B-1-211]
32        •    At the conclusion of the public hearing, Riverton City will consider whether to adopt a
33             resolution to approve creation of the Riverton Fire Service Area. [See: Utah Code Ann.
34             §17B-1-212].
35
36   Council Member Stewart explained that if the above process was not approved, the funding of
37   the Fire Service Area would be through the City’s General Fund. He said he recommended the
38   above described process for creating a Riverton Fire Service Area in order to avoid co-mingling
39   Fire Service Area Funds with the City’s General Fund.
