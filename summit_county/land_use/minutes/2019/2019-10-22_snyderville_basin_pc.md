2. Public hearing and possible action regarding proposed amendment to the Silver 
Creek Village Development Agreement; Southeast corner of I-80 and US-40; Matt 
Lowe, applicant– Jennifer Strader, Senior Planner     
 
Planner Strader said the applicant is requesting approval for three amendments to the 
Silver Village Development Agreement (DA).  These relate to: 1) how the height of a 
structure is measured, 2) the maximum height allowed, and 3) the percentage of 
affordable units to market units.    
 
Regrets: Canice Harte, Joel Fine

Planner Strader said these amendments were included in a larger group of amendments 
that were reviewed by the Planning Commission during a work session held in 
February.  Since that time, Staff has determined some of the amendments qualify as 
technical amendments to the DA. They can be reviewed administratively.   
 
The amendments they are discussing this evening relates to Parcel 22.1.  It is an 
agricultural parcel that is located in what is known as the Lot 2 subdivision.  It is also 
referenced as Lot 80.  For ease, she will refer to it as Parcel 22.1.  The development on 
this lot is restricted to townhomes.  The structures are limited to 32 feet.      
 
The first amendment is to change how the height is measured.  Currently the height is 
measured as outlined in the Development Code.  It specifies that height is measured 
from either existing or finished grade, whichever results in the shorter measurement.   
 
The reason for this amendment is due to the location of some old sewer ponds.  The 
applicant is proposing to fill those in to create a more buildable pad.  It will bring the 
grade into alignment with the grade of the existing road.  The amount of fill is estimated 
to range from 8’ to 14’.  Under the existing Code, the height would be measured from 
the grade before any fill dirt is added.   
 
The second amendment would increase the parcel height allowed on Parcel 22.1 from 
32’ to 45’.  This amendment is being proposed so that the previously approved plans for 
the Mountainlands project on Lot 9 can be reused on this lot.  The applicant noted that 
other parcels within the development are allowed a height of 45’.   
 
Planner Strader displayed a slide and pointed out the location of the parcels where a 
height increase is allowed; however, these parcels are located in a more concentrated 
area.  Parcel 22.1 is on the boundary of the Silver Creek Village Center.  She encouraged 
the Commission to keep in mind that in addition to the height request, the applicant is 
also requesting to measure from the requested fill dirt.

The last request is allow the units have a mixture of 65% affordable housing to 35% 
market-rate.  The agreement specifies an upper limit of affordable units; however, there 
are other sections of the agreement that have to be taken into consideration.   One is for 
a variety of unit type and styles.  No more than 60% of a style will be developed 
throughout the project.  She referred to the last sentence of the second paragraph that 
states the workforce units will be generally located throughout the project integrated 
within the community.      
 
Staff believes the purpose of the workforce section of the agreement is to ensure that 
workforce housing is spread throughout the development with a variety of unit types 
and sizes.  Planner Strader said there have been no proposed development plans for 
Parcel 22.1, which means that Staff can’t evaluate the impacts of these amendments.  
Staff has not provided a recommendation.  If the Planning Commission decides to 
forward either a positive or a negative recommendation, findings of fact will have to be 
created.
