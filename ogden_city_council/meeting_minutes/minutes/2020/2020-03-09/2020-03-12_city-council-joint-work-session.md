          The purpose of the joint work session was to discuss an inter local agreement between Ogden City and Ogden School
District pertaining to recreation; hear District plans for new schools; discuss potential City Youth Council/Leadership Academy;
and discuss other topics of general interest.

lnterlocal Agreement - Recreation
          Council Chair Choberka invited Ogden City Recreation Manager Bridge to discuss the concept of developing an
interlocal agreement pertaining to recreation.
          Mr. Bridge stated an internal committee has been created to exainine current recreation offerings of the City and any
barriers to access of recreation prograins that apply to diverse populations of the community. The committee contracted with
Weber State University (WSU) to perform a study to evaluate these barriers and found the four main barriers are: travel, cost,
location, and time. He then noted his staff has reached out to schools in the community to provide basketball mini cainps to
students and they found that many kids' skills were underdeveloped because of limitations on their opportunities to participate.
However, these outreach efforts have gotten kids excited about various pro grains and opportunities in the City and participation
from different areas of the City has increased. This data was exainined by the internal committee who felt it would be meaningful
to offer recreation programs within Ogden schools; this may be the only opportunity that some kids have to participate in
organized sports, which can create many benefits for the youth and the community at large.
          Discussion of this type of program centered on the resources needed to support it in the long term; opportunities for
developing complimentary programs that youth could participate in outside of school hours; any required involvement of the
parents whose children may be participating in the prograin; and volunteer support.
          Mr. Bridge then concluded Ogden City Administration proposes the execution of an interlocal agreement that will
provide for joint use of athletic facilities for sports programming.
          Ogden School District Superintendent Nye stated the interlocal agreement captures the intent of the type ofprograinming
that Mr. Bridge has discussed; he feels shared access to facilities will provide a great benefit to the City and the District alike. The

New Schools
          Progress on New Schools: Ogden School District Superintendent Nye used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
provide the group with a report on progress on school projects that are being funded with bond proceeds approved in the 2018
election; this includes a rebuild of Horace Mann and T.O. Smith Elementary Schools, an overhaul of Polk Elementary, and the
Ben Lomond High School Athletic Facility. The District's construction manager provided infonnation about each of the four
elementary school projects:
               • Wasatch Elementary school will receive a new classroom building to replace the existing campus portables,
                    secure office entryway, new fire alarm and smoke upgrades, along with new kitchen equipment, and the
                    electrical service upgrade. The board also approved the design alternates with improvements to the
                    Kindergarten, Media Center, Administration areas, new carpet and paint in the existing building, and parking
                    with parent drop off areas. The project construction is underway in both the existing building and the new
                    addition. The new addition and improvements are slated to open Fall of 2020.
               • Horace Mann Elementary School. The existing school will be demolished and replaced. The campus is
                    cunently planned to remain open during design and construction. The project was recently approved for
                    construction by the Board and Construction will start in February. The new school is slated to open Fall of
                    2021.
               • Polk Elementary school will retain the existing 1926-27 original building shell but will be made safe
                    structurally and be brought up to modern standards. The remainder of the campus, except the 1993 addition,
                    will be demolished and rebuilt into a 21st century learning enviromnent. Design is scheduled for completion in
                    March. The new school is currently slated for opening in Fall 2022.
               • T.O. Smith Elementary School. The existing school will be demolished and replaced. The new school will be
                    built on the existing campus location, necessitating the closure, and demolition of the existing campus. The
                    school will be a 3-section sized facility. The schematic design is complete and is being evaluated while the
                    architect is progressing into design development. The new school is slated to open Spring or Fall 2022,
                    depending on bid date and other progress decisions.
               • Ben Lomond High School Athletic Facility. Groundbreaking on the project was done in February 2019 and
                    completion/occupancy of the first phase is slated for October. The second phase is demolition of the existing
                    facility, which will be followed by the installation of tennis courts, softball field, and hardscape in April 2021.
          Discussion among the group centered on topics such as potential school boundary adjustments for each of the four
schools; the fiscal impact of increased construction costs; sports programming that can be accommodated at the Ben Lomond
facility; public access to the Ben Lomond facility; and after school programming at any of the new facilities upon project
completion.
          Sidewalk plans for New Schools: Public Services Deputy Director Anderson provided the group with a report of
infrastructure and sidewalk work occuning around these new school projects. Federal funding will be allocated to realign the
intersection of Polk Avenue and 35 th Street near Polk Elementary, install sidewalk, and address stonn water issues in the area.
Additionally, the City received grant funding to extend sidewalk along Harrison Boulevard and 36tl1 Street to provide connectivity
and safe pedestrian routes for students. For Heritage Elementary School, the City received a State grant to fund sidewalk along 2nd
Street and Wall A venue to connect to existing sidewalk used by students. Several other school sidewalks are planned for the near
future and he looks forward to continuing to work with District staff on those endeavors.

Potential City Youth Council/Leadership Academy
          Council Executive Director Eller-Smith provided the group with information about a proposal from the community to
create a Youth Council/Leadership Academy. Ogden City Council staff has discussed the creation of a group that could give local
students in the community access to training regarding a wide range of governmental operations and functions. Council staff
would like to work with the District to identify the target group for this type of position and the criteria for selecting students who
may apply to be part of the program. Ms. Eller-Smith concluded staff feels that the appropriate size of the group is two to three
students from each of the six high schools in the community and that the timing of creation of the group has not been detennined.
She invited the Board members or District staff to reach out to her or other members of Council staff if they have
recommendations pertaining to creation of the group.
