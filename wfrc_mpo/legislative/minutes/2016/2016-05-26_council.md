Budget Committee
PUBLIC HEARING AND ACTION: Adopt Fiscal Year 2017 Budget and Unified Planning
Work Program (UPWP)
Councilman Michael Jensen, Budget Committee Chairman, reported that Council members
were provided with a draft FY2017 Budget and Summary Unified Planning Work Program
(UPWP) in their packet of materials and noted this information was also discussed at the March
24, 2016 Council meeting. He said the draft Budget and Summary UPWP were distributed to
the County Councils of Government for their review and also made available to the public for
review and comment. Councilman Jensen said there have been no changes to the budget from
that which was provided.
The UPWP outlines the work tasks that the WFRC will pursue during FY 2017 in collaboration
with local agencies, UDOT, UTA, and other partners. The UPWP was updated to include
additional input from Utah Transit Authority that included technical changes, definitions and
updates to program work they are going to do over the next few years.
Councilman Jensen said that WFRC is required to provide an opportunity for public comment
and made a motion to open the Public Hearing portion of the meeting to provide for public input
on the FY 2017 Budget and Unified Planning Work Program. Since there were no public
comments, Councilman Jensen moved to close the Public Hearing.
Councilman Michael Jensen made a motion to adopt the Fiscal Year 2017 Budget and Unified
Planning Work Program (UPWP) as proposed. Mayor Brent Marshall seconded the motion and
the motion passed unanimously.
Regional Growth Committee
Information: Wasatch Choice 2050 and 2019-2050 Regional Transportation Plan Goals
The WFRC adopted Regional Goals are a critical element in the work of the Wasatch Front
Regional Council. With the development of a new Wasatch Choice Vision and 2019-2050 RTP,
both efforts look out ten additional years and represent a fundamental update to these products.
The first draft of the Regional Goals relates to existing WFRC RTP Goals, the Wasatch Choice
for 2050 Growth Principles, and federal transportation legislation, MAP-21 and the FAST Act.
Ted Knowlton, WFRC, said the Goals state desired regional outcomes which can be
meaningfully affected by WFRC, our member communities, and partners. They will be
translated into specific and measurable metrics to inform all decisions of WFRC. The Goals will
also be used to understand progress being made along the Wasatch Front toward achieving the
Wasatch Choice 2050 Vision.
Mr. Knowlton discussed the initial results from the ten Small Area Meetings that took place over
the last several weeks. The first round of meetings focused on understanding needs and
objectives in each part of the metropolitan area. The results will inform a working set of Goals
that also reflect feedback from the Regional Growth Committee meeting held on May 19. He
discussed and asked for comments on the following Wasatch Choice 2050 Draft Goals:
1) Economic vitality/ Jobs and opportunities accessible within a reasonable commute; 2) Livable
and vibrant communities; 3) Manageable and reliable traffic conditions; 4) Quality transportation
choices; 5) Safe streets for all anticipated users; 6) Good air quality; 7) Housing choices and a
low cost of living; 8) Accessible parks, green spaces, and recreational opportunities; 9) A
sustainable and healthy natural environment; and 10) A state of good repair.

ACTION: Approve Surface Transportation Program (STP), Congestion Mitigation Air
Quality (CMAQ), and Transportation Alternatives Program (TAP) projects for 2017-2022
TIP; Review Transportation and Land Use Connection (TLC) Program awards
The Wasatch Front Regional Council is responsible for programming three sources of Federal
Highway Administration (FHWA) funding and coordinating those federal programs in the
Transportation Improvement Program (TIP) for the Salt Lake-West Valley and the OgdenLayton urbanized areas. The sources of funding are the Surface Transportation Program
(STP), the Congestion Mitigation and Air Quality (CMAQ) Program, and that Transportation
Alternatives Program (TAP). Trans Com reviewed the results of the evaluation process and the
project recommendations from the Salt Lake-West Valley and the Ogden-Layton Technical
Advisory Committees (TACs) at their April 21, 2016 meeting. A copy of the tables with
recommended projects was included in Council member packets and may also be viewed on
the WFRC website at www.wfrc.org.
Mayor JoAnn Seghini moved to approve the Surface Transportation Program (STP), Congestion
Mitigation Air Quality (CMAQ), and the Transportation Alternative Program (TAP) projects as
presented and as recommended by Trans Com be added to the draft 2017-2022 STP, CMAQ,
and TAP Programs. Mayor Mike Caldwell seconded the motion and the motion passed
unanimously.
The Transportation and Land Use Connection (TLC) Program is a partnership between WFRC
and Salt Lake County to help communities address their most challenging planning needs,
integrate land use and transportations considerations, and improve growth and transportation
outcomes. The TLC Program provides staff time, consulting, and training to local communities
for planning, implementation, and visioning efforts.

Julia Collins, WFRC, said that the TLC Program is in its third year and reported that 32 letters of
intent were received from 23 municipalities or townships. There were $1.3 million in requests for
assistance for both the Ogden-Layton and Salt Lake-West Valley urbanized areas. Project ideas
range from ordinance support, town center specific area planning support, Transit Oriented

from across the region.
Ms. Collins said there are twelve TLC Program recipients for FY2017 and highlighted the

on the WFRC website at www.wfrc.org.
Air Quality Committee Report
Mayor JoAnn Seghini, Air Quality Committee Chairman, reported the following:
• Tier 3 vehicles will be on the market by this fall. Look on the window sticker for a smog
rating of eight or higher.
• Tesoro is moving forward with Tier 3 fuel production by 2020. Sinclair out of Wyoming will
also be providing low sulfur Tier 3 gasoline to the Utah market.
• The annual Clear the Air Challenge will be held in July. This is a great opportunity to get
involved and informed about improving air quality. Attendees were encouraged to register
and participate in the 2016 Clear the Air Challenge at http://cleartheairchallenge.org.
Active Transportation Committee Report
Mayor Mike Caldwell, Active Transportation Committee Chairman, highlighted the following
information:
• Our region submitted a TIGER grant application, led by UTA that requests $28 million
specifically for active transportation. The grant focuses on first-last mile connections to
transit within communities from across the region. Grant decisions will be made late summer
2016.
• The kickoff of the Mobile Active Transportation Tours (MATT) was on May 11 in Bluffdale
City. The ride was a success with over 25 participants. The next MATT will be June 24,
2016 in Logan and will look at how to connect to recreation using active transportation.
• UDOT has hired Heidi Goedhart to fill the Active Transportation Manager role. She is taking
over for Evelyn Tuddenham.
Cameron Diehl, Utah League of Cities and Towns, reported that the League would be hosting
one of the Mobile Active Transportation Tours on September 15 as part of their Planners Day
during their September Conference scheduled for September 14-16 in Salt Lake City. He
invited Commissioners and County Councils to participate.
