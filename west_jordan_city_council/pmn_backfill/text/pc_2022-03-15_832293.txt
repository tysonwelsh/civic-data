2.     Elevated Care Day Program; 5500 West Bagley Park Road, Unit K; Conditional Use
       Permit for Adult Day Care, general; P- F Zone; Elevated Care LLC/ Travis Lemperle
         applicant) [#26613;   parcel 26- 01- 377- 011]


Travis Lemperle, applicant, explained their program for special needs adults that provides
opportunities for community involvement and to help accomplish personal goals. They want to rent
one of the vacant buildings at West Ridge academy for their business meetings and activities. Clients
are provided transportation to the meeting location and then they usually go out into the community

Elevated Care is on the property the youth will be in school, so there is no interaction.

Mark Forsythe said the building is located east of West Ridge Academy' s main administrative
building. The P- F zone is primarily intended for government buildings and large- scale utilities, but also
for uses that do a social or public good. There should be no impacts to the surrounding residential
neighborhoods. There is a 6- foot masonry wall around the campus. The building was formerly a
residential dorm room converted to a common space for gathering, meetings, classes, etc. The other
part of the program takes place off-site. Since the building is changing use, parking requirements for
striping and accessibility have to be provided.

Staff recommended that the Planning Commission approve the Conditional Use Permit for the
Elevated Care Day Program, located at 5500 West Bagley Park Road in a P- F zone, with the

                                                          why he couldn' t build the fence on the property line.

The code does not explain the reason for the requirement, but it may have been for road widening. The
owner was concerned that he could not then use the 7, 000 square feet of property outside of the fence.
The amendment would allow for a fence to be built on the right-of-way line.

Duncan Murray said if there is a bona fide issue of a road widening based upon a master planned
street, it is better to handle it through other mechanisms in place such as with development of a master
planned road or a boundary street, and not through an ordinance like this.

Larry Gardner explained that this code only applies in commercial, professional office, and industrial
zones. This amendment will not affect the arterial streetscape ordinance and is not the same issue.


Based on the analysis and findings contained in the staff report, staff recommended that the Planning
Commission forward a positive recommendation to the City Council for this application.

Kent Shelton asked if the new ordinance would still allow for the buffer to remain in place if a road is
scheduled for expansion.


Duncan Murray said there are multiple provisions in the city code that would still accomplish this: 1) if
there is a master planned road in a proposed development, then the developer has to build it; 2) a
developer has to build boundary roads; 3) requirements for landscaping along arterial roads in new
development. These provisions already deal with road installation. The code being addressed tonight is
more general and is redundant because there are already ordinances that address road expansion, etc.
This amendment helps to remove a provision that could be problematic for the City.

Kent Shelton was grateful for staff in being proactive and preserving land use rights.

The amendment would not usurp any safety requirements such as line of sight.

2- 1- 4: TERMS; TERM LIMITS; AND TRANSITION TO COUNCIL- MAYOR FORM OF
MUNICIPAL             GOVERNMENT:

  A. Committee Member Terms: The terms for each member of a committee shall be three
3) calendar years, unless otherwise specifically provided by law. The initial terms for
members of newly created committees may be shortened or lengthened by the city council, so as
to achieve a staggering of term expirations and to maintain continuity of the committee. The term
of each committee member shall expire: 1) on December 31 of the last year of the expiring term;
or 2) in the circumstance where city residing is a requirement, the date when the said member
ceases to have primary domicile in the city.
  B.        Support Organizations: Support organizations are separate legal entities and the terms and
elections of trustees/ directors/ managers shall be as provided in the entity' s organizational
documents and in applicable Utah law.
  C.        Term Limits:
       1.    Basic Term Limit: Committee members may only serve for two ( 2) consecutive three ( 3)
year terms and/ or parts of terms on any one committee.
    2.       Six ( 6) Year Service Limit: No committee member may be appointed or reappointed if
the consecutive years of service would exceed six ( 6) consecutive calendar years on that
committee. For purpose of the six ( 6) year limitation, service for part of a year shall be deemed
service for a full year; further, service rendered on a committee to fill the unexpired term of
another member shall be included in the six ( 6) year limitation calculation.
    3.       Reappointment After Service Break: A qualified person may be reappointed to the same
committee after serving six ( 6) consecutive years if there has been a break in service of at least
one calendar year.


    4. Council Waiver: Notwithstanding the provisions in subsections Cl through C3 of this
section, and upon the recommendation of the mayor, the city council may waive the application
of these provisions as to any committee member by resolution of reappointment expressly
waiving the application of these provisions.
    5.       Support Organizations: Board members and officers of support organizations are elected
annually, and any limit on the number of years such a board member or officer may serve shall
be provided in the entity' s bylaws.
 D. Transition To New Form Of Government: The terms for each member of each committee
appointed before the change of government effective date shall be unaffected by the change to
the council- mayor form of municipal government ( see definitions in section 1- 3- 2). This
subsection shall not be construed to limit the mayor's authority, after the change of government
effective date, to terminate         or   remove a committee member pursuant        to section 2- 1- 18. ( 2001

2- 1- 6: QUALIFICATIONS;            RESIDENCY REQUIREMENTS:
Unless otherwise specifically provided in this title, the following conditions and restrictions
apply to all committees and support organizations:
  A. Residency:
       1.
       Committee Members Generally Residents: Except as expressly provided otherwise,
voting membership on any committee or support organization is limited to residents of the city
appointed by the mayor, with the advice and consent of the city council, who are persons
eighteen( 18) years of age or older.

       2. Business And Property Owners: For purposes of committee voting membership, an
owner of a business located in West Jordan and an owner of property located in West Jordan,
even though the owner does not actually reside in West Jordan, are deemed residents of the city.
  B.        Nonresidents:
       1.
             Nonresident Nonvoting Members: Nonresidents may be appointed as nonvoting members
if the mayor finds that special expertise, unique needs of the committee or support organization,
or special circumstances make such a nonresident appointment to be in the public interest.
       2. Nonresident Voting Members: Committees with five (5) or fewer members may include
one nonresident voting member and committees with more than five ( 5) members may include
two ( 2) nonresident voting members. Committees may seek special consideration for additional
nonresident voting members from the mayor in extraordinary circumstances.
       3.
             Nonresident Members Prohibited: Nonresidents may not be appointed to the planning
commission        or   West Jordan youth council.   (   2001 Code § 2- 6- 107; 2009 Code § 2- 1- 7; amd.
Ord. 10- 08, 2- 24-2010; Ord. 10- 12, 4- 28- 2010; Ord. 11- 04, 2- 9- 2011; Ord. 19- 16, 4- 10- 2019,

WEST JORDAN PLANNING COMMISSION RULES QUIZ




T   F 1. It is mandatory under state law for every city to have a planning commission.

T   F 2. According to the Open and Public Meetings Act, every planning commission meeting
       should have an agenda and minutes.

T   F 3. According to state law, planning commission rules may be adopted which change
       some of the provisions of the Open and Public Meetings Act.

T   F 4. Regarding a city' s public meetings, efficiency is more important than transparency.
T   F 5. In addition to the Open and Public Meetings Act, there are a lot of mandatory rules in
       the Utah Code Ann. for cities as to how public meetings should be held.

T   F 6. A city council or planning commission may choose to not adopt public meeting rules.

T   F 7. A city council has discretionary authority to adopt almost whatever public meeting
       rules they want to adopt.

T   F 8. In the City of West Jordan, the planning commission may adopt public meeting rules
       consistent with the Utah Code and City Code.

T   F 9. Good public meeting rules are generally simple to understand, provide for civility, and
       plan for adversity.
T   F 10. The purpose of a planning commission public hearing is only to inform the public;
      the purpose is not for the public to inform the planning commission.
T   F 11. In the City of West Jordan, under exigent circumstances, and if technology is
      available and functioning, a planning commission member may participate electronically
      in a planning commission meeting.
T   F 12. Under current planning commission rules in the City of West Jordan, for a given
      development application on the agenda, the application/ proposal is normally introduced
      by the chair, then staff presents its findings and recommendations, then the applicant/
      developer presents the application/ proposal, and then the public hearing is held.
