PUBLIC HEARING: INTENTION TO DESIGNATE A SPECIAL ASSESSMENT
AREA IN THE HARMONY HEIGHTS SUBDIVISION

Washington County Engineer Todd Edwards explained that it has been proposed to pave
the area known as the Harmony Heights Subdivision which would then be designated as
the Washington County Harmony Heights Paving Project Special Assessment Area. Cost
estimates have been prepared and were mailed to the residents of the area to determine the
desire to create the Assessment Area.

Washington County Deputy Attorney Eric Clarke further explained that if approved, a lien
would be recorded on all affected properties and the cost would be included as part of the
property owners’ tax bill. A public hearing must be held as part of the process, after which,
Washington County Commission

any property owner within the Assessment Area may submit a written protest within sixty
(60) days. If forty percent (40%) of the owners protest the area, the County Commission
may not take action in regards to implementing a Special Assessment Area. Property
owners that do not directly have frontage access to the road will be assessed at half the cost
of owners with frontage, and consequently protests from these owners will be weighed at
50% of protests from owners with frontage to the proposed project.

MOTION: Motion by Commissioner Iverson to Open the Public Hearing. Motion
seconded by Commissioner Almquist and carried by unanimous vote,
with Commissioners Cox, Iverson, and Almquist voting aye.

Peter Rowley spoke against the Special Assessment Area mentioning that he and his wife
enjoyed the rural feeling of the area and mentioned that paving the road would destroy that.
He also mentioned that the notice sent to residents was confusing and would like further
clarification.

Richard Firmage spoke against the Special Assessment Area and stated that not everyone
opposed to the road is opposed to change, however, they also enjoy the rural feeling of the

area.

John Lee expressed concerns with his property’s potential double assessment, mentioning
that his property was recently rezoned to be combined into one (1) parcel.

Pete Odella spoke in favor of the Special Assessment Area mentioning that the road is in
need of maintenance due to its current condition.

Daniel Powers spoke in favor of the Special Assessment Area mentioning the severity of
the dust caused by the current road.

No one else came forward to speak in the public hearing.

MOTION: Motion by Commissioner Almquist to Close the Public Hearing.
Motion seconded by Commissioner Iverson and carried by unanimous
vote, with Commissioners Cox, Iverson, and Almquist voting aye.
