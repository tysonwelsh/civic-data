NEW BUSINESS

Jason Hindman - Fence Height Exception Request

Zachary Smallwood presented the request for a fence height exception at the property addressed at
6010 South Afton Avenue, located in the R-1-8 zone. Mr. Smallwood said that Mr. Hindman is
requesting to increase his fence height from six feet (6’) to eight feet (8’). City zoning code
17.64.090(D) does allow for the Planning Commission to grant additional fence height for buffer
fencing between commercial and residential zoning districts to a maximum of eight feet. Mr.
Smallwood stated that planning staff don't currently have a mechanism to bring this kind of request
to the Planning Commission. The City Attorney’s office recommended proceeding with a text
amendment to allow residents to go up to eight feet when adjacent to a commercial zoning. The
process for the text amendment to be approved could take time. For this application, the City
Attorney’s office suggested to process it as a new business item. Staff are recommending that the
Planning Commission approve Mr. Hindman's request for a rear-yard fence height exception up to
eight feet for the property located at 6010 South Afton Avenue, as allowed in code 17.64.090(D) for
fence height exceptions.

Mr. Smallwood and the commissioners discussed what formal paperwork should be required for the
Planning Commision to sign off on the request. Mr. Smallwood said he would discuss that with the
City Attorney.

Commissioner Hristou made a motion that the Planning Commission approve Mr. Jason Hindman’s
request for a rear-yard fence height exception up to eight feet for the property located at 6010
South Afton Avenue, as allowed in section 17.64.090(D) Fence Height Exceptions.

Vacating Arrowhead Lane Condominiums Amended & Amending and Extending the Pontiac
Subdivision - Project # 24-052

John Morris was present to represent this request. Mr. Smallwood presented the application
requesting approval to vacate the Arrowhead Lane Condominiums as well as amend and extend the
Pontiac Subdivision. He showed a map of the properties and existing plat, which are located in the
G-O, General Office zone, as well as the boundaries after the subdivision amendment. He said that
the condominium subdivision will be removed and vacated. Two lots will remain, under the
ownership of UEA (Utah Education Association) and Valcom Properties. He said that the property
already meets the requirements of the G-O zone. This request is a legal requirement to change the
ownership and property boundaries. Staff recommends approval of the property amendment and
extension.

Commissioner Henrie asked Mr. Smallwood about the underground power line. He wanted to know if
that will affect the power easement.

Mr. Smallwood said there is not an easement where the power line is currently. The power connects
to the building. It isn’t part of this application, however.

Commissioner Milkavich asked where the engineering and county comments are located for the
applicant to review.

Mr. Smallwood said those are on the city website but will also be provided directly to applicant as
part of the review. The applicant will then be required to address each comment.

John Morris, representing UEA, approached the podium. Vice Chair Hacker asked if he had
reviewed and can comply with the conditions. He said that he could.

Vice Chair Hacker opened the public comment for this agenda item.

Glen Bancroft asked for clarification on the purpose of the application. He said that they’ve gone
through two phases already and is trying to understand this phase. He asked if there would be
construction involved and new buildings.

Mr. Smallwood said they don't have an application for any new buildings or construction at this time.
The purpose of this request is to clean up property lines that were previously held by multiple
property owners. Eventually UEA may come back and submit an application for site improvements
and parking improvements.

Vice Chair Hacker closed the public comment period for this agenda item.

Commissioner Hristou made a motion that the Planning Commission grant approval to vacate the
Arrowhead Lane Condominium amended subdivision and to amend and extend the Pontiac
Subdivision on the properties located at 864, 872, 874, 875, 878, and 887 East Arrowhead Lane,
subject to the following conditions.

DISCUSSION ITEMS

Planning Commission Policies, Procedures, and Protocols Review
Review and Recommendations for an Update to the Policies, Procedures, and Protocols

Mr. Smallwood presented training about the policies, procedures, and protocols of the Planning
Commission. He said this guide is meant to help commissioners in their roles. He directed the
commissioners to the guide provided in the packet.

He discussed the protocol for meeting adjournment, stating that it does not require a second or a
vote. He also said that, when a commissioner votes, “no” on an application, they are permitted to
provide an explanation as to why.

Vice Chair Hacker requested that he’d like to have Mr. Smallwood add a section with information
about each commissioner, such as the district they represent.

Mr. Smallwood and the commissioners had a discussion regarding the section stating, “provided by
law.” They agreed that the correct verbiage should be“Provided by the Open Public Meetings Act”
under the administrative section.

Mr. Smallwood, the commissioners, Mark Richardson (the city deputy attorney) had a discussion
regarding findings of fact presented during a meeting. Ideally, the findings of fact for a given agenda

item are supposed to be presented during the same meeting as the agenda item is presented, not
the following meeting. They discussed keeping the procedure as it is.

A discussion was had regarding the procedure when both the chair and vice chair absent. They
determined that a procedure should be added.

The commissioners and Mr. Smallwood discussed the policy regarding the chair not being able to
succeed themselves in office. They discussed that the policy wasn’t clear regarding the office of vice
chair, but that they’d prefer to have both positions have the same policy.

The commissioners discussed the amount of time for the premeeting and what should be discussed
during that meeting. They decided that the bulk of the discussion regarding agenda items should
take place openly in the planning commission meeting. Discussion items can include such things as
typos in minutes or questions about roles as a commissioner. They agreed that the amount of time
allowed for the premeeting is generally appropriate. On rare occasions, the chair may call for a
longer premeeting.

The commissioners and Mr. Smallwood discussed the procedure for meetings with a large number
of agenda items when approaching the end of the meeting with some items not having been
presented. Mr. Smallwood informed them that they are required to hear items until 10 p.m. At which
time, they must finish hearing the agenda item that’s being presented and then may conclude the
meeting. A motion can be made to table the additional items until the next meeting. They may
choose to go past 10 p.m. if a unanimous vote is passed.

Mr. Smallwood and the commissioners discussed changing the order of business in a meeting. A
vote isn’t required, but they agreed that it is a good idea.

A discussion was had regarding voting an application down. They agreed to remove the section
stating that the commissioners need to read the appeal process during the meeting. They said that
the application will receive a letter of denial with information on how to appeal.

Commissioner Pehrson asked Mr. Smallwood to specify which types of votes are roll call versus
verbal majority, stating the verbal majority vote could be used more often to save time.

The commissioners and Mr. Smallwood had a discussion about following Robert’s Rules and how
strictly to follow those. They agreed to keeping the term to “generally” follow in the procedures.
Then they don’t have to adhere strictly to those rules for meetings.

A discussion was had regarding the wording on record keeping of the minutes. The commissioners
agreed that it wasn’t necessary to explicitly state where the minutes would be kept or for how long.
They agreed that keeping the wording more general and stating that the minutes are kept in
accordance with state law was appropriate.

A discussion was had regarding the planning commission not accepting public information at a
meeting. Mr. Smallwood said he’ll review this requirement with Mr. Richardson, he feels it’s
inconsistent to allow residents to make a public comment at a meeting, but if they provide their
comment in writing it needs to be submitted in advance. He wondered if allowing staff to review
written submissions during the meeting would be acceptable.
