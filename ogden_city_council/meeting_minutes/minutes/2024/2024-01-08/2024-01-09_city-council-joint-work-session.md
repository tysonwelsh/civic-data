          The purpose of the joint work session was to review the agenda for the special City Council meeting scheduled to begin
at 6:00 p.m.; receive an Administrative update and Planning update; discuss zone text amendments pertaining to group dwellings;
discuss the Fiscal Year (FY) 2023-2024 first quarter financial report; discuss Council/Board business; and hear Council/Board
comments.

Agenda Review
        Chair Choberka and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the special City Council
meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Administrative Update
         Mayor Nadolski discussed his participation with 27 newly elected Mayors across the United States to participate in a
program offered by Bloomberg Center for Cities at Harvard University. The program is called Program for New Mayors: First 11
Days. The cost for his participation in the program was free of charge and he has been selected as one of 13 Mayors given the
opportunity to host teams of three students from Harvard’s Kennedy School of Government who will study the City and prepare a
report with their recommendations and findings for the upcoming State of the City address.
         Chief Administrative Officer Brown provided the Council with an Administrative update; she introduced Utah Transit
Authority (UTA) Regional General Manager Camille Glenn, who made a presentation regarding an update to the UTA website
and the Ogden Express project and route. Ms. Brown then discussed the City’s efforts to advertise the OGX routes and
connections to points of interest throughout the community.
         Ms. Brown then provided an update on the demolition of the building on 25th Street, which was found to be unsound and
unsafe; the contractor has been working with City Administration to determine what changes need to be made to the building in
order to gain City approval for its reconstruction.
         Mayor Nadolski reported the matters that he has been working on over the past several days since being sworn into
office; he has enjoyed getting to know City employees better and preparing for the upcoming 2024 Legislative Session. His
emphasis will continue to be on developing meaningful partnerships in the community.

Planning Update
         Planning Manager Brierley provided the Council with an update regarding projects underway in the Planning Division of
the City as well as the status of various development projects in the community. There was brief discussion among the Council
and Mr. Brierley regarding the Council’s involvement in the General Plan update project, with Council member Hyer asking for
consideration of regulations imposed on restoration of historic properties in the Plan.

Zone Text Amendments – Group Dwellings
          Senior Planner Rypien used a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the proposed amendments. Mike Bastian submitted
a petition to update the City’s group dwelling ordinance in order to provide better standards for development. Group dwelling
developments are those that have more than one residential building on a lot, and which are planned as a single development. The
group dwellings ordinance, Chapter 10 of the City’s zoning code, provides standards for the development of small lot group
dwellings, neighborhood group dwellings, and mixed-use group dwellings. The petition indicates updated regulations would allow
for the development of a better multiple family residential product. The amendments being proposed address the issues in the
petition as well as other elements of the group dwelling ordinance such as updated definitions, changes to lot area requirements,
setback and building orientation amendments, landscaping requirement changes, amendments to parking requirements, open space
and recreation space updates, and the removal of density bonuses for group dwellings. The ordinance itself also contains a number
of grammatical updates and changes to the wording of certain sections which are not substantive and do not change the underlying
policy within this section of code.
          For a copy of the PowerPoint presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Discussion among the Council and Mr. Rypien took place throughout the presentation regarding topics such as setbacks,
driveway depths, building heights, building types allowed in a group dwelling project, the percentage of a dwelling unit that can
be dedicated to the garage, required open space in a group dwelling project, and the minimum property size needed to qualify for a
group dwelling project.
          The Council also heard from the petitioner, Mr. Bastian, regarding the catalyst for this proposal; he indicated that he
believes these changes will provide for better multi-family projects that will benefit the community in the long term versus
standard townhome projects allowed on multi-family zoned properties. This led to a discussion among the Council, staff, and Mr.
Bastian regarding the implications of the proposed text amendments, after which Council Deputy Director Symes indicated if no
additional discussion is needed, the item can be scheduled for action on January 23. The Council indicated a desire to discuss the
proposal further; Council member Graf stated he would like to have more discussion about the different housing types listed in the
proposed ordinance amendments. Council member Hyer agreed.

Fiscal Year 2023-2024 First Quarter Financial Report
         Management Services Director Stout used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to present the City’s first quarter FY2024
financial report; the report summarizes the City’s budgetary expenditures, revenues, and general financial information as of
September 30, 2023. Revenues are projected to meet or be slightly below budget.
         For a copy of the PowerPoint presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
         Vice Chair Richey asked if the Council will be receiving an opinion from the auditor regarding the matter of annual
enterprise fund transfers and what the impact would be to the City’s budget and financial condition of those transfers ceased in
future years. Council Executive Director Eller-Smith stated she reached out to Administration about that subject before Christmas
and the auditor will have information available to the Council soon.

Council/Board Business

the Council with a brief overview of his professional and personal background.
        Council Calendar: Ms. Eller-Smith reviewed the Council calendar for the month of January.


         The meeting adjourned at 5:58 p.m.
