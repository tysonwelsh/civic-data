Community and Economic Development Project Update
Community and Economic Development Director Cooper used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide
Department project updates; he started with information about the Quality Neighborhoods Initiative, reminding the Council of the
purpose of the program. The goal is to facilitate a wide variety of housing types across a spectrum of affordability. He noted a
recent Weber County Housing Study provided statistics about housing; the current median household income in Ogen is $56,369,
compared to $63,046 in Riverdale and $75,717 in Weber County. Housing types provided in Ogden can be classified as owner-
occupied and renter occupied, with 4.8 percent of the City’s housing units vacant. Ogden has the most diverse housing stock in
Weber County. Ogden provides 65 percent of Weber County's affordable housing, far more than any other individual
municipality. Roy has the next highest at 6.5 percent. Mr. Cooper provided the definition of affordable housing, noting that Ogden
has a surplus of 4,452 housing units that can be defined as moderate housing. The City has a deficit of 3,000 units that are 100 to
125 percent of the average median income (AMI) marker. He then discussed the Own in Ogden program, which provides down
payment assistance between $10,000 and $20,000 for those looking to purchase a home with a purchase price of $380,000 or less.
Senate Bill (SB) 40 was recently passed and can be considered complimentary to the Own in Ogden program; the new purchase
price limit is $450,000 and first home buyers are eligible to receive an additional downpayment assistance loan. He then
concluded Ogden City provides more diversity of housing across a broader range of affordability than any other city in Weber
County. Weber County Housing Authority is primarily focused on Ogden City, and he would encourage all elected officials to
\ communicate to the Weber County Housing Authority that Ogden City already provides its fair share of low to moderate income
_-” housing and their efforts should be focused on other areas of Weber County.
Discussion among the Council and Mr. Cooper centered on Own in Ogden program guidelines and the validity/currency
of the data included in the most recent housing study.

Proposed Text Amendment — Airport Commercial Zone

Senior Planner Rypien explained the current proposal is to amend the Zoning Ordinance for the Airport Commercial (A-
C) Zoning District, removing limitations on the maximum floor area of a building that can be used for manufacturing and storage
of materials within an enclosed building, and allow warehousing and distribution uses within buildings that are 50,000 square feet
or less within the district. The ordinance is revised only by omitting two sentences within the seventh item under "Manufacturing".
He presented a map to orient the Council to the three properties, two of which are owned by City — all surrounding the Ogden
Airport — that are currently assigned the Airport Commercial zoning designation. The applicant and petitioner, Asilia Investments,
owns 30.35 acres of Airport Commercial (A-C) Zoned property on the south side of the Ogden-Hinckley Airport. This property is
home to Northrup Grumman and Ogden Gateway Center, which includes Kemp Jet Services. Having previously met with Ogden
Administrative staff, Asilia Investments is proposing to amend the allowed land uses in the A-C zone due to issues in developing
remaining property within those uses. The proposed amendment removes limitations on how much of a building’s floor area can
be used for manufacturing and indoor storage of materials. The proposal also allows warehousing and distribution in buildings that
are 50,000 square feet or less and does not restrict or limit Ogden City from developing its property according to the Ogden-
Hinckley Master Plan and according to previous agreements with the FAA. Community and Economic Development Staff support
the proposed amendments to the A-C zoning district’s land uses. Mr. Rypien reported the Planning Commission reviewed this
item on January 4, 2023, and voted to recommend approval of the zoning ordinance amendment. The Commission found that the
proposal was consistent with General Plan, the purposes of the zoning ordinance, and state and federal laws. Commissioner
Schade was the lone no vote on the item, explaining that he felt that efforts had been made to remove warehousing from around
the Airport, and it would not be advantageous to allow warehousing in the A-C zoned properties. No public input was received in
the PC meeting on January 4, 2023.

Council member Hyer noted he was a member of the Planning Commission when the original A-C zone was created and (
at that time it was important to maintain the opportunity for aeronautical or ancillary uses around the Ogden Airport property; he
is concerned about changing that precedent and relaxing the code. Mr. Rypien stated that Commissioner Schade shared Council
member Hyer’s concerns. He noted, however, that the applicant and the City have both tried unsuccessfully to recruit businesses
that do support aeronautical uses; for that reason, the idea is to expand the zone to allow for other uses that may be compatible to
the Airport, but are not necessarily aeronautical in nature. The alternative is to allow the property to sit vacant for another 10 or 20
years. Council member Hyer stated he understands the need for complementary uses, such as restaurants or retail centers. He
asked if that is what City Administration envisions with this text amendment. Community and Economic Development Director
Cooper noted that Administration contemplates three primary uses at the Airport: commercial air service, general aviation, and
commercial passenger service. Previous to now, the City has been constrained to locating all of those uses on the east side of the
Airport property, but with the opening of the west side of the Airport, other options are available for supporting the mission of the
Airport. He added that there is a small portion of the Airport that is located in Roy City and development can occur on that
triangle based upon the land use code of Roy; Ogden City is attempting to get all Airport land located in Ogden City, but until that
occurs Administration wanted to be flexible and allow other types of development that can be complementary to the Airport.

Council member White asked if this zone text amendment will violate the City’s own Title Eight, which limits businesses
that are not aeronautically appropriate. Mr. Cooper answered no; Title Eight applies to land owned by the City that is located
inside the Airport boundary while the zone text amendment applies to land outside the Airport boundary.

Council Executive Director Eller Smith asked if the Airport Community Reinvestment Area (CRA) will impact any of
the properties identified by Mr. Rypien. Mr. Cooper stated that the CRA includes all Airport property that is inside the Ogden City
boundary; the small triangle of property that is in Roy City will not be included in the CRA.

Chair Choberka invited input from the applicant. Brad Boardman, Asilia Investments, stated Asilia has partnered with the
Kemp family, who has owned property near the airport for some time. The idea is not to build distribution centers, but to develop

but there is some ambiguity, and he has worked with staff to propose a text amendment that provides clarity. He then noted that
his site is not directly adjacent to the Airport but is to the south along 4400 South. He is not sure who or what the end user will be,
but reiterated he is seeking a bit of clarity and flexibility relative to the types of uses that can be located on the properties.

Proposed Honorary Street Name Ordinance
Council Deputy Director Symes explained Council staff has drafted a proposed honorary street name ordinance response ;
to a Council initiative from Chair Choberka. The ordinance was developed to provide standards for the review and consideration
of honorary street names within Ogden City. He noted Chair Choberka’s initiative asked for consideration of how and who is able
to make request in collaboration with the Diversity Commission and to incorporate the Diversity Commission’s recommendation
on the issue. The Diversity Commission’s recommendation was to revise the ordinance to create equity and inclusion in the core :
of the street naming process. Mr. Symes used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the current City ordinance
governing the honorary street naming process and offered a comparison between the current ordinance and Council staff's
proposed ordinance; key elements of the proposed ordinance include the following:
e Allows Administration to propose honorary street names.
e Allows for Council initiative to propose honorary street names.
e Allows a petition by Ogden City resident.
* Uses categories similar to Landmarks for historic resource designation: Historical, Cultural, Neighborhood -
significance.
e Provides general naming standards and standards for honoring individuals or groups.
e Provides standards for Diversity Commission review and involvement.
e Outlines costs and responsibility for installation.
Additionally, the proposed ordinance addresses the Diversity Commission’s review of an honorary street name proposal;
an application may be submitted to the Diversity Commission, but that is not a requirement. He noted the language in the
ordinance has been changed from ‘shall’ to ‘may’ relative to submitting the application to the Diversity Commission and that is
due to a split among Council members regarding whether Diversity Commission review should be mandatory or optional. Based
upon the draft proposal, the Diversity Commission is able provide input on the positive and potentially negative impacts including
\ historic and cultural influences that may affect how the name is perceived by minority and historically marginalized individuals or
/ communities. The Diversity Commission has communicated a preference for their review to be required.
Chair Choberka stated she is willing to compromise on changing the language from ‘shall’ to ‘may’ relative to soliciting
a recommendation from the Diversity Commission due to the fact that she feels strongly that an ordinance needs to be adopted to
regulate this issue. Council member Blair stated he is also comfortable with the language change, but asked who will determine if
an application should be submitted to the Diversity Commission. Mr. Symes stated that if a majority of the Council would like to
involve the Diversity Commission, the applicant would be sent to that group and a recommendation would be solicited. The
ordinance also provides broad parameters for the Commission to consider the positive and negative impacts of the street name
change, but if there is something else the Council would like the Commission to consider, there is nothing in the ordinance
prohibiting the Council from making a more specific request. There is also nothing in the ordinance that would prevent the
Council from requesting a recommendation from another body, such as the Landmarks Commission or Weber Heritage
Foundation.
Council member Hyer stated he spent some time with the petitioner for the last honorary street name application and it
has reinforced his feelings that an honorary street name designation should be initiated by an elected official; residents have the
perception that if they pay the application fee and follow the process, the designation will be approved, but that has not been the
case and recent application processes have been very divisive and painful and he wants to avoid that. He feels that the Council
should first set policy that communicates whether Ogden City wants multiple honorary street name designations throughout the
City or require honorary street name applications to be sponsored by elected officials. Council member Nadolski agreed and noted
he is willing to do whatever is necessary to avoid divisive situations for the community and everyone involved in the application
process. Chair Choberka stated that the challenge she is facing is that the City Council has already set a precedent by approving an
honorary street name designation in recent history on 2™ Street; she noted that process was also painful and somewhat divisive,
but the result was more positive than she could have imagined. She noted she does not see the process being less divisive if it is
initiated by an elected official rather than a citizen or group or residents. Council member Blair stated that he thinks the honorary

Fiscal Year 2023-2024 Financial Principles: Council Deputy Director Symes explained each year the Council and
Mayor jointly adopt financial principles to guide and direct budgetary and financial management decisions throughout the Fiscal
Year (FY). Prior to FY2015, the principles were adopted with the budget. Beginning in FY2015, the Council began adopting the
principles prior to receiving the Mayor’s budget as a way to provide additional guidance during preparation of the Mayor’s
proposed budget. Updates to the financial principles have typically been adopted prior to the Mayor’s presentation of the budget;
however, during the 2022-2023 fiscal year, the Council approved one change related to employee salaries stating that excess
compensation and benefits funds resulting from vacancies would not be transferred to other department operating accounts
without Council approval. This year, Administration is proposing one change to Operating Reserves. The change would increase
the City’s goal for a minimum reserve by an additional one percent per year until the reserve reaches 20 percent of the annual
General Fund reserves. This is an increase from 10 percent of the annual General Fund reserves. Utah State Code requires a
minimum ofa five percent reserve and caps the maximum reserve at 35 percent. This reserve includes unassigned, committed, and
assigned funds in the General Fund. Administrative staff feel increasing this reserve provides better planning for economic
volatility, inflation, risings costs and other unanticipated expenses. The proposed amendment adds the following language to the
Operative Reserve principle: “Operating Reserve. The City will maintain an operating reserve in the General Fund of at least five
percent, and not greater than 35 percent of annual General Fund revenues as required by Utah Code, Section 10-6-116(4). The
minimum five percent and the maximum 35 percent include amounts reserved in the unassigned, committed, and assigned
categories of General Fund fund balance. Revenues permitting, the City will set a goal to increase the minimum reserve by an
additional one percent per year until the reserve reaches at least 19-percent-20 percent of annual General Fund revenues.” \ }

There was very brief discussion among the Council regarding the proposed change; the Council concluded to
support the amendment requested by Administration.
