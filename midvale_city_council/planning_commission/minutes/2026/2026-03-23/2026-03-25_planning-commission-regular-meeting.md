REGULAR MEETING

Chair Edwards called the Planning & Zoning Commission meeting to order at 6:00 p.m. He
explained how the meeting would proceed. First, the Planning Department would brief the
Commission; then the applicant would speak to the Commission; the item will be opened to the
public for their comments on public hearing agenda items; the Midvale Deputy City Attorney
will then brief the Commission on their role on the given item; and then the Commission would
deliberate and decide.

      Midvale City Code 17-3-1(E) outlines the criteria necessary for the amendments to the zoning
      map as follows:

            1. Proposed rezoning promotes objectives of the general plan.
            2. Existing zoning was either the result of a clerical error or a mistake of fact, or that it
               failed to take into account the constraints on development created by the natural

DISCUSSION:

Daniel Van Buege, Deputy City Attorney, explained that the proposed Conditional Use Permit is
an administrative decision to be made by the Planning Commission. He stated that the Commission

PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION MARCH 25, 2026.

may impose conditions, as staff has proposed, to ensure compliance with the requirements of the
Midvale City Code. He further noted that if the proposed project meets all applicable code
requirements, the Planning Commission is obligated to approve the Conditional Use Permit, and
that denial would only be justified if the code requirements are not met.

Vice Chair Liedtke stated that the proposed parking provisions within the application appear
sufficient to accommodate the business activity, which has been a primary concern raised by the
public. He also noted potential concerns related to light and sound impacts and suggested that the
Planning Commission consider requiring a six-foot sight-obscuring fence along the east and south
property boundaries to help mitigate these impacts on adjacent properties.

Commissioner Anderson stated that the proposed parking may impact surrounding areas,
particularly considering future plans for additional business activity on the first floor of the
building.

Commissioner Brauchie and Commissioner Anderson expressed concerns regarding potential
conflicts in business hours between the Lunatic Lounge and any future businesses on the site. They
noted that shared parking resources may be insufficient if multiple businesses operate
simultaneously, and that vehicle activity during off-hours for surrounding businesses could
contribute to additional light impacts.

Brad Williams stated that a café is not currently part of their operations but is anticipated as part
of the long-term use of the property. He explained that the café and the Lunatic Lounge would not
operate during the same hours. He further described planned activities, such as poetry and art
nights, which are expected to minimize potential noise impacts.

Commissioner Brauchie asked whether loud music would be part of the business operations,
considering earlier concerns regarding noise impacts on nearby residents and businesses.

Chair Edwards requested clarification regarding whether alcohol would be sold or consumed on-
site as part of the business operations.

Brad Williams responded that no alcohol would be part of the proposed business activities, music
would be played; however, as the primary activities would occur in the basement and are intended
to encourage conversation, the volume would not be excessive.

Chair Edwards expressed agreement with Vice Chair Liedtke’s concerns regarding potential light
and sound impacts and supported the recommendation to require a six-foot sight-obscuring fence
along the east and south property boundaries to mitigate potential impacts on surrounding
properties.

To the Members of the Midvale City Planning Commission,

I am writing to provide my thoughts regarding the requested Conditional Use
Permit for The Lunatic Lounge to operate an assembly hall at 159 W Center
Street. While I am not opposed to the proposed assembly hall in the
basement or the café planned for the upper floor, I have significant concerns
regarding the potential impact on neighborhood parking.

According to the staff report, the property currently has 21 parking spaces.
The report also notes that the assembly hall use requires 1 stall per 5 seats,
totaling 6 stalls for the 30-seat capacity. However, the email exchange
included in the packet between City Planner Jonathan Anderson and
Madisyn Mower suggests that the total requirement for both the basement
assembly hall and the 2,800 sq. ft. upper-floor café would be approximately
14.4 stalls.

While the applicant suggests there are 21 available spaces, I am concerned
that the actual parking demand during performances will overflow into the
surrounding residential streets, specifically Grant Street. I have observed
that other nearby areas, such as Roosevelt (between Center and 7758
Roosevelt), have already had to implement "red paint" no-parking zones to
manage traffic. I worry that without proactive measures, Grant Street will
face similar congestion issues. It already is difficult for cars to exit and enter
Grant Street with the Pedestrian Traffic Island and is increasingly difficult
with a narrow road with cars parked on either side of the street.

The applicant’s email mentions that "if needed, there is approximately four
more parking spaces on the street in front of the bank". Relying on limited
street parking for a commercial and assembly use is not a sustainable
solution for the permanent residents of this neighborhood.

I ask the Planning Commission to consider the following:

       Stricter Parking Enforcement: How will the city ensure that patrons utilize
       the 21 on-site spots rather than residential street parking?

       Capacity of Adjacent Streets: Has a formal study been done on the impact of
       this "assembly use" on the capacity of Grant Street and the immediate
       residential vicinity?

       Drive-Through Conversion: The applicant mentions potentially converting
     drive-through lanes into four additional stalls. I would recommend that this
     conversion be made a formal condition of approval to ensure the maximum
     number of off-street spots are available.

Thank you for your time.

Mike Williams - 7907 S Grant Street
