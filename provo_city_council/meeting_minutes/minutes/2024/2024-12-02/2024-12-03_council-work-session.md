Item 1: Resolution Approving an Amendment to the Interlocal Cooperation Agreement for
the Utah County Major Crimes Task Force (24-105) 0:06:08

Chief Troy Beebe presented the item, providing a background on the Utah County Major Crimes
Task Force. He explained that the task force primarily addresses drug trafficking, noting that
many major crimes are related to drugs.

Key achievements this year include:

       The capture of 250 pounds of methamphetamine.
       The capture of 52 pounds of heroin.
       Response to 42 overdose incidents.

Councilor Bogdin inquired about the use of Narcan. Chief Beebe confirmed its use and
emphasized its role in saving lives.

Chief Beebe also addressed the impact of inflation, reporting an increase in costs by $9,151.41,
which was absorbed by the operations budget. He stated that this expense would be included in
future budget requests.

Item 2: Resolution Approving a $30,000 Budget Allocation from the General Fund to the
Police Department for the Police Language Incentive Program (24-099) 0:17:22

Captain Brian Taylor presented the resolution, providing background on Spanish-speaking needs
within the Police Department. He reported that the department documents approximately 115
Spanish-speaking calls for service each month, though this figure does not represent all
interactions with Spanish-speaking individuals.

Current Challenges:
Captain Taylor explained that the department has traditionally relied on recruiting Spanish-
speaking officers but has not taken proactive steps to help current officers utilize or maintain
their language skills effectively.

Program Overview:
The proposed Police Language Incentive Program would utilize the Foreign Language
Achievement Testing Service (FLATS) test provided by Brigham Young University (BYU) to
evaluate officers’ language proficiency. Compensation levels based on test results would be as
follows:

    1. Basic communication: $50/month
    2. Moderate proficiency: $100/month
    3. Fluent: $150/month

Captain Taylor outlined the program's incentive structure, anticipated benefits, and potential
costs. He expressed hope that the program would motivate employees to develop Spanish-
language skills, addressing a gap not adequately accounted for in the current budget.

Councilor Questions and Discussion:

       Councilor Christensen asked if the program would compensate for languages other than
        Spanish. Captain Taylor confirmed that FLATS offers tests for other languages, and
        compensation would be available for those as well.
       Councilor Christensen noted that FLATS emphasizes comprehension and listening over
        dialogue and asked why it was chosen. Captain Taylor stated that FLATS was the most
        readily available test through BYU but expressed openness to exploring alternative tests
        better suited to the department’s needs.
       Councilor Handley suggested that while FLATS is a good starting point, alternatives
        could be considered over time. Captain Taylor confirmed that the department has not
        made any contracts with BYU and remains flexible regarding testing options.
       Councilor Handley reflected on a previous foreign language town hall, commending the
        interest shown by foreign language community members in participating with the city.
       Councilor Whipple expressed support for the program and encouraged further outreach
        to diverse language speakers. Captain Taylor highlighted the department’s shift from
        organic growth to a more structured approach in developing language skills.

Recruitment and Operations:
Councilor Whipple asked whether applicants receive extra points for language skills. Captain
Taylor explained that while points are not awarded, language competencies are emphasized
during the application process, particularly for roles like the front counter records clerk. Both
sworn and non-sworn employees can participate in the program, though dispatch is excluded due
to existing digital language tools.

Councilor Whipple inquired about tracking preferred languages during interactions for data
purposes. Captain Taylor stated that it is feasible but requires caution to avoid asking sensitive
questions about individuals’ heritage.

Program Challenges and Future Steps:

       Councilor Whipple asked about reliance on interpreters. Captain Taylor acknowledged
        the heavy burden on Spanish-speaking officers, noting the need for private interpreters
        and additional Spanish reading materials.
       Councilor Bogdin asked if the program discriminates against non-Spanish-speaking
        officers. Captain Taylor emphasized that all employees have the opportunity to learn and
        that language skills are not a factor in assignments.
       Councilor Bogdin raised concerns about balancing Spanish-speaking officers across
        shifts. Captain Taylor responded that shift assignments are based on seniority, and
        changes could cause contention.
       Councilor Bogdin questioned why the funding request was not made during the regular
        budget season. Captain Taylor cited the program’s importance and financial struggles as
        reasons for the timing.

Item 3: Ordinance Amending the Zone Map Classification of Real Property Located at 577
East 400 South from the One-Family Residential (R1.6) Zone to the Low-Density
Residential (LDR) Zone (PLRZ20240234) 0:47:34

Mary Barnes, Planner, presented the item, noting that nearby parcels in the area have single-
family homes at the front with additional residential units in the back.

Discussion:

       Councilor MacKay asked about the number of additional residential units on nearby
        parcels. Ms. Barnes stated that one parcel has three units and another has two. She also
        highlighted a nearby area with multifamily housing that had undergone similar zoning
        requests.
       Ms. Barnes skipped over concept plans, as they had been reviewed previously, and
        recommended approval of the ordinance, stating that it aligns with city goals.

Councilor Concerns and Questions:

       Councilor Whipple raised concerns about access via the back alley, which is not
        maintained by the city. She expressed frustration with the use of gravel for quick fixes
        instead of addressing the issue comprehensively. Ms. Barnes acknowledged the alley's
        condition but noted that it is not among the worst in the city and confirmed it would not
        receive city maintenance. She also clarified that the alley is intended for garage and
        owner parking access, with visitor access routed through the front lot on 400 South. The
        developer agreed to re-landscape the gravel in the alley. Despite her concerns, Councilor
        Whipple supported the project, emphasizing its importance in providing homeownership
        opportunities.
       Councilor MacKay visited the site and noted that the alley is narrow, accommodating
        only one car. She expressed concerns about potential neighbor objections and highlighted
        that the units would likely be rented, not owned.
       Councilor Handley asked if the property owner planned to have his children occupy the
        units. Councilor MacKay confirmed that the children are too young, and Development
        Services Director Bill Peperone stated that they may inherit the property in the future.
       Councilor Handley compared the design to housing common on the East Coast,
        emphasizing that alley access is a typical feature in such developments and expressing
        strong support for the project.
       Councilor Bogdin agreed with Councilor MacKay's concerns, noting that the alley feels
        too dense for the area.

Item 4: Ordinance Amending Provo City Code Regarding Signs Permitted in the Freeway
Commercial (FC3) and Regional Shopping Center (SC3) Zones (PLOTA20240271) 1:03:28

Aaron Ardmore, Planning Supervisor, presented the proposed ordinance amendment, which
addresses sign regulations in the Freeway Commercial (FC3) and Regional Shopping Center
(SC3) zones.

Discussion:

       Councilor Bogdin asked about the distance between the proposed sign and nearby
        residential areas. Mr. Ardmore displayed a map and measured the distance as 704 feet
        from the residential zone. Councilor Bogdin also inquired if additional signs would be
        placed. Mr. Ardmore confirmed that all signs would be located in the specified area.
       Councilor Bogdin asked whether the proposed sign complies with existing code. Mr.
        Ardmore clarified that while the sign’s distance from the neighborhood is not explicitly
        addressed in the code, this particular section of the parcel is the only location where a
        sign of this size would be permitted.
       Councilor Bogdin suggested adding language to require signs to be at least 500 feet from
        residential neighborhoods. Mr. Ardmore stated he would discuss the suggestion with the
        applicant during the evening meeting.
       Councilor Bogdin emphasized that including the proposed change would secure her
        support, but without it, she would vote against the ordinance.
