Council/Community and Economic Development Department
          Discussion then shifted to the Community and Economic Department; Director Christopulos provided the Council with a
report of major and minor economic development projects and business recruitment efforts. There was a focus on the impactthat
COVID-19 has had on different industries, such as aerospace, manufacturing, and healthcare; there is a trend towards automation
in various industries, though many businesses have expressed they do not anticipate long-term impacts or challenges associated
with COVID-19. However, small businesses and some retailers have been nearly decimated and may not recover.
          Discussion among the Council and Mr. Christopulos centered on the potential for many businesses previously located in
other states or even outside the United States to move to this area; this included a focus on the need to increase housing
opportunities for employees of the businesses that are moving "to this area. Mr. Christopulos stated the City has a unique
opportunity to facilitate economic development in the City while also moving new residents into housing projects in the Central
Business District (CBD) and his staff is working to respond to that opportunity as quickly as possible.
          Chair Choberka then invited Mr. Christopulos to talk about the Coronavirus Relief Fund Business Grant Program, which
will be acted upon later tonight during the regular meeting. Mr. Christopulos stated that because the Nation's small businesses are
facing an unprecedented economic disruption due to the Coronavirus (COVID-19) outbreak, on March 27, 2020, the President
signed into law the CARES Act, which provided $376 billion in relief for American workers and small businesses. A portion of
that funding was allocated to the states under the Coronavirus Relief Fund (CRF) to cover necessary costs associated with or
resulting from the pandemic. Necessary expenditures include not only direct emergency response activities, but also expenses
related to the secondary effects of the virus such as the wide-spread business interruptions. The State of Utah was awarded $1.25
billion in CARES Act funding and is distributing a portion of its funding to counties and municipalities on a population-based
formula. Ogden City is eligible for a total allocation of$7.8 million dollars with one-third, or $2.6 million, received in the initial
distribution. The remaining amounts may be received in two additional distributions at a later date, subject to the availability of
funding. He noted his Department is proposing to use a portion of the funds to create a grant program supporting local businesses
impacted by COVID-19; grant applications will be accepted from locally owned companies, Ogden residents as employees, sole
proprietorships, and businesses in the CBD that must make physical modifications to their space to accommodate physical social
distancing. Brief discussion between the Council and Mr. Christopulos centered on the manner in which the grant program will be
advertised to local businesses and any terms surrounding the application period and process. Council member Lopez thanked City
Administration and other leaders in the City and Council for their efforts at improving communication with Hispanic residents and
business owners in the community regarding these types of programs.

Proposed Rezone of 4400 Harrison Boulevard
         Council Deputy Director Symes explained a petition has been filed to rezone just under three acres of property located to
the southeast of the 4400 South Harrison Boulevard intersection. The proposed rezone would reclassify the property from R-3
(Multi-family Residential) to C-2 (Community Commercial). The petitioner requesting the rezone has indicated that the intent is
to develop a hotel on the site. The petitioner has been working with Weber State University (WSU) to develop the property. The
rezone to C-2 is consistent with the proposed Southeast Ogden Community Plan, scheduled for Council consideration on July 14,
2020.
         Planning Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the purpose of the petition; if
the zone change is approved, the petitioner would like to proceed with development of a 100-room hotel adjacent to WSU. The
area to be rezoned is 2.9 acres and contains five parcels; the rezone would require re-platting of the properties. The applicant is
working with WSU to provide hotel amenities close to the campus similar to the type of layouts present on several college
campuses throughoutthe State and the nation. He presented an aerial photograph of the property to illustrate its proximity to the
Dee Events Center, the future Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) route, and storm water infrastructure. The Planning Commission
considered compliance with the City's General Plan and zoning policies; they examined whether the rezone would strengthen the
commercial edge between Harrison Boulevard and the University. They found the area includes several adjacent commercial (C-
2) properties and the rezone will result in split zoning on parcels, which is why the new plat would be required to correct

    Zions Bankcorp Capital Lease
              The Administration is proposing that the City enter a lease purchase agreement, or capital lease, with Zions Bank to fund
    the purchase of heavy equipment to support various City services and functions.
              Treasurer Johnson explained the City has leased several key pieces of heavy equipment from Wheeler Machinery for the
    past four years. This agreement has been an operating lease or a rental agreement where the equipment is returned to Wheeler at
    the end of the term of the agreement. As the City was reviewing the benefit of continuing this practice and renewing a new
    operating lease or rental agreement for this equipment, the City also evaluated the cost and benefit of purchasing the equipment.
    With the help of the City's financial advisor, Lewis Young Robertson and Burningham (LYRB), the City issued a request for
    interest rate proposal to fund the purchase of equipment. The proposal asked respondents to provide a six-year and eight-year
    amortization. The City received four competitive proposals and it was detennined Zion's Bankcorp offered the best terms. Upon
    the approval of the resolution, the City will enter into a lease in the amount of $1,003,000 to fund the purchase of $983,900 in
    equipment outlined below and the fees associated with the debt instrument, this will be a six-year capital lease with an annual
    interest rate of 1.99 percent. At the end of the lease, the City estimates the equipment will still have a useful life of approximately
    four years. The annual payments under these terms will be approximately $190,000, with the exception of year one, which will be
    approximately $90,000. The lease payments with the costs of an annual maintenance agreement for this equipment will increase
    the cost to the City by approximately $16,000 annually over the operating lease. The City will absorb these costs through the
    current replacement budget in fleet. By entering into a capital lease, the City will build equity that can be used to help fund the
    replacement of this equipment in future years.
              Laura Lewis of LYRB reviewed the lease financing sources and uses; she summarized the debt payment schedule
    highlighting the amount of principal and interest paid in each fiscal year from 2021 to 2026; the total interest paid in that time
    frame is $76,322, or 2.06 percent. Financing fees for the lease total $10,000.
              Council Policy Analyst Mabey noted if the Council is comfmtable proceeding, she will include an action item on the July
    7 meeting to grant approval of the lease agreement. Ms. Lewis added that if the Council approves the agreement on July 7, the
    transaction will be closed on July 9.
