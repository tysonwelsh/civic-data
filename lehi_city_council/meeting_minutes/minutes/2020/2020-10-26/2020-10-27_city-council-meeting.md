Pre-Council, 4:00 p.m.
1. Welcome and Invocation
Mayor Johnson welcomed everyone and noted that all Councilmembers were present.

2. Presentations and Reports
2.1 Central Corridor Transit Study Presentation
Shane Marshall gave an update on the Central Corridor Transit Study that’s being conducted in
Utah County. He discussed Bus Rapid Transit from Lehi to Orem and where that might be. He
stated that in Lehi, they intend to use the right of way along the rail.

2.2 Discussion of the fiber feasibility study from Magellan Advisors
John Honker, with Magellan, addressed the Council as a follow up to the presentation at the work
session meeting earlier in the month. Mr. Honker stated that the finding of the study showed that
the regulatory risks suggest that the City should avoid proving retail ISP services. Instead, the City
could partner with one or more retail ISPs. He stated that they estimated the take rate would be
45% as long as the fees are competitive. With that 45% take rate they expect an estimated 9 million
of surplus over 20 years. Mr. Honker then detailed how the services would be provided in a
partner/wholesale model.

Councilor Hancock inquired about the difference between the City building its own network,
versus have a company like UTOPIA doing the construction. Mr. Honker stated that the biggest
difference is the control.

Mayor Johnson stated that they would prefer to move forward with the wholesale model because
as the study shows, it presents a moderate risk with a high reward and the City would control the
system.

There was discussion about the 9 million surplus and if that is worth the risk. Mr. Honker explained
that the real benefit is having the control and owning the system, because there are other things
that you can do with the system, such as using it for schools, internal applications, and
transportation. Mayor Johnson stated that with a UTOPIA model, they may not even have
representation on the board. Councilor Koivisto stated that she likes having the control.

Councilor Condie stated that if he remembers right, UTOPIA estimated that the buildout would
cost 50 million, which is less than the projected cost for doing it ourselves by about 30 million.
Mr. Honker stated that they don’t know how UTOPIA estimated their cost. He said that Magellan
did a thorough in-depth analysis of the city’s current system and estimated costs based on that. He
stated that there would be some cost that the city would incur that UPOPIA would not such as
equipment cost and working capital. He also stated that they included contingencies in their
estimate, so he believes they are not comparing the same costs. He also said that they are very
conservative on construction costs. He said that he could provide some comparison points.
Councilor Condie stated that it’s not necessarily a bad thing for someone else to own the system
and would like to know why the estimates were so different.

Mayor Johnson noted that the information provided to UTOPIA for their estimate was not the same
information that Magellan was able to produce during their study.

Jason Walker, City Administrator, stated that if the city owned the system or if UTOPIA owned
it, the payment obligation is the same. Mr. Walker also noted that after the bond is paid for then
the City could have the option to lower its rates.

There was discussion regarding the timeline for engineering and construction costs and how
quickly the city could move forward with a bond. Mayor Johnson recommended that the Council
reach out to the study group with questions. Mayor Johnson stated that he wants the Council to
feel comfortable moving forward with a decision.

3. Agenda Questions
None.

4. Administrative Report
4.1 Capital Lease Update from Finance
Dean Lundell, Finance Director, stated that in the budget this year they included an option to
purchase 3 pieces of large equipment through a capital lease and that will be on the next Council
agenda for approval.

5. Mayor and Council Reports
The Mayor and Council gave a report on the committees they serve on.

Mayor Johnson asked Steve Marchbanks, Parks Director, to give an update on the most recent
concept for Family Park. Mr. Marchbanks presented a rough draft of a concept plan that will be
shown to residents at the ribbon cutting ceremony on Saturday. He stated that they are making
this a leisure park based on feedback from the residents.
