 4             Update on Green Well Project – Mayor Staggs
 5
 6   Mayor Staggs said that he had invited Total Water Management who was awarded the RFP for
 7   the Greenwell project to give an update.
 8
 9   Mitch Curtis, Total Water Management, said that they are in Phase 1 of the Greenwell treatment
10   project. He said Phase 1 is final project planning and a key component is seeking to get a
11   Jordan River discharge permit. He said the study establishes the volume and what they can
12   discharge at the Greenwell. He said they have been able to do testing of membranes and
13   establish performance and recovery rates. He said they had compiled that data and presented
14   that to the water department and are moving forward with final economics for review of the
15   treatment proposition. He said the permit has been finalized and submitted to the state. He
16   said that this project will receive public comment within the next month or two and then there will
17   be a 30 day period where the comments will be evaluated.
18
19   Mayor Staggs asked Mr. Curtis to send the electronic documents to the City Council.
20
21   Mr. Curtis shared the Riverton City Update documentation. (Documents included in the packet)
22
23   Mayor Staggs said that he has been working with Salt Lake County and others to try to get
24   funding for the Greenwell project. He said the County is looking at congressional approval of
25   funding to pay for this project or they will use their relief funds to pay for this project. He
26   believes this could be a win, win scenario if the city could get this Greenwell for no cost and the
27   Greenwell would give the golf course water and the City would find a way to distribute that water
28   for Riverton residents. He said this option would be considerably less than the conservancy
29   district and there would be a great amount of savings. He said the City would not have rate
30   increases for the next five years if Salt Lake County funds this project. He said the TDS
31   hardness and selinene levels are the concern but even option 3 will bring the City in line with the
32   conservancy district.
33
34   Councilmember Buroker asked Mr. Curtis to clarify that in order to get to the current taste of the
35   water you can only recover 50% of the water.
36
37   Mr. Curtis said from a taste standpoint, option 3 would be fine with a 70% recovery.
38
39   Mayor Staggs said that in order to get the chloride rate to every resident equal to the
40   conservancy district then there would only be a 50% recovery rate. He said that to get the
41   residents water that is comparable then there would be a 68% recovery rate. He said the
42   amount of water that would spill off into the Jordan River would be 600 gallons per minute. He
43   said that it is naturally flowing at 300 to 400 gallons per minute. He said this is a decision the
44   Council would need to make later in the process. He believes that Option 1 would treat the
45   water way too much. He said the actual cost of the unit would be $2.5 to $2.7 million dollar
46   range to get the technology in place.

21             Stormwater Utility Ordinance Discussion – Ryan Carter, City Attorney
22
23   Ryan Carter, City Attorney said that he had prepared a fairly lengthy memo regarding
24   Stormwater. He said he wanted to drive home the point that this is an important program that is

26   program it has now become a discussion as to how the City should build the Stormwater fee.

28   Mr. Carter said that in order to safely discharge the water to the Jordan River, the pollutants
29   coming from residential and commercial property into the Stormwater must be managed. He

32   residential units. He said that the Stormwater ordinance would provide a fee structure for
