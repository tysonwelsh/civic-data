Unified Police Department Report

               Chief Del Craig reviewed the crime statistics for the month of March, stating the
number one concern is domestic violence. Calls have increased, despite the UPD trying to
manage them better. There were some recent violent incidents in Magna, the most prominent
was a homicide near the Entheos Academy, where there was a dispute between two adult males.
The UPD has identified the suspect and is close to putting him in custody. In addition, there was
a child endangerment issue where a young woman from Cypress High School was taken from
there to Texas. These situations were very labor intensive, and resources were brought in on
both events. He also reviewed the reorganization of the Magna Precinct, which has gone to a
problem-oriented policing model, divided into three beats. All of the detectives will be reassigned
to the beats, and investigators and patrol officers will be assigned to teams to work those areas.
Each officer will be given assignments to proactively reach out and identify issues, which will be
discussed weekly.

                Council Member Pierce asked how the Council could set benchmarks to show
that the work it is doing in the community is decreasing the crime statistics.

              Chief Craig stated the precinct does not have any benchmarks now, but it could
establish some as it moved along. The team concept will address patterns and trends and be
able to have measured success.

                Council Member Barney stated anytime a program is implemented, there needs
to be a baseline. Then, the program needs to be tracked to see whether there is a downward
trend in time from where things are at today.

              Council Member Hull suggested using grant funds obtained by the Magna Safety
and Success group to involve South Valley Services or another entity that works with domestic
violence, and maybe offer free classes on domestic violence to problem families.

              Chief Craig stated the precinct will incorporate domestic violence discussions into
its weekly meeting and try to determine what some of the causes are. It will consider ethnicity,
socioeconomic, and environmental issues. The precinct will also be suggesting some new or
amended ordinances that are needed, such as camping and parking ordinances.

               Mayor Peay stated he received a call from James Pappas today who said police
officers were at his neighbor’s place twice this weekend.

              Chief Craig stated he has not gotten the details of what occurred last weekend.
However, that case will be followed up on weekly by the lead investigator.

                 Council Member Prokopis stated trash is being dumped in the middle of the road
in his beat. A friend actually went through some of it and found an address, so with a little legwork,
some of these people could be caught and fined. If that was publicized, it might help to reduce it.
He would reach out to the detective on that beat.

Current Codes and Prioritizing the Order of Code Updates

               Lupita McClenning, Planning & Development Director, MSD, delivered a
presentation on the Metro Township Code update for 2020-2023, which is a complete zoning
revision project that will include the goals, polices, and vision of each metro township’s general
plan. Planning & Development Services’ goals are to have the code easy to understand;
streamline the development review process; provide clear direction to project applicants;
effectively implement the townships’ general plans; ensure the ordinances are consistent and
working with other ordinances, that the land use conforms to requirements of Utah State law, and
that the code is usable; and that there be public engagement to ensure the development is what
the community wants. Planning & Development Services would like the Council and Planning
Commission to work with it on doing research, reviewing existing conditions, and looking at
redlines; determining how to include public engagement, education, and outreach; identifying its
preferred approach and what a successful code update should look like, taking time to look at its
general plan to make sure that vision can be implemented, and letting the Planning &
Development know how it would like to get future updates; and then going through the approval
and adoption process.

             Paul Ashton stated it would be helpful if the Council designated Council Members
who have a real interest to be its point of contact. Under the statute, the Planning Commission
must take the first step; however, the Council needs to have eyes and ears throughout the
process.

               Council Member Barney stated he would want to be updated frequently with
where Planning & Development was at in the process, a projection of how long it would take to
write the code, any roadblocks or missing information, or anything that sped the process along.

Request to have Ivory Homes Address Identified Deficiencies in the Mahogany Ridge
Development Agreement

               Paul Ashton reviewed this request, which is a follow up from some of Council
Member Barney’s concerns about parking and other problems in Ivory Home’s Gabler Grove
development. When Ivory Homes came to the Council with the development request, the Council
was concerned with parking, due to setbacks and other things. The Council was told residents
were not allowed to park their cars in driveways; they had to park their cars in garages. Since
that was specified in the master development agreement, if people are not parking their vehicles
in their garages, Ivory Homes is technically in breach of the agreement. He asked for the
Council’s approval to allow him to draft a letter to Ivory Homes requesting it enforce the clauses
in the agreement.

With regard to Mahogany Ridge, the Council signed that master development agreement in
October 2021, and once an agreement is signed, a city has to allow the developer to develop as
long as they are in compliance with the agreement. The question is whether there were
deficiencies in the master development agreement, which is almost identical to the Gabler Grove
development agreement. He has set up a meeting with Ivory Homes to see about refining that
agreement.

              Council Member Prokopis stated he liked the idea of learning lessons from
Gabler Grove and taking the opportunity to talk to Ivory Homes. There will be plenty more Ivory
Home developments in this community, so he thought Ivory Homes would be receptive to meeting
with the Council.

              Mr. Ashton stated Ivory Homes has in the past, worked with the Council when it
has pointed out a problem.

Council Reports

Unified Police Department (UPD) / Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA)

                 Council Member Prokopis stated the UPD had a finance committee meeting
Thursday where it talked about compensation. It wants to do another salary increase this year
and talked about a 10 percent raise for the civilian staff and anywhere from 3 to 8 percent for the
officers, targeting the ranks that are most out of whack. If it does that, the Magna Metro Township
could be looking at a 9 percent increase for its share of UPD. A normal year is a 3 to 5 percent
increase. The UPD could also continue to look at other funding options. It was unable to get the
Legislature to rase the cap for the Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Services Area (SLVLESA),
so it would have to get creative, such as impact fees, fees, or writing more tickets.

Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District (WFWRD)

                Council Member Barney stated WFWRD is now its own local district and is
completely separated from the County. Salt Lake County Council Member Jim Bradley has retired
from WFWRD’s Governing Board and Council Member Laurie Stringham is taking his place. He
reviewed WFWRD’s satisfaction survey results, which were extremely high, except there was
dissatisfaction that the dumpster program was now a reservation system. WFWRD moved to the
reservation system to control costs. Those who have used it, raved about it.

               Council Member Pierce stated she liked the old system too because she could
dump more stuff. Also, the reservations go fast. She thought maybe the number of dumpsters
could be increased over time.

                  Mr. Barney stated WFWRD has acknowledged deficiencies with this process and
will try to fine-tune and tweak it over time. There are a limited number of bins, and some areas
are not getting the need met. Then, some areas have a larger window because their population
is greater, but the question is whether they are utilizing the bins.

              Council Member Prokopis stated he liked the new process. He used to hate
having a random dumpster dropped in front of his house because it was overflowing in six hours
and the area was not cleaned up the way he would have liked. Now, there is less illegal dumping
with the dumpsters in driveways.

               Council Member Hull stated residents can get dump passes at the library, but
that is not widely known. She asked how expensive it would be to mail out a dump pass to
everyone.

               Council Member Barney stated WFWRD could try to better advertise that
program.
