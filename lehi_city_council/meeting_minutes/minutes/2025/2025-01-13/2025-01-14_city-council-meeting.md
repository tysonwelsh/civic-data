2. Presentations and Reports

2.1 Presentation and Update on Alpine School District- Stacy Bateman and Joylyn Lincoln
The Alpine School District Board representatives provided an overview of the schools located
within Lehi, highlighting the balanced enrollment between the two high schools and the growth
in enrollment at the junior high level. They discussed concerns about two elementary schools
exceeding 1,000 students and noting that it may need to be addressed through reconfiguration
and new school construction. The presentation also covered the district's strong concurrent
enrollment program, which allows students to earn college credits while in high school, as well
as the district's high graduation rates and ongoing capital projects to maintain and update school
facilities.

Mayor Johnson asked for a motion to go into the Lehi Redevelopment Agency Meeting.

    Councilor Newall, Yes; and Councilor Stallings, Yes. The motion passed unanimously.

3. Agenda Questions
Councilor Stallings mentioned that appointments to the Lehi Municipal Water Board are
typically discussed in a closed session, as it allows the Council to review qualifications and
provide more information. She noted that state code specifically states the legislative body (City
Council) is responsible for making these appointments, rather than just the Mayor. Councilor
Stallings expressed a desire to have more information about the proposed appointees before
approving the resolution, and the Council agreed to go into a closed session to discuss the
appointees.

4. Administrative Report
Kim Struthers provided an update on efforts to secure additional pedestrian access into the
eastern portion of Family Park. He explained that the city has been negotiating with property
owners, including one on a cul-de-sac and another along a gas line easement, to obtain six-foot
wide pedestrian access points. Struthers noted that an amended plat would need to go through the
formal approval process, including a Planning Commission hearing on January 23rd. He wanted
to make the Council aware of this in case they received any questions or concerns from nearby
residents about the proposed access points.

2. 20 Minute Citizen Input
Brianne Beckstrom expressed frustration over her home-based business being accused of illegal
activity despite having a valid business license. She proposed a code amendment to address the
issue and challenged the city to grandfather over 500 current home occupation licenses,
emphasizing the need to recognize the contributions of these businesses to the community.

Steven Gunther inquired about the process for the redistricting of the new school district board
boundaries. He expressed concerns that the formation of the groups to discuss the new districts
was not well publicized, and the general public was not aware of the opportunity to participate.
Councilor Newall suggested reaching out to the County Commissioners for more information on
the redistricting process.

Andrew Jackson offered his support and willingness to help with the home occupation issue. He
mentioned that he has a home occupation permit that may be in violation and expressed interest in
being part of the decision-making process as a sounding board. The Mayor advised Jackson to
provide his input during public comment periods for specific applications.

5. Consideration of Resolution #2025-03 approving an agreement with Geneva Rock for
   the construction of a box culvert at Willow Park.
   Allred explained that the project aims to address a bottleneck in the waste ditch by removing
   the existing 60 or 72-inch steel pipe that was installed in reverse grade, causing the ditch to
   back up significantly during runoff season. The new box culvert will be the same size as the
   other box culverts in the waste ditch, which will lower the flow line and eliminate the choke
   point. Allred noted that this will make an enormous difference in the area's flood control
   capabilities.

    Motion:     Councilor Hancock moved to approve Resolution #2025-03 approving an
                agreement with Geneva Rock for the construction of a box culvert at Willow Park.
                Councilor Albrecht seconded the motion

    Roll Call Vote: Councilor Albrecht, Yes; Councilor Condie, Absent; Councilor Hancock, Yes;
    Councilor Newall, Yes; and Councilor Stallings, Yes. The motion passed with 4 in favor and

6. Consideration of Resolution #2025-01 approving a second amendment to the agreement
   between Lehi City and Utah County for the Dry Creek Lake Regional Recreation Area.
   Lorin Powell, City Engineer, explained that the project includes improvements such as
   bathrooms, a four-plex, and fishing areas. Powell highlighted that the project has a primary
   purpose of flood control, and the improvements downstream have made a significant
   difference in the area's ability to handle flooding. He noted that the county's tourism tax is
   funding the project, as they believe it will stimulate tourism in the region. Overall, Powell
   emphasized the importance of the project for both recreational and flood control purposes.

    Motion:     Councilor Newall moved to approve Ordinance #67-2024, a Development Code

7. Consideration of Resolution #2025-05 approving an easement agreement between Lehi
   City and Geneva Rock Products on a section of Flight Park Road.
   Brad Kenison, Assistant City Engineer, explained that the easement agreement with Geneva
   Rock Products is related to the Alta Vista development. The current development code requires
   a second point of public access once a development reaches 50 units. The only current access
   is off Mountain Point Boulevard, so this easement will allow Lehi City to build a second access
   point along the currently gravel Flight Park Road. He noted that this is a critical access point
   for infrastructure improvements.

8. Consideration of Ordinance #01-2025 the Gordon Zone Change on 1.63-acres located at
   860 North 2300 West changing the zoning from A-1 to R-1-22 and Neighborhood
   Commercial.
   Kevin and Sarah Gordan presented their application to the Council. The zone change would
   allow for a residential lot in the back and retain the front portion as Neighborhood Commercial
   for a potential daycare center. The property was previously approved for a development
   agreement limiting the commercial use to a daycare. Councilor Stallings raised concerns about
   the safety of the proposed flag lot configuration, but was assured it would meet standards and
   provide better fire access. The current trailer storage on the property is not in compliance with
   the land use, so they would need to comply before the zone change is recorded.

    Motion:     Councilor Newall moved to approve Ordinance #01-2025 the Gordon Zone Change
                on 1.63-acres located at 860 North 2300 West changing the zoning from A-1 to R-

                Committee Comments. Councilor Albrecht seconded the motion.

    Roll Call Vote: Councilor Albrecht, Yes; Councilor Condie, Absent; Councilor Hancock, Yes;

10. Consideration of Ordinance #02-2025 the Public Facility Rezone 2024 located at various
    locations.
    Kim Struthers presented the public facility rezone for several city-owned properties,
    including the Family Park property. Struthers explained that this rezone helps ensure these
    properties remain in public use and makes it easier to identify city-owned land on the zoning
    map. The Council had a brief discussion, with Mayor Johnson noting that there were some
    gaps between parcels in the Family Park area. He suggested consolidating the property into
    one or two larger parcels to resolve any overlaps or gaps. Overall, the Council was
    supportive of the public facility rezone, recognizing it as a helpful "housekeeping" measure
    to maintain accurate zoning records for city-owned lands. The Council approved the
    resolution, excluding a small piece of property that was still being addressed.

    Motion:     Councilor Hancock moved to approve Ordinance #02-2025 the Public Facility
                Rezone 2024 located at various locations, except for a small pedestrian access that
                is still being addressed; including all Development Review Committee Comments.
                Councilor Newall seconded the motion.

    Roll Call Vote: Councilor Albrecht, Yes; Councilor Condie, Absent; Councilor Hancock, Yes;
    Councilor Newall, Yes; and Councilor Stallings, Yes. The motion passed with 4 in favor and
    1 absent.

12. Consideration of Resolution #2025-06 approving an Interlocal Cooperation Agreement
    with Utah County for an effort to aid the unsheltered.
    Mayor Johnson discussed the interlocal agreement, explaining that the city would contribute
    $9,000 based on the city's approximate population of 90,000 residents. This contribution will
    support the operations of the county's shelter for the unsheltered, including providing
    donations like blankets. The Council requested a report back in a year on how the funds were
    used and the progress of the effort. Overall, the Council recognized the importance of
    addressing homelessness and providing necessary resources.

    Motion:     Councilor Albrecht moved to approve Resolution #2025-06 approving an Interlocal
                Cooperation Agreement with Utah County for an effort to aid the unsheltered.
                Councilor Newall seconded the motion.
