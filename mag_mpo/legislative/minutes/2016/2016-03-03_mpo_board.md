Utah’s Unified Transportation Plan - Shawn Seager & Muriel Xochimitl, WFRC This is the third edition of the Utah’s Unified Transportation Plan. All the MPOs in the state, UDOT, and UTA have assembled their individual transportation plans into one document. Utah’s Unified Transportation Plan provides a summary of anticipated 30-year needs for road capacity and maintenance as well as transit improvements and operations for Utah’s metropolitan and rural areas. The Unified Plan responses to the anticipated population and job growth, and maintains and preserves the systems, road, transit, bike and pedestrian infrastructure, that we have in place. The Unified Plan is financially constrained and is based on reasonable cost and revenue assumptions. Wasatch Front Central Corridor Study Update - Shawn Seager & Lisa Zundel, UDOT I-15 is the principal north/south corridor along the Wasatch Front currently with 250,000 vehicles trips per day. TRAX and FrontRunner run parallel to I-15 with combined 80,000 riders per day. This study is Mountainland MPO Regional Planning March 3, 2016 Page 4 looking how to accommodate future travel demand out to 2050. The study evaluates all potential strategies for the entire corridor to include transit, roadway, active transportation, freeway/surface street connectivity, transit/roadway/ped/bike connectivity, and operations. The study corridor runs from Woods Cross Davis County, through Salt Lake County and ends in Lehi Utah County. The study started in the summer of 2015 and should be completed by the end of 2016. The initial scenarios are being developed are managing travel demand and meeting travel demand. Some of the different scenarios being looked at include double decking the freeway, coordinating ramp meters, double tracks for FrontRunner, linking vehicles (testing on I-80 with trucks), etc. The study is also looking who and when people are using the freeway. Two outreach local government workshops are planned to review 2050 transportation challenges, understand study process and timeline, review initial scenarios, gather additional ideas, and review next steps. Rocky Mountain Power Sustainable Transportation and Energy Plan (STEP) - Jeff Larsen, Rocky Mountain Power The Sustainable Transportation and Energy Plan (STEP) establishes mandatory utility investments in electric vehicle infrastructure, clean coal research and development, and sets up an equitable for large customers. STEP is bill SB 115 currently in the legislature. STEP would extend and reallocate $10 million to focus on clean air programs, research technologies to improve the grid, evaluate the viability of clean coal, stimulate economic development and continue a lower level of solar incentives. The $10 million comes from the five-year Utah Solar Incentive Program, already funded by ratepayers, and puts that money for the next five years into this new pilot program. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning March 3, 2016 Page 5 Electronic Poll Vote, March 29, 2016-April 6, 2016 MPO staff are requesting an RPC electronic poll vote to change the timeline process for the current TIP Project Selection Process. We are requesting this now because the MPO Regional Planning Committee is not meeting in April and the next meeting in May would be past the proposed change. Both the MPO Finance Committee (March 10, 2016) and the MPO Technical Advisory Committee (March 28, 2016) unanimously recommended at their meetings to make this proposed change. Please email your vote back by Friday April 1st. Vote “Yes” to make the change or “No” to not make the change. Background: At the last MPO Finance Committee meeting (March 10, 2016) a proposal was made to change the timeline of the current TIP Project Selection Process. Currently the MPO Technical Advisory Committee (MPO TAC) ranks the projects and creates the Project Ranking List. MPO staff use the ranking to apply available funding from the top of the list until funds run out. The MPO TAC then reviews and approves the Funding List. Then both lists are presented to MPO Regional Planning Committee (MPO RPC) for approval. The proposed change would first have the Project Ranking List approved by both MPO TAC and MPO RPC before the Funding List is created. This allows the MPO RPC time to propose any adjustments to the Project Ranking List before funding is applied. Information: What are the Project Ranking List and Funding List? TAC creates Project Ranking List as part of the TIP Project Selection Process based on technical info and field review MPO staff use Project Ranking List to assign funding County funds (most desirable) go to the top ranked projects UDOT/UTA are not eligible for county funds Some projects that should not be federalized also can use the county funds The Project Funding List is the Project Ranking List funded from the top until all projected funds are programmed Why the Proposed Change? Currently TAC recommends the Project Ranking List and Funding List at the same time to RPC It is easier for RPC to make changes to Project Ranking List prior to specific funding being applied Matching funding to projects is complicated There are 7 different funding sources all have different rules Some funding is more desirable What is Proposed?
