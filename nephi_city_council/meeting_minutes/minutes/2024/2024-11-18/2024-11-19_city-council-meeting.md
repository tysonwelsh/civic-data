RETIRING NATURAL GAS SUPERINTENDENT DREW MCPHERSON HONORED:

   Mayor Pro Tempore Skip Worwood read a tribute honoring Drew McPherson for his public service to
Nephi City. Mr. McPherson was hired as a Utility Tech in the Gas Department in September 2001. In
March of 2018 he was promoted to the position of Superintendent. Mr. McPherson had been a loyal,
hardworking and dedicated employee and had served the city with distinction.
   Mr. McPherson was presented with a wooden plaque and a gift. Mr. McPherson expressed his
gratitude to the council for their support and stated that he enjoyed his years with the city.

FY 2024 AUDIT REPORT PRESENTED BY CODY POWELL FROM LARSON AND COMPANY:

   Mr. Cody Powell, representing Larson and Company presented to the council and mayor the certified
annual audit report and financial statements for the fiscal year ending June 30, 2024. He reported that
the city received an unqualified or unmodified audit opinion, and the audit team believes the financial
statements are free from material misstatement. The audit report stated that there were no internal
controls or state compliance findings.

   James Ockey stepped forward and showed two agreements that he had with Nephi City. He stated
that one of the agreements was breached with the floods of 1983 and 1984. He stated that Attorney
Kasey Wright had told him that the one agreement was replaced with an agreement entered in 1985.

meeting to meet with the mayor and council to consider his proposal.

LIBRARY GHOST STORIES VOLUNTEERS HONORED:

   Library Director Ruth Bonzo honored Gale Ludlow who was one of the original organizers of Ghost
Tours. Ghost Tours was a Library sponsored Halloween event that has run for the past ten years. Ms.
Bonzo said that this year they changed from Ghost Tours to Ghost Stories. Director of the Daughters of
the Utah Pioneers (DUP) Museum, JoAnn Dansie gave the library permission to have the Ghost Stories at
the museum.

  The proceeds totaling $240.00 from the Ghost Stories were presented to the DUP for the preservation
of the old jail near the museum. Ms. Bonzo invited Vernice Winn who shared some highlights and
history of the old jail. The jail was built in 1896 and is the largest and most well-preserved jail in the
United States.

ORDINANCE 11-19-2024-A REZONE OF MOUNTAIN COUNTRY EQUIPMENT PROPERTY:

 Mr. Atkinson updated the council that the city had received an application request to zone properties
owned by Joe Humphrey for property, located at approximately 960 N Main Street, from the CU-1
(Combined Use 1) zone to the HC-1 (Highway Commercial 1) zone in anticipation of an automotive
repair business. He stated that the rezone application sits on the edge of a potential highway
commercial pod area that would be an appropriate zone for this area.
   Councilor Travis Worwood moved to adopt Ordinance 11-19-2024-A rezoning the area from CU-1 to
HC-1. Councilor Callaway seconded the motion. The motion passed on the following roll-call vote:
        Yes: Jeramie Callaway, Skip Worwood, Travis Worwood, Shari Cowan
        Absent: JD Parady

ORDINANCE 11-19-2024-B REZONE OF NEPHI WESTERN FEDERAL CREDIT UNION PROPERTY:

ORDINANCE 11-19-2024 BEER LICENSE CODE AMENDMENTS ADOPTED:

   At the council meeting on November 5th, the council had directed Mr. Atkinson to draft an ordinance
amending the Nephi City Code governing beer sales on Sundays. A restaurant owner had recently
contacted the city about receiving a beer license. The State allows local governments to enact
regulations regarding the sale of beer in their community. This restaurant has requested a Class C and D
beer license to accompany a liquor license that will be issued by the State of Utah in preparation for a
full-service bar in the restaurant. When the owner of this restaurant was examining the restrictions for a
Class C and D beer license in the code, it was noted that there were some unusual restrictions regarding
beer sales on Sundays. Ordinance 11-19-2024 was presented to the council for their approval. The
ordinance eliminated the section 3.2.10 (M) that prohibited beer sales on certain Sundays.
  Councilor Cowan complimented the Cody’s Gastro Garage Restaurant owner for researching the city
code and finding the outdated restrictions. Councilor Travis Worwood said that he had received a few

WORK SESSION: 8:35 p.m.


    1. Mr. Atkinson informed the council that Civil Science, the firm that was hired to design the Red
       Cliffs Park and the Crimson Flats ball fields, and the recreation department had put together a
       questionnaire that they would like to submit to the public to help with design for the Red Cliffs
       Park. The survey will be a first step in putting together a design for the park and seeking input
       from the public in that process.
        Mr. Atkinson asked for some feedback from the council. The council directed staff to put the
       survey out and they approved of the questions.

    2. Mr. Atkinson explained to the council that new Utah State requirements needed to be added to
       the School Resource Officer (SRO) contracts. He said staff would like to bring the contract back
       for final approval at the December 3rd council meeting. Attorney Kasey Wright explained that
       school administrators will coordinate to differentiate between school disciplinary issues (school
       administrator responsibility) and criminal issues (SRO responsibility) and respond appropriately,
       de-escalating school-based incidents whenever possible. The purpose of the SRO is to provide
       for and maintain a safe, healthy, and productive learning environment for School District
       students, staff, and patrons. The SRO should act as a liaison between law enforcement and the
       school.
       Councilor Travis Worwood asked Attorney Wright for an update on the parking issues at the
       school. Mr. Wright said by and large the new enforcement and signage has been effective.

    3. The council discussed a draft of a new Subdivision Code Ordinance. Mr. Atkinson stated that
       due to some changes in state law municipalities have had to revise their subdivision code.
       Through the old subdivision approval methods it opened it up to public opinion that the public
       had no power to change anything because if the subdivision met the city code requirements, the
       public, the planning commission or city council could not stop the subdivision.
       Mr. Atkinson shared a diagram of the flow of an application process for a subdivision. Councilor
       Cowan stated that she would still like to be informed on what subdivision applications are
       coming in and the public needs to know when they do have a say on things and when they
       don’t. The suggestion was made to have a link on the city web page better informing the public.

    4.    Seth asked the council if they were in favor of having a study done to connect to the Clover
         Creek substation. The council directed staff to move forward with a study.
