Pre-Council, 3:30 p.m.
1. Welcome and Prayer
Mayor Johnson welcomed everyone and noted that all Councilmembers were present expect for
Councilor Albrecht who will join the meeting at 7:00 p.m. Councilor Hancock gave the Opening
Comment.

2. Tour of Spanish Fork Community Network Fiber
The Council attended a tour of Spanish Fork’s Fiber network department. The Council was able
to receive helpful information about their department, installation process, and customer service.
Mayor Johnson stated that he is grateful that the City is taking the time to research and study the
City’s fiber network options so that they can determine what the best decision will be for the City.

3. Agenda Questions
None.

4. Administrative Report
The Planning Department is identifying some possible areas in the city to consider for a
matching grant that would preserve open space. Options could include a farm, canyon areas, and
along Dry Creek. They are planning to submit an application soon.

Shayne Ruitenbeek gave a legislative update to the Council which included amendments to the
Property Ombudsman Office.

The meeting recessed at approximately 6:30 p.m.
The meeting reconvened at approximately 6:40 p.m.

6. Consideration of Ordinance #09-2020, approval of the Jonsson Park Towns Zone
   Change, approximately 5.3 acres located at 700 South 1900 West, changing the Zoning
   from TH-5 (Transitional Holding) to R-3 (high density residential).
   Brad Mackay was representing the applicant. He stated that they are changing the name to The
   Towns at Snow Springs.

    Thad Pinkerton doesn’t see the zone change as a betterment to the community. He also
    expressed concern that there's no traffic study on 700 South 1700 West. He would like to see
    the city do their due diligence and conduct a traffic study. He was concerned that this is an
    island and that it doesn’t fit in the area. He was also concerned with the infrastructure. He
    disagrees with Alpine School District’s estimates of the average student per household.

    Mr. Powell stated that there is capacity in the sewer and water systems. Mr. Struthers stated
    that 1700 West and 700 South are both collector streets and they are or will be designed to
    handle 7,000 trips and they are well below that threshold right now, so they are built to handle
    additional traffic load. He also stated that they will look at the data they’ve collected for car
    counts in the area.

    Councilor Koivisto stated that this is an island and they won’t have access to mass transit here.
    She believes it’s not inline with the goals and with the overall zoning plan in Lehi.

    Mr. Struthers stated that they already amended the General Plan, so the overall policy was
    changed in December and this zone change is to match the General Plan.

    Mr. Powell stated that they don’t just look at transit for high density area. There are lots of
    other factors to consider. In this area the schools, churches, park, cycle track, and trails were
    all considered as a benefit to this high-density development. Mr. Powell stated that this was

    Councilor Koivisto expressed concern that there was lot of input against this development and

    Mayor Johnson stated that they have had emails and discussions with residents over concerns
    with the mink farms for many years and those are all considered public input too. The
    discussions to solve these solutions are difficult and uncomfortable. There have been many
    discussions on how to mitigate the conflicts with the mink farms. He understands that some
    people are going to be upset, but he believes this is the best resolution to satisfy the
    circumstance as a whole. He stated that he was very careful to ensure that this project would
    not overwhelm the infrastructure.

    Motion:     Councilor Southwick moved approve to Ordinance #09-2020, approval of the
                Jonsson Park Towns Zone Change, approximately 5.3 acres located at 700 South
                1900 West, changing the Zoning from TH-5 (Transitional Holding) to R-3 (high
                density residential); subject to the completion of all Development Review
                Committee and Planning Commission comments. Councilor Hancock seconded the
                motion.

    Councilor Hancock suggested adding to the motion the findings of fact that it enables the 700
    South cycle track to be build, that it will allow a portion of the powerline trail to be constructed
    and the ballpark lights can be turned on.

    comments. Councilor Hancock seconded the amendment to the motion.

8. Consideration of Ordinance #10-2020, approval of the Pine Springs General Plan
   Amendment for 3.09 acres of property located at 225 South 600 East, changing the land
   use designation from LDR (Low Density Residential) to HDR (High Density Residential).
   Dan Ford was the applicant and was representing the Mortimore family whom own the
   property. He stated that they looked at location and surrounding uses and believes this project
   would be a good buffer. He stated that people have historically considered high density housing
   as an affordability play whereas right now they are looking at a change in lifestyle play. He
   also said there is access to transportation corridors and walkability. He stated that there was
   high attendance at the neighborhood meeting, and they were able to answer questions.

    Councilor Koivisto stated that these are not affordable. Mr. Ford clarified that he did not say
    this project was affordable, but rather this would be workforce housing. Councilor Koivisto
    stated that high density can work in the right area, but she doesn’t agree that this is a good
    place for this based on the surrounding zones.

    Judy Evans stated that her property is to the south of this development. She requested that she
    be given some considerations, such as a privacy fence and asked that the access road be moved
    so that it’s not right next to her bedroom window. She was also concerned with losing the front
    of the property if a sidewalk is installed.

    There was discussion about the zoning of Mrs. Evans’ property and why it was zoned
    commercial.

    Emily Mortimore stated that her family has owned the property since 1971 and they’ve seen
    many changes. She didn’t want to see the community change, but she appreciates the people
    that have been brought into her life through that change. She stated that her and her neighbors
    to the east submitted an application for commercial which was not recommended for approval.
    The Planning Commission suggested, and she agrees with them, that higher density here would
    make a good buffer between the commercial and residential properties.

    Skyler Barry stated that he owns the residential property to the east and he would hope that his
    property be considered for higher density in the future. He stated that the other property owners
    to the east are also in approval.

    Richard McAlister is in the neighboring R-1 zone and approves this change. He stated that this
    will be a better place with less impact. He also hopes that the developer can work with Judy
    Evans. It checks all the boxes for a high density location.

    Councilor Condie inquired about the parking concerns from the Karl Malone Center. Mr. Barry
    stated that the overflow parking from the center doesn’t go this far east.

    There was discussion about creating a developer’s agreement to protect the neighbors. Mayor
    Johnson was concerned if ownership changes. There was discussion about the fence along the
    neighboring property to the south and that the placement of the road not be along that same
    property.

    Mr. Ford stated that he agreed to the privacy fence, but he didn’t want the approval to be tied
    to the road placement at this time. He also stated that they want to address the parking concerns
    and they are still working through the concept plan.

    Councilor Albrecht stated that many old time Lehi citizens have seen their community change
    and they probably didn’t ask for that and would now prefer to go somewhere else, so she tries
    to see past the developer and look at the property owner and what their motivations might be.

    Councilor Koivisto stated that she is not against growth or development but that they need to
    be aware of the surrounding zones. Density can be good if it’s done correctly.

    Councilor Hancock stated that it provides a transition from the commercial development, and
    the neighbors to the east would like to convert to high density as well. He stated that even
    though it’s not located by mass transit, it’s as close to two freeway entrances as you can get,
    without driving through residential. Councilor Southwick agreed with his comments.

    Mr. Wood suggested that the developer’s agreement be tied to the zone change.

    Motion:     Councilor Condie moved to Ordinance #10-2020, approval of the Pine Springs
                General Plan Amendment for 3.09 acres of property located at 225 South 600 East,
                changing the land use designation from LDR (Low Density Residential) to HDR
                (High Density Residential); subject to the completion of all Development Review
                Committee and Planning Commission comments. Councilor Albrecht seconded the
                motion.

    Roll Call Vote: Councilor Albrecht, Yes; Councilor Condie, Yes; Councilor Southwick, Yes;
    Councilor Hancock, Yes; and Councilor Koivisto, No. The motion passed 4-1.
