CONSIDERATION OF ORDINANCE NO. 2022-1217-O, AN ORDINANCE ADOPTING
THE 2022, WASHINGTON COUNTY _ AFFORDABLE HOUSING PLAN AS THE
MODERATE INCOME HOUSING PLAN ELEMENT OF THE GENERAL PLAN

Community Development Director Scott Messel explained that the moderate income housing plan
element of the General Plan incorporated updated state requirements for a moderate-income
housing plan. The proposed plan outlines strategies to meet the goals and objectives to improve
affordable housing options in Washington County.

The following goals are recommended to maintain adequate attainable housing choices for all
those who wish to reside in Washington County:

e Amend land use regulations to allow for higher density and new, affordable residential
development in commercial or mixed-use zones near major transit investment corridors.
Implement zoning incentives for affordable housing units in new development.

e Demonstrate creation of, or participate in, a community land trust program for affordable
housing.

CONSIDERATION __OF__ RESOLUTION NO. ___R-2022-2998, _A | RESOLUTION
ALLOCATING AND APPROVING THE DISTRIBUTION OF FUNDING FROM THE
USDA AND THE STATE TO WASHINGTON COUNTY FROM THE SECURE RURAL
SCHOOLS (SRS) ACT

Public Works Director Ron Whitehead explained that the SRS Act transfers a percent of forest
grazing and timber sales receipts from the federal government back to the states and counties. Once
received, the County elects to distribute the funds as follows: Eighty-Five percent (85%) of the
SRS Act funds for Title 1 projects for schools and roads; eight percent (8%) of the SRS Act funds
for Title II for special projects on federal land; and seven percent (7%) of the SRS Act funds for
Title II projects for specific purposes of the County including emergency services on federal lands.

CONSIDERATION OF RESOLUTION NO. R-2022-2999, A RESOLUTION REGARDING
THE BLM’S NEPA PROCESS RELATED TO A LAND EXCHANGE IN EMERY

COUNTY

Deputy County Attorney Devin Snow explained that the Bureau of Land Management (BLM)
invited Washington Count to participate as a cooperating agency in a National Environmental
Policy Act (NEPA) process that analyzes a land exchange in Emery County between the BLM and
School of Institution of Trust Lands (SITLA). As part of the exchange it may be necessary to
equalize value between BLM and SITLA to facilitate the exchange in Emery County and other
areas. To do that BLM and SITLA may use equalization parcels in Washington County. This letter
would request cooperating-agency status.

MOTION: Motion by Commissioner Snow to Adopt Resolution No. R-2022-2999, A
Resolution Regarding the BLM’s NEPA Process Related to a Land Exchange
in Emery County. Motion seconded by Commissioner Almquist and carried
by unanimous vote, with Commissioners Iverson, Snow and Almquist voting

aye.

DISCUSSION AND POTENTIAL APPROVAL OF LETTER REGARDING ELECTION
SECURITY

Commissioner Snow recognized that there have been concerns expressed from citizens in regards
to election integrity. To provide additional transparency of the elections process, the
commissioners would like to send a letter to call on the Lieutenant Governor in conjunction with

the State Legislature to:

e Exercise its exclusive authority to perform primary election and general election audits in
sample counties across Utah for future or past elections. Currently, 27 of the 29 counties
utilize ES&S ballot counters. All elections should be audited in addition to a traditional audit
process of random selection. All counties perform pre-canvas machine count totals, but not
all counties are able to provide individual batch summaries and there is not a process for an
independent, third-party to validate the election results. We recommend the Legislature
direct the Lieutenant Governor’s office to enlist a reputable third-party auditor to validate
the accuracy of ballot counter machines with a sample hand count as part of the audit process.

e Establish an Election Commission to annually review and implement measures to further
strengthen and secure election processes and to make all findings and policies open to the

public.

MOTION: Motion by Commissioner Snow to Approve the Letter Regarding Election
Integrity. Motion seconded by Commissioner Almquist and carried by
unanimous vote, with Commissioners Iverson, Snow and Almquist voting aye.

08/19/2022 @ 1530, Flooding — Zion National Park — Man Hours — 32

SAR responded to Zion National Park on a Flash Flood. Reports were several people
were caught in the flood. SAR assisted the Zion SAR to account for everyone. Were were
release when we were notified they believe everyone was accounted for and out.

08/21/2022 @ 0530, Missing Person — Zion National Park —- Man Hours — 332

SAR responded to assist Zion National Park on a missing 30 year old female related to the
flash flood from two day prior. SAR Teams worked for two day searching the Virgin
River from the Narrows to Virgin. We also had Drones and pilots working with Zion
SAR. The victim was located around 1530 hours on Monday. She was 6.2 miles down
river from the last known location. She did not survive the flood.
08/23/2022 @ 2100, Stranded Climbers — Kolob Terrace Road - Man Hours — 28

SAR responded to four stranded climbers off Kolob Terrance Road. One climbers ropes
tangled on a rappel and he was unable to descend. That stranded the other 3 climber a
couple of hundred feet up the cliff. SAR located the subjects and assisted the stuck
climber down. They rappelled the other climbers off without incident. No EMS was
needed.

08/27/2022 @ 0853, Mountain Bike Accident — South Hills Santa Clara — Man Hours — 52

SAR responded to a report of a male mountain biker with a traumatic injury south of
Santa Clara in the desert reserve area. SAR members arrived and made their way to the
patient. It was reported the patient went off of a cliff and was down the hill. Life flight
was requested and arrived. Life Flight was unable to land at the subject. It was reported
the patient had a possible broken neck previous injuries to his back. The DPS helicopter
was requested so the patient could be safely lifted to where the life flight helicopter was
located. SAR members helped with securing the patient for hoist. Once hoisted, SAR
members helped with getting the patient onto the life flight helicopter for transport to the
hospital.

09/03/2022 @ 1947, ATV Accident — Toquerville Falls - Man Hours — 42

SAR responded to a ATV accident at Toquerville Falls. A female and 7 year old child
were on a ATV and were thrown. The 7 year old male was in critical condition. Life
Flight responded and flew the child to SGRH. The mother was brought out to a waiting
ambulance by SAR. The child did not survive his injuries.

09/12/2022 @ 1048, Mountain Bike Accident — Gem Trail — Man Hours — 24

SAR responded to a 56 year old male that crashed on the Gem Trail. The male failed to
negotiate the switchbacks and fell around 100 feet. SAR and Hurricane EMS located him
and began treatment. Life Flight was called to assist. The male was packaged up and
placed in the helicopter. He was flown to SGRH for further treatment. He was not
expected to survive.
09/13/2022 @ 2204, Stranded Hiker — Cedar Pockets, Az. - Man Hours — 36

SAR was requested by the Mohave County Sheriff’s Office to assist on a stranded 60 year
old male in Cedar Pockets. The male had gone across the Virgin River earlier in the day.
When he returned the river was flowing between 1500-2000 cubic feet. Another storm was
rolling in and he had no supplies or shelter. The SAR Swift Water Team set up a rescue
plan and made it across the river. They put the male into safety gear and brought him
back across. The subject was release to a Mohave County Deputy. No EMS was needed.

09/18/2022 @ 1119, Injured Hiker — Sand Hollow State Park — Man Hours — 12

SAR responded to a 32 year old male that fell near the jumping rocks at Sand Hollow

State Park. The male had an apparent broken leg/ankle. SAR and EMS stabilized his
injury. He was carried to a SAR Boat and take to the main boat ramp. He was turned
over to the ambulance and taken to SGRH for treatment.
