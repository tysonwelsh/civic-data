      Mayor Kristie Overson had attended two ribbon cuttings at Legacy Plaza-- at PetCo and Five Below
in October. There would be one more later in the week and then Legacy Plaza would be full. She enjoyed
meeting with the business owners and appreciated the support of council members who joined her there, as

Taylorsville         can   fit into ChamberWest'   s goals   and   strategies   for the coming year.

She attended a professional development event the day before where Congressman Burgess Owen spoke
about key issues. She appreciated how supportive he had been of Taylorsville, and she mentioned that there
was a good chance he might approve a grant application for some new police equipment for TVPD.

Taylorsville had recently celebrated its employees at Top Golf where everyone had a great time relaxing
and golfing. She appreciated the Council' s understanding of the importance of valuing city employees and
agreeing to budget for the event.

Taylorsville' s legislative team had met with Rep. Jim Dunnigan a couple of weeks before to discuss land
use and affordable housing, among other things. Rep. Dunnigan had been working closely with the city and
asking questions, with city representatives sharing Taylorsville' s priorities.

At the invitation of Salt Lake County Council Member Suzanne Harrison, she and Council Member
Knudsen had attended a community town hall. In addition to Council Member Harrison, they were joined
by Salt Lake County Deputy Mayor Erin Litvack and West Valley Mayor Lang. Together they had a good
discussion about the issues that were important to each entity.

Speaking of Salt Lake County, the final recommendations for the next round of TRCC funding( Tourism,
Recreation, Culture and Convention) would be made at the end of the week. Taylorsville had submitted an
application and Mayor Overson was confident that we would be awarded some funding, although how
much was unclear at this point. That recommendation would be forwarded to Salt Lake County.

Taylorsville' s annual art show was held the previous week at the Mid-Valley Performing Arts Center. It
was a remarkable event with many talented artists. She thanked the Council Members for supporting the
Arts Council, and specifically Council Member Burgess for his work on the Board. She also wished to
thank Ms. Heineman for helping set up for the event.

        The Mayor explained that 4700 South continued to be beautifully transformed and said it was amazing
to see what the addition of some rocks and trees could do. She was delighted that the city could help beautify
I-215 along with the state.

Regarding     the     Mid- Valley   Express (
                                           formerly BRT), Taylorsville had the opportunity to add some

The 2024 General Election would take place on Tuesday, November 5`h. There was no " early voting" in
Taylorsville this year, although city hall would be a vote center on election day from 7am to 8pm.

3.         REPORTS


          3. 2        Taylorsville- Bennion Improvement       District— Mark Chalk




          General Manager Mark Chalk explained that every water system in the country was required to
provide a lead service line inventory to the Environmental Protection Agency, as required under the Lead
and Copper Rule Revisions which aim to significantly reduce exposure to lead through drinking water.

The District had 17, 354 water meters. They randomly selected a sample size of service lines to ensure a
statistical   confidence   level of 95%   and contacted   those customers.   The service   line material   needed to be
verified in three distinct locations: between the main and the meter, between the meter and the home, and
inside the home itself. Mr. Chalk said that no lead pipes had been found in Taylorsville thus far and he was
almost positive there were none. But they still needed to go through the verification process.

Mr. Chalk wrapped up his presentation by announcing that the District had been named Best Overall Water
by the American Waterworks Association.
