4          An ordinance amending the zone map classification of real property, generally located at
           2000 N Canyon Road, from the one-family residential (R1.8) zone to the one-family
           residential (R1.6) zone. Pleasant View neighborhood. (PLRZ20250097) 0:35:00

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2025-35, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Nancy Robison, Planner, presented a request to rezone the property from R1-8 to R1-6 to allow for 19
additional single-family building lots at roughly the same elevation as Canyon Road. She explained that
grading the hillside would make it possible to extend utilities from nearby parcels, which aligns with the
Public Works Department's preferences. Staff support the request, noting that Provo needs more single-
family housing and that the proposed subdivision would benefit the area. With direct access to Canyon
Road, the project would not impact surrounding neighborhoods.

Chair Garrett asked what geotechnical work has been completed so far for the proposed development
and what additional studies or evaluations would be required before any permits could be issued.

Ms. Robison responded that, while she is not an engineer, the applicant has submitted a geotechnical
report that has been reviewed by the city's engineering staff. The report indicates the soil is stable, and
reducing the elevation of the hill at the front of the property would not pose a problem for surrounding
properties. She also referenced a visual diagram showing the current and proposed elevation changes.

Councilor MacKay asked how many feet there are between the existing homes at the top and where the
hillside would be cut.

Ms. Robison said she did not have that exact information but emphasized that this stage is only for the
re-zone. She noted that city engineers would still review all necessary measurements to ensure safety.

Councilor MacKay clarified that the proposal would not come back to the Council after this, and Ms.
Robison confirmed that it would not.

Councilor Christensen asked how concerns related to the nearby water canal and related issues are
being addressed as part of the proposed development.

Ms. Robison explained that the applicant is aware of the issues related to the water canal and would be
responsible for addressing them, either by piping or rerouting, based on the direction of the Canal
Company or Provo Public Works. The Canal Company conducts its own engineering review and has the
final say on how the irrigation water is protected. Public Works would be involved with other grading-
related matters, but decisions regarding the canal fall under the authority of the Canal Company.

Chair Garrett invited the applicant to add anything to the discussion.

Tyson Reynolds, applicant, explained that the canal runs through the property and will be redirected to
follow its current general path, with adjustments made to accommodate the development. Each lot will
have access to pressurized irrigation, and sufficient water shares are available to be allocated to future
homeowners. He confirmed they are waiting for final design approval from the Canal Company. He
clarified that the request to rezone from R1.8 to R1.6 is due to the land’s topography. While the current
zoning would typically allow for about 21 lots on flat ground, the slope and grading challenges make that
impractical. The rezone would allow for more suitable lot shapes and a more aesthetically pleasing
layout.

Councilor MacKay asked if there would be an HOA, if the homes would all be sold, and if there would be
homeowner occupancy required within the HOA.

Mr. Reynolds confirmed that there would be an HOA and that all of the homes would be sold. He
addressed concerns about rental restrictions, stating that he does not support imposing such limitations
on single family homes. While he understands their place in apartment or condominium settings, he
believes restrictions would be a burden on homeowners and could limit who is able to live there. He
added that the higher price point of the homes, due to land and development costs, will likely deter
issues related to rentals, as the homes will be geared toward owner-occupants.

Councilor MacKay asked what the price point would be for the homes.

Mr. Reynolds said they are looking at close to $1 million per home.

Chair Garrett asked if they were intending to market and sell the lots or build the homes and sell them.

Mr. Reynolds stated that he plans to build most of the homes himself and then sell them, noting that
due to costs and market conditions, this approach makes the most financial sense. However, he is open
to allowing individuals to purchase a specific lot and use their own home plan and builder if desired.

Councilor Whipple asked for clarification about a small 2,000-square-foot section, which had been
briefly discussed earlier in the work meeting. She wanted to know what the plan is for that specific piece
of land.

Mr. Reynolds said he is considering using the small 2,000-square-foot area as a community amenity,
such as a playset or picnic gazebo, maintained by the HOA. He agreed it would serve well as a buffer
between the new development and the nearby apartment complex.

Chair Garrett opened public comment.

Matt Wheelright, of Provo representing District 1, spoke on behalf of neighbors in the area affected by
the proposed development. He commended the developer for their continued engagement and noted
that, overall, residents are pleased with the direction of the plan and supportive of the zone change. He
acknowledged that earlier versions of the project caused significant concern, especially due to proposed
retaining walls. However, after reviewing the updated grading plan at the Planning Commission, much of
that anxiety subsided. He noted there is still some lingering concern about hillside stability and
suggested a bond or similar assurance for landscaping and potential impacts to nearby homes. Overall,
he conveyed that the community views the current plan as a major improvement and is generally
supportive.

Paul Evans, of Provo, praised the progress made on the proposed development, especially the shift
toward creating multiple single-family lots, which aligns with the community's goal of increasing
residential opportunities in Provo. He noted that the location is well-suited for this type of housing.
However, he also raised a concern based on recent trends in the neighborhood, where homes are
purchased by parents and deeded to student children, who then live in the home and rent out additional
space—essentially turning single-family homes into multi-unit student rentals. He warned that while the
current plan is positive, there is a long-term risk that these homes may not remain owner-occupied. He
concluded by commending Mr. Reynolds for his continued engagement and dedication to the project.

Chair Garrett closed public comment and invited a council discussion.

Councilor MacKay expressed strong support for the development, commending the developer for
building high-end, single-family homes in a suitable location. She said she is confident the homes will sell
well and emphasized that this type of housing is much needed in the community.

Chair Garrett called for a vote.

      Vote:         The motions passed 6:0 with Councilors Bogdin, Christensen, Garrett, Handley,
                    MacKay, and Whipple in favor.
