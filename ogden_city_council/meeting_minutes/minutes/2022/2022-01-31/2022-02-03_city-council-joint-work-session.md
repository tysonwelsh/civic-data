Pathway High School/Ogden School District and Ogden-Weber
Technical College Partnership

Ogden School District Assistant Superintendent Chad Carpenter shared the concept for the Pathway High School project
and said the District has partnered with leaders of the Ogden-Weber Technical College on this project. The group is working to
locate a building on the Tech College campus for the school and students will be able to take courses that align with a specific
career pathway of their choosing. Pathway students will complete core education classes such as math, science, and English. The
target enrollment is 150 students, but there is a greater need/demand for a space at the school and the District will consider a
lottery system to select students. Superintendent Rasmussen stated he is very excited about this project; it will enable students to
expedite their high school diploma while also working towards a trade certificate. He expects the program will have lasting
positive impacts for the entire community.

Council member White inquired as to the career pathways students will have access to. Mr. Carpenter stated they Tech
College offers cosmetology, manufacturing, digital marketing, and many more; students will take their core education classes on
the campus but will also have access to the services they need to transition to post-secondary education.

Council member Choberka asked if transportation will be provided to students attending the school that are not of driving
age. Mr. Carpenter stated that the District will provide transportation from their regular school to the Pathway school and once on
campus, students can walk or other forms of transportation to get to the building in which their trade services are located.

Land Use Committee

Ogden School District Business Administrator Zane Woolstenhulme reported on recent project to demolish old school
facilities; as a result of these projects, there are several parcels of vacant land that are being unused. The District has assembled a
Land Use Planning Committee that will make recommendations regarding the disposition of properties. According to the law, if
the District decides to declare the parcels of surplus property, the City will have the right of first refusal to acquire them. He hopes
that the Committee will have a final recommendation to present to the Board within six months.

Demographic Study

Ogden School District Superintendent Luke Rasmussen then provided a review of the District’s demographic study
intended to provide guidance to the District for school years (SY) 2021-203 1; this information is used to inform school boundary
adjustments responsive to growth throughout the District. Due to open enrollment policies and charter schools, parents have
options where their child is educated. He reviewed a chart indicating how many births in the county translate into an enrolled
kindergarten student five years later. The market share line indicates the yearly percentage, forming a trend line over time. For
example, in 2016, 3,877 children were born in Weber County. It is estimated that those children will begin kindergarten five years
later, SY2021. He noted 802 students made up the K-class during the reported fall snapshot date, October |, 2021. Therefore, the
Ogden School District captured 20.7% of those estimated students. Closely related to the Student Yield Factors are planned
residential development units. Planned residential development data is collected to determine the number of new residential units
that will be built over the time frame of the student population forecasts. The District’s consultant was provided information from
the local municipalities regarding planned or active construction. Currently, there are approximately 2,515 active, planned, or
future units expected in the district over the next ten years. He concluded the data is very helpful to the District in determining
appropriate school boundary adjustments over the coming years. He thanked Ogden City Administration for their partnership in
providing information that was considered in this study.

Energy Program (CREP) survey; the CREP is the first of its kind in the nation and its goal is to serve as a model for local

Ms. Eller-Smith then noted that the survey will be conducted by the Weber State University (WSU) Community Engaged
Learning Center under the direction of Dr. Katharine French-Fuller. The purpose of the survey is to determine whether business
owners and residents of Ogden City would participate in the CREP and what financial burden business owners and residents are
willing to bear to participate. About 10,000 surveys will be delivered to select households and businesses and WSU needs about
1,500 responses for a +/- four percent margin of error. They are also seeking a proportional sample from four of the City’s
Municipal Districts. Surveys will be available in both English and Spanish and links to an electronic version of the survey will be
sent to households for which email addresses are presently available. Post cards with QR codes will also be sent to households for
which the City does not have an email address. Door-to-door surveying will be managed by WSU students and the survey period
is approximately 30 days. Additional public outreach efforts will include advertising in the City’s water bill, on the City marquee,
and on the City’s various social media pages.
Members of the Board indicated they are interested in learning more about the Program and would like to hear the survey
results once available.
