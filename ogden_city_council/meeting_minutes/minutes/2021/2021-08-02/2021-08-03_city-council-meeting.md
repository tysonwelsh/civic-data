    Gender Equity Study
              Representatives from Management Services Department will present the results of the Gender Equity Study completed
    by Evergreen, LLC. This study was requested by the Council in preparation for the Fiscal Year (FY) 2022 Budget discussions, and
    in anticipation of changes and recommendations resulting from implementation of the 2020 Classification, Compensation, and
    Benefits Study.
              Hmnan Resources Manager Judkins explained that in February of 2021 the Council requested that the Administration
    complete a Gender Equity Study to determine whether there were any significant differences in compensation between male and
    female employees; the study was completed and transmitted to the Council in April of 2021. She presented a graph illustrating
    compensable factor scoring from the job assessment tool (JAT), which indicates that employees are, for the most part, being
    compensated fairly for the level of work perfonned. She then presented a chart comparing Ogden City's general population based
    upon data from the US Census Bureau versus the gender makeup of the Ogden City employee workforce for general employees,
    Police, and Fire. The data showed there is a slightly lower proportion offemales in the Ogden City workforce compared with the
    general population of females in Ogden City. The difference was more pronounced in public safety positions. Evergreen also
    evaluated wages for male and female employees based on tenure. Evergreen found that across all job classifications, females are
    paid 10.1 percent less than males. Evergreen fmther evaluated the individual job classifications that employ both males and
    females. Evergreen found that in the positions that have both male and female employees, females are paid on average 0.5 percent
    less than men. Evergreen found no evidence of intentional gender bias in the compensation system. Evergreen also detennined
    that many anomalies would be addressed by implementing the recommendations from the full Evergreen Classification,
/   Compensation, and Benefits Study. Evergreen made three recommendations related to the Gender Equity Study:
                  1. Identify key data points and modify existing systems to record and regularly update gender salary infonnation.
                  2. Research the underlying cause for any data anomalies and take corrective action, as appropriate.

Lynn Community Plan Amendment
          A developer has petitioned to amend the Lynn Community Plan at an area near 2nd Street and Wall Avenue. The
developer has also applied to rezone the property from R-1-6 to R-3/CO to be able to build a series oftownhomes. However, the
rezone is contingent upon the approval of the Lynn Community Plan because the R-3/CO zone is not compatible with the current
community plan. The Planning Commission has recommended to approve the petition to amend the community plan despite the
fact that a similar plan amendment was denied in 2019. The Planning Commission placed a condition that all properties located
within the amended area must be acquired by the developer and developed under a single development agreement.
          Planning Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the Plan amendment petition.
He noted the petitioners are John Strickler, Renae Woods, Betty Jones, Shawn Strong, and Bob Giboney and he identified the
locations of the properties they own in the Community Plan area. He added that Mr. Strong has acquired Mr. Strickler's property
since the petition was first filed. The group proposes to amend the Community Plan to include the option to rezone from R-1-6
(single family) to R-3/CO (multiple-family/conditional overlay). There is a separate petition to change the zoning of properties at
the comer of 2nd Street and Wall A venue. The group also proposes the same conditions apply to property under a conditional
overlay zoning, with a development agreement, at the property at 120 West 2nd Street, but that the conditions extend beyond the
comer property. He discussed the history of previous petitions submitted for zoning of these subject properties, noting that they
were seeking R-5 zoning in 2019. Additional petitioners have signed on to this current petition and additional surrounding support
has been obtained. In November of 2020 the Council considered a development proposal from Mr. Strong that included 50
townhomes in the initial phase of the development project; he presented a rendering of that proposed development and noted that
the project would actually be a second phase to current developments in the area. The project would address properties that have
fallen into disrepair by providing new construction that reflects the historical architecture of the area. However, staff was
concerned about the isolation of remaining single-family homes in the neighborhood as the City has a policy prohibiting the
creation of peninsulas or islands of zoning. The Planning Commission considered the Community Plan amendment in November
of2020 and voted to amend the Community Plan by allowing the option to rezone properties beyond the comer parcel from R-1-6
to R-3/CO, with the recommendation that the plan language include conditions that rezoning does not isolate single-family
properties; that there is an appropriate transition between single-family neighborhoods and family developments; and that all
properties between Fort Bingham Subdivision, Wall A venue, and the north side of 2nd Street must be acquired and developed
under one development agreement. The three dissenting Planning Commissioners cited the following concerns:
                 1. The Lynn Community Plan should be revisited beforeadding more amendments.
                2. R-1-6 zoning already provides for moderate housing and offers the best opportunity for home ownership.
                 3. The amendment would isolate homes, and the extension oftownhomes would not create a good transition to the
                     existing single-family homes.
          The Council discussed an email they received from property owners in the area, the Keogh's, which cites their concerns
about the proposed change as they feel that it negatively impacts attempts to preserve the history of the area. Additionally, Council
member Hyer stated that he has heard from a realtor who cautioned against approving the change and allowing the townhome
development based upon his assessment that there is a market for the historical homes; many buyers are looking to purchase these
types of properties to restore/renovate them. He added that he is concerned about the dramatic increase in density and high-density
developments throughout the City. Mr. Montgomery stated he understands those concerns and indicated the Council must try to
balance the points that have been raised on both sides of this issue; in this case there are four parcels of property, the size of which
is difficult to fmd in the current real estate market. The only way to proceed with the type of development Mr. Strong is proposing
is to combine the four properties. The Council must consider the goals of the Community Plan and determine whether new
development in the Plan area should be small lots or multi-family developments, or if a mixture of development types is
appropriate for the community. There were many more people who spoke in opposition to the petition than were in favor of it and
their comments reflect the concerns voiced by Council member Hyer. Council member Hyer stated he would like for Mr.
Montgomery to take time to review the proposal that has been developed by other property owners in the area. Council member

1117 Country Hills Drive Rezone
          A petition was submitted by a developer to rezone approximately .75 acres at 1117 Country Hills Drive. The property is
currently located in the P-I (Professional/Institutional) zone, and the proposal is to change the zoning designation to R-4 (Multi-
Family Residential). Doing so would allow the developer to build more multi-family housing and convert an existing commercial/
residential building into a higher density residential building. The rezone request does not conflict with the Southeast Ogden
Community Plan, and the Planning Commission unanimously recommended to approve the rezone petition.
          Planning Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the petition; the applicant is
seeking to rezone two parcels from PI to R-4; the PI zone allows development of multi-family dwellings located on the floors
above professional and business offices. A rezone is required to develop additional residential units on the property and replace
existing office space with three new units. The subject parcel measures 32,636 square feet with 92 feet of frontage along Country
Hills Drive. The existing building is 24,000 square feet in size; it contains 3,963 square feet of office space and four apartment
units. The rezone would give the developer the ability to convert commercial space in the existing building into three residential
units and construct an eight-unit multi-family housing project on the property. It is the responsibility of the Planning Commission
to verify that this petition follows City code and all associated community plans. On April 7, 2021 the Planning Commission met
and recommended approval of the rezone. The recommendation is based on the following fmdings:
                1. The rezone follows a defmite edge to development and buffering is provided between different types of uses.
                2. The rezone reflects the prevailing character of the area and matches a variety of mixed uses that are located
                     nearby.
                3. The zoning boundaries do not cut across individual parcels. 4. The R-4 rezone supports the Southeast Ogden
                     Community Plan.
          The Southeast Ogden Community Plan states that rezone possibilities for this property include commercial and multi-
family options. Because the R-4 zone is a multi-family designation, the Planning Commission found the request to be in
compliance with the community plan.
          The Council briefly discussed the development concept, focusing on road improvements and parking accommodations,
and concluded to place an action item on the August 17 meeting agenda.
