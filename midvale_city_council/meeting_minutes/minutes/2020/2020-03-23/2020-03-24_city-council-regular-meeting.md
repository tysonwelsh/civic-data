A.	CONSIDER RESOLUTION NO. 2020-R-2011 TEMPORARILY MODIFYING THE CITY’S ORDINANCE REGARDING ELECTRONIC MEETINGS DUE TO THE ONGOING EMERGENCY
Lisa Garner said on March 6, 2020 Governor Gary Herbert issued an Executive Order declaring a State of Emergency regarding the COVID-19 Virus.  On March 13, 2020, Mayor Robert Hale declared a Local State of Emergency due to the global outbreak of COVID-19. Lt. Governor Gary Herbert suspended the provisions to the Open Meetings Act.  In order to maintain transparency and public participation in the meetings while not creating public gatherings.  This resolution has been prepared for the council’s approval to suspend the rules to temporally modifying the city’s ordinance regarding electronic meetings due to the ongoing emergency. Employees will be attending electronically.  Individuals interested in participating in the meeting will be remotely and in the open portions of the meeting by live audio or video transmission by Facebook live streaming method or listening to live audio on www.midvalecity.org.  All available ways to participate in the meeting will be listed on the meeting agenda and at www.midvalecity.org.  All of the participants except for the City Council and select city staff will be muted in order to minimize noise and maintain order.  Residents that are interested may submit questions and comments by electronic means during the appropriate portions of the meeting. Public comments will be collected by email or on a streaming platform.  The comments will be read to the council. If a video conference becomes disruptive, or disorderly the council may vote to remove the participant.  If further disruption occurs, city council may vote to remove all participants.

Lesley Burns said the Midvale Planning and Zoning Commission is comprised of five full time members and two alternate members. The Commission is responsible for administering specific adopted land use regulations and making recommendations to the City Council on land use policies and regulations as established in Utah Code 10-9a-301 and 302. Commissioners are appointed for four-year terms and are expected to participate in land use/commissioner training, as well as prepare for and attend meetings two times a month. For a City volunteer position, it requires a substantial commitment.

Richard Judkins and Kass Wallin, two longstanding Full Members, resigned from the Commission effective February 29 leaving two vacant seats. The City solicited residents
interested in applying to be on the Commission through the City’s website, social media, and word of mouth. Nine Planning Commissioner applications were submitted and reviewed, and five applicants were called for interviews. The two Alternate Members on the Commission, Robyn Anderson and Dustin Snow, were also including in the consideration to fill the two vacancies.

After careful consideration of all of the applicants and the current Commission, I am forwarding the following appointments to the Planning and Zoning Commission for the City Council’s consent:

• Robyn Anderson appointed as a Full Member through March 2024
• Dustin Snow appointed as a Full Member through March 2024
• Candice Erickson appointed as an Alternate Member through March 2024
• Bart Benson appointed as an Alternate Member through March 2024

Appointees Biographies

Robyn Anderson has served on the Planning and Zoning Commission as an Alternate Member since January 2019. She grew up and spent her early adulthood in Midvale before moving to other areas including time spent out of the country. Most recently she has lived in Midvale for 18 years and currently resides in District 1. Robyn brings a great knowledge of the area and understanding of the community’s values to Commission discussions. 	B.	CONSIDER FINAL SUBDIVISION APPROVAL FOR 3-LOT JORDAN 	BLUFFS LOT 201 AMENDED SUBDIVISION LOCATED AT 983 WEST 	CENTER STREET
Alex Murphy said the proposed Jordan Bluffs Lot 201 Amended Subdivision plat includes 3 lots on approximately 150 acres bounded by Center Street on the north, Bingham Junction Boulevard on the east, and the Jordan River on the west and south. This request for final subdivision plat approval was submitted by Ben Seastrand on behalf of Gardner Jordan Bluffs, LC to dedicate roadway and park space, which results in the creation of new lots.

All subdivisions require a review and recommendation from the Planning Commission and approval from the City Council with public hearings held by each body. The Planning Commission conducted a public hearing on this subdivision plat and issued a recommendation to approve the preliminary plat on February 12, 2020. The Council conducted a public hearing on the requested subdivision on March 3, 2020 and approved the preliminary plat with the following conditions:

1. The applicant shall prepare a final subdivision plat to be reviewed and approved by the City Engineer, Fire Marshal, and City Council.
2. Reconstruction of the Bingham Junction Boulevard median at the new intersection with Binary Way shall be addressed during site development plans. A note to this effect shall be included on the plat.
3. The applicant shall provide evidence that courtesy notices have been sent to Dominion Energy, Rocky Mountain Power, Xfinity, Utopia, and Century Link regarding the utility easements on the subdivision plat prior to final approval.
4. Prior to recording the final subdivision plat, the applicant shall:
a. Obtain duty to serve letters for water and sewer service.
b. Remove the construction trailer and associated improvements located near the intersection of Ivy Drive and Bingham Junction Boulevard.

The applicant has prepared a final subdivision plat, including the required note, and provided evidence the required courtesy notices have been provided to the affected utility companies. The City Engineer and Area Fire Inspector have reviewed and approved the plat. All that remains is preparing the plat Mylar for signatures and removing the construction trailer and associated improvements before recording the plat.
