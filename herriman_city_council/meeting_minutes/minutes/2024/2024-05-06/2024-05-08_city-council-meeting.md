         4.1. Property Encroachment Enforcement Update – Brent Adamson, HPD operations
              Commander
May 8, 2024 – City Council Minutes                                                    Page 3 of 13


      HPD Operations Commander Brent Adamson provided a detailed update on the City’s efforts
      to address property encroachments over the past two years. He outlined the process that
      had been implemented, which followed City Ordinances and utilized specialized software for
      case management. Initially his team faced 84 cases, and since the last update provided, 18
      cases have been successfully resolved with 25 remaining active. Commander Adamson
      offered an overview of the process where notices are served to property owners, allowing
      them ten days to respond. If no response is received, a second notice is issued, resetting the
      timeframe. The code enforcement officers act as liaisons, explaining the issue and negotiating
      solutions with property owners. Special requests or issues raised by homeowners are
      forwarded to Commander Adamson for appropriate handling within city departments.
      Commander Adamson explained the approach is flexible, allowing property owners to guide
      the resolution process. He emphasized communication and cooperation rather than imposing
      strict deadlines. Some cases have taken over 600 days to resolve, reflecting the city's patient
      approach, particularly understanding seasonal constraints like winter weather.

      Unresolved cases where property owners fail to respond or cooperate after repeated notices,
      the city escalates the process to an administrative law judge. Hearings are convened where
      both sides present their case, and the judge aims to facilitate compliance rather than impose
      penalties. Default judgments have been issued in cases where property owners failed to
      engage in the process despite notices. Commander Adamson addressed concerns about
      financial burdens on homeowners, acknowledging cases where costs exceeded $20,000. The
      City has allowed flexibility in timelines for compliance based on individual circumstances,
      avoiding unnecessary expenses like surveys unless critical.

      The Council questioned potential fines or external services for abatement. Commander
      Adamson clarified that while such measures are under consideration, the focus remains on
      achieving compliance and minimizing financial impact on residents. He emphasized the
      collaborative nature of the process, involving multiple city departments and officials to
      ensure fair and effective enforcement. He stated the city's goal was to achieve compliance
      through dialogue and understanding rather than punitive measures, maintaining a balance
      between regulatory requirements and community needs.

          4.3. Herriman City Streetlight Banner Program and External Printing Services –
               Wendy Thomas, Assistant City Manager
      Assistant City Manager Wendy Thomas emphasized that recent calculations show
      discrepancies in costs despite initial estimates provided by the Finance Department in
      relation to the street light banner program. She outlined the operational challenges,
      particularly on streets without shoulders, which require police assistance for safety during
      banner changes. Assistant City Manager Thomas highlighted the expenses per banner pole,
      including removal costs not initially factored in. Mayor Palmer expressed his surprise at the
      shortfall in covering costs and the potential limitation of allowing only governmental banners
      to avoid First Amendment complications.

      The Council discussed quality concerns with banners provided by schools and other groups,
      noting issues like poor durability in inclement weather. Assistant City Manager Thomas
      suggested co-branding banners with local schools to enhance community pride but raised
      concerns about the City subsidizing these initiatives. Councilmembers Shields and Hodges
      proposed limiting banner placements to safer streets and implementing time restrictions to
      streamline operations and reduce costs.

      The Council discussed the logistical challenges such as coordinating banner rotations
      between different school groups and ensuring compliance with safety standards. They
      considered setting specific timeframes for banner installations to minimize staff workload
      and operational disruptions. Mayor Palmer stressed the importance of balancing community
      engagement with practical resource allocation, acknowledging the strain on city resources
      caused by extensive banner management.

      The Council debated whether to allow banners during specific school sessions and discussed
      potential safety risks and community expectations. The Council recommended having a clear
      policy in place to regulate banner placements, ensuring they align with City priorities and
      operational capacities. The Council also considered outsourcing banner printing to alleviate
      some operational burdens and costs associated with the program. The Council looked for
      flexibility in the policy implementation and wanted to be responsive to the community while
May 8, 2024 – City Council Minutes                                                     Page 5 of 13


      maintaining efficiency during operations. Mayor Palmer underscored the importance of
      managing expectations around banner placements to maintain safety and operational
      integrity within the city's resources.

         4.4. Utah Legislature 2024 Interim Schedule and Items – Nathan Cherpeski, City
               Manager
      City Manager Nathan Cherpeski highlighted key topics of concern, notably focusing on
      housing discussions that were expected to dominate the session. He underscored the
      importance of monitoring proposed sales tax changes to ensure they wouldn't undermine the
      City's financial operations. Mayor Palmer also drew attention to potential revisions in impact
      fees and conditional land use ordinances, emphasizing their significance for future City
      planning and development.

      The Council discussion evolved into a strategic discussion about the level of engagement
      required from the Council and City staff during the sessions. The Council deliberated whether
      to attend in person or monitor online, depending on streaming availability. Mayor Palmer
      stressed the preliminary nature of these discussions and the need for cautious observation
      as significant bills often stemmed from these interim sessions. Assistant City Manager
      Thomas relayed concern of legislative items like water management and transportation bills.

      HPD Deputy Chief of Police Cody Stromberg discussed ongoing challenges related to school
      safety legislation from previous sessions, highlighting potential financial implications for the
      City. City Manager Cherpeski acknowledged the complexity of these issues and emphasized
      the need for proactive monitoring by relevant city departments.

      Councilmember Shields raised pertinent questions about the correlation between housing
      density policies and their impact on property values and affordability. Councilmember
      Hodges added his perspective on broader community planning needs beyond housing,
      advocating for balanced growth that includes infrastructure, jobs, and public services.

      Mayor Palmer encouraged Council to review the agenda thoroughly and provide feedback on
      specific items of concern.

          4.5. Discussion of the requested fiscal year 2025/2026 Public Safety budgets – Kyle
               Maurer, Finance Director
      Finance Director led the discussion of the requested fiscal year 2025 to 2026 public safety
      budget and outlined the purpose of the discussion. He emphasized the need for feedback and
      direction regarding the proposed budgets, inviting input on potential additions,
      disagreements, and the philosophy surrounding the recently approved property tax increase.

      Director Maurer proceeded with an overview of the police department's budget structure,
      highlighting a reduction in full-time equivalents from the previous year due to budget
May 8, 2024 – City Council Minutes                                                   Page 6 of 13


      adjustments, including the elimination and subsequent reinstatement of a mental health
      position contingent on performance indicators. He noted the modest expenditure increases
      for both fiscal years 2025 and 2026, attributing significant portions to capital requests such
      as a speed trailer and potential future investments like a SWAT vehicle.

      He turned the discussion to operational costs, including personnel-related expenses driven
      by cost-of-living adjustments and retirement contributions necessitated by updated actuarial
      studies. Director Maurer stressed the department's efforts to streamline operating expenses
      through detailed budget reviews.

      Regarding funding mechanisms, Director Maurer proposed a conservative property tax
      increase assumption to mitigate potential revenue shortfalls, with specific earmarks for
      capital reserves and operational enhancements discussed during the Truth in Taxation
      hearing. The Council engaged in a nuanced debate about separating new growth expenditures
      from inflationary pressures in budget planning, emphasizing the need for sustainable fiscal
      practices and clarity on funding priorities. Concerns were raised about ongoing maintenance
      costs and alternative funding sources, with consensus leaning towards strategic use of
      budget surpluses to address immediate operational needs versus long-term capital
      investments.

      HPD Deputy Chief of Police Cody Stromberg emphasized the need for an armored vehicle,
      citing operational requirements and safety concerns. He highlighted the effectiveness of their
      SWAT team and the frequent requests for assistance from neighboring agencies,
      necessitating adequate equipment and resources. Mayor Palmer relayed support, arguing
      that regional trends and operational necessities justify the purchase despite budgetary
      concerns. Councilmember Hodges expressed reservations about the timing and fiscal
      strategy. He raised issues regarding budget philosophy and adherence to earlier Council
      directives on tax allocations for new growth versus general purposes. He noted there was a
      difference between earmarking funds for specific uses like mental health specialists and new
      officers versus broader operational needs like inflationary costs and equipment upgrades.

      The Council discussed whether the proposed armored vehicle fits into the long-term financial
      plan and whether funds allocated but unspent from previous budget cycles should be
      redirected or preserved. Councilmember Shields questioned the strategic use of tax increases
      and whether funds should be reallocated based on changing needs or strictly adhered to the
      initial allocations. The Council wanted to find a balance between immediate operational
      needs, long-term financial sustainability, and adherence to prior budget commitments. The
      Council was contending with philosophical questions about fiscal responsibility and the best
      use of taxpayer funds amidst evolving community needs and economic conditions.

      Councilmember Henderson emphasized the need to have a cautious approach to spending
      public funds and advocated for clear metrics and careful consideration before hiring
May 8, 2024 – City Council Minutes                                                   Page 7 of 13


      additional personnel or acquiring new equipment. He underscored the importance of fiscal
      prudence to avoid compounding financial issues, urging a strategic approach that
      incorporated planned budget increases and addresses inflationary impacts.

      The Council’s dialogue revolved around several key decisions such as whether to allocate
      resources for planned increases, how to cover inflationary costs without burdening
      taxpayers, and whether to commit to purchasing new equipment over two budget cycles.
      They discussed using reserve funds, such as new growth money, to offset costs or maintain
      financial stability. Councilmember Shields underscored the importance of anticipating future
      expenses and using funds wisely to avoid financial strain.

      Deputy Chief of Policy Stromberg presented a new budget item of $60,026 for a versatile
      online reporting service, emphasizing its potential to enhance efficiency within the police
      department. This artificial intelligence driven tool would allow community members to report
      incidents online, potentially reducing the need for additional officers by handling lower
      priority calls more efficiently. The initial cost would cover implementation, with an ongoing
      subscription cost being expected. Councilmember Ohrn raised concerns about budget
      priorities, highlighting her commitment to allocating acceptable training budgets for officers
      while advocating for responsible spending on essential safety equipment. The Council
      discussed the effectiveness of speed trailers and other operational needs within the
      department, emphasizing data collection and community engagement.

          12.1. Discussion and Consideration of a Resolution to Adopt the Tentative Fiscal Years
               2025 and 2026 City of Herriman Budget and Request to Schedule a Public Hearing
               on May 22, 2024 – Kyle Maurer, Finance Director
          Finance Director Maurer began by outlining the extensive budget process, which had
          commenced the previous November with departments submitting their budget requests.
          These requests were then compiled and discussed in meetings starting from February
          onwards, involving both the Council and department heads. He expressed gratitude
          towards department heads for their efforts in compiling the budget, highlighting the
          complexity and dedication required.

         Director Maurer provided a high-level overview of the budget, and acknowledged an error
         in the budget classification, specifically concerning fines and forfeitures, which needed
         to be corrected to align with intergovernmental grants used for debt servicing on the Main
         Street bond. Director Maurer highlighted revenue projections and proposed financial
         measures, such as a bond issuance for capital projects. He noted a future discussion
         would delve more into the Capital Improvement Plan.

         Director Maurer noted a recent tax impacting the water fund and outlined conservative
         estimates for sales and property tax revenue growth. He continued with expenditure
         highlights which included a modest increase in general fund revenue, primarily directed
May 8, 2024 – City Council Minutes                                                  Page 12 of 13


         towards capital projects, which constituted 40% of the proposed budget. Personnel costs
         were noted as significant but prioritized less than public safety expenditures. Director
         Maurer emphasized that no new full-time employees were recommended for hire, except

         Director Maurer outlined planned steps for the future meetings including upcoming work
         sessions and a public hearing scheduled for May 22, 2024. The final adoption of the
