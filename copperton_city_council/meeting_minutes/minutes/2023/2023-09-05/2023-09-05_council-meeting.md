Copperton Law Enforcement Services

               Mayor Clayton asked if everyone had a chance to think about their top priorities
for law enforcement services.

               Council Member Bailey wondered if Copperton would have a vote on the
Board.

               Council Member Olson stated response time and police presence were
important to him.

             The Council discussed possible metrics for measuring police presence and
expressed a desire to have a police presence during the hours school children are at bus stops
and community events.

             Council Member Stitzer stated it would be helpful to implement the steering
committee the Council previously agreed to. She would also like to get a survey out to the
community.

               Mayor Clayton stated the Council would need to decide how much to spend on
the survey, and this could be discussed during the regular council meeting. He asked the
Council to identify five values to bring to the steering committee. They were: Response time;
Community Engagement/Policing; Presence; Staffing; and Cost.

               Council Member Olsen asked if Chief Mazuran would be given an opportunity
to respond to the Council's priorities at the next meeting.

              Mayor Clayton stated he has no opposition to Chief Mazuran attending the next
meeting and giving an update, but asking him to come and respond to the five priorities was still
premature.

               Council Member Stitzer agreed to get some survey bids before the next
meeting.

Interim Legislation for Copperton to Become a Town

                Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Copperton Metro Township, delivered a few
updates. He stated he recently heard back from Bob Reese, the drafting attorney for the
political subdivisions committee. He is working on things, but this issue probably will not be put
on the interim agenda until October.

Additionally, there is talk at the Municipal Services District (MSD) staff about the possibility of
providing a Justice Court. This would require separate legislation, though. He wondered if it
should be included in the bill for the metro townships to become cities/towns.

                 Mayor Clayton stated he would like to see the legislation come from a separate
bill, so the existing bill is not slowed down. He also does not want to establish a precedent of
adding different components to the bill.

                Nathan Bracken stated another option was for the town to set up its own justice
court, but to keep in mind that this would entail providing prosecutors and defense attorneys.
