       a. Discussion regarding 9000 South and New Bingham Highway Intersection

          environmental impact study were done regarding a redesign of the 9000 south and New

          including pros and cons.
               •   Option 1 – No build
                      o Would not accommodate future traffic demand.
                      o City staff and UDOT did not support this option.

          Bingham Highway leading into 9000 South was a UDOT road, and some discussion had
          taken place about possible jurisdictional transfer. It was suggested that not participating
          in the project would jeopardize West Jordan’s ability to obtain funding for other projects.
               •   Option 2 – Concept Study
                      o Connect 9000 South to New Bingham Highway and Duck Ridge Road and
                         install traffic signal.
                      o City staff and UDOT supported this option.

       b. Presentation of proposed form-based zoning (Corridor Zone [CZ]) for Redwood Road
          Senior Planner Tayler Jensen said several months ago the Council asked staff to consider
          a zoning option that would allow specific projects to occur without requiring a Senior
          Overlay (SO) component. Mr. Jensen presented a proposed form-based zone, Corridor
          Zone (CZ), for Redwood Road. Corridor Zone would be added to the Balanced Housing
          Chapter (13-8-23) as an exception. The new form-based ordinance for Redwood
          Corridor:
              • would not be an overlay zone;
              • would require rezoning to replace an underlying zone;
              • would require a Future Land Use Map amendment; and
              • would require an MDA/MDP.

          The primary focus of the hybrid form-based code would be on the built form, but unlike
          a true form-based code, would still have a use table. Mr. Jensen said the Floor Area Ratio
          (FAR) and the maximum height would be determined by the City Council. He explained
          FAR was recommended over density to accommodate mixed-use. Council Member Green
          suggested a one-story building with a rooftop patio, with a taller building behind, should
          be allowed in certain conditions. Mr. Jensen said the CZ was designed to accommodate
          and drive more high-density development to Redwood Road.

          Council Member Whitelock said she did not see evidence of a ‘consistent’ streetscape in
          the materials provided. Mr. Jensen said perhaps a better word to describe the desired
          streetscape would be ‘interesting’. Council Member Whitelock said CZ did not seem to be
          a tool to create a commercially vibrant area. She said she suspected the proposed zone
          would result in a lot of high-density housing. Council Member Whitelock said she was not
          opposed to the density, but wanted to ensure an appearance and sense of place and
          community so that individuals knew when they had entered West Jordan. Council
          Member Jacob said he disagreed, and said he believed a hodge-podge variety of design
          helped create a sense of place. He expressed the opinion that structures did not need to
          be similar or follow a certain design, and said he liked when buildings all looked different,
          which he found more interesting.

          Mr. Jensen said staff were sensitive to established residential neighborhoods behind the
          Redwood Corridor and tried to focus development along the corridor to avoid a creeping
          change of character to the neighborhoods. The Council discussed potential impact of
          development along Redwood Road on established neighborhoods in the area. Council
          Member Shelton said the proposed zone would allow for both residential and commercial,
          and the market was currently showing more need for residential. Staff pointed out the
          proposed zone option would need to be considered separately with each individual
