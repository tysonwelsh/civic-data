2. Presentations and Reports
2.1 Transit Fresh Look Study Presentation- Mountainland Association of Governments
Kendal Willards, with MAG, presented on the study. The study is being conducted by a
partnership of communities and transportation agencies, including MAG, WFRC, UTA, and
local cities. It aims to focus on regional transit connections like high-capacity light rail and bus
rapid transit, while also considering local bus service.

The study will gather the latest growth and development data from cities to inform the transit
planning. The partnership will establish goals and metrics to evaluate and prioritize potential
transit projects. The goal is to create a unified vision and plan that the communities can then
advocate for funding from the state legislature and federal sources.

The study is taking a more collaborative approach compared to previous transit studies in the
area. The key focus is on developing a shared, implementable transit plan that the cities and
transportation agencies can collectively support and fund going forward. The study should be
completed by the end of the year ahead of the legislative session.

2.2. Presentation by Heritage Arts Foundation
Bob Hansen and Scott Streadbeck presented this item. The Heritage Arts Foundation has been
around since 1999 and focuses on public art, art education, and advocacy.

2.4 Chief Wakara Statue Presentation
Lisa Clark Price and sculptor Gary Price presented this item. They brought a replicate of the
sculpture and stated that they would like to find a permanent place for the 7 -foot bronze statue in
Lehi

The statue is intended to honor Chief Wakara and the Timpanogos tribe, who were instrumental
in helping the early Mormon settlers survive in the area. The statue, titled "A New Day", is
designed to inspire themes of unity and world peace.

The Prices are not asking for funds, rather they are seeking a letter of support from the City
Council that they can use to help secure additional donations and funding for the project.

3. 20 Minute Citizen Input
Ross Carter addressed the Council expressing concerns with Hadco’s heavy equipment operation
that has expanded over the years, despite being in a light industrial zone. There have been
numerous complaints over the years about issues like dust and noise. He was frustrated the city
keeps "kicking the can down the road" and allowing the operation to expand, rather than taking
action to address the negative consequences for the surrounding residential area.

Jose Zuniga, a union carpenter, was concerned that the city was allowing companies to use
subcontractors that pay cash, rather than hiring union labor for construction projects. He believes
this practice is depriving local union workers of job opportunities in their own community. He
asked the council to be more aware of and address this issue.

    Scott Bishop with DR Horton stated that he’s in support of the lighting standards. He noted
