       a.     Southwest Visioning Study, Phase 1 Results – Mayor Staggs.

Mayor Staggs informed the Council Members that they should have received the Southwest
Visioning Study document. To date, there have been five Steering Committee Meetings with the
six scheduled for the following day at West Jordan City Hall where they will address Phase 1 in
its entirety.

Communications Director, Casey Saxton reported that 300 to 400 Riverton residents responded
to the survey, which represented approximately 42%. Of the municipalities that participated in
the study, Riverton had by far the most participation. The Consultant, Logan Simpson, identified
four areas they wished to target in the study. Phase 1 consisted of the existing conditions,
Phase 2 will include visioning, and Phase 3 will involve recommended implementation.

The major findings associated with Phase 1 involved doubling the number of households and
jobs in the southwest communities. It was noted that the findings do not include projects that
have not yet been approved. It was anticipated that the number of households will increase from
90,000 to around 184,000. The potential impacts were discussed. The desire was to look
regionally at opportunities, implement traffic calming techniques, expand, and add connections.

Mayor Staggs reported that what is proposed is a vision rather than a plan. It was anticipated
that recommendations will be put forward that the cities can agree on. The primary issue was
east-west connectivity. The most recent study conducted in 2007-2008 showed 9000 South
being the east-west connection at a cost of $2 billion. The result of the previous meeting was
that it will cost about $600 million to complete Bangerter Highway and $1 to $1.2 billion for
Mountain View Corridor. It was reported that from 2018-2020 this area of the valley is expected
to receive about 70% of all spending for Region 2. There was also a push for a $400 million
transportation bond. Mayor Staggs commented that the proposed funds do not include transit or
east-west connectivity.

With respect to work and economic development, Mayor Staggs stated that there are is not much
shopping, gathering places, and entertainment in the City. Regionally, the goals are to attract
new employers and create walkable commercial centers on the west side. Mayor Staggs
addressed some of the findings contained in the summary. He explained that they are still
engaged and will likely have the remainder of the year to complete the study. The Council
Members should have been contacted as key stakeholders.

         b.         Discuss Compensation of Elected Officials for Attending Board Meetings –
                    Kevin Hicks and Ryan Carter.

Administrative Services Director, Kevin Hicks provided a summary based on his interpretation
and understanding of the motion. With respect to special service districts, the law specifies that
board members cannot earn more than $5,000. Mr. Hicks reported that that is what South Valley
Sewer based its maximum on. If the law changes, their pay will increase to match the maximum
allowed. City Attorney/Interim City Manager, Ryan Carter stated that there is also a small
amount allowed for per diem. By comparison, it was reported that the South Valley Sewer
District pays $5,000 for attendance at 12 meetings per year and board members are paid
regardless of whether they are in attendance. Board members are paid an additional $60 for
attending special meetings. Based on the motion, Mr. Hicks set the annual maximum at $5,000
with a per meeting rate of $416.67. A tracking system was also proposed and described to the
Council. Mr. Hicks suggested that it be included in the normal payroll distribution and
reconciled quarterly.

There was discussion of spreading out assignments. Currently, Council Member Stewart serves
on the UFA and the Unified Fire Service Area (“UFSA”) Boards; Council Member McKay on
the TRCC Board and the Association of Municipal Councils; and Council Member Buroker on
Mosquito Abatement and the Jordan River Commission. It was suggested that a list be provided
of how often meetings are held so that the work load can be spread more evenly among the
Council Members. It was noted that representation is also needed on the Healthy Riverton
Board, the Western Growth Coalition, and the Utah League of Cities and Towns. Mr. Hicks

         c.         Dog Park Discussion - Sheril Garn.

Parks Public Services Director, Sheril Garn sought direction from the Council. She explained
that about 18 months ago, two staff members went before the TRCC Board to request funding
for a Dog Park. There was a lot of miscommunication between both entities who have
conflicting documentation. The first made it appear that they were simply looking for a
feasibility study and a location. The second proposed building the Dog Park for $100,000. Prior
to the City contracting with an Architect, Ms. Garn reported that she took the Parks and Trails
Committee on a field trip where they looked at existing parks and City-owned property that is
unimproved. The recommendation of the Parks and Trails Committee was to pursue the
Retention/Detention Pond property on 3600 West and 12000 South for a Dog Park. The
Architect then came up with a design that included sections for small and large dogs. The far
east portion was set aside for water retention.

         a.         Entry Point Presentation - Mayor Staggs.

Jeff Christiansen from Entry Point was present to discuss the broadband plan presented last year.
Mr. Christianson stated that the Council allocated funds for a study to be directed in 2020,
towards a City or municipal-owned broadband infrastructure. They have taken a six-month
break and are proposing that the next two months be focused on creating definitive plans for
implementation of the project. There was a question revolving around the degree of participation
in the network, whether it be voluntary, an opt-out, or given to each resident.

Mr. Christiansen reported that two items would be planned and implemented with the project.
The first was the replacement of the current iNet. The City is currently paying just under
$80,000 a year to Comcast. With this project implementation, Comcast costs will be replaced in
8 to 10 years. The second item is to layout a pilot of the program that would include 200 to 300
homes in the City. This would provide proof of the public interest and help define what the
model would be. The pieces that were not completed last year were financial pro forma and
specific finances around definitive projects. If these two items were determined to move
forward, a definitive financial structure for the plans would be completed and returned.

Mayor Staggs asked if the $5,000 left on the contract awarded to Entry Point could complete the
next phase of the project. Mr. Christiansen confirmed that it would. He also stated that a
decision would need to be determined if the City would like to operate with UDOT. If so, a
portion of the budget would pay for the initial connection.

                    of the Municipal Territory of Riverton City from the Unified Fire Service
                    Area – Council Member Stewart.

Council Member Stewart reported that earlier in the day UFA approved moving forward with the
Franchise Tax Bond. The Parameters Resolution would be prepared and presented to the
Council. $379,000 would be shown as the net addition to fund balance annually. Two of the
stations that would be built as part of the bond do not have staffing. As a result, at some future
date, there would be a $5 million take that would require an estimated $550,000 to $600,000 tax

increase. Riverton would cover the cost of the stations in other areas of the UFA service area.
The discussion was whether to continue with UFSA as the funding mechanism for UFA services
or begin the withdrawal process to continue in a direct relationship with UFA.

UFA Chief, Dan Peterson expressed his support for this type of work. He noted that what has
been done over the last three years has involved focusing on making sure they are a member of
the service area or a direct member through UFA. It allows communities with disparate values
to be served by one agency. Prior to July 2017, the City operated with three stations rather than
two. Draper City had left and, in the restructure, there was work required to redo the staffing
model. During this time, Station 120 was one of the stations that was closed. Stations 121 and
124 became four-person stations. It was important to Chief Peterson that Station 121 be
preserved as a fire station. Four-person crews allow for a slightly longer drive time because they
are able to make up the difference in actual scene time. The evaluation tool being used showed a
reasonable overlap between Stations 121 and 124 that made him comfortable moving forward.

Chief Peterson reported that they began monitoring the change and spent one year to ensure that
the data was accurate. The first year they saw a minimum impact but 18 months post-closure,
serious concerns were identified. There was a 26% increase in response times in Station 120 and
a 10% increase in response times overall. Steps taken to address the issue were described. Chief
Peterson stated that implementing the ambulance helped somewhat and has brought response
times down from 9:18 to 9:00 at the 90 percentile. At the 50 percentile, it made a greater
difference. Future impacts were described including a 5.9% increase in call volume between
2018 and 2019. Congestion continued to have an impact, particularly the east-west movement.

Chief Peterson commented that Station 124 was built with a regional concept in mind and with
Draper as part of the system. Last fall, Council Member Stewart talked about being fiscally
responsible and asked that the Council consider closing Stations 120 and 124 and building a new
station that is better situated. Chief Peterson stated that that was one way to improve response
times and provide better overlap. In the fall, they presented consideration of software that would
allow them to predict and evaluate the impact of traffic changes, increased congestion, increased
call volume, and placement of stations. The new station was budgeted for through UFSA and
two vendors applied. Dark Horse from Edmonton, Canada was awarded the bid with the new
station expected to be completed in about 10 weeks.

In response to a question raised by Council Member Wells, Chief Peterson stated that most of the
ambulances are placed with an engine. The location of each station was described and the
staffing of Station 120 was discussed. It was noted that the Council would need to decide at
some point in the future whether it is important to staff that station. Currently, the cost to staff a
four-person crew is $2.5 million. If Riverton is in its own system they bear all of that cost. If
they are part of UFSA they pay just over 12%. Chief Peterson explained that much of the
revenue that comes into UFSA in the unincorporated area comes from the centrally assessed
Kennecott mine. Part of the need is the access points from Riverton, Herriman, and Magna to
feed into what is active with the mine today. Chair Peterson reminded the Council that two years
ago, Riverton was upside down in its service. There was a tax increase and they have reversed
that situation, which is common in any community.

Mayor Staggs commented that at the time, many in UFA were upside down. They were taking
from fund balance and drew down to a level that was causing issues for UFSA’s ability to bond.
As a result, they made a decision to impose an increase across the board. He pointed out that
with respect to UFA, there were originally five votes and now there are 16 of which the City has
only 1. What is proposed allows the City to control their destiny and make decisions about
staffing. The Mayor commented that the reason for the discussion was to address decisions that
can empower to Riverton City. His personal opinion was that if they do not make a decision to
take action now, they will bind future Councils since future tax increases will be needed and
making a change later will be more difficult.

Mayor Staggs commented that the intent tonight is to adopt a resolution to initiate withdrawal
with conditions, find a way to reach an amicable agreement, or go through the feasibility process
and complete the withdrawal.

Council Member McCay asked that Mr. Hicks review the analysis. UFA Chief Financial
Officer, Tony Hill was also present. Mr. Hicks explained that the analysis represents a moment
in time. He made no changes to revenue and stated that with respect to the property tax
calculation, he was using 100% collection. Currently, the UFSA has a slightly higher collection
rate than the Riverton Law Enforcement Service Area but the two are very close. With regard to
the other fee in lieu impact fees, he explained that if they were to make their own Riverton Fire
Service Area or pursue it as part of the property tax for the City itself, they will not hit that
revenue unless they pay for an impact fee study and have similar impact fee rates. The UFA
member fee calculations were presented that were provided about one year earlier. Mr. Hicks
stated that the capital maintenance estimate is 11.71% of the previous total. If the City owns all
of the buildings and there is a catastrophe, they will bear the full cost.

Council Member Stewart was of the opinion that the costs even out over the years. The Mayor
commented that total expenditures will increase if they remain with UFSA by $500,000 to
$600,000 for staffing. Staffing options and costs were discussed. Council Member Wells
commented that the additional cost associated with staffing will directly benefit the City. He
also noted that the 3 ½ to 4 percent increase would be the same if they went with the UFA direct
model versus the UFSA.

Council Member Stewart commented that the City’s voice has become diluted and he had no
impact regarding moving forward with the bond even though he was opposed to it.

Council Member Buroker thanked Chief Peterson for spreading a positive message. Regardless
of what decision is made, she stressed the need to keep the station on 2700 West staffed
appropriately. The cost of staffing with a four-person crew will cost $2.5 million. Under the
UFSA model, the cost would be about $300,000. She read the contract and acknowledged
Council Member Stewart’s issue with ensuring that they have strong representation on the Board.
She also felt positive about that response in that the contract was changed and voted on by all
members to allow the vote to be more weighted.

Chief Peterson agreed that there has been a lot of change but stated that they have had very few
split votes or challenges. There are fire stations in the system that are in disrepair that need to be

Mayor Staggs thanked Chief Peterson and the increased level of transparency and cooperation,

the decisions pertain to the ability to deliver a regional service with a disparate rate. That allows
the regional model to work inside of this taxing system.

Council Member Stewart stated that he has made himself available since September and would

Chief Peterson explained that if Riverton is a member of UFSA, taxes will increase and then
remain stable for a time. He noted that some communities are growing extraordinarily fast.
Eagle Mountain, for example, is growing at 24% in value as a whole so they are slowly
increasing their need. Riverton was the third fastest-growing this year but the sixth fastest in
taxable value. Costs associated with staffing the third station were discussed. It was clarified
that with the member fee, the City would be apportioned 100% of the cost of Stations 121 and
124. The ambulance is at Station 120 and as that station builds out and more calls go to
Herriman, Riverton’s member fee may drop.

Council Member Buroker asked where the funding will come from if they decide that more
service is needed and want to increase staffing. Chief Peterson stated that it would depend on
which model they are in. The UFSA model would involve staffing being done system-wide.
The direct model would require Riverton to budget the staffing. To staff the third station, the
member fee individually would be $8 million. It was noted that if the other stations are staffed,
the member fee will remain the same but the tax rate will need to be increased.

Council Member Stewart commented that with respect to new growth, revenue will be generated
from new developments that is not anticipated in the calculation. He also pointed out that under
the current model, UFSA has subscribed to the four-handed crew. In Station 120 they could
keep with the standards of cover, which is a three-handed truck, and take one hand off of
Stations 121 and 124, purchase a truck at a cost of $650,000, and in a few years purchase a
pumper truck with fund balance. Those decisions could be made by the Council versus the
UFSA board. He thought the matter had been prolonged long enough and suggested that a
decision be made.

         b.         Rules of Order and Procedure – Ryan Carter.

In response to a question raised by Council Member Stewart, Mr. Carter explained that the City
Manager is the individual to whom all employees report, however, a statutory power was
reserved under the Mayor. It can be interpreted that in the absence of the Mayor’s direction, the
Chief of Police would answer to the City Manager with regard to daily affairs. Mr. Carter
clarified that the City has a part-time Mayor but a full-time City Manager. The Mayor retains the
power under the statute. It is the Mayor’s responsibility to ensure that the laws are being
faithfully executed. In terms of daily application, if there are daily reports that go from the Chief
of Police to the City Manager, the Mayor can review those at his discretion and require they be
brought to his attention. In the absence of the Mayor, the City Manager acts. Both work with
the Chief of Police and the Mayor can be involved to the extent he chooses to in the day-to-day
affairs of the City. The reporting, however, goes to the City Manager.

Council Member Stewart was of the opinion that communication from the Chief of Police has

         c.         Review Job Description of City Manager – Ryan Carter.

Council Member McCay commented that in looking at the job description, she agreed with
Council Member Stewart. When Mr. Carter served previously as Interim City Manager, the
Police Chief made the decision with regard to who to notify. Mr. Carter stated that the focus
should be the level of detail they can include in a report that goes out to the Council versus the
level of detail they can include in a report to the Mayor and City Manager. They can include
generalized statements about an event as it is happening that sent to the entire Council but when
they get into the specifics of identifying a suspect by name, job distinctions become more
important.

It was noted that an agreement was in draft form and was in the process of being signed. Once it
is executed, the position can be posted.

Council Member Stewart suggested that the City Manager’s role ensure that communication is
routine with the Council and others on an established frequency. He suggested that the position
be advertised locally for two to three weeks and then report back to the Council on the responses
received before releasing it nationally.

Council Member McCay suggested the job be posted nationally if the expense is not too great.

It was reported that the last time the position was posted approximately 120 applications were
received. Changes made to how jobs are posted will result in a large number of candidates.

Mayor Staggs commented that at some point the City needs to move toward a full-time Mayor.
He noted that that may involve a change to the role of the City Manager and the potential
applicant pool.

Council Member Buroker commented on communication and stated that if the City Manager
were to communicate with all of the Council Members, the information could become inaccurate
very quickly. Her preference was for the information to go directly to the Communications

the potential change to a full-time Mayor, now would be the best time to take action. In terms of
communication, he would not want to get information out too quickly to ensure that the
information disseminated is accurate.

Council Member Stewart commented that in a prior administration they reviewed the needs of
each district at least quarterly. He recommended that the job description include the verbiage
that the City Manager will prepare the City for a transition to a full-time Mayor in two years.

Council Member McDougal commented on the communication issue and saw no point in having
a Communication Department if they are not filtering information there. His preference was to
have citizens go to a central location for information. He was uncomfortable with the change to
a full-time Mayor. He agreed, however, that the Mayor has done a great job. Making the
change, however, will dramatically limit mayoral candidates long-term. The hope was that a
good hire will significantly alleviate the Mayor’s stress and workload.

Council Member McCay had concerns about moving in the direction of a full-time Mayor. She
pointed out that West Jordan went that direction when its population reached close to 100,000.
Mayor Staggs commented cities such as Murray and South Salt Lake City are substantially
smaller than Riverton and have full-time mayors. Eagle Mountain offers two paths at the time of
an election where voters can elect a full-time or part-time mayor. He thought the City would be
well served by having a full-time Mayor.
