NEPHI CITY PLANNING COMMISSION
                             MEETING MINUTES
                           June 8, 2022 @ 7:00 p.m.
                        21 E. 100 N. Nephi, UT 84648


Attendance:
Staff: Seth Atkinson, Shauna Talbot, Lisa Brough
City Council Member: Skip Worwood
Commissioners: Heather Robertson, Fran Petersen, Alan Hancock, Cory Thomson
Excused: Ann Peterson, Karl Brough, Kent Heap
Scribe: Lisa Brough/Shari Cowan

1.    Cory Thomson, acting Planning Commission Chair Pro Tempore, opened the meeting
      and welcomed all present. Prayer given by Commissioner Heather Robertson.

2.    Public Hearings

      a.     Home Occupation Permit- The Skin Studio
      Owner Andrea Blackett addressed the council about what business activity would be
      going on in her home. Services will include botox treatments, as well as esthetician
      services offered by her daughter. Botox injections require an RN certification, which
      Blackett has secured. Treatments will be available by staggered appointment only,
      which will facilitate ample parking.

      Motion: The motion was made to approve a home occupation permit for The Skin
      Studio
      Made by: Alan Hancock Second: Fran Peterson Outcome: Passed unanimously

      b.     Title 11 Subdivision Regulations Section11.3.2 “Exceptions”
      Taylor Wharburton commented (recording and script available for comments)

      Motion: The motion was made to accept the Title 11 exceptions
      Made by: Heather Robertson Second: Cory Thomson Outcome: Passed unanimously

      c.     Rezone of portions of parcel number(s): XA00-2129-121 and XA00-2131-
      located at approximately 1500 North to 1620 North and Main Street to 175 East
      from the CU-1 (Combined Use) to a strip of HC-1 (Highway Commercial) along the
      highway; a strip to the East of that as R-3 (Residential) with a possible PUD
      overlay zone and a strip along the south of 1500 North to the R-3 (Residential) with
      a possible PUD overlay zone.

Rezone request by Terry Messersmith from CU-1 to HC-1 along the highway, and then a
strip to the East of that as R-3 with a possible PUD overlay zone and a strip along the
south of 1500 North to the R-3 with a possible PUD overlay zone.

Planner Talbot reviewed with the commission the different zone uses. She reviewed a
plan that Mr. Messersmith has suggested doing in the area. Proposing 8 units in each
structure.

Donald Ball commented about the need for affordable housing.

Mr. Messersmith commented on his site plan and said this had always been his concept
plan. They will be 1000 to 1300 square foot units. The dynamics of current housing
needs dictate that condominiums have more value than in the past.

Richard Peterson asked how many stories the units would have.
Talbot said it would be mandatory that they have CCR and HOA

Peterson asked if they would be single family units on 200 East.
Worwood asked if these units would be similar to the Hunter Village Condos.

West side of 200 East will be duplex units.
Mike Dunn asked if the lots on the south side of 1500 East will be duplexes or
townhomes. Five eight-plexes are being considered in the concept plan.
Maximum height is 45 feet. Travis Worwood said that the PUD overlay would require
some amenities. Now that the PUD has been revised, it is really nice.

Richard Peterson said that this development does not affect him personally, but he is
concerned for other residents, especially with the 45 foot height.
Fran Petersen said she does like that it is tucked back, and she would like to talk more
about the PUD overlay because that provides for more open space.

Fran Peterson would like to have a work session on these issues.
Talbot said that the zone change request is vested. So they cannot table the decision,
but can deny the request.

Fran said that she likes the concept plan, but she feels that the details of the zones need
more revisions to meet the goals of the commission.

Messersmith said he feels like he’s been held hostage from this body because of the
moratorium. “Now you are going to hold me hostage again and this has been unfair. At
some point you need to understand what you are doing to me financially.”

Mike Cannell voiced his concern about how this will affect our infrastructure. Is there
enough power available?

       Mr. Atkinson reported that the subdivision on the north end of the city has been
       completed in order to serve this area. We have about 3 million more gallons of water
       storage now that the water project has been completed.
       Cannell said you can build all the storage you want but, if you don’t have the water, it
       doesn’t matter. Richard Peterson said he would prefer the strip south of 1500 to be R3
       because of the height issue.

       Brett Ludlow said a twin home looks exactly like a single residential, but it has two
       homes with a zero lot line. Maria Peterson is concerned about the possibility of the R3
       zone not being developed according to the concept plan presented.
       Messersmith said that he lives in the community and he cares about what it looks like.
       He stated that he has been approached by big developers, but had to back out because
       he was losing control of the project.
       The city has made good revisions to code that provides more desirable living.
       Alan Hancock said he likes the concept plan.
       Thomson said that with the way it is zoned today, we have very little control with what
       would go in there. But if we go with the zone change, we would have more control with
       regards to open space and other amenities.
       Atkinson said he is recommending the zone change request because the code has been
       revised to more of the vision of the community.

       Public Hearing closed at 8:00 p.m.

       Motion: The motion was made to approve the rezone property located at approximately
1500 North to 1620 North and Main Street to 175 East from the CU-1 (Combined Use) to a strip
of HC-1 (Highway Commercial) along the highway; a strip to the East of that as R-3
(Residential) with a possible PUD overlay zone; and a strip along the south of 1500 North to the
R-3 (Residential) with a possible PUD overlay zone.

      Made by: Alan Hancock Seconded: Heather Robertson Outcome: Passed
unanimously

     Subdivision

     Anything over a single family dwelling would require 3 parking spaces.
     Require two off-street parking spaces for single family units.
     Talbot discussed a national standard for parking space per use.

8.   Discussion about changes to the Land Use Code for Farm Animals

     Animal Rights- Chicken ordinance

      Mr. Atkinson introduced the ordinance draft concerning chickens.
      Reduce the amount of chicken to four per single family home.
      No roosters.
      Only for the consumption of eggs.
      Four square feet per chicken.
      Enclosure side yard or backyard with a sight-obscuring fence.
      Myrna Trauntvein commented that she would prefer the chicken enclosure 10 feet from
      the rear or side yard.
      Thomson would only like the chicken enclosure in the rear yard and not in the side yard.
      The commission agreed to not require permitting for the chicken owners.

      Public hearing opened at 9:38 p.m.

      Shari Cowan stated that she has chickens and the smell is contained as long as the
      coop is closed. Laying chickens are not excessively noisy.
      Myrna said adopting this ordinance would just make what everyone is doing legal.

      Myrna said she had a neighbor suggest that she pass on to the commission…..

      Public hearing closed at 9:43 p.m.

      Motion: Motion was made to recommend to the Council that the chicken ordinance be
      adopted without permit requirement
      Made by: Fran Petersen Second: Alan Hancock Outcome: Unanimous vote

9.    Zone Change for Northridge Phase C- Action was taken above after public hearing

10.   Concept Zoning map for general plan update

      Atkinson presented a map for conceptual zoning that showed commercial pods
      surrounded by combined use zones. The map showed current exits and possible future
      exits off I-15. Higher density would be located closer to transportation corridors.
      Industrial would be located near the airport and the sewer lagoons.

      The commission agreed to June 15 at 6:30 p.m. for a work session.
