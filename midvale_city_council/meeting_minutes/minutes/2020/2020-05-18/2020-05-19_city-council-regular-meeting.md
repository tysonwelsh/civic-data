SHEET FOR THE CONTRACT EXTENSION AND AMENDMENTS WITH
ACE DISPOSAL
Glen Kennedy said in 2010 Midvale City (City) and ACE Disposal (Ace) entered into an
agreement for Ace to provide solid waste services for the City. That agreement, and the
extensions and amendments since that time, are in need of updating to match the current
needs of the City pertaining to its solid waste services and programs. Over the last few
| months, City staff have presented information relating to the sanitation fund and the City’s
solid waste programs and services. From those presentations and discussions, the
determination was made that services and rates need to be adjusted. Council was
presented several options with four main guiding factors.
V Limiting the amount of necessary rate increase
V Providing a bulky waste service
V The protection of stormwater and regulatory compliance
V Maintaining a recycle program
The Council indicated the preferred option would be to make changes to only the curbside
bulky waste program and leave all other solid waste programs as is.
Staff have coordinated with Ace to rework the pricing structure of services to include the
elimination of the current weekly curbside bulky waste program an
FISCAL IMPACT:
The changes to the bulky waste program will reduce costs to the sanitation budget by
approximately $100,000 annually. Whereas a fee increase for solid waste services is still
required, it limits the amount.

E. CONSIDER ORDINANCE NO. 2020-0-08 EMERGENCY ZONING
ORDINANCE FOR STATE STREET ZONE

Alex Murphy said in 2001, following the City’s annexation of the Fort Union area, the

zoning ordinance was rewritten to fold the City and County zoning ordinances together.

As part of this effort, the State Street (SSC) Zone was created and given the following

goals:

A. Facilitate transition of the State Street corridor from underdeveloped commercial

parcels to a more vibrant mix of commercial and residential uses;

B. Provide an identifiable entry corridor for the city;

C. Assure and enhance the investment of State Street property owners;

D. Promote economic development;

E. Protect surrounding neighborhoods from the potential adverse effects of more

intense land uses; and

F. Require thematic landscaping to provide a distinctive visual quality to the area.

Consistent with these goals, the SSC Zone was written to encourage mixed-use

development. However, while reviewing the current ordinance language at the request of

a potential developer, Staff determined the Zone does not establish a minimum parking

| = requirement for the residential component of a mixed-use project, a standard seen in
13

Proceedings of City Council Meeting
May 19, 2020
almost all the City’s other zones. In combination with the other standards in the Zone, this
could lead to projects that provide inadequate parking and significant impacts on the
public street network and adjacent properties/neighborhoods, both of which would be
inconsistent with the goals of the Zone.
In cases where there is a compelling, countervailing public interest, both the Midvale City
Municipal Code and Utah Code allow the City to adopt an emergency or temporary zoning
ordinance, effective for no longer than 6 months, without going through the typical
ordinance amendment process. To avoid the potential negative outcomes associated with
a mixed-use project with no residential parking standard, Staff has prepared a temporary
ordinance that would establish a parking calculation for residential uses as follows:
° 1.5 spaces per 1-bedroom unit;
° 2 spaces per 2-bedroom unit;
° 2.5 spaces per 3-bedroom unit and greater; and
° 1 space per every 4 units for guest parking
This calculation matches the highest calculation used in the current zoning ordinance,
that of the RM-25 zone, while still retaining the other existing parking- and mixed-use-
related requirements in the SSC Zone. Placing this temporary ordinance into effect would
allow Staff time to develop parking standards appropriate for the project types intended
for the State Street corridor and have those standards considered by the Planning
Commission and City Council through the typical ordinance amendment process. ]
