Max Chang stated it has been a year since the Chapter 3 and Chapter 4 update of the Code 
and asked the Planning Commission to revisit the addition of the Recreation Commercial 
(RC) Zone. The time is ripe for the RC Zone because last year the State of Utah designated 
Echo as a State Park. Federal and state governments have invested seven million dollars to 
upgrade facilities at Echo, including the reservoir and the rail trail, which are prime 
examples of recreational commercial amenities. These amenities have the potential to 
become a robust economic driving force much needed by Summit County. Eastern Summit 
County should have the foresight to include recreational/commercial type of projects in the

pipeline. Instead of pushing these projects into a box that does not really fit and having to 
create variances and exceptions, the Planning Commission is encouraged to proactively 
implement an RC Zone with clearly delineated criterion, objectives, and guidelines to allow 
projects to be better thought out and have a more positive impact on all stakeholders. 
Recreational/commercial activities range from boating, fishing, waterskiing, horseback 
riding, trails, off-road vehicles, camping, etc. Currently, no zone can capture the uniqueness

additional due diligence, various versions of recreational commercial zoning could be 
created to suit the needs of the population in Eastern Summit County.

1. **Discussion regarding amendments to 11-4-5 “Subdivisions, Condominiums, 
Subdivision Plat Amendments, Parcel Boundary Adjustments, and Division of Land 
for Non-Development Purposes” and 11-4-12, “Master Planned Developments” as 
they relate to wastewater systems.** – Ray Milliner, Principal Planner 
 
Attorney Strachan shared the background for this discussion and included draft 
amendments to the Code to present to the Planning Commission for feedback. ESAC 
(Eastern Summit County Water Conservancy Special Service District) was created to get a 
better handle on wastewater and understanding of individual septic systems in Eastern 
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission

Summit County. ESAC is made up of members from Engineering, Attorneys Office, 
Community Development, Planning Commission, Planning Staff, and members of the 
community. The group was created to provide a recommendation to the County 
Commission on what to do. ESAC recommended the creation of a Special Service District. 
Under the General Plan at the time there was a policy with regulatory language stating that 
all major development be connected to either a municipal sewer system or a package 
treatment facility. A body politic is required to sponsor large wastewater management 
systems. The large Indian Hollow Subdivision put pressure on the County to develop a 
Special Service District, which was created. This Special Service District included all of 
Eastern Summit County except for Promontory and all the municipalities. Indian Hollow 
has now created their own Special Service District, but was dissolved last week. 
The County Council is the final approving body for wastewater systems that consist of more 
than ten lots. The General Manager of the District (Tom Fisher) is the final approving body 
for any wastewater system less than ten lots. ESAC is an advisory board that makes 
recommendations to the final approving body.  
ESAC has essentially duplicated what the Health Department has been doing, so there is an 
effort to make the process flow better. The Special Service District is not really functioning. 
Staff has approached the County Council to propose creating a full administrative control 
board through ESAC, including policy and procedures and a fee structure. The County 
Council wanted to maintain control to see how the process works. Minor changes were 
made to Title 2. Staff is asking for a recommendation from the Planning Commission for the 
proposed changes to Chapter 11-4-2: Master Planned Developments. The proposed

changes for Title 2, Chapter 20 include: approval for wastewater systems for developments 
of three lots or less go through the Health Department; wastewater systems for four or 
more lots with lots of ten acres or more will be approved through the Health Department; 
wastewater systems in close proximity to public wastewater systems will also go through

Chair Clyde stated that municipalities will not want to allow connection to their sewer

Commissioner Wilde asked what the detriment would be of the proposed overlay zones 
creating their own sewer system. Director Bondurant answered that cannot be 
determined yet. The infrastructure study is intended to determine what resources are 
already available. DEQ (Department of Environmental Quality) paid to have the Coalville 
sewer plant updated to allow growth for the entire valley. The regulations in the Code of 
Health are intended to look beyond today to what will happen in the future and have 
something in place to manage the bigger developments that will be coming. The 
infrastructure needs to be in place so connections can be made when the resources become 
available to connect to larger plants. Regionalization needs to be planned with a Special 
Service District in place with locals on the Board who understand wastewater and 
development on the East side of the County. Smaller developments will probably stay with 
individual septic systems; the developer will make their case to ESAC for what system is 
appropriate. Community systems can be contracted with a third-party operator with funds 
from the landowners. Developers will incur the cost of wastewater infrastructure upfront. 
The cost comes from excavation for a sewer line. Developers need to do a cost-benefit 
analysis to present to the Health Department or ESAC. Commissioner Sonntag suggested 
that wastewater systems could be boded upfront and paid out through taxes over time for 
areas approved by the County. 
Director Bondurant stated that the Health Department is in the process of writing a small 
water system rule to help with management for drinking water to help protect public 
health. 
The Planning Commission supports the proposed changes. A public hearing will be held.

2. Discussion of possible Code Amendments relating to the regulation of Contractor’s 
Yard in Eastern Summit County. – Ray Milliner, Principal Planner 
 
Planner Milliner shared the background of this discussion. There was a good discussion at 
the previous work session about a junk ordinance and contractor’s yards. Current zoning 
allows a contractor’s yard as a Conditional Use Permit. Staff is working on making criteria 
for regulations of a contractor’s yard and would like input from the Planning Commission 
about whether some zones should be prohibited from having contractor’s yards. 
Commissioner Sonntag stated that contractor’s yards should not be allowed in the AG5 
zone. There are some nonconforming contractor’s yards already in the AG5 zone. 
Commissioner Wilde stated that some AG10 zoning areas may not be appropriate for a 
contractor’s yard and suggested that contractor’s yards be prohibited in any platted 
subdivision. 
Chair Clyde stated that a contractor’s yard is an industrial use and should be prohibited in 
any residential area. Conditional use runs with the land and the next person to own a 
property may not protect the neighbors like an original applicant would. Planner Milliner 
agreed that industrial zoning is the most appropriate for a contractor’s yard. 
Director Putt suggested another option could be to establish a Low impact permit to allow 
smaller contractor’s yards and come up with criteria for those to be allowed in other zones. 
Light industrial commercial development areas for future development could be 
considered.

Attorney Strachan stated that having a Conditional Use Permit allows the Planning 
Commission to impose conditions that would be sufficient to shut down a contractor’s yard 
if the owner did not comply with the conditions. 
Chair Clyde stated that a temporary use permit or business license may be appropriate 
processes and would like to have the Conditional Use permit for contractor’s yards pulled 
from residential zones. 
Planner Milliner stated that an amendment to the Use Table can be made. Contractor’s 
yards will remain in the industrial/commercial use with major and minor classifications 
made. In Salt Lake there are special exceptions and Planner Milliner will see if that would 
be legally feasible to take the place of a Conditional Use Permit.  
Attorney Strachan stated there is a provision for home occupation that is incidental to the 
home. 
Planner Milliner stated there is an application for a Conditional Use Permit for a
