 3            Grant Discussion
 4
 5   Cary Necaise, Public Works Director, explained that the City’s grant process is managed by a
 6   dedicated grant administrator who researches opportunities using tools like Lexipol, with support
 7   from multiple staff members. Grants range from federal, state, local, and private sources, often
 8   requiring matching funds and support letters, and projects need to be “shovel ready” to ensure
 9   timely use of funds. Once awarded, the finance team tracks spending and reporting requirements
10   until completion. Over the past three years, the City applied for about $8 million in grants and
11   received nearly $2 million, with roughly a 50% success rate, and staff plan to provide regular
12   updates on active and potential grants.
13
14            Jordan Basin Improvement District 13400 South Project
15
16   Mr. Necaise reported that the City is coordinating with the Jordan Basin Improvement District
17   (“JBID”) on the 13400 South project, which is approaching the intersection of 2700 West. Two
18   options are being considered: a partial closure over four weeks with daily openings for school
19   hours, or a full intersection closure during spring break to complete the work in one week. Both
20   options will significantly impact traffic, particularly access to Walmart and surrounding areas, and a
21   traffic control plan with detours will be developed.
22
23   A majority of the Council supported a full one-week closure of the 13400 South and 2700 West
24   intersection during spring break, recognizing it would allow JBID to complete the work in a single
25   excavation and backfill cycle, reduce overall costs, and limit prolonged disruption compared to a
26   partial closure over several weeks.
27
28            Capital Facilities Plan
29
30   Kevin Hicks, City Manager, outlined the City’s plan to create a comprehensive Capital Facilities
31   Plan which focuses on capital needs, consolidating past impact fee facilities plans, long-range
32   planning, and master-planned projects into a single, cohesive framework. The Capital Facilities
33   Plan will include transportation, parks, stormwater, and other infrastructure projects, prioritize
34   timing with construction seasons, and track external funding, impact fees, and City funds, while
35   excluding operating, staffing, or maintenance needs.
36
37   Councilmembers emphasized the importance of chronological organization and including grant
38   match amounts for budgeting clarity. Council discussed sustainability concerns for parks and
39   noted the importance of committing impact fee funds within required timelines to avoid returning
40   money to developers.
41
42   Mr. Hicks explained that the plan will continue to be refined and updated as additional studies,
43   transportation master plan updates, and funding opportunities arise.
