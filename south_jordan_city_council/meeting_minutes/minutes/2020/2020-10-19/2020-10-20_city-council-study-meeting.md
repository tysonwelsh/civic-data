Council Member Harris asked what initiated the text amendment for the model homes? Planning
Director Schaefermeyer said they have one builder, possibly two, that decided to make the model

    E. Discussion Item: Overview of PIDS (By Craig Smith)

Mayor Ramsey said the ULCT Board discussed this issue yesterday.

Craig Smith, attorney, reviewed a prepared presentation on Public Infrastructure Districts (PIDS).
See Attachment A.

Laura Lewis, financial consultant (Lewis Young Robertson and Burningham), said she is concerned
about doing a PID with property tax. It is doable, but they should be very cautious with that. Mr.
Smith concurred.

Ms. Lewis said some places are pushing the idea of PID’s. It is a low cost means for the developer to
put in infrastructure. It adds a tax burden to the property within the PID. With a PID, they are
agreeing to tax those citizens a property tax that does not go away until the bonds are paid off. It does
not necessarily impact the sales price of the home. She referred to an article in the Denver post from
2019 where the PID increased people’s tax bills substantially. There are reasons to do a PID, but it is
not the answer for all infrastructure needs in the city.

Council Member Marlor said if the cost of the infrastructure is being paid over time, the cost for the
resident to get into the home should be lower. Ms. Lewis said she does not think the developer will
lower the price much. The city can’t control the sales price.

Council Member Harris asked if the developers are doing it to lower their capital or increase their
profit margin? Ms. Lewis said both.

City Attorney Loose said when asked about the price of the homes in the PID’s, the developers say
that they can’t mess with the market or capitalism. They don’t anticipate a change in sales price.
Right now, the city is the gate keeper, but there are serious discussions about removing the city as the
gatekeeper.

Mr. Smith said currently, the city has full discretion to not approve a PID. He said PID’s can be a
good tool, but they should be limited in how they use it.

Council Member Shelton asked if they see PID financing as a move to eliminate impact fees? Ms.
Lewis said there could be an argument for that. Mr. Smith said it could be used as a way to fund the
impact fees.

City Attorney Loose said there are discussions about changing impact fees. The hard part about using
a PID for an impact fee is that for an impact fee, it is for the impact for the entire city. A PID is for
infrastructure financing for that development. Ms. Lewis said there could be an argument to be given
a credit for some impact fees with PID financing. She said there could be an argument for doing PID
financing at times, but if they want low cost funding, there are other ways to do that. She noted that
the tax for a PID is to pay for debt only, not operation and maintenance. The O and M is the burden
of the public owner.

Mayor Ramsey said the PID is to be approved by 100 percent of property owners in the area, but if
all of the property is owned by a developer, no one has to say yes but the developer. Mr. Smith said
yes, that is why PID’s are designed for new development.

Mayor Ramsey shared the language that the property right’s coalition is currently proposing. They
are proposing that PID’s could be done without the consent of the local government. PID’s may
annex neighboring properties to the PID area without approval of the local government. There is no
need for disclosure until property conveyance.

Ms. Lewis used the example of a $400,000 home, it would increase the tax of that home $880, per
year.

Council Member Zander asked if there are groups lobbying against PID’s? City Attorney Loose said
there is attention on the issue, but he is not sure how much lobbying there will be until they see actual
language. Mayor Ramsey said they need to see the bill language drafted. From a ULCT standpoint, it
was not well received.

Ms. Lewis said she understands that interest in PIDS were started because a city in Utah county
denied a developer an assessment area. Then, the city did not want to create the PID. She said the
city should be cautious about doing PIDS and should be involved in legislative changes.

City Attorney Loose said there are administrative costs with an assessment area that a PID does not
have. Ms. Lewis said they would also have a good interest rate with an assessment area. City
Attorney Loose said he has been told that PIDS are helpful if a developer only has an option on the
property.

Council Member McGuire asked how this is not considered taxation without representation? Mr.
Smith said because the owner and taxpayers have to consent. He said an elected board comes on, but
not until everything is done. Mayor Ramsey said they aren’t represented until the taxes are in place.
Ms. Lewis said they might not want cities to have oversight over PIDS because developers are not
getting what they want.

City Attorney Loose said not everyone that serves on special district boards are elected. He said the
development community will contend that they passed the law on PIDS, but no one will give it to
them.

City Attorney Loose said Apple Valley is trying to enable a decent size development. If they put the
majority of the city in a PID, it could be a useful tool for them. Provo did a PID for a hospital use.

It was noted that large developers in the state are pushing this. City Attorney Loose said the
developer has to own the property on the front end to do the PID.

Council Member Marlor said there needs to be a balance between interests of developers and
residents and there is not a balance. He is concerned about the PIDS. If they don’t have to disclose
the additional property tax until they go to closing, that is ridiculous.

Council Member Harris said he feels it will be legislated, regardless. He said they need to consider
how to manage it so it meets the city’s best interest. He feels PIDS will come one way or another.

Council Member Shelton said he feels like this is a tool to transfer wealth from people who don’t
have it to people who do. Philosophically, he is opposed to it. He concurred with Council Member
Harris, and said they want a seat at the table. If they just turn away and not develop their own policy,
that will not allow them a seat at the table to have a conversation. He said their lobbying efforts
should fight against this.

Council Member McGuire concurred with the previous comments. He is opposed to the PIDS. He
said they need to make sure that they have a seat at the table and are working for the city’s best
interest and the best interest of their future residents.

Council Member Zander concurred that they need to lobby on this issue. She said she does not like
making decisions out of fear. She said if a PID is laid on a small portion of residents, that does not
feel right. She said a PID is a politically charged tool. Putting a PID in place could be easier,
politically, than doing a tax assessment. She does not want to put a huge burden on a few when the
project benefits masses. They need to proceed cautiously and not take the easy path to get something
that they may regret later.

Mr. Preece noted that if a person sells an existing home within a PID, that may not even be disclosed
at all.

Infrastructure Financing Tools



• Historically, there have been three methods for a City to finance
  new development related infrastructure:
     Tax Increment Financing (Redevelopment Agency)

     Assessment Areas

     Exactions and Impact Fees

•   The Public Infrastructure District Act is a fourth tool.




                                                                  3
An Introduction to PIDs


• Act grants cities and counties in Utah the power to create
  Public Infrastructure Districts (PIDs) to finance public
  infrastructure.
• PIDs can issue debt (bonds) repayable from property
  taxes or assessments on the property owners within its
  boundaries.
• PIDs, like any tool, needs to be used thoughtfully and properly.




                                                                     4
Limitations of Existing Tools


Cities, Counties, and Redevelopment Agencies
thoughtfully consider issuing bonds to pay for
development infrastructure due to expense, risk,
pledge of security, and the potential of
foreclosure if bonds are secured by property.




                                                   5
Distinction from Existing Local Districts


• PIDs are similar to Tax Increment Financing and Special
  Assessment Areas, with a couple of exceptions:
   PIDs are local districts under Title 17B and constitute an
    independent political subdivision, not a component unit of
    the city or county that created the PID.
   Debt issued by a PID is not a legal liability of the city,
     county, or the State, therefore PID debt is not a debt of the
     city or county that created it.
   Debt is repaid from a Limited Property Taxes or
     Assessments imposed by the PID.

                                                                     6
Distinction from Existing Local Districts, continued


   Formation by the City requires 100% consent of property
    owners and any voters in the PID boundary. Making this
    tool likely used for new development and not
    redevelopment.
   PIDs have fairly broad powers to finance public
    infrastructure of many types generally including any
    improvement that will be owned by a state or local
    government, including the PID.
   City has discretion whether to create a PID and should create
    a policy as to the appropriate use of PIDs.


                                                                7
Scenario 1 – PID with Tax Increment


                       • City has large areas of vacant
                         land without infrastructure.
                       • Cost to install infrastructure
                         prohibitive to City desired
                         development.
                       • PID financing may reduce up-
                         front costs and incentivize the
                         development.
                       • Tax Increment used to pay
                         PID debt.


                                                       8
Scenario 2 – City Betterments



• Developer approaches city about a
  large scale development.
• City wants betterments that developer
  is not required to provide.
• City creates PID to allow developer to
  finance the infrastructure at a lower
  cost in exchange for betterments.




                                           9
PID Formation and Governance


•   Creation
       At the discretion of the City.
       Requires consent of 100% of property owners and voters within the PID boundaries.
•   Policy to evaluate requests to create a PID
       Statutory Criteria. Utah Code Section 17B-2a-204(7)
               (a) historical performance of the district applicant;
               (b) compliance with the creating entity's master plan;
               (c) credit worthiness of the district applicant;
               (d) plan of finance of the public infrastructure district; and
               (e) proposed development within the public infrastructure district.
       Policy should further objectives of City.
       Bond counsel and financial advisor will be required for bonds issued by PID.



                                                                                            10
Governing Document


• In addition to the statutory requirements, PIDs are governed by
  a Governing Document agreed to at the time of creation with
  the Creating Entity.
• The Governing Document should:
   Include limitations on the powers of the PID

   Establish reporting requirements

   Establish debt and mill rate levy limits

   Incorporate other limitations in accordance
     with the City’s policies and objectives


                                                                11
Governing Document, continued


• The Governing Document is enforced contractually
  through an Interlocal Agreement entered into between
  the City and the PID.
• Again here, the City and the property owner will each be
  represented by counsel.
• City may require property owner to pay all costs of City
  in creation and operation of PID.




                                                             12
Governance


• PIDs are governed by a board with members initially
  appointed by the Creating City or County.
• Initial board members must be property           owners or
  designated representatives of property owners.
• The appointed board then transitions to an elected board as
  new electors reside or own property in the PID, as
  established by the Governing Document.




                                                                13
PID Bonds



  • PIDs may issue:
     Special Assessment Bonds
     General Obligation Bonds
     Property Tax Bonds




                                 14
Limited Tax Bonds



• Limited Tax bonds:
   Are paid with property taxes by owners
   Not subject to a fair market value ratio prior to issuance
   Repaid from a limited ad valorem property tax
   Statutorily, this rate may not to exceed 15 mills
   A lower limit may be established by the City or County in
    the Governing Document or in the bond documents
   Must mature within 40 years of issuance



                                                                 15
Property Tax Bonds, continued



• In the event the proceeds of the property tax are insufficient to
  meet annual debt service as it comes due:
   Not an event of default.
   Bondholder has no statutory remedy to require additional taxes
     or fees from City.
   No statutory recourse to the property or the property owner.
• Due to higher risk, the Act limits sale of property tax bonds to
  qualified institutional buyers or to be sold in denominations of
  $500,000. Likely higher interest rate due to higher risk.




                                                                      16
Considerations for Policy and Governing
Document


• The Act only establishes the outer limits of PIDs additional
  limitations or requirements may be imposed through a policy
  of City.
• City should also use the Governing Document to ensure
  that its policies and objectives will be met.
• City should determine the types of infrastructure a PID is
  allowed to finance




                                                                 17
Considerations for Policy and Governing
Document, continued

• Financial Advisor is a Key participant in decisions on PIDs
• Include Policy:
   Any general obligation bonding (very rare).
   Procurement (including requirement of third-party engineer
     certification for acquisitions of improvements by PID)
   Transition to elected Board
   PID lifespan and dissolution if no
     activity within a certain timeframe



                                                                 18
Considerations for Policy and Drafting
Governing Document, continued

• City input into enhanced disclosure to future property owners
  within the PID
• Proper legal tax analysis over all Governing Document negotiations
  to ensure eligibility for desired tax-exempt financing and that the PID
  constitutes a “separate political subdivision” for tax purposes
• Ability to leverage with TIF, assessment, and other economic
  development revenues
• Interface with impact fees
• PID property tax can only be used for debt and not for ongoing O&M as
  this would fall to the City/County.
