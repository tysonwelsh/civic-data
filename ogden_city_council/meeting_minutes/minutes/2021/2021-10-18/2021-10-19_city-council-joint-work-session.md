Catholic Community Services- Refugee Resettlement
          Jennifer Gnagey, Adjunct Professor of Economics at Weber State University (WSU), discussed the efforts of Catholic
Community Services (CCS) to resettle Congolese refugees in Weber County; resettlement will resume again early in 2022 and this
will include approximately 150 Congolese and Mghan refugees. She briefly reviewed the resettlement process, which includes
fmding a home for refugees and providing full fmancial support for the six months immediately following the resettlement. Job
support staffhelps the refugees fmd a job and helps to emoll youth in local schools; ongoing monitoring is perfonned to ensure
that refugee families are able to get on their feet. The program has been very successful to date; in 2016, all families were
employed and paying their rent after six months. One family purchased a home in Ogden and an individual from that family is
scheduled to graduate from WSU this December. She stated she is seeking support from the City Council regarding welcoming
refugees into Ogden City.
          There was brief discussion about the background of the families or individuals that will be settled in Ogden, with Ms.
Gnagey noting that there is a broad range of education levels for those moving to area, but typically each family has one member
that can speak fluent English. Ongoing discussion centered on other community partners that are being informed of the new
resettlement plans, as well as recognition of volunteers in the community who have been vital in supporting the refugees locating
in Ogden City. The Council concluded by offering their support for the efforts ofCCS and the resettlement of refugees.

Proposed Rezone of 550 East 900 North
          Council Policy Analyst Watkins explained a developer has petitioned to rezone a vacant church property from R-1-6
(single-family residential) to R-3 (multi-family residential). The purpose of the rezone is to demolish the vacant church and build
townhomes. The Planning Commission determined that this rezone request does comply with the General Plan, and that
townhomes would create an effective transition from higher density housing at Washington Boulevard to single-family housing to
the east of the property in question. It was also noted that a new development would be preferable to a vacant church lot, which
could fall into disrepair and negatively impact the neighborhood. The Planning Commission unanimously recommended rezoning
the property from R-1-6 to R-3. The Council voted to extend this item during the October 5, 2021 Council meeting. Due to

Stipend/Bonuses Ordinance.
         Council Executive Director Eller-Smith stated that per the Council's direction, staff is proposing an amendment to
Municipal Code §2-6-8(G) to limit the bonus amount an employee may receive in any given year as follows:
                   "The total of all performance-based bonuses or stipends received by an employee shall not exceed the
                   employee's salary range authorized by the pay plan by more than ten percent (10%) in any single fiscal year."
         The staff proposal also includes removing the word "annual" from Section (G) to allow a bonus to be awarded any time
during the year. A report for the current fiscal year listing the number of employees who have or will be receiving annual bonuses
in each department and division, and the amount of and purpose for each respective bonus will be provided as part of the annual
budget process in April or May of each year.
         Ms. Eller-Smith thanked Council member Lopez for raising this issue several months ago; when she first reviewed the
ordinance, she did not realize there was no cap on the amount of a bonus. She asked the Council if they are comfortable acting on
staffs proposal. No objections were voiced by the Council.

Council/Board Business
          Conservation Easements: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith noted Anna Keogh wants to place her property within
a conservation easement and she needs a letter from the City Council granting authorization of such. She is up against a deadline
and Council staffhas considered the best way to respond. Council member Hyer wrote a letter indicated he is comfortable with the
conservation easement, but that it would take some time for the matter to work through the Council's process. This was six weeks
ago and since that time she has conducted some research that led her to believe that there is a Planning process required for this
type of issue. However, she heard from Council member Hyer yesterday asking about the status of a recommendation from the
Planning Commission. She contacted the Planning Division as well as representative from the Quality Growth Commission, a
state entity that manages conservation easements. She noted that Ms. Keogh applied for agricultural protection ofher property 20
years ago and that action was considered by the Planning Commission; that body denied the request because under the existing
zone (R-1-6), farming was still permitted, and it was not necessary to place the property in an agriculture protection area.
However, the City Council took action to grant Ms. Keogh permission to place a conservation easement on the property. The two
are not the same thing; agricultural protection areas are addressed in City ordinances, but conservation easements are not. She
discussed the key differences between the two property protection mechanisms, after which she presented a map to illustrate the
proposed boundary of the easement. In order for the Quality Growth Commission to approve the conservation easement, they need
a resolution from the Council approving such, but they need this action prior to the end of the month. She asked the Council if
they are willing to hold a special meeting on October 26, 2021 for this action. The Council discussed the purpose of a
conservation easement, whether a working farm is the most appropriate use in this location, and why the Council must approve
this type of issue for a private property. While concerns were expressed about the permanent nature of a conservation easement,
the Council ultimately concluding they were willing to meet on October 26, 2021 to discuss and possibly vote on the matter.
