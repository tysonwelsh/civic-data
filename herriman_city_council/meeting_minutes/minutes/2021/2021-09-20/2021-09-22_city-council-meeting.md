    2.2. Discussion regarding a proposed amendment in Herriman City Code Title 10 Chapter 29 to
          comply with Utah State Code for single family design standards - Michael Maloy, City Planner
City Planner Michael Maloy offered a background of the proposed changes for the single-family design standards
based on the previous legislative session. He highlighted the interim session discussions related to House Bill
1003. City Planner Maloy reviewed the proposed draft amendments to be considered. The first amendment was
to determine whether the proposal was consistent with goals, objectives, and policies of the General Plan. City
Planner Maloy indicated the Herriman General Plan notated a vision statement to improve design standards to
reflect the “countryside flavor” and roots of Herriman. Though this change would be less restrictive than the
current code, it would allow individuals to create a “countryside flavor” if desired. The second proposed
amendment was harmonious with the overall character of existing development in the vicinity of the subject
property. Staff recommended these changes surrounding properties would not have the existing design
restrictions. This could potentially create a harmonious feel; however, incompatible designs could be potentially
apparent on adjacent properties. The next proposed amendment could adversely affect adjacent property and
would increase the flexibility in single-family and two-family dwelling designs. Adjacent properties would no
longer be limited in their design choices. Lastly, the adequacy of facilities and services intended to serve the
subject property, included roadways, parks and recreation facilities, police and fire protection, schools,
stormwater drainage systems, water supplies, and wastewater and refuse collection. Staff recommended a change
in the design standards as they would not affect facilities or services. The Council agreed.

requested to highlight recent changes for public and Council consumption. This item, in one form or another,
was discussed in ten of the last 14 meetings, there have been 25 working group meetings conducted, five drafts
of the proposed development agreement and design guidelines have been circulated, the Planning Commission
held a public hearing on the master development agreement and the design guidelines, and the City Council held
a public hearing at the previous meeting and subsequently continued to tonight’s meeting. The communications
team had held several community meetings regarding the development, booths were at Herriman Towne Days
and the farmers market to solicit public input, and an online form was available for residents to submit comments
to be read into the public meeting.

City Attorney Andrizzi addressed the major changes conducted since the previous meeting.
    • Density on the west bench area shall be capped at 20 units per gross acre.
    • Commercial development required would have three different possible scenarios:
           o Alignment of U-111/12600 South intersection fell within city boundaries.
           o Alignment of U-111/12600 South intersection fell outside city boundaries.
           o Alignment of U-111 merged onto an existing roadway.
    • Preserving certain transportation corridors, specifically 12600 South, and working mutually with the

    • Secondary water would be required in zones three and four.
    • Off-site improvements for 6400 West would be reimbursed to the developer through impact fees if they
        chose to install.
