Discussion Items

       Budget Discussion

Mayor Staggs asked the Council to decide how they would like the budget discussion to go.
Councilmember McCay asked for a side-by-side comparison of the Mayor’s budget to the City
Manager’s Budget.

Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, explained that fund 10-41-310 includes a request
for an Economic Development position. Mayor Staggs removed money for this request because
he thought that Mr. Lethbridge could take on this position hire a Planner instead.

Mayor Staggs commented that he meets weekly with Mr. Lethbridge about ongoing projects and
development. Mr. Lethbridge said that the Planner position was opened and filled already.

Mr. Hicks explained that the Mayor’s budget cut fund 10-43-211 which is the membership to the
Utah Leagues of Cities and Towns. Councilmember McCay said that ULCT does a good job
lobbying for the cities they represent and provides education benefits, land use academy,
training, and networking. She would like to continue to be a member of the league.

Mayor Staggs commented that he feels like the league is always playing defense. After
discussion, all Councilmembers agreed to add this line item back in.

Councilmember McCay agreed with Mayor Staggs about the Economic Development position.
She thinks that Mr. Lethbridge would do a good job with economic development and does not
think a new position is warranted. All Councilmembers agreed.

Mr. Hicks explained that fund 10-43-675 is the City Council contingency fund with $100,000.
The purpose of this fund was to give the City Manager the ability to pay for projects that come
up unexpectedly.

Mayor Staggs said he was very much against having this contingency fund because of
transparency. He believes that Councilmembers should get support from each other and place
any projects they would like into a specific line item in the budget. He believes that this fund
causes Councilmember to go and direct staff and does not think this makes for good practice.

Councilmember McDougal asked if there is capability in the budget to handle small item issues
that need to be addressed quickly. Mr. Hicks said it depends on the issue and whether there is
budget in a particular category. Councilmember McDougal agreed with the Mayor that large
projects should be specifically called out in the budget.

Councilmember Buroker said that the amount budgeted in this contingency fund is too high and
would like to see it budgeted at $5,000.

Councilmember Stewart said that he initially thought this line item was necessary and explained
that the contingency fund allowed $10,000 per district. He was an advocate for this contingency
fund but is now speaking against it. He would like to see this money used to enhance specific
categories in the budget.

Councilmember Wells agreed to cut this line item as long as there was a way to accomplish
some of the smaller projects that could come up during the year.

Mr. Hicks explained that fund 10-60-312 designated money for Christmas light metering and
fund 10-46-318 budgeted money for the monthly power bill for the Christmas lights.

Mayor Staggs explained that he would like a different solution for Christmas lights including
solar or other methods. He does not like that Rocky Mountain Power charges the City for the
metering. Councilmember Buroker agrees with the Mayor and wants to see the budget cut for
Christmas light metering.

Mayor Staggs said that he doesn’t like spending so much money on technical equipment when
everything is stored in the cloud.

Mr. Hicks explained that fund 10-64-700 is for capital outlay and included lighting for Tithing Hill
Park parking lot. He said that staff had determined that the parking lot lights were not needed.

Councilmember Stewart said the bollards on 4600 West 12600 South on the trail had issues for
years. He would like to use this money to replace these lights with in-ground lighting or find

other areas in the budget were increased for hospitality.

Mr. Hicks explained that line item 10-65-350 for community events was increased because the
Mayor wanted more money for Hope Week and the Live in Real Life Event. Councilmembers
supported this.

Mr. Hicks explained the request for a part-time court clerk in the judicial wages and benefits
category. The Mayor does not want to include this position because the court is in transition
right now. Judge Bown will be retiring as of July 1. He would like to wait and see if there is a
need once the court transitions.

Mr. Hicks explained that the public works line item includes seasonal employees and the new

Councilmember Stewart explained that Riverton City is assigned part of the wildland costs

Fuels Mitigation crew.

Councilmember Buroker said that in the past the City has received grants to manage weed
control along the Jordan River Trail and she wants to make sure there are sufficient funds to
address the weeds. Mr. Necaise said that there is adequate money to handle this.

would clean and lock the restrooms and this position has been eliminated because of the
contract.

Mr. Hicks explained that fund 10-90-665 was cut in relation to a full-time facility maintenance

this fiscal year.

Chief Hutson expressed concerns about the Mayor cutting other parts of the police budget. He
said that he was okay with the K-9 budget being cut. He was concerned about the guns/ammo
line item, the fuel cost line item and the capital outlay line item all being cut and explained his
reasoning. Councilmembers agreed to budget guns/ammo and the fuel costs up to the Chief’s
request and to budget $20,000 for capital outlay. Councilmember Stewart asked about the 4-

Councilmember Buroker agreed that this was rollover money.

weeks.

improvements are not made. Mr. Necaise also said they will look for grants to help pay for the
costs.

grants for the remaining costs.

Councilmember Buroker said she is confident that the company will be able to get grants.
Councilmember McCay suggested waiting on this project until they could go there as a Council

Public Hearings

         Ordinance No. 22-15 – Kunz Rezone, General Plan Amendment & Rezone of
         Approximately 3.25-Acres Located at 11944 South 3600 West from the General
         Plan Designation to Low Density Residential to Medium-High Density Residential,
         and Zoning from R-3 to RM-6

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director explained this was a public hearing for a
General Plan Amendment and Rezone application brought by the applicant for three adjoining
parcels totaling 3.25-acres located near 11944 South 3600 West. The subject property sits on
the west side of 3600 West on the north bank of Midas Creek. To the north of this property is
the recently approved Ivory development “Harvest Gardens”. Ivory Homes rezoned the property
that makes up Harvest Gardens to RM-6 SD. During the rezone hearing it was mentioned that
the Kunz property may also want to develop in the near future. Part of Ivory’s approval was to
include a stub road south to Kunz property, but the Kunz property was not a part of the Ivory
rezone, or the Harvest Gardens development. The subject property is currently zoned R-3,
which is a single-family residential zone, with 14,000-sf lots. The same R-3 zoning applies to the
subdivision to the west, and the next few parcels to the south. To the north is the RM-6 SD
mentioned above. To the east across 3600 West Street the Zoning is RR-22 and R-2 both
single-family residential zones with large lots over 20,000-sf.
