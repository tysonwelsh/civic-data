Commissioner Lee opened the public hearing to public comment, and County resident Raphael Millet came forward.
Mr. Millet works in the Utah County Recorder’s Office and declared he is commenting as a private citizen, taking
time from work and missing a medical procedure for a family member to be present. Referencing county meetings
conducted the prior week, Mr. Millet explained he was called into a meeting with his supervisor, Department Head
(Assistant County Recorder Andrea Allen and County Recorder Jeff Smith, respectively), and County Human
Resources Director Cammie Taylor before leaving the office yesterday to be chastised and intimidated; he was told to
limit his comments and refrain from outbursts during Commission meetings. Mr. Millet asked Commissioner Lee
whether he had advised the Human Resources Director to relay this message. Commissioner Lee replied, ‘no,’ and
Mr. Millet suggested the Board investigate whether other employees are being treated similarly. He explained he is
passionate when speaking about employee productivity and retaliation against those who voice concerns; Mr. Millet
knows professionals have left county employment due to their treatment by authority figures.

(Track A, 10:00)
Pam Bair of the Utah County Sheriff’s Office mentioned she had commented during the Commission Work Session:
Salary Study meeting which took place the previous week on Monday, December 4, 2017. Ms. Bair listed several
Commission meeting minutes from the past: April 17, 2007 – Work Session; September 25, 2007 – Regular Agenda;
December 4, 2007 – Public Hearing; June 10, 2008 – Public Hearing; and June 17, 2008 – Regular Agenda, wherein
the county 401(A) Plan was discussed and ultimately taken away from non-public-safety employees. She explained
there were issues with hiring employees and retention, and this was proposed as the solution: exchanging 13.9 percent
of retirement benefits for a twelve-percent raise which would not cost the county more money. This change went into
effect January 2008, and was followed by market studies and a subsequent pay freeze for several years. Ms. Bair
pointed out the elected officials were not omitted from these changes; in 2008, the Commission retained the 401(A)
while also taking a 9.7% pay increase, making them the highest paid Commission the state of Utah. She noted it was
a prior Board that made the decision but the current members benefit from it. Ms. Bair summarized the Board members
have 401(K) and 401(A) Plans and high salaries, yet still have issues with employee hiring and retention and are now
considering adoption of a budget which will not address those employee concerns. Utah County Clerk/Auditor Bryan
E. Thompson and Merritt Fullmer of the Utah County Human Resources Department clarified elected officials would
have to opt out of state retirement to utilize those benefits – the 401(A) no longer exists. Ms. Bair reiterated the
Commissioners are paid at the height of their market range while others who have worked at the county long-term are
‘topped out’ and unable to progress financially.

(Track A, 16:35)
Raphael Millet returned to state for the record, ‘that we need to know what everything is in the past, to know in the
future.’ He explained total gross compensation for elected officials should be readily available for the public.
Commissioner Lee noted the figures are available online.

(Track A, 17:50)
Provo resident Robert Stevens summarized he has read through the proposed budget and ‘can see the “what,” but I
don’t understand the “why.”’ He suggested clear labels be used to prevent citizen uncertainty. It can be assumed the
County Sheriff’s Office expenses are to protect the public, but other department needs and functions are not as easily
identified. Mr. Stevens thanked the Board for their time and expressed trust that most people elected to positions of
power want to serve their communities.

10.    APPROVE ONE OPTION PRESENTED BY MCGRATH HUMAN RESOURCES GROUP WITH AN
       IMPLEMENTATION IN 2018 (Continued from the December 5, 2017 Commission meeting)
(Track A, 42:30)
Utah County Human Resources Director Cammie Taylor came forward with Merritt Fullmer of her office to present
to the Board. Commissioner Lee mentioned county employees have been given the opportunity to review draft
versions of the market study, and Ms. Taylor summarized an outside consultant has recommended a new salary
structure centered around fair market value for county positions. The Human Resources Office has outlined five
options to implement the results of the compensation market study.

Cammie Taylor explained ‘Option 1’ is to place employees on their current step within the new system. Ms. Taylor
stated this option would primarily impact those paid below the lowest point in the market value range of their position.
She explained the appropriate starting point to determine budget impact and increase is $54,089,417.83, when taking
into consideration 2018 projections and the 2.35% distribution included in the 2018 Tentative Budget. Ms. Taylor
and Commissioner Graves discussed the methods for calculating that figure, with Commissioner Graves voicing
concerns that employee years of service are not being represented; newer hires would be favored with increases
comparable to people who have been at the county for ten or more years. Ms. Taylor explained the steps do take years
worked into consideration, and they discussed their views. Ms. Taylor detailed this choice would impact the budget
by $976,481.77 in wages, for a 2018 compensation total of $85,508,000: approximately $55,000,000 in wages,
$18,000,000 in benefits, and $12,000,000 through elected options. She and the three commissioners reviewed those
numbers and various percentages with input from Utah County Clerk/Auditor Bryan E. Thompson. They determined
the actual increase of implementing Option 1 would be approximately 1.4- to 1.5-million dollars when using 2017
actual wage and benefit rates as the base figures.

(Track A, 1:01:55)
Commissioner Graves noted adopting Option 1 would mean adopting the new salary schedule, which the county
cannot currently afford. Commissioner Lee suggested the options be reviewed before deciding on the schedule, due
to multiple variables. Commissioner Graves mentioned ‘Option 2’ would cost $700,000 more than Option 1, when
including the Utah Retirement Systems (URS) numbers, and Commissioner Lee confirmed that amount to be
$2,015,289.00.

(Track A, 1:06:50)
Cammie Taylor described ‘Option 3’ as taking certain current employees who have worked six years or more and
placing them at the current market pay, increasing the budget by $2,100,000. ‘Option 4,’ taking the expected employee
raise of 2.35% and placing them within the new schedule at that rate, would cost $2,200,000. Commissioner Graves
estimated to correct the figure to $2,700,000 when taking public safety benefits into consideration. Lastly, Ms. Taylor
reviewed ‘Option 5:’ use the fair market value provided by the independent consultant for individual employees, shift
the position range by that many steps, and identify current placement within the scale based on years of service. Ms.
Taylor summarized this would impact the budget by $4,316,411.87 for wages, and Commissioner Graves added
benefits for total impact of approximately $5,500,000. Commissioner Lee reviewed the options and budget impacts:
       Option 1: $1,400,000
       Option 2: $2,000,000
       Option 3: $2,100,000
       Option 4: Between $2,500,000 and $2,700,000
       Option 5: $5,500,000

(Track A, 1:10:55)
Commissioner Lee asked for additional details regarding Option 4, and Commissioner Graves expressed interest in
Option 5. Commissioner Graves stated Option 5 is the one that implements the market study to compensate employees
based on experience. Merritt Fullmer of the Utah County Human Resources Office confirmed this would be based on
a 13-step pay plan with adjusted job titles. Commissioner Graves pointed out employees had approached him with
concerns about the titles, and Cammie Taylor explained ‘Victoria’ (Dr. Victoria McGrath, McGrath Human Resources
Group) was directed to provide streamlined titles for internal consistency. Commissioner Graves also mentioned Dr.
McGrath’s study found the average county employee to be underpaid by about eight percent when compared to the
market. Commissioners Lee and Graves talked about how titles might have affected the results of the study, with Ms.
Fullmer commenting on job descriptions versus titles.

(Track A, 1:16:40)
Commissioner Lee voiced concerns that the compensation study was intended to compare the current county salaries
to the general market, but it keeps being presented as a combination study and new pay scale – those are two separate
issues. He gave an example of certain job titles being ‘flagged’ in the study for pay differences of more than forty
percent when compared to the market, but that percentage not reflecting another individual with more responsibilities
under the same title. Commissioner Lee pointed out other positions which suggested zero change to pay, and Cammie
Taylor confirmed no adjustments were made by her department; they only double-checked that all data was input
correctly. Commissioner Lee stated there is an imbalance between newer and long-term employees. Ms. Taylor
replied the imbalance is perpetuated by conducting a market study and not implementing changes, and Commissioner
