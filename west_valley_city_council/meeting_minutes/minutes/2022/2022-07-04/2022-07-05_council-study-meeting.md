A.   ACCEPT PUBLIC INPUT REGARDING APPLICATION ZT-4-2022,
     FILED BY WEST VALLEY CITY, REQUESTING A ZONE TEXT
     CHANGE BY REDUCING THE MINIMUM AREA REQUIRED FOR
     SHOPPING CENTERS IN SECTION 7-7-124 FROM ONE ACRE TO
     THREE QUARTERS OF ONE ACRE
     Mayor Lang informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular Council
     Meeting scheduled July 12, 2022 in order for the City Council to hear and consider
     public comments regarding application ZT-4-2022, Filed by West Valley City,
     Requesting a Zone Text Change by Reducing the Minimum Area Required for
     Shopping Centers in Section 7-7-124 from One Acre to Three Quarters of One Acre.

       Proposed Ordinance 22-27 related to the proposal to be considered by the City
       Council subsequent to the public hearing, was discussed as follows:

       ACTION: ORDINANCE NO. 22-27, AMEND SECTIONS 7-103 AND 7-7-124
       OF THE WEST VALLEY CITY MUNICIPAL CODE TO AMEND
       CERTAIN REQUIREMENTS CONCERNING SHOPPING CENTERS
       Steve Pastorik, CED Director, discussed proposed Ordinance 22-27 that would
       amend Sections 7-103 and 7-7-124 of the West Valley City Municipal Code to Amend
       Certain Requirements Concerning Shopping Centers.

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

              At the request of the City Council, City staff is proposing an ordinance
              amendment to reduce the minimum area required for shopping centers in
              Section 7-7-124 from one acre to three quarters of one acre.

              The current one-acre minimum shopping center size was adopted in March
              of 2017. At the time the City had reviewed many L-shaped strip malls that
              were proposed on smaller commercial parcels in several locations. Prior to
              March 2017, there was no minimum area specific to shopping centers. After
       MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JULY 5, 2022
                            -3-


              having several discussions on this topic, the City Council directed staff to
              change the ordinance to allow shopping centers on properties that are at
              least three quarters of one acre in area.

              The proposed ordinance also amends the definition of shopping centers to
              be more objective and clarifies that the area of adjoining parcels that share
              parking and access can be combined to meet the minimum shopping center
              area.

       Councilmember Huynh asked if two or more tenants in a single building must be
       retail businesses. Steve replied yes.

       The City Council will consider Ordinances 22-27 at the Regular Council Meeting
       scheduled July 12, 2022 at 6:30 P.M.

ORDINANCE 22-28: AMEND SECTIONS 23-6-104(1) AND 23-6-106(1) OF THE
WEST VALLEY CITY MUNICIPAL CODE TO COMPLY WITH STATE LAW
REGARDING ANIMAL CONTROL
Layne Morris, CPD Director, presented proposed Ordinance 22-28 that would amend
Sections 23-6-104(1) and 23-6-106(1) of the West Valley City Municipal Code to Comply
With State Law Regarding Animal Control.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       It is proposed that Code West Valley City Code 23-6-104(1) be amended to read:

              Domesticated animals shall be impounded for a minimum of three full
       calendar five business days before further disposition, except as otherwise
       provided herein. Reasonable effort shall be made to notify the owner of any
       animal wearing a license or other identification during that time. Notice shall be
       deemed given when sent to the last known address of the listed owner. Any animal
       voluntarily relinquished to the Animal Control facility by the owner thereof for
       destruction or other disposition need not be kept for the minimum holding period
       before release or other disposition as herein provided.

       It is proposed that Code West Valley City Code 23-6-106(1)(a) be amended to
       read:
       MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JULY 5, 2022
                            -4-


              If the animal was impounded, it must be held for a minimum of three
       working five business days prior to being eligible for adoption. Animals that have
       been signed over to the City by their owners are eligible for adoption
       immediately.

       Utah State Code 11-46-103 requires that unidentified or unclaimed animals be held
       for a minimum of five business days prior to making any final disposition of the
       animal.

       The changes to our ordinance are necessary to remove any discrepancy between
       city and state code.

The City Council will consider Ordinance 22-28 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
July 12, 2022 at 6:30 P.M.

RESOLUTION 22-120: AUTHORIZE THE PROCUREMENT OF SERVICES
FROM WILSON & COMPANY, INC.
Coby Wilson, Public Works, presented proposed Resolution 22-120 that would authorize
the Procurement of Services from Wilson & Company, Inc.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       West Valley City will receive federal Surface Transportation Program funds to
       assist with the design and construction of a roadway reconstruction project on
       Lancer Way (3650 South) from 3200 West to 2700 West. The amount of federal
       participation on this project is $5,771,489, which requires a local match of
       $419,103.08 from West Valley City. In addition to the local match, it is the city’s
       responsibility to complete an environmental study for the project prior to the
       distribution of the federal funds.

       The proposed project will reconstruct and widen Lancer Way from two-lanes to
       three-lanes, including a center turn-lane. The project will include the addition of
       bike lanes, curb and gutter, sidewalk, street lighting, streetscape, and drainage
       improvements.

       This agreement will enable the environmental consultant, Wilson & Company, Inc.,
       to complete the tasks associated with the performance of a UDOT approved
       environmental study. Upon completion of the environmental study, the WVC
       Engineering division will coordinate with UDOT and FHWA to access the federal

       that construction will begin in 2025.

Jamie Young, Acting Parks and Recreation Director, presented proposed Resolution 22-
121 that would authorize the Acceptance of a 2022 Utah Outdoor Recreation Grant

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       The West Valley City West View Park has undeveloped land directly adjacent to
