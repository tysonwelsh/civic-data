2.      An ordinance amending Provo City Code Title 14 for all references of the sign code.
        Citywide application. (PLOTA20220287) (0:06:57)

Javin Weaver, Planner, presented. This ordinance amendment will shift all references of the sign
code to Chapter 14.38. Many individual zone chapters include elements of the sign code, so this
would simplify the city code by consolidating everything in one place. Mr. Weaver noted several
changes that are being made as well, with the sign approval process being changed to the
building permit/building division (rather than the Planning Commission). For signs designating
historic properties, monuments, or sites of historic significance, these signs will go to the
Landmarks Commission for approval rather than the Planning Commission. There are several
provisions in the Manufactured Home Park zone which will be shifted to an administrative
process. Councilor Shannon Ellsworth asked if this ordinance would change several processes to
become administrative processes, which Mr. Weaver indicated was correct. He also indicated
that this includes the sign provisions for the Campus Mixed Use zone as well. Presentation only.
This item was already scheduled for the Council Meeting on October 18, 2022.

5.      A presentation regarding the Utah Municipal Power Agency proposed Integrated
        Resource Plan. (22-078) (0:13:57)

Travis Ball, Provo Power Director, presented. He gave an overview of the Integrated Resource
Plan and what it entails. The plan includes information about resource planning and electric load
forecasting, summary of power sources, analysis of future power resources, and information
about demand management energy efficiency.

Councilor Travis Hoban asked if the city has any stranded or wasted energy, or does Provo sell
all its excess energy. Mr. Ball explained that if energy is not used when generated, it is wasted.
They try to minimize waste, but it is more challenging with intermittent resources such as solar
they don’t have control over. Mr. Hoban asked what the main source of stranded energy was. Mr.
Ball said it might be some of the resources they are taking off during the day. Natural gas can
allow them to react more quickly, but hydroelectric, coal, and nuclear are baseload products and
it is harder to react to intermittent changes. Hydroelectric power has been very reliable in the
past, but it has decreased greatly this year due to drought conditions. Mr. Ball explained most of
Provo’s hydroelectric energy comes from the Colorado River.

Councilor Shannon Ellsworth thanked Provo Power for their terrific public event. She said it was
very well attended and it was exciting for the public to learn more about this essential service.
Mr. Ball gave a shoutout to Kat Linford, who was involved with putting on the event. Councilor
George Handley asked where to find the Integrated Resource Plan—Mr. Ball indicated it was
available on the UMPA website. Mr. Handley asked about the prospects for nuclear power and
whether it was on the horizon. Mr. Ball indicated there were two different competing
technologies and that some cities in Utah have been involved in building a small nuclear reactor
in Idaho. He noted that there are always considerable overhead costs for nuclear power.
Councilor Rachel Whipple noted that the plan is mislabeled on the UMPA website, and she
pointed out the error. Presentation only.

9.      An ordinance amending Provo City Code regarding Accessory Dwelling Units.
        Citywide Application. (PLOTA20220263) (1:53:34)

Melia Dayley, Policy Analyst, presented. She highlighted the purpose of the presentation, which
was to share the updates to the draft Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) ordinance and allow for
Council discussion prior to the Council meeting where the ordinance would be considered. Ms.
Dayley highlighted additional questions which had been received and addressed. Ms. Dayley
indicated that city code already has parking requirements tied to the number of residents in a
household. State law HB82 did not allow for HOA-specific exclusions. Specific zone exclusions
are already listed in Chapter 14.30; the SDP-5 zone (Broadview Shores development) was
already excluded in city code as passed in December 2021. Regarding minimum lot sizes, the
Housing Committee discussed allowing ADUs on residential zoned properties with a minimum
size of 8000 square-foot lots, however, there was not a firm conclusion about whether the
committee wanted to move this provision forward.

Ms. Dayley addressed a question about the term “immediate properties” as referenced in the
petition process outlined in the ordinance. The Housing Committee has updated this language to
be more specific and it is included in the version of the ordinance being presented. Ms. Dayley
indicated there were some questions surrounding the logistics after the petition process has taken
place and she highlighted how this was addressed in the most recent draft.

Councilor Shannon Ellsworth asked if there was a square foot minimum in the current ordinance;
Ms. Dayley said there was not. Ms. Ellsworth also asked about how the permit for ADUs is
different than a conditional use permit. Ms. Dayley responded and Brian Jones, City Attorney,
provided additional context on this type of permit. Mr. Jones outlined some of the requirements
of this permit type and indicated that the permit was tied to the property rather than to the
property owner. Brandon Larsen, Planner, and Mr. Jones addressed whether this should instead
be tied to the property owner, as one of the requirements is that the owner sign an
acknowledgment confirming they will comply with the parking standards as well as have an
owner occupant on the property. Mr. Larsen indicated the current draft did not require recording
the agreement, so they would keep them on file at their office. Mr. Jones clarified that the way
the ordinance is written, it is mandatory to issue a permit if the conditions are met. One of the
conditions is maintaining a rental dwelling license—so the new owner would have to obtain a
rental dwelling license, and at that time they could have them sign a new acknowledgment. Ms.
Ellsworth asked what the required parking was for a duplex versus an ADU. Mr. Larsen said it
would require 3 parking spaces for each unit, so a legal duplex would require six parking spaces;
an ADU would require 4 parking spaces.

Councilor David Shipley asked about the cost for a rental-dwelling license and a special use
permit. Mr. Larsen said the special use permit was not in the city code at this time—Mr. Jones
indicated that this would require an update to the consolidated fee schedule. Ms. Dayley talked
about the committee having a one-time fee for the special use permit of $300 (whereas RDLs
have an annual fee). Mr. Larsen indicated that RDLs are $20 for one unit and $60 for multiple
units. Mr. Shipley asked about the time frame and what people should expect from the
application process. Mr. Larsen said it is an administrative application process, which takes
