3.1   Todd Shoemaker, resident, stated that he spoke to the Point of the Mountain State
      Land Authority board meeting on January 11, 2022. He was one of four Utah
      citizens in favor of some prison preservation to speak to the board. The chair of
      this meeting was Utah Lieutenant Governor, Deidre Henderson, with Alan
      Matheson, Executive Director of the Point, in attendance. Draper Mayor Troy
      Walker was also present and represents Draper as a member of the board. Mr.
      Shoemaker then shared the following portion of the aforementioned board
      meeting minutes:

         [VERBATIM]
         As identified, my name is David Emmett. I’m speaking to you today as a
         representative of Preservation Utah, which, of course, is a non-profit that
         advocates for historic preservation across Utah. I actually had the opportunity
         to address all of you approximately two years or so ago, about saving the
         historic core of the Utah State penitentiary. Of course, I’m well aware that
         over the last few months you have decided to save only a guard tower to
         represent this larger complex and its equally complex story. As I articulated in
         the letter I asked Mr. Matheson to distribute to all of you last week,
         Preservation Utah staff, Board of Trustees, and constituents feel that saving
         the prison chapel, in addition to, if not in place of the guard tower, would be a
         better option. As I stated in the letter I distributed to Mr. Matheson, this chapel
         was, in fact, not paid for with taxpayer dollars, but rather with funds donated
         by the people of Utah. The chapel has been a site where a tremendous amount
         of service, assistance, education, effort, and aid were offered by the people of
         Utah to the incarcerated population. And, of course, the chapel also offered
         venue for members of this incarcerated population to aid and assist each other.
         I feel this is the building that deserves to stand, to represent the prison and

3.2   David Bennett, resident, stated that he lived between the amphitheater and Oak
      Hollow Elementary School. Mr. Bennet stated that recently, the City put in extreme
      “no parking” signs throughout the whole neighborhood. The neighborhood was
      built in 1996 by the third-largest homebuilder in the United States. He said that
      there are 81 lots, each of which has a primary home, an attached garage, and at
      the time of construction ADUs were also allowed. Therefore, there are
      approximately 130 residential units impacted by the “no parking” signs. Mr.
      Bennett stated that the neighborhood was upset by this. The street width is
      standard: 25 feet. However, these parking restrictions do not exist elsewhere in
      the City. Mr. Bennett stated that parking was needed in his neighborhood because
      it was such a dense housing area. He stated that the lower socioeconomic housing
      and residents were being treated punitively and differently than other residents
      and neighborhoods in Draper. Mr. Bennett implored the City’s officials to
      investigate and address the issue, and in so doing to obtain input from the affected
      residents. He concluded by noting that Draper City police were harassing his
      neighbors for parking on the street, but were not patrolling Oak Hollow Elementary
      School during drop-off and pick-up times when the street was packed with cars.

3.3   Quin Cook, resident, indicated he was part of the same neighborhood as Mr.
      Bennett. He reiterated the concern that the “no parking” signs have caused him
      and his neighbors. He was especially concerned that the residents had not been
      allowed to offer the City feedback on the matter. Mr. Cook stated that in the past,
      the residents had been involved in the process of creating ordinances for the area.
      He noted that he and another neighbor were in the process of putting together a
      petition.

5.0   Public Hearing: Approval of Resolution #22-08, a Resolution amending
      the adopted budget of Draper City for Fiscal Year 2021-2022.

5.1   Bob Wylie, Finance Director, presented nine budgetary line item changes as part
      of this proposed resolution.

      Mr. Wylie explained that staff was looking at taking $60,000 from the current Arena
      events operational budget, along with park impact fees totaling $300,000, to
      create a capital project account for improvements. These improvements would
      include canopy structures, picnic tables, and other items to finish the plaza area.

5.2   David Dobbins, City Manager, stated that the information being presented was the
      best option. He explained that the proposed budget would help fulfill the vision of
      the Arena as it was originally intended. He expressed the concern that if the City
      did not allocate sufficient resources to the Arena, it would not meet the vision for
      the Arena and the City could potentially end up regretting that decision.

5.3   Councilmember F. Lowry asked if everyone had seen the drawings and plans for
      the Arena.

5.4   Councilmember T. Lowery stated that Councilmember Vawdrey was wondering
      about stairs going down. Mr. Dobbins said that in visiting the site today, he felt
      there needed to be a way to get from the west bleachers to the other side. He did
      not know a cost estimate for this.

5.5   Rhett Ogden, Parks and Recreation Director, provided a ballpark estimate of $12
      per square foot for concrete. Mr. Dobbins suggested approving a general budget
      amount for the stairs and then modifying that number once a better estimate is
      determined. Mayor Walker added that the City should also look at the gates as
      well. There was subsequent discussion on the matter.

6.0   Action Item: Resolution #22-09, a Resolution adopting amended
      Planning Commission Bylaws and Rules of Procedure.

6.1   Mike Barker, City Attorney, presented an annotated, redlined copy of the above
      document. The proposed changes eliminated redundant language and simplified
      processes based on how business was being conducted. The Planning Commission
      reviewed the proposed changes and forwarded a positive recommendation to the
      Council.

      Mr. Barker shared the Commission’s discussions concerning duties of the Vice-
      Chairperson: specifically, whether or not they vote. Mr. Barker noted that
      generally, the Vice-Chair has not voted if there is a full panel present; however, in
      cases where there aren’t enough members present, then they have voted. The
      suggested language stated that the Vice-Chair “shall have the power to vote” as
      opposed to the Vice-Chair “must vote.”

      Mr. Barker continued that the Planning Commission’s role was not to interact with
      the public; rather, this was the Council’s role. The majority of the Planning
      Commission’s tasks were administrative.

      Next, Mr. Barker presented amendments to the amount of time given during public
      comment periods. He also discussed protocols for continuing items, as well as
      voting abstentions.

6.2   There was subsequent discussion regarding the proposed changes as presented
      by Mr. Barker.

6.3   Councilmember T. Lowery moved to approve Resolution #22-09.
      Councilmember Vawdrey seconded the motion.
