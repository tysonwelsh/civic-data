 3            Speed Bumps Discussion
 4
 5   Kevin Hicks, City Manager, explained that Councilmembers Pierucci and McCay requested a
 6   discussion regarding the City’s ordinance on speed bumps. The ordinance, adopted
 7   unanimously in 2018, required an inventory of all existing speed bumps and prohibited the
 8   installation of new ones after October 1 of that year. He said there was some confusion about
 9   whether speed bumps that existed before 2018 should remain after street overlays, stating his
10   interpretation was that they should stay in place. However, he acknowledged that the previous
11   public works director may have interpreted the ordinance differently. He requested Council
12   direction on whether to consider revising or repealing the ordinance and recommended that, if
13   changes are pursued, a clear policy be developed to determine when and where speed bumps
14   could be installed.
15
16   Councilmember McDougal asked for clarification on what research shows about the
17   effectiveness and impacts of speed bumps, noting conflicting claims about their effects on
18   emergency vehicles and overall safety. In response, Cary Necaise, Public Works Director, said
19   that research exists on both sides but stated that no surrounding cities currently allow
20   permanent speed bumps. He explained that other cities avoid them due to issues such as noise
21   complaints, slower emergency response times, snowplow damage, and wear on vehicles.
22   Residents living nearby also tend to dislike them because of the noise from vehicles driving over
23   them.
24
25   Fire Marshal Watkins explained that speed bumps perform their intended function by slowing
26   emergency vehicles down, which can delay response times. While speed bumps can cause
27   vehicle damage depending on the rate of speed, trained engineers know how to minimize that
28   risk.
29
30   Councilmember McDougal explained that removing existing, grandfathered speed bumps would
31   likely upset residents who rely on them, but he was not convinced that adding new ones would
32   effectively solve speeding problems.
33
34   Councilmember McCay expressed a desire for more comprehensive data on the effectiveness
35   of speed bumps. She suggested that the Transportation Committee could study this further,
36   including conducting speed tests before and after installation, and examining the impact of
37   removable bumps that are temporarily taken out during winter months.
38
39   Councilmember Haymond asked about the Lampton View speed dip, noting its V-shaped profile
40   and resident complaints that it caused noticeable jostling. Staff explained the dip had been
41   designed to follow the existing roadway and storm-drain geometry.
42
43   Councilmember Pierucci expressed support for reintroducing speed bumps as a tool for traffic
44   calming. He emphasized that while speed bumps aren’t a universal solution, they could
45   address certain speeding issues effectively. He advocated removing the ordinance language
46   that restricts speed bumps so the City could use them where appropriate, with a formalized
47   process and community input.
48
49   Councilmember Buroker noted that the original speed bump ordinance was well-intentioned and
50   based on research regarding vehicle safety, but acknowledged that removing existing speed
51   bumps caused long-term dissatisfaction in neighborhoods that had them. She emphasized the
     Page | 3 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1   need to revisit the policy and explore alternatives, specifically highlighting a solution used in Salt
 2   Lake City, speed bumps with cutouts designed to allow fire truck axles to pass without slowing,
 3   while still calming general traffic. She recommended considering this option as part of future
 4   discussions.
 5
 6   Mayor Staggs reflected on his previous support for removing speed bumps in 2018 but
 7   acknowledged that having the option available in the “toolkit” for traffic calming, as stated by
 8   Councilmember Pierucci, is valuable. He expressed support for revisiting the ordinance to allow
 9   speed bumps through a formal process and recommended preparing a budget to review the
10   inventory of removed speed bumps and estimate costs to reinstall them, specifically noting
11   Lampton View and Shaggy Peak Drive as priorities.
12
13            UDOT Triangle Piece
14
15   Mr. Hicks clarified that the “triangle piece” of land is now officially in the City’s name but is
16   physically within Herriman City’s boundary. In discussions with Herriman, they were comfortable
17   with the land being used as open space or park space, as long as it wasn’t developed for other
18   purposes. The Council’s intention has generally been to extend Mountain View Park onto this
19   parcel. He also mentioned past considerations for a BMX or mountain bike-style park, though
20   the current zoning designation in Herriman needs to be confirmed.
21
22   Councilmember Pierucci shared enthusiasm for developing a BMX park on the triangle parcel.
23   He noted that the timing aligns well with broader discussions about skate parks and similar
24   recreational facilities.

 1   Mayor and Council Reports
 2
 3   Councilmember McDougal announced that, as of October 1, the Trans-Jordan facility on the
 4   east side transfer station is now fully open and operational for all residents. He reminded
 5   residents that fees at this location are slightly higher than at the Herriman facility due to the
 6   additional compaction and transport costs.
 7
 8   Councilmember McCay said she attended the VW Car Show and the animal shelter ribbon
 9   cutting. She also attended the Wasatch Front Regional Council meeting to discuss a long-term
10   plan for connecting the Daybreak TRAX station to Draper, as well as several TRCC meetings
11   and the Jordan River Commission’s wrap-up of the “Get To the River” event.
12
13   Councilmember Buroker said she attended the Jordan River Commission meeting because the
14   discussion focused on homelessness and safety along the trail. She highlighted a new initiative
15   to install uniform signage at quarter-mile intervals along the trail, which would help emergency
16   responders locate incidents more quickly.
17
18   Councilmember Pierucci reported that last week he led a panel discussion with leaders from
19   across the country on the use of AI in local government. He noted there was significant interest
20   in Riverton’s approach and suggested that other city attorneys might reach out to Mr. Carter for
21   more information. He emphasized that the discussion provided ideas for improving efficiencies,
22   reducing costs, and making government more responsive to residents.
23
24   Councilmember Haymond thanked UDOT for quickly completing the overpasses on Bangerter
25   Highway. He noted that residents throughout Riverton greatly appreciate having the overpasses
26   reopened.
27
28   Mayor Staggs provided updates on City projects, noting that full lanes on Bangerter Highway
29   are expected by October 24, improving East-West traffic flow. Trail development continues, with
30   a segment opening between 12600 South and 13200 South. Progress on the development
31   across from City Hall was highlighted, including City-contributed property and a detention pond,
32   with the project expected to generate about $100,000 in City sales tax, $42,000 in property tax
33   for police and fire, and additional tax increment revenue over 25 years.
