              SUMMARY:      Bryon Prince, Ivory Development, is requesting a General Plan map amendment for the
                            property located at 4202 S. Riverboat Road from Business Park to Medium-Density
                            Mixed Use.
                            The Taylorsville General Plan is intended to guide land use and development decisions
                            that address the present and future needs of the City and to accomplish coordinated,
                            efficient, and harmonious growth and development within the City. The City Council, at
                            their discretion, may make revisions to the General Plan that it considers appropriate and
                            that are in the best interest of the City and its residents (per Section 13.06.030: General
                            Plan Adoption and Amendments).
                            Page 3-6 of the Taylorsville General Plan states the following, “All amendments to the
                            Proposed Land Use Map should be required to consider and not be in conflict with the
                            following principles:
                                • Promote economic sustainability
                                • Promote efficient use of land and public infrastructure
                                • Provide community amenities and benefits
                                • Protect environmentally sensitive lands
                                • Support alternate modes of transportation including pedestrian
                                • Create safe attractive neighborhoods and protect residential quality of life
                                • Minimize non-compatible adjacent land uses
                            The applicant is seeking a General Plan map amendment with the intention to develop
                            the property with approximately 142 townhome units, a playground, multi-purpose field,
                            and pedestrian enhancements to the Jordan River Parkway trail adjacent to the project.
                            The applicant’s original proposal included two commercial buildings each with a 20,000
                            square foot footprint and a fitness/daycare center; however, the applicant has eliminated
                            the nonresidential components from the concept development plan. Exhibit E includes
                            the most recent development concept illustrating proposed development if this General




                                                 1
                              Plan map amendment and a subsequent rezone request were approved by the City
                              Council.
                              The current zoning designation of the subject property is Industrial (ID). The applicant
                              submitted two separate Zoning Map amendment applications (File #8Z20 and #13Z20).
                              The first (File #8Z20) requests to change the zoning to Mixed-Use (MU). A negative
                              recommendation was forwarded to the City Council by the Planning Commission on
                              September 8, 2020 (see Exhibit I). The Planning Commission encouraged the applicant
                              to work with city staff to submit an SSD proposal in lieu of using the Mixed-Use zoning
                              designation.
                              A new application was filed on December 8, 2020 (File #13Z20) requesting a rezone to
                              the Site-Specific Development (SSD) zoning district. This item has not been scheduled
                              for consideration by the Planning Commission. Instead, the applicant has requested that
                              the General Plan map amendment be considered by the City Council prior to scheduling
                              a hearing for the SSD request in order to weigh the merits of the larger development
                              concept.

     STAFF RECOMMENDATION:    Staff recommends denial of Ordinance No. 21-03 based on the following findings:
                                    •   The Planning Commission forwarded a negative recommendation on
                                        September 8, 2020 based on the findings of the staff report (Exhibit I)
                                    •   The proposed concept development plan (Exhibit E) does not include a
                                        nonresidential component; therefore, the “Medium-Density Mixed-Use”
                                        General Plan map designation is not appropriate;
                                    •   The proposed development plan (Exhibit E) includes a single vehicle access
                                        that poses challenges regarding resident safety; and
                                    •   The proposed development plan (Exhibit E) would disturb environmentally
                                        sensitive lands. The subject property is in “high liquefaction potential” area,
                                        FEMA 100-year flood hazard zone, FEMA regulatory floodway, and a
                                        Sensitive Area Overlay zone.

       WHEREAS, the Taylorsville City Council (the “Council”) met in regular session on April
21, 2021, to consider, among other things, approving a General Plan map amendment from
Business Park to Medium-Density Mixed Use for 24.30 acres of property at 4202 S. Riverboat
Road; and

        WHEREAS, pursuant to TAYLORSVILLE MUNICIPAL CODE Section 13.35.030, notice of a
public hearing before the City’s Planning Commission was published in the Salt Lake Tribune and
Deseret News on May 2, 2020; and

       WHEREAS, the notice of a public hearing before the City’s Planning Commission was
also published on the Utah Public Notice Website on May 2, 2020; and

       WHEREAS, the City’s Planning Commission held a properly noticed public hearing on
the proposed amendments on May 12, 2020; and

      WHEREAS, the notice of a second public hearing before the City’s Planning Commission
was published on the Utah Public Notice Website on August 28, 2020; and

       WHEREAS the notice of a second public hearing before the City’s Planning Commission
was also published in the Salt Lake Tribune and Deseret News on August 28, 2020; and

        WHEREAS, the City’s Planning Commission held a second properly noticed public
hearing on the proposed amendments on September 8, 2020, during which the Planning
Commission voted 7-0 to forward a negative recommendation to the City Council regarding this
matter; and

        WHEREAS, although not required by City ordinances, the City Council heard courtesy
public comment on the proposed amendments during its regularly scheduled meeting on April 21,
2021; and

        WHEREAS, after careful consideration, the Council has determined that it is in the best
interest of the health, safety, and welfare of the citizens of Taylorsville to approve the proposed
amendments.

       NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED by the Taylorsville City Council that the
proposed General Plan map amendments are hereby approved. The amendments are articulated in
Exhibit “A.”

        from “Business Park” to “Medium-Density Mixed-Use.” The applicant is seeking this General
        Plan Map amendment to allow for the development of 142 townhome units, two
        commercial buildings each with a 20,000 square foot footprint, a fitness/daycare center, a
        playground, and a multi-purpose field. A conceptual site plan illustrating the proposal is
        included as an exhibit in this report.

        Site Description
        The subject property is 24.30 acres and is currently undeveloped. There are several
        constraints impacting the potential future development of this property. A primary area of
        concern for Taylorsville City and Unified Fire Authority is limited vehicle and pedestrian
        access. The only vehicular access to the subject property is via Riverboat Road that
        terminates at the southern edge of the property. There are few opportunities to improve
        connectivity to the site because the property is bounded by the Jordan River to the north
        and east and a Salt Lake County-owned golf course to the west. The subject property has
        one means of ingress and egress via Riverboat Road posing a significant public safety
        concern. Pedestrians may access the property via Riverboat Road or the Jordan River
        Parkway. Prior to authorizing future development for this property, the City requires that a
        second means of ingress/egress be provided, which has been communicated to the
        applicant.

        The property is within in a “high potential” liquefaction zone and a FEMA 100-year flood
        zone. Portions of the property immediately adjacent to the Jordan River are included in the
        FEMA regulatory floodway. High-power transmission lines and an associated easement also
        traverse the length of the parcel along the western edge. Surrounding zoning designations
        are as follows:

            North               South                  East                   West
            Murray City         RM-12                  Murray City            Open Space (OS) and
                                                                              Research and
                                                                              Development (RD)

        General Plan Analysis
        The City has adopted a comprehensive, long range General Plan for the present and future
        needs of the City and for the general purpose of guiding and accomplishing coordinated,
        efficient, and harmonious growth and development of all or any part of the land within the
        City. The City Council may make revisions to the General Plan that it considers appropriate.

        This request is to specifically amend the Proposed Land Use Map in the General Plan, which
        sets the foundation for all land uses within the City of Taylorsville. The land use plan
        identifies general land use classifications and in some cases the intensity within the
        classification. Each land use classification on the Proposed Land Use Map corresponds with
        compatible classifications on the official Taylorsville Zoning Map.

        The subject property is designated as “Business Park” on the Proposed Land Use Map. The
        General Plan includes the following statement describing the intent of the Business Park
        designation (pg 3-4):

               “The Business Park (BP) designation differs from Professional Office (PO) in
               that traditional industrial uses are permitted, often in “hybrid’ space for
               companies and organizations that do not fall neatly into one category of use.
               Uses may include headquarters management, research laboratories,
               prototype development, assembly and fabrication, light-manufacturing, and
               warehouse distribution facilities.”

        The applicant’s proposal for a mixed-use development including commercial and residential
        land uses is not consistent with the adopted policies and goals in the City’s General Plan for
        this property. The General Plan includes the following statement describing the intent of the
        Mixed-Use designation (pg 3-5):

               “Mixed use zones can contain a mix of land uses, such as residential,
               commercial and office within the same area. Land uses in mixed use zones
               may be varied either vertically (i.e. mix of uses in one building such as an
               apartment over a retail establishment) or horizontally (i.e. single use
               buildings with different uses placed next to each other). The Medium
               Density Mixed Use allows a residential density of up to 8.9 units per acre in a
               mixed use setting.”

        The subject property is within one of several designated master plan study areas currently
        underway as part of a larger General Plan update effort. The City has contracted with KGRW

        approved until update efforts are completed and a clear strategy is identified and adopted
        by the City Council.

        Goal 3-1: Achieve efficient use of land and public infrastructure and promote economic
