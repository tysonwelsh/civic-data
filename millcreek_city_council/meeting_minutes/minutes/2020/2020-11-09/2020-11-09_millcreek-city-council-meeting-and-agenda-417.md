2: House Bill 411 “Community Renewable Energy Act” Status Report; Mayor Silvestrini,
John Brems.

Mayor Silvestrini said that the House Bill 411 process is working its way through two
committees. The governance committee is designing a framework on how to make decisions
moving forward. It is a complex process based on the size of cities and their electrical loads.
Deference is being given to population, membership, and the size of the electrical load. Nothing
has been agreed upon yet said Mayor Silvestrini. The other participants include Salt Lake City,
Park City, Summit County, Castle Valley, Ogden, and a few other jurisdictions that have come in
and out. Mayor Silvestrini explained that one jurisdiction gets one vote, and there is a safety

Millcreek City Council Meeting Minutes 09 November 2020 Page 2 of 9

valve that two jurisdictions can call for a weighted vote based on population and electrical load.
This can change based on if jurisdictions drop out. There are three different time periods. The
first is organization and filing and application with the public service commission, during that
period one jurisdiction gets one vote. The voting control can change explained Mayor Silvestrini.
The formula will shift based on what kind of power is being bought, there will still be a function
of power and load. Mayor Silvestrini explained that changing the program will require a super
majority vote to effect change. Salt Lake City has some more ideas they will present soon. The
cost of the program is a big factor. It costs money to hire legal counsel to represent cities when
cities present to the public service commission. They have come up with a formula that charges
larger entities more and smaller entities less said Mayor Silvestrini. The cost to Millcreek would
be about $60,000, spread out over a few fiscal years. Samantha DeSeelhorst said that phasing
costs over time is more manageable. Ms. DeSeelhorst explained that the amount of participants
and electric load, and noticing costs create a cost of $37,000 for Millcreek. All customers need to
be noticed so they can decide. The noticing costs will be borne by the communities based on
their population. The total cost for Millcreek amounts to about $56,000 assuming current cost
sharing between municipalities remains the same. Mayor Silvestrini explained that smaller
entities have smaller costs, larger entities have larger costs. Mayor Silvestrini said the goal is for
this to be affordable and not price smaller communities out from the beginning. Mayor
Silvestrini said that given the prospects this is still worthwhile to stay in and find out the rest of
the costs, Millcreek would still have the option to leave the agreement. Ms. DeSeelhorst
explained the estimates are the high-end estimates, she hopes the fees will be lower. Mayor
Silvestrini said all jurisdictions involved are mindful of the costs and understand that if the cost
goes too high then cities will drop out.

4. Millcreek City Hall Project Presentation; Kyrene Gibb, Y2 Analytics

Rita Lund introduced Y2 Analytics. Ms. Gibb from Y2 Analytics explained that Y2 Analytics
conducted a survey for the City. Most respondents are aware the Millcreek City hall is a
convenient space. Furthermore, respondents support the City in finding a new space for City
Hall. Respondents voiced concern about a ““Taj-Mahal” type City Hall. Support for City Hall
increased after exposure to informative messages and is strong across all districts. Nearly 2/3 of
respondents support a mixed-use building for cost savings. Ms. Gibb gave a quick overview of
the survey methodology. 582 residents responded. Ms. Gibb said data was weighted to ensure
that the sample is representative of the City as a whole. The survey was administered between
October 10" and November 2"4, 2020. Y2 Analytics is confident that the sample is
representative. 84% of residents felt that the City is headed in the right direction, and 16% felt
that residents felt that the City is not. Ms. Gibb explained this statistically matches a survey taken

last year. 85% of respondents said they approved of the Mayor and City Council. 84% Millcreek
residents said that the City is headed in the right direction.

Ms. Gibb related that 29% of respondents said they had visited Millcreek City Hall, and 57%
said they were aware of the location of City Hall. 40% of residents said that City Hall is
conveniently located. Thinking about the future of City Hall, 72% of residents said that the City
should find a new space to own. Residents responded that their most important considerations for
a new City Hall are that the new City Hall must be a wise investment and that it should be
accessible to all residents, said Ms. Gibb. 39% of residents said it was important that City Hall be
a building the City owns. A landmark was not particularly valuable to residents, said Ms. Gibb.
Y2 Analytics asked how important it was to residents that City Hall be centrally located. District
1 and District 2 residents had strong opinions with 42% saying it is very important that City Hall
be located centrally.

Y2 asked residents top to create a hierarchy of considerations. 38% of respondents said that City
Hall should be a wise investment of tax dollars. Residents were asked about ownership of City
Hall. 55% of residents supported building a new City Hall that was owned. Residents were asked
what amenities they would like to see around City Hall. 56% of residents surveyed said they
would like to see parks and green space, 43% preferred community rooms. Ms. Gibb said that
residents were asked about the future of City Hall. 76% prefer a fiscally wise investment. A
future City Hall as a community resource was important to 60%. 45% of residents surveyed said
that it was extremely or very important to have a public-private membership to offset costs of
city hall. Ms. Gibb said it seemed that respondents were ambivalent towards the idea of a public-
private ownership.

Ms. Gibb stated that it is very clear that residents want to be involved with the new vision for
City Hall. 50% of residents in District 1, 39% District 2, 47% District 3, and 27% District 4
support building a new City Hall. Ms. Gibb said that the balance of support makes sense.
Residents were asked about what option they preferred for a new City Hall. 63% said they would
like to see a mixed-use building that would result in some cost savings, explained Ms. Gibb. Ms.
Gibb said there was a 50% male and 50% female split in respondents. 65% of respondents were
married, 91% were white or Caucasian. 39% said they had lived in the city for 21 or more years.
56% are employed full time. 42% of respondents were college graduates.

Ms. Gibb asked if the City Council had questions. Council Member Catten asked if the survey
was random and if residents who completed the survey in 2019 answered the survey in 2020. Ms.
Gibb said about 300 people had completed the survey in 2019. Ms. Gibb explained that the other

5. Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District Annual Report; Pam Roberts, WFWRD
General Manager/CEO
Pam Roberts, Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling General Manager explained that the board
met last Monday and tentatively adopted the 2021 budget. She said there are some fee increases
for green waste and trailer rental, she said it was very important to be transparent as possible. To
recap stats outcome with containers in driveway. WFWRD delivered 9,500 containers and
collected 8,500 tons, compared to 11,00 containers in 2019 and almost 11,000 tons collected in
2019. Ms. Roberts recommended to the board that WFWRD modified their container reservation
service for 2020 due to the pandemic. There were four days less of container reservation services
because WFWRD shares trucks and saved nearly $350,000 because of changes they made. They
had planned on having 21 drivers but averaged 12 drivers.

WEWRD wanted to hear from residents, said Ms. Roberts. They launched a survey in multiple
locations. Most people in the survey responded that they used the services. 542 Millcreek
residents responded out of 2,700 total respondents. Millcreek’s satisfaction was 79%. District
wide there were 2000 comments. 278 Millcreek residents left comments. 108 residents supported
the way the program worked, 36 said they did not support it. Respondents desired to have more
container availability. Residents that were on a waiting list to receive a container were contacted
when there was a cancellation. Many residents asked if they could have the container put in the
street rather than their driveway. Some residents did have minor scrapes and scratches on their
driveway from the containers. Council Member Marchant said the council is appreciative of the
desire to look for solutions. Mayor Silvestrini thanked WFWRD for providing help after the
windstorm in September. Council Member Marchant commented that the city has received better
service for less cost. Mayor Silvestrini said that he heard concerns from residents about waste
trailers damaging driveways, he said that WFWRD did a great job of explaining that the trailers
only weigh as much as a car. Council Member Uipi thanked Pam for their beneficial services
brought to residents. Messaging was important, especially after waste management told
customers the containers weighed the similar amount to a full-size pickup truck. Rita Lund,
Communication Director, said that the leaf collection program is immensely popular among
Millcreek residents, she said it is a great service provided by WFWRD.

2.1 Discussion and Consideration of Millcreek State of Local Emergency Directive No. 6

dated November 9, 2020.

Mayor Silvestrini explained that Gov Herbert signed an executive order stating that Utah is in a
state of emergency and a second executive order for a mask mandate. Governor Herbert strongly
recommended that people not have social contact outside of their household group. Mayor
Silvestrini explained that the idea behind this is if the State does this for two weeks then the State
of Utah can get on top of COVID-19 infections. Because of this, Mayor Silvestrini said that he
implemented more stringent measures at City Hall shall be done by appointment only. All
department heads have given plans to direct who will work from City Hall and who will work
from home. Otherwise, staff will be working remotely. Millcreek City employees have been
granted an exception to idling rules because of the cold weather when they work in their cars.
City Hall will remain open to the public by appointment only. Mayor Silvestrini said that this
directive is to set a good example.

Council Member Jackson moved to ratify item 2.1, Millcreek State of Local Emergency
Directive No. 6. Council Member Uipi seconded. The Deputy Recorder called for the vote.
All Council Members voted yes. The motion passed unanimously.
