49   3.1) Public hearing and recommendation of Cole Peck's request for review of the Lakeview
50   Land and Rock General Plan amendment on 12.81-acres of property located at
51   approximately 600 East 600 South, changing the land use designation from LDR (low density
52   residential) to Heavy Commercial and Commercial.
53   Commissioner Jackson reviewed instructions for decorum and public comment.
54
55   Katie Bussell presented information on the agenda item.
56
57   The applicant, Tony Trane, was present. He stated the development agreement was in place so
58   they could start planning in detail for the site concept. At this point, the request is to give
59   recommendations of what needs to be in the development agreement and to make the building
60   look like a barn-type building. Mr. Trane clarified he will make feasible changes from an
61   engineering standpoint.
62
63   Commission Chair Jackson opened the public hearing.
64
65   Keith Southard questioned whether Peck's property belongs to the county and how the city can
66   legally make decisions regarding it. He also wanted to know where the tax money from these
67   contracts would go. Additionally, Southard expressed worries about the extension of 570 South,
68   especially regarding traffic and businesses. He suggested considering safety by routing 570
69   South through the county instead.
70
71   During the meeting, Alexa Jarrett expressed her worries about the proposed route of 570 South,
72   which would pass through a neighborhood and cross a school boundary where children walk to
73   school. She pointed out that the county lists the area as agricultural land. When she and her
74   family moved into their home, they checked the General Plan Amendment (GPA) and saw that it
75   had to be compatible with the zoning regulations. She mentioned that the Development Review
76   Committee (DRC) initially opposed the recommendation, and she believes more residents would
77   be affected by the change. Lastly, she mentioned that the Peck family feels they are not
78   complying with the law.
79
80   Liz Anderson mentioned that several properties nearby had ongoing heavy equipment
81   construction, which affected their property. She expressed her support for the proposed zone
82   change. Additionally, she noted that the road was originally intended to have a dead-end. Liz
83   pointed out that the Peck property existed before the surrounding properties were developed. She
84   emphasized the importance of property rights and stated her preference for maintaining the
85   current level of development rather than seeing more homes built, as she has grown accustomed
86   to the presence of heavy equipment in the area.
87
88   Becca Fordham shared that she and her family purchased their home 5.5 years ago and enjoy
89   living in the industrial area. She mentioned that the truck drivers using the narrow road near their
90   home have been respectful and haven't caused any issues, as they are mindful of the limited
91   space and slow down. However, she expressed concern about other drivers who need to drive
     Lehi City Planning Commission                    2                                   Macrch 14, 2024

 97   number of commercial developments. He mentioned a preference for more residential
 98   development. Skylar's house is situated near the property line, and he is worried about potential

126   what they need to do and take what they are planning over what is there now.

128   During the meeting, Cole Peck clarified that their previous request for light industrial zoning was
129   mainly due to the necessity for outside storage, as it was the only zone permitting it. He believes
130   that Lehi is deviating from its focus on light industrial development and disagrees with the staff's
131   assessment on this matter. When Tony Peck bought the property, it was specifically for the yard.
132   Cole acknowledges concerns about safety but questions the notion that one neighborhood should
133   be prioritized over another in terms of safety measures. While he would prefer not to have 570
134   South connected, he recognizes the potential benefit in rerouting in case of a train accident.
135   Ultimately, he seeks to operate his business and is currently in the process of annexing into Lehi

202   Tony Trane stated the intent to get the agreement where it needs to be to submit the annexation it
203   needs to be submitted before the Planning Commission before it can be submitted to the County
204   for annexation. He stated they need direction for the General Plan for the development
205   agreement.
206
207   The road must be connected to adhere to block length requirements; it's essential for
208   undeveloped properties to have connectivity. According to the code, connecting roads provide
209   crucial access for traffic and emergency services. Moreover, connectivity standards necessitate
210   this connection. Currently, the Peck property is isolated within the county. Future plans designate
211   the area for Low-Density Residential (LDR) zoning within the city limits, requiring annexation.
212   To determine the annexation's specifics, the city examines the General Plan. This is why they're
213   seeking a change. Additionally, he discussed the impact of the annexation on neighboring
214   properties.
215
216   Tony Trane stated while they were going through the process, the City advised to hold off while
217   they were adopting some processes. They want to get involved and get it finished, they just want
218   to make sure it is going in the right direction, there has just been a time delay.
219
220   The Commissioners want to make it clear they respect the applicant's property rights. They also
221   want the applicant to understand others in the community who surround the area. Zoning plays a
222   lot in the development. The two issues are the right to petition to zone, the other issue is to
223   preserve what the other property owners have considered.
