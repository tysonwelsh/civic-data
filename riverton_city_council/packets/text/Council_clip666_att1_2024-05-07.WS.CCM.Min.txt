36          Southwest Aqueduct Reach 2 Project Team Presentation
37
38   Kevin Rubow, from Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District presented details about the
39   Southwest Aqueduct project. The project involves installing approximately two miles of 66-inch
40   diameter welded steel pipeline, running from 13400 South to 11800 South. The new aqueduct
41   aims to meet the increasing water demands of the communities in the Salt Lake Valley. This
42   presentation is included in the packet.
43
44   Christina Nelson, Bowen Collins Associates, and Brittany Kidd, Horrocks Engineering, provided
45   examples of typical aqueduct easements, showcasing how the pipeline will be laid down along
46   3200 West. In some areas, the pipeline will be in the public right-of-way, potentially requiring
47   minor disturbances to curb, gutter, and sidewalk.
48
49   Councilmember McDougal asked if permanent structures affected by the construction would be
50   restored. Ms. Nelson said that only allowed uses will be replaced such as sod and gardens.

17            Budget Review and Establish Time Line for Budget Process
18
19   Mayor Staggs provided an overview of the proposed budget. He highlighted several key points,
20   including the historical analysis of the general fund over the past decade, which showed an
21   average annual increase of around 6% or 4% when excluding stormwater. The proposed budget
22   includes funding for additional personnel, such as police officers, code enforcement, animal
23   control, and other positions.
24
25   Mayor Staggs also addressed the topic of recycling, proposing to eliminate curbside recycling in
26   favor of regionalized recycling efforts to reduce contamination rates. He emphasized the
27   importance of maintaining financial prudence and highlighted various projects included in the
28   budget, such as the culinary water Greenwell and improvements to secondary water systems.
29
30   Mayor Staggs clarified that adopting the tentative budget does not represent final approval, as
31   the Council will finalize the budget by the second meeting in June as required by state law.
32
33   Councilmember McDougal provided an update on recent developments from the Trans Jordan
34   board meeting regarding potential fee increases. He explained that due to higher-than-
35   anticipated construction costs and inflationary pressures, there may be a need for a mid-year
36   fee increase. Trans Jordan charges the City a mass rate per ton for waste disposal, with the
37   City's trucks being weighed and tonnage assigned accordingly. The Council must decide
38   whether to cover overages through the general fund or pass the cost along to residents on a per
39   capita basis.
40
41   Councilmember McDougal also emphasized the importance of regional recycling efforts, noting
42   that such initiatives could improve recycling effectiveness by reducing contamination rates.
43
44   Councilmember Haymond suggested addressing the potential subsidy of waste management
45   services sooner rather than later to avoid a situation where a large fee increase becomes
46   necessary in the future, similar to what happened with sewer fees. He recommended
47   considering smaller adjustments or regional solutions to address the issue proactively.
48
49   Councilmember Pierucci expressed agreement with the Mayor's perspective on budgeting and
50   fee increases, highlighting his reluctance to raise rates for residents when there are sufficient
51   funds available in other areas. He views it as a matter of reallocating public funds rather than
     Page | 3 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1   additional burden on residents. Additionally, he inquired about whether the budget accounts for
 2   the rate increase from the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District, ensuring that it is
 3   appropriately reflected in the budget.
 4
 5   Councilmember McCay expressed frustration with the varying levels of commitment to recycling
 6   among residents and suggested exploring options to address this issue. She proposed ideas
 7   such as making residents request recycling bins and agreeing to abide by recycling guidelines,
 8   and possibly charging a higher fee for those bins. She also raised concerns about the
 9   decreasing cushion in the general fund and suggested considering a small fee increase to cover
10   costs.
11
12   Mr. Hicks said the current contract with Waste Management involves a sharing arrangement,
13   although the specifics are not readily available. Implementing an opt-in solution for recycling
14   would likely require renegotiating the contract with the hauler and possibly even going out to bid
15   for the option. Additionally, regarding the general fund percentage, it's expected to be at 27% by
16   the end of fiscal year 2025, factoring in proposed positions and other considerations.
17
18            Public Comment Discussion
19
20   Councilmember McCay said she had some concern about disruptive behavior in Council
21   meetings, but also a desire to balance the right to free speech with maintaining order and
22   productivity. She believes that continuing the discussion and exploring ways to address
23   disruptive behavior while still allowing for public input could be beneficial.
24
25   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, addressed the issue of managing disruptions in meetings,
26   emphasizing the importance of clarity and adherence to existing policies and procedures. He
27   noted that Utah law provides a procedure for ejecting individuals from meetings for disorderly
28   behavior, expressing support for lobbying the legislature for improved procedures. He
29   highlighted the need to focus discussions on topics within the City's jurisdiction and suggested
30   cutting off individuals who veer off-topic.
31
32   Councilmember Pierucci suggested that a time limit could help manage disruptions by providing
33   a clear endpoint to public comment sessions. He inquired about the feasibility of incorporating
34   such a policy within existing Utah statutes and policies.
35
36   Mr. Carter clarified that the Council operates within the framework of a limited public forum,
37   allowing regulations on time, place, and manner during public comment sessions. He explained
38   that time regulations, such as setting a time limit for each speaker and an overall cap for the
39   public comment session, are permissible as long as they apply uniformly to all speakers. He
40   proposed reducing the current three-minute time limit to two minutes and implementing a 15-
41   minute cap for the entire public comment session. He emphasized that this approach would
42   operate on a first-come, first-served basis, with speakers who do not have the opportunity to
43   speak in one meeting given priority in the next.

 4            Ordinance No. 24-06 – Amendment to Sections 18.215.030(2) and 18.135.300,
 5            updating standards and requirements related to exterior commercial lighting
 6
 7   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, provided an update on the proposed
 8   changes to the City's ordinance regarding exterior commercial lighting. He explained that the
 9   Planning Commission had recommended several amendments to address deficiencies and
10   changes in the City. The key changes focused on defining fully shielded fixtures, regulating
11   lighting oriented towards residential properties, and addressing commercial holiday lighting. The
12   ordinance specified that exterior lighting on buildings facing residential areas must be turned off
13   at 11 pm or at the close of business, whichever is earlier. Temporary holiday lighting during
14   November to January would also require site plan approval and must be turned off by 11 pm or
15   at the close of business.
16
17   Mr. Lethbridge clarified measurement standards for residential properties within 100 feet of
18   commercial buildings and discussed the proposed holiday lighting regulations, including
19   designated holidays and time limits. He emphasized that while the ordinance included
20   recommendations from the Planning Commission, additional changes could be made based on
21   Council input.
22
23   In response to clarifying questions by Councilmember McCay, Mr. Lethbridge noted that most of
24   the current lighting in the City already meets the proposed standards, which are based on
25   industry norms. Input was gathered from cities with similar standards and ongoing projects to
26   ensure practicality and accessibility of compliant lighting options.
27
28   Councilmember McDougal suggested adjusting the timing requirement to 10 o'clock, aligning it
29   with existing noise ordinances and other lighting regulations. Councilmember Pierucci was not
30   supportive of this change.
31
32   Mayor Staggs called for public comments;
33
34   Heather Bennett explained that she was supportive of aligning the timing of exterior lighting with
35   noise regulations, specifically for the side of buildings facing residential properties. The intent is
36   to address concerns related to noise and ensure a peaceful environment for residents, without
37   impacting the operations of businesses.
38
39   Mayor Staggs called for additional public comments; there being none, he closed the Public
40   Hearing.
41
42   Councilmember McDougal MOVED that the City Council adopt Ordinance No. 24-06 –
43   Amendment to Sections 18.215.030(2) and 18.135.300, updating standards and
44   requirements related to exterior commercial lighting with modification in Exhibit A,
45   Section 3 to be 10:00 PM. Councilmember Haymond SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs
46   called for discussion on the motion. Mayor Staggs called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as
47   follows: Haymond-yes, McCay-yes, McDougal-yes, and Pierucci-no. The motion passed with a
48   3 to 1 vote.
