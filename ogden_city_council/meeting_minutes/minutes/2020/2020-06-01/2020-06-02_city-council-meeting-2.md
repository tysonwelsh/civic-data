         Proposed Resolution 2020-13 scheduling a public hearing to allow public
         input regarding proposed transfer from the City's Utility Enterprise Fund to
         the General Fund
                   A memo from Council staff came before the Council to consider a resolution approving enterprise fund transfers. The
         memo stated as required by Utah State Code §10-6-135.5, a city that is proposing to transfer funds from an enterprise fund to
         another fund for a purpose not directly related to the goods or services provided by the enterprise fund must provide notice of the
         transfers to the users of the enterprise fund. The City must also hold a public hearing on the transfers prior to adoption or the
         amendment of the City's budget. In Ogden, a budgetary transfer from the City's Water, Sanitary Sewer, Storm Sewer, and Refuse
         Utility Enterprise Funds to the General Fund has been done for many years; however, the requirement to provide notice of the
         transfers and to hold a separate public hearing is a recent change to state law. These utility funds are accounted for as enterprise
         funds in Ogden and are operated with user fees charged for goods and services. In the proposed FY2 l budget, the City is
         proposing a transfer from these utility funds into the City's General Fund to act as the collection of franchise and property tax. To
         comply with state law requirements, a public hearing on the proposed transfers is scheduled for June 23, 2020.
                   Council Deputy Director Symes summarized the memo and showed a presentation explaining the proposed utility fund
         transfers. He reviewed the notification that has been provided to Ogden City utility customers regarding the proposed transfers and
         noted the proposed transfer for the FY2 l budget is $5 .4 million. If the transfer is not made, utilities rates may be lowered;
         however, property taxes would have to be raised to remain fiscally solvent. Anticipated property tax revenue in FY2 l is
         approximately $15. 7 million. If these utilities were independent, the City would receive revenue from the collection of franchise
         and property taxes. In previous years - from 1996 to 2016 - the City considered transfers from utility funds for public art. That
         method was a collection of an additional one percent of capital project costs, but that method was changed to a transfer based upon
         historical averages. Public arts are no longer funded from utility funds. The City Council is required to hold a public hearing to
         receive input regarding the proposed transfers. No action will be taken tonight, instead, approval or denial of the transfers will
         occur with adoption of the fmal FY2 l budget. The City will send a follow up notice to utility customers once the fmal budget is
         adopted.
                   Comptroller Stout presented a spreadsheet detailing the amount of the transfer from each utility fund to the General Fund;
         transfers account for six percent franchise and six percent property taxes from each fund, for a total transfer of$5,402,275.
                   Council member White asked who will author the notice ofutility transfers that is sent to utility customers. Mr. Symes
         answered he collaborates with the City Recorder and City Attorney but is ultimately responsible for the notice.
                   Chair Choberka asked if a physical notice is mailed to all utility customers, even if some have opted for electronic

Proposed Zone Text Amendments pertaining to Drinking
 Establishment Definitions
          Council Deputy Director Symes explained a petition has been filed by Martin McFadden with Blue Ox Development to
amend the defmitions related to drinking establishments as well as the regulations for drinking establishments in the Business
Exchange Historical (BEH) zone. While these requests are closely connected, the two requests will be separated into different
items for Council consideration. The proposed amendments with this item would eliminate the defmitions for tavern and private
club and replace them with a new defmition for a drinking establishment. The proposed defmition for drinking establishment in
the City's code would cover the different types of alcohol-serving establishments defined in state code and regulated in the City's
code. The proposed defmition for drinking establishments would also clarify that for the purpose of the City's zoning and number
of drinking establishments per linear block, alcohol sales must exceed 50% of total annual receipts. A similar proposal to update
the code related to the defmition of taverns and private clubs came before the Council in 2016; however, no fmal action was taken
on that proposal. The 2016 amendments came in conjunction with a petition to amend the defmition of a linear block.

          Planning Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the petition and the Planning
Commission's recommendation. The proposed amendments are indirectly tied to petitions 2019-24 and 2015-9 as well as State
law changes; current City ordinances need to be updated to reflect State liquor laws; additionally, the proposed ordinance
amendment would acc01mnodate proposed use for Ogden's own liquor-only bar with no beer sales. Utah State laws provide
definitions for restaurants and bars; restaurants must have receipts for 70 percent food sales and three types ofrestaurants can
serve alcohol: limited service, beer only, and full-service businesses. Bars and taverns are different; taverns are beer-only bars.
Bars must have food available, but patrons are not required to order food to purchase alcohol.
          Vice Chair Blair asked if the City's ordinance conformed with State Code when it was initially adopted. Mr.
Montgomery stated the ordinance was initially in accordance with the State, originating back to 1970; both sets ofregulations
have changed since that time. Deputy City Attorney Stratford stated that it is important to understand that definitions used by the
City are used for a different reasons than the State's definitions; State definitions are used to detennine the type of State license a
business must obtain while City definitions are used for zoning purposes. In the past the two definitions correlated with one
another, which is fine, but it is not necessary for the two definitions to be the same in perpetuity.
          Mr. Montgomery then noted the current City Ordinance classified businesses with more than 50 percent beer only sales
as taverns and private clubs, while restaurants have more than 50 percent food sales. The petitioner is proposing to operate a liquor
tasting room/bar only with no beer sales and that type of use does not fit within either of the City's ordinance definitions. The
proposed wording changes in the ordinance include:
              •    Replace tavern and private club with Drinking Establishment;
              • Revise definitions;
              •     City's definition follows present city standards of 50 percent rather than State's of70 percent; and
               •    Revise wording in land use sections.
         Additionally, the defmition ofrestaurant will be changed to clarify Ogden's distinction from the State's. He summarized
the effects of the ordinance amendment proposal; the City ordinance would create a niche by allowing 'adult only' restaurants.
Restaurants that serve between 50 percent and 70 percent food sales are classified by State law as bars, not restaurants, and do not
allow minors. The regulation limiting the use to two per linear block applies only to 'drinking establishments' which sell more
than 50 percent alcoholic beverages per City ordinances. The ordinance proposal does not change development standards that the
downtown has developed under for the past 20 years. He concluded the Planning Commission voted unanimously to recommend
approval of updating City ordinances to adopt 'drinking establishments' as a replacement for 'taverns' and 'private clubs'.
         Mr. Symes indicated that if the Council is comfortable proceeding, an action item will be included on the June 23,
2020 meeting. There were no objections voiced by the Council.

Proposed General Plan Amendments pertaining to the Airport
         Council Deputy Director Symes explained the proposed amendments to the City's General Plan are the related to the
recent adoption of the 2020 Ogden-Hinckley Airport Master Plan. The master plan, adopted May 26, 2020, provided direction for
the airport's future development. As part of the master plan development, administrative staff identified areas of the City's
General Plan that needed to be amended to reflect updated information as well as the recommendations made in the master plan.
         Planning Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the proposed amendments. The
General Plan was developed in 2000-200 I; it creates objectives and strategies to guide future actions, but is amended as changes
take place in the City. The 2020 Master Plan updating the vision for the Airport has spurred consideration of a two-part plan
amendment; first is to update information in the Plan to be more relevant and the second is to update the Plan to reflect amended
background and strategies for the Airport. New strategies are being added to the plan and two existing strategies are being
amended. Discussion of these amendments has led to questions about the architectural character oflate 1940's art modem tower at
the Airport as a unique design feature. Adjustments in the Plan do not impact the tower's use, but protects the building's character.
Planning Commission consideration of the proposed amendments include consistency, meeting Airport Master Plan objectives,
and building/directing the future direction of the Airport. Planning Commission voted unanimously to recommend approval of the
General Plan amendments based on revisions being consistent with the General Plan and adopted Master Plan.
         Council member Stephens asked if these amendments address the need to develop a joint plan with cities and property
owners surrounding the Airport. Mr. Montgomery stated the Plan does call for a coordination of future transition of land uses
around the Airport to uses that are better situated as being located adjacent to the airfield. This relates most closely to properties in
Roy City. Community and Economic Development Director Christopulos noted that the Mayor and Administrative Staff at Roy
were very involved in development of the Airport Plan and these adjustments to the General Plan; they understand the value of the
property surrounding the Airport and that it is potentially a catalyst for future economic development in their city.
