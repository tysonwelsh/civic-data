Proposed Ordinance 2022-10 adopting salary schedules for sworn fire
employees, merit employees, and non-merit special employees of the City;
AND

Proposed Ordinance 2022-11 amending the budget in the amount of
$765,675 for an increase in salaries and a cost-of-living adjustment

A memo from the Management Services Department regarding proposed adjustments to employee salaries and cost-of-
living adjustments came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the Council will consider an amendment to the
Fiscal Year (FY) 2022 Salary Schedules for all sworn fire, merit, and non-merit employees to provide a two percent cost-of-living
increase for all steps within the respective ranges. The Administration is also proposing to implement Phase 3 of the Evergreen
Classification, Compensation, and Benefit Study for the remaining employees which will move employees to the correct step
within their respective ranges. The effective date of the new salary schedules would be the pay period beginning April 2, 2022. An
amendment to the FY2022 Budget to cover costs associated with these increases will be considered in a separate action.

An additional memo from the Management Services Department regarding a proposed FY 2022 Budget amendment
pertaining to employee salaries came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the Administration is proposing an
amendment to the FY 2022 budget to fund a two percent salary increase for sworn fire and general employees. This also includes
non-sworn police employees. An increase for sworn police officers was approved by the City Council in October 2021. This
increase is proposed as part of the implementation of Phase 3 of the Evergreen Classification, Compensation and Benefits Study.
The total impact on the FY 2022 budget is $765,675 with $481,625 in General Fund revenues coming as an adjustment to

increased sales tax received. The remaining funds are proposed to come from existing funds in the associated enterprise or internal
service funds. If approved as proposed, the increases would take effect with the pay period beginning April 2, 2022 and fund
increases through the end of the fiscal year.

Human Resources Manager Judkins summarized the memo and stated these proposals would implement Phase 3 of the
Classification, Compensation and Benefits Study and would implement full range penetration for sworn fire, merit employees,
non-merit special employees which includes the Mayor and City Council. It should be noted that non-sworn police employees are
also included in the proposal.

Senior Accountant Sorensen then reported the total funding needed for the proposed increase between April and the end
of the fiscal year is $765,675. The annual ongoing increase to the budget for these changes is estimated at $3,062,700. The
funding needed to implement the proposed increases will come from recognizing additional revenue in the General Fund from
higher than anticipated sales tax revenue and from existing enterprise and internal service funds. Funding sources from enterprise
and internal service funds will pay for the increases within those funds. As proposed, $481,625 in General Fund revenues will be
used with the remaining $284,050 coming from non-General Fund sources.

Council member White noted that the Evergreen study was completed in 2019 and she asked if Administration felt the
study was helpful and whether it should be completed again. Ms. Judkins stated that has been a topic of discussion recently;
Administration recognizes that the data from the study is outdated, and it will likely be necessary to consider a data refresh to
evaluate current market conditions.

Council member Hyer stated that it is important to communicate and recognize the value of City employees; he is
supportive of approving increases that have been proposed.

Chair Nadolski then asked Deputy Council Executive Director Symes to report on updated information regarding salaries
for elected officials. Mr. Symes reported the Council office recently contracted with Evergreen to perform a compensation study
for elected officials; because that work was still underway at the time that the City-wide compensation proposal was presented by
Administration, the Council opted to leave salary adjustments for elected officials out of this action. However, staff has been made
aware of an ordinance requiring elected officials to receive COLAs when regular employees receive a COLA. Exhibit C to the
ordinance identifies a two-percent COLA for the Mayor and City Council. A substitute exhibit C has been provided to the Council
and should be mentioned in a motion to adopt the ordinance before the Council tonight.

Proposed Ordinance 2022-12 amending Project ARO14 for additional work
to the terminal at the Ogden Hinkley Airport

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed amendments to the Fiscal
Year (FY) 2022-2026 Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated

Minutes of Regular Meeting of Council of Ogden City, Utah April 5, 2022

Administration is proposing an amendment to CIP Project ARO14 — Modify Terminal Building and TSA Holding Room. The
amendments include changes to both the scope and the budget of the project. As federal funding has become available, additional
money may be allocated to the expansion of the airport terminal and holding facility. This expansion is needed to handle multiple
commercial flights simultaneously. The project was approved as part of the amended FY 2021-2025 Capital Improvement Plan
with a one-time budget of $2,070,000 consisting of $1,550,000 of federal grant money at $520,000 from the city’s CIP funding.
The proposed amendment includes a total of $5,000,000 over a five-year period consisting entirely of federal funding.

Deputy Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo. The initial project scope included more modest
improvements to the airport terminal and facility; however, as additional federal funding has become available through the
Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security Act of 2020 (CARES), the Coronavirus Response and Relief Supplemental
Appropriations Act of 2021 (CRRSAA), and the bipartisan Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act of 2021, further funding may
be appropriated to the airport for infrastructure improvements. The initial scope and budget for the project was more modest and
included a one-time total budget of $2,070,000. The modified project scope and budget includes an annual allocation of
$1,000,000 per year for the five-year length of the Capital Improvement Plan. The expansion of the TSA holding room and
terminal facility is needed to accommodate multiple simultaneous flights. The current capacity of the passenger holding room is
less than 270 persons. Each of the carriers’ flights seat up to 180 passengers making it difficult for more than one flight to be
processed at the same time.

Airport Manager Garrett reported on the manner in which monies will be used to improve the Ogden Airport, which
should make it possible to accommodate three airlines at the facility.

Public Comments

Kerry Wayne spoke to his ongoing concerns regarding the development of the property on the west side of Business
Depot Ogden (BDO), which has been very negatively impactful to Marriott-Slaterville residents. The response from The Boyer
Company has been inadequate and there continues to be mud and standing water issues, excessive dust that is landing on homes
and properties in Marriott-Slaterville. The residents would like a timetable and some communication about things that the City and
The Boyer Company plan to do in response to these issues.

Alan Walker and Mike Miller discussed their efforts to build a garage in the area of 7" Street and Wall Avenue; they
continue to encounter codes that prevent them from making the improvements they desire on their property. Metal siding is
disallowed while vinyl siding is. They have applied for a permit, but it has taken a great deal of time to get adequate responses
from City staff. Mr. Walker provided the Council with materials illustrating his concerns and the code amendments that he would
like to see in order for him to proceed with his project. He concluded that he only wants to help improve the appearance of his
neighborhood.

Rena Jordan stated she and her husband moved to Ogden 24 years ago from New Orleans; they now live on 25" Street
and were surprised to hear that a builder there is executing plans for a five-story building. She cannot understand how such a tall
building will fit into the historic nature of the neighborhood as there is no other building of similar height on or around what is an
iconic street. Clearly at some point this project was approved and she is dismayed. The building will obscure her view, but she is
more concerned about the integrity of the beautiful neighborhood. She hopes it is possible for the Council to consider some action
that would address the matter.

Heath Satow stated he would like the City to pay proper attention to Kerry Wayne’s comments; the City’s efforts to help
The Boyer Company pursue developments that are causing harm to neighbors are shameful. He congratulated the Mayor on the
fact that he will receive an extra $1,000 each month due to the Council approved COLA for elected officials, but he would like
him to earn that money by displaying leadership and addressing actions that have harmed the life of those in Marriott-Slaterville.

Council member Hyer referenced comments made by Mr. Walker and Miller and stated that the Council adopted an
