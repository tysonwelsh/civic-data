   1. A discussion regarding a firefighter market adjustment appropriation

Daniel Softley, Human Resource Division Director, presented.

Mr. Softley is asking for a budget appropriation for the paramedics and fire department. This is
because of the changes in Utah County. In February, he is scheduled to give more information to
the Council on compensation. Today’s recommendations are based on a survey comparison of
salaries.

He said that they look at other agencies and are keen on what their direct neighbors are doing.
They pay close attention to what other cities close to Provo are doing. This is important to HR
because there is a shortage of paramedics. They are recruiting at a lower EMT certified level.
This means new hires are not able to do what a certified paramedic can do. This is not ideal.
Provo City wants to get trained paramedics right off the bat. Training a lower-certified EMT will
take more time and money to reach the level of a paramedic if supported by the city.

As other agencies have adopted this strategy of sending EMTs to paramedic school, it has put
market pressure to keep their trained paramedics. It becomes tempting to see what other cities are
paying for paramedics.

Mr. Softley showed a document that shows the proposed changes he is making to the pay for
paramedics. He said that he wants to make sure the pay range is competitive with other cities. He
surveyed what other agencies are doing with their paramedics. Provo City is comfortable with
staying within a 5% plus or minus pay when compared to other cities.

He wants to increase pay starting by 5%. Orem city in July made some significant changes in
how they approach their paramedics. They boosted the top of their grade and the bottom of their
grade. They introduced an intermediate level that gave their paramedics accelerated pay growth.
When someone gets hired at an intermediate level, they move to a level 2. They also increase to
another level at 6 years and then a master paramedic by year 9. Orem has a higher starting rate
and an accelerated pay rate. Provo is not as competitive because of this strategy when compared
to Orem.

Mr. Softley showed the differences in pay between Orem and Provo. At some points, Provo is
10% behind the pay that Orem is offering their paramedics. Provo has not lost anyone yet, but
they are still vulnerable to losing paramedics to other agencies. To remain competitive in Utah
County, he recommends the following pay adjustments:
He recommends increasing starting and top of grade pay by 5%. He recommends implementing
an intermediate classification between paramedic and senior paramedic to accelerate pay. He
recommends boosting actual pay as follows to avoid compression and leapfrogging:
    - 5% pay for entry level paramedic and will receive an additional 5% when they advance to
        level II.
    - 10% for those who qualify to be at the new paramedic level II.
    - Up to 10% for those at the senior and master paramedic levels (most will be 5% because
        they will be topped out).

Councilor Gary Garrett asked about benefits. He asked how Provo City compares to other
agencies in terms of benefits. Mr. Softly said they do benefits separately from pay. Benefits are
competitive when compared to other agencies. He added that people are mostly looking at the
starting salary. Mr. Garrett asked if they had lost employees to other cities. Chief Headman said
that they have not lost any firefighters to neighboring agencies. They had a couple firefighters
that went to Nevada.

Mr. Softley said there is balance. His biggest concern is salaries. One of the reasons why he
came before the Council is because he wants to keep trained paramedics. There are costs to
losing trained paramedics.

Councilor Rachel Whipple asked if other agencies have poached employees from other agencies.
Mr. Softley said he is not sure how well that has gone over. Ms. Whipple asked if other agencies
have done something similar to what Mr. Softley is proposing. Mr. Softley said that Provo has
been competitive in the past, but they need to re-adjust now. Ms. Whipple asked how the
proposal would affect COLA for all employees. Mr. Softley said that they do COLA separately.
He said that a lot of agencies have not decided what they are doing for COLA yet. He said that
when other cities decide on COLAs, it helps him know what to do for Provo. He said that they
survey what other cities are doing for COLA. Ms. Whipple asked if approved, would it increase
salaries for this year and continue to next year and give employees COLA. He confirmed.

Councilor Craig Christensen asked about the spread in new % differences. Mr. Softley said that
when they make adjustments, they have pay grades table and all the grades are 5% apart but the
steps are 2.5% apart. When they make new adjustments, they have to fit within the increases set
in the existing table and policy. He said that when you account for all of those factors, Provo
City gets as close as it can to the market within pre-established guidelines.

Councilor Becky Bogdin asked if the Council voted yes, this mean taxes would have to be raised.
Mr. Softley directed that question to the Mayor. Ms. Bogdin asked if this accounts for in the
budget. Councilor Katrice MacKay invited Kelsey Zarbock to answer the question. Ms. Zarbock
said that this would be an ongoing cost and they would have to build it into the base budget.
They would have to consider what people are going to be paid next year and then from there,
they give everything to the Mayor with projected revenues and from there they decide how to
account for increases. Ms. Bogdin asked if it was too early to know if there is a tax increase
needed. Ms. Zarbock confirmed that. Ms. MacKay said that it is a good thing that the
administration understands the situation. Ms. Bogdin added that this increase will have to come
out of somewhere.

   3. A discussion regarding short-term rental properties in Provo (23-04)

Melia Dayley, Policy Analyst, presented.

Ms. Melia Dayley reviewed changes to the Short-Term Rental Dwellings. In the October
meeting, a motion was made to not expand short term rentals. The motion was to create a new
license for STRs and separate it from the rental dwelling license. This would also require regular
building inspections. It would also require proof of insurance and 24/7 contact info. After
deliberation with other departments in the City, 311 created a new and separate license. Ms.
Dayley showed the new license. She said it is similar to the current rental dwelling license. It
includes proof of insurance and 24/7 contact info.

Ms. Dayley showed the proposed fee of $125. She said that this fee is equivalent to the time and
work required for this license. She showed that the license requires a state tax number and the
federal EIN number.

Ms. Dayley showed the proposed changes to the code. She also shared a new bill from the State
and said it is an extensive STR bill. She read it and there are a few provisions that might affect
their licensing and code. She said if the bill goes through, it will be easy to make changes to the
code. She does not think the provisions mean they have to pause and wait for the proposed
changes to the code.
Ms. Dayley said that changes in the code include things pulled from the rental dwelling license
resources so that everything is in one place. Ms. Dayley went over the parking requirements
section. She showed the current minimum parking requirements for rental dwelling units. She
said that her question with the section revolves around the fact that parking situations are not the
same throughout the City. She said that other cities have parking plans that are needed to be
submitted with a rental dwelling license submission. Ms. Dayley asked if there was anything
they wanted to add to parking requirements for STR.

Mr. George Handley said that he likes what Ms. Dayley is proposing for parking and would
appreciate feedback from the Development Department. He asked about the definition of STR

knows a lot of faculty at BYU who leave for the summer or for as long as 6 months and asked if
renting their property would be considered as a STR. Ms. Dayley said that would not be a STR.

Mr. Handley said that one thing that has come up and shared that a citizen was interested in
owner occupied short term rentals. His argument was that if the owner is living on an upper level
and renting out the bottom level, the owner will make sure that their STR will not cause the city
any disturbances. Ms. Bogdin said that some in her neighborhood have done that and that it has
not been received well in her neighborhood.

Ms. Whipple said that she wants to add something to 6.27.030 to show how many people the
STR will allow for so that they can enforce it if that limit is broken.

Ms. Whipple asked if they required a parking plan, would they need a parking section in the
code. Ms. Dayley said she would check and see what other cities are doing. Ms. Whipple said it
would be good to have guidelines for people to follow.

Mr. Brian Jones clarified what Ms. Whipple was asking about in regard to the maximum
occupants allowed. Ms. Whipple said that she is not sure, but it could be based on square footage
or number of bedrooms. She said that in a hotel, if there are two queen beds, four people is the

STR. Ms. MacKay asked what the next step was. Ms. Dayley said they will pass what they have

Mr. Jones said that they may have to make changes if the State passes something that affects

Ms. Whipple asked if they could use software to let people know they are not in compliance with

Mr. Handley asked if they have looked at the data where STRs are popping up illegally. Ms.

   4. A discussion regarding Chapter 14.30 Accessory Dwelling Units. (24-016)

Melia Dayley, Policy Analyst, presented.

Ms. Melia Dayley said this item is a result of motions made at a previous meeting. The four
motions were first to review zones that are included or excluded in chapter 14.30. This would
look at PRO Zones, RC zones, and others. The second motion was to look at the Pathway
Program for ADUs. This would look at expanding this into neighborhoods or to make other
decisions about it. The third motion was to have an overview of the history of ADUs in Provo
and in the State. The fourth motion was to come back to a work meeting by July to look at
second kitchen agreements, occupancy limits, tandem parking and more.

Ms. MacKay said she quickly wanted to ask about not allowing new developments to allow
STRs. Ms. Dayley said she would look into that.

Ms. Dayley included an ADU overview. She said that the housing plan calls out ADUs. She said
it is the second strategy in the top three housing strategies for Provo City to promote a mix of
home types and sizes and price points. She also expounded on arguments to permit or to not
permit ADUs. To permit would include increasing the housing stock/options for renters without
adding primary infrastructure. It would also supplement income for homeowners. It would also
shift the focus onto making sure ADUs are safe and meeting requirements. However, the
arguments to not permit revolve around on-street parking increased. It would also increase
density in neighborhoods and add another type of rental type for code enforcement to manage.

Mr. Handley asked about the code compliance. He said he would like to know why code
compliance becomes more difficult if they allow ADUs. He does not think they have much data
to know for sure that code compliance would become more difficult. Ms. Dayley said she is not
comfortable speaking to that. She said that is a better question for the Development department.
Mr. Travis Hoban shared a conversation he had with Bill Pepperone. Bill Pepperone said that a
common scenario is where a person lives in a home but then moves to someplace else but still
rents out the original property. This is why ADUs can become a problem in the long term. Mr.
Handley said he would like to have more data on that. He mentioned the Tree Streets in Provo
and how ADUs have stabilized that neighborhood especially since certain requirements and
limitations were added. He said that he had not seen the tendency that Mr. Hoban brought up.

Ms. Bogdin said that she has seen the problem that Mr. Hoban brought up in the Franklin
neighborhood. Mr. Handley said he knows that it does happen, but he wants to see the rates of
that happening. Ms. MacKay said she doesn't think they have the data. Mr. Hoban added that in
Northpark and other neighborhoods that have been decimated by absent landlords, ADUs impact
certain types of neighborhoods more than others. Some neighborhoods are better at policing each
other and others are not going to turn other people in. He said that his impression is that the
lower socioeconomic neighborhoods are impacted more by ADUs because they are more
transient. This is coming from conversations with neighborhood chairs.

Mr. Handley clarified his question and said that his question is that are those problems existing
in neighborhoods where ADUs are not allowed. Additionally, if they allow ADUs and owner
occupancy is enforced violations would go down or increase.

Mr. Bill Pepperone said that he doesn't know that they have existing data but can see what he can
gather from the data they have. He said that in the established neighborhoods where they have
allowed ADUs, those neighborhoods are good at policing ADUs. That takes the load of
Development services and code enforcement officers. He said he would have to do more
research to fully answer Mr. Handley’s question.

Ms. Dayley showed the history and context of ADUs in Utah and in other cities. She said that
they can restrict up to 66% of residential areas. This is because of the college-town rule. They
have to require at least 33%. The state code also allows for requiring a permit/license. She said
they cannot have HOA specific exclusions as well. She shared examples from other cities as
well.
Ms. Bogdin asked how effective Orem’s requirements are. Ms. Dayley said she doesn't know but
that she can reach out to Orem. Mr. Handley pointed out the website for Orem that has more
information about ADUs in Orem. Ms. Dayley said that nothing in the 2024 Legislative Session
has come up yet about ADUs. Ms. Bogdin asked if it is in their best interest to have a separate
license for ADUs. Mr. Pepperone said he thinks that would not matter.

Ms. Dayley expounded on the history of ADUs in Provo. She touched on previous bills that were
passed. She said that in 2022, the Housing Committee allowed ADUs on the Westside and a
special use permit. She added that 54% of residentially zoned areas across the city permit ADUs.

She then showed the zones that permit ADUs and zones that do not permit ADUs. Ms. Dayley
showed a map where ADUs are allowed and not allowed. She talked about the RC zone and how
they do allow ADUs. She also talked about the R2PD zone and said that it was added when they
permitted the R2 zone. Ms. Dayley said they could exclude that zone but that the Council needs a
motion.

She touched on the SDP5 zone as well. Mr. Hoban asked how much of that zone was developed.
Mr. Pepperone said there is a map and Mr. Hoban said it looks about to be 25% developed. Mr.
Hoban said that he knocked on the doors of a street in this zone and he asked how they would
feel about ADUs. He said there were concerns around parking. He said that if areas are built with
ADUs in mind, it could overwhelm the already existing density of the area. Mr. Pepperone said
that if the developer came in with that in mind, the City would not permit them unless there is
adequate parking.

Ms. Bogdin said that her concern is turning these areas into duplexes and is interested in putting
in a certain percentage of new builds can turn into ADUs.

Ms. MacKay said that her issue with ADUs is changing someone’s existing zone. She is all for if
someone wants to do an ADU development. She said that will help people know what they are
getting into and going to be around.

Ms. Whipple said that change is inevitable. She said that the City has to be adaptable. She said
that allowing ADUs in single family areas is a gentle way to increase density. She said that just
because some things were not permitted when someone bought a property, does not mean that it
will never change. She said that if they add ADUs throughout the city, it does not change how
people use their personal properties. She said that some houses are more adaptable to it and she
does not see that as a bad thing either. She said that there is a demand for more affordable

that would bring people into compliance. She said that the other thing she wanted to add is the
way they talk about parking. She said that the way they talk about it makes it sound like parking
is the biggest issue in the city. She said that the city can regulate public streets and that if they
have decided there is a certain off-street parking limit then they have the standard set. If people

street parking. She said she would rather do that.

Mr. Hoban made a counterpoint and said that just because people do a lot of U-turns with their
