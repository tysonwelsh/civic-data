                                  SPECIAL CITY COUNCIL
                                 WORK MEETING MINUTES
                                    Tuesday, February 15, 2022
                                     Approved May 11, 2022

The following are the minutes of the City Council Electronic Meeting of the Herriman City Council. The
meeting was held on Wednesday, February 15, 2022, at 5:00 p.m. in the Herriman City Hall Council
Chambers, 5355 West Herriman Main Street, Herriman, Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting, as
required by law, was posted in the City Hall, on the City’s website, and delivered to members of the
Council, media, and interested citizens.

Presiding: Mayor Lorin Palmer

Councilmembers Present: Jared Henderson, Teddy Hodges, Sherrie Ohrn, and Steve Shields.

Staff Present: City Manager Nathan Cherpeski, Assistant City Manager Tami Moody, Assistant City
Manager Wendy Thomas, Police Chief Troy Carr, City Recorder Jackie Nostrom, Finance Director Alan
Rae, City Attorney Chase Andrizzi, Communications Manager Jonathan LaFollette.

5:00 PM:
   1. Call to Order
Mayor Lorin Palmer called the meeting to order at 5:13 p.m.

    2. Administrative Reports
          2.1. Strategic planning process
City Manager Nathan Cherpeski hoped to provide an opportunity to establish a plan and interim goals
as the City heads into the budget season. He touched on the concerns surrounding strategic plans due
to the failure rate, the long process to draft and ultimately it does not address the need in the
community. With Herriman City changing rapidly, the plan could become obsolete prior to being
completed and the City needed to come up with a process which had frequent checks and balances
with the Council to ensure effectiveness. City Manager Cherpeski indicated this plan would need to be
done differently and did not recommend having a consultant put a strategic plan together. The plan
should be viewed as a tactical plan for the community and City Manager Cherpeski suggested it should

be reviewed semi-annually or annually to identify the effectiveness of the plan. He looked to the
Council for direction.

Councilmember Ohrn opined if the City was in the initial stages of planning for a new City, it would
make sense for a traditional process utilizing a consultant; however, noted it didn’t feel like it was the
appropriate time for those specific services. The Council agreed. Councilmember Shields noted there
were aspects which had yet to be fully vetted regarding past decisions rendered. He observed recent
issues which came because of those decisions. He felt from a high level, it was important to understand
the unknown obligations which could derail plans financially. Councilmember Shields stated the
financial stability of the City was a significant factor in his ability to make decisions moving forward.
Mayor Palmer agreed with the statements offered and acknowledged a plan would still need to be put
in place with the realization hurdles could come up. Councilmember Shields indicated the budget
discussion was important to identify the goals for the community including parks, amenities, public
service, and projects in addition to primary services.

City Manager Cherpeski observed the various iterations of the City’s mission and vision statements
and recommended putting the resources together and have a single statement. Councilmember
Henderson asked what the goal was and stated he had not heard anything about a plan. The vision and
direction of the community changed every two years with election cycles. He reported it was the City’s
job to deliver the services as efficiently as possible. City Manager Cherpeski responded the idea was to
create a plan which could be adopted to identify the goal of the Council and to capture the vision of
what the Council desired. Councilmember Shields pointed out the City should be filled with residents,
not renters. It should be a community where people love to live and work as it would have employment
centers and innovative businesses to carry the City forward. He desired to see a beautiful City and
know people were proud to live in a clean, efficient community which would have a mix of housing
which would serve the entire spectrum of salaries. He agreed the goals needed to be defined, after
which, constraints needed to be identified.

City Manager Cherpeski indicated the plan would continually look to the next five-to-ten-years in the
future. Councilmember Shields suggested the City needed to plan to be a “Smart City” through
broadband. Councilmember Henderson expressed his frustration of having the same conversation over
the last six years. He iterated basic services including secondary water, water, and roads are what
people expect the City to provide and noted the needs had to start with the budget including the
available funds after obligations. A financial roadmap showing what could be controlled was needed.
Councilmember Henderson liked the thought to have a longer-term budget to take care of projects and
noted his discomfort when additional staff was requested. He opined the goals needed to be
quantifiable. Councilmember Shields felt this could be accomplished simultaneously and understood the
frustration when a project is in the early planning stages and learn the funds had already been
committed to other projects. He indicated he wanted to understand the financial obligation to get
caught up with commitments. City Manager Cherpeski concurred and noted he would bring a list of
projects to determine the priority of the Council.

          2.2. Budget priorities
City Manager Cherpeski turned the conversation to the kinds of projects the Council felt were the
priority and highlighted infrastructure failures in streets, water, storm drain and parks. He offered
scenarios of different failures and the causation of the specific failure.

Councilmember Henderson questioned if the City could meet the level of service for road maintenance
along with the associated costs. Councilmember Shields suggested potentially having to stop
constructing new roads until existing ones have undergone necessary repairs as there were no funds
available and noted the possible issues could cause. Councilmember Henderson suggested a long-term
plan needed to be outlined. City Manager Cherpeski relayed his disbelief every failing road could be
fixed within the next five years. Councilmember Shields felt the City was so far behind the PCI report
indicated Herriman would need to spend $3.4 million annually for maintenance and noted the budget
was severely lacking in response to road maintenance.

Councilmember Henderson wanted to see a plan to progress the circumstances; however, planning for
something specific and watching it continually change caused frustrations. He opined the City should
start with the main concerns and work up from there. City Manager Cherpeski looked for direction
regarding the coming budget to deal with the failing roads and what the Council wanted to see the
resources allocated. Councilmember Ohrn requested the maintenance budget be increased to deal with
the failures and allocate the revenue stream to accomplish the goal.

Councilmember Hodges interjected the strategic plan was not tied to the budget and felt the Council
should outline goals and desires to move the community forward. Mayor Palmer agreed.
Councilmember Henderson felt the strategic plan needed to be tied to the budget. In response to a
question posed by Councilmember Shields, Finance Director Alan Rae reported over the last ten-years
the Council had approximately $3 million to discretionally allocate. Councilmember Shields stated
contractual obligations needed to be done, and from there, the Council would need to prioritize
projects to catch up on maintenance in order to provide adequate service.

Councilmember Henderson expressed his feeling staff should recommend the prioritization of the
projects to the Council based on professional expertise. He highlighted the question of the level of
service the Council wanted to support and stated the Elected Officials did not have enough information
to decide. The Council ensued a discussion about the current level of service compared with industry
standard.

City Manager Cherpeski continued the budget conversation with staffing needs and observed the
following departments were short staffed: Human Resources, Legal, Prosecution, and Court. It was
noted Judge Farr submitted his letter of resignation and the Council discussed options available to the
Court. Councilmember Shields relayed his desire to ensure the City was sufficiently staffed. City
Manager Cherpeski relayed his concern about the lack of space within City facilities for staffing to
grow.

          2.3. City Council meeting process
Mayor Palmer talked about the length of City Council packets and the redundant information provided
therein and how long it takes for a project to go through the legislative process. He recommended
removing the presentations from the packet and to adjust the process of having a work meeting on
every item prior to being noticed for consideration. He observed these were not standard practices.
City Manager Cherpeski clarified if an item appeared on the general meeting agenda, did not mean an
action had to be taken. Councilmember Henderson agreed each item didn’t need to be taken to a work
meeting prior to being on the agenda for consideration. Council consensus determined to remove the
presentations from the packet and to allow projects to be heard during the general meeting rather than
having the process delayed.

          2.4. City Council roundtable
Mayor Palmer relayed his desire to increase resident involvement and looked to create a diversity
committee as well as an economic development committee to improve partner relationships. He indicated
his plan was to draft the initial outline of the proposed committees and bring them back to a future
meeting for Council discussion.

   3. Adjournment
Councilmember Hodges moved to adjourn the City Council work meeting at 8:08 p.m. Councilmember
Henderson seconded the motion, and all voted aye.
