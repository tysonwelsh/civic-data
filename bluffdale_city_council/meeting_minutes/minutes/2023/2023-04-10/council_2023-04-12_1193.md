Notice is hereby given that the Bluffdale City Council, Planning Commission, Local Building Authority, and Redevelopment
Agency Board will hold a combined meeting on Wednesday, April 12, 2023, at Bluffdale City Hall, 2222 West 14400 South,
Bluffdale, Utah. The meeting will begin at 6:00 PM or as soon thereafter as possible. This meeting will also be broadcast live
to the public at: www.bluffdale.com. The public may comment at the meeting or by emailing comments to
councilmeetingcomment@bluffdale.com by 4:00 PM the day of the meeting. Emailed comments will be submitted to the
City Council but will not be read at the meeting. Notice is further given that access to this meeting by the City Council may
be by electronic means.
In the event the meeting is disrupted in any way that the City in its sole discretion deems inappropriate,the City reserves the
right to immediately remove the individual(s) from the meeting and, if needed,end virtual access to the meeting. Reasons
for removing an individual or ending virtual access to the meeting include but are not limited to the posting of offensive
pictures, remarks, or making offensive statements, disrespectful statements or actions, and any other action deemed
inappropriate.

         2.1   Discussion on Draft 2023 Economic Development Strategic Plan. (Staff
               Presenter, Grant Crowell),


City Planner/Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell, reported that earlier in the year,
staffworked on the 2023 Economic Development Strategic Plan document. The intent oftonight's
meeting was to consider items that might have budget implications. There are topical areas such
as transportation, planning and zoning, marketing, and economic development. The Bluffdale
Boulevard Master Plan has been worked on for some time by City Engineer, Michael Fazio. The
plan for the one-lane bridge was prepared and the one-lane bridge east to 1-15 has already been
budgeted as part ofthe Corridor Study. It was noted, however,that more information is needed.

Mr. Crowell commented that clarity is needed to deal with traffic coming out of The Pointe. He
and Mr. Fazio have been in contact with HDR Engineering and are attempting to contact the Utah
Department of Transportation ("UDOT") to present the Corridor Study. They were currently
trying to address issues with the intersection at Porter Rockwell Boulevard and 14600 South. There
was no new budget necessary for that. As for Redwood Road to the one-lane bridge, that study

within the fiscal year, there would be a proposal to move it to the next fiscal year. Consulting
assistance would be needed for the work, which was already part ofthe plan. Mr. Crowell reported
that the Transportation Plan would also be updated within the next year.

The 2023 Economic Development Strategic Plan document was further reviewed. Mr. Crowell
shared information from the Transportation and Infrastructure section of the status document,
which referenced support strategies for the 1-15 interchange. All of the regional transportation
plans and meetings were being tracked. There did not need to be an allocation for that work.
Another item listed was to complete updates to the City's Trails and Active Transportation Plan.
He explained that trails are a component ofactive transportation,but active transportation was also
on roads and streets. It was important to make sure that the Transportation Plan update develops
the active transportation dialogue further. All of the plans need to connect. The Transportation
Plan update would come from Impact Fees whereas the Parks and Trails Plan update would come
from Parks Impact Fees. Those items had been budgeted.

Council Member Kallas wondered if the Trails and Active Transportation Plan would be separate
from the Parks and Trails Plan and the Transportation Plan. Mr. Crowell explained that parts of
the Trails and Active Transportation Plan would be contained within each. Staffdid not believe it
was necessary to do a standalone Trails and Active Transportation Plan so that information would
be incorporated into the others plans that would be updated. It had been many years since the
Parks and Trails Plan had been updated. Approximately 8,000 more residents had been added
since the last update. He saw active transportation as a subset ofboth plans.

Mayor Hall noted that there was a finalist for the Parks and Trails Plan. The contract for that had
been drafted and the work continued to move forward. Mr. Crowell noted that the City used Hales
Engineering for the previous version of the Transportation Plan and there was a bid from them to
update that. Hales Engineering was also assisting with the Impact Fee Plan. There would be
Impact Fee Plan documents presented to the City Council in approximately one month.

The Planning and Zoning section was reviewed. Mr. Crowell noted that there were some
interesting ideas such as ensuring that the zoning along key corridors and gateway nodes reflected
the economic development goals of the City. He believed that for the most part, the zoning is
appropriate. However, a new idea was to discuss the development of a Town Center/Main Street
Plan in a central area of the community. The document suggested appljdng for a Wasatch Front
Regional Council("WFRC")Transportation and Land Use Connection("TLC")Consultant Grant.
Another goal was to develop a land use area plan or study along Bluffdale Boulevard that took into
account the impact of The Point on Bluffdale. It was unlikely that applying for more than one
grant with WFRC would be successful but the City could apply for one. Staff would bring a
mapping exercise to the City Council over the summer as a way to discuss Bluffdale Boulevard.

The third option under the Planning and Zoning section was to update the zoning, road design,
subdivision, and property disposition plan for City property on Redwood Road and Bangerter

community survey for parks costs approximately $20,000. That was likely the amount it would
cost for a statistically significant survey related to any specific topic. There is a lot involved in the
process. Although he believed it would be worthwhile to speak to a professional about a possible
survey, the Council must consider how the data will be used.

this and reiterated that it might be worth considering doing a larger survey that would provide
robust data to the City. Mayor Hall agreed that making decisions based on data is a wise choice.

wanted answers to. This would make it easier to understand what the City is trying to discover.
From there, it would be possible to determine if that data could be collected in-house or if an
outside professional was needed. Mr. Crowell explained that some sample survey questions could
be obtained from Y2 Analytics. Mayor Hall asked that potential questions be gathered from the
relevant departments to determine what is needed.

on something like a survey,the City must have the ability to utilize the information. The company
that the City is looking at, which costs approximately $20,000 for one survey, is high-end and
provides useful information. He was not certain, however, that there was anyone on Staff that
