Consideration of a proposed rezone of approximately 1.36 acres or property located
at 310 West 1400 North from Mixed Residential Low (MR-12) to Mixed residential
Medium (MR-20) West Pointe Subdivision (Bridger Neighborhood) – Ordinance 22-
06 – Tanya Rice – Planner

Planner Tanya Rice addressed the Council regarding the proposed rezone. Ms. Rice
summarized the proposal and rezone of the subdivision. The reason for the rezone is the
MR-12 permits for 16 units the subdivision has 20 units. The parcel was developed in
1994 with 26 units. There are five buildings with four units each a total of 20 units,
(condominiums). The applicant's intent is for subdivision, but a rezone is required first.
The underlying purpose of the subdivision is to allow the owners to own the land
underneath the condominiums and make the property more marketable and easier to sell.

Since 1994 the Bridgerland area has been downzoned twice to MR-12 zoning permitting
12 Units per acre. The condominiums are existing buildings but are non-conforming to
the current zoning. In order to subdivide, the parcel must meet current zoning
requirements. The request is for MR-20 rezone which allows 27 units on site.
The staff is concerned about neighborhood combability. There is no other MR-20 zoning
in the surrounding area. Bridger neighborhood contains a high percentage of multi-family
residential development. A previous negative impact on the elementary school was due to
the high rate of transient occupancy. And Logan City authorized zoning actions to
"downzone" Bridger to prevent additional, high-density projects.

The PC (Planning Commission) unanimously recommended a denial of the rezone 6 to 0.
The discussion points recommended a deed restriction, a downtown immediately
following the zoning to prevent spot zoning and potential impact. The subdivision is
contingent upon the rezone. If the rezone goes away so does the subdivision. If the rezone
passes, the subdivision must comply with MR-20 zoning and subdivision standards. The
subdivision does not at present meet the standards, especially a requirement of 30% open
space.

Jeremy Raymond, an attorney representing one of the proponents, Theron and Julie
Stoddard. Mr. Raymond first emphasized the purpose of the municipality's general land-
use authority and the Municipal Land Use Development and Management Act, (Utah
Code Section 10-9-102).

Secondly, the proponent is not seeking to redevelop the property, add more buildings, or
demolish any of the existing buildings. The proponent is seeking to increase the density
in order to file a subdivision plat to permit the development to become a traditional
subdivision rather than remain as a condominium subdivision. To alleviate any concerns
of the council and surrounding neighbors, the proponent proposes to enter into a
restricted covenant (deed) applicable to the property that would restrict any additional
development that would increase the density beyond of what it is today. In other words,
the density could not be increased further than the units that exist at present. The
restricted covenant (deed) would be tied to the land and future owners would be subject
to it. Furthermore, the proponent would request a rezone after the subdivision has been
reported (as a plat).
Thirdly, MR-20 is compatible with surrounding areas since there is multi-family housing
in the area. If the zoning is granted, it would permit the property to become a conforming
property. Nothing would change from a physical sense except in a legal sense.

Fourth, the characterization of the development as a condominium has adversely
impacted the owners. Their ability to obtain financing, insurance, refinance, etc. The
underwriting requirements for condominiums have increased since the 2008 housing
requirements.

Finally, it is for the benefit and the interest of the city, owners, neighbors, and the
community at large. The conversion of the property into a traditional subdivision will not
harm the city rather it will permit the owners to improve and maintain the aesthetics,
character, and convivence of the party; to preserve and potentially increase the tax
increments of the city, and permit the property to be marketed, financed, etc. It will also
encourage consistent long-term ownership. And requests the city approves the
proponent's request.

Ordinance 22-04 Land Development Code Amendments

On March 1, 2022, the Municipal Council held a public hearing on the proposed Land
Development Code Amendments (17.08, 17.11, 17.37 & 17.62) governing Short Term
Rentals. A final decision was continued to April 19, 2022, to allow time to reach out to
the Neighborhood Councils soliciting their input on this issue. We sent out a packet of
information (Council's packet & power point) to all Neighborhood Councils with a
comment deadline of 4/8/22. The comments that I have received are included with this
                                                                                   9 | Page
              Logan Municipal Council Minutes ~ Logan, Utah ~ April 19, 2022
report. Throughout the Planning Commission and Council processes, there have been a
variety of opinions about short term rentals, most of which have been favorable towards
permitting short term rentals, but with conditions.

Based on comments received and discussions at the last public hearing, we modified the
proposed language in LDC 17.37. Under Subsection B, we added a CUP requirement for
all short-term rentals in residential zones, established a minimum distance between short
term rentals of 500', and placed a citywide limit on the total number of licenses available
(3 licenses per 1,000 people). The CUP requirement will notify adjoining property
owners of a proposed short term rental and give them the opportunity to bring any issues
to the Planning Commission in their decision making process, while the minimum
distance between short term rentals and the maximum number of licenses will limit the
overall density of within the City. The parking component in Subsection C was updated to
clearly state that all parking requirements necessary for a specific short-term rental shall
be accommodated on site. Also added language about the appearance/character of the
residential unit not changing and a limitation on the number of consecutive rental
contracts by the same person or groups. The updated LDC Chapter
17.37 is below:

Chapter 17.37: Additional Development Standards

§17.37.130. Short Term Rentals (Vacation Rental)
A.     The purpose of this Section is to regulate short term rentals to ensure
       compatibility within residential neighborhoods and to ensure they won't
       unreasonably reduce housing opportunities. These regulations do not apply to
       hotels, motels, or bed and breakfasts.
B.     General Requirements.
1.     A short-term rental is temporary lodging or accommodations typically located in
       a residential dwelling for a fee and for a rental period of less than 30 continuous

       otherwise make available a short-term rental without first obtaining a Logan City

       obtain a Conditional Use Permit prior to securing a business license. New

5.     Limit on Total Number of Short-Term Rentals in Residential Zones. Pursuant to

Micromobility devices are defined as small, lightweight personal vehicles operating at
speeds below 20 mph. Micromobility devices includes, but is not limited to, bicycles,

 Micromobility does NOT include motorcycles, motor scooters and other similar gas-

Micromobility sales are increasing every year and with advancements in electric motor
technologies, the range of users has expanded to all stages of life. Micromobility e-
devices are very capable transportation alternatives to cars, provide recreation and
healthy exercise, are relatively inexpensive to own and operate, and require minimal
space to operate/park in the City.

Logan City’s current bicycle code section is outdated in terminology and applicability
concerning micromobility devices. This proposal creates a legal framework to better
regulate where they can safely operate and where they should not be allowed. National
for-rent scooter share companies are interested in establishing a program in Logan/USU.

pathways. Current collaboration with other interest groups.

TITLE 10 VEHICLES AND TRAFFIC CHAPTER 10.68 & 10.70 BICYCLES
SKATEBOARDS Proposes new definitions for micromobility devices, infrastructure,

Proposes that micromobility users are subject to applicable traffic laws, speed limits,

Ordinance 22-08 Logan Municipal Code Amendments

Included with this packet and listed as a separate workshop item on the Council agenda
is the proposed Section 5.15 in the Logan Municipal Code contained in Ordinance 22-08
(Attached). This proposed section deals with the business licensing aspect of short-term
rentals which is different than the land use aspect covered in the LDC amendments. The
reason why this is trailing the LDC amendments considered under Ord. 22-04 is that we
couldn't finalize the licensing requirements until we knew the direction of the general
land use requirements.

In summary, proposed LMC Section 5.15 establishes the following:

•      An annual business license is required to conduct a short-term rental in the City
•      Minimum license application requirements & submittals
•      Licensing fees (Proposed $100.00 new/$50.00 renewal)
•      Annual license renewal requirements
•      Requires a local property manager
•      Requires posting of rental license in unit
•      Limitation on total numbers of short-term rental licenses 3/1,000 population,
       annual adjustments, waiting lists, sales & transfer of property, new licenses
•      Minimum requirements - meets building codes, CUP required
•      Minimum inspections - one at time of application & as needed
•      Penalties - 3 violations & loss of license

While our concerns still remain regarding the potential use of a short term rental to
circumvent long term residential usage and occupancy standards as well as potential
impacts to residential neighborhoods, the proposed approach outlined in both Ordinance
#22-04 & #22-08 should temper potential problems, provides a clear & legitimate path
for property owners who want to use their residential properties for short term rentals,
provides an opportunity for public input, and gives the City the ability to revoke licenses
if we do find problematic short term rentals.
