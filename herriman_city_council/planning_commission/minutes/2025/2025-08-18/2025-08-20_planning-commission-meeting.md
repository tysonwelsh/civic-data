The following are the minutes of the Herriman Planning Commission meeting held on Wednesday,
August 20, 2025, at 6:00 p.m. in the Herriman City Council Chambers, 5355 West Herriman Main

Hall, on the City’s website, and delivered to members of the Commission, media, and interested

6:00 PM WORK MEETING (Fort Herriman Conference Room)

Chair Andrea Bradford called the meeting to order at 6:05 p.m.

1.   Commission Business

     1.1. Review of City Council Decisions – Michael Maloy, Planning Director

Planning Director Michael Maloy provided an update on recent City Council decisions regarding text
amendments that were previously forwarded by the Planning Commission. He reported that the
Strangling Brothers proposal for a longer-term temporary use was denied by the City Council. He
explained that while the Council seemed sympathetic to the applicant, they felt it wasn't an

appropriate transition given the lack of facilities and that it would modify the intent of zoning tools
in a way inconsistent with what the city was trying to achieve.

Regarding the accessory structure amendment initiated by a resident building too close to the
property line, he stated this item was tabled. The Council appeared comfortable with the
Commission's recommendation to allow reduced setbacks adjacent to open space, but not for
interior side yard setbacks. The applicant is interested in asking the Council to consider maintaining
a proportionate ratio of setback versus height, changing it from a 1:1 to a 2:1 ratio (for every
additional 2 feet in height, one foot in setback would be required). Due to the applicant's schedule,
this item won't return to Council until October.

Commissioner Ferguson arrived at 6:16 p.m.

For the wireless amendment, Maloy reported the Council accepted all of the Commission's
recommendations. The applicant also accepted the changes despite their preference for making
these sites permitted uses. The Council agreed with the Commission that all wireless facilities
should be conditional uses, meaning any stealth or monopole application will come to the Planning
Commission for review.

Planning Director Maloy clarified that the new wireless ordinance focused on scenarios when
providers cannot find public or quasi-public property. He explained that operators must first seek
public locations, and only if unavailable can they utilize this new code for nonresidential, non-public
sites (commercial or industrial zoned properties). The existing abbreviated code for public and
quasi-public uses will remain in place. He noted that a monopole proposal would be coming before
the Commission in the second meeting in September.

When asked about the retaining wall amendment, he explained it was continued because the city
attorney felt the draft language didn't adequately clarify how height is measured.



    1.2. Discussion regarding an amendment to Herriman City Code Chapter 10-12: Commercial and
         Office Zones – Blake Thomas, Community Development Director

Community Development Director Thomas presented a follow-up to an earlier joint meeting
discussion regarding amendments to create a Commercial Flex (CF) zone. He displayed a map
showing undeveloped commercial sites in the city's manufacturing zones and explained these areas
remain undeveloped due to various challenges.

Director Thomas described a "missing middle" in the city's commercial zoning, explaining the
proposed CF zone would create a flexible space for small businesses currently operating from
homes who need to expand but don't require large industrial spaces. He showed a sample site layout
with a reception building, small bay flex spaces, drive-up storage, and indoor storage areas that
would create opportunities for micro businesses, assembly operations, car detailing, commissary
kitchens, and similar uses.

Several definitions would be added to the code, including assembly of goods, car detailing,
commissary kitchen, drive-up self-storage, indoor self-storage, and print and distribution. He

walked through the proposed code changes section by section, discussing building materials,
lighting, fencing, signage, and setback requirements.

The Commission engaged in extensive discussion about how to balance the business incubator
purpose with concerns about storage uses dominating these developments:

Commissioner Sickles questioned the storage component: "Is the purpose of this new zone to
accommodate business or storage? If storage is the goal, then that's a different conversation."

Planning Manager Spencer proposed a strategy to prevent the space from being dominated by
storage units, suggesting that the allocation should allow a certain proportion of storage units—such
as a 1:1 or 2:1 ratio—relative to the larger storage units. Furthermore, he stipulated that these small
storage units must be leased exclusively by the entities utilizing the larger units.

Commissioner Rypien highlighted the necessity for varied facade treatments: "The guidelines for
building materials stipulate that facades facing public streets must mainly utilize brick, stone,
glazing glass, and architectural features. Should we consider extending these requirements to
encompass areas beyond public streets?"

There was general agreement that the zone should prioritize small business space over storage, with
storage being an accessory use. The Commission suggested tying storage allowances to a larger
minimum lot size, establishing a ratio of business units to storage units, requiring more architectural
treatment on buildings visible from public areas, and ensuring that at least some units have
storefront glazing.

Director Thomas said he would revise the draft to incorporate these suggestions and bring it back to
a future meeting.

   1.3. Presentation and discussion of potential amendments to Chapter 10-10 Residential Zones,
        and Chapter 10-16 Table of Uses in Title 10 Land Development Code to create additional
        residential zones in Herriman City – Michael Maloy, Planning Director

Planning Director Maloy provided an update on the small lot development tool the city has been
working on. He explained that this item connects with the next agenda item on street connectivity,
as they want to consider how block faces and connectivity work with this infill tool. Director Maloy
reported that he recently met with the Wasatch Front Regional Council about potential grant
funding for what he termed "right-size zoning." The city has done numerous smaller lot
developments through Master Development Agreements (MDAs), but they want to create zoning
tools that more accurately reflect the actual land uses on the city's zoning map. The city is also
preparing to surplus property near Hidden Oaks that could benefit from this new small lot tool. The
property would connect with Pod 20 of Hidden Oaks, where lots are approximately 6,000 square
feet. The current smallest lot zone in Herriman is 10,000 square feet (quarter-acre), which creates
inconsistency with adjacent developments.



The Commission discussed various aspects of the potential small lot zone, including lot sizes
potentially ranging from 5,000-7,000 square feet, maximum home sizes and lot coverage (30%
maximum coverage was mentioned), garage placement (potentially rear-loaded to improve

streetscape), driveway width limitations, and building height considerations. Commissioners debated
whether to allow front-facing garages or require rear garages to improve walkability and aesthetics.
They also discussed whether smaller homes would actually be built, as many developers tend to
maximize home size regardless of lot size. Director Maloy indicated he would bring several models
with dimensions to a future meeting for the Commission to review and provide further direction.

   1.4. Presentation and discussion of potential “Connectivity Standards Development Code”
        amendments in Title 10 Land Development Code of the Herriman City Code – Michael
        Maloy, Planning Director

Planning Manager Spencer presented information on potential connectivity standards for the city's
development code. He explained that this topic frequently comes up during site plan and subdivision
reviews, yet the current ordinance doesn't require specific features or amenities for walkability and
connectivity. He referenced a study conducted by Wasatch Front Regional Council, Mountainland
Association of Governments (MAG), Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT), and Utah Transit
Authority (UTA) that documented benefits of walkability including increased property values, transit
use, obesity prevention, and emissions conservation. He outlined several pros of connectivity and
walkability: health benefits, economic benefits, environmental benefits, improved emergency
services access, enhanced safety, and better traffic flow when roads are connected. Spencer
presented a connectivity index used by Lehi City that measures links (street segments) versus nodes
(intersections or cul-de-sacs) to calculate a connectivity score. He showed examples from Herriman
neighborhoods with scores of 1.34 and 1.67, noting that a score of at least 1.8 would be desirable in
suburban settings. The Commission identified several problem areas in Herriman, including
commercial areas that are difficult to navigate as a pedestrian, particularly around Vasa Fitness;
residential areas with long blocks and limited connections; and a lack of pedestrian connections
between cul-de-sacs or trails. Recommendations for potential code amendments included block
length requirements, limitations on cul-de-sacs, requirements for pedestrian connections between
cul-de-sacs and arterial streets, connectivity score requirements, enhanced sidewalk and street
design standards, requirements for pedestrian islands at large crossings, and trail connections.
Appropriate exceptions for topography and other natural features were also considered. The
Commission was supportive of developing connectivity standards, with Commissioner Fenn stating
that while they can't change the past, they should be mindful of these principles in all future
residential and commercial plans. Manager Spencer mentioned that he would begin drafting an
ordinance to bring back to the Commission at a future meeting.
