The net result would be approximately $1.5 million available for appropriation at the beginning of

Council Member Hyland asked why Mr. Jurges would recommend going above 15% in a
challenging budget year. Mr. Jurges stated that 15% is the lowest he would recommend. Most
property tax revenue is received in November through January, which is in the middle of the budget
year, and sales taxes are received approximately 50 days in arrears. Although the funds are
technically commingled, retaining a 15% Fund balance would result in a low actual General Fund
balance between July and November. That could cause delays in purchasing new police and public
works vehicles, for example, if they were not financed. UFA is also paid in the first month of
every quarter. Mr. Gerber stated that Staff was not recommending 25% this year, but they would
be more comfortable if the City had a goal to reach that number within the next few years.

Council Member Hyland asked if it was an accounting cash-flow problem or a real cash-flow
problem. Mr. Jurges clarified that it is only an accounting problem if there is a reasonable number
of ongoing capital projects going on. Capital projects tend to be spread across budget years.
Typically, funding is received, then an engineering study is conducted to determine the scope of
work, and the money is not actually spent until the next budget year. In that case, the City can
borrow from itself and use that money to support other areas. However, utilizing those funds
would decrease the interest allocation to the General Fund. There was approximately $1.5 in
flexibility available. The City could legally go below 15%, but he was uncomfortable with that
option and would recommend delaying some expenditures until December or January to ensure
that mandatory costs are covered.

Council Member Hyland stated that $1.5 in flexibility was the difference between a 15% Fund
balance and the required 6%. Mr. Jurges clarified that the statutory requirement is 6% plus
compensated absences. There was some flexibility, but it would be inadvisable to use it in the
Budget.

                               operational costs. In recent years, the City benefited from unique and
                               temporary financial conditions. Federal recovery funds and two years

                               COVID-19 pandemic helped offset rising costs. A large portion of the
