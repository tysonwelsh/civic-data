       WOREL HAVAS FOR LITIGATION AGAINST THE MANUFACTURERS AND DISTRIBUTORS OF OPIOIDS.
        Bryan Baron, Deputy County Attorney, who sat in the proposal review committee, said that these firms were well
        qualified to represent the county. Matt McCune, of Magleby Cataxinos & Greenwood, stated that there is a contingency

    8. CONTRACT WITH LSI BUSINESS DEVELOPMENT, INC. TO ASSIST IN INTERGENERATIONAL POVERTY EFFORTS.
      Marlin Jensen, Weber County Intergenerational Poverty (IGP) Advisory Committee Chair, gave a presentation and stated
      that Lt. Governor Cox had held a rally to initiate work related to IGP at the county level. Weber County, along with eight
      others, was selected to create its own Welfare Reform Commission (Reform Commission) about 1½ years ago that
      includes members from Workforce Services, Weber-Morgan Health Department, Weber Human Services, and
      superintendents of both school districts. The pilot program is to begin with about 50 families and there are about 175
      poverty-serving resource organizations in the county, but they often are quite siloed, and the county can provide
      leadership. This work is at a point that they cannot move forward without some professional facilitating, thus this
      proposed contract. Commissioner Gibson wants to avoid some of the failures of previous consulting contracts. He
      expressed confidence in LSI but is concerned that the contract doesn’t specify who will administer the contract on a day
      to day basis. Mr. Baron indicated that the contract doesn’t need to specify as the commissioners can decide
      administratively who should be responsible for the contract. Mr. Jensen said that the county could give LSI direction
      through the Reform Commission, that this contract could be administered by said Commission, and there has been
      discussion about the county formalizing that Commission (which is chaired by a Weber County commissioner), and the
      advisory committee. Reform Commission and advisory committee quarterly meetings are a requirement, facilitated by
      LSI. Commissioner Gibson expressed other concerns with specific items in the contract and Mr. Jensen, Julie Fisher, of
      LSI, and Bryan Baron, Deputy County Attorney, provided input and suggested amendments. LSI will also take the
      primary role of writing grants and secure funding. LSI will pursue other funding opportunities to support IGP and will
      receive the additional funding budgeted for administrative costs. LSI had requested the contract’s effective date to be
      April 1, 2018 and Chair Harvey concurred; LSI has done some work already. Commissioner Gibson requested that the
      contract have a 1-year term with the option to renew annually for a maximum of three years. He also expressed concern
      over a provision regarding additional opportunities to contract. Mr. Baron explained that the county was not obligated
      to hire LSI for work outside of the IGP scope. Ms. Fisher agrees with LSI reporting to the county quarterly. The hourly
      rate increased from $100 to $125, which Ms. Fisher said is a much reduced rate. Regarding travel-related expenses,
      Commissioner Gibson wanted a structure to vet this. This contract was tabled for amending and review by the parties.
