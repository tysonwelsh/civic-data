38          Code Enforcement and Law Enforcement Discussion
39
40   Mayor Staggs asked David Brickey, City Manager, to lead the discussion.
41
42   Mr. Brickey explained the difference in civil code enforcement issues versus criminal. He said
43   that civil cases seek compliance rather than imposing a fine or a sentence. The City currently
44   has only two officers taking care of civil issues. One officer is handling animal cases and one
45   officer is handling code cases. He may ask for an additional officer to help with civil issues. Mr.
46   Brickey asked Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, to discuss the code
47   enforcement side and Ryan Carter, City Attorney, to discuss the criminal side.
48
49   Councilmember Wells said that at least two residents are in attendance that are concerned
50   about parking issues in the City. He said the prosecutor and the judge are throwing out parking
     Page | 2 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1   cases. Mr. Wells talked to code enforcement in Draper and Draper’s code enforcement is much
 2   different. He would like the City to adopt an ordinance that can be enforced.
 3
 4   Mr. Brickey explained that issuing parking tickets is difficult because of property rights. He said
 5   prior councils have had strong opinions about strict liability crimes and did not want citations
 6   issued unless police saw the crime in progress. If that is no longer the position of the Council,
 7   he asked the Council to make this known.
 8
 9   Councilmember Wells said the specific issue he is concerned about is a bailer attached to a
10   vehicle that is parked across the sidewalk and no citation has been issued. The City needs to
11   do something because citizens have a right to use the sidewalk. He would like the ordinance to
12   be changed so the City can enforce parking problems.
13
14   Mayor Staggs agreed that code enforcement might need an additional officer to help with code
15   and animal cases.
16
17   Councilmember Stewart asked what prior council gave the direction about strict liability crimes.
18   Mr. Brickey said this was a generalization that was shared with him. Councilmember Stewart
19   said he thought parking problems were being enforced on public streets, however, the judicial
20   branch refused to enforce.
21
22   Mayor Staggs said that in years past, code enforcement has been “complaint driven.” The
23   Council would like certain violations enforced swiftly.
24
25   Councilmember McDougal asked the departments to explain the current code before the
26   Council gets into specific cases.
27
28   Mr. Carter explained there are a variety of issues to consider with a parked vehicle. He believes
29   that a tractor is considered a motor vehicle and is subject to impound. It is not required that an
30   officer observe a vehicle for 48 hours but the officer could mark the tires to know whether the
31   vehicle is there for more than 48 hours. There are instances where no parking signs are
32   displayed and if the officer does not observe who parked the vehicle in the no parking zone, the
33   case is thrown out because they cannot prove “beyond a reasonable doubt” who parked the
34   vehicle. Salt Lake City steps outside the criminal area of the law and uses a different court
35   system to enforce parking. The City prosecutor’s office in years past has put together
36   ordinances that make it a civil penalty to park and these ordinances have never been adopted.
37   These have not been pushed forward since Mayor Staggs was elected Mayor.
38
39   Mayor Staggs asked if civil cases would be heard in justice court. Mr. Carter said it would be
40   heard in the justice court but set at different times and days from the criminal cases. Mr. Carter
41   said the City needs to determine how much they would like to invest in these types of cases. In
42   regards to parking tickets in the justice court, some citizens pay these tickets, however, if they
43   decide to fight it then the City dismisses it because they cannot prove beyond a reasonable
44   doubt that the owner parked the vehicle.
45
46   Mayor Staggs said that maintenance of property and animal bites are also civil issues. Mr.
47   Brickey said that dog bites are criminal matters whereas a dog at large would be a civil issue.
48   He explained that an animal license cannot be taken until there are three incidents. He believes
49   that after one bite, the animal license should be revoked. Mayor Staggs, Councilmember
50   McDougal, and Councilmember Buroker all agreed with Mr. Brickey. Councilmember McCay
51   said she agrees if it is severe enough.

 1            VECC Update
 2
 3   Scott Ruf, Executive Director of VECC Agencies, was hired by VECC two and a half years ago.
 4   He said that staffing and the job market has made service very difficult.
 5
 6   Mayor Staggs explained that VECC stands for Valley Emergency Communications Center
 7   which dispatches incoming emergency calls for both police and fire. Mayor Staggs expressed
 8   concern about Riverton’s participation jumping from $80,000 to $260,000. He asked Mr. Ruf
 9   what the City can expect going forward.
10
11   Mr. Ruf said he looked at the budget as soon as he became the Executive Director of VECC
12   and realized there was a $3 million shortfall. He immediately made a proposal to the Board of
13   Trustees. In fiscal 2020, the Board approved a 12% increase to all agencies except for Unified
14   Police which was -5%.
15
16   Mr. Ruf said in preparation for fiscal 2021, they met with chiefs and the board, created a finance
17   and audit committee, and looked at the member assessments. In looking at the assessment
18   models, they realized the UPD was being charged at a higher rate than Riverton or Herriman.
19   In order to settle the budget, there were large increases. In fiscal year 2022 with the model in
20   place, there were increases because of compensation, health insurance, changes to Versaterm,
21   and dispatch systems. For fiscal year 2022, there was an 8.8% increase overall, including
22   pass-through for shared service agreements. For fiscal year 2023, there is a 5% increase
23   overall.
24
25   Mr. Ruf explained the legislature had a giant spotlight on VECC for their performance and they
26   have been able to turn that around and focus on emergency services. They have made
27   significant improvements, have a positive balance and hope to have a 10% reserve by 2025.
28
29   Mayor Staggs said he would like to see the formula that VECC uses to determine fees. He
30   expressed concern over VECC using the number of police officers to determine the cost and
31   would prefer that the number of calls be used to determine the cost.
32
33   Councilmember Stewart would also like to see the formula. He doesn’t believe the numbers
34   add up and feels like all of the other agencies are subsidizing Unified Police Department. He
35   believes Riverton is one of the lowest call volumes in the valley and one of the lower crime
36   areas in the valley. He doesn’t understand why Riverton had a 300% increase.
37
38   Chief Hutson said there has been a lot of debate about the formula. However, a dispatcher
39   must sit at the phone no matter how many calls come in. The formula is a 50/50 split between
40   sworn officers and calls for service. When Mr. Ruf said there was a 12% increase, he was
41   referring to the entire VECC budget. If you take the assessment portion and apply the formula
42   then some agencies go up and some go down.
43
44   Councilmember Stewart does not like the model and feels that paying $265,000 for VECC
45   seems out of balance.
46
47   Mayor Staggs would like to receive the model to understand better how the fees are assessed.
48   He expressed concern over dispatch times and feels like Riverton is forced by statute to be part
49   of VECC. He is encouraged that VECC is taking steps to ensure there are no significant
50   increases in the future.

50   Mr. Lethbridge explained this was a public hearing for a Development Agreement. Riverton
51   City is proposing approval of a Development Agreement establishing requirements, standards,
     Page | 8 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1   and parameters for development of approximately 5.5 acres located at 13265 S Lovers Lane,
 2   allowing for subdivision of that property at lot sizes consistent with the current zoning. Utah
 3   State Code allows for the adoption of a Development Agreement which allows for and facilitates
 4   development of property, provided such an agreement is adopted in a manner consistent with a
 5   zoning or ordinance amendment. The property is currently zoned RR-22, a single-family
 6   residential zone, with half-acre lots and animal rights. The General Plan designation for the
 7   area is Estate Density Residential.
 8
 9   Mr. Carter explained that a development agreement can be used to design a property in the
10   same way that a comprehensive set of ordinances would apply to this specific property. The
11   legislature has said that if the development agreement creates distinctions from the way the
12   code would apply to the property then the City must go through the same process as it would for
13   a change to the ordinance. The unique standards that apply to this property are “not much.”
14   The private lane width standards are smaller in some areas. The developer would also like to
15   develop in phases. Because phase 1 is only adding one more lot then he will not be required to
16   asphalt the private road. When phase 2 is developed, he will need to asphalt the private lane
17   completely.
18
19   Councilmember Wells asked about access roads in and around the development and about any
20   litigation claims with Mr. Olson. Mr. Carter explained the different access roads that could be
21   potentially in and around the development and said this development agreement will resolve the
22   litigation matter.
