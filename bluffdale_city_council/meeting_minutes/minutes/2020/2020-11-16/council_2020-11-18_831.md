Mr. Holmes reported that they have been working with Messrs. Reid and Paddock since late 2018.
They developed a needs assessment that identified the goals and needs of the Public Works
Department as well as ways to facilitate the current and future needs of residents and businesses.
They also addressed future staffing requirements and identified project goals including current and
future growth, supporting internal operational growth, providing adequate workspaces for staff,
supporting future municipal services, and expanding parks and recreational services.

Mr. Holmes reported that their main goal is to protect the City’s investment in equipment for
services the Public Works Department provides. They studied fleet maintenance in other
municipalities and found that with proper care the life of the equipment was significantly extended.

Mr. Holmes stated that renewable energy sources will also be provided similar to those being
utilized at the Fire Station. Staff planned to have the systems either come online with the
construction of the facility or at some point in the future. Another goal was to minimize public
disruptions since the project may be near residential areas.

Mr. Holmes explained that identifying spaces for each department involves allocating where to
provide flexible spaces for staff, who may be short-term or seasonal. Areas were also sized to not
only reflect an efficient floor plan and building design but related to the cost of the project.

Mr. Holmes confirmed that previous site surveys were conducted including one on a property near
Fire Station Number 2 north of the Industrial Park. The property did not seem to be available for
purchase at that time. They also evaluated the Hatch property but it ultimately fell through. They
were currently looking at a property on 13800 South. Mr. Holmes noted that all the needed utilities
are located nearby. Buffer zones were also being created to limit interaction with nearby residents.

Mr. Holmes presented an example of a draft summary and stated that the total building gross area
is just under 40,000 square feet. The site element and baseline parking requirements were also put
forward. The proposed fleet area included tool storage, a heavy-duty maintenance bay, a Fleet
Manager’s office, and a vehicle wash bay. The wash bay was an important aspect of extending
the lifespan of City equipment. Wash equipment would be accessible by other City staff.
Mr. Holmes explained that keeping salt off of undercarriages will greatly increase the longevity of
equipment. Other unique characteristics of the spaces will be updated and added as the design
process continues.

Mr. Holmes reported that the test fit was done in May at which time they divided the project into
two phases to meet the current and future needs. Included were common administrative office and
staff support spaces, locker rooms, offices, and a multi-purpose training room. The training room
was to consist of two spaces that can be divided to accommodate multiple meetings. Auxiliary
storage space was provided on the south end.

The adjacent area to the west will be for fleet operations for internal Public Works operations or
City staff. Vehicle storage was provided south of the administrative space. Equipment will be

4.     PUBLIC HEARING – Consideration and Vote on Ordinance Number 2020-27, a
       City-Initiated Text Amendment Regarding Business Licensing and Home
       Occupations. Staff Presenter, Todd Sheeran.

City Attorney, Todd Sheeran reported that drafting the ordinance has been a long process. He
explained that cities have regulations to provide services such as streets, law enforcement, and
utilities. There are also zoning regulations that distinguish what uses are appropriate next to other
uses and what uses are not appropriate. When a request is submitted, it must be consistent with
the General Plan. A table was prepared showing uses that are permitted, conditional, accessory,
and not permitted.

In preparing the draft, numerous meetings were held with staff, the public, the City Council, and
the Planning Commission. Hundreds of hours were spent creating the ordinance. Mr. Sheeran
explained that a home occupation involves the use of a dwelling or accessory building for
commercial activities.

A Council Member helped draft the Purpose of the Home Occupation, which is to give Bluffdale
residents the opportunity to operate home occupations as an accessory use when they are
compatible with the neighborhoods in which they are located. The desire was to encourage home
occupations as an appropriate form of economic and community development by guiding business
activities that are not compatible with neighborhoods to appropriate commercial zones. In
addition, the intent was to protect the peace, quiet, and safety within all residential neighborhoods
by minimizing adverse impacts from businesses.

Mr. Sheeran explained that the desire is not to have car wraps in front of every home occupation
advertising the business. Some advertising is permitted including a four-square foot sign placed
on vehicles. Deliveries would be restricted to hour daytime hours. Hours of operation were
discussed. Staff felt that between the hours of 7:00 a.m. and 10:00 p.m. was reasonable.

With regard to outdoor storage, equipment must be running and enclosed. Yard space may be
utilized for the business. Clarification was given for operations versus storage. The yard space
would be part of the business operation.

Mr. Sheeran explained that the current ordinance includes minor and major home occupations.
Under State law, cities cannot charge a fee to a minor home occupation. It was proposed that a
Business Licensing Committee be established to review special exceptions. The committee would
include representation from Business Licensing, Planning, Engineering, Legal, and a
representative from the Fire Department. They will look at the property as a whole, ensure that it
fits within the neighborhood, and identify road widths and traffic impacts.

Mr. Sheeran reviewed each specific type of regulation for home occupations showing what is
included in the current ordinance, what is proposed to be changed, and what other cities are doing.
In terms of traffic, the Code specifies that a minor home occupation specifies there shall be no
excess traffic, which is difficult to interpret. A major home occupation would allow eight vehicles
to come to the home each day and would include any type of vehicle. Additional vehicles could
also be provided for by creating two categories consisting of 12 vehicles and 17 vehicles.

A Conditional Use Permit can be applied for that allows for up to 24 trips. Mr. Sheeran reported
that Draper City is very limited in terms of their home occupation, but their Permitted and
Conditional Use Permits allow for no excess traffic. Sandy City allows two vehicles to come to a
home per hour and up to 16 vehicles per day. That number can be increased to 24 through a
Conditional Use Permit.

With regard to parking, the current ordinance specifies that there shall be no trailers or heavy
equipment. A major home occupation prohibits on-street employee parking. What is proposed
for minor and major home occupations is off-street parking. A special exception can be sought to
allow parking on the street but would be limited and cannot impede vehicular traffic. In addition,
the street must be at least 28 feet wide and there must be enough room for fire truck access.

The issue of employees was next discussed. Ms. Sheeran explained that under the current
ordinance, a minor home occupation does not allow employees to work in the home. A major
home occupation allows one employee with the Planning Commission authorized to increase that.
No guidance was provided in terms of what the Planning Commission should consider. He did
not like the ambiguity. It was noted that a special exception would allow for up to four employees.

With regard to customers, a minor home occupation would not allow customers to come to the
home. However, three or fewer with a Childcare use qualifies as a minor home occupation. With
Childcare, four to 12 children are allowed. With a use other than Childcare, up to three clients are
allowed per day. The third category was classified as a Seasonal Group Child Activity that would
involve group lessons to be taught to children from their home. Up to 16 children per day are
allowed with eight at one time. It was proposed that that amount be increased. With childcare, up
to 16 per day are allowed and 12 at a time. In addition, the Seasonal Group Child Activity use
allows up to 24 clients per day and 12 at a time. The current ordinance only allows 12 per day.

In response to a question raised, Mr. Sheeran stated that a definition should be provided for
Preschool using the State’s definition. The regulations of neighboring cities were discussed.

In terms of vehicles, the current ordinance allows for one business vehicle for minor and major
home occupations. Mr. Sheeran proposed that that be increased to two for a major home
occupation. He noted that there are additional regulations regarding what is classified as a business
vehicle. There would also be length and weight restrictions. A special exception could be applied
for that would allow up to three business vehicles.

With regard to animals, the current home occupation ordinance does not allow for animals. Under
the proposed ordinance for a minor home occupation, no Animal Grooming or Training uses would
be allowed. This was because animals create noise. A major home occupation could be applied
for where up to five animals per day would be allowed for Training or Grooming with two at a
time. Increasing the number of animals that can be on-site at any given time would decrease the
amount of traffic, which is important. A special exception could be applied for that would allow
for 10 animals per day. Five animals at a time would be allowed for Training or three for
Grooming.

The area of the home was next addressed. Mr. Sheeran stated that the intent is to maintain the
residential character of the home. To do this, they will regulate the amount of space dedicated to
a commercial business in a residential home. The current ordinance allows for up to 25%. That
percentage was being reduced for a minor home occupation but provides the ability to utilize 10%
of the garage and 25% of a detached building. For a major home occupation, those amounts were
increased. A special exception would increase the area of the home to 35%. The garage would
remain at less than 50% since the desire was for people to park in the garage. They were also
proposing that an accessory building be completely used for home occupations.

Mayor Timothy’s preference was to have the business take place in a building rather than outside.
Mr. Sheeran explained that they are allowing for an accessory building to be completely used with
a special exception. Mayor Timothy suggested there be no differentiation between minor and
major home occupations if there is no impact on the neighbors. Mr. Sheeran stated that the
definition of a minor home occupation specifies that it does not have an impact on the neighbors
and the activity taking place will be noticeable to the neighbors. With a major home occupation,
there may be some activity, but the impact would not be significant. Another distinction was that
with respect to fees, Utah State Code requires that if a use fits within a minor home occupation,
that the City not charge a fee. A fee would be associated with a major home occupation.

With a special exception, there may be some impact on the neighbors. Mr. Kartchner explained
that the intent of the special exception was to allow for a specific plan to deal with the impact.
Potential impacts on neighbors were discussed as well as individual circumstances.

Potential prohibited and undesirable uses were identified. Mark Hales asked for further
clarification of storage uses. Traci Crockett asked about tattoo parlors and saw no reason for them
to be prohibited if they are properly licensed. Mr. Sheeran stated that tattoo parlors are restricted
in other cities and the list was based on what other cities are doing. Council Member Crockett
considered the ordinance to be outdated.

5.     PUBLIC HEARING – Consideration and Vote on Ordinance Number 2020-28,
       Adopting an Amended Consolidated Fee Schedule for Administrative, Service, and
       Development Fees Charged by the City of Bluffdale. Staff Presenter, Bruce
       Kartchner.

Mr. Kartchner reported that in going reviewing the New Business Ordinance, staff noticed that
changes needed to be made to the Business License Fee sections of the Consolidated Fee Schedule.
When the State initially determined that minor home occupations would not be charged for a
Business License, they did not provide clarity. As a result, Bluffdale and several other cities
decided to charge an Application Fee rather than a Business License Fee. The law was later
changed by the State to eliminate minor home occupations from being charged anything. Since
then, the City has included the $50 base fee application as part of the fee schedule rather than
continuing to charge a separate fee. The amount shown in the packet excluded hotels and motels
from that additional fee. The change made was highlighted.

Mr. Kartchner clarified the proposed changes and stated that a $150 charge was added per
exception requested fee. The City is required to evaluate the fees and ensure that they are
appropriate for the services provided to the business. He observed that there is a $6 per employee
fee being charged to businesses with a large number of employees, which seemed counter to what
they were trying to accomplish by encouraging businesses to come to the City. Because the City
was not providing any service related to the additional employees, it was stricken.

Mayor Timothy opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public hearing
was closed.

Dave Kallas moved to approve Ordinance 2020-28, adopting an Amended Consolidated Fee
Schedule for Administrative, Service, and Development fees charged by the City. Wendy
Aston seconded the motion. Vote on motion: Dave Kallas-Aye, Mark Hales-Aye, Wendy
Aston-Aye, Jeff Gaston-Aye, Traci Crockett-Aye. The motion passed unanimously.
