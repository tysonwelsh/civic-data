   2.2. Herriman Preserve Project Overview (Laguna/Malibu) - Blake Thomas, Community
   Development Director
   Community Development Director Blake Thomas Presented the Proposed Herriman Preserve
   Development, formally known as the Laguna/Malibu Property. He then displayed a map of the project
   location in the Southeast portion of the city, approximately 970 acres, extending from Juniper Crest to
   Camp Williams. He pointed out three properties he would be referring to and roughly what their sizes
   were: Laguna Point (80acres), Laguna (546 acres) and Malibu (269 acres). He passed out maps amongst
   the Council and Commission to indicate the current General Plan and Zoning for the properties. Zoning
   is what allows for development right now and the General plan is the guide. He explained a rezone
   would be necessary if anything were to change. He then read from the code the purpose of the Hillside
   overlay Zone, which is “To Protect the natural and scenic environment of the southern part of the city.
   Its intent is to assist the development of steep slopes in the south mountain area to become desirable
   and a safe place to live and recreate. The city intends to preserve as much of the existing natural beauty
   as is feasible and still allow for compatible development. The natural resources should be taken
   advantage of without significantly detracting from the enjoyment of the hillside area.” He stated that
   with that purpose the overlay does restrict the development of the property. He then explained the
   tables on the exhibit, amongst the information handed out, show the developable area outside of the
   thirty percent overlay, then inquired if the council/commission had any questions up to that point. With
   no further questions he displayed a map of the proposed land use that was included in the application
   for the community master plan and explained the current working group meetings in which they have
   addressed questions and problems for the draft master plan.
   Director Thomas informed the council and commission the staff and developer were inquiring feedback
   on whether to allow development in the Hillside Overlay and if so to what extent, and to keep that in
   mind as they continue with the presentation. With that he continued, the largest component in the
   project would be open space and trails as he displayed an open space plan for where those parks and
   trails could be located as well as visual of the Road Cross Sections. He then detailed the bus stop concept
   discussed with the School District.
   Continuing he described three different trail cross sections as trails have become a large part of the
   community. He showed some sample lot layouts that the developers had prepared, what each of them
   looked like and why. While viewing these layouts Director Thomas spoke of a specific property, with
   access to Juniper Crest, explaining it was held back from developing while they were waiting for a water

Plan. He ended with a slide on proposed development. He Briefed over some discussions from the
MDA meeting’s, the most recent was regarding DRC Concept. A couple options discussed were: First,
the Developer having their own DRC, a staff member would be placed on that DRC, design guidelines

Mayor Palmer thanked Director Thomas and asked for feedback from the rest of the council and

Commissioner Jacobson offered his opinion of there should be as much public open meetings as
possible, preferring the traditional way of going through planning commission
Commissioner Berbert questioned where the Buffer zone was and asked about grant money provided
for a buffer. Director Thomas clarified there was a 100-foot buffer being proposed, but there was no
existing buffer, there was ACUB funding but there needs to be a willing seller. Attorney Andrizzi made
note Camp Williams was their development partner, but there were also property owner interests as
well. The City cannot decide what they do with their property if they’re within the zoning rights they
have and or the development agreement negotiated. The Developer and the camp would need to discuss
how to work out that buffer.

agreed with Commissioner Jacobson to keep open public meetings to allow transparency. Commissioner

Berbert asked if there needed to be a fire station up top. Councilmember Ohrn asked if it would be the
same with police. Councilmember Hodges responded they did discuss the response time for emergency
personal to the city and a possible special service area for the city.
Commissioner Ferguson referred to the previous DRC discussion, asking if each single-family home
would come back to the commission if they did not have a DRC. Director Thomas responded it would
be the overall development first and then depending on the MDA it could be by pod or property.
They all moved on to a discussion regarding the Hillside overlay. Commissioner Berbert felt they should
keep it saying the Hillside overlay was intended to protect the native landscape. Commissioner Powell
asked if it would be adjusted to meet the thirty percent since it was currently based on assumption.
Director Thomas confirmed there would be a survey done to identify a new survey overlay line.
Commissioner Jacobson brought up concern with the highest density being placed on the overlay zone.

2.3. Discussion of a commercial development proposal on ±20.3 acres located approximately at
15101 S Mountain View Corridor in the C-2 Zone - Michael Maloy, City Planner
City Planner Michael Maloy identified the vacant property in question and where it was located. He
briefed over potential uses but do not fit in the current C2 Zone. He explained they were looking at
two design options and the difference in design two was a potential hotel. Other sites have attracted
interest for hotel users because of this there was concern regarding a hotel site, making design option
March 30, 2022 – Joint City Council/Planning Commission Minutes                            Page 5 of 7


one more suitable. The C2 Zone has requirements for forty percent brick or stone which has been a
challenge as well as the requirement that the uses cannot include wholesale as a primary use. The
development partner is more interested in a M-1 rezone, however the general plan for that area does
not encourage that. Planner Maloy posed the question do they investigate making modifications to the
C2 Zone to help this potential project fit into the city or a rezone into a M-1. Commissioner Berbert
asked how much of the development, or all of it they are wanting to zone as M-1. Planner Maloy
responded this was just a high-level concept of all of it being M1 but, another option would be to do
part M-1 and part C-2. He stated neither is perfect. The question would be do they introduce wholesale
on property, or should it be, what would be allowed in the C2 zone. Councilmember Henderson
commented it would all depend on what they want there. They need more detail and a plan to decide.
City Planner Maloy responded they do realize this and have talked about providing tax revenue examples
for these uses. Councilmember Ohrn agreed with Councilmember Henderson they were open to ideas
to bring in tax revenue. Councilmember Hodges commented the examples of the C2 Zone shown in
the presentation are great designs. Planner Maloy mentioned they could to a planned development
overlay to work with the C2 zone, then bring it to the council for approval, which they can then modify
the standards based on specific proposals.
The Developer offered information regarding a hotel. The Council and Commission discussed the hotel
idea. Commissioner Berbert referred to the M-1 verses C2 discussion stating he didn’t see a lot of
concern on architecture or design and posed the question what the better route would be with what
they have been shown so far. Councilmember Hodges questioned if Porter Rockwell was an M-1 zone.
Planner Maloy confirmed it was. Commissioner Berbert expressed his concern with all of it being
changed to the M-1 zone were the long-term effects, preferring part M-1 and part C-2. Commissioner
Ferguson offered an example, from experience, of a volleyball space not working out after a few years
then being converted into office space. He then posed the question if the space is zoned as M-1 what it
would then convert to. Councilmember Hodges commented it was a good idea to figure out the
secondary uses, but it can’t be fixed with just a zoning tool. The Developer mentioned they would be
happy if a portion was zoned for flex space, then added they would like to avoid unnecessary congestion.
Planner Maloy thanked everyone as they had gotten good feedback. Mayor Palmer added there may be
other potential hotel sites in the city as well. Councilmember Henderson commented a hotel would be
best thing for taxes.

2.4. Discussion and prioritization of pending Title 10 Land Development Code amendments -
Michael Maloy, City Planner
City Planner Michael Maloy introduced the current Legislative Requests. He stated the Auto mall
removed a request for the general plan and zoning amendment and mentioned he was unsure if they
would be focusing on commercial. Teton commercial, on 11800 South by Herriman high, is underway
and haven’t yet submitted a formal application. He then moved along to potential amendments
discussed internally and invited discussion from the commission and council on any current concerns
or what they would like staff to research. Planner Maloy referred to last year when the Utah State
Legislature took away regulation on Single family homes with such things as architecture, appearance,
and materials. Although, if a development partner enters into a negotiated agreement, they could
March 30, 2022 – Joint City Council/Planning Commission Minutes                              Page 6 of 7


reintroduce regulating design and materials. Currently the planned development overlay requires a
minimum of fifteen acres. If they were willing to lower the acre size, they could do more projects and
design. Councilmember Henderson questioned if there were any other benefits besides design standards
as he did not like that idea because of their previous decision. Planner Maloy responded with just Overall
design. Councilmember Ohrn did not like the idea as they would have to give too much for what they
get in return.
Planner Maloy then commented that if the were to tie a planned development to commercial property
the planned development currently requires twenty percent open space. However, in a commercial
setting they would not want Twenty percent to be open space. He questioned if they could discuss an
amendment with commercial spaces or taking out the requirement for commercial. Commissioner
Berbert agreed with eliminating the requirement if there is walkability layout for all commercial.
Planner Maloy referred to a previous comment regarding the push back from development partners on
brick and stone standards which had caused concern over losing commercial projects. He questioned if
it was time to revisit that standard to recommit or get rid of it. Councilmember Henderson mentioned
that was put in place to make it look different as everything looked the same. Commissioner Jacobson
commented the real intent may have just been better quality. The Council and Commission debated on
what standard they were trying to establish. Commissioner Jacobson liked the standard that they have,
and the good quality would help ensure the developer does a good job and the business will be sound.
Councilmember Henderson agreed having a higher standard would give them a starting point to have
something good. Councilmember Hodges agreed they need a baseline but, if they are losing business
because of the standard it may need to change. Councilmember Ohrn commented brick or stone doesn’t
matter it is more about quality. Councilmember Hodges suggested the developer prove the quality of
the materials to move forward. Commissioner Berbert agreed the material should be equal or better
quality than the standard. Commissioner Jacobson agreed the developer should prove the better-quality
material. More discussion ensued on the topic. Commissioner Jacobson summarized the consensus
amongst the commission and council seemed to be to keep the standard.
Planner Maloy moved along to the next topic of Potential Amendments. He offered some brief
administrative updates which included: Code enforcement, business licensing, zoning interpretation, and
state code compliance. He informed the group; Planning Commission would be having a public hearing
to change the names of the agricultural and residential zones to be more consistent. He also talked about
the discussion of changing the density of the A-.25 zone.
