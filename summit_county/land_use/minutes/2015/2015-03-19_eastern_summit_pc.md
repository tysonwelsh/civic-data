Chair Brown said that since this is a work session, no formal action will be taken.  He turned

Director Putt said in going through this mapping exercise, they have applied four standards to 
determine where they will potentially apply the zoning districts.  In most situations it is 
impossible to have every property meet all of these standards.     
 
1. What are the existing parcel sizes? 
2. Does the area have an existing development pattern?   
The zoning should reflect what is already there.  They don’t want to create new non-
conforming parcels.     
3. Do the parcels in areas have a property size pattern?   
4. Will this help to eliminate the non-conforming lots?   
Many parcels located in the AP-40 zone are less than 40 acres.  60% to 75% of the 
properties are non-conforming.     
 
Director Putt said they have considered the local municipalities and their long range zoning 
plans.  He explained the State requires municipalities to have annexation declaration areas 
(ADA) to accommodate future growth.  These are areas that may or may not come into the 
cities at some point.  Until they are taken into a city’s jurisdiction, they are still part of the 
unincorporated County.  The County doesn’t want to propose uses that are radically different 
than the vision of the municipalities.   The County doesn’t want to create infrastructure 
problems.   
 
The physical characteristics of the properties are another area they are looking at.  Steep 
hillsides, wetlands, and soil instability is being considered.  Additionally, what is the existing 
utility situation?  Is water, sewer, or electrical available?  Are there areas that are being 
targeted for economic development?     
 
Director Putt said the maps are a work in progress: they don’t represent the final decision.  
When the base zoning is completed, the story isn’t finished.  They want to propose to have an 
additional list of possible rezones.  These are zones that could be applied for at a later time.

place.  They don’t think the existing Code provides a reasonable set of opportunities for

Commissioner Clyde said if they go to densities other than a 1-to-1 or a 1-to-6, it will

Chair Brown said it would be a totally different process than what occurs now.  Currently, 
the SPA doesn’t have to do with infrastructure; it has to do with money.  The criteria for a 
SPA is so vague, they have never been able to approve one.  With the rezone, the criteria will 
be established in the Code.  If they discover that it isn’t working, they won’t create internal 
policies to make it work.  They will hold more public hearings to get input from the public.   
 
Director Putt said the SPA process doesn’t establish a minimum or maximum density.  The 
zones they are working on will have something that can be measured.  There will be a 
maximum amount that can be requested.  There will be specific things that would be required 
such as a road system, water, waste water, and utilities.  Everybody will know going into the

receive.

they need to be sure there is enough water available.  The quality of life can change with 
increased density.
