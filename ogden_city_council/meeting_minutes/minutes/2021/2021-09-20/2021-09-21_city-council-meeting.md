     Municipal Redistricting
                Every ten years the Council is required to engage in adjusting and approving new voting boundaries in Ogden City.
     According to Utah law, the City Council is required to approve boundaries for City Council districts, Ogden School Board
     districts, and Voter Participation Areas. Population data is provided by Weber County. Due to issues with the Census Bureau, data
     was not transmitted in a timely manner, and the County/City have limited time to compile information and establish voting
     districts.
                Council Executive Director Eller-Smith used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to facilitate discussion among the
     Council regarding municipal redistricting. There are several different terms used relative to this type of action, including:
                    •    Voter participation area- related to referendum law;
                    •    Voter precinct- established by the County, must have less than 1,250 active voters;
                    •    School Board voting districts - seven districts, separate from municipal districts, equal in population, as
                         contiguous and compact as practicable; and
                    •    Municipal voting districts - four districts, separate from School Board districts, equal in population, as
                         contiguous and compact as practicable.
                There are different deadlines for adopting each; voter participation areas must be adopted by January 1, 2022. School
     Board voting districts must be adopted 60 days before candidate filing deadline, which is March 17, 2022. The four Municipal
     voting districts must be adopted six months after the State legislature is fmished with its redistricting process, which was done
     November 15, 2021; the deadline is approximately May 15,2022, but Council staff recommends action be taken by April30,
     2022, to avoid budget season conflicts. There are certain limitations for Municipal and School DistJ:ict voting districts; they cannot
     cross state established district lines, including those for federal and state districts. Districts must also be equal in population, must
     be contiguous, and must be as compact as practicable. Ms. Eller-Smith then discussed historical changes in Ogden City
     government that have influenced Municipal district boundaries, after which she and Weber County Election Official Ryan Cowley
     reviewed historical district maps to orient the Council to the changes that have been made to municipal districts over the year. Ms.
     Eller-Smith then noted Council staff needs direction from the Council on how they wish to proceed with municipal redistricting.
     Relative to municipal districts, the Council detennined it acceptable for the County to prepare the proposed district map for fmal
     consideration and action by the City Council. Ms. Eller-Smith asked the Council if they would like to create a sub-committee to
     work on voter participation areas, which must be adopted the soonest- prior to January 1, 2022. The sub-committee would make
     a recommendation to the entire Council for consideration and action. The Council debated the option, ultimately concluding to
     forego the creation of a sub-committee and directing Council staff to analyze the proposal prepared by Weber County before
     submitting it to the Council for consideration and action.

Ogden City Housing Strategy Summary
          Council Deputy Director Symes used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide background infonnation on the
subject of creating an Ogden City Housing Strategy; the Council has asked for the creation of a 'housing plan' and much work and
research has been done, dating back to 2019. Weber State University, along with other entities, have completed housing studies,
many of which focus on specific elements ofhousing. While there is no overall housing plan, City housing policies exist within
other policy documents, including:
               •    General Plan
               •    Community Plans, District Plans
               •    Quality Neighborhoods Initiative
               •   Five-Year Consolidated Plan and Annual Action Plans
               •   Redevelopment Agency
               •   MAKE Ogden Downtown Master Plan
               •    Zoning Ordinance
         Rather than develop something new, the idea was to summarize existing policy concepts in a single document; the
summary explains the documents and plans and identifies guiding Principles that emerge from the various documents. He
identified the following Ogden City Housing Strategy Summary elements:
               •   Executive Summary
               •   Purpose
               •   Housing in Ogden
                         o Ogden as a Built-out Community
                         o Population and Housing
                         o Housing in the Region
               •   Housing and Economic Development
               •   Housing Policy Documents and Plans
              •    Housing Policy Guiding Principles
         The following policy documents infonned the Ogden City Housing Strategy Summary:
              •    Involve Ogden General Plan
              •    Community Plans and District Plans
              •    Quality Neighborhoods Initiative
              •    Five-Year Consolidated Plan and Annual Action Plans
              •    Redevelopment Agency
              •    MAKE Ogden Downtown Master Plan
              •    Zoning Ordinance
         Guiding principles include improving the fmancial health of the City; establishing a balanced housing stock; creating
neighborhoods of choice; incentivizing reinvestment in the community; leveraging federal dollars; and maintaining quality
affordable housing. Mr. Symes expounded on each of the guiding principles, identifying language from various policy documents,
after which he facilitated discussion among the Council do determine if they are interested in pursuing further development of the
document, if the summary approach is helpful, what is missing, and if the Council wants to change current policy given that the
summary document reflects such policy. The Council generally expressed support for the summary document, but there was a
focus on opportunities to expand policies relating to affordable housing.

Employee Stipends/Bonuses
          The Council discussed options for amendments to Ogden Municipal Code § 2-6-9(G) relating to employee bonuses or
stipends. Council members want to ensure that bonuses are dish·ibuted to employees fairly, equitably, and transparently while still
providing the Mayor flexibility to reward or retain high performing employees.
          Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the City's current ordinance relating to employee classification and
pay plans; the current ordinance does not limit the bonus or stipend amount an employee may receive. Additionally, the terms
'stipend' and 'bonus' have been used interchangeably, causing some confusion. Bonuses or stipends were provided to nine
employees in calendar year 2020, this is 1.4 percent of all employees and this number does not include employees at the top of
their ranges who may have received a perfonnance bonus. Of those nine employee bonuses, three were awarded for additional
work during the legislative session, three were awarded for additional work related to COVID-19, one was awarded for serving in
a defacto acting position, and two were paid to retain valuable and highly skilled employees that had job offers from other entities.
The City's new compensation plan will not be fully implemented until Fiscal year (FY) 2023, leaving a few valuable employees
vulnerable to recruitment by other jurisdictions or the private sector. She noted the following considerations for the Council
relative to the subject or employee stipends or bonuses:
               1. How much flexibility should the Mayor have in awarding bonuses to employees?

              Council staff is proposing one option as a way to limit the bonus amount an employee may receive in any given year
    while still allowing the Mayor some flexibility in providing bonuses. The following sentence could be added to Municipal Code
    §2-6-8(G):
                       "The total of all performance-based bonuses or stipends received by an employee shall not exceed the
                       employee's salary range authorized by the pay plan by more than eight percent (8%) in any single fiscal year."
              The specific percentage will be detennined by the Council. The staff proposal also includes removing the word "annual"
    from Section (G) to allow a bonus to be awarded any time during the year. Ms. Eller-Smith facilitated discussion among the
    Council regarding the proposal; some Council members expressed the need for transparency pertaining to compensation
    infonnation, with others noting that it is not necessary to know the name of the employee receiving as stipend or bonus, but it is
    important to know the amount of the stipend or bonus. Discussion of the Council staff proposal continued, and the Council
    determined that performance-based bonuses or stipends should not exceed the employee's salary range authorized by the pay plan
    by more than 10 percent in any single year; if Administration is proposing a bonus or stipend greater than 10 percent, the matter
    must be discussed in a closed executive session. Ms. Eller-Smith stated she will work on an ordinance amendment for the Council
    to consider at a future meeting.

)            Boxing Program Discussion: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith reviewed the background of ongoing discussions
    pertaining to a boxing program in the City. Administration has expressed concern regarding the liabilities associated with a boxing
    program, but it may be possible to overcome those issues and if the program is to move forward, it is necessary for the Council to
    discuss the facility in which the program would be hosted, target participants for the program, and administration of the program.

    bags that can be used for training by those not participating in activity in the ring. Ms. Eller-Smith stated that the most difficult
    thing may be the process for selecting the contractors or group that will oversee the program. This led to discussion of matters
    such as cost of purchasing equipment for the program; cost for participation in the program; options for addressing liability
    associated with the program. Council member Lopez then noted that he hopes the Council and Administration will look for ways
    to make the program happen rather than placing roadblocks in the way; he feels if this were a program Administration was
    interested in, they would have found a way to implement it by now. Council member Lopez spoke to the importance of a boxing
    program for the community, after which Ms. Eller-Smith suggested that additional details of the program be discussed in an
    upcoming work session meeting.
