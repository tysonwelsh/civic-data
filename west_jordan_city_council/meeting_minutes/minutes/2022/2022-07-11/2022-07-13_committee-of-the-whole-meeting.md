 2. DISCUSSION
         a.   Station Area Planning

              Senior Planner Tayler Jensen stated Utah House Bill 462 (2022) required the completion
              of station area plans for one-half mile surrounding all fixed rail transit stations. Eight
              stations currently fell under that designation for West Jordan – six within West Jordan
              boundaries, and two outside City boundaries but within one-half mile (Midvale and
              South Jordan). Cities found to be in noncompliance with provisions of HB 462 on January
              1, 2026 would be ineligible for TIF and TTIF funds. Mr. Jensen said the goal of HB 462
              was to align State transportation funding with local plans, pair with SB 34 and the
              requirement that cities plan for moderate income housing, and maximize development
              potential in appropriate areas through a collaborative city-led planning approach. Mr.
              Jensen emphasized that station area plans would be tailored to each station.

              For each station area plan, cities must put forward a strategy to:
                 • Increase availability and affordability of housing;
                 • Promote sustainable environmental conditions;
                 • Enhance access to opportunities for residents; and
                 • Increase transportation choices and connections.

              Mr. Jensen reported that South Jordan representatives indicated a desire to work with
              West Jordan, but not until closer to the end of the year. He reported on two meetings with
              a Midvale representative regarding a joint combined station area plan for West Jordan
              City Center, Historic Gardner, and Bingham Junction. Mr. Jensen said Wasatch Front

             Mr. Jensen explained that each station area plan must include a vision, map, public
             involvement, 5-year implementation plan, and a statement promoting the four objectives
             listed above. He presented deadlines for adoption of station area plans. However, if a
             qualifying application were received, the City would have 12 months to complete a
             station area plan for that station. A qualifying application would: involve land within ½
             mile of a Trax Station; involve land that had not yet been station area planned; proposed
             development area would be greater than five contiguous acres; proposal would require
             the changing of the General Plan or require a Zoning Designation to be changed; propose
             a higher density than currently allowed; contain new residential units, at least 10% of
             which were designated for moderate income housing; and the applicant requested the
             initiation of a station area plan.

             Vice Chair Green said he wanted to be sure the West Jordan Council and the Midvale
             Council (and eventually the South Jordan Council) were working together so that all
             involved were in agreement and could support the station area plans.

        b.   Discussion on establishing a City-wide process for applicants to obtain monetary
             and non-monetary assistance
             During the budget process earlier in the year, the Council directed staff to schedule a
             discussion of fee waivers at a future work session. Council Office Director Alan Anderson
             reported the City did not currently have a formal process for fee waiver requests.
             Currently, when a fee waiver request was received, staff would prepare paperwork for a
             request to Council, and the Council would hold a public hearing and make a decision. Mr.
             Anderson presented five questions for the Council to consider:

             1) Mr. Anderson asked if the Council had a desire to grant fee waivers, and if so, with
             what requirements. Council Members Worthen and Pack said they were in favor of
             granting fee waivers to nonprofit entities as long as the entities provided a service or
             enhanced the community. Council Member McConnehey said he would be willing to
             consider granting fee waivers. Council Member Bloom said she believed it would be
             important to be able to evaluate requests on a case-by-case basis. Council Member Jacob
             said he did not see a need to change the current practice.

             Vice Chair Green said he would not want to restrict fee waivers to nonprofit entities, and
             mentioned the possibility of a neighborhood requesting a fee waiver for a neighborhood
             cleanup. Assistant City Attorney Jared Tingey said Code did not define “not for profit”,
             and said he believed there could be a valid legal argument that a neighborhood was not
             for profit. Vice Chair Green said he would be comfortable leaving the term “not for profit”
             in the ordinance if “not for profit” were clearly defined.

             Mr. Anderson said staff created a draft application form, and asked what requirements
             the Council would want included. Vice Chair Green suggested the form should include
             the question, “how does this benefit West Jordan?”. Chair Whitelock summarized that
             Council Members had suggested applications should be broad, and should be considered
             on a case-by-case basis.

             2) Mr. Anderson asked if the Council would support a process allowing the Mayor to
             waive fees, and if so, what the processes and limitations would be. Vice Chair Green said
             he would not support allowing the Mayor to waive fees. He said it was a budgetary issue
             belonging to the City Council. Vice Chair Green said he wanted to be very careful with
             staff time, and wanted the Council to keep track of all fee waivers.

             Chair Whitelock said she believed the same application and process should be available
             to everyone applying. Council Member McConnehey said he would be open to hearing
             suggestions from the Mayor’s Office. Assistant City Attorney Jared Tingey commented
             the City Council may end up receiving several $25 fee waiver requests in a single week,
             which was different from a $35,000 fee waiver request. Mr. Tingey expressed the opinion
             that fee waiver requests for small amounts could be handled in the office.

             City Administrator Korban Lee stated there was a de minimis amount at which the cost
             of staff time to process a request would exceed the amount requested to be waived. He
             suggested the Council could choose an amount below which authority could be delegated
             to the Council Chair, or whoever the Council chose. He said he did not like to see staff
             time wasted on $25 fee waivers. Vice Chair Green suggested Council leadership or a
             Council subcommittee could consider fee waiver requests below a certain value without
             a formal meeting. He repeated that he was not comfortable delegating legislative roles
             to the executive branch.

             Mr. Anderson commented that current Council Rules dictated that any fee waiver
             received by the Council would first show up on a Council agenda as a new business item,
             would be noticed as a public hearing, and would then be presented to the Council two
             weeks later as an agenda item with public hearing. The process would take about a
             month from the time an applicant submitted paperwork to when it would be presented
             in Council meeting. Mr. Tingey stated the Council could determine in open Council

             meeting a de minimis amount below which a public hearing would not be required for
             fee waiver consideration, as long as a public hearing for such determination were noticed
             and held.

             Mr. Lee said $25 was probably not a good example, but the Mayor’s Office frequently
             received requests to waive fees for use of City facilities, park pavilions, and
             neighborhood dumpsters. Mr. Anderson said 24-hour public noticing was required for a
             public hearing for a fee waiver. Mr. Tingey explained current staff involvement in the
             process. Chair Whitelock said she believed determining the dollar value of what would
             be provided to the City should be the responsibility of the applicant, not City staff. Mr.
             Anderson asked if the Council wanted staff to validate the numbers. Mr. Lee explained
             that some of the dollar value estimates did require staff involvement.

             Mr. Lee emphasized that administration had received fee waiver requests, but had not
             granted fee waivers. He said that in some situations private citizens, businesses, City
             employees, or elected officials had volunteered to pay the fees. Council Member
             McConnehey expressed the opinion that not having a straight forward process had, in
             some situations, given the impression the City was antagonistic against the requesting
             entities.

             Council Member Bloom asked if $500 would be a good threshold. Chair Whitelock said
             she would be comfortable with fee waivers for individual rooms, but not for larger
             venues such as Pioneer Hall.

             Council Member McConnehey suggested the application form should emphasize service
             in lieu of fees, said he would drop the “not for profit” language, and would start by having
             everything brought to the Council rather than trying to guess at what applications might
             be received. He said he anticipated that many of the procedural details could be figured
             out along the way.

             Council Member Jacob expressed the opinion that if the City had a fee that was so low it
             was frequently requested to be waived, and frequently waived, the fee should not be
             charged to begin with. He suggested establishing a clear standard to apply equally to all.

             Mr. Lee said rates to rent City Hall rooms ranged from $25 to $125 per hour. Rental rates
             for Pioneer Hall ranged from $250 to $450 per day. Council Member Bloom said she
             agreed with establishing a streamlined application process, with everything brought to
             the Council initially.

             City Attorney Rob Wall said he had seen councils in other cities approve a list of facilities
             that could be used for rental with an established fee, with the council office or mayor
             delegated the ability to waive fees up to a certain amount. The council would also have
             designated what service they recognized as appropriate consideration for waiver of the
             fee to avoid discrimination. He advised that discrimination based on the message of the
             applying entity could not occur.

             Mr. Anderson summarized that the Council wanted a simple form, with service in lieu of
             fees emphasized, and everything initially brought to the Council.
