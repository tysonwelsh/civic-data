Proposed Fiscal Year 2025 Annual Action Plan Amendment #1
          As a requirement to receive federal funding from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD), the
City’s Community and Economic Development Department prepares and submits to the Council what is known as a five-year
Consolidated Plan (ConPlan) and an Annual Action Plan. The Annual Action Plan (AAP) is used to outline how federal
Community Development Block Grant funds, HOME funds and other federal and non-federal program funds will be used by the
City in the upcoming fiscal year.
          Community Development Project Coordinator Fuentes explained the amendments proposed with this mid-year update
address the following:
              • Increase the Own in Ogden HOME budget by $250,000 with an increase to the goal for down payment
                   assistance from 25 to 50 for the year.
              • An overall proposed increase of $674,100 to the Annual Action Plan HUD Budget. This is due to actual carry-
                   over amounts, updated program income estimates, and allocation of the corresponding budget adjustments.
          Ms. Fuentes used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide a review of the ConPlan and AAP, as well as to
expound on the implications and purpose of proposed Amendment #1. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the
information packet for the meeting.
         There was a brief discussion among the Council and staff regarding the public input the City has received regarding
proposed Amendment #1 during the public comment period.

Proposed Fiscal Year 2024-2025 Budget Amendment – Annual Action Plan
Amendment #1
        Finance Director Sorensen explained this item is related to the previous agenda item; Administration is proposing an
amendment to the Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 City budget to reflect the budget changes adopted with the Fiscal Year 2024-2025
Annual Action Plan Amendment #1. The proposed budget amendment reflects changes resulting in an overall increase of
$674,100. He presented a spreadsheet to illustrate the specific changes – if the budget amendment is approved – to the different
programs listed in the Major Grants Fund of the City. He noted a public hearing can be scheduled for December 17, after which
Administration requests Council approval of the budget amendment.

Proposed Conveyance of Significant Parcels from Ogden City to Ogden
Redevelopment Agency (RDA) – 1820 Washington Boulevard; and
Proposed Land Transfer and Development Agreement with Parkway Group,
LLC.
          Economic Development Director Meess explained a multi-family residential and commercial development located at
approximately 18th Street and Washington Boulevard is being developed which is anticipated to include up to 110 units in two 4-
story residential buildings and a small commercial building where the Pioneer Heritage building (Piccadilly’s building) was
located. The overall project approvals have included a rezone to C-3/CO, a street vacation of River Drive east of Washington
Boulevard, both of which were approved by the Council on September 26, 2023, property sale and transfer from the City, RDA,
and Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT) to the Parkway Group, and a participation and incentive agreement which were
approved by the City and RDA on November 23, 2023. This specific action is for the Council to hold a public hearing on the
transfer of four parcels from Ogden City ownership to the Ogden City Redevelopment Agency to facilitate the larger transfer of
property to the Parkway Group, LLC. Three small remnant parcels and a portion of a larger parcel must be conveyed to the RDA
to fully execute the Land Transfer and Development Agreement. Because these properties are within 100 feet of the Ogden River,
these properties constitute a “significant parcel” under Ogden City code. A public hearing is required to be held but there is no
action needed by the Council. The Mayor, as manager of real property, may convey property in compliance with City Code
without the approval of the Council. Certain exceptions exist if a property qualifies as a significant parcel as outlined in section 4-
3A-5.C of the Ogden City Code.
          Ms. Meess used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide a project overview, including conceptual renderings of
the project; public improvements included in the project; an illustration highlighting the properties involved in the conveyance
action; and the terms of the Land Transfer and Development Agreement (LTDA) with Parkway Group, LLC. For a copy of the
presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.

Proposed Conveyance of Significant Parcels from Ogden City to Ogden
Redevelopment Agency – 18th Street and Wall Avenue and Lincoln Avenue
south of the Ogden River; and Proposed Land Exchange Agreement with
Lotus 1900 Lincoln, LLC.
          The Mayor has provided notice of a proposed property conveyance of six parcels of Ogden City property totaling
approximately 3.5 acres to the Ogden City Redevelopment Agency. The conveyance of property located at 18th and Wall Avenue
and south of the river on Lincoln Avenue will facilitate development of additional housing in the Ogden River RDA project area.
Per Ogden Municipal Code §4-3A-5 the Council is required to hold a public hearing to allow residents to comment on the
proposed conveyance. Per Utah Code Ann. §17C-1-207 a 15-day notice prior to the conveyance is required. Additionally, Ogden
City Administration is proposing to transfer 4.09 acres of RDA owned property to Lotus 1900 Lincoln, LLC in exchange for 2.45
acres of property owned by Lotus for the development of Phase 3 of the Riverwalk development in the River Bend
Redevelopment Area. The proposed land transfer would exchange RDA owned property on the block between Wall Avenue and
Lincoln Avenue south of 18th Street for property owned by Lotus located east of Lincoln Avenue south of 18th Street. The
properties are within the Ogden River Redevelopment Area and are subject to the design standards of the Ogden Bend
Redevelopment Master Plan and Design Guidelines and the Mixed Use (MU) zoning ordinance. The RDA Board recently
approved amendments to the Ogden Bend Master Plan to accommodate the proposed land transfer in conjunction with
amendments to the MU zoning ordinance approved by the Council.
          Community and Economic Development Department Director Johnson used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
discuss previous efforts of Lotus Company to develop property within the River Bend Redevelopment Area; the benefits of the
proposed property exchange; the Master Plan for and the types of housing products to be included the project area; the types of
housing products to be included in the project area; unit counts and parking plans for the development; environment cleanup needs
on the property currently owned by the RDA; and the appraised values of the properties. For a copy of the presentation in its
entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Discussion among the Council and staff centered on the history of the ownership of the properties subject to the land
trade; development plans for the properties; the potential to pursue open space preservation within the River Bend Redevelopment
Area; and the timing of the proposed land transactions for this project area.

Proposed Offer to Purchase Real Estate and Acceptance Contract with the
General Services Administration for purchase of the Forest Service
Building; and
Proposed Resolution of Intent to Reimburse Redevelopment Agency with
Bond Proceeds Issued to Purchase the Forest Service Building
           City Administration is proposing the purchase of the U.S. Forest Service Building (FSB) located at 507 25th Street. In
March 2023, the General Services Administration (GSA) designated the FSB as surplus property. The City submitted a Notice of
Interest in a negotiated sale on April 17, 2023. The GSA accepted the city’s Notice of Interest and recently issued an Offer to
Purchase Real Property and Acceptance Contract to the City. The purchase price for the FSB is $3.6 Million. The deadline to
accept GSA’s Offer to Purchase is December 16, 2024 (extended from November 25, 2024). If the Board chooses to move
forward with the purchase, additional actions will be necessary to fund the purchase.
           If the Board approves the Offer to Purchase Real Estate and Acceptance Contract with the General Services
Administration (GSA), an earnest money payment in the amount of $360,000 must accompany the executed Contract. The
deadline for execution of the Contract is December 16, 2024 (extended from November 24, 2024). The Agency must close within
thirty days. Because issuance of the bonds cannot occur within the thirty-day timeframe, the Agency will need to advance funds
for the earnest money payment and purchase of the building. Bond Council has prepared a Resolution of Intent to reimburse the
Agency with bond proceeds which is required under the IRS rules governing the issuance of tax-exempt bonds. Adoption of the
Resolution of Intent does not commit the Agency to issue the bonds.
           Economic Development Director Meess used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss Administration’s goals for
preserving the Forest Service Building; the use of tax increment financing (TIF) for the project; the manner in which the
acquisition of the building fits into the Adams Community Reinvestment Area (CRA) Plan; the GSA’s property disposal policy
and process; reuse proposals for the property; and the timeline for the acquisition of the property. For a copy of the presentation in
its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
           Discussion among the Board and staff centered on funding sources for the property acquisition and repayment of those
funds upon the re-sale of the building; storage of hazardous materials on the property, if any; and the terms of the contract and any
opportunities for the City to cancel the contract if deemed necessary.
           Laura Lewis, Lewis Robertson Burningham (LRB), the City’s Financial Advisory, then discussed the potential to issue
bonds to fund the purchase of the building, if there is not sufficient TIF for the transaction. Administration is requesting that the
Board authorize a Resolution of Intent to Reimburse the Agency for the down payment and purchase price of the FSB with bond
proceeds. Bond Counsel has prepared the Resolution in accordance with IRS rules related to the issuance of tax-exempt bonds.
Adoption of the Resolution does not commit the Agency to issuance of the bonds.
           Chair Richey asked if issuance of the bonds could impact the City’s bond rating. Ms. Lewis stated she has been informed
that this bond will not result in a reduction in the City’s bond rating; however, she would caution against any additional significant
sales tax bonds until the WonderBlock project is well underway and revenues are being generated at that project for debt service
payments. Council member Myers asked if Administration is planning to request additional bonding actions in the near future.
Management Services Executive Director Stout stated the next big project for which Administration would seek a bond issuance
would be related to the Union Station, but that project is approximately three years out.
           Chair Richey noted that action on both matters related to the U.S. Forest Service building is scheduled for December 3.
