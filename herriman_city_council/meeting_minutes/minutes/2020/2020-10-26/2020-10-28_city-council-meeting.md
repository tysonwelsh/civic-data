2.1 Army Compatible Use Buffer (ACUB) Update – Heather Upshaw, Economic Development
Manager
Economic Development Manager Heather Upshaw provided an update on the Army Compatible Use Buffer
program the City had been involved with, in partnership with Camp Williams. The City had been working
with Camp Williams for close to 20 years, and with this specific program for five years. Over the last four
years, the City had received closed to $18 million in funding, and almost $13 million in landowner donations.
With this funding, they were able to purchase close to 1,500 acres on the hillside. This property would create
recreation opportunities, and provide a buffer for Camp Williams. Manager Upshaw presented maps depicting
property ownership from 2015 and 2020. There were approximately 70 acres left on the Bluff’s contract, and
staff anticipated closing the contract next year. When purchasing properties, the City requires a review
appraisal. Manager Upshaw asked the Council if they desired to continue the practice.

The Council asked if there was a benefit in discontinuing the review appraisal. Manager Upshaw said many
times the appraisals came in without much change from the first appraisal. In essence, meant the City was
spending money on review appraisals simply to meet the policy. It was roughly $500 per review appraisal. It
was suggested they consider doing review appraisals every 90 or 180 days. They would be following the current
policy, but they wouldn’t have to do a review appraisal on every single property. Councilmember Henderson
asked if they could do review appraisals on several properties at once, such as properties owned by a single
owner, and Manager Upshaw answered affirmatively. Mayor Watts felt they should be consistent with what
had been done in the past. The policy was in place to ensure that the City Council was being a good steward
of public money.

Camp Williams General Smith expressed appreciation for Herriman City and the City Council for their
partnership. He briefly described on where funding was coming from, what it was used for, and generally what
kind of activities went on at Camp Williams.

2.2. Storm Water Management Outreach Overview - Jonathan Bowers, City Engineer
City Engineer Jonathan Bowers provided a background of this discussion item. Based on previous comments,
staff created a cost benefit analysis pertaining to the coalition. Staff researched what it would take for Herriman
City alone to meet Minimum Control Measures as required by the State, and they found that it would cost a
bare minimum of $7,000. To meet the level of service that the City was currently getting, the cost would be
around $20,000. The County provided an analysis of the return on investment, from their perspective, and
they estimated that they were providing roughly $60,000 worth of services to Herriman City. Engineer Bowers
said one of the greatest benefits of jumping on the coalition was the cost, which was $5,295.45. He confirmed
the City was currently receiving more than the $7,000 model or the $20,000 model. If the City Council was
interested in being part of the coalition, they needed to approve an interlocal agreement. He opined this was
a worthwhile partnership.

2.3. Discussion regarding the Auto Mall and the potential Public Infrastructure District and Tax
Increment Funding options for Herriman 73 Partners, LLC - Chase Andrizzi, City Attorney and Larry
Myler, Applicant
City Attorney Chase Andrizzi stated the staff report included a lot of information regarding this item, and he
wouldn’t reiterate the information unless the Council had specific questions. In this discussion, he intended
to gain specific instruction from the Council regarding the Automall, with feedback on how to move forward.
He noted a few of the documents included in the staff report.

Councilmember Smith said a developer and partner of the City had expressed a desire for this process to
continue. They wanted the City to consider a Public Infrastructure District (PID) for the Automall project
because they believe it was vital to the success of the overall project. He had heard that there was some
discussion about this at the last Community Development meeting. He encouraged the Council to discuss
their goals for the development and the partnership.

Councilmember Shields provided a report on the discussion at the aforementioned Community Development
meeting. The discussion was centered around whether or not the City should be giving direction to their
partner regarding the timing of an application for a PID. They also discussed how a PID would be approved.
There was no specific letter of intent, so the discussion was general. Councilmember Henderson added they
also discussed the premise of the PID, and whether or not to have a policy in place. The developer opined if
the City had at least a policy in place regarding the PID, it would give them the ammunition they need to
engage a dealership. There was a disconnect between Community Development and the developer as to what
was an appropriate “good faith step”. He and Councilmember Shields agreed they were not interested in
encumbering the property with a tax burden unless there was a dealership ready to sign.

Mayor Watts commented Herriman City had been a willing partner in this development. He didn’t feel like
they were in a position to approve a PID at this point. Councilmember Henderson and Councilmember Shields
agreed things would be different if there was a dealership willing to sign. The developer was told they could
submit an application, if they desired.

The Council all agreed they didn’t want to encumber the land at this point in the process. Councilmember
Smith said having a PID could lure in a dealership, but the City wasn’t willing to consider a PID unless a
dealership was already onboard. It was a tough situation for the developer to be in. The Council wanted this
project to succeed, but they needed to consider the cost. Councilmember Henderson commented the
participation agreement was intended to give the developer incentive for potential dealers. Mayor Watts
requested a memorandum regarding the PID approval options and other related information.

Councilmember Shields expressed a concern for the City if the developer failed to repay the bond for
infrastructure improvements, which was estimated to be $6.7 million. Councilmember Henderson wanted to
work with their partner to create a successful project. They should strive to be flexible and continue to look
at creative solutions, and they needed to determine financial obligations. He wanted more information from
the partner before he was willing to consider moving forward with a PID. Councilmember Shields

required the Community Development Committee to provide feedback to the applicant, and the Committee
is required to approve the Letter of Intent. The City Council would be the deciding body for the governing
documents. That policy could be changed, if the Council desired.

2.4. Discussion of Athlos Academy Conduit Bond Proposal - Alan Rae, Finance Director
Finance Director Alan Rae explained staff received a request from the attorneys representing Athlos Academy
to see if the City would be willing to act as a conduit bonding agency to assist Athlos in obtaining a bond. A
bond would allow the Academy to pay off the developer that built the building, and it would decrease their
lease payment. They had been turned down by the Utah Charter School Finance Agency due to reputational
risk. After some discussion, the Council agreed not to put the City in this position.

2.5. City Manager Job Description Discussion - City Council Roundtable
Councilmember Ohrn requested this discussion be added to the agenda because November may be a good
time to post for the position. At the previous meeting, there was a sentiment they should wait until after the
holidays because they may not receive any application, but she felt that it would be wise to post sooner. If
there are people looking for employment now, they may apply. Human Resource Manager Travis Dunn agreed.
December was a difficult month to find people, but November and January usually saw more applicants.

Councilmember Smith asked Manager Dunn where he saw the strengths and weaknesses in the current job
description. Manager Dunn said the job description itself was solid. He wanted to know what the Council
wanted to focus on when seeking an applicant. They could look at the person’s vision and drive for the City,
experience in economic development, experience with building culture, or communication style.

Councilmember Smith thought the candidate should be able to enhance the existing culture of Herriman, and
communication was very important. Councilmember Ohrn agreed communication was key. She also agreed
that culture was important, but the candidate needed to be able to come in and make it their own as well.

Mayor Watts noted some changes could be made to the list of educational qualifications. He would be willing
to consider someone with four years of city government experience rather than a Master’s in Public
Administration. He would also be willing to consider someone that had multiple degrees in appropriate fields.
Councilmember Henderson added he was open to someone with comparable education and experience, as
opposed to only those with government experience. There were good executives who might be looking to
jump from the private sector to government. Councilmember Shields requested a few linguistical changes to
the job description, which the Council agreed upon. Staff noted these changes.

Interim City Manager Wendy Thomas requested the Council request any possible changes before the job
description was sent to the recruiting firm. There was discussion regarding the salary range. The Council
agreed that getting a recruiting firm on board would help drive the Council in the right direction. Staff was
directed to solicit a recruiting firm.

    10. Discussion and Action Items
        10.1. Discussion and consideration of an Ordinance authorizing a text change proposal based
              off the application submitted by Judd Messenger on behalf of Academy Village Land
              Holdings, LLC and in partnership with John Lindsley requesting the approval of a draft
              Village Mixed Use (VMU) Overlay Zone to regulate signage within commercial
              development that contain a minimum of 40 contiguous acres of property (File No.
              Z2020-044) - Michael Maloy, City Planner
City Planner Michael Maloy explained the City started working with this applicant in January of this year,
making a number of amendments to the proposal. At the October 14th work meeting, the Council was briefed
on this item. The Planning Commission provided a recommendation of approval. The applicant was
proposing to create an overlay in the text, but a second request would be made to apply the overlay to a specific
property.

Planner Maloy addressed the proposed text, explaining that the proposed overlay ordinance would limit the
number of planned center signs to two, and there were distance requirements between signs and away from
residential developments. The minimum acreage for the overlay was 40 contiguous acres. The ordinance also
required proximity and orientation toward Mountain View Corridor.

Staff had researched similar ordinances from surrounding cities and found that the proposal was consistent
with what these other cities allowed along high traffic corridors.

Regarding the Planning Commission discussion and recommendation, Planner Maloy stated there was one
Planning Commission member who voted against the recommendation of approval because they wanted to
limit the number of tenants on a sign. This idea wasn’t supported by the rest of the Planning Commission.
The Planning Commission requested that the language be clarified to show the Planning Commission as the
body that reviews and approves the master signage plan.

Mayor Watts expressed concerns about the required distance from residential dwellings, and the number of
other signs allowed within the overlay. He requested that the language prohibit electronic signs from
advertising for businesses or parties that were not located in the development. Staff noted the changes.

Councilmember Shields moved to continue the item to the next appropriate City Council meeting, as determined by staff.
Councilmember Ohrn seconded the motion, and all members present voted aye.

        10.2. Discussion and consideration of a Resolution approving an amendment requested by
                John Lindsley to the Wasatch South Hills Master Development Agreement - Blake
                Thomas, Community Development Director
Community Development Director Blake Thomas presented the vicinity map for the Wasatch South Hills
Development and provided a timeline of the Master Development Agreement (MDA). The developer was
proposing amendments to the MDA, including the addition of 161 acres of property, which was equivalent to
731 acres at 4.5 units per acre. He identified the properties and pods which would be added. The proposed
amendments also included commercial technical guidelines for Academy Village, which didn’t exist in the
previous MDA. The amendments would also modify the text in Section 4.3 of the MDA, update the land use
plan, and update the alignment of South Hills Boulevard.

Councilmember Smith commented the commercial technical guidelines going into the MDA should meet the
minimum standards of the current City Code. He didn’t want to see something less restrictive than what
already existed. The Council engaged in a discussion regarding the ability to transfer density, and they wanted
to include a provision requiring the applicant come back to the Council for approval of such a transfer.

Director Thomas briefly addressed changes to the technical guidelines the City had requested of the developer,
including flag lots, storm water regulation and design standards, allowable driveway depth and slope, park strip
landscaping, house lighting, updating road cross-section, erosion control guidelines, and trail cross sections.

Councilmember Shields was concerned about the loss of low-density lots between the previous MDA and this
proposal. Previously, there were 96.64 acres of low-density property with 408 units. Now there were 67 acres
of low-density housing with 44 units. He also wanted a better definition of what kind of medium density
housing was being proposed.

Applicant John Lindsley provided additional information regarding the proposed amendments. Regarding the
text changes to Section 4.3, he explained the idea behind this change was to do what they’ve done with
Herriman City in the past, which was to engage in a dialogue and create a better product for the community.
If the Council was uncomfortable with the language, it could be removed. However, removing the language
October 28, 2020 – City Council Minutes                                                 Page 8 of 9
would basically require the developer to follow the plan, and he wasn’t sure that was wise. The intention was
not to circumvent any sort of process or give the developer the ability to do something that wasn’t in the MDA.

Mr. Lindsley explained the MDA entitled them to bring new property into the agreement with a density of 4.5
units per acre. The number reflected in the revised MDA reflected their maximum number of units. They
were required to bring a site plan to the City with this amendment, which was included in the proposal. They
intended to put large lots on the hillside, because the area was perfect for such development. The City wasn’t
necessarily approving the density in each area by approving these amendments. The developer would bring
back a plan for Planning Commission approval once they know how an area would lay out.

There was some further discussion regarding the applicant’s positive reputation in the community, and the
proposed changes. Mayor Watts requested the City Attorney make adjustments according to the feedback
shared during the meeting and bring the item back for approval at a later date.

Councilmember Shields moved to continue the item to the next appropriate meeting, as determined by staff. Councilmember
Henderson seconded the motion, and all members present voted aye.
