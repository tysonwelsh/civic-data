2023 Utah Legislative Session Update: Presenter: Cameron Diehl, Utah
League of Cities and Towns
​       Mr Diehl is reporting on the 2023 Legislative Session. 575 bills were passed and 244 of
them were tracked by the ULCT. Of the 244 tracked bills, 152 passed and 82 failed. Mr. Diehl
will focus on four points: 1) Year-round legislature. The Unified Economics Opportunity
Commission (UEOC) April 12. 2) The State of Utah has a surplus but cities do not resonate. We
went from bills negatively impacting cities to the tune of $118 million to a net increase in
revenue for cities/towns. 3) “Partner with local government to plan for growth” resonated. 2nd
highest amount of aggregate dedicated transportation money for cities for the last 20 years.
ULCT secured outcomes on big issues that respect the role of local government with the

understanding that the state expects more of local governments. 4) Increase Rural Resources.
There will be a local administrative advisor and changes to the Rural Transportation Investment

         HB 301 Transportation Tax Amendments aligns transportation revenue with evolving
consumer behavior through 3 key changes to transportation related taxes and fees. 1. Increase
vehicle registration by $7/year, 2. Implementing Electric Vehicles Charging Tax to be levied on
retail charging stations, 3. Temporarily reduces the motor fuel tax by reading the rate.
        SB 174 is a product of the Land Use Task Force and addresses 3 key topics. 1) Requires
municipal subdivision review and approval, 2. Assesses a fee of $250/day for cities who fail to
submit a MIHP, 3. Modifies and clarifies IADU policy to include garbage if it shares a wall with
the primary residence.
​      SB 175 creates and funds the Rural Transportation Infrastructure Fund providing
transportation funding for 3rd-6th class counties.
​      SB 185 provides the requirements and funding to establish the Utah Trail Network.
Creates long term stable funding source and funding to be used for planning, design, construction
and maintenance.
​      SB 260 modifies the 0.02% local option transportation sales tax. It eliminates the
imposition deadline of June 2023, allows counties with transit services to divide funds, and
allows counties without transit to divide funds.
​       HB 364 is a product of the Land Use Task Force and addresses the policy &
administrative challenges that arose from HB 462 in 2022. It modifies the MIHP reporting
timeline, it clarifies MIHP requirements and municipalities reporting requirements, and it
clarifies the definitions, use, transfer, and calculations of state low-income housing tax credits.
​      HB 406 is a product of the Land Use Task Force. It makes several technical and
substantial changes to the local government land use process.
​       HB 499 addresses three aspects of the homeless policy. It shows clarifications of winter
response plans and expansion to second class counties, it shows implementation of Code Blue
and it shows modifications to the shelter city mitigation fund.
       SB 75 provides additional financial resources to localities with gravel pits to help
mitigate the impact through two key policy changes. It establishes the point of sale for gravel
and ready made concrete and it reallocates the revenue that would traditionally be distributed
based on population to be reallocated.
       Discussions were held between Mr. Deihl and the City Council members about the bills
presented. Housing issues were part of the discussion, as well as transportation. Mr Diehl
reminded the city council that the 2023 ULCT Midyear Conference is April 20-21, 2023 at the
Dixie Center in St. George, Utah.
Station Area Plan Kick Off: Presenter: Christie Oostema Brown, People+Place, Grant Allen &
Matt Taylor, Senior Planners
​      Mr. Allen introduced Ms. Oostema Brown from People+Place who was hired by the City
of Orem to help with the Orem Station Area. The bill HB 462 objective is to maximize

         City Council Minutes – April 11, 2023, (pg.2)A complete video of the meeting can be found at www.orem.org/meeting
development potential in appropriate areas, to have a collaborative city led planning approach
and for cities to determine how best to meet shared objectives. The shared objectives are to
increase the availability of housing, to promote sustainable environmental conditions, to enhance
access to opportunities and to increase transportation choices and connections.
​       The requirements for the bill is that cities with FrontRunner, Trax, and Streetcar stations
develop SAP ½ mile around the stations. It also requires the cities with Bus Rapid Transit
stations to develop SAP ¼ mile around stations. The Station Area Planning (SAP) has 5
components. 1. Vision-the development of existing and future land. 2. Map-Area subject to SAP.
3. 5-Year Implementation Plan-get an action plan, identify responsible parties, promote
sustainable environmental conditions, Enhance access to opportunities and increase
transportation choices. 4. Statement Promoting Shared Objectives include availability and
affordability of housing, promote sustainable environmental conditions, enhance access to
opportunities and increase in transportation choices. 5. Public involvement and stakeholder
engagement.
       Ms. Oostema Brown asked the following questions about the FrontRunner area in Orem.
What is working? What is not? What do you hope to see more of? What do you hope to see less
of? What big questions need to be answered? What are the challenges? What are the
opportunities? Discussions were held with City Council members about these questions.
       Orem’s process to move forward should include a stakeholder committee, a UVU/UTA
partnership, robust public engagement, scenarios built from feedback, vision and plan created
cooperatively and implementation and adoption of new plan. Members of the City Council
would like to be part of the stakeholder committee, Mr. Spencer, Mr. Peterson and Mr.
Macdonald will become part of that committee. First meeting will be April 27th at 6 pm.

Municipal Wastewater Planning Program Presenter: Reed Price, Assistant Public Works
Director and Giles Demke, Water Reclamation Section Manager
​      Mr. Price is presenting the annual Wastewater Municipal Planning Program (MWPP).
The MWPP uses an annual survey to assist owners of municipal wastewater collection systems
and Treatment facilities to evaluate and summarize the technical, operational and financial
conditions. It helps communities better understand business operations and can identify and
resolve problem areas before they become serious and costly. It evaluates 3 areas, financial
evaluations, collections systems and treatment facility. Mr. Reed went through the survey
questions with the City Council.

Opioid Class Action Settlement: Presenter: D. Jacob Summer, Deputy City Attorney
        Mr. Summer gave history on overdoses in the past years and that most of those are from
prescription opioids. The State of Utah has worked to address this issue. In 2021, Orem joined a
settlement with pharmaceutical companies. Half of the settlement is to go to the State and the
other half to the counties. Currently Utah State has entered into another class action lawsuit.
Utah could receive money from this settlement. All counties will need to join the class action
lawsuit to maximize the funds. If we choose to opt in then the paperwork must be submitted by
April 18, 2023. Mr. Summer is asking if the city council has any concerns with joining.
