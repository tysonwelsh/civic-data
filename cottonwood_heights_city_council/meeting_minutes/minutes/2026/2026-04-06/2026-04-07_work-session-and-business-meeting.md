          3.1    Central Wasatch Commission - Lindsey Nielsen, Executive Director.

Executive Director, Lindsey Nielsen, presented the Central Wasatch Commission (“CWC”)
Annual Report. The CWC is an interlocal governmental entity that grew out of the Mountain
Accord process signed in 2015. Cottonwood Heights became a member in 2017. Since that time,
they have worked to convene the major jurisdictions along the Wasatch Front and Wasatch Back
to discuss issues such as transportation, recreation, environment, and the economy of the Central
Wasatch Mountains. Members include area cities and counties, as well as non-voting members
High Valley Transit, Utah Transit Authority ("UTA"), and Metro Water, and special advisors from
Solitude Mountain Resort and Save Our Canyons.

The CWC’s portfolio of work has grown over time, including the creation of the Ski Bus Priority
Access Program in 2021. Since its inception, ski bus travel time has decreased by an average of
50 minutes.

The Central Wasatch Symposium is an annual two-day conference with expert panels, interactive
workshops, and networking. Over 200 people attended in its first and second years. In 2026,
Shoshone elder Darren Parry was the keynote speaker.

The CWC’s short-term project grant program was created in 2020 and has disbursed hundreds of
thousands of dollars for projects such as:

      •   Trailhead bathroom maintenance;
      •   Backcountry ski shuttle in Little and Big Cottonwood Canyon;
      •   Maintenance of fixed-rock anchor hardware on climbing routes across the Central
          Wasatch; and
      •   Abatement of graffiti tags across the canyons.

The Central Wasatch National Conservation and Recreation Area Act (“CWNCRA”) is the CWC’s
cornerstone project. The issues that the Mountain Accord was created to address were still present

The CWC is funded through member contributions and legislative appropriations. Since its

severe deficit in the current fiscal year and required drastic measures. To address the issue, Sandy

membership contributions across jurisdictions, which historically had been based on the original
Mountain Accord agreement. The qualitative analysis considered the quality of life the CWC
provides to each member jurisdiction, its stake in the CWNCRA legislation, and the placemaking
value of a healthy Wasatch.

Cottonwood Heights’ scores were as follows:

    •   Quality of life: 3
    •   Stake in legislation: 4
    •   Placemaking value: 3
    •   Total score: 10

Based on the analysis, the total quantitative and qualitative contribution for Cottonwood Heights
was $59,000, a slight increase over the City’s historic contribution of $50,000.

        3.3      Health in the Heights Committee – Chelsey Voss, Chair.

Health in the Heights Coalition Chair, Chelsey Voss presented the Committee Report. Her
background is in nursing and population health, and improving citizens’ health and quality of life
is very important to her.

The Coalition follows the Communities that Care framework, a nationally recognized framework
that includes five phases. Phases 1 and 2 were completed in 2025. All board members completed
a three-part community board orientation where they learned about prevention science, risk and
protective factors, and created a vision statement and workgroups.

They were currently completing Phase 3, Developing a Community Profile, which include:

    •   Establishing workgroups and moving through workflows.
    •   Attending data workgroup workshops to understand Student Health and Risk Prevention
        (“SHARP”) data and selecting coalition priorities.
    •   Writing and distributing a Community Assessment Report based on the above data.
    •   Forming a Resource Assessment Workgroup to identify resources and assess gaps in
        programming.

In response to a question raised by Council Member Hyland, Ms. Voss reported that the SHARP
survey is completed every two years by students in the Brighton cone, which includes Brighton
High School and all schools that feed into it. Public Health Coordinator, Sondra Stephens added
that students in grade six and higher opt in to the survey and must have parental consent. It was
previously conducted in odd years but will be conducted again in Fall 2026. The data is protected
and specific information cannot be provided in a public meeting, but it is discussed in closed
meetings.

Ms. Voss reviewed Phase 4, Create a Plan.

    •   Develop a community action plan.
    •   Select tested and effective programs, policies, and practices to target selected priorities.
    •   Identify and secure needed resources and funding.

Cottonwood Heights was officially recognized as a Healthy Utah Community in October 2025.
The designation is valid through 2028, at which point they will need to select new strategies and
reapply.
