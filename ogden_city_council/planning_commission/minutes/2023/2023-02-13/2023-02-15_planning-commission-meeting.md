1. Training on land use decision making

   Mr. Brierley presented that there are two types of decision making that the Planning
   Commission utilizes, legislative and administrative. If the item says recommendation to City
   Council on the agenda, it is legislative, in which the Commission acts as an advisory body. It
   is up to the City Council to decide what is in the best interest of the public if it is within the
   bounds of State Law and the Constitution. He stated if the agenda lists an item as a Final
   Action or going to the mayor, the decision will be administrative. In an administrative
   decision, facts are applied to preexisting standards and criteria. Mr. Brierley explained that
   standards are objective, and anyone who looks at it should arrive to the same conclusion.
   Criteria are subjective, where different conclusions may be met. He then described how to
   address subjective criteria. Using an example property, the Commission discussed how to
   mitigate the issues. In explaining how to set conditions, Mr. Brierley stated that conditions
   must be related to the standards and criteria, must be connected to the impacts of the
   proposal, and must be “roughly proportional” to the impact.

   Mr. Brierley explained that factors which cannot be considered in administrative decisions
   include: the wholesomeness of a use; the applicant’s past performance; an overabundance
   of a particular use; and public clamor. Mr. Tanner added that public clamor is a way to get a
   gauge on what the issues are. Examples of how to mitigate public clamor were presented
   and discussed. Mr. Brierley stated that an administrative land use decision will be upheld if it
   is supported by substantial evidence on the record and is otherwise legal. Findings of fact
   should explain how and why the city reached its decision, with reasoning being verbalized.

   Explaining the definition of a conditional use, Mr. Brierley reiterated that a conditional use
   shall be approved if reasonable conditions are proposed, and can be imposed, to mitigate
   the reasonable anticipated detrimental effects of the proposed use, in accordance with
   applicable standards. Examples of appropriate mitigations were discussed. Mr. Brierley
   indicated that at times the Commission may need to approved two separate factors with two
   sets of criteria and gave the example of a conditional use with a site plan. Commissioner
   Shinoda inquired about what the consequence would be if the Planning Commission denied
   a conditional use. Mr. Brierley stated that the recourse would be to appeal to the Board of
   Zoning Adjustments and beyond that is to go to the District Court.

2. Addressing Commissioners

   Mr. Brierley stated that Commissioners should not be addressed by their first name, but
   rather, their title and their last name. Examples: Commissioner Shale or Chair Sandau.
   Addressing commissioners in this way is a visual que that members of the Commission have
   authority and should be treated with respect. He stated that staff should be addressed by
   using Mister or Miss.

Commissioner Shinoda inquired about the current legislative session and if there would be a
meeting to discuss the items, including short term rentals. Mr. Brierley confirmed that a meeting
would be held. Discussion continued regarding short term rentals within the city, and the
regulations of them.

As there was no additional business before the Commission, the meeting was adjourned at
6:41pm.
