1.     AMENDED PLAT (Public Hearing – Legislative). Review and hold a public hearing for a

       parcels affected are GMSR-14-A-HV and GMSR-15-A-HV, currently zoned RA-1 (Residential
       Agricultural, 1-acre min lot size). The applicants are Eric Hustead and Nick Paras.

       Recommendation:

       Staff recommends that the Planning Commission approve the applicants request to partially
       amend the Grassy Meadows Sky Ranch subdivision plat and public utility easement vacation
       based on the following findings and conditions:

2.    KOLOB CONSERVATION PROJECT UPDATE & EXTENSION (Public Meeting –
      Administrative). Review a request to extend and review updates to the Kolob Conservation
      Project, formerly known as Above Zion. This project is generally located on Kolob Mountain.
      The applicant is Kevin McLaws.

      Recommendation:

      Staff has reviewed the request to extend the time for complete processing of the two
      conditional use applications (formerly Above Zion and Zion East), now combined and known
      as Kolob Conservation, and recommends that the Planning Commission approve a one-year
      extension for Kolob Conservation based on the following findings:
      1. The proposed changes in the Kolob Conservation project CUP decreases the density and
         impact the project will have on Kolob Mountain, and proposes to conserve 1500 acres
         of the project as undisturbed open space through a Conservation Easement.
      2. The new development group, Kolob Conservation, is working with landowners and
         stakeholders to create a project that is sensitive to the community and environment.
      3. The one year extension will be sufficient time for the new developer to finalize and
         resubmit the revised application and project details, time for the county to review and
         process the new project plan, and time to conduct necessary public meetings for

3.     TITLE 10 MISC CHANGES TO ZONING REGULATIONS FOR MINING & GRAVEL (Public
       Hearing - Legislative). Review, discuss and make recommendation to the County
       Commission regarding miscellaneous changes to zoning regulations. Specifically, changes to
       Title 10, Chapter 12 Mining and Gravel zone.

       Staff Report:

       Ms. Hales summarized changes and additions to the Mining and Gravel Zone. Some of these
       changes were hours of operation, setbacks, road standards, corrections to typos, dust control
       plans, good practice plans, rehabilitation and reclamation plans and so forth.

       Questions from Commissioners were answered by Ms. Hales regarding the changes to Title
       10 zoning regulations for mining and gravel. Some issues that were raised by commissioners
       were dust and health issues for workers at the Mining and Gravel pits, which Ms. Hales
       advised was OSHA regulated. Another issue raised was stipulations about reclamation of the
       property, which Ms. Hales advised has already been addressed in the ordinance.

       Chairman Gaston opened the Public Hearing

       Ms. Rosemary D’Amato of Central:

       She raised the issue of ‘Brown Fields’ in residential areas and suggested provisions be in place
       for clean up. She expressed concern for the children and residents due to dust particles, silica
       in the air as well as environmental issues caused by mining and gravel.

       Mr. Tim Taylor of Hurricane:

       His concern was regarding there being no explanation for those beyond the boundaries of
       these zones. eg. there are hours for limitations of explosives but no limitations on distance
       from nearest houses. Hours of operation are generous to the operators not to the residents
       around it. He raised several more concerns such as: total size limitations to zoning, the need

for regulations for not only dust but sound and light pollution. His biggest concern was that
there should be a plan before the zoning is put into place.

Ms. Martha Honey of Sky Ranch in Hurricane:

Ms. Honey stated as they have an aviation easement/flight plan in their area, the regulations
might be different to other areas. She expressed that the dust and blasting would not be a
fitting combination with airplanes flying in and out. She raised the concern of fault lines in
the area, dust, noise patterns and requested that these be considered in the regulations. Ms.
Honey expressed gratitude for the work that has been put into the mining and gravel
ordinance.

Mr. Ed Love of Central:

Mr. Love is concerned about the effects of traffic due to mining and pollution. He expressed
much concern and regarding the proximity of these operations to his residence

Ms. Patty Nation of Sky Ranch, Hurricane:

Ms. Nation was concerned about the proximity of mines from each other and requested there
be a provision in the code for how far they can be from each other.

Mr. Jason St. John of Hurricane, Utah:

Mr. St. John queried about access to mines.

Mr. Don McDonald of Hurricane:

Mr. McDonald suggested the County get a bond from corporations in the event that they
dissolve the corporations at the end of operations to have them responsible for reclamation
to help protect the County. He also brought up access and route control.

Brian Bachman & Nicole Alexander of Hurricane:

Mr. Bachman brought forward that the zoning was being issued before the planning and felt
this was inverted or backwards. He queried whether the zone remains a mining and gravel
zone after the project or mining is completed and reclamation is actioned. He also felt the
fines were too light to be effective as a deterrent. Mr. Bachman voiced that the annual
inspection seemed insufficient for effective oversight, specifically around daily limitations.
Mr. Bachman was concerned with how these will be monitored and enforced effectively and
did not think that an annual inspection would suffice. He was not satisfied that effective
consequences were in place when limitations were breached.

Mr. Slade Hughes of Veyo:

Mr. Hughes expressed that it is important to have zones that allow for mining and gravel
activities as the materials are used throughout the County which allows it to flourish.

Ms. Julie Croak of Hurricane:

BLM and Interstate Rock have decided to do mining in Hurricane Cliffs which will affect
everyone in the County. Ms. Croak wanted transparency with the process between these two
entities.

Mr. Tom Mayes of Central:

Mr. Mayes agreed that resources are needed for everything we do, he raised how the water
system has been put under stress due to increased activity and traffic. He talked about what
effect or environmental impact this will have on the community.

Staff responded to the public comments:

Mr. Messel responded land use decisions have two sides and all concerns need to be
mitigated. He explained that regulations are set up at the State level which affect how and
what the County can enforce and it has been deemed at State level that gravel pits are a critical
infrastructure. He voiced that the proposal made today are for regulations that were not in
place prior to today and it will be determined by the Planning Commissioners if these changes
provide a good balance. He addressed the access issues and heavy machinery and agreed that
it can damage roads and cause dust issues.

Commissioner Kelsch:

Commented on the feedback given regarding no limitation on explosives and explained that
most of these pits are ‘open access’ meaning the pit itself is an easy access to gravel and
explosive usage is rare. He commented on “a plan before a zoning change” and expressed
that this is the defined use and we are taking it out of a ‘Conditional Use’. Commissioner
Kelsch expressed that he was in favor of moving this forward.

Chairman Gaston:

Chairman Gaston asked about the possibility of an overlap of these zones and whether there
was a State regulation regarding this.

Mr. Messel responded to Chairman Gaston stating that there is legislation at the State level
that if the gravel pit company has an existing pit and they also own the land around it, then
they can expand onto the existing property which would be considered ‘grandfathered in’.
He commented that there is a big impact on the residents which can be frustrating for them
especially if they have one adjacent or close to them. He stated that as a County and Planning
Commission, finding that balance of property rights is something that needs to be done.
