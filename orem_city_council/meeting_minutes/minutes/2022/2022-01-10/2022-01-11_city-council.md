DISCUSSION — Palisade Traffic Calming

Mr. Kelly started his presentation by introducing the Palisade Traffic Calming project taking
place on Palisade Drive. He stated around 20 years ago, citizens voiced concern with excessive
speeding on the street and 9 speed bumps were installed. Mr. Kelly stated there were recent
complaints about the speed bumps, such as emergency vehicles having trouble with them.
Because of this, the City decided to redo traffic calming on the road. Mr. Kelly said a city open
house was held regarding the subject. Through this open house, speed tables / raised crosswalks
with choker islands were decided upon to replace the speedbumps. Mr. Kelly stated they decided
on this method because it helps not only with speed calming, but pedestrian safety as well. He
then went into detail on different design aspects of the speed tables and showed where similar

examples have been installed in the city. Mr. Kelly stated some citizens have expressed distaste
for the choker islands, saying they are dangerous and look unsightly.

Mr. Macdonald stated though many neighbors in the area are supportive of these traffic calming
devices, there are also some against these measures. Mr. Macdonald stated there were a few
citizen from both sides attending the meeting, and asked if Mayor Young would like to hear their
opinions.

A resident from the Palisade area who originally paid for a large amount of the speed bumps put
in around the year 2000 stated that before the speed bumps were put in, the average speed on the
road was around 35 miles an hour with occasional speeds of up to 70 miles an hour. He stated the
city pledged to maintain these speed bumps when they put them in over 20 years ago and asked
the city to maintain that pledge.

Bruce Cameron, a resident from the Palisade area expressed his concern for the new choker
islands. He stated cyclists and other non-car commuters are forced into the middle of the lanes.
Mr. Cameron said this could be dangerous, and he has had skateboarders swerve in front of him
in his car. He also stated he has done surveys of his own and said there’s over 100 neighbors in
the area that are concerned with the choker islands as well.

Mrs. Lauret asked Mr. Cameron if the neighbors are still concerned with speeds. Mr. Cameron
stated the neighbors are still concerned with speed, and are fine with the speed tables, but think
that the choker islands are dangerous. Mr. Cameron concluded by asking the council to keep the
speed tables, but remove the choker island.

Mr. Spencer asked Mr. Kelly if they could remove the choker islands, but keep the white poles
which are currently on top of them. He expressed this could keep cars in the center of the lanes,
but allow cyclists and other non-car commuters to pass through them.

General discussion ensued regarding possible options of removing the chokers and the direction
the city should go from here. The council decided they should do a more all-encompassing
survey and Mr. Clark stated the city contracts with a PR firm that could go door to door and
collect accurate opinions.

DISCUSSION — Utah Fit Premises Act

Mr. Downs stated he presented this discussion back in November, but would like to go through it
again seeing there has been a change in council.

Mr. Downs introduced the Utah Fit Premises Act to the council. The Fit Premise Act sets a
standard for livable conditions that Landlords must meet for their property to be rentable. Mr.
Downs then showed examples of properties that do not meet the Fit Premise Act, such as
dangerous mold growing in an apartment complex in Orem, and stated this act would enforce the
basic upkeep of rental property.

General Discussion ensued regarding the reporting, enforcement, and the fixing of these issues.

Mayor Young expressed concerned that Landlords could raise rent on those who report to try to
balance out costs.

PERSONAL APPEARANCES

Mr. Lavenstock stated he moved to Orem from California around 30 years ago. He expressed
how he feels the city has been going in the wrong direction for a while and he hopes this council
will correct the course. Mr. Lavenstock then went on to state he does not want to see any more
high-density housing in the city, and does not want to see any low-income housing either.

Ken Messmer stated he has been a resident of the city for over 25 years. He expressed how there
is no other place he’d rather be than in Orem and expressed thanks to the new council.

Fred Axelgaurd started by congratulating the new Mayor. He then expressed concern with
eliminating an independent Planning Commission. He asked the council to keep the planning
commission as it is and not establish a new legal counsel within the city.

Margret Black expressed her concern with the Planning Commission resolution on the agenda as
well as the Administrative Counsel Ordinance. She stated the selected individual for the
Administrative Counsel was not selected properly and does not have the credentials needed for
the job. She then expressed her concern with the Resolution changing the Planning Commission
appointments.

Quinn Mecham expressed his concern with the Office of the Legislative Counsel Ordinance and
stated the appointment was not properly done and is a waste of taxpayer money. He then went on
to voice his concern with stripping the Planning Commission of its political independence. Mr.
Mecham finished by urging the council to vote against these proposals.

SCHEDULED ITEMS

ORDINANCE — Amending portions of Section 2-15-4, 2-15-6, and 2-15-10 of the Orem
City Code pertaining to the Appointment, Removal and Compensation of Planning

Commission Members

Mayor Young stated that up until this point, the City Manager has been in charge of appointing
members of the Planning Commission and explained that this Ordinance would switch that
responsibility onto the council instead. He stated the council will have to vote as a majority to
appoint members of the Commission.

Mr. Macdonald stated the council already has to vote in majority to approve a Planning
Commission member and is happy with the way it currently is.

Mr. Peterson stated it won’t be just the Mayor picking who is on the Planning Commission, the
City Council will need to vote in majority to approve them.

www.orem.org/meetings

Mrs. Lauret stated she likes the independence of the Planning Commission and said the way it
currently is offers more diversity and differing opinions. She finished by stating she is
comfortable with the way it is.

Mr. Spencer expressed he feels the Planning Commission has been rude to Orem residents
recently and expressed support for the Mayor and Council to appoint Planning Commission
members.

Mrs. Millet stated that even though the Council may be appointing the members, they will ensure
they have a diverse background and are well qualified.

General discussion took place regarding how board members are reviewed and appointed.

Mrs. Schriever presented amendments made to the proposed Ordinance that had been previously
discussed.

Mr. Spencer moved to by Ordinance to amend portions of Section 2-15-4, 2-15-6, and 2-15-10
of the Orem City Code pertaining to the Appointment, Removal and Compensation of Planning
Commission Members as amended on the record. Mr. Peterson seconded the motion. Those
voting aye: David A. Young, Terry Peterson, David Spencer, Jeff Lambson, and LaNae Millet.
Those voting nay: Debbie Lauret and Tom Macdonald. The motion passed.

RESOLUTION — Establish the Office of Legislative Counsel and Appoint a Director to
the Mayor and City Council

Mayor Young stated this ordinance sets up the Mayor and City Council to have their own legal
representation. He expressed he would like to see an alignment between the residents, the
council, and the management. Mayor Young stated this full-time employee would attend city
meetings and update the council from there.

Mr. Peterson stated he is in favor of another set of ears and more opinions.
Mrs. Lauret expressed her concern with hiring someone she’s never met and the fact that they’ve
never held an open hiring process for this position. Mr. Peterson stated they don’t have time to

do a full hiring process. Mrs. Lauret responded that she’d rather take the time to do it right.

Mr. Lambson stated he does not know anything about the selected individual and does not want
to hire him sight unseen. He stated he wishes they had time to vet this individual.

Mrs. Millett stated because of the situation the city is in, they need to get this process done as

Mr. Peterson stated they have to get the moratorium issue right and asked why they would not
