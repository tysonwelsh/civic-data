SECTION I. The amendments made here are designated by underlining 
the new substituted words.  Words being deleted are designated by brackets 
and interlineations.
SECTION II. Section 19.15.140 of the Salt Lake County Code of 
Ordinances is amended as follows in order to allow separate metering for 
detached accessory dwelling units (“ADUs”).
19.15.140 - Retention of single -family residence status and verification of 
water and sewer.
A. ADUs are part of a single -family residence and shall not be used as a 
multi-family residence.  
B. [ADUs] IADUs may not be separately metered apart from the 
single-family residence.  Detached ADUs may be separately metered so long 
as the property owner is billed for both.
C. Applications for ADUs must include submittal of written verification 
from water and sewer providers that water and sewer are available to the 
ADU (or in the event of a septic system, verification from the Salt Lake 
Health Department that the system meets applicable regulations and is 
performing properly).
D. ADUs may not be sold or subdivided separately from the single -family 
residence.
SECTION III. This ordinance shall become effective fifteen (15) days after 
its passage and upon at least one publication of the ordinance or a summary 
thereof in a newspaper published and having general circulation in Salt Lake 
County.

10.2 Consideration for Approval of an Annexation that Creates a 
Peninsula - Cottonwood Heights (“Tavaci” Annexation)
25-2765

Peninsula Map Tavaci
TAVACI ANNEXATION SUMMARY 
MEMO.Final.04.03.2025
Presenter: Lisa Hartman, SLCo Assoc. Deputy Mayor. Scott Baird, SLCo 
Public Works Director.
(Approx. 2:05PM, 10 Min.)
Discussion/Direction
Ms. Lisa Hartman , Associate Deputy Director of Regional Operations, 
reviewed the resolution, authorizing an annexation of 96 acres of 
unincorporated County into Cottonwood Heights City, called the Tavaci 
annexation, which includes more than just the Tavaci residential development .  
The annexation is sponsored by citizens.  
The annexation would create a peninsula, which is the access road that goes 
up to Salt Lake City Public Utilities' water tank.  Salt Lake City Public 
Utilities did not want to be part of the annexation, but it would be neutral on 
the annexation provided it could keep its access and the water tank in Salt 
Lake County.  The County typically does not like to create peninsulas 
because the County had to maintain the roads.  However, the roads in this 
development are private, and the access road is owned and maintained by 
Salt Lake City Public Utilities.
Walker Development LLC owns 50 acres of vacant land within the 
annexation area, as well as property above and to the west of the annexation 
property.  Walker Development joined the annexation because it would like 
all its property in one municipality.  Walker Development and Cottonwood 
Heights have confirmed there are no current plans to develop that property .

Most of that property has over 30 percent grade and is not developable.  The 
property is zoned FR -20, which has a minimum lot size of 20 acres, so if 
development did take place, it would likely be a couple of residential lots that 
would match what was in Tavaci.  
There was also concern that the annexation would lead to development 
creeping up the canyon on the property to the east of the annexation, but that 
land is owned either by the Forest Service, Salt Lake County, or Salt Lake 
City Public Utilities, so it is unlikely there would be “creep” up the canyon.
When Cottonwood Heights incorporated in 2005, it included the Tavaci 
development in its boundaries.  In 2012, that development deannexed from 
Cottonwood Heights and it came back into the County.  If the County 
approved this annexation, the development would be going back to 
Cottonwood Heights.  
The Cottonwood Heights Council approved the annexation in February.  It 
wanted the annexation to be official by July 1st, which meant it would need 
to submit the annexation to the Lieutenant Governor’s Office by April 19th.

Rabbi Avremi Zippel  stated the idea behind education and sharing began in 
the late 1970s to mark the birthday of the Lubavitcher Rebbe.  The purpose 
is to urge local elected bodies of government, as well as governments on the 
state and national level, to encourage their constituents, residents, and 
children to use a day to build stronger communities that are founded on the 
values and virtues of education, and to share and contribute to one another .

Salt Lake County Council Proclamation Recognizing Education and Sharing 
Day Wednesday, April 9, 2025 
WHEREAS, the basis for the continuity of any society is education; and in 
the great state of Utah the education of our youth is a priority; and 
WHEREAS, in order to achieve its highest goals, education must not only 
impart knowledge but also teach the students how to live, forming and 
strengthening their moral character to make a better life for themselves as 
individuals and for society as a whole; imparting moral and ethical values that 
have been the bedrock of society since the dawn of civilization, including the 
values known as the Seven Noahide Laws; and 
WHEREAS, a global spiritual leader and leading advocate for the 
advancement of education, the Rebbe, Rabbi Menachem M. Schneerson, of 
righteous memory, stressed that a moral and ethical education empowers 
every individual to develop their full potential in making the world a better 
place; and 
WHEREAS, such an education can nurture the unity of diverse peoples 
through encouraging increased acts of goodness and kindness, imbued with 
the awareness that even a single positive act of an individual can change the 
world and usher in an era of global peace; and

this year marks the 75th anniversary of his leadership of the 
Chabad-Lubavitch movement. “Chabad” is an acronym of three Hebrew 
words for “wisdom, understanding and knowledge .” The name "Lubavitch" 
comes from the city which served as the movement ’s headquarters for over a 
century and means city of love. Of all the ethical values which inform our 
civilization, none is more important than love - love of wisdom, love of our 
fellowman, and love of our Creator. These values, have guided the 
Chabad-Lubavitch movement throughout its history, and are the essence of 
education at its best, and we should be certain to pass on this precious 
heritage to all young Americans; and 
WHEREAS, "Education & Sharing Day" is observed each year on the 
Rebbe's birthday in recognition of his outstanding and lasting contributions
