FROM:        Ryan Carter, City Attorney

ITEM:        Resolution establishing a community renewable energy program;
             authorizing the Riverton City Manager to explore opportunities with Rocky
             Mountain Power to create a community renewable energy program; and
             related matters.

SUMMARY:

In the spring of 2019, the Utah Legislature enacted the “Community Renewable Energy
Act” (HB 411), which is enclosed following the proposed Resolution in the Council’s
packet. The purpose of the Community Renewable Energy Act is to enable an electrical
utility provider, such as Rocky Mountain Power, to restructure its power generation
system for delivery of electric power sourced from renewable energy facilities. This
naturally leads to the future construction of power generating facilities which do not rely
on fossil fuels.

To this end, the legislation calls for, but does not require, municipalities to opt into a
city-wide renewable energy program. If the Riverton City Council is interested in
launching this program, it must state its interest in seeing a supply of renewable energy
be provided to Riverton Residents by adopting a resolution which “states a goal of
achieving an amount equivalent to 100% of the annual electric energy supply for
participating customers from a renewable energy resource by 2030.” This opt-in
resolution does not carry with it any financial obligation. The Community Renewable
Energy Act requires the Council to approve such a resolution by December 31, 2019, if
the Council elects to support such a program.

Sometime following the adoption of the resolution, the City will then be presented with an
agreement which does financially obligate the City to pay for “third-party expertise
contracted for by the [Utah Division of Public Utilities], and the [Office of Consumer
Services], for assistance with activities associated with initial approval of the community
renewable energy program.” [See: Utah Code § 54-7-903]. It is premature to know what
the costs of such a contract would be, but adopting a resolution does not require the City
to execute the agreement. Following adoption of a resolution and approval of an
agreement to pay for expenses described above, the City will also need to adopt an
ordinance which enforces the agreement and any additional terms or conditions required
by the Utah Division of Public Utilities. Ultimately, the costs of delivering power sourced
from renewable energy, if any, will be borne by rate payers within Riverton City.
The latter components (i.e. the agreement and ordinance) for launching a community
renewal energy program may take another year to develop. Therefore, the costs
associated with entering an agreement and the ultimate costs which ratepayers would
experience by funding renewable energy remains to be determined. In the event the
costs become too high, adoption of the enclosed resolution will not prevent the City from
opting out of the program in the future.

                                  RESOLUTION NO. 19-80

    A RESOLUTION ESTABLISHING A COMMUNITY RENEWABLE ENERGY
   PROGRAM; AUTHORIZING THE RIVERTON CITY MANAGER TO EXPLORE
      OPPORTUNITIES WITH ROCKY MOUNTAIN POWER TO CREATE A
   COMMUNITY RENEWABLE ENERGY PROGRAM; AND RELATED MATTERS

       WHEREAS, Riverton City is populated with approximately 44,000 residents, and

      WHEREAS, with few exceptions, Riverton City residents obtain electrical power service
from Rocky Mountain Power; and

         WHEREAS, the majority of power supplied by Rocky Mountain Power to Riverton
residents is not derived from renewable energy sources such as wind or solar power generation
facilities; and

      WHEREAS, the Riverton City Council finds and determines that the primary purpose of
municipal government is to protect the health, safety, and welfare of the community; and

        WHEREAS, to this end, the Riverton City Council has adopted Strategic Priorities to
guide the City’s direction in administration of municipal government; and

       WHEREAS, Strategic Priority No. 1 calls for “safe and healthy neighborhoods”; and

       WHEREAS, Strategic Priority No. 5 calls for a “well connected community with
properly maintained utilities and infrastructure;” and

       WHEREAS, the Riverton City Council finds and determines that creating a community
renewal energy program advances Strategic Priorities No. 1 and No. 5, and conceptually
connects these priorities with a single goal; and

        WHEREAS, In the year 2019, the Utah Legislature passed legislation known as House
Bill 411, which supports the future development of electric utility services which supply
electrical power from renewable sources; and

       WHEREAS, said legislation requires municipalities which support renewable energy
development to adopt a resolution which “states a goal of achieving an amount equivalent to
100% of the annual electric energy supply for participating customers from a renewable energy
resource by 2030” [See: Utah Code § 54-17-903(2)(a)] and;

        WHEREAS, the Riverton City Council finds and determines that adoption of the
resolution required by the Utah Legislature under House Bill 411 will further protect the health,
safety, and welfare of the community, and advance Strategic Priorities 1 and 5;

        NOW THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the City Council of Riverton City, Utah,
as follows:
      1. The Riverton City Council authorizes and directs the City Manager to work with
         Rocky Mountain Power to continue to seek mutually beneficial, financially feasible
         methods to develop and use efficient renewable energy supply sources which deliver
         electrical utility service to Riverton residents.
      2. The Riverton City Council authorizes and directs the City Manager to work with
         neighboring cities and other public and private partners to jointly explore renewable
         energy development options with Utah Power, including innovation of best practices
         for shifting towards renewable energy sources.
      3. The Riverton City Council hereby establishes a goal to achieve 100% renewable
         electrical supply for the City at large by 2030.
      4. The Riverton City Council authorizes and directs the City Manager develop a plan to
         pursue a goal of achieving 100% renewable electrical supply for the City at large by
         2030 (the “Renewable Energy Plan”), without compromising the principles of
         affordability, efficiency, and reliability.
      5. The City Manager is authorized and directed to include within the Renewable Energy
         Plan an analysis of fiscal impacts, and a method to deliver annual progress reports.
      6. This resolution shall become effective immediately upon passage.
