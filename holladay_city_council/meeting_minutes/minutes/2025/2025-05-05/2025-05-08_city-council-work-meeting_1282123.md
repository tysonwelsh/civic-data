II.      Discussion on Tentative Budget.
         a.      Stormwater Fee Review and Discussion – Fred Philpot
Mayor Dahle introduced Fred Philpot from LRB Public Finance Advisors to the City Council and
explained that he would share information about the Stormwater Fee. He explained that the City
previously looked at options to address the failing storm drain infrastructure. There was a lot of data
reviewed at that time and many projects were ultimately achieved using the bond money. The
previous recommendation was to focus on inflationary increases every few years, but there was no
justification to ask for more before it was needed. However, sales tax has now flattened out and the
bond money has been spent. This seems like an appropriate time to revisit the previous discussions.

Mr. Philpot presented the Storm Financial Sustainability Plan: Updated Scenario Analysis slides to
the Council. Mr. Philpot shared highlights from the previous analysis. There was an assumed $6.50
rate per equivalent residential unit (“ERU”) and there has not been a change from that at this point.
In the previous analysis, there was a recommended 9.5% increase each year to address the Capital
Improvement Plan (“CIP”) and the anticipated cost increases.

The assumptions have since been updated to include the budget actuals. Mr. Philpot reviewed some
of the updated assumptions, including $1.2 million as the current estimate for CIP in the next five
years. There were also some updated assumptions made to the ERU analysis. Since this was a new
utility and there was no demand data, the previous analysis was conducted by looking at impervious
areas. All of the non-residential parcels were mapped and there was a survey of the residential parcels.
That does not necessarily correlate to the actuals, so when the utility was instituted and data was
received from Rocky Mountain Power, the impervious area did not translate into the number of ERUs
anticipated. The analysis saw almost 4,000 commercial ERUs while the reality was approximately
2,300. He suggested refining the non-residential ERU base and moving forward with this process.

Additional discussions were had about the ERU counts and what can be done in the future. It is
possible to check all of the accounts, match them with parcels, and see if any parcels are not being
assessed. Three scenarios were presented to the City Council for consideration:

Mr. Philpot shared information about the baseline scenario. The charts in the presentation slides make
it possible to understand whether the Storm Enterprise Fund is actually covering the obligations that
have been assigned to it. He noted that the chart shows the coverage target is not being met.
Typically, a bond document will set that at 1.25x coverage. That target was set here at 1.5x and the
amount is below that. The other chart shows that not enough revenue is generated to fund the capital
improvements and operations. This indicates that something needs to be done, but the question is
what should be done. One of the options is a rate change, but it is also possible to reduce the level of
service or to change the CIP horizon. An assumption has been made that the City wants to take action.

Scenario #2 information was shared, which is an upfront rate increase of 30% in 2027. With that
upfront increase, all of the targets are met and the coverage issue is addressed. Mr. Philpot shared
information about what the ERU fee would look like, which is an increase to $8.50 in 2027. Some
context was provided through other communities. The Salt Lake County average is $8.00. The $8.50
amount would be above that average, but Holladay would not be the highest in the County. Different
cities have taken different approaches. Some want to wait and see while others want to address issues
more immediately. While this information provides an economic comparison, there are variables.

Scenario #3 shows an annual increase of 5% starting in Fiscal Year 2026. This starts to build revenue
sufficiency with the debt coverage and gets closer to the target. Mr. Philpot shared information about
what that would look like over time. For instance, it would be $6.83 in 2026, $7.20 in 2027, $7.60 in
2028, $8.00 in 2029, and $8.40 in 2030. If the City Council chooses this option, he would suggest
$8.50 as the target point. He shared a comparison chart with other cities, which indicates that
Holladay would be around average in the area. Discussions were had about the different options. Mr.
Philpot explained that there are some pros and cons to consider with all of the scenarios presented.
He stated that the intention is for the enterprise fund to operate on its own without receiving a subsidy.
Stormwater is an area where that is murkier because there are challenges with storm infrastructure.
However, most will look at this matter from a sustainability and self-sufficiency perspective. These
enterprise funds should cover their obligations. That is what is attempted to be shown in the slides.

Mr. Philpot presented some next steps to the City Council. It is possible to create unlimited scenarios
using the model for utility rates. Mr. Philpot noted that the Council can discuss an upfront increase
versus an annual increase. Alternatively, the scenarios can be modified based on Council Member
direction. Final recommendations can then be brought back. Typically, the final recommendations
are presented and then there is a public hearing held. He explained that a Final Rate Study will also
be prepared and asked for Council Member comments.

Mayor Dahle noted that another scenario could include a review of the ERUs in the City. That process
could determine whether some are not paying the fee that should be. Effort could be put into making
sure as many properties as possible are being captured. That being said, that kind of process might
delay the implementation of a fee increase. Mr. Philpot confirmed that it is possible to choose a
gradual increase. During that time, it would also be possible to further study the ERUs. Mayor Dahle
believes that everyone should be paying their fair share. Mr. Philpot noted that if there are budget

Discussions were had about the scenarios that were presented. Mayor Dahle noted that the City
Council needs to determine which option makes the most sense for the community. Mr. Philpot
shared additional information about Scenario #2 and the upfront increase. There are several options
that the City can consider. He shared an example situation that happened in Highland. Council
Member Emily Gray asked if most cities choose a gradual increase. Mr. Philpot confirmed that a lot
will choose that option, but some choose a hybrid scenario where there is some of that increase seen
upfront and then there are inflationary increases after. The gradual increase can be easier to absorb,
so that tends to be the preferred alternative for cities. That being said, he has seen upfront increases,
hybrid approaches, and even a delayed approach. He referenced what happened in Centerville many
years ago where there was a significant increase to the rate. Mr. Philpot reminded the City Council

         b.     Overview of Budget – Gina Chamness
Finance Director, Christian Larsen, presented the Budget Overview document. He shared
information about the different funds in the City. Approximately 66% of expenditures are related
to the General Fund, which includes items such as the Unified Police Department (“UPD”), Unified
Fire Authority (“UFA”), administration, and parks. The second largest for the Fiscal Year 2026
budget is the Stormwater Fund at 7.78%. However, he expects to see that decrease next year, because
the last of the bond proceeds are being spent. There is also the Cottonwood Mall RDA, Capital
Projects Fund, Grants Account, Debt Service Fund, Holladay Village RDA, and Arts Fund listed. As
for Spring Lane and the remodel of City Hall, those items have been left off of the Tentative Budget
at this point due to uncertainty about the Federal earmark. Once there is more clarity about that, an
amendment can be made.

Mr. Larsen next reviewed the Fiscal Year 2026 All Funds Revenue Summary, which shows where
most of the City revenue comes from. Approximately 60% comes from taxes, such as sales tax,
property tax, and franchise tax. As for the Intergovernmental item listed, that is primarily the road
funds but also includes tax increments within the RDAs. The Contributions item relates to the internal
transfers made within the City as well as grants. The Charges For Service item primarily has to do
with the Stormwater Fund. Mr. Larsen shared a chart that reviews the General Fund by Department.
The largest line item shown there is Law Enforcement, with 34.85% of the General Fund budget. The
second largest line item is for the Fire Department. He next reviewed a chart that looks at the General
Fund by Expense Type.

Mr. Larsen reviewed the Personnel Costs, which shows a breakdown of the wages, benefits, and
additional pay. Approximately 30% of the compensation is in the form of benefits. Almost all
personnel costs are coming out of the General Fund, but some are in the Stormwater and Arts Fund.

Mr. Larsen stated that approximately 89% of the expenditures are for Staff and 10% are for elected
officials. Some of the major personnel changes were reviewed, including a 3% cost of living
adjustment (“COLA”). This represents an increase of $72,559.

Other Major Expense Changes were reviewed. Mr. Larsen explained that there will be discussions
about UPD and UFA at the City Council Meeting on May 15, 2025. There is also a $90,513 increase

        c.      Revenue – Gina Chamness
Mr. Larsen shared a General Fund Revenue Sources chart with the City Council. Approximately 79%
of the General Fund revenue comes from taxes and approximately 9% comes from Intergovernmental.
Next was Licenses and Permits, Justice Court Revenue, Other Revenue, Contributions, and Charges
For Service. Mr. Larsen explained that there is a desire to have a split between property tax, sales
tax, and other revenue sources. There is a fairly even split, which is positive. Mr. Larsen reviewed
the Sales Tax Forecast information. It looks at the sales tax by quarter over the last few fiscal years.
The blue line represents the actuals and there is a significant increase seen between 2021 and 2023.
He pointed out that in 2024 and 2025, there has been a leveling off of the sales tax numbers. What is
shown in the Tentative Budget is a conservative number. He next referenced the Property Tax New
Growth line item, Motor Vehicle Fee-in-Lieu, and the Salt Lake County Road Funds 5th of 5th.

The Franchise Tax Enbridge line item was discussed. Mr. Larsen shared information about what was
budgeted in the past, but at this point, there has been a significant falloff in the amount. There were
several years where it was fairly stable, but that has since changed. This line item will bring the
budget back down to be a little more conservative. As for the Building Permits line item, there is a
slight increase over the prior budget. The prior year’s actuals were significantly greater than the
budget. Several large projects are anticipated to be completed in Fiscal Year 2026. Mr. Larsen
discussed the Millcreek Reimbursement line item. Some additional officers were hired who are
generating revenue in the form of tickets. There are also small adjustments listed in the document.

Council Member Gray asked if it is possible to establish a standard for the City to hold the stormwater
