     monopine. Staff recommends the diameter of the array be reduced to be within 2’ of the ends of

3. Continuation of Public Hearing for Petition to rezone from R-1-6 to R-3/CO property at 140-152 W. 2nd
   Street and 139 N. Wall Avenue. (Attachment D)

  Mr. Greg Day stated since this item was tabled at the November meeting, they have incorporated some
  of the public comment into a new design, keeping single family lots on the frontage of 2 nd Street in an
  effort to maintain the integrity of the existing neighborhood. The number of townhomes also has been
  reduced, and the rezoning is limited to the townhome area.

  Mr. Simpson stated this petition had been tabled at the November meeting, and the applicant is now
  proposing to retain single-family lots along 2nd Street. He reviewed the proposed layout, stating these
  new lots would have two or three frontages along a road, which is not allowed by the Subdivision Code.
  He stated the application shows the number of townhomes has been reduced from 43 to 34 and
  includes five single-family lots. A Development Agreement is provided, with similar standards to those
  of the existing townhome project on the corner. This is the first step towards development, and the
  Commission will be required to review both the subdivision and group dwelling application for the
  proposed project in the future. He reminded the Commission at the November meeting, they had
  considered and recommended approval of an amendment to the Lynn Community Plan which would
  allow the multi-family zone to extend west of the corner into the neighborhood. At that time, the
  Commission found edges could be created so long as the project does not exclude existing residential
  lots along 2nd Street and would not isolate any particular property. The applicant had then requested
  the zoning petition be tabled so they could meet this requirement. The current proposal would retain
  properties at 136 W 2nd Street and 152 West 2nd Street as single-family residential, as well as developing
  new single-family lots along West 2nd Street. The proposal also would affect an existing right-of-way
  which extends to the single-family lot at 152 West 2nd, but there is no documentation the current owner
  has consented for this to occur.

  He then referred to the history of the area, stating while Bingham Fort was significant to the history of
  this area, some of the homes have become dilapidated and the individual historic significance is
  questionable. Others remain in good condition and should be preserved ands incorporated into the
  proposed development project. He also suggested the brick color used in neighboring townhomes
  match that of the historic home at 140 West 2nd Street. The applicant is also proposing to retain the
  history of the existing dirt access road by creating a trail into the project. He suggested the owner work
  with both Weber Heritage Foundation and Ogden City Landmarks Commission in order to identify
  features of the area which should be preserved as part of the development plan. He provided two
  options for the Commission to consider to assure double-fronted lots are not created and historic
  features of the area are preserved. Staff recommendation is for approval of the petition to rezone the
  properties from R-1-6 (single-family) to R-3/CO (multiple-family/conditional overly), subject to the
  following conditions:
  1. The rezone only apply to the property consisting of the townhomes/row-housing portion of the
     development and the single-family lots at the southwest end of the development retain the R-1-6
     single-family zoning.
  2. The subdivision of the property be completed so that zoning boundary lines follow property-lines.
  3. The townhome/row-housing portion of the development incorporate the existing historic single-family
     home at 140 W 2nd Street as shown on staff’s recommended site plans (2 options provided).
  4. The single-family lots proposed at the southwest end of the development be oriented east and west
     to prevent the creation of double-fronting lots as shown on staff’s recommended site plans (2 options


                                                    [3]
   provided).
5. The petitioner work with the Weber County Heritage Foundation and Ogden City Landmarks
   Commission to plan amenities throughout the project that recognize the historic nature of the site
   and these specific features be presented to the Planning Commission with the future group-dwelling
   application for approval.
6. The townhomes/row-housing implement varying earth-toned brick (brick being the main exterior
   material) colors among the different units that are compatible with the existing historic single-family

7. Legal access be maintained through the project for the large single-family property at 150 W 2nd

  constructed to screen the venting system and other utility boxes. She stated it is anticipated a mural
  would be painted on the east wall of the building, and the stucco finish would provide a blank screen for

  contact with various artist groups and are hopeful a mural can be designed which would link the
  building to the new plaza.

  materials, and the intent is to unify the surface and cover the scarring of the hotel wall surface. He
  stated there is now a large stucco surface on the east side of the hotel, and the intent is to match its
  color. He stated the hotel is on the corner, and as is typical of buildings in the downtown, the street-side
  surfaces have more detail in their appearance, while the appearance of the rear surface is lacking in
  detail and material, with stucco or cement finishes being typical. The stand-alone stucco wall is
  intended to screen the venting system and other utilities as the building has exposure on three streets.
  Staff recommendation is for approval subject to all site review and all departmental comments being
  satisfied.

  Commissioner Blaisdell commented most of the architecture in the downtown is brick and felt the
  stucco appears out of place. Commissioner Southwick asked about the color of the stucco, stating it
  would blend better with the architecture of the hotel if it were to match the vertical banding rather than

                                                     [8]
  the existing panel. Mr. Simpson stated while the building is now exposed on three street sides, the east
  wall is considered the back of the building. The Commission is to determine whether the proposed

  visible as the front and side and will somewhat be screened with landscaping and trees and other

6. CBD Site Plan, Phase 1 Overall Development Plan for Wonder Block development of Block 19.
   (Attachment G)

  Mr. Brock Loomis stated he had received the recommendations of Staff and found them acceptable.
  Mr. Robb Berg ten described the details of the project, stating care is taken to implement both the Make
  Ogden downtown plan and current redevelopment patterns of the area. The project will consist of
  residential, retail, office and hotel space, encompassing much of the existing block between Grant and
  Lincoln. Both pedestrian and vehicular connectivity is important to the plan with three access points to
  parking structures as well as mid-block pedestrian crossings. He stated the first phase is to create a
  parking structure to accommodate the District Court building, with the second phase being construction
  of residential units along 26th Street and Lincoln with office and retail uses on the ground floor along
  with additional parking structures. The final phase would be construction of a hotel on Historic 25 th
  Street. The project will accommodate the 25th Street Design Standards, the Make Ogden Plan and will
  also accommodate the new reconstruction of 26th Street in this area.

  Commissioner Southwick asked if the residential units could accommodate owner occupancy or if they
  were strictly rentals, stating he felt there is more of a need for owner occupancy in the downtown. Mr.
  Loomis stated the intent is for market rate rental units.

  Mr. Montgomery stated the Commission is to review both the overall development plan to determine
  whether it is consistent with the CBD while specific building design review will occur at a later date. He
  identified locations where pedestrian/vehicle conflicts may occur which need to be addressed as the
  design is finalized. He reviewed both the landscaping and parking requirements, stating the Ordinance
  requires 10% on-site landscaping, and if the Commission determines the proposed roof-top amenities
  can count as landscaping, this requirement is satisfied. The Commission also has the authority to
  reduce parking requirements in the CBD if it is found that parking occurs during different peak hours
  and can be shared between residential and other uses. He stated the parking provided satisfies the
  needs of the courts building and provides 1.23 spaces per residential unit which fits with the intent of
  walkability in the downtown. The plan meets the standards of the Make Ogden Plan by limiting ground

8. Request to Amend Conditions for Nonconforming Use Expansion, for Stop & Go Market, 809 28th Street.
   (Attachment I)

  Ms. Hina Yamin stated when this item was last reviewed by the Planning Commission the hours were
  limited from 7am until 9pm. This request is to extend those hours to be from 6am until midnight. She
  stated many of the existing neighborhood markets have later hours and the limited hours affects both
  the business and the neighborhood as services are not always available when needed. She stated no
  complaints have been received from the neighborhood, and much work has taken place to satisfy other
  conditions of the previous approval at an expense to the owner.

  Mr. Simpson stated this is a request to amend the conditional use permit which was approved in March
  2020 to extend the hours of operation. All other conditions of that approval have been satisfied, with
  new curb, gutter and sidewalk being installed as well as screening and landscaping. He stated other
  neighborhood convenience stores have no restrictions on their hours and at the time of previous
  approval, Staff had researched other neighborhood markets and made their recommendation based
  that information. It has since been found that the advertised hours of operation are not always the
  reality of what is happening and many are open later in the night-time. He stated crime data indicates
  only complaints relating to potential theft and minors attempting to purchase alcohol. He stated much
  of the business tapers off in the later hours, and there is typically not a stacking problem. He stated
  noise and lights are shielded by the existing masonry wall. Staff recommendation is for approval of the
  hours of operation being extended so long as delivery and trash service vehicles are restricted to the
  hours listed on the previous approval.

  Mr. Montgomery stated he had received a telephone call from a neighbor to the east of the business
  asking the hours not be extended due to music played from vehicles has an impact on his young family
  and their ability to sleep.

  Commissioner Sandau stated the hours had been limited due to concerns of neighborhood residents
  and felt reluctant to extend those hours. Commissioner Southwick stated he would support one hour
  earlier and one hour later but was concerned with the effect of extending the hours until midnight on the
  surrounding neighborhood. Commissioner Boykin stated the business provides a service to
  neighborhood residents, and as the owners have been compliant with the previous approval, he would
  support the hours being extended as requested. Commissioner Stoker expressed concern that there are
  no restrictions on many of the other existing markets, and agreed with the applicant the hours would
  create a more even playing field. Commissioner Southwick asked if these other markets were also
  nonconforming. Mr. Stratford stated most are nonconforming, but were already in place and there was
  no review of their hours of operation. The change or expansion of the nonconforming use triggered
  Planning Commission review at which time the Commission felt some of the impacts to the
  surrounding neighborhood could be mitigated by limiting the hours of operation. He stated these are
  reviewed on a case-by-case basis to address impacts of a particular use on surrounding land uses and

                                                   [12]
   not based on other uses in other neighborhoods.

   Ms. Yamin stated the only complaint at the previous meeting was from one neighbor who has since
   moved, and she felt other residents are not concerned with the hours.

   Commissioner Sandau expressed support of extending the hours until 10:30 or 11pm, but was reluctant
   to extend until midnight. Commissioner Boykin asked if neighborhood residents were informed of this
   request and if there were any complaints or concerns. Mr. Simpson stated notices of the meeting were
   sent to property owners and tenants within a 300’ radius and Staff had received only the one telephone
   call. Commissioner Safsten noted the police data indicates the owners have refused sales to minors
   and there have been no issues relating to night-time crime. Commissioner Boykin stated some
   convenience stores are open 24 hours and others until midnight or 1am, and asked where the
   discomfort of extending the hours is a concern as there have been no neighborhood complaints.

   MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Blaisdell to deny the request to extend the hours from
           6am until midnight, but to approve extending the hours from 6am until 10pm based on the
           findings there is good cause to extend the hours of operation and the increase in hours will not
           impact surrounding properties with the recommendations of Staff, but extending the hours
           until midnight would impact surrounding neighbors. Approval is subject to Staff
           recommendations as follows:
           1. Deliveries and trash pick-up on the site be limited to between 7am and 8pm;
           2. Hours of operation occur only between 6am and 10pm; and
           3. All other conditions of the previously approved expansion of the nonconforming market be
                maintained.
           Motion was seconded by Commissioner Schade and passed 6-3 with Commissioners
           Blaisdell, Safsten, Sandau, Schade, Southwick and Stoker voting aye and Commissioners
           Boykin, Garner and Graf voting no. Commissioners explained their negative vote was based on
           their feeling there is no data identifying trouble at this location and they were supportive of
           extending the hours until either 11pm or later.
