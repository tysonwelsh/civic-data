          The purpose of the work session was to review the agenda for the City Council meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.;
discuss fireworks and open flame restrictions for 2024; review the Management Study of the Ogden Prosecutor’s Office; discuss
the compensation and benefits proposal for Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 budget; receive an update from Administration; discuss
Council business; and hear Council comments.

Agenda Review
        Acting Chair White and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council
meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Fireworks and Open Flame Restrictions – 2024
          Fire Chief Mathieu reported that during the June 18, 2024, work session, Fire Marshall Brown presented the 2024 fire
restrictions and summarized the notices that would be published in the City. Since that time, there has been several structure fires
and wild grass fires in and around Ogden City and the Fire Department made the decision on June 24, 2024, to implement a ban
on fireworks and open flames in certain geographical areas of the City effective through October 15, 2024.
          Fire Marshall Brown stated the restrictions impact the following geographical areas:
               1. All areas east of Harrison Boulevard from the south city boundary, north to 2 nd Street, west on 2nd Street to
                   Monroe Boulevard and then north to the Ogden City boundary.
               2. All wooded areas along the Ogden and Weber River Parkway beginning at Harrison Boulevard and continuing
                   along the pathways to the west city boundary and the south city boundary. This also includes all associated
                   parks along the river corridor.
               3. All of Fort Buenaventura, the city baseball park and dog park area located off A Avenue.
               4. The old landfill property located at approximately 2550 A Avenue, near Fort Buenaventura.
              5. All open fields, vacant lots, wooded areas, and brush covered hillsides throughout the City.
         Fire Marshall Brown also presented a map identifying the affected areas. He also added that the Department is also
working to develop a ‘stop-light’ notification that can be used to inform the public regarding the types of fires and whether
fireworks are allowed based upon whether the City is in a the green, yellow, or red status.
         The Council engaged in discussion with Fire Department staff regarding the conditions throughout the City that have
contributed to the fireworks/open fire ban.

Management Study of the Ogden City Prosecutor’s Office
          A memo from Council staff explained the City Attorney requested this study to gain insight into division operations and
needs, and to receive recommendations for increasing the efficiency and effectiveness of the office. The information from the
study will be used to develop policies and procedures, identify any additional staffing needs, and acquire better technology to
assist the office.
          A representative of Tanner, LLC Consulting Firm, Mackey Smith, used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
summarize the findings and proposed final report on the Management Study of the Ogden City Prosecutor’s Office. There are
several recommendations relative to processes of the Prosecutor's Office, policies, technology, community justice, and short- and
long-term staffing. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Throughout the presentation there was discussion among the Council, the consulting team, and City Attorney Williams
regarding appropriate staffing levels based upon caseload; the types of cases prosecuted by the Prosecutor’s Office; and access to
suggested technology.

Fiscal Year 2024-2025 Budget – Compensation and Benefits Proposal
          A memo from Council staff explained the work session of July 2, 2024, will be an opportunity for the Council to review
the preliminary findings of the Compensation Study being completed by McGrath Consulting, Inc. The study is being finalized
and a full presentation from a representative of McGrath will take place on July 9, 2024. Within the Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025
budget, Administration has set aside a total of $4,657,950 for compensation and benefits adjustments. This includes $3,131,275 in
the General Fund and $1,526,675 in all other funds. The McGrath study is intended to provide recommendations on all salary
ranges and position and/or title changes needed to provide competitive wages for City employees. When the budget was proposed
in May of this year, Administration did not know how much funding would be needed to bring employees to the new minimums
recommended by McGrath, bring public safety employees to the appropriate step, and provide any merit increases. With the
preliminary figures known, Administration is beginning to have a more accurate estimate of the need and will be discussing those
findings with the Council.
          Human Resources Manager Briskey used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss preliminary findings of the
McGrath Consulting study; the presentation focused on compensation terms and definitions, assessment of current conditions,
employee turnover rates, salary schedule findings, modernizing titles, and estimated implementation costs. The employee
compensation proposal for Fiscal Year (FY) 25 includes $4.6 million for market adjustments to accomplish the following:
               • Implement recommended salary tables and pay grades for FY25 (August);
               • Bring all employees to the recommended McGrath Compensation Study pay grade minimum or closest step;
               • Implement recommended job title changes;
               • Up to 4% for general employees who didn’t receive at least 4% with the ‘bring to minimum’ implementation;
               • Eligible for step increase on anniversary date for sworn fire and police.
          Ms. Briskey concluded McGrath Consulting has provided the following recommendations:
               • Salary Schedule increased by the Consumer Price Index-Urban (CPI-U) percentage or by a local economic
                   indicator, if preferred.
               • Annually, the Salary Schedule should be adjusted for economic reasons.
               • A Salary Schedule has a typical life span of 3-5 years, at which time market conditions typically necessitate a
                   review.
               • The City can strive to prolong the life of their Schedule if it continues to commit to maintaining its
                   competitiveness with the external market by ensuring market updates occur.
               • Given the current competitive market, the City is recommended to conduct a market update in three (3) years.
                        o Analyzing turnover and other human resource type metrics should help indicate if an external market
                            update is required sooner or can be pushed back a year.
          Throughout the presentation, there was discussion between the Council and Administrative staff regarding the process by
which employment positions are classified in the City’s salary schedule, the reasoning behind recommended title changes, benefits
packages, and consideration of private sector wage scales for some positions. Ms. Briskey and Department Directors also
answered questions from the Council regarding any new positions being created throughout the City.
