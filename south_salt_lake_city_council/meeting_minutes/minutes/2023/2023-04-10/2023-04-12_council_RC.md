       6. INFORMATION.

              a. Flood Preparation Update
                 City Engineer, Dennis Pay, and Stormwater Division Manager, Corby Talbot,
                 both gave information, a copy of which is attached and incorporated by this
                 reference, to the Council for their update on the anticipated run off. The
                 material showed this year’s snow water equivalent (SWE) in comparison to
                 previous years. It also showed flows on Millcreek at 460 West from the
                 beginning of the month up until the present day. The current flow is at 35
                 cubic feet per second. The normal highwater flow at Millcreek is around 65
                 cubic feet per second. Mr. Pay said that experts consider the flood stage to
                 be at 295 cubic feet per second. He said that although waters are rising
                 things are still looking good as of right now.

                 Stormwater Division Manager, Corby Talbot, said that to date, City
                 employees have filled 5,000 sandbags. The sandbag filling machine will
                 arrive by the end of the week and will help to increase numbers.

                 With the help of City IT and the Police Department, they have now installed
                 a camera that will provide live feed of Millcreek from about 650 East and
                 3300 South so that they can monitor the rising levels.

                 The Council members asked about the checking of stormwater drains for
                 debris. Mr. Talbot said that they have been pretty good about checking the
                 City’s stormwater system and said that the good thing about South Salt Lake
                 is that it’s at the bottom of the valley and not next to the hillside.

                 They shared a resident’s story who noticed that their storm drain was
                 ponding and removed the surrounding debris. They said that it’s a good idea
                 for all residents to do.

                 Mr. Talbot also pointed to another resource through the Salt Lake County
                 Flood Control website that has a link for ‘Flood Preparedness’ that can be
                 found at https://slco.org/flood-control/flood-preparedness-manual/. It has
                 information on the weather, snowpack, stream flows, videos on sandbag
                 placements.

                 Council Member Siwik asked if there was anything else that residents can do
                 to help the City and community stay ahead.

                 Mr. Pay answered that keeping curbs and gutters clear of leaves, debris will
                 be a tremendous help.

                 Mayor Wood added that the County has sent out mailers to residents who
                 live along the creek asking them to also keep debris away from Millcreek
                 and that the City has also posted ‘Swift Water’ signs at Fitts Park.

Unfinished Business

   1. A Resolution of the South Salt Lake City Council to Participate in the Municipal
      Alternate Voting Methods Pilot Project (Ranked Choice Voting).
      City Recorder, Ariel Andrus, went over the information that was discussed at the last
      meeting regarding the upcoming election. She said that she also received an email
      confirming that Salt Lake City, Millcreek, and Midvale are confirmed to be participating
      in Ranked Choice Voting. Riverton and Cottonwood Heights will be discussing the option
      in their upcoming Council meetings as well. She referenced the County’s contract that
      was provided to the Council that shows the estimated costs. The deadline to let the
      County and State know is April 28th.

       Council Member Siwik asked what the difference would be between the costs of holding
       a traditional election over Ranked Choice Voting.

       Ms. Andrus said that for a traditional election, with a primary, the cost would be around
       $38,000. For Ranked Choice Voting, without a primary, the initial cost would be $24,000
       and would also fluctuate based many cities choose to participate.

       If South Salt Lake is the fourth city to opt into Ranked Choice Voting, the additional cost
       would be around $9,000 but would still have an overall savings in comparison to a
       traditional election.

       The filing periods would also change with either choice. The filing period for Ranked
       Choice Voting would be from August 8th-15th, which gives additional time for people to
       decide if they want to run and, in turn, shortens the election cycle. Whereas the filing
       period for a traditional election is from June 1st-7th and has a longer election cycle.

       Council Member Huff made a motion to approve this Resolution.
