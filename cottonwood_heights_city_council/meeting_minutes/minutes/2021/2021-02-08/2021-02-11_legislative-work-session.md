MINUTES OF THE COTTONWOOD HEIGHTS CITY COUNCIL LEGISLATIVE
WORK SESSION HELD THURSDAY, FEBRUARY 11, 2021, AT 9:00 A.M. THE
MEETING WAS HELD ELECTRONICALLY, WITHOUT A PHYSICAL LOCATION
DUE TO THE CURRENT COVID-19 PANDEMIC AND AS AUTHORIZED BY THE
GOVERNOR’S EXECUTIVE ORDER DATED 18 MARCH 2020

1.      Welcome and Determination – Mayor Peterson.

Mayor Mike Peterson called the meeting to order at 9:01 a.m. and welcomed those listening. He
read in its entirety the declaration giving the Council the authority to hold the meeting via Zoom,
pursuant to Utah Code Annotated §52-4-207(4).

2.      Legislative Update and Discussion with City Lobbyists Mr. Greg Curtis, Mr. Brian
        Allen, and Ms. Chantel Nate.

City Lobbyist, Brian Allen, presented a legislative update and reported that the Accessory
Dwelling Unit (“ADU”) Bill is still being negotiated. Discussions would continue between
Representatives Ward and Schultz. He stated that they may not get everything they want but will
be much closer than where they started.

Two billboard Bills were currently being negotiated. The League of Cities and Towns (“ULCT”)
will meet and move toward a consensus on one of the Bills. He believed it was closer to resembling
the Cottonwood Heights City Ordinance and was moving in the right direction. The second Bill
had not yet been addressed.

The Property Tax Bill included a third substitute that further mitigates a portion of the impact to
the City. The initial Bill included a $16 million to $32 million impact on local government. The
third substitute has a $2 million impact on local government. Mr. Allen reported that Senator
Harper put forward a Bill that would increase the property tax deduction for businesses from
$15,000 to $50,000 and will have a significant financial impact on local government. After
negotiations, the Senator agreed to reduce it to $25,000 and reinstate a previous provision. There
will be a break for small businesses and the hit to local government is much reduced. Mr. Allen

Representative Paul Ray’s Bill on permitting and inspecting was next discussed. Mr. Allen
believed they will reach a consensus that the ULCT seems to be willing to support. He had not
reviewed the language and stated there is no substitute. He reported that Senator Andrews’
Affordable Housing Bill is still in Rules and has several issues they are trying to work through.

City Lobbyist, Greg Curtis, reported that Representative Dunnigan’s Bill was substituted and
passed out of the House on Tuesday with broad support. The Bill will direct $300,000 to
Cottonwood Heights over the next 15 years. The outlier was Taylorville, which was represented
by Representative Dunnigan as well as Senator Harper. This has caused consternation in the
Senate and they are meeting to discuss it further. Mr. Curtis believed they will carry the day with
how it is currently drafted. The Bill will go to the Senate Committee for a vote and with no
amendments, will pass, and return to the House, if amended.

Funding for Wasatch Boulevard was next addressed. Mr. Curtis stated significant funding
decisions are being discussed but no decisions have been made. There is a Bill in the House that
would move the Office of Outdoor Recreation to the Department of Natural Resources and change
who directs how the money is disbursed. There has been concern expressed with giving the
Executive Branch $100 million for Parks, Trails, and Open Space without any legislative direction.
The Office of Outdoor Recreation has a match component where they evaluate the local
government contribution. He reported that President Adams is reluctant and wants a new dedicated
revenue stream using ongoing money to bond.

In response to a question raised regarding police reform, Mr. Allen stated that there is a group
called Listen, Love, Lead that has promoted multiple Bills that have been moving through the
process. He believed there could be as many as 40 police reform Bills. Those in law enforcement
and advocates have worked together and are moving fairly quickly through the process with broad
support. Multiple Bills have not had a hearing and will continue to be monitored. The Use of
Force Bills were advancing as well. Mr. Allen reported that the Citizen Review Board Bills are
similar with one passing and the other failing due to exclusion of running for public office.

Mr. Curtis reported that there may be a large amount of money designated to the Office of Outdoor
Recreation. He suggested that City officials initiate talks with those at the County. He explained
that Salt Lake County receives .05%, which equates to $12 million, plus an additional $12 to $13
million per year. He believed that some of it can be used for active transportation, including trails.
Mayor Peterson noted that the City received a $1.5 million grant previously for the Bonneville
Shoreline and they have tried to leverage that. The City will continue to look for additional
opportunities to partner and collaborate with the County.

Council Member Mikell noted that she and Mayor Peterson met with the Utah Department of
Transportation (“UDOT”) several weeks ago. She believed there was a notion of mitigation
payments if a mass transit hub were to be located in Cottonwood Heights. Even if the gondola or
cog rail ends up immediately adjacent to the property purchased, there will be impacts to
Cottonwood Heights. She asked if there was a way language can be included with any UDOT

Mr. Allen stated that the ADU Bill is close to receiving a final offer with involvement from

3.      Adjourn.

MOTION: Mayor Peterson moved to adjourn. The motion was seconded by Council Member
Mikell. The motion passed with the unanimous consent of the Council.

The Work Session adjourned at 9:35 a.m.
