 2. DISCUSSION TOPICS
         a. Agenda structure for various meetings held by the Council and meeting expectations
            Chair McConnehey asked what the Council was hoping to see regarding structure of
            various Council meetings. Vice Chair Bloom said her thought was that holding work
            sessions on off-meeting nights would help make regular Council meetings shorter and
            more effective.

            Council Member Whitelock said it had been her understanding that the Council would
            continue with work sessions prior to regular Council meetings, with additional work
            sessions on off-meeting weeks. She said in her opinion, the most important thing was for
            the Council to have good dialogue on important issues and give good feedback to staff.
            Council Member Whitelock said she was comfortable allowing the Chair to determine
            when work meetings were needed, but said she believed the Council needed to agree on
            a set end time for the meetings.

            Council Member Green expressed the opinion that word smithing and going back and
            forth regarding policy needed to be done in work session to make Council meetings more
            efficient. He said he agreed with scheduling work meetings prior to regular Council
            meetings, with the possibility of additional work session discussion following regular
            meetings. Council Member Green suggested setting the end time at 8:00 or 8:30, and
            emphasized that citizen and staff-involved issues should be scheduled up front.

       c. Introduction of the 2023 Draft General Plan
          Public Liaison and Policy Analyst Cassidy Hansen suggested the Council take a month to
          review the 2023 Draft General Plan, with in-depth discussion at the March 15 Committee
          of the Whole meeting, giving staff time to make requested revisions in preparation for
          review and adoption on March 22, 2023. Council Member Green said he did not want to
          schedule the public hearing for the same evening as intended adoption. He suggested
          scheduling and advertising the public hearing for March 8. A majority of the Council
          indicated support. Ms. Hansen thanked staff, the General Plan Committee, and the
          Planning Commission for their work on the Draft General Plan.

          Chair McConnehey requested to move Agenda Item (d) to later in the meeting and
          requested discussion of agenda item (e) next.

       e. Discussion of proposed Amendments to Title 1 related to change in form of
          government and other text changes
          Chair McConnehey said one of the proposed amendments to Title 1 would change the
          campaign finance disclosure date from June to April, making the question timely. He said
          the remaining proposed amendments did not have the same timeline, and asked how the
          Council wanted to proceed. Responding to a question from the Council, City
          Administrator Korban Lee said the proposed campaign finance disclosure date change
          would spread out the work load for staff.

          Council Member Jacob asked if the June campaign finance disclosure date really created
          a significant burden for staff. Council Member Green responded that reports required
          staff time, particularly in an election year. He pointed out the Council had already
          discussed the proposed campaign finance disclosure date change. Council Member Green
          said he preferred to review and approve all proposed changes that evening.

          Council Member Green referred to page 13, line 215 Section C regarding qualifications for
          City Council, and requested the words “and Mayor” be removed since the mayor was

       f. Discussion regarding a petition by Waleska Iglesias requesting a General Land Use
          Map amendment, Zone Map amendment, and Concept Development Plan for
          Redwood Housing on 0.56 acres located at 9222 S Redwood Road
          Associate Planner Lisa Elgin asked if the Council was interested in formally considering
          or willing to offer additional guidance regarding: (1) the request for change in the Land
          Use Map from Community Commercial to Professional Office; (2) the request to add the
          Senior Housing Overlay District to the existing Professional Office Zone District; and (3)
          considering a development agreement with the applicant, which would delay the
          applicant’s request for utility service until after completion of the new Cemetery Tank.

          Ms. Elgin showed conceptual images of the proposed Redwood Housing and the proposed
          Future Land Use Map. Council Member Green believed the Land Use Subcommittee had
          seen the proposal twice and both times did not like the plan. Council Member Whitelock
          believed Council Member Worthen liked the senior housing idea but agreed the Land Use
          Subcommittee definitely had concerns.

          Regarding the first question asked by staff, Council Member Whitelock said she did not
          like that the proposal would put Professional Office right in the middle of Commercial,
          with intent for the Professional Office to be residential. She expressed interest in knowing
          what the General Plan Committee had envisioned for the subject area. Staff showed the
          Future Land Use Map as revised by the General Plan Committee, which showed the
          subject property zoned Commercial. Council Member Green said he agreed with Council
          Member Whitelock.

          Council Member Jacob said the request to change Community Commercial to Professional
          Office did not bother him, but he was not in favor of adding Senior Housing Overlay
          District to the Professional Office Zone District. Chair McConnehey said he agreed. Council
          Member Green commented that the subject area was a gateway to the City, and he was
          not sure he wanted that particular gateway to be high-density residential apartments.

          project and believed it would be the first apartment complex in West Jordan approaching
          60 units per acre. The proposed density and the planned ERUs concerned him.

          Chair McConnehey moved to suspend Council Rules to give the applicant up to three
          minutes to address the Council. Council Member Green seconded the motion, which
          passed by unanimous vote (6-0).

          Brett Anderson, attorney for Waleska and Mayor of Farmington City, worked with
          Waleska on similar projects. He shared his experience with a similar project in
          Farmington and noted the type of occupants involved in a Senior Housing project were
          great neighbors and provided a daytime population for the commercial. He said a positive
          synergistic energy was possible between the two types of developments.

          Chair McConnehey thanked Mayor Anderson for his comments. Chair McConnehey was
          concerned that the proposed Senior Housing Overlay would be on the opposite side of the
          street from the commercial core, whereas in Farmington the Senior Housing project given
          as an example was in the center of commercial development. He also expressed concern
          regarding the proposed scale and said if it were larger than a half-acre, he may be more
          supportive. Council Member Jacob said he agreed, and believed a Senior Housing Overlay
          had a place in the City, but not as proposed.

          Vice Chair Bloom preferred the project at a different location, such as downtown, and
          believed the proposed development in the right place would be awesome.
          Council Member Whitelock suggested the Senior Housing Overlay would work in Jordan
          Landing. She could not imagine seniors crossing Redwood Road or 9000 South to get to
          the commercial. Council Member Whitelock agreed the proposed project would be nice
          in the right location.

       g. Discussion regarding a petition by DAI Partners regarding a General Plan
          amendment, rezone, and Master Development agreement/plan for Bland Recycling
          – approximately 53.2 acres located at approximately 7141 South Highway 111
          Senior Planner Tayler Jensen showed the subject 53.2 acres on the Zoning Map, currently
          zoned Business Research Park, and showed that the current Future Land Use Map
          designated Research Park Zone. He showed a proposed conceptual plan and explained
          the request to rezone the property to High Density Residential and Community
          Commercial, using the Integrated Housing Ordinance on the property. Council Member
          Green commented that including commercial was a requirement of the Integrated

          Housing Ordinance. Mr. Jensen noted that the project would include 335 proposed
          residential units (230 townhomes and 105 single-family homes).

          Mr. Jensen asked the Council the following questions and any additional feedback they
          felt would improve the project:
              • Does the concept meet the intent of the Integrated Housing Ordinance?
              • Is the Council okay with the proposed percentage of multi-family housing
                 (exceeding standard of 13-5M-4C)?
              • Is the proposed 4.2-acre commercial parcel integrated and large enough to serve
                 the proposed development (meeting the Council’s goals and objectives)?

          Chair McConnehey wanted to make sure the need for sound walls was understood and
          included. Council Member Green said the project had been reviewed by the Land Use
          Subcommittee twice, and the Subcommittee really liked the second concept presented.
          He said the Land Use Subcommittee had suggested writing the Integrated Housing
          Ordinance to help make the second proposal work.

          Council Member Jacob said the subject property was in his neighborhood and his District.
          He did not like the proposed density for the area and was not in favor of the traffic impact
          expected on the existing neighborhood. He did not believe commercial made sense
          wedged between two residential areas. Council Member Jacob suggested there were
          better ways to integrate townhomes and single-family homes in a development.

          Vice Chair Bloom liked the changes that had evolved so far and believed more could be
          done to make the development better. She agreed with Council Member Jacob regarding
          integrating townhomes and single-family homes more cohesively. Vice Chair Bloom was
          excited with how the proposal looked and what was coming to the west side of the City.

          Council Member Green said the townhomes were put all together because of the
          topography of the land (old gravel pit). He explained part of the Integrated Housing
          Ordinance and said the proposed development would have different sizes of single-family
          lots. Part of the proposal was to provide world-class amenities for the benefit of the entire
          development and the proposed open space along U111 was intended to provide a buffer
          for noise. Council Member Green said the applicant included more parking spaces than
          required.

          Vice Chair Bloom asked how the proposed greenspace would be affected by the planned
          expansion of U111. She asked if the property would experience settling issues similar to
          another development in West Jordan. Council Member Green responded the project was
          not far enough along to have Geotech specifications. He said the applicant wanted at that
          point to know how much of the ordinance exception the Council was willing to approve.
          Council Member Green commented that he had never seen a more detailed draft MDA,
          and said the applicant intended to set aside an appropriate percentage of townhomes to
          meet affordable housing ordinance requirements.

          Mr. Jensen referred to the single proposed connecting road between the two housing
          types, and asked if the Council were comfortable with one, or would want to add another

          issue if the housing were truly integrated. Council Member Jacob said he did not like the
          idea of all the townhomes being rental units without integrating the townhomes with the

          Chair McConnehey wanted to look closely at proposed entrances to U111. Staff responded

          amenities in the townhome area. He believed the ordinance required 5% of the space to
          be commercial, and the proposed design was at approximately 8% commercial.

          Council Member Green encouraged the Council to remember that all residential in the
          project would be maintained by an HOA. Vice Chair Bloom liked where it was going.

       h. Discussion and feedback about future South West Quadrant apartments and park
          design buffer between commercial uses and existing single-family homes at Echo
          Ridge located at approximately 6700 West 9500 South within approximately 19.8
          acres
          City Planner Larry Gardner oriented the Council with objectives for the South West
          Quadrant (SWQ). He showed proposed land use compared to existing land use, and
          showed proposed phasing of the SWQ. The SWQ anticipated a residential buffer between
          Commercial Industrial Job Center uses and existing residential.

          Mr. Gardner showed conceptual renderings, and shared feedback received at a recent
          open house. He asked if the conceptual park plan provided an adequate buffer between
          the existing single-family homes of Echo Ridge and the conceptual multi-family. Vice
          Chair Bloom said she liked the plan and believed the conceptual park plan provided an
          adequate buffer. A majority of the Council expressed agreement.

          Public Liaison and Policy Analyst Cassidy Hansen continued the review of proposed

          just refer to the code. A majority of Council Members agreed.

          Chair McConnehey asked to amend lines 189-190 and end the sentence with “shall be
          retained permanently” and deleting the rest of the sentence.

          Referencing line 215, letter C. Qualifications for City Council, Council Member Whitelock
          asked that the section be reworked; separating the Election Timing from the
          Qualifications for City Council.

          A majority of Council Members asked that lines 295 through 302 be combined with 279
          through 294 and coordinate with the City Recorder to match the requirements of new
          finance reporting software being purchased for that purpose.

       b. Review of questions pertaining to policy on city survey
          Cassidy Hansen asked for Council feedback regarding proposed questions for a
          residential survey. Chair McConnehey suggested using directional terms (NE, SE) instead
          of quadrants, and Council Member Green suggested including designations such as ‘south
          of 7800 South, and west of Bangerter, rather than quadrants.

          Council Member Whitelock asked if the Council could have time to take the questions
          home for further review and consideration. City Administrator Korban Lee responded
          that the original intention had been for the survey to go out in November or December
          2022, with responses available in January prior to the budget process. Due to a number
          of factors, the survey was delayed. He said if the survey were not to go out for another
          month, responses would not be received in time for consideration with the FY2024
          budget process. Chair McConnehey suggested discussion of the issue following the next
          regularly scheduled Council meeting.

          Council Member Green expressed the opinion that the survey needed to help determine
          a baseline for how the City was doing and what could be improved. He suggested many of
          the questions were too wordy and recommended keeping the questions simple and short.

          Chair McConnehey requested changes for consistency and readability.

          Council Member Green suggested a need for a question regarding property tax and
          enterprise funds (utility fund transfer).

          Ms. Hansen thanked the Council for their comments, reminded them about the desired
          timeline, and noted a desire to keep with that schedule.

       d. Review of Council Rules Subcommittee’s Council Rules revisions
          Vice Chair Bloom briefly presented proposed revisions to Council Rules as drafted by the
          Council Rules Subcommittee. A majority of the Council indicated support for bringing the
          Council Rules revisions back to a Council meeting as a business item for possible action.

       a. CONTINUED - Agenda structure for various meetings held by the Council and
          meeting expectations
          A majority of the Council indicated support for a consistent start time of all Council
          meetings (6:00 pm). The Council discussed the possibility of setting a target length or end
          time for Council meeting evenings. Council Member Whitelock said she trusted Council
          Leadership to put together a good agenda and was happy to stay as late as needed to get
          the work of the City done in the best possible way. Council Member Green and Pack
          expressed support for adding time sensitive items to a work session after Council
          meetings. Chair McConnehey expressed the desire for meetings to be as efficient as
          possible.
