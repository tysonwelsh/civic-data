Council Member Bradshaw asked that the following comment be included in the record 
for this meeting:
Mr. Chair, as leader of the Democratic caucus, I would like to provide 
comment.
As Democrats, Ann, Jim and I, are usually considered the political left. I 
for one am proud of that characterization, in no small part because I 
believe that we on the left greatly value diversity. We recognize, and 
celebrate, the distinctive attributes that make up each individual, and the 
richness diverse communities contribute to our Salt Lake County family. In 
fact, the lapel pin that I am wearing, created by former Democratic Mayor 
Peter Corroon, says “Diverse” Salt Lake County in acknowledgement of 
those facts.  
Yet, we do seek a sameness. We believe that regardless of your gender 
identity or expression, you should have the same access to comprehensive 
healthcare as anyone else. We believe that regardless of your race, 
ethnicity, nation of origin, or citizenship status, you should have the ability 
to feel safe in our community. That regardless of its composition, your 
family should have the legal protections of the state. That every worker 
should have the dignity of their work rewarded with a wage that keeps 
them out of poverty. Quite simply, we believe that the value of every 
individual must be valued. When we speak of equity and equality it is 
within the spirit that our differences are not a hindrance to the same 
American dream for which we all strive. 
We further believe that the desire for these outcomes are shared by 
well-intentioned elected officials across the political spectrum, and that 
while we may have meaningful debate and varying policy perspectives on

how best to achieve them, rhetoric that dismisses the pain and isolation of 
our marginalized community members perpetuates the divide that have 
made solving our biggest challenges so elusive, and further makes our 
residents believe that government doesn’t care about them.
It is for those reasons that we condemn the comments made by our new 
colleague, because those words do not reflect the traditional embrace of 
diversity by this Council and this county government. Among the many 
reasons I ’ve been proud to serve as an elected official at Salt Lake County, 
is the fact that regardless of our party labels we are able to work together, 
advance our regional priorities, and even forge friendships with those of a 
different perspective. It is our hope that it is that example that this Council 
will continue to display to our entire community, and also to other levels of 
government. We are hopeful the contention of the past few days is an 
anomaly and that we do not further strive to inject national disfunction into 
our work here. 
This week has reminded all of us that we can do better in this regard. That 
as elected officials representing a large diverse community, our words have 
impact, and have consequences. We are appreciative of Council Member 
Alvord’s willingness to engage in dialogue and education since his post, 
and for the apologies he has extended to us personally. We ask that he 
continue to learn, to further seek understanding of why his comments were 
hurtful, and as he asked of the three of us at his swearing in, “to stay open 
minded and flexible .” We pledge, to continue to work with all of our 
elected colleagues, for the good of the county.
Thank you Mister Chair.

3.5 Legislative Update 21-0282
Attachments: Staff Report
Presenter: Kara Trevino, Legislative Director, Council (Approx. 30 mins). 
Informational
Ms. Kara Trevino , Intergovernmental Relations Manager, Council Office, 
reviewed the following legislative bills:
- SB 214 Official Language Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this bill allows the government to print material in languages other 
than English.  It passed in both the Senate and the House.  
- SB 240 County Recreational Area Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this is the Mountainous Planning District bill.  It passed in both the 
Senate and the House. 
- 4th Sub HB 433 Amendments Related to Infrastructure Funding
Ms. Trevino stated this is the $1.1 million Transportation Bond.   
- 5th Sub HB 244 First Class County Highway Road Funds Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this bill passed in both the Senate and the House as well.
Page 5 of 14
Committee of the Whole Meeting Minutes March 9, 2021
- HB 294 Pandemic Emergency Powers Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this bill provides for the termination of emergency powers and public 
health emergency powers related to COVID -19 when the following thresholds have all 
been met:
- Utah’s 14-day COVID-19 case rate is less than 191 per 100,000 people
- Utah’s 7-day average of COVID-19 ICU utilization is less than 15 percent
- 1,633,000 prime doses of a COVID-19 vaccine have been allocated to Utah.
If trends continue as they have been, it is expected that by April 10, 2021, these 
thresholds will all be meet.
Mayor Jennifer Wilson stated she is worried about children because they will not 
receive vaccines by this time. 
Mr. Mitchell Park , Legal Counsel, Council Office, stated the Council needed to 
remember that Salt Lake County never imposed a mask mandate; it was done at the State 
level. The Council can do a mask mandate if the three conditions have not been met by 
April 10, 2021; however, Salt Lake County might be close to meeting all three thresholds 
by the 10th of April so it probably would not matter.  This bill expires on July 1, if the 
thresholds have not been met by that time. 
- 5th Sub SB 18 Property Tax Exemption Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this bill passed with the compromises the County supported.   
Mr. Chris Stavros , County Assessor, stated the original bill cut revenue to the County 
by approximately $ 1 million, with the compromise the cut to the County would be 
approximately $125,000 to $150,000.  This was a big win for the County.
- 1st Sub HB 390 Urban Farming Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this bill modifies the definition of urban farming to include livestock 
production.  It applied to 3rd to 6th class counties.  When the bill was heard on the 
Senate floor, leadership wanted it to apply to Salt Lake County, so the bill was amended 
to include all counties.  It passed.  The County Assessor and County Attorney will be 
looking into the bill to see if the County can adopt an ordinance so this definition is not be 
abused.

Mr. Stavros stated he is optimistic that a County ordinance can be adopted that will 
work through the issues relating to this bill. 
- 3rd Sub SB 195 Emergency Response Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this bill creates checks on executive emergency powers without 
hindering rapid response.  It applies state policies to local levels in relation to health 
departments, chief executives, and legislative bodies. This bill passed.
Mr. Park stated it will be necessary to look at County ordinances and make sure the 
State follows this bill.
- 5th Sub HB 82 Single-family Housing Modifications
Ms. Trevino stated this bill is the Accessory Dwelling Unit bill.  It includes flexibility for 
cities and counties on parking regulation, additional enforcement provisions, and allows 
cities and counties to require business licenses.  This bill passed.
- 2nd Sub HB 256 County Land Use and Development Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this bill provides that a provision in a county development agreement 
is unenforceable if the provision requires the initiation of annexation processes as a 
condition for issuing building permits.   This bill passed.
- 2nd Sub HB 52 Property Tax Deferral Modification
Ms. Trevino stated this billed failed.  It will be worked on over the interim.

Presenters: Lisa Hartman, Associate Deputy Mayor and Darrin Casper, CFO 
and Deputy Mayor (Approx. 10 mins). 
Discussion/Direction
Ms. Lisa Hartman , Associate Deputy Mayor, Mayor ’s Administration, 
stated this resolution on the agenda today relates to Coronavirus Aid, Relief, 
and Economic Security Act (CARES) funds.  The County distributed 
CARES funds to all municipalities including townships to aid in their 
response to the pandemic.  However, due to the restrictions placed on how 
the CARES funds could be used, the townships were not able to fully spend 
their allotments and had to return funds back to the County.  This resolution 
and subsequent agreements will allow the townships to fully utilize their 
CARES dollars.

Member Stringham, that this agenda item be approved and forwarded. to the 
4:00 PM Council meeting for ratification.  The motion carried by a unanimous 
vote.
