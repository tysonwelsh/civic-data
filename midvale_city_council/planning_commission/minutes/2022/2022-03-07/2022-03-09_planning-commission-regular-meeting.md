1.     City-initiated request to amend Chapters 17-7-3.7 Multifamily Residential-Medium
       Density Zone (RM-12), 17-7-4.7 Multifamily Residential-Medium to High Density

       9.12.3.13.D Silver Refinery Zone, 17-7-10.11.7 Jordan Bluffs Subarea 4 Residential
       Development, 17-7-10.12.6 Jordan Bluffs Subareas 1-3 Residential Development, 17-
       7-11.9.B.1 Main Street Form-Based Code (MS-FBC), 17-7-12.1.5 Regional
       Commercial Residential Overlay, 17-7-15.8 State Street Overlay Zone (SSOZ), and
       17-7-17.7.G Transit Oriented Development Overlay Zone (TODO) of the Midvale
       City Municipal Code.(TXT-22-02; Adam Olsen)

Mr. Adam Olsen stated that the City proposes an amendment to the above listed chapters of the
Municipal Code. The amendment would stipulate that minimum on-site parking requirements
(including visitor parking) for multi-family residential projects-whether stand-alone or part of an
overall mixed-use development-not be assessed an additional fee to tenants and/or residents
outside of the base sale, lease, or rental price of a unit. While all multi-family and mixed-use
developments must provide a minimum parking count, some developments are charging an
additional fee to use such parking; thereby creating an unintended spill-over of parking onto
adjacent streets. Staff researched the issue and found that Sandy City has a similar provision in
their Code, prohibiting additional fees for required parking counts.

DISCUSSION:

Mr. Garret Wilcox stated that because this is a text amendment, this is a legislative decision in
which the Commission has a lot of flexibility in approving, denying, or amending the proposed
text amendment.

Chair Snow asked if many of the large multi-family developments are charging their tenants for
use of parking stalls.

Mr. Olsen stated that he does not know how many of the developments are charging for parking
but stated that staff and City Council have received numerous complaints regarding the charge of
an additional fee for the minimum standard parking requirements set forth by City Code for
developments of these types. Due to this, it appears that many of these parking stalls are going
largely unused and tenants are parking on adjacent streets.

Commissioner Benson inquired about guest parking.

Mr. Olsen stated that Midvale City Code requires a quarter space per unit be provided for guest
parking. In other words, there is one guest space for every four units. He added that while it is not
required that the spots be labeled as guest parking, planning staff does required that it be identified
on the site plan to ensure that the minimum spots are being provided.

Mr. Wilcox stated that the proposed text states that the minimum parking requirements outlined in
the code must be provided without an additional fee, this includes the required parking for guests.
Any stalls provided by the developer above the overall minimum can have an assessed fee.

Commissioner Liedtke asked if the City could require that the developers provide signage for guest
parking as he added that many tenants may have more than one vehicle and tenants may utilize
those spots reserved for guests to park their additional vehicles.

Mr. Olsen stated that they can add that guest parking be clearly marked.

Mr. Garrett Wilcox stated he does not see a reason the City could not require the development to
designate the spots however he is not sure if the City is in a position to go onto to private property
to enforce that issue.

Mr. Olsen stated that is could be something that planning staff looks at when signing off on the
Certificate of Occupancy. He added that he will have staff look at adding a provision into the
proposed text.

Commissioner Liedtke asked if there was a mechanism that the City could use to enforce the new
requirements on past projects?

Mr. Garret Wilcox stated that he has been working with Mr. Olsen on various options however
they have not come to a solid approach to addressing the past projects that currently charge for
providing the minimum parking spot required by Code. He added that Utah Code does allow the
City to phase out the new parking requirements to past developments as long as the City provides
a way for the project to recoup the costs they would have otherwise collected.

Commissioner Liedtke clarified that, that would mean that new lease agreements would need to
include the cost of providing the minimum parking within the base lease or rental fee rather than
imposing an additional cost for parking.

Mr. Wilcox agreed. He stated that they could look at requiring that new lease agreements include
the cost of parking as a part of the total cost, however this would be hard to enforce on a personnel
level. Another option would be to give a timeframe to past developments, in which they would
need to come into compliance with the proposed text.

Chair Snow asked why they would need to recoup costs if these developments were already
approved because they provided to the City that they were planning to provide the required amount
of parking for tenants?

Commissioner Tippetts echoed this statement and added that she recalls that the development on
Cottonwood Street assured the Commission that parking would be provided to tenants, but it
appears now that they are in fact charging for those spots.

Mr. Olsen stated that staff can go back and look at that specific development agreement to verify.

Chair Snow said that this might be a gray area because the Commission approved the project based
on the parking requirements however, they never specified if parking stalls would be provided at
an additional cost.

Commissioner Erickson inquired about how this item was noticed.

Mr. Olsen stated that the item was noticed to the affected entities along with a publication on the
City’s website.

The Commission then discussed the various options and the potential issues with regards to
enforcing these new requirements on past developments. The Commissioners all agreed that they
would like to address the past developments retroactively to ensure that all current and past
developments are in compliance with the proposed text.
