¢ ASI Road Improvement Proposal — Executive Buttars has been in negotiations with
Autonomous Solutions which is in violation of the county code due to an inadequate
road. The average ASI salary is in the mid to upper $70,000.00 range and Buttars
feels the business deserves economic support from the county. An economical
study of the benefit to the county's tax revenues by the University of Utah produced a
projected 5-year $1.6 million in new net revenues and a projected 15-year $7.4
million in new net revenues. Buttars and Council member Potter met with Ben Hart
and the Torrie’s and proposed using the budget reserve account for economic
development in the amount of not more than $150,000.00 for a 20% participation in
the road improvement needed for Autonomous Solutions. The state would contribute
the same amount and ASI will fund the remaining amount.

Council member Potter expressed support of the proposal as a worthwhile endeavor.

Executive Buttars added that Ben Hart is willing to come before the Council to
explain the benefits to the county of the proposal. Buttars said as the county grows,
it will see a need for economic development assistance.

Council member Zilles pointed out the sales tax revenues for ASI are very limited.

Council member White spoke in opposition stating the road was supposed to be
done when they started the business, but there was no enforcement by the county so
they continued in violation. The University of Utah report is based solely on statistics
given by ASI. White is not opposed to granting tax relief, but he is not in favor of
$150,000.00 up front. If approved, Cache’s $150,000.00 should be the last money to
be put in.

Director Runhaar explained that the original conditional use permit eleven years ago
indicated use access would be from the north and the county would provide grading
once a year, but no other services. ASI wanted to be removed and away from a lot
of activity, now they have grown from 43 employees to over 100. There is a lot of
development occurring in the area as well. High Country Estates’ residents use the
road as a shortcut to a church in Mendon. Cox Honey also uses the road. Runhaar
observed the proposed road improvement will be on the north end with a possible
closure for property access only on the south end. It would be a seasonal closing
with keys given to the landowners for farm access. The total bid for the road is just
under $800,000.00.

The majority of the Council wants the proposal listed in next year’s budget for their
review. Robison and White were opposed to its being included in the proposed
budget.

Request for Tax Relief Filing Deadline Extension — Clerk/Auditor Zollinger explained
that the state deadline is September 1st, but can be extended by the county until

December 31st. Zollinger recommends November 30th as the deadline extension date
because that is when the property tax is due and the date worked well last year.

¢ 2017 Budget Priorities — Executive Buttars brought the following highlights in the
budget and asked the Council for their expectations regarding budget workshops this
year.

Buttars said the estimated tax revenue from new growth is $232,000.00 which is the
amount that can be utilized without a truth in taxation hearing. The estimated additional
property tax of $473,000.00 (which includes the $232,000.00) would result in holding the
rate the same in 2017 as 2016, but because the county would take in more taxes than
the previous year, a truth in taxation hearing would be necessary. The hearing would
have to be posted in the paper with a Public Hearing regarding it.

There are still some issues to be worked out as far as salaries and health insurance are
concerned. A health insurance increase of 35% this year will be necessary to receive
the same plan as the county currently has. The Compensation Committee is trying to
negotiate a 16% increase.

The shortfall from the transportation/road funding due to the “counties held harmless”
circumstance is still being addressed. Buttars and Council member Potter said the
League of Cities and Towns has better discussions on the issue than UAC. Attorney
Swink will pass that information along. Buttars believes there is a firm commitment to
fund counties at the level they were expecting for 2016.

Buttars asked the Council how they want the budget workshops to be handled — all
departments or only the larger ones? Chairman Merrill suggested Buttars present the
proposed budget with an explanation in summary form on each department. That would
help the Council prioritize what departments they want to attend the workshops. Potter
recommended having the larger departments first and then see if any others need to
attend.

Council member White reminded the Council they promised to decide on compensation
and health benefits first in the budget process.

The first budget workshop will be September 27" at 3:00 p.m. and the Sheriff's Office
budget will be the first to be reviewed. Summaries of the department budgets will be
received at that meeting for the Council to review and prioritize.

MEMORANDUM

TO: Cache County Council
FROM: Dianna Schaeffer, Chief Deputy Auditor
DATE: September 13, 2016

SUBJECT: Request for Tax Relief Filing Extension

Every year Cache County processes hundreds of tax relief applications for various indigent abatements, exclusive
use exemptions, military exemptions, blind exemptions, settlements, and deferrals. The application deadline is
September 1° in most cases, but may be extended until December 31* if the county finds good cause.

¢ Low Income Abatement - State law §59-2-1107 through §59-2-1109 permits counties to abate taxes for
indigent persons subject to proper application, documentation, and deadlines. Allowable extension
§59-2-1109(2)(d).

¢ Blind Exemption - State law §59-2-1106 provides counties authority to abate taxes for the blind subject to
proper application, documentation, and deadlines. Allowable extension §59-2-1106(2)(b)(ii).

¢ Military Exemption - State law §59-2-1104 and §59-2-1105 allows counties to abate taxes for veterans and
active duty armed forces subject to proper application, documentation, and deadlines. Military applications
are allowed an “automatic” one year extension per UCA §59-2-1105(2)(b)(i).

¢ Non-Profit Use Exemption — State law §59-2-1102 provides counties authority to abate taxes for religious
charitable, and educational organizations. Applications were due by March 1st and have been processed.

* Settlement/Deferral - No deadlines are mandated in §59-2-1347.

After the tax notices are mailed at the end of October, the county will be besieged by citizens in dire need of tax
relief. We must be compliant with the law of the land while maintaining the intent of the law to help those in
need. Therefore, it is in the best interest of the community that the Cache County Council extends the filing
deadline for low income and blind tax relief to November 30, 2016. By extending the filing deadline, Cache
County can continue helping eligible citizens and remain compliant with the law in regards to applications for tax
relief. This would solve the problem of processing impoverished applicants while remaining legally compliant.

Request is: Approved. % Tabled. Denied
