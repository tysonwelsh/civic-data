4. Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance Amendment, to allow online auto sales as a permitted use in the Regional
   Commercial C-3 zone. (Attachment G)

  Ms. Olson indicated the Commission had tabled action on this proposed ordinance amendment and asked
  Staff to address some potential concerns with the use. The Commission was concerned about the effect of
  these if several were located in a particular area, as well as how vehicles are delivered and any potential
  impact on existing uses. She stated while Staff had initially recommended the amendment apply to both the
  CBD zone and the C-3 zones, in again reviewing the existing ordinances, it was found that auto sales are
  already allowed in the CBD zone. She stated new and used auto sales are currently not permitted in the C-1
  and C-2 zone but area conditional use in the C-3 zone. While that regulation appears appropriate, online auto
  sales typically are limited to an office use and often there is no requirement for the display of vehicles. She
  stated Staff is suggesting between three and six on-site parking spaces be provided for vehicles for sale, but
  no signage or advertising would be allowed. The proposed amendment would address the previous concerns
  of the Commission by limiting online sales to commercial buildings, space for vehicles must be provided on
  the same site as the commercial building and no signage or advertising would be allowed on the vehicles,
  creating the appearance parking of vehicles for sale would have the appearance of typical commercial parking
  space and would be compliant with the parkng standards of the Ordinance. Parking would be allowed for
  between three and six vehicles and may not be used for any type of maintenance work, including detailing,




                                                                                                                      4
  repair, muffler, brake, paint, body and fender, upholstery or transmission work. Only functional and operable
  vehicles are allowed to be parked on the site, and no damaged vehicle can be parked on site. She stated as
  the ordinance as proposed would require the use of a commercial building, it would eliminate the potential of a
  home occupation use in the C-3 zone.

  She reviewed the General Plan and policies and stated the intent of the proposed amendment would to allow a
  commercial/office use in the C-3 zone while creating the appearance of a typical commercial parking lot for
  display vehicles. She stated the Commission had asked about the delivery and pick-up of vehicles, and
  indicated this can occur in a variety of ways, depending upon the source of the vehicle. While some sales
  might be shipped directly between an off-site vendor to the purchaser, others may be driven to the site by the
  busines owner or delivered by truck. Staff feels this is typical of other uses allowed in the C-3 zone and would
  create little effect on other commercial businesses, stating the zone currently allows some auto related uses.
  Staff recommendation is for approval of the language as proposed.

As there were no public comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Graf to close the public hearing.
Motion was seconded by Commissioner Schade and passed unanimously. Chair Southwick commended Staff
on the research completed and indicated the proposed ordinance addressed their previous concerns.

MOTION:         A motion was made by Commissioner Blaisdell to recommend approval of the language as

                General Plan and other exiting zoning requirements and policies. Motion was seconded by

  Mr. Jim Darling stated he and Mr. Stephenson have been working with City Staff in meeting the conditions of
  approval, and felt the project is now ready for the final approval stage.

  Mr. Montgomery stated for the past 15-20 years there have been several development proposals for this
  property. The area is between the canal line and existing homes which back onto the mountainside. Four
  acres of the property have steep slope and are undevelopable, and are proposed to be dedicated to Ogden
  City.

  Mr. Darling reviewed the development proposal, stating a total of 38 units are proposed, six buildings being
  twin homes and the remaining will be single-family. Parkland is provided for the use of both the residents and
  surrounding community, which will include public parking. Connection to existing trails is retained as well as
  easements to serve the water system. Three styles of homes are proposed, with the floor plan size to be
  around 2,200 square feet.

  identified. A geotechnical report has been provided and the owner is in the process of creating a hold
  harmless agreement for Pine View Water as well as acceptance from the Bureau of Reclamation. He reviewed
  the PRUD ordinance stating some standards apply only to the twin homes, but an overall development theme
  is provided for them. Public access to the trail system is retained, and the developer has agreed to both
  reconstruct a new driveway for the Woods home and to monitor the existing retaining wall on the Baldree
  property. An existing dry riverbed has been identified as storm water retention and planting material is
  designed to minimize potential fire spread. The connection to Simoron has been moved to align with the
  existing Hudson right-of-way which will also remove some of the existing soil being held by the retaining wall.

  construction process to assure it will not be damaged. He stated conditions of the preliminary PRUD approval

6. MU Site Plan, for Current Townhomes, 326 Park Blvd. (Attachment I)

  Mr. Jeremy Draper, representing Reeves and Associates, stated this request is for a 30 unit townhome project
  at the corner of Park Boulevard and Grant Avenue, consisting of a mix of one-, two- and three-bedroom units.

  Mr. Joe Torman stated the project will meet the River Project Design Guidelines by providing a mix of
  architecture, including transitional modern, craftsman and Victorian. Some units are designed to face Park
  Boulevard and Grant Avenue, while others will be oriented toward the River. The project includes amenities
  such as a barbeque area, a water feature and widened sidewalks to access the promenade on Grant Avenue.

  Mr. Montgomery stated this review is to determine whether the project complies with both the standards of
  the MU zone and the Ogden Riverbend Design Guidelines in terms of setbacks and architectural style. He
  stated the plan continues the open access between Park Boulevard and the Ogden River according to the
  Master Plan. While a variety of architectural styles are proposed, the window and door designs of the Arts &
  Crafts and Victorian units appear the same and felt more detail should be added to distinguish the different
  architectural styles. He stated at the previous meeting the Commission had made a recommendation to

7. Discussion, Make Ogden Plan. (Attachment J)

  Mr. Montgomery stated he has provided the Commission with the goals and strategies of the Make Ogden
  Plan. He identified changes he has proposed, eliminating the potential use of Crossroads of the West funding
  as it is no longer appropriated, but indicated there may be options for other Federal funding sources. He
  stated historic tax credits may be applicable if a property is designated as historic, which may be a means for
  some to be nominated to both the National and Local Registers.

  Commissioner Graf asked about the proposed increase in tree grate size. Mr. Montgomery stated many
  existing trees are unhealthy and it appears not enough oxygen is getting to the roots. He stated when trees
  were planted on Historic 25th Street, the soil was not prepared and holes were not deep enough. He stated the
  intent is to remove the existing 5x5’ tree grates and new mulch added, and then to install 15x5’ grates to allow
  more space for tree roots.

  Commission asked about the increased height allowed if buildings were designed to have a step back but not
  being appropriate on Historic 25th Street, indicating the proposed new build on the lower block does have a
  step-back appearance. Mr. Montgomery stated while it does have that design, it is within the height limit of
  the zone for Historic 25th Street.

  Mr. Montgomery stated the full plan is available on the City website to allow for public comment, and asked
  the Commission to email any comments, stating the citizens of Ogden are more familiar with the details of the
  downtown than the consultants and some of the proposed districts might be re-defined. He stated the intent
  is for these strategies to be adopted as a guiding document for the downtown, implemented into both the
  General Plan and Zoning Ordinances for the area. He stated the July work session will be a continued
  dialogue for the plan and asked members to become more familiar with its details. The recommendations
  from that work session will then be forwarded to the consultant and final language to be made available to the
  City Council on August 14th
