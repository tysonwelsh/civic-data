1.        WELCOME, INTRODUCTION AND PRELIMINARY MATTERS


Chair Barbieri called the meeting to order at 6: 31 p.m. and welcomed those in attendance. A roll

          1. 1   Opening Ceremonies — Pledge/ Reverence — Council Member Barbieri

Anna Barbieri directed the Pledge of Allegiance before Jay Ziolkowski recited a compilation of
speeches made by Abraham Lincoln which addressed the meaning of liberty among our nation of

particular appreciation to Council Coordinator Kris Heineman for the constant aid she provided
to the entire city council.

          1. 2   Mayor' s Report


 113     Mayor Kristie Overson reported a ribbon cutting at Quick Emissions which had recently
moved from another Taylorsville location.


The ChamberWest Annual Leadership Institute had recently taken place, and both she and
Councilmember Barbieri had spoken to Taylorsville business leaders at the event. The topic was
Leading Out Through Local Government. The Chamber' s Spring Conference also took place, and
five additional city employees were able to attend that event as well.

Sorenson Research Park had provided an exciting opportunity to " Get to Know Your Neighbors"
at Taylorsville' s Utah State University campus. The mayor represented Taylorsville as did Chief
Cottam, Wayne Harper and Jean Ashby.

Taylorsville High School recently unveiled a stunning mural of their new Lady Warrior. It was

located in the main hall down from the office and was created by THS alumni and the school' s art
department. Additionally, the City was recently invited to attend military career day at the high
school where they interacted with current service members and were able to discuss with students
the importance of military service to both our state and our country.

The Mayor and Chief Cottam recently had the opportunity to learn about Ramadan at the Alrasool
Islamic Center here in town. She enjoyed being at the Center and was excited to announce that as
of the previous month, the building was now listed on the National Register of Historic Places.
She wished to thank Susan Yadeskie who was instrumental in working with the Center and
jumping through the hoops that were necessary to achieve this distinction.

The Taylorsville Exchange Club was recently recognized with a prestigious National Exchange
Club Service Award. They were responsible for several required service projects. She greatly
appreciated the club, and particularly their partnership with the Youth Council.

Council Chair Barbieri and Councilmembers Burgess, Cochran and Knudsen had joined Mayor
Overson at the Utah League of Cities and Towns ( ULCT) conference in St. George, and she said
it was a great way to network with leaders of other government entities and learn more about their
responsibilities as elected officials.




One thing she missed while in St. George was taking part in the Labrum Park project, but she was
proud of the level of community involvement and expressed her appreciation for everyone' s hard
work.




The Mayor and Councilmember Harker had recently had the opportunity to tour the new prison
facility which was nearly complete. She found it to be a very sobering experience.

Like Chair Barbieri, she wished to extend her most sincere appreciation to the administrative
professionals who worked in city hall as well as all the other members of Team Taylorsville.

Chair Barbieri mentioned to those gathered, just how many opportunities there were for fun and
family-friendly activities that would take place in the coming months in Taylorsville.

3.       REPORTS


         3. 1    Taylorsville Police Department Reports–            ChiefBrady Cottam

     Chief Cottam wished to publicly acknowledge the" Employees ofthe Month" for the previous
quarter and invited Deputy Chief Todd Gray to join him at the podium.

Just a few days     after   Officer Marty Thomas     was    sworn   in,   he used his " extensive medical
experience in the United States military" to render aid to a West Valley officer who had been shot
by a barricaded suspect. Sgt. Bennett indicated he was extremely impressed by Thomas' " calm
and deliberate actions in    treating the detective." It was later learned from trauma physicians at the
hospital that Officer Thomas'       rapid response    and professional     medical     treatment   were "   life-

saving" to the detective. Subsequently Officer Marty Thomas was named the December 2021
TVPD Officer of the Month— again, just a matter of days after being sworn in.


Detective Alexa Baty was named as the January 2022 Officer of the Month by one of her peers
due to her outstanding work on a sex offense case. She not only put together a new case for
prosecution but made it possible to revive an older case that had fallen through the cracks. Chief
Cottam credited Det. Baty for her extensive experience working Special Victims Unit cases and
commended her for her work on both these cases that could now move forward in the prosecution
process,   hopefully to a successful conclusion.

used to ensure that districts remained substantially equal in population. Mr. Cowdell pointed out
that Taylorsville' s population was approximately 60, 400 people so it was important that each
council district be comprised of approximately 12, 100 people. Additionally, districts were to be as
compact and contiguous as possible and could not divide voting precincts. Finally, the city council

Mr. Cowdell asked that the city council adopt the tentative maps that evening so that the council,

the tentative maps had been a very data-driven process. The city was fortunate to have Karyn
Kerdolff on staff who had worked with Utah' s Independent Redistricting Commission. She later
helped Mayor Overson and Mr. Taylor come up with the tentative maps now up for consideration.
The justice department had indicated that a municipality' s most and least populated districts
should not have more than 5%        deviation from the median. These tentative maps showed a deviation
of approximately 2. 5%.

Councilmember Cochran asked if the council could adjust adopted maps in the future as the city
population changed. Mr. Cowdell responded that redistricting could only take place as a result of
census data, and census data was only gathered every ten years.
