The following items are discussed in these minutes:

Chair Cochran asked Mr. Bench to introduce Agenda Item 3.1:

Agenda Item 3.1 was a request for the Planning Commission to approve the preliminary plat of Avila Subdivision
with two lots located generally at 665 East 800 South in the R8 zone.

Staff Presentation: The applicant is proposing to subdivide their existing property to create a deep lot. The proposed
deep lot is 12,137 square feet. A twenty foot (20’) wide access will go along the west side of the property. The proposed
front lot is 8,734 square feet. The existing home will continue to meet all required setbacks.
There is a utility pole in the south-west corner of the existing lot. The proposed access drive will go around the pole.

Recommendation: Staff recommended the Planning Commission approve the preliminary plat of Avila Subdivision
with two lots located generally at 665 East 800 South in the R8 zone.

Chair Cochran invited the applicant to speak via telephone. Antonio Avila introduced himself.

regarding the property. An interested buyer in their property asked that they subdivide the property prior to selling it,
which has brought them to this point.

frontage road.

of 800 South. He understood that the frontage access is close to the proposed development, however, he indicated that
the traffic engineers felt comfortable and confident that this development with the proposed location of the access for
this deep lot.

Mr. Sakar agreed with Mr. Kelly. He indicated that he visited the site and thought the frontage street would help with
traffic.

twenty-eight feet (28’) tall.

Mr. Cook brought up a concern. He noted that the existing ‘Mixed Use Area’ was originally allocated 112 units and
was proposed to be dissolved. He also noted that the existing ‘Mixed Use Towers Area’ was originally allocated 158
units and was proposed to be reduced to 139 units. He asked if the applicant owned the open pad. He also asked if by
approving this change, the Commission would be taking away the ability of someone else to construct in favor of this
particular development.

Mr. Bench described that the ‘Mixed Use Area’ was originally designated with this plan. Since that designation, it has
been entirely built out and does not contain any residential. All these areas with the potential to be built as mixed use
were not. Instead, the individual property owners chose to build this entire area as commercial only. Therefore, Mr.
Dickerson is taking these units from an area that is not being used as residential at all. If the proposal was to pass, it
would leave 139 units for the only remaining unbuilt pad in this zone. In order to build 139 units on this pad would
require an extensive amount of density that would require multiple stories in the form of a high rise. This pad is really
small and in the opinion of City staff, reallocating some units from this small pad would not significantly limit the
potential of this property for a residential tower. It may not even be possible for the applicant to be able to use all 139
units there at this time. The property owner of that open pad was notified of both the neighborhood meeting that was
required to be held prior to application and the Planning Commission meeting. He has not reached out to comment.

Mr. Cook asked for clarification if a high rise is a permitted use in that area. He was concerned that another request
for a change might need to be made.

Mr. Bench answered that in the original plan for this area, that pad was designated as a ‘Mixed Use Tower Area’ with
a maximum allowed height of ninety-six feet (96’) from the beginning. It was always designed to have a high rise
tower on it.

Chair Cochran asked the Commission if they had any questions or comments for Mr. Bench or Mr. Dickerson. When
there were none, he opened the public hearing and invited those who wanted to make a comment on the item to send

Mr. Bench answered that the tower area does allow for a ninety-six foot (96’) tall structure. In the opinion of the City,
that is a tower. Whoever builds on that vacant pad could construct a ninety-six foot (96’) tall structure that could
contain the remaining 139 units for this area. Whether those are condos or apartments, those are permitted in the
current zoning.

Chair Cochran indicated that he liked height.

Mr. Clark reminded the Commission that whatever is constructed there would still have to meet parking requirements.

    ● “Good with the proposal.”

Mr. Sakar commented that the proposed adjustments of the Hospital Overlay zone make sense, however, it did not
make sense to him why the hospital would want to change the General Plan and the Zoning Map to become Medium
Density if they were not planning to sell their property.

Chair Cochran asked if the applicant was on the line. Tom Uriona introduced himself. Chair Cochran asked if he
would address Mr. Sakar’s comment.

Mr. Uriona answered that the purpose behind the rezone is purely asset management on the part of IHC for the property
to bring the property to what they think is a more consistent zoning with the surrounding properties. He confirmed
that there are no plans to change or sell the property in the foreseeable future. He stated that the intentions of IHC are
to hold the existing properties for either the future expansion of the hospital or for the company to redevelop the
properties and sell them off at some distant future date. In the long term, if redevelopment were to happen, it would
mean that IHC has determined that they don’t need all of the property for medical use. IHC does not know yet what
the future of this property will be. This rezone is purely asset management of the site to bring it into what IHC thinks
is conformity with the surrounding properties. They intend to keep the soccer fields as R8 for the foreseeable future
as well.

Mr. Sakar commented that zone changes such as this are normally done because there is some future change that is
being asked for. He asked how the neighbors feel about this request. He noted that this change would increase the
value of the property. He also wanted it clearly noted on the record that there are no plans for the site to develop.

Chair Cochran noted that the R7.5 zone does surround this property. The only portion that is not R7.5 is Geneva
Elementary School, which is zoned R8. If the school was to request to be rezoned to R7.5, that gap would be closed.
He asked if Geneva Elementary School was SITLA land.
