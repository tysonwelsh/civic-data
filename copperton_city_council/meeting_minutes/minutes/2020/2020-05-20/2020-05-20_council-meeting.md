National Flood Insurance Program

               Mayor Clayton stated he has asked if Copperton had a floodplain and found that
there is one that includes his home and the home of Council Member Stitzer.

               Kathy Holder, Floodplain Manager, State of Utah, explained the provisions of the
insurance program. It is a no-cost program. It is more for communities to be aware of a flood
risk. For doing that, they get the added benefit of having low cost flood insurance that can be
purchased through the program. The program came into play because insurance companies did
not want to provide insurance to homes in flood-prone areas. There are some carrot and stick

                                               3
DATE           WEDNESDAY                                MAY                             20, 2020

aspects to mitigate flood risks. If homeowners have a federally backed mortgage, they are
required to have flood insurance. They also get mitigation and disaster insurance with this. The
Council would need to adopt an ordinance, resolution, and application to join and indicate the
township will build responsibly in those floodplains.

               Mayor Clayton asked if some existing structures are grandfathered in.

                Ms. Holder stated existing structures can stay the way they are. If insurance is
purchased before a homeowner moves in, they can get a low-risk price even if the house is in a
high-risk area.

               Greg Baptist, SWPP Grading Supervisor, MSD, stated Copperton is a second-tier
floodplain. However, with Rio Tinto, there could be floods coming through at any time. This
insurance would address floodplains, mudslides, and landslides. This is a great thing for the
township to do. By adopting the County ordinance, the township’s floodplain ordinance is in place.
He noted that he received an application from Nathan Bracken.

               Council Member Pazell stated he supported this program. It is very voluntary.

              Council Member Severson asked what the difference was between primary and
secondary floodplains.

              Mr. Baptist stated a Primary Zone or Zone A is an area next to a stream that floods
regularly. A Secondary Zone is the next step down.

               Council Member Pazell, seconded by Council Member Bailey, moved to adopt the
following resolution for the National Flood Plain Program.

RESOLUTION NO. 20-05-01                                                      Date: May 20, 2020

       A RESOLUTION OF INTENT FROM THE COPPERTON METRO TOWNSHIP
       REGARDING THE NATIONAL FLOOD INSURANCE PROGRAM

       WHEREAS, certain areas of Copperton Metro Township are subject to periodic flooding,
causing serious damages to properties within these areas; and

       WHEREAS, it is the intent of the Copperton Metro Township Council to require the
recognition and evaluation of flood hazards in all official actions relating to land use in areas
having these hazards; and

       WHEREAS, the Council has the legal authority to adopt land use and control measures to
reduce future flood losses pursuant to Utah Code Ann. § 10-3-701.

       NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, effectively immediately, that the Copperton
Metro Township Council:




                                                4
DATE           WEDNESDAY                                MAY                             20, 2020

1.      Assures the Federal Emergency Management Agency that it will enact as necessary, and
maintain in force, in those areas having flood hazards, adequate land use and control measures
with effective enforcement provisions consistent with the criteria set forth in Section 60 of the
National Flood Insurance Program Regulations; and

2.     Vests the Mayor with the responsibility, authority and means to:

       (a)    Assist the Administrator, at his/her request, in the delineation of the limits of the
              area having special flood hazards.

       (b)    Provide such information concerning present uses and occupancy of the
              floodplain, mudslide (i.e., mudflow) or flood-related erosion areas as the
              Administrator may request.

       (c)    Maintain for public inspection and furnish upon request, for the determination of
              applicable flood insurance risk premium rates within all areas having special flood
              hazards identified on a Flood Hazard Boundary Map (FHBM) or Flood Insurance
              Rate Map (FIRM), any certificates of floodproofing, and information on the
              elevation (in relation to mean sea level) of the level of the lowest floor (including
              basement) of all new construction or substantially improved structures, and include
              whether or not such structures contain a basement, and if the structure has been
              floodproofed, the elevation (in relation to mean sea level) to which the structure
              was floodproofed.

       (d)    Cooperate with Federal, State and local agencies and private firms which
              undertake to study, survey, map and identify floodplain, mudslide (i.e., mudflow)
              or flood-related erosion areas, and cooperate with neighboring communities with
              respect to management of adjoining floodplain, mudslide, (i.e., mudflow) and/or
              flood-related erosion areas in order to prevent aggravation of existing hazards.

       (e)    Upon occurrence, notify the Administrator in writing whenever the boundaries of
              the community have been modified by annexation or the community has otherwise
              assumed or no longer has authority to adopt and enforce floodplain management
              regulations for a particular area. In order that all FHBM’s and FIRM’s accurately
              represent the community’s boundaries, include within such notification a copy of
              the map of the community suitable for reproduction, clearly delineating the new
              corporate limits or new area for which the community has assumed or relinquished
              floodplain management regulatory authority.

       (f)    Submit an annual report to the Administrator concerning the community's
              participation in the program, including, but not limited to the development and
              implementation of floodplain management measures.

3.     Appoints Lupita McClenning, Planning and Development Services Director for the Greater
       Salt Lake Municipal Services District, with the responsibility, authority, and means to
       implement the commitments as outlined in this Resolution.

4.     Authorizes the Mayor to take such other official action as may be reasonably necessary
       to carry out the objectives of the adopted floodplain management measures and to
       participate in the National Flood Insurance Program, including but not limited to completing
       any applicable applications.

RESOLUTION NO. 20-05-02                                                        Date: May 20, 2020

       A RESOLUTION OF THE COPPERTON METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL
       CLARIFYING PROCEDURES TO DETERMINE WHETHER A CONFIRMED
       CASE OF COVID19 EXISTS IN COPPERTON FOR THE PURPOSES OF
       COPPERTON’S COVID19 ACTION PLAN

       WHEREAS, on March 13, 2020, the Copperton Metro Township Council adopted an action
plan and declared a public health emergency for the coronavirus disease of 2019 (“COVID-19”)
pursuant to Chapter 2.86 of the Copperton Metro Township Code; and

      WHEREAS, Copperton’s COVID19 action plan calls for certain actions based on the
number of confirmed COVID19 cases in Copperton; and

        WHEREAS, the Council desires to clarify that confirmation from the Salt Lake County
Health Department or the Utah Department of Health is required for a confirmed case of COVID19
to exist in Copperton for the purposes of its COVID19 action plan.

       NOW, THEREFORE, IT IS HEREBY RESOLVED, effective immediately by the Copperton
Metro Township Council that verification from the Salt Lake County Health Department or the
Utah Department of Health is required for a confirmed case of COVID19 to exist in Copperton for
the purposes of its COVID19 action plan

             Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA)

               Council Member Pazell reported on discussions surrounding the weighted vote
matter. It is becoming very contentious and is not likely to go away until a compromise is met.
He reviewed the issues of contention including the Sheriff as Chief Executive Officer (CEO) of
UPD and the possibility of some cities leaving. He supported a motion to raise officers’ salaries.
The issues with UPD are political and are not a reflection on the officers, who do a great job.

                Nathan Bracken stated he has spoken with attorneys for other municipalities and
they agree the problems are not all about the weighted vote. There are fundamental governance
issues at play, including lack of adequate fund balance, questions and disputes over officers pay,
and no real clear understanding or explanation about pooled or shared services. Administration
is top heavy and budgets keep growing. A weighted voting will not fix any of this.

He is concerned with an outdated interlocal agreement. He recommended taking the time to fix
it. If more cities leave, UPD will become insolvent. The challenges are to fix the problems and
keep UPD whole.
