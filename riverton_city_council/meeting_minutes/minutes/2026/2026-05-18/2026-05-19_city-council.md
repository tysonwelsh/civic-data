Presentations/Discussions

       Stormwater Discharge 12600 South CRA Project

Kevin Hicks, City Manager, presented a preliminary proposal to allow a developer within the 12600
South CRA Project to direct storm water from a future development into an existing City retention
pond that has excess capacity. He explained the developer would pay all associated infrastructure
costs and that future agreements would address maintenance, engineering standards, and liability
protections. Councilmembers and the Mayor supported the concept, viewing it as an efficient use of
existing infrastructure that could encourage desirable development in the area, while also
discussing the need for proper safeguards and confirming that City engineers had determined the
pond has adequate capacity.

         Main Park Entrance Discussion

Cary Necaise, Public Works Director, discussed potential improvements for the vacant area at the
entrance to the Main City Park following removal of the skate park, emphasizing the desire to
eliminate the current dirt lot and create an attractive, functional space before upcoming summer
events. He presented eight conceptual options ranging from simple sod replacement and additional
trees to decorative landscaping, pathways, parking, recreational courts, community gardens,
cornhole areas, and a conservation garden. He noted that the simplest options could potentially be
completed before Town Days and would provide additional seating space for attendees.

Councilmembers generally favored moving forward with Option #1 as a temporary but visually
appealing placeholder while longer-term plans for the area are considered. Several
Councilmembers expressed interest in exploring future recreational amenities such as futsal or
street hockey courts if outside funding became available. Mr. Necaise confirmed that simpler
options could likely fit within the current parks budget, whereas more elaborate proposals would
require future budget planning and additional Council discussion.


         Park Water Use and Service Level Discussion

Mr. Necaise City explained that parks are among the City’s largest water users and emphasized the
need to balance conservation efforts with community expectations. His plan is to prioritize
preserving trees, athletic fields, gathering spaces, and cemetery landscaping, while reducing
irrigation in lower-use areas such as park strips, detention ponds, and passive open spaces. He
noted that recent upgrades to the City’s irrigation system, including weather-based controls and
moisture monitoring, have already improved efficiency and are expected to help meet reduction
goals. Parks and landscapes were categorized into four service levels, with irrigation reductions
ranging from 10% in high-priority athletic and civic areas to 25% in low-priority open spaces and
retention areas.

Councilmembers discussed how the City should manage splash pads during drought conditions,
noting that splash pads use culinary water that is not recycled. Staff proposed reducing operating
hours and potentially alternating operating days at certain splash pads, especially the less-used Old
Farm location, while still maintaining recreational opportunities for families. Councilmembers
generally supported reducing hours to approximately noon to 8 p.m. and exploring staggered
schedules between splash pad locations. Councilmembers emphasized the importance of
communicating the City’s conservation efforts clearly to residents and supported using maps and

City Council Agenda Review

Mayor Buroker suggested that the Council could discuss, in advance of the formal public hearing, a
proposed budget adjustment for police compensation that had been brought forward by the police
chief following internal review and discussions with officers. The revised proposal would provide
officers with a 1.5% cost-of-living adjustment and a 4% step increase, totaling a 5.5% increase
funded through the police fund rather than the general fund.. Councilmembers responded positively,
emphasizing appreciation for the flexible and transparent budget process and noting that it allowed
adjustments based on updated information without disrupting the overall budget timeline.

Mr. Necaise reported that a City monitoring system detected a reverse flow alert in the culinary
water system, which led crews to investigate and discover an illegal cross-connection between a
property’s irrigation system and the drinking water supply. The connection was immediately shut off,
and in coordination with the Division of Water Quality, the City issued a precautionary boil order
while water samples are tested, with no adverse results confirmed yet due to standard testing
timelines. He emphasized the issue was caught early through upgraded technology and that all
residents must follow the boil order because the system is interconnected, including areas partially
served by wells. He also noted that future prevention efforts may include installing additional
backflow protection devices, such as dual check valves or upgraded meter yokes, to better isolate
systems and reduce the risk of similar incidents.

Mayor/Council/Manager Reports

Mayor Buroker reported that a 48-hour precautionary boil water order is in place due to a detected
mixing of secondary and culinary water systems, though no contamination has been confirmed and
City staff, in coordination with state water quality officials, responded immediately to isolate the

all students feel seen and valued. Over the course of eight months, he handwrote 2,336 letters,

         Tentatively Adopted Budget

Nick Geer, Administrative Services Director, discussed an amendment to the tentatively adopted
budget to incorporate a revised compensation proposal for the Riverton Police Department. The
proposal would provide all officers with a 1.5% cost-of-living adjustment and a 4% step increase,
resulting in a total 5.5% pay adjustment. He explained that the adjustment would be funded
through the City’s RLESA fund, reducing its fund balance from about 5% to 1.5% for the year,
while still maintaining some capacity for unexpected needs or future adjustments.

Council members expressed support for the change, noting its role in retaining officers,
maintaining competitiveness in the job market, and appropriately valuing current staff.

Councilmember Pierucci MOVED to amend the tentatively approved budget to include the
revised COLA and step adjustments for the Riverton Police Department. Councilmember
Smith SECONDED the motion. Mayor Buroker called for a vote; the vote was as follows:
Haymond-Yes, Johnson-Yes, Pierucci-Yes, and Smith-Yes. The motion passed unanimously.
