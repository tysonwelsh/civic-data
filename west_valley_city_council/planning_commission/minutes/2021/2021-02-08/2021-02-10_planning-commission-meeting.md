GPZ-1-2021
Alex Winder
5718 W 3500 S
General plan change from commercial/office and office/education to light manufacturing
and zone change from C-2 to LI
5.35 acres

Alex Winder has submitted a General Plan/zone change application for a 5.35-acre parcel
located at 5718 West 3500 South. The property is currently zoned C-2 (general commercial)
with a General Plan designation of commercial/office and office/education. The proposed zone
is LI (light industrial) and the proposed General Plan designation is light manufacturing. The LI
zone is more restrictive than the M (manufacturing) zone and does not allow any outdoor uses
or heavy industrial. For reference, a copy of the commercial and manufacturing use table is
included with this report. The LI zone requires that at least one building relief treatment,
building design treatment, and roof treatment from the commercial design standards be
incorporated into buildings within the zone.

This property is addressed in the Hunter Town Center Small Area Plan that is part of the City’s
General Plan. The Hunter Town Center Small Area Plan was first adopted in 2010 and later
updated in 2017. The commercial/office and office/education future land use designation for the
subject property assumed the redevelopment of the old Kmart building. However, this building
has since been converted into storage units. Given the recent investment into the property by
Alpine Storage, the redevelopment of this building is very unlikely for the foreseeable future.

The property is surrounded by C-2 zoning on all sides. Surrounding land uses include the
Mountain View Corridor which is currently under construction to the west; a privately owned
storm water retention pond to the north; a commercial center to the east that includes Alpine
Storage, Vasa Fitness, Salt Lake Community College, and other uses; and the same commercial
center and UDOT property to the south. The subject property is vacant.

Included with this report is a letter and concept plan from the applicant describing the proposed
use as well as reasons to support this application. The applicant is proposing to develop the
subject property as flex space with 80 to 90% warehouse space and 10 to 20% office. As
pointed out by the applicant, the current C-2 zone allows a building with up to 50% warehouse
with the balance being office. The LI zone allows for a building to be 100% warehouse. Access
to the property is gained through two easements – one from the east going through the Alpine
Storage property to 5600 West and the other to the south connecting to 3500 South.

Staff is supportive of this change for the following reasons:

      Retail and office would likely struggle with difficult access and no visibility from either
       3500 South or 5600 West.
      Single family homes would be awkward sandwiched between the back of a commercial
       center and the Mountain View Corridor. Residents would have to drive through the
       commercial center to get to their homes.

      Multi-family residential would also be awkward and this property is not eligible for RM
       zoning based on the criteria found in the ordinance in Section 7-5-101.

      There are no residential neighbors that would be negatively impacted.

      Flex space would take advantage of the visibility from Mountain View Corridor. Similar
       uses have been built in other communities where a property has freeway visibility with
       limited access.

       During the study session the Planning Commission asked several questions about this
       application. These questions are listed below along with a response from the applicant in
       italics.

      Do you anticipate semi-truck traffic? If yes, will the site accommodate this? We don't
       anticipate a lot of semi-trucks but would design the site to handle them. With our current
       easements I don't think a 53' tractor trailer could make it in off the 5600 West access. We
       would have them come in off of 3500 South. Our target market are the smaller businesses
       so I would imagine it would be more fed ex and ups sized box trucks and vans coming in
       and out of the site.
      What is the anticipated building height and overhead door height? The max height we
       would go is 35'. With the way our lot slopes it may be closer to 28'. We really want a 25'
       clear height.
      Are there any specific tenants that you anticipate coming to the site? Currently we don't
       have any tenants lined up. Our research shows that there aren't many 2000-3500 sq ft
       spaces. They are all 1800 or less or 5000 or more. We really want to hit the smaller
       business owners that don't need a huge space but need somewhere to start. Honestly if
       your office/warehouse requirement in the c2 zone wasn't 50/50 we wouldn’t even need a
       zone change. But because we want to me more of a 70/30 or 80/20 ratio of warehouse to
       office it pushes us into this light industrial zone.

One of the design standards in the LI zone is that “Overhead doors shall not be oriented toward
High-Image Arterial Streets as listed in Section 7-10-102.” The Mountain View Corridor is one
of the High-Image Arterial Streets listed in Section 7-10-102 and the proposed building does
have overhead doors that face the Mountain View Corridor. Staff notified the applicant of this
issue and explained that either the overhead doors would need to be removed from the west side
of the building or they could propose a development agreement where building enhancements
were proposed in exchange for allowing overhead doors on the west side. The applicant
indicated that he is fine with adding enhancements to keep the overhead doors on the west side
of the building. Under staff alternative 1 below, staff has included some recommended
enhancements.


       Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval. This application should be approved subject to a development agreement that
      includes the following standards:
          a. Along the west façade, the width of overhead doors shall not exceed 35% of the
             overall width of the façade.
          b. The LI zone requires at least one treatment on the primary or street facing façade
             from the building relief treatments (Section 7-11-311), building design treatments
             (Section 7-11-312) and roof treatments (Section 7-11-313) for a total of three
             treatments. This development shall include at least two additional treatments
             (total of 5 treatments) that can come from one or more of the three Sections
             referenced above.

   2. Continuance. This application should be continued for reasons determined during the
      public hearing.

   3. Denial. The C-2 zone should remain.



Discussion:   Steve Pastorik presented the application. Steve noted one issue pertaining to the
              subject property being in a LI (light industrial) zone. This zone has a provision
              stating the street-facing side of the building cannot contain only overhead doors.
              The subject property will have storefronts mixed in with overhead doors. Steve
              explained, if the Commission approves this proposal, a development agreement
              could allow for an exception to the overhead door rule by adding other items into
              the development agreement addressing the aesthetics. Those additional items are
              addressed in the staff report.

              Steve explained from a staff perspective; an office/warehouse building is a good
              use for this location. He stated that this property's visibility from 5600 W. and
              3500 S. is limited and would not be optimal for a retail business. There is no
              surrounding residential area, and you need to drive through a commercial center
              to reach the subject property, which would be a challenge for a single-family
              development. The code does not allow for a multi-family development.

              Applicant: Alex Winder stated there is a high demand for an office/warehouse
              building, and he wants to help fill that need.

Motion:       Commissioner Winters motioned to approve GPZ-1-2021 subject to the
              development agreement that includes two standards outlined in the staff report.

C-1-2021
Shupe Builders Office/Warehouse
1875 West 3600 South
C-2 Zone (.45 Acres)

The applicant, Jared Shupe representing Shupe Builders LLC, is requesting conditional use
approval for an office/warehouse building at 1875 West 3600 South. This property is zoned C-2,
General Commercial, and the West Valley City General Plan designates this area as General
Commercial. The adjacent property to the south is zoned RM and the remaining areas are C-2.
The surrounding uses include the Homestead Farms residential development to the south, the
east and west sides are similar office/warehouse uses and there are medical offices to the north.

This site is Lot 6 of the Plaza 3600 Business Park which was approved by the Planning
Commission in 1998 (C-26-98). Access to the site is gained off of 3600 South, which is a private
road. The original approval specified that each development must be reviewed as a separate
conditional use before the Planning Commission since the tenants were not known at the time of
the overall approval, furthermore this particular use is also listed as a conditional use in the C-2
zone.

Shupe Builders, LLC is a small to mid-size home building company with 8 employees. They are
proposing to develop the site for their office/warehouse and also provide additional lease space.
The proposed building will be approximately 4845 sq. ft with 2423 sq. ft. of office space and
2422 sq. ft. of warehouse. The building would be divided into four potential spaces. Shupe
Builders would occupy two of those spaces and lease the remaining two. Each unit will consist
of 2-3 individual offices with a conference room and a bathroom, with an optional second
bathroom. The warehouse space for each unit will have a 12’x12’ overhead door with 16’ clear
space inside. There shall be no outside storage.

The building will be constructed out of split face and flat CMU masonry with steel architectural
accents and store front type windows. The building design must comply with the Commercial
Design Standards. The east side of the building will only be partially visible from the street due
to the location of the adjacent building so staff recommends that the visible portion also comply
with the secondary façade requirements in the Commercial Design Standards.

The parking for the site has been calculated for office/warehouse uses. Therefore, 13 spaces are
required and 14 have been provided.

The landscaping on site must meet the 15% minimum area as required in the C-2 Zone. The
landscape plan indicates 21.8% has been provided. The landscaping/setbacks along the frontage
of this parcel were approved as part of the original overall shopping center landscaping concept.
This includes a minimum setback for the building of 40’ from the property line, which is the
center of the private road, as well as a 7-foot landscaped setback for the parking to provide an
area for street trees that were originally planted when the roadway was improved. The site will
also have a 5’ strip of landscaping adjacent to the parking lot on the east and west sides and a 10’
wide landscaped area adjacent to the residential property to the south. These areas must all
include 50% live plant material and trees.

Since the property is adjacent to residential a 6’ tall solid masonry wall must be constructed
along the entire southern boundary. The wall will be constructed of CMU Block to match the
building and a similar enclosure will also be constructed around the dumpster located on the site.

All exterior lighting is wall mounted on the west side of the building. A lighting plan will be
provided to ensure that the site complies with the standards set forth in the West Valley City
Code including the light levels adjacent to residential uses.

Only wall signage is proposed for this site. All signage shall comply with the West Valley City
Sign Ordinance, to include no more than 15% signage on the front building face, and 5% on the
remaining sides, and no more than 50% of the window area shall be covered in signs. A building
permit must be issued for all signage. Ground signage has not been proposed at this time.

Staff Alternatives:

Approval of the conditional use for an office/warehouse subject to compliance with all of the
City’s zoning ordinances and the following:

   1. No more than 50% of the gross floor area shall be used for warehousing and no outside
      storage is permitted.

   2. The landscaping must be completed per the approved site plan and in accordance with
      applicable standards in the West Valley City Municipal Code including but not limited to
      a minimum 40’ front building setback measured from the front property line and a 7’
      wide landscaped area along 3600 West with three trees per C-26-98.

   3. The building must be designed in accordance with the Commercial Design Standards
      which shall include secondary façade treatments on the visible portions of the east side of
      the building.
   4. There must be adequate parking on site for the proposed tenant mix.

   5. A 6’ masonry wall shall be installed along all residential zone boundaries.

   6. The dumpster location shall be at least 20’ from any residential boundary and completely
      secured with a 6’ tall masonry enclosure.

   7. All lighting shall be designed per Title 7 and not negatively impact adjacent residential
      uses.

   8. All signage must comply with the West Valley City Sign Ordinance.

   9. Must meet requirements of all affected departments and agencies and is subject to review
      upon valid complaint.

Continuance for reasons determined in the meeting.

SA-1-2021
Decker Station Subdivision
3100 South Decker Lake Drive
RM Zone
1 Lot

BACKGROUND
David Draper, representing Rockworth Companies LLC, is requesting preliminary approval for
the Decker Station Subdivision. The subdivision will amend all of lots 2E and 2F and a portion
of lot 2G, E-Center Retail Subdivision Lot 2 Second Amended. The purpose for the subdivision
is to consolidate existing lots, vacate the public utility easement and to dedicate right-of-way.
ISSUES:
The E-Center Retail Subdivision, Lot 2 second amended was recorded with the Salt Lake County
Recorder’s Office in July 2015. That subdivision plat consisted of 3 lots on 5 acres. The
proposed subdivision before the Planning Commission today will amend the aforementioned
subdivision by consolidating lots 2E and 2F and a portion of 2G into 1 lot approximately 2.8
acres in size. A new development known as Decker Lake Station Apartments will be constructed
on the new lot. The recorded public utility easement running parallel to the property line
separating lots 2E and 2F will be vacated.


A few weeks ago, the Planning Commission reviewed and approved a conditional use for the
Decker Lake Station Apartments. During the review for the apartment project it was determined
that a number of issues needed to be resolved prior to the approval of that development. They
are as follows:


       1. Consolidation of lots into 1 developable lot.
       2. Vacation of the public utility easement.
       3. Right-of-Way dedication along 3100 South and Decker Lake Drive.


Access to the subdivision will be gained from both Decker Lake Drive and 3100 South. There
are other easements within the boundary of the subdivision that will remain but will not affect
the apartment project. All other aspects of development were addressed as part of the
conditional use application.




STAFF ALTERNATIVES:
1.       Approve the Decker Station Subdivision subject to a resolution of the staff items noted in
         the analysis.
2.       Continue the application for reasons determined in the Planning Commission meeting.


Discussion:     Steve Lehman presented this application. In review of the apartment application, a
                public utility easement that runs east to west through the subject property was
                identified. He stated to abandon this easement; a formal vacation needs to be
                approved by the City Council. Steve told the Commission that there will need to
                be an amendment to the E-Center Retail Subdivision. Steve explained with the
                approval of this application by the Commission, the request to vacate the public
                utility easement can be presented to the City Council. He reminded the
                commission that this application will do three things: 1. Vacate the public utility
                easement, dedicate right-of-way along Decker Lake Drive and 3100 South and to
                consolidate the two lots into one.
