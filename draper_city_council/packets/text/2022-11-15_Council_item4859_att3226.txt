                                                     EXHIBIT “A”

                         Section 3020 – Probationary Employment Period Policy
                                          (Amended 11/01/2022)

General Policy Statement:

     All newly hired, transferred, or promoted full time employees working ≥30 hours per week are required to successfully
complete a probationary employment period. The probationary employment period provides an opportunity for the supervisor
and employee to assess the employment relationship, and allows the supervisor to oversee the employee’s work and offer
guidance to increase effectiveness. During the probationary employment period, employees are considered at-will and may be
terminated at any time with or without cause.

Policy:

1.0 Probationary Employment. All full-time employees shall be required to successfully complete a probationary employment
    period in accordance with this section.

     1.1    All full-time employees, except for sworn police officers, immediately upon hire shall complete a six-month
            probationary employment period.

     1.2    All full-time sworn police officers immediately upon hire shall complete a 12-month probationary employment
            period.

2.   Promotions – New Position. Employees who promote or assume a new position requiring new job qualifications or job
     skills, shall complete a six-month probationary employment period. The probationary employment period does not apply to
     promotion or new position change due to a career ladder (career path advancement) e.g., move from Building Inspector I
     to Building Inspector II, involuntary job restructurings, or job reclassification within the same department with similar job
     duties, e.g., change in job title from Passport Agent to Passport Coordinator.

3.0 Expectations for Competency. By the end of the probationary employment period, the employee is expected to be
    performing at an acceptable level of performance in their position.

     3.1    Supervisor’s Guidance. During the probationary employment period, the employee’s supervisor should provide
            close supervision, necessary training and guidance, and should keep the employee informed of progress made and
            opportunities for success.

     3.2    Formal Performance Review. At the end of the initial 90 days, a formal documented check-in should be completed
            by the supervisor, and at the end of the successful completion of the probationary employment period, a formal,
            written performance review shall be completed and discussed with the employee. The review should include:

            3.2.1 Assessment of job knowledge, skills and abilities, and
            3.2.2 Determination of the employee’s continued employment and goals set, or termination of employment.

4. Completion. Upon successful completion of the probationary employment period, the supervisor requests approval from
   the Department Director to retain the full-time employee in the position for which they are serving. Upon Department
   Director approval, the completed probationary employment review shall be forwarded to human resources for retention in
   the personnel file.

5. Written Notice of Extension. Probationary employment period may be extended for up to 30 days. Upon recommendation
   of the supervisor, the Department Director shall discuss the reason for extension with the Human Resources Director and
   City Manager prior to approving. In such event, the employee will receive written notification of the reason for and length of
   the extension from the supervisor and approval from the Department Director. Exceptions beyond 30 days, require City
   Manager approval.
6.   Job Transfers. Application for a job transfer to another position within the city is generally not permitted until after
     successful completion of the probationary employment period. Exceptions require City Manager or designee approval.

7.   Termination of Probationary Employees. All terminations of probationary employees shall be reviewed by the Human
     Resources Director and must have prior approval of the City Manager.
