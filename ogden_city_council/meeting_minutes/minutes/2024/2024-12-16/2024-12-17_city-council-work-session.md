         The purpose of the work session was to review the agenda for the City Council meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.;
receive the Sustainability Committee Sunset Report; discuss the PATH (Participate, Apply, Test, Help) to Citizenship Program;
discuss the Fiscal Year (FY) 2023-2024 4th Quarter Capital Improvement Plan Report; discuss the FY 2024-2025 1st Quarter
Capital Improvement Plan Report; discuss the FY 2024-2025 1st Quarter Financial Report; receive an Administrative Update;
discuss Council business; and hear Council comments.

Agenda Review
        Chair Richey and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council meeting
scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Sustainability Committee Sunset Report
         City advisory committees have sunset provisions in their enabling ordinances which terminate their existence on a certain
date, unless reauthorized by the City Council. The Sustainability Committee sunsets on December 31, 2024. Administration is
recommending the committee be extended; if approved, the new sunset date for the committee would be December 31, 2026.
         Sustainability Coordinator Long recognized the members of the Committee who were present at the meeting and thanked
them for their service and dedication to the City’s sustainability efforts. He then introduced the Committee Chair, Dan Bedford,
and invited him to provide the Committee’s Sunset Report. Mr. Bedford used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss
Committee highlights and activities for the past three years, as well as to outline the program for the Committee for the next two
years. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
         Discussion among the Council and Mr. Bedford centered on changes in recycling programs in the community; water
conservation efforts; and the impact that creating walkable communities can have on the local environment.

PATH to Citizenship Program
         Community Engagement Administrator Lartigue summarized a proposal to formalize the PATH (Participate, Apply,
Test, Help) to Citizenship Program. The goal of the PATH program is to remove obstacles to obtaining citizenship and to educate
new citizens on their civic responsibilities. A pilot program was initiated in 2023 supported by grant funding from Salt Lake
County allocated as part of a statewide effort to expand naturalization for legal immigrants. Several community partners pooled
resources and in 2024, 17 Weber County residents became naturalized citizens of the United States. The Council will consider
proposed program guidelines and a preliminary budget of $21,000. Funding will come from grants, private donations, and Ogden
City. Ms. Lartigue used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the eligibility requirements for naturalization, provide
naturalization statistics for 2019-2023, and to discuss the resources needed to implement the program. For a copy of the
presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Discussion among the Council and Ms. Lartigue centered on the budget for the program; the entities in the City that are
partners in the program; and the program guidelines. Chair Richey concluded that action on the proposed program has been
tentatively scheduled for the Council’s first meeting in January 2025.

Fiscal Year (FY) 2023-2024 4th Quarter Capital Improvement Plan Report
and FY 2024-2025 1st Quarter Capital Improvement Plan Report
         Finance Director Sorensen used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide the Capital Improvement Plan Reports
for the fourth quarter of FY 2023-2024 and the first quarter of FY 2024-2025. The report provided the status and pertinent
financial information on all funded CIP projects for the pertinent quarters. The projects are separated into General Fund and
Enterprise Fund projects with the General Fund projects further divided into projects by City department. Projects funded with
bond proceeds are highlighted in the Enterprise fund. Projects that included federal funding are also noted. The report identifies
the amount funded in the previous fiscal year and shows the total amount available for expenditures on all General Fund and
Enterprise Fund projects. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
         There was discussion among the Council and Mr. Sorensen regarding the different types of funding sources for projects
at the Ogden Airport; the status of the Marshall White Center project; and the purpose of the Marriott-Slaterville Fund that
contains $20,000.

Fiscal Year 2024-2025 1st Quarter Financial Report
         Director Sorensen used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide the FY 2024-2025 first quarter financial report.
He touched on General Fund revenues and expenditures to date; the status of the City’s Enterprise Funds, internal service funds,
and grant funds; and all other funds, including the Capital Improvement Fund, Redevelopment Agency fund, Municipal Building
Authority fund, special assessment funds, and the Weber-Morgan Narcotics fund. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see
the information packet for the meeting.
         The Council engaged in discussion with Mr. Sorensen regarding sales tax revenue trends; the timing of the receipt of
property tax revenues; formulas used to calculate franchise tax revenue and the City’s inability to include a franchise tax on
broadband utilities; and the primary areas of focus for Administrative staff in terms of financial trends.

Administrative Update
          Chief Administrative Officer Brown provided the Council with an administrative update focusing on the status of the
paid parking program for downtown Ogden, including the publication of a request for proposals seeking a firm that will manage a
parking hardware and software system, and the scheduling of a meeting with stakeholders on 25th Street to discuss their concerns
regarding paid parking guidelines. She also provided an update on the status of the construction of parking garages on
WonderBlock. The Council engaged in discussion with Ms. Brown and Management Services Executive Director Stout regarding
the feasibility study and funding model that was prepared in the past by Kimley–Horn regarding the paid parking program and the
timing of the City’s receipt of revenue generated by the program; the Council wondered whether that funding model needed to be
adjusted based upon the delayed implementation of the program and Ms. Stout stated she believed that would be appropriate.
          Ms. Brown then reported on the completion of housing projects by the J. Fisher Company; many units have come
available for sale and have already sold to buyers. There has been a great deal of interest in the homes from single buyers, couples,
and families and the average sales price was $481,500.
          Ms. Brown concluded that the City Engineering Division has visited 20th Street in light of recent public feedback
regarding the completion of the construction project on the street that has limited on-street parking for residences along the
roadway. She noted once she has received a report from the engineers working on the project, she will report back to the Council.
This led to broad discussion about other construction projects in the areas surrounding 20th Street and limited access to streets and
properties in the area.
          Public Services Executive Director Anderson then reported on the timing of the Ogden Canyon waterline project, which
is scheduled to commence in January of 2025. He discussed the scope of slip lining existing pipes in the area of the well field and
the water treatment plant.
          Vice Chair White thanked Ms. Brown for her communication on vital issues in the City.


         The meeting adjourned at 5:44 p.m.
