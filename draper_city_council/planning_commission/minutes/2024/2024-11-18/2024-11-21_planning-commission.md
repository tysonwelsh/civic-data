1.     Items for Commission Consideration.

       A.      Public Hearing: Effervescent Life LLC, Home Occupation Conditional Use
               Permit Request (Administrative Action)
               On the Request of Joy Martin Representing Effervescent Life LLC, a Home
               Occupation Conditional Use Permit Request for a Home Occupation Providing
               Personal Care Services for Stress Management on 0.23 Acres, located at 2529 E.
               Springtime Road. Known as Application 2024-0331-USE. Staff Contact: Nick
               Whittaker, (801) 576-6522, nick.whittaker@draperutah.gov.

City Planner, Nick Whitaker presented the Staff Report and displayed the Vicinity, Aerial, Land
Use, and Zoning Maps of the subject property. The property is designated Residential Medium
Density and zoned RM. The Site Plan indicates proposed off-street parking in front of the third-
car garage, leaving unimpeded access to the two-car garage. Customers will enter through the
front door and services will be provided in a room to the right of the entry foyer. The applicant is
proposing an 18” x 12” sign in front of the home under a separate application. That application is
pending approval of this Home Occupation Conditional Use Permit (“CUP”). Mr. Whitaker
reported that the driveway is currently not in compliance as it exceeds the 30-foot maximum

allowed by Draper City Code. Mr. Whittaker recommended to the Planning Commission that they
include as a condition of approval that the applicant bring the driveway into compliance with
driveway standards found in the DCMC 9-10-075 within six months of the date of approval.

Identified impacts include customers coming to the home, customer vehicle parking, and related
traffic. The applicant provided the following five ways to mitigate those impacts:

       1.      All customer appointments shall be made in advance and online.

       2.      Limiting customers to one at a time, with a 30-minute buffer between appointments.

       3.      Bringing the driveway into compliance with the width requirements of the City
               Ordinance within six months of receiving the Planning Commission’s approval of
               the Home Occupation Conditional Use Permit.

       4.      Providing one off-street parking stall in front of the third car garage for customer
               vehicles.

       5.      Limiting hours of operation to between 8:00 a.m. and 5:00 p.m., with extended
               evening hours up to 9:00 p.m. on Tuesdays only.

       6.      Business signage will be properly permitted.

The applicant, Joy Martin stated that the business is focused on stress reduction and no noise will
be generated that can be heard outside of the home. There is a 30-minute buffer between
appointments so cars will not be entering and exiting the property simultaneously. She is
committed to following all City regulations and is very sensitive to how her business may impact
the neighborhood.

In response to a question raised by Commissioner Green, Ms. Martin indicated that Effervescent
Life, LLC utilizes a vibroacoustic therapy bed, one of only two in Utah. The bed aids in relaxation
by using a combination of music and vibration. She also utilizes red light therapy, which assists
with Seasonal Affective Disorder and also provides skin therapy.

Vice-Chair Fowler opened the public hearing.

Terelle Wickham commented that she beta-tested the therapy bed. After her daughter passed away,
she was given the opportunity to use the bed as often as she needed. She described how the therapy
bed and red lights have helped with her depression and anxiety. She reported that she drives over
one hour for the therapy and Ms. Martin is very professional.

There were no further public comments. The public hearing was closed.

       B.      Public Hearing: Public Hearing: 13081 S. Minuteman Drive Site Plan
               Amendment and Conditional Use Permit Requests (Administrative Action).
               On the request of Adam Orme of Gould Architects representing MicroFlex 1
               Springville LLC, a request for a Site Plan Amendment to modify the existing site
               to build a new office building and a Conditional Use Permit for an outdoor storage
               yard on approximately 2.93 acres, located at approximately 13081 S. Minuteman
               Drive, known as applications 2024-2023-SP and 2024-0322-USE. Staff contact:
               Maryann Pickering, (801) 576-6391, maryann.pickering@draperutah.gov.

City Planner, Maryann Pickering presented the Staff Report and stated that the subject property
has a Land Use designation of Community Commercial and is zoned Regional Commercial. The
site plan was reviewed, and it was noted that the site includes the existing Utah Disaster Kleenup
Draper City Planning Commission Meeting
November 21, 2024

(“UDK”) building. The vacant portion will be developed, and perimeter landscaping will be
updated as part of the project.

Elevations for the proposed office and commercial space were reviewed. Ms. Pickering reported
that the applicant is in the process of creating a Preliminary Plat to be considered by the Planning
Commission at a future meeting. No changes were being requested to the existing building.
Parking will be reconfigured to conform with the existing Code including natural deviation as
allowed by the City Code. The new commercial and office condominium units will be built at the
rear portion of the site, and the proposed building meets all City Code and design standards for
commercial development.

Staff could not find evidence of a CUP for the existing open storage yard, so the applicant was
asked to apply for one. The storage yard appears to have been in use since approximately 2007.
If approved, the CUP would legalize it.

The Conditions of Approval were reviewed. Ms. Pickering noted that several conditions were put
in place to address the 10% reduction in parking taken by the applicant as a natural deviation. The
conditions include the following:

   •   Parking is approved based on the uses shown on the Site Plan.

   •   All tenants will need to show that they meet parking requirements with their Business
       License application.

   •   UDK must use the open storage yard for employee parking.

   •   All walls and fences shall comply with applicable Draper City Municipal Code
       requirements.

   •   The storage yard shall be screened per City Code requirements.

   •   An updated lighting and landscaping plan showing compliance with all standards shall be
       submitted.

   •   An unobstructed fire lane shall be required.

In response to a question from Vice-Chair Fowler, Ms. Pickering reviewed the location of the
storage yard as well as the proposed fencing and sliding gate to enclose and secure the yard. She
noted that a Fire Department turnaround is not provided for the new building, so the parking lane
will need to remain open to provide space for emergency vehicles.

In response to questions from the Commissioners, Ms. Jastremsky stated that City Code was
modified to reduce potential parking deviations for commercial properties and eliminate potential
deviations for residential properties. The applicant has reduced parking stalls by 10%, which is
within the range allowed by ordinance. Staff will require a parking analysis with each Business

License application for the property. If a license is requested but no parking is available for that
specific use, the City could deny an application based on insufficient parking.

With regard to traffic concerns, it was clarified that there are presently no restrictions on 13065
South. It is a collector road with additional capacity. There are more traffic issues at the
intersection of Minuteman Drive and 12300 South. The subject intersection has not been an issue
in the past, and Staff had no traffic concerns for the site.

Project Architect, Adam Orme spoke on behalf of the applicant and noted that Matt Kelly and
Shivam Shah are the project developers. They are proposing a two-story professional office
building similar to Draper Cove. The building will consist of 11 for-sale condominium units with
four parking stalls per 1,000 square feet. The developer has entered into a lease agreement with
the property to the east for 22 shared parking stalls. 10 units have been presold.

With regard to the CUP, Mr. Orme indicated that the current storage yard will be reduced in size.
There is a 30-inch grade difference between the two areas of the property, which will be separated
by a precast concrete retaining wall. Precast fencing and gates will be installed along the front to
enclose and secure the storage yard. Existing loading areas will be maintained. Stormwater
drainage for the existing building will be incorporated into the landscaping, and the new building
will have an underground stormwater system.

In response to a question from Chair Andrews, Mr. Orme confirmed that they did not anticipate
the office building utilizing any parking stalls currently designated for UDK, but the UDK property
is part of the Owners Association, and those stalls can be used if necessary.

Chair Andrews opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public hearing
was closed.

       1.      The proposal complies with the standards for approval found in DCMC
               Section 9-5-080(E) and potential negative impacts are mitigated through the
