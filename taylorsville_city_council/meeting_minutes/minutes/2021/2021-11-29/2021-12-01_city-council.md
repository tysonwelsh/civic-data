The agenda for the City Council Meeting was reviewed.

Councilmember Harker reviewed items that the Parks and Recreation committee planned to be
involved in over the next year. Specifically, the committee was interested in expanding the annual
5k run to include a bicycle race and an activity in the Jordan River. Other Council members gave
enthusiastic approval. City Attorney Tracy Cowdell stated the committee would need to make
sure safety issues were considered and that the activity was approved by the City’s insurance
carrier. The committee was scheduling five movies in the park for the summer and asked about
the possibility of food trucks being involved as well. Mayor Overson suggested Councilmember
Harker contact Jen Andrus to make those arrangements.

Finally, someone had reached out to the committee chair and expressed interest in hosting a spike
ball tournament. There would be no cost to the city although the tournament would be held at a
city park.

     City Administrator John Taylor indicated that the interested party would just need to reserve
the park as would anyone else.

Councilmember Armstrong asked that Salt Lake County be asked to trim the trees at Swensen
Park, specifically along the trail so that walkers and joggers did not have to duck to avoid hitting
branches. Additionally, he pointed out that there were low hanging trees along roadways which
could cause damage to tall vehicles. Mr. Taylor asked that Councilmember Armstrong let
administration or code enforcement know of specific problem areas so that they could be
addressed. Councilmember Armstrong indicated that the sidewalks along 3200 West were
particularly bad.

Another issue brought forth by Councilmember Armstrong was that he felt that in comparison to
other municipalities, Taylorsville’s streets were dark and needed more streetlights at night. He
mentioned 2700 West specifically and conceded that it would be an expensive endeavor. Mr.
Taylor indicated that Mayor Overson was already looking at what the best standard of lighting
would be and the associated cost of implementing that standard. Finally, he asked for increased
Christmas lighting at Centennial Plaza as well as at city parks. Mayor Overson loved the idea and
asked that the council keep that idea in mind for the next budget.

    Councilmember Armstrong brought up Christmas Tree Lane and asked how traffic was being
handled and whether there had been complaints from neighbors. Mr. Taylor was not aware of any
complaints.

Councilmember Armstrong stated that the businesses around 6200 South Bangerter Highway felt
ignored and were not sure they’d survive due to the impacts of the long-term construction in the

Mayor Kristie Overson directed the Pledge of Allegiance before Tracy Cowdell offered a

        1.2      Mayor’s Report

     Mayor Kristie Overson reported on the new signage at the Plymouth fire station. Also, she
had recently had the opportunity to represent Taylorsville at the Women in Business conference
and the Mountain America Credit Union Athena Awards. MACU was a great partner to the city
and had four locations within Taylorsville’s borders.

Mayor Overson explained that the Youth Council had dropped off a Thanksgiving Day meal to a
local family of five and she extended her thanks to Merari Lopez of the Taylorsville Police
Department who identified the family’s need. The Youth Council also helped the Historic
Preservation Committee decorate the museum for Christmas as well as the tree at City Hall. She
thanked Kris Heineman for decorating the Council Chambers and other areas within City Hall.
The Youth Council’s Brandon Sorensen spent time on a job shadow of Mayor Overson and in
return, she would be reciprocating and shadowing him at Taylorsville High School soon.

Mayor Overson praised the now completed Shop Local program and explained that 95% of the
“bucks” were spent. She thanked the city council for allocating the funds for this successful
program and hoped the council would choose to fund the program next year as well.

      The mayor commented on the Youth Council’s plan to participate in a Sub-For-Santa event
for a Taylorsville family in need. She appreciated their priority of service to the community.

The owners of the SpeedCo Laundry Mat in Taylorsville had recently reached out to the police
department (as they did each year) to help them identify five families who they could provide
meals for. She stated Wes and Susie were grateful to give back to the community who had been
loyal to their business.

TVPD Sgt. Ryan Carver (who had previously worked for West Valley Police) and WVPD Sgt.
Ammon Fox continued a five-year tradition of providing a hot breakfast to public safety members
on Thanksgiving morning. The mayor enjoyed stopping by and seeing the activity in action.

Aiden, the son of a TVPD officer had started a program called Angel Hug Kids after experiencing
a traumatic event. That experience left him with a desire to help other children who experienced
difficulty or trauma. He created fifty “comfort kits” with a blanket and toys which he then gave
to police officers to distribute as needed.

7.      OTHER MATTERS

        7.1      Resolution No. 21-37 – A Resolution of the City of Taylorsville approving the
                 K9 Policy for the Taylorsville Police Department – Chief Brady Cottam

        7.2      Resolution No. 21-38 – A Resolution of the City of Taylorsville approving the
                 Property and Evidence Policy for the Taylorsville Police Department – Chief
                 Brady Cottam

     Chief Brady Cottam presented both Resolution No. 21-37 and No. 21-38 at the same time.
He was very excited about the K9 program because of the benefit it would bring to Taylorsville.
He had wanted to take the time to ensure it was done right, especially with the current climate
around the country. The program was primarily used as a locating tool for both contraband and
criminals. He was happy to break down the resolutions individually and welcomed any questions.

     The Property and Evidence policy provided for proper collection, storage, and security of
found property as well as evidentiary items. It also provided protection to the chain of custody
and the destruction of property where appropriate.

Councilmember Harker asked how much physical storage space was needed. Chief Cottam
responded that he was comfortable with the amount of space currently available and felt it would
meet future needs as well. He pointed out that there were many laws and procedures that needed
to be followed and he was very grateful for Evidence Manager Paula Lozano’s skill and experience
in this area.

Chief Cottam discussed the timelines for property retention and mentioned that some cases such
as homicide required that evidence be retained permanently. He stated the police department
worked hand in hand with the Taylorsville Justice Court to clear out evidence in misdemeanor
cases as soon as possible once those cases were adjudicated.

Tracy Cowdell suggested that if councilmembers had more in-depth questions about the storage
of evidence and chain of custody, they should feel free to contact Chief Cottam for more
information rather than discuss security matters in a public meeting.

The chief also took a moment to ask the public to take the time needed to document serial numbers
on tools and other expensive items in their homes so that in the event they were stolen and then
recovered, the police would be able to return the items to their rightful owners.

Councilmember Armstrong inquired about the K9 officers and how they were doing. Chief Cottam
responded that the dogs were doing well. Two had been certified and one was still in training.
They were dual purpose animals in that they would be able to assist their handlers with both
narcotics detection and criminal apprehension. He explained that Sgt. Jake Palmer had put
together an excellent K9 program.

Councilmember Harker remarked on how very thorough the K9 policy was.

        7.3      Resolution No. 21-39 – A Resolution of the City of Taylorsville approving an
                 Interlocal Agreement Transferring TRCC Funds from Salt Lake County to
                 Taylorsville for the Purpose of Renovating Taylorsville Park – John Taylor

      John Taylor explained that the city had previously applied for two grants. One was for city
center improvements and the other was for improvements to Taylorsville Park. Both grants were
awarded, but the process had been put on hold when COVID hit. The award process for the park
was now ready to move forward.

Councilmember Harker asked if the funds would need to be spent each year. Mr. Taylor responded
that $500,000 could be spent in each of three years or the city could wait for three years and seek
the reimbursement of the full $1.7 million at that time.

Councilmember Harker expressed disappointment that more funds were not being awarded to the
city for the park project. Mr. Taylor explained that an agreement to transfer ownership of the park
from Salt Lake County to the city was currently being ironed out. If the city wished to seek
additional funding for the park, we could return to the county and potentially seek additional
funding. Mr. Taylor pointed out that the TRCC Board (Tourism, Recreation, Culture and
Convention) had been very kind to Taylorsville in the past and had been great to work with. He
recommended that the council accept the agreement but agreed that additional funding would
likely be needed for the project. He also clarified that the city would need to enter into contracts
for the work but would then submit reimbursement requests to Salt Lake County.

Councilmember Barbieri suggested waiting 6-9 months to spend the funds since costs were
currently so high. Mayor Overson and Mr. Taylor agreed that the city was not currently ready to
spend the funds but merely needed to have the council decide on the resolution before moving
forward with project planning. Still, the mayor hoped for some project completions sooner rather
than later.

Councilmember Harker asked when the park property transfer would take place. Mr. Taylor
responded that Salt Lake County wished it to be finalized by the end of 2021.

Councilmember Burgess expressed his gratitude for the funding and looked forward to park
improvements. Mr. Taylor noted that the original funding request had been for $4.5 million. Mr.
Cowdell reiterated that the city and county funds needed to be spent by September 30, 2024.
