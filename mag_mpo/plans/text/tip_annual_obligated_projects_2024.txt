1 INTRODUCTION
The MAG Metropolitan Planning Organization (MAG) has responsibility for all projects within the MPO boundary,
which includes all the incorporated municipalities within Utah County, the unincorporated areas that are adjacent to and
between the municipalities, and the area contiguous to Redwood Rd/HWY-68

2 PLANNINGPROCESS
The MPO is responsible for providing a continuing, comprehensive, and cooperative transportation
planning process for the expenditure of Federal funds within the MPO. The planning process is carried
out through coordination between the MAG and its regional partners.
The MAG MPO Board serves as the policy-making body for the MPO and is responsible for officially
adopting all plans. The Board has a Technical Subcommittee consisting of planners and engineers from
the same agencies.

3 THE ANNUAL OBLIGATION REPORT
This report serves as an organizational and monitoring tool and works to promote planning for an
efficient and cohesive transportation system. In creating these reports, the MPO collaborates with other
state and local organizations, including UDOT, and the Utah Transit Authority. The 4-year TIP lists the
type and location for all federally funded and regionally significant transportation projects scheduled
over the four-year period, as well as their funding sources.
Since there is potential for slight variations in the TIP due to changes in cost and timing of projects, one
requirement of the transportation act passed by Congress in 2005 titled the Safe, Accountable, Flexible,
Efficient Transportation Equity Act: A Legacy for Users (SAFETEA-LU), and carried forward with the
current transportation act, is an Annual Listing of Projects, including investments in pedestrian walkways
and bicycle transportation facilities, for which Federal funds that have been obligated in the preceding
year shall be published or otherwise made available by the cooperative effort of the State, transit
operator, and Metropolitan Planning Organization for public review. The listing shall be consistent with
the funding categories identified in each metropolitan Transportation Improvement Program (TIP).”
In accordance with Federal requirements, the MPO has published its “Annual Listing of Federally
Obligated Transportation Projects” for federal fiscal year 2024 (October 1, 2023 – August 31, 2024)
based on the Federal Financial Management Information System (FMIS). The purpose of this report is to
provide to the public, as well as State and local officials, information regarding federal spending on
transportation projects within the Indianapolis region, along with a progress report and disclosure of
project delivery occurring over a shorter planning period of only one year.
If a project is “Federally obligated,” it means that the Federal government has made a legal
commitment to pay or reimburse the state and/or local jurisdictions for the Federal share of a project’s

eligible costs. The normal split for Federal and local funds contributed to transportation projects is
93.3% Federal and 6.7% local.
