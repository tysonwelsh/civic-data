G. ACTION ITEMS:
     1. APPROVAL OF AN INTER-LOCAL COOPERATION AGREEMENT WITH WEBER-MORGAN
        HEALTH DEPARTMENT AND WEBER HUMAN SERVICES FOR THE OPERATION OF THE
        PROSPERITY CENTER OF EXCELLENCE.
            Melissa Freigang (Prosperity Center of Excellence): changes in Section 2 moving administration oversight
            from the Health Department to the Commission. Additional comment on successes from the past two years.
            Commissioner Jenkins: most counties have privatized their equivalent Prosperity Center of Excellence
            efforts. Utah State Homeless Services Coordinator, Wayne Niederhauser, said Weber County’s system is
            only one in the state that works, only county in the state where a Commissioner is actively involved; none of
            the other counties are that way and every single one of them struggles.

            Commissioner Jenkins moved to approve the Inter-local Cooperation Agreement by and between Weber
            County, Weber-Morgan Health Department and Weber Human Services for the operation of the Prosperity
            Center of Excellence; Commissioner Froerer seconded.
            Commissioner Froerer – aye; Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Harvey – aye
     2. REQUEST FOR APPROVAL OF A MEMORANDUM OF WITH OGDEN CITY TO MAKE A
        JOINT APPLICATION FOR AN EDWARD BYRNE MEMORIAM JUSTICE ASSISTANCE
        GRANT (JAG).
            Lt. Cortney Ryan (Sheriff’s Office) explained this is a yearly grant, Weber County is subgrantee, Ogden
         City is grant custodian. Weber County to receive $10500 ($5300 for maintenance/upkeep training program;
         $5206 toward policing tools).

         Commissioner Froerer moved to approve the Memorandum of Understanding with Ogden City to make a joint
         application for an Edward Byrne Memoriam Justice Assistance Grant (JAG); Commissioner Jenkins seconded.
         Commissioner Froerer – aye; Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Harvey – aye

         2. Public hearing to discuss amendments to the operating and capital budget of Weber County for the 2021
            calendar year.
                  Scott Parke (County Comptroller) presented regular adjustments to 2nd Quarter 2021 operating and
                  capital budgets.
                   Largest adjustment is the American Recovery Plan Act (ARPA) funds.
                           o $25M already in hand
                           o Plan due at the end of 2024 of how funds will be spent
                           o Execute plan by the end of 2026.
                   $179k from the state of Utah for election equipment.
                   $50k to add fiber optic to Golden Spike to fix internet connection.
                   $578k for heavy rescue vehicle paid with cash from Paramedics account, saving over $70k in
                      interest over finance period.
                   $140k from fund balance to finish repairs on Bybee Drive.
                   Total expenditures $26.29M, total revenue $25.49M
           Commissioner Froerer: Another $25M ARPA funds available in one year, totaling $50M the county has
           for priority projects. State also received $1.5B of which a portion is intended for priority projects. ARPA
           funds intended for long term projects that would not be afforded otherwise.
