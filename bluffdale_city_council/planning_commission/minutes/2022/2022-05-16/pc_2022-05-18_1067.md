Mr. Sheeran addressed property rights, which is a topic that generates a wide range of opinions. The
debate includes what people should be able to do on their prope1ty and what people believe other
property owners should not be allowed to do because it interferes with their ability to enjoy their own
prope1ty. The solution can range from heavy to no regulation. Typically, there should be a "sweet
spot" in the middle that allows for equitable enjoyment.

Mr. Sheeran made a PowerPoint presentation that provided a hypothetical scenaiio that represented
typical occmTences in Bluffdale. The example, however, included way too many elements for just
one piece of property. Property owners must always comply with the zoning requirements of their
respective properties. It was noted that zoning standards change over time. If an application does
not work because of the zoning requirements, the applicant has the following options:

       1.      The person can request a zoning change to find a zone that does work.

       2.      A text amendment can be requested to change the language that does not work to
               something that does work.

       3.      The person can request a variance. Obtaining a variance is very difficult because of
               the stringent requirements specified in the Utah State Code. An unacceptable, but all
               too common, the option is to make the change illegally.

Mr. Sheeran reviewed the land uses allo~ed in the R-1-43 zone to see how his requests would align
with the pennitted or conditional uses of that zone. The hypothetical property also included a portion
of land zoned Commercial. In that case, a zone change would be necessary to make it all R-1-43.
City Planner/Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell, stated that the Planning Commission
typically deals with issues contained in Title 11 or 12, the Subdivision Ordinance, and the Land Use
Ordinance. On rare occasions, the Planning Commission may encounter a situation covered in Title
3, which deals with business licensing. Title 5, the Animal Control Ordinance, would rarely if ever,
become an issue for the Planning Commission to consider.

Mr. Sheeran reported that some of the text in the City Code is so old that there is no historical
knowledge of how it came to be. Often text amendments occur when the City Council is trying to fix
an issue that has come before them. Historical research can reveal fascinating laws that were created
in the past that no longer apply. When the Planning Commission considers a zone change proposal,
it needs to consider the scope of what is being presented. Zone changes are legislative decisions that
fall under the purview of the City Council, with the recommendation of the Planning C01mnission.
Conditional Use Pennits are administrative decisions; therefore, the Planning Commission is the Land
Use Authority. Variance requests are considered by a Hearing Officer.

When the Planning Commission reviews issues for decisions or recommendations, those issues have
first been analyzed by City Staff to inform the decisions/recommendations to be made. Chair Cragun
brought up the issue of selling flowers. She asked if the volume of sales should be a consideration.
Mr. Sheeran acknowledged that that was a good question and deferred it to the New Bluffdale City
Attorney. It is important to get a complete picture of what is being proposed and how it fits within
the business licensing process. Business hours and traffic generated are important considerations.

Mr. Crowell said that State Code needs to be taken into consideration. If a Conditional Use Pe1mit is
denied, substantial findings need to be articulated to show how the detrimental impact of the proposed
use ca1mot be adequately mitigated. The key is to have verifiable and legally defensible data to
establish how it aligns with the City and State Codes. Case law has established that anger from
"public clamor" is not an acceptable justification for denying a conditional use. Mr. Crowell said that
the use of the word "seem" must be avoided.

Mr. Sheeran noted the example of the flower stand and stated that traffic would not be a suitable
reason for denial since the traffic generated by a nearby school already makes traffic a problem. The
flower stand would not "create" the traffic problem because it already exists. Conditional uses are
permitted, so the burden of proof to deny a conditional use rests on the City using legally defensible
data. All laws need to be taken into account to ensure that proposed uses are also legal.

Mr. Sheeran explained that appeals to legislative decisions go straight to the District Court. Typically,
developers will not appeal a legislative decision because of the cost and complexity of an appeal.
Appeals to Conditional Use Pennits, which are administrative decisions, would go to the Hearing
Officer. The Hearing Officer would review the public record to verify whether the denial of or the
imposition of conditions to, the Conditional Use Permit was based on legally defensible evidentiary
standards. If the decision of the Hearing Officer is contested, it goes to the District Court. To
minimize challenges to Conditional Use Permits, thorough preparation is essential, both by the
applicant and by Staff.

Mr. Sheeran next addressed the issue of variances. The burden of proof for a variance is onerous
and rests on the applicant. There are five c1iteria enumerated in Utah Code Almotated § 10-9a-702,
Section 11-3-4(A)(l)(ii), all of which must all be met to grant a variance:

       (i)      literal enforcement of the ordinance would cause an unreasonable hardship for the
                applicant that is not necessary to carry out the general purpose of the land use
                ordinances;

       (ii)     there are special circumstances attached to the property that do not generally apply
                to other properties in the same zone;

        (iii)   granting the variance is essential to the enjoyment of a substantial property right
                possessed by other property in the same zone;

        (iv)    the variance will not substantially affect the general plan and will not be contrary to
                the public interest; and

        (v)     the spirit of the land use ordinance is observed and substantial justice done.

If even one criterion is not met, the variance must be denied. In that light, it has been a long time
since a variance has been granted in Bluffdale. The solution in some instances has been a text
amendment to the City Code, as approved by the City Council. If an appeal to a variance denial
occurs, it goes straight to the District Court. Mr. Sheeran shared examples of variance denials that

6.     Planning Commission Business (Planning Session for Upcoming Items, Follow Up, Etc.)

Mr. Crowell reported that Staff is working on the General Plan with the City Council. A few meetings
still need to take place before it is voted on. The budget was a hot topic with the City Council. The
Centrum might have room for some more units, so it was possible that there would be revisions to the
Development Agreement to consider.

A Special District proposal on the south end of Independence was being crafted on property that is
owned by Geneva. A great deal of work still needed to be done before it comes before the Planning
Commission. The City was also working on how to comply with the requirement to have three
identifiable strategies to make moderate-income housing more readily available. A presentation
provided by the League of Cities and Towns ("ULCT") was being used to help with the creation of
that report. The State Legislature was also expecting cities to provide and measure implementation
strategies. One topic that has been fraught with controversy was whether the upper floor of a detached
garage can be converted into an apartment. Ms. Oakman added that efforts need to be made to bring
illegal dwelling units into compliance with the City Code. Mr. Crowell stated that there are many
options and issues to consider. The State Legislature was trying to ensure that moderately priced
housing is located close to where people work. The Legislature will likely leverage its authority to
encourage more moderately-priced housing.

Chair Cragun reported that she was in New York City recently, and the concept of high density along
the Wasatch Front is laughable compared to the high density that exists in Manhattan. Mr. Sheeran
stated that there is no consistent definition of what "high density" means. For example, in Utah, six
units per acre is high density. In New York City, 1,000 units per acre is considered high density. Mr.
Sheeran stated that developers wield a lot of influence at the Utah State Capitol to advocate for higher
density development. National conversations also have an impact on what takes place in Utah. A lot
of misconceptions exist about what is taking place in the South Salt Lake Valley. Growth and
development are highly controversial and will not go away.
