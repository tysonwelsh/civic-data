       5.2     Training on Open and Public Meetings Act. (StaffPresenter^ Fred Donaldson),
               [LBA and RDA Training on Meeting Procedures Held in Conjunction!.

Mayor Hall reported that the training on the Open and Public Meetings Act would cover the training
for the City Council, LBA,and RDA meetings. City Attorney, Fred Donaldson, was asked to prepare
training that included more detail than some previous trainings. Mr. Reid pointed out that Council
Members and the Mayor would receive the same kind of training on other committees they serve on.
It is done annually.


Mr. Donaldson reported that the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act governs the meetings of public
bodies. General purpose language in the act states that "It is the intention of the Legislature that
political subdivisions take actions openly and conduct deliberations openly. Therefore, meetings of
public bodies are open to the public unless properly closed." He explained that meetings could only
be closed for specific reasons. A meeting is defined as the convening ofa public body with a quorum
present (simple majority). In the case of the Bluffdale City Council, a quorum is three Council
Members. A meeting includes a workshop or Executive Session for the purpose of discussing,
receiving comments from the public, or acting on any matter over which the public body has
jurisdiction or advisory power. A meeting can be conducted in person, or electronically, or it can be
a hybrid meeting.


A meeting is not a chance meeting or social gathering. Mr. Donaldson explained that a meeting does
not include a meeting of a public body with legislative and executive responsibilities where no public
funds are appropriated, and the meeting is solely for administrative/operational matters. He clarified
that the latter was not something that happens often. Mayor Hall noted that the City Recorder
normally notices ribbon-cutting ceremonies and events such as the recent holiday event. Whenever
something involves a quorum,there is appropriate noticing.

Mr. Donaldson reported that a public body is a city administrative, advisory, executive, or legislative
body that is created by the Utah Constitution,statute, rule, ordinance, or resolution. It consists oftwo
or more persons and is spent, distributed, or supported by tax money. Additionally, a public body
had the authority to make decisions on behalf of the public. Examples in Bluffdale include the City

The noticing requirements were reviewed. Mr. Donaldson explained that at least 24 hours' notice is
necessary, and the notice needs to include the agenda, date, time, and place of the meeting. The
agenda also needs to be reasonably specific. A body could discuss a matter not on the agenda, but it
is not possible to take final action on that matter during the meeting. It would need to be rescheduled
for a future meeting to take action. The notice must be posted at City Hall and on the Utah Public
Notice website. There was also encouragement to provide notice through other electronic means. In
addition, a regular meeting schedule for a public body must be noticed once per year. Mr. Reid noted
that there is a website feature where those interested can receive an email whenever a new notice is

posted. Those interested could sign up for that.


Mr. Donaldson reported that there are procedures for emergency meetings. If an emergency meeting
is necessary, regular notice is not required as long as the best notice possible is provided. He next
discussed the Meeting Minutes. Mr. Donaldson reported that Meeting Minutes are required to be
kept. The current meeting was being recorded and would be converted into minutes that would be
reviewed and approved at a future meeting. The Meeting Minutes need to include information such
as the date, time, and place of the meeting as well as the members present, the substance of the
discussions, and the individual votes. The written minutes are the official record ofthe meeting. The
Meeting Minutes must be detailed enough to understand what took place.

Bluffdale can satisfy the Meeting Minutes requirements by maintaining a publicly available online
version and providing a link to the recording. The Meeting Minutes are classified as public under the

Government Records and Management Act("GRAMA"). Pending versions ofMeeting Minutes need
to be made available to the public within 30 days. Approved public materials distributed at the
meeting and the actual Meeting Minutes need to be posted on the Utah Public Notice website, made

Mr. Donaldson reviewed the recording requirements and reiterated that all open meetings need to be
recorded and the recording needs to be complete and unedited. Meetings can also be recorded by
members of the public. The recording is considered a public record under GRAMA. Meetings can
be conducted electronically but the notice requirements still apply. That came up frequently during
the COVID-19 pandemic where electronic meetings were held more frequently to avoid gatherings.
However, even with an electronic meeting, members of the public still have to have a way to attend
and participate in meetings. There also needs to be a resolution, rule, or ordinance governing the use
ofelectronic meetings. In Bluffdale, an ordinance was adopted for the City Council to hold electronic
meetings. During an electronic meeting, all votes need to be conducted by roll call with the exception
of unanimous votes.


As for electronic messages during meetings, it was clarified that members of a public body cannot
transmit electronic messages (text messages, emails, or instant messages) to other members of the
public body during an open meeting. Mr. Donaldson next discussed disruptions during meetings.
The City can remove someone from a meeting if that person is willfully disrupting the meeting to the
extent that orderly conduct is seriously compromised. The Bluffdale City Code also includes
provisions regarding disorderly and/or disruptive conduct.


Mr. Donaldson reviewed information about Closed Meetings and explained that meetings are open
unless closed for specific reasons. No local governmental meetings are legally required to be closed,
which means the decision to close a meeting is discretionary. There are a few exceptions to that that
pertain to certain State entities and State statutes. To close a meeting, a quorum of the public body
needs to be present and two-thirds ofthe members present need to vote to approve closing the meeting.
Two-thirds is 66.7%, which means four out of five Council Members on the Bluffdale City Council

A violation of the Open and Public Meetings Act is a known or intentional violation, which is a
Class B Misdemeanor. A court can void any action taken in violation of the Open and Public
Meetings Act but a violation can also be cured during a subsequent meeting. Enforcement was done
by the Attorney General and the County Attorney. A private citizen who is denied a right may also
commence a lawsuit. A party has 90 days to file a suit to void an action and 30 days to file a suit to
void an action concerning a bond, note, or other debt.

be kept confidential. Each individual participant needs to respect the confidentiality of the Closed

individual being discussed heard about it via the media. It was a difficult and uncomfortable situation.
He stressed the importance of confidentiality.

Mr. Donaldson reported that the next training pertained to meeting procedures. There are Code
provisions that provide guidance. Bluffdale City Code 1.60.020(1) states "Except as otherwise
provided by law, it is the intent of the City Council that its meetings be conducted in an orderly,
efficient, and fair manner without undue formality. To that end, the City Council shall conduct its
meetings generally consistent with Robert's Rules of Order." He noted that in Bluffdale,the decision
was made to not have meetings that are overly formal. That was the reason for the terms "generally
consistent" rather than "strictly." It was intended to be a guide.
