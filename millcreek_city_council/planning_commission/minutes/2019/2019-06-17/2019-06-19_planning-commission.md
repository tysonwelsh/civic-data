Blaine Gehring
Blaine Gehring said the property was rezoned to MD (Mixed Use) in January 2018 for the

make findings as they make a decision regarding a zoning map amendment, development
agreement, or city initiative. Gehring said this project meets many of those essential
questions. Clearwater Homes, LLC would like to have a new development agreement
adopted to allow for 139 three-story townhomes with 2,200 square feet of commercial space

2. The setbacks for the builds will be as established on the site plan.

3. The project will include parking stalls for the development in accordance with City standards
and further defined on the site plan to include separate parking for the park in addition to
required residential and commercial parking.

4. The project will consist of 35 percent of the project site in common open space and 25% of the

unit developments in §19.78.030(K) of the Millcreek Code.

5. The open space will include the following residential amenities, some of which will be included

6. The project will consist of 139 two-bedroom townhome units with not less than 50% of the units
for sale.

7. The developer will install street lighting in accordance with Millcreek City standards. Street and
parking lot lighting will be compliant with dark skies best practices.

8. The developer will provide a second access onto Central Avenue, either through an access
agreement with adjoining properties or through other means.

9. The developer will install fencing for individual units and in accordance with Millcreek City
standards.

10. Uses will be limited to multi-family residential, live/work style commercial units and general
commercial retail.

11. Landscaping will be provided as per Millcreek Zoning Ordinance Chapter 19.71.

12. The developer will pursue an energy efficient design that will greatly reduce CO2 emissions
resulting from the operation of the project.

13. Snow removal be managed so that guest parking stalls are not used for snow storage.

2.5 Consideration of ZT-19-002, Amendments to Millcreek Code Title 19 Noticing and
Hearing Requirements for Land Use Hearings Planner: Francis Lilly
Francis Lilly said there have been questions by residents on land use hearing determinations.
Lilly said the ordinance would give the hearing officer criteria for the decision making and
create noticing and participation requirements for the hearings. He went over the list of what
the hearing officer can make decisions on, which were:
a) Variances in General (19.92.050)
b) Variances to the Requirements of Floodplain Hazard Regulations (19.74.190)
c) Appeals of Planning Commission Decisions (19.76.035, 19.88.140)
d) Appeals of Conditional Use Decisions (19.84.070)
e) Appeals of Decisions by the Planning Director regarding Single-and Two-Family Dwellings
(19.76.290)
f) Appeals of Decisions by the Planning Director regarding Minimum Parking Requirements
(19.80.040)
g) Certain approvals of lots and buildings on private rights-of-way (19.76.080)
h) Clarification on zone boundaries (19.06.040)
i) Disputes of decisions made regarding the Geologic Hazards Ordinance (19.75.040)
j) Permits for the establishment of lots that are smaller than the minimum required by zone
(19.76.090)
k) Alterations, Enlargements, Relocations, or Reconstructions of a Noncomplying Structure or a
Structure Occupying a Nonconforming Use (19.88.040, -070)
Lilly said staff wanted to make changes to the process and then he went over the proposed
concepts. He said cities have an obligation to let the public know about the decisions made.
He proposed the City mail written notice to property owners within 150 feet of a property
subject to a land use hearing. The nature of variances and appeals are predicated on a
property right, the notice should be limited to those most impacted by the decision. Notices
will also be sent via email to the community council chair and these will be noticed 14 days
in advance of the hearing. The reason for that is that people who have standing in the matter
must provide advance notice of evidence. He said because the hearing is a legal proceeding,
he did not recommend that it be heard in a public meeting before the Planning Commission
or City Council, however the community council could submit evidence and participate in
the hearing. The land use hearing officer would be required to make additional findings
ensuring that the structure complies with the City’s Residential Compatibility Overlay Zone
(RCOZ). The ordinance will empower the land use hearing officer to impose limitations upon
the issuance of a permit to mitigate adverse effects on other properties in the neighborhood
instead of implied power. Staff also recommended the addition of language that stipulates
that anyone seeking to participate in a land use hearing must file an application to intervene
within five days of the hearing.
Lilly said the community councils reviewed the ordinance in March and three of the four
recommended adoption with a unanimous vote. The Millcreek Community Council preferred
a 300-foot notice parameter and that all members of the community council be noticed
instead of only the chair. Lilly recommended one point of contact. Lilly shared that the
Millcreek Together General Plan includes a goal in the Unique Neighborhoods element (Goal

residents during zoning and rezoning process, capital improvement planning, and decisions

“Create and implement state-of-the-art, transparent, clear, and cost effective methods to

Commissioner Stephens asked about section 19.88.070(3) on additions, enlargements,

uses that come to the Planning Commission, but other nonconforming use approvals would
go through this process. Chair Healey recommended that the whole community council be
notified, not just the chair. Commissioner LaMar said 300 feet should be the standard for
mailed notification. He asked if one hearing officer could override a decision by a
Commission of nine members. Brems said yes, if the decision was arbitrary, capricious, or
illegal. Commissioner LaMar asked for subsection 19.92.030 (3)(a) and (b) in Hearings and
Due Process be tied together because one refers to a timely request and the other says five
days, so it could be confusing. John Brems said intervention has to be based on facts and
evidence. The Commission requested that the mailed notification be to property owners
within 300 feet for consistency with other notices. Lilly expressed concern about the range
being too large for it to affect someone. Brems said he liked the 300-foot notice because
those noticed for the Planning Commission meeting would not be noticed for the appeal.
Commissioner Reid asked about nonconforming uses and noncomplying structures. Lilly
provided examples of each. Lilly said the non-conforming use ordinance is generous, it came
from Salt Lake County, and he would like to tighten it up. Brems said he has done about ten
hearings and they have been on making an improvement to the community and the property.
Commissioner Reid recommended changing the words “not detrimental” to “beneficial” in
subsection 19.88.070 (2)(c). Commissioner Allen asked how enlarging a structure is
beneficial to the community. Chair Healey said the investment of a property is an
enhancement to the neighborhood value. The Commission decided to keep the proposed
language as “not detrimental.” Elyse Greiner requested references to the City Recorder be
changed to the City Recorder’s Office since the Deputy Recorder currently processes the
hearings. The Commission was in favor of that change.
