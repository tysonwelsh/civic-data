1. A Report from the Provo Citizens' Sustainability and Natural Resources Committee (24-
009) 0:05:27

Don Jarvis, Chair of the Provo Citizens' Sustainability and Natural Resources Committee,
presented a report detailing the committee's recent activities and initiatives. He highlighted the
committee's outreach efforts, which include educational displays and visits to elementary schools
aimed at increasing awareness about air quality. Mr. Jarvis also introduced the green business
awards program as a means to recognize environmentally conscious businesses in the
community.

In his presentation, Mr. Jarvis discussed Provo Energy’s initiatives, including the ARC tool
exchange program, which facilitates access to energy-efficient tools, and the heat pump rebate
program designed to promote energy-saving technologies. He also mentioned the shared solar
options available to residents. Notably, he reported that Provo’s energy portfolio currently
consists of approximately 50% renewable energy sources, significantly outpacing the 20%
renewable energy benchmark of Rocky Mountain Power.

Mr. Jarvis emphasized the critical need to consider externalities when assessing the
environmental impacts and associated costs of various activities and technologies. He illustrated
this point by providing examples of pollution sources, such as lawn mowers and heating systems,
that contribute to environmental degradation yet are often excluded from traditional cost-benefit
analyses.

2. A Discussion Regarding Slate Canyon Drive Parking (24-031) 0:14:21

Melia Dayley, Policy Analyst, presented an overview of the parking situation on Slate Canyon
Drive. She outlined the primary goals of addressing congestion and regulating over-occupancy in
rental units. Ms. Dayley summarized feedback from a neighborhood meeting held on April 11,
2024, which provided valuable insights into resident concerns and preferences.

Based on staff analysis and a cost-benefit assessment, Ms. Dayley recommended implementing a
parking regulation area. The proposal included banning on-street parking between midnight and
6:00 a.m., as opposed to establishing a permit program.

Council members engaged in a discussion regarding the proposal.

Motion

Councilor George Handley made the following motion:

“I move that we adopt the parking regulation proposal as recommended by staff and also request
that staff develop a strategy for increased zoning enforcement and follow-up to the residents
about the efforts.”

The motion was seconded by Councilor Craig Christensen and was approved unanimously with a
7:0 vote.

Ms. Dayley confirmed that staff would prepare a proposal for sign implementation to be
presented at a May meeting, with the regulations set to take effect on June 1, 2024.

3. A Discussion Regarding the Accessory Dwelling Unit Pathway Process (24-016) 0:42:42

Michael Sanders, Policy Analyst, provided an update on the Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU)
pathway process, detailing the methods available for obtaining ADU approvals, such as the
signature path and non-signature path options. Council members engaged in discussions about
several critical aspects of ADU regulations:

Enforcement Challenges Concerns were raised about the difficulties in enforcing owner-
occupancy requirements, which have implications for the effective management of ADUs in
residential areas. While ADU owners are required to live on the property, enforcement largely
depends on responding to complaints. Additionally, complexities arise when properties are
owned by LLCs, complicating the determination of true ownership.

Neighborhood Character and Housing Affordability The council reflected on how ADUs
might alter neighborhood dynamics and impact affordable housing. There were mixed
perspectives, with some members concerned that unregulated ADUs could lead to increased

illegal duplex conversions, potentially reducing owner-occupied housing stock. Others
emphasized the benefits of allowing legal ADUs to address housing shortages while providing a
regulated mechanism for improving housing options.

Renter Protections The council deliberated on protecting renters evicted due to landlords' illegal
use of properties as ADUs or duplexes. It was noted that renters often lack knowledge about the
legality of their units, putting them in vulnerable positions when evictions occur due to
compliance actions. Discussions highlighted the potential for enhancing legal mechanisms to
support affected tenants.

Motion 1

Councilor Craig Christensen proposed the following motion:

“I move that we take four months to evaluate effective and creative cross-departmental options
for education and enforcement of illegal ADUs. During such time, the Council intends to make
no decisions on new multiple properties OTAs.”

The motion was seconded by Councilor Katrice MacKay and was approved 5:2, with Councilors
Whipple and Handley opposed.

Motion 2

Councilor Rachel Whipple proposed the following motion:

“I move that we direct Council Staff to work with the Legal Department to explore possible
protections for renters evicted due to landlords’ illegal use of property, including a private cause
of action or enhanced penalties against non-compliant property owners, contingent upon state
law constraints.”

The motion was seconded by Councilor Craig Christensen and was approved unanimously 7:0.

Both motions were successfully passed.

4. A Discussion Regarding a Resolution to Place Approximately 0.061 Acres of Vacant
Land at 480 West Center on the Surplus Property List (24-047) 0:54:52

Tara Riddle, Ombudsman, presented information about a vacant property located at 480 West
Center Street. The property, approximately 0.061 acres (2,600 square feet), became the city's
responsibility after the condemnation and demolition of the previous building on the site. Ms.
Riddle explained that the city placed a lien on the property to recover the demolition costs.
Historically, the site was rented as The Art Guild, a restaurant, before being deemed
uninhabitable.

Following demolition, the city maintained the lot, occasionally using it for temporary structures
or parking. Recently, the property was included in a Request for Proposal (RFP) related to the

area around City Hall. This effort resulted in a proposal from Provo Cinema LLC to develop a
community theater on the site. Before moving forward with any development agreements, the
city must formally designate the property as surplus, a necessary step to comply with city codes
requiring surplus property to be sold at appraised value.

During the discussion, council members expressed enthusiasm for the community theater
proposal, noting its potential cultural and communal benefits. They deliberated on the
significance of placing the property on the surplus list and emphasized the need for compliance
with city codes. Members also discussed the option for council oversight of sale terms prior to
finalization, though the preference appeared to allow the mayor and city administration to
proceed within the framework of the resolution and applicable codes.

One council member raised considerations regarding the theater’s alignment with downtown
aesthetics, municipal plans, and parking infrastructure. Quinn Peterson, a representative for
Downtown Provo, assured the council that the theater proposal supports longstanding goals to
enhance cultural venues downtown. He explained that peak theater attendance times would likely
avoid conflicts with regular office and retail operations, minimizing parking concerns.

The council reached a consensus to proceed with placing the property on the surplus property
list. Members expressed anticipation for further details about the theater project and excitement
about its potential contribution to the city’s cultural landscape.

5. An Ordinance Amending the Zone Map Classification of Real Property Located at 5610
N University Avenue from the Agricultural (A1.5) Zone to the Arbors on the Avenue
Project Redevelopment (PRO-A10) Zone - North Timpview Neighborhood
(PLRZ20230325) 2:02:51

Aaron Ardmore, Planning Supervisor, presented a proposal to amend the zoning classification of
property located at 5610 N University Avenue. The proposal aims to reclassify the land from the
Agricultural (A1.5) Zone to the Arbors on the Avenue Project Redevelopment (PRO-A10) Zone
to facilitate new development within the North Timpview Neighborhood.

Mr. Ardmore noted that the Planning Commission had recommended denial of the proposal due
to significant infrastructure deficiencies, particularly inadequate capacity in the existing sewer
system to support additional development. Gordon Haight, Public Works Director, provided
additional insights, emphasizing that resolving these constraints could require an estimated
financial investment of $5 to $8 million. He stated that staff are collaborating with developers to
explore funding mechanisms and joint efforts to address these challenges.

During the discussion, council members examined the potential traffic impacts of the proposed
development, including questions about future road improvements to integrate traffic flow with
existing patterns on Canyon Road and 5600 North. Concerns about increased congestion were
raised, with requests for clarification on whether traffic studies or road widening had been
incorporated into the development plan.

outlined the developer’s intention to include both owner-occupied and rental units. Council

9. A Discussion Regarding Tiered Water Rate Recommendations (24-045) 3:13:12

Gordon Haight, Public Works Director, and Keith Larson, Consultant, presented information on
proposed tiered water rate recommendations. They emphasized the need for increased revenue to
address infrastructure needs, inflation, and reduced water sales due to conservation efforts.

During the presentation, council members explored various options and discussed key issues
surrounding the tiered rate structure. Concerns were raised about the financial impact of potential
increases on residents and ensuring equitable treatment across different user groups. Staff cited
that a minimum 7% rate increase would align with the master plan, while a 15% increase was
strongly recommended to avoid deferring necessary maintenance, estimated to total $33.4
million over the next decade.

The discussion highlighted the impact of transitioning from seasonal rates to a tiered system.
Some council members supported a 3-tiered structure to encourage conservation, recognizing the
strain high water users place on infrastructure. This system would provide affordable indoor
water for residents with lower usage, though concerns were expressed about unintended
consequences for larger households or properties. Councilor Travis Hoban sought clarification
on how peak usage relates to service costs, ensuring the structure addresses the utility’s peak
demand expenses.

After discussion, Councilor Rachel Whipple made the following motion:




Provo City Municipal Council Work Meeting – April 30, 2024                                 Page 6 of 8
“I move that we vote on doing a 15% revenue increase. Just leave it at that, and then we would
explore different ways to get there.”

The motion was seconded by Councilor George Handley, who emphasized the necessity of
maintaining infrastructure to prevent costlier retroactive expenditures and regulatory risks. The
motion passed narrowly with a 4:3 vote:

       Approved 4:3 (Councilors Garrett, Christensen, and MacKay opposed).

In accordance with the council’s direction, staff will prepare budget proposals based on a 15%
revenue increase. These proposals will undergo further review and community engagement to
finalize implementation strategies.

department’s proactive maintenance approach and the policies guiding vehicle retirement. He
noted the challenges associated with adopting alternative fuel vehicles for certain types of
equipment, citing limitations in available technology and cost considerations.

The presentation provided council members with a comprehensive understanding of the Fleet
Department’s operational needs and the financial considerations shaping the FY2025 budget
proposal.
