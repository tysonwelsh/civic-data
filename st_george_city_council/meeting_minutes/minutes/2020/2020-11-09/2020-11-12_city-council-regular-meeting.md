PRESENTATION REGARDING THE FAMILY HEALTHCARE BOND:
     Administrative Services Director Trevor Coombs stated the Family Healthcare, a
     501c-3, is looking to have a non-interest-bearing bond, similar to what RamCo did a
     number of years ago.

      Randy Larsen with Gilmore & Bell explained that per state law, cities have the ability
      to be a conduit issuer for specific types of project projects. These are federal
      government programs that are codified under Utah law under the Utah Industrial
      Bond Act. The Family Healthcare is seeking financing approval for a 30,000 sq ft
      medical office building. They cannot obtain this tax-exempt financing it to have the
      City be a conduit issuer, which requires Council approval. He outlined the
      requirements for the Family Healthcare to apply for the financing. The City is not
      obligated to make any payments on the bond; state law actually prohibits the City
      from making a payment. He outlined the what the resolution authorizes and the
      next steps in the process.

      Mayor Pike provided a brief history of the Family Healthcare clinic.

      After being asked by Councilmember McArthur, Mr. Larsen stated there is no limit on
      how often the City can utilize this tool; however, there is a limit to manufacturing
      companies.

      After being asked by Councilmember Larkin, Mr. Larsen explained the applicant has
      secured property for the new building.

      Ryan Stevenson with Zions Bank added they are excited to be a part of this project
      and helping out the Family Healthcare organization fulfill a perfect role in the
      community.

      Mr. Coombs noted that he appreciates Zions Bank and the financial analysis done for
      Family Healthcare. He does not want them to enter into a bond obligation and not
      be able to make the payments.

AMEND NUMBER OF ALCOHOL BUSINESS LICENSES IN THE ARTS DISTRICT
OVERLAY ZONE/ORDINANCE:
     Consider approval of an ordinance amending the number of alcohol business
     licenses available in the Arts District Overlay Zone.

      Deputy City Attorney Paula Houston explained the City changed this ordinance a few
      months ago to allow one (1) Bar Establishment alcohol business license in the Arts
      District Overlay Zone for every 15,000 people living in the city. After concern being
      expressed by residents that lived near this area, evaluating the size of the area and
      the number of businesses in the area, it was determined that this area should be
      restricted by a set number of licenses instead of being based on population. The set
      number of Bar Establishment alcohol licenses being recommended is four (4).

      After being asked by Councilmember Larkin, Ms. Houston explained the state limits
      based on population; however, the City can set the parameter as they want.

      Councilmember Larkin commented she is okay with changing the number back to
      four (4), but if the four (4) do well, she would like an opportunity to readdress this
      issue at a later date. She acknowledged drinking is allowed for those over the age of
      21, and the state has put restrictions in place for bar establishments to be held
      accountable. She does not want to hamper the free market for people to responsibly
      participate in these types of establishments.

      Deputy City Attorney Paula Houston remined the Council, with the state’s limited
      number based on population, that limits the number of total licenses throughout the
      state. Because of this, local establishments can wait a long time to obtain their
      license from the state. She indicated that the draft ordinance states the limit is up to
      four(4) licenses, she will change that to say there four (4) licenses allowed.

      MOTION:
          A motion was made by Councilmember Hughes to approve the
                    ordinance amending the number of bar establishment licenses
                    available in the Arts District Overlay Zone to four (4).
      SECOND:
          The motion was seconded by Councilmember McArthur.
      VOTE:
          Mayor Pike called for a roll call vote, as follows:

                    Councilmember Hughes – aye
                    Councilmember Randall – aye
                    Councilmember Smethurst – aye
                    Councilmember McArthur – aye
                    Councilmember Larkin – aye

                    The vote was unanimous and the motion carried.
