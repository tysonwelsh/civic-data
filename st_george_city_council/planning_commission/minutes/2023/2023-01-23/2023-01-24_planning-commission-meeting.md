   2. PLANNED DEVELOPMENT AMENDMENT (PD-A) (Public Hearing) Legislative

      Consider a request to amend the PD-C (Planned Development Commercial) zone for the purpose of
      adding a new 1,680 square foot storage building to this site which is generally located at approximately
      2158 W. 1800 N. Please see map on back. The applicant is DJ Four LLC, c/o Don Ipson, and the
      representative is Jeff Mathis. The project name will be known as DJ4 Hobby Huts. Case No. 2023-
      PDA-001. (Staff – Carol Davidson)

      Carol Davidson presented the following:

      Carol Davidson – This is near Snow Canyon Pkwy, the PD-C is both properties, but we are not taking
      about the one to the west. They did provide a landscape plan most of it is existing landscape, but they
      will need to set this building 10 ft back and landscape that. There are 2 garage doors and 2-man doors
      on this there will be a light added above each. The exterior will match the existing buildings. We did
      receive Citizen comments. Staff does recommend approval.

      Chair Kemp – I noticed that the names were redacted off of the letters.

      Carol Davidson – That was done in error.

      Chair Kemp – When was this approved?

      Carol Davidson – It was started in 2007 and approved in 2008. It was designated as just a PD at the
      time. It was found to be a PD-C.

      Commissioner Kemp – When were the houses approved?

      Carol Davidson – They were approved after the original approval of this PD.

      Jeff Mathis – This is an approved use for the zone. Once I started hearing the comments I went out there
      to measure. The difference between our site and the other properties is 2.5 feet lower than the adjacent
      properties to the north.

      Chair Kemp – What are the dimensions of the building?

      Jeff Mathis – 24 x 72

      Commissioner Chapman – What is the height of the building?

      Jeff Mathis – It was 20 ft; the midline is 18 ft.

      Commissioner Chapman – Is the purpose for personal use or to rent it out?

      Jeff Mathis – Personal use only.

        Wallingford v. Moab City,
                        2020 UT App 12

“…municipalities may not contract around public hearing
requirements found in statute or ordinance.”

“The grants of power to municipalities make clear that cities
may not take action that is “repugnant to law,” id. § 10-8-
84(1), or that is “expressly prohibited by law,” id. § 10-9a-
102(2).”


   City of St. George
   Legal Dept.

            Wallingford v. Moab (cont)
One type of requirement that appears in statutes and ordinances, as
applicable in certain contexts, is the requirement that
municipalities conduct some of their business in
public, so that the citizens of the municipality may know how the
public’s business is being conducted. See, e.g., Hutchinson, 624 P.2d at
1121 (stating that “[t]he ultimate limitation upon potential abuses by
local governments is the people themselves,” whose “vigilance and
sound judgment” restricts and directs “all democratic governments”).

                Walker v. Tooele County
(Ruling & Order) 3rd District Court, Case No. 18301487
“The Court concludes that, as a matter of law, the Tooele County
Commission’s approval and execution of the Southside Agreement
was illegal contract zoning.”

“Tooele County (including members of the [County] Commission)
and Southside engaged in private discussions that produced the
Southside Agreement.” – they had been doing so for 2 years.

“due process demands a new trial when the appearance of unfairness
is so plain that we are left with the abiding impression that a
reasonable person would find the hearing unfair.”

   City of St. George
   Legal Dept.

          Summit County v. Hideout
       (Order on Amended Motion to Show Cause)
          4th District Court, Case no. 200500072

“. . .Summit County demonstrated a likelihood of success on the
merits that Hideout violated the Open and Public
Meetings Act (“OPMA”), Utah Code Ann. §§ 52-4-101, et seq.,
by conducting its deliberations about the July Pre-Annexation
Agreement, modifications of the July Pre-Annexation Agreement,
and the decision to adopt the July Pre-Annexation Agreement
outside of the public meeting where the July Pre-Annexation
Agreement was adopted.”


   City of St. George
   Legal Dept.

 Summit County v. Hideout (cont)

“The type of harm that the provisions of that act are intended to
guard against are the passage of resolutions or other decisions made
by a municipal body without notice to those affected by those
decisions. See Utah Code Ann. § 52-4-102 (“It is the intent of the
Legislature that the state, its agencies, and its political subdivisions
(a) take their actions openly; and (b) conduct their deliberations
openly.”)”
