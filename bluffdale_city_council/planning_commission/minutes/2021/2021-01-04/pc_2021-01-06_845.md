3.     PUBLIC HEARING, CONSIDERATION, AND VOTE on a Preliminary Subdivision
       Application for Sage Estates Phase 3 for 25 Lots in the R-1-10 Infill Overlay Zone
       located at approximately 14135 South Loumis Parkway, Stonefly Land Company,
       LLC, Applicant (Application 2020-45) Staff Presenter, Jennifer Robison.

Senior City Planner, Jennifer Robison, presented the staff report and described the location of the
subject property. It was rezoned in April 2020 because it was consistent with the development
pattern of the smrnunding subdivisions. Mrs. Robison identified the locations of the connections to
the subject property. She then reviewed the proposed subdivision plat. The Infill Overlay allows
for moderately different development standards from those specified in the R-1-10 zone. The R-1-
10 zone requires a minimum lot size of 10,000 square feet. The Infill Overlay allows for different
development standards that are in the current zone. Mrs. Robison confirmed that the Preliminary
Plat meets the Concept Plan that was presented with the zoning change. It still has the required two
street connections. In the future, Loumis Parkway will be widened, so the developer had to deed an
additional 10 to 12 feet to the City. Another problem for lot owners was the potential for trail
connections, so the proposed connection was removed from the proposed plat. The developer was
also working with the Engineering Department to address storm drains. Resolution of those
technical details was included in the conditions of approval.

With the needed changes, Mrs. Robison stated that the frontages for some of the lots for Phase 3
will be about 95 feet instead of ranging from 97 feet to 100 feet as stipulated in the Infill Overlay
requirements for the area. One of the conditions of approval is to have the developer work with the
City Council on a resolution to changes from the original proposal resulting from deeding land for
the widening of Loumis Parkway and thereby varying the size of the frontages from 94 to 100 feet,
and the removal of the currently proposed trail connection for placement to be determined at a
future date. Other than those two issues, Mrs. Robison stated the proposed plat meets all of the
zone and overlay requirements. Staff recommended approval of the preliminary plat.

Mrs. Robison next reviewed the requirements for the R-1-10 Infill Overlay. She noted that the
proposed development will be included with the Spring View Farms homeowners association. In
response to Commissioner Walston's question regarding the frontage requirements, Mrs. Robison
stated that some of the lots cannot meet the 97 to 100-foot requirement. The request for those lots
was to allow for 94-foot frontages. The number of lots (25) would remain unchanged from the
original Concept Plan. If the City Council does not approve the change, the developer would have
to return to the Planning Commission to amend the plat. The lot frontage change was the only
exception from the current standard being requested. Mrs. Robison confirmed that the subject
property is 7.97 acres in size and not 8.67 acres as stated in the staff report. The zoning was based
on 7.97 acres.

In response to a request for clarification by Chair Cragun, Mrs. Robison confirmed that the
proposed development meets all of the requirements of the current zone with the exception of the
frontages for some of the lots. Those exceptions would need to be approved by the City Council.

4.     PUBLIC HEARING, CONSIDERATION AND VOTE on a Preliminary Subdivision
       Application for Vista Meadows for 29 Lots in the R-1-43 Residential Zone located at
       approximately 14253 South 3450 West, Riverside Development, Applicant (Application
       2020-41) Staff Presenter, Ellen Oakman.

Associate City Planner, Ellen Oakman, presented the staff report and stated that the property is
zoned R-1-43 and has a minimum lot size requirement of one acre. She next reviewed the
preliminary plat, which includes 29 lots. There are two access points at 3450 West and 14400

lots within 1,000 feet. The proposed subdivision contains 29 lots but is 2,600 feet long, which

park strip design.

In response to Commissioner Flynn's request to confinn that 3450 West has been established as a

from 1987 in conjunction with a development that had taken place in that area. It said that a 50-foot

which specified the installation of curb and gutter. The original developer did not want to do that,

which was the responsibility of the property owners. Mrs. Robison stated that the documentation is
part of the public record. She added that the City has never called for the curb and gutter to be
installed.

Mr. Sheeran confirmed that the Planning Commission is the Land Use Authority for preliminary
subdivision plat approval, which is an administrative decision, and not a legislative decision. If the
application meets all of the City Code criteria, the Land Use Authority has the legal obligation to
approve it, even if the deciding body does support the proposal. This is not a conditional use
application in which the Planning Commission would have discretionary authority to mitigate safety
concerns and other detrimental effects.

In response to C01mnissioner Walston's question regarding the viability of the crash gate
suggestion, Mr. Sheeran stated that the cun-ent City Code does not include any criteria regarding
when crash gates should or could go in. He cautioned the Planning Commission against making a
crash gate a condition of approval because it would likely violate City Code, which requires two
access points for the proposal under consideration. As a result, having a crash gate and only one
entrance would be a violation of City Code. Mr. Fazio concun-ed with Mr. Sheeran and stated that a
public right-of-way would have to be opened, per State Statute.

In response to Chair Cragun's question as to why crash gates exist in other parts of Bluffdale,
Commissioner Loumis stated that in some instances, the road is a split highway, which creates two
access points. Mr. Fazio did not think that was a valid argument or explanation because a divided
highway is still considered one right-of-way. He did not know why the crash gate was allowed at
Spring View Farms. The cun-ent City Code includes the number of houses and length of the road as
the criteria for whether one or two access points are required.

Gary Barlow thanked Mr. Horsley for routing all construction traffic to 14400 South. As a

                               Wednesday, January 6, 2021

James Dunn served on the City Council 20 years ago when the City dealt with its growing pains.
There was a lot of anger and hostility over the development of Bangelier Highway and Redwood
Road. At that time, 3450 West was deemed a private lane, which meant that the City provided no
services for the street.

Rachel Tatum expressed appreciation for Mr. Horsley's commitment to have the construction traffic
use 14400 South. She referenced the comment about the residents potentially having to install curb,
gutter, and sidewalk themselves, which would be a huge financial burden. She stated that the
cmTent residents never signed such a letter.

Mr. Sheeran explained that the documents referenced by Mrs. Robison were recently found in the
City's archives. Whether the City would have a legal right to impose that commitment upon the
current residents would be very questionable. If the curb, gutter, and sidewalk were to be installed,
a determination would have to be made as to who would bear the cost. Since 3450 West is not
owned by the developer of the proposed subdivision, the City could not legally obligate the
developer to install the curb, gutter, and sidewalk.

Dixie Kartchner had several concerns. She stated that she and her husband were the original
owners on 3450 West and they spent a great deal of money on the road. The sign was blue for some
time, indicating that it was a private street. The sign is now green, identifying it as a public street.
The City has maintained the road for a few years, but it is worn out. Since it is now designated as a
public street, she asked if the residents will be required to repave it. Mr. Fazio responded that the
City is planning to repair the road in 2024. That work will be done at the City's expense.

James Dunn stated that on Pheasant Hollow Lane they do not have curb, gutter, or sidewalk, and the
residents signed a Delay Agreement indicating that the residents would have to install curb, gutter,
and sidewalk at their own expense if required by the City. He stated that the Delay Agreement
should be carefully examined since it is not binding on the current residents if it is not on the public
record. There may also be a statute of limitation that would have rendered the Delay Agreement
null and void.

With regard to a question raised by Ms. Kartchner about irrigation, Mr. Horsley offered to revisit
that issue with her privately.

There were no further public comments. Chair Cragun closed the public hearing.

Commissioner Flynn suggested possibly tabling the item to address outstanding issues.

Commissioner Walston state that because it is a preliminary plat, it would have to meet all other
requirements and conditions. If the applicant meets all of the zoning requirements, the Planning
Commission has a legal obligation to grant approval.

Commissioner Flynn referenced the crash gate and asked if it would require a revision to City Code.
Mr. Sheeran stated that the applicant is already vested in the current City Code with the submission
of the application and would have to comply with the current standards.

Chair Cragun had considered tabling the matter but took into account the fact that if the applicant
has met all of the City Code requirements, the Platming Commission would be legally obligated to
approve the preliminary subdivision application. She urged the developer and residents to meet
while the process is underway.

Commissioner Luker stated that the crash gate idea is not viable because of the current City Code.
He also believed that the Planning Commission could not legally deny the application since the
applicant has met all of the requirements. He would like to see the developer and residents find a
way to move forward to mitigate traffic on 3450 West.

Debbie Cragun moved to approve the Vista Meadows Subdivision Preliminary Plat
Application 2020-41 subject to the following:

Conditions:

       1.     All requirements of the City Codes and adopted ordinances are adhered to for
              the application.

       2.     The plat complies with the Bluffdale City Engineering Standards and
              Specifications and recommendations by the City Engineer and Public Works
              Department for all relevant construction and plat drawings.

       3.     All construction drawings shall be approved and stamped by the City Engineer
              prior to any construction of improvements.

       4.     Prior to approval of the Final Plat Application by the Land Use Authority, final
              construction drawings for storm drain and driveway access to Vista Meadows
              Drive and Marigold Lane shall be approved by the City Engineer.

       5.     The applicant shall resolve all Engineering Department subdivision review
              comments prior to construction.

       6.     All construction drawings shall be approved and stamped by the City Engineer
              prior to any construction of improvements.

       7.     The applicant shall provide approval from South Valley Sewer District for
              sewer service to this development prior to construction.

       8.     The applicant shall provide access to the subdivision that meets City standards
              and specifications, in the name of the City, and for public use, for the
              connection to 14400 South, prior to construction.

       9.     The applicant shall provide a project Geotechnical Report that meets City of
              Bluffdale standards and specifications prior to construction.
