    1.           WELCOME, INTRODUCTION AND PRELIMINARY MATTERS

      Vice-Chair Anna Barbieri called the meeting to order at 6:30 p.m. and welcomed those in

         1.2     Mayor’s Report

      Mayor Kristie Overson explained that a ribbon-cutting had recently been held for Jersey
Mike’s Subs. She was excited to support the business and welcome them to Taylorsville.
Additionally, a ribbon was cut at Summit Vista’s Meadow Peak skilled nursing facility. The City
had been delighted to help celebrate the remarkable success of Summit Vista, the staff of which
had been very complimentary of city employees who aided them in the planning, permitting, and
building process.

The Utah League of Cities and Town’s mid-year conference was fast approaching. ULCT had
requested a 10-20 second video spotlighting their member communities. The Mayor had assigned

that task to Communications Director Kim Horiuchi and the city’s video (along with those from
other cities and towns) would be shown at the league conference.

The Council had the opportunity to tour the Mid-Valley Performing Arts Center with former
elected officials. Everyone had played a part in making the Center a reality. She appreciated the
City’s partnership with Salt Lake County on this significant project.

Mayor Overson also expressed gratitude for the vision and the planning of those at the city’s helm
25 years ago when Taylorsville initially incorporated. The City was recently presented with a
painting donated by the Namba family which showed the Namba Farm, upon which the city
campus had been built. Previous elected leaders chose the location because it was very close to
the geographic center of the city. The Taylorsville spirit reached back 25 years and would continue
moving forward.

The Mayor thanked the Council for participating in Let’s Talk Taylorsville the previous week.
She said there had been a lively discussion and she hoped that the participating citizens had left
the meeting feeling heard. It was a great opportunity for city leaders to listen and learn.

Unified Fire Chief Peterson had recently met with Scott Harrington and the Mayor regarding a
very sophisticated and fascinating tool to help determine the closest unit response in the Salt Lake
Valley. The Chief had given them an in depth look at fire response in Taylorsville and throughout
the valley. Mayor Overson explained what a great appreciation she had for the city’s relationship
with UFA and the work that agency did daily. She also took a moment to thank Captain Rich for
teaching CPR to members of the Youth Council earlier in the day.

               1.21 Child Abuse Awareness Proclamation
      Mayor Overson went on to proclaim April as Child Abuse Prevention month. She expressed
thanks to the Exchange Club, the Youth Council, and the Unified Police Explorers who had
provided and planted pinwheels outside city hall in honor of child abuse victims. The Mayor
read the proclamation into the record and presented a copy to Executive Director Jocelyn De La
Rosa who accepted the proclamation on behalf of the Center.

        1.3      Citizen Comments

       Vice Chair Barbieri invited Doug Anderson to step forward. Mr. Anderson explained that
he had been caring for the trees on Centennial Parkway for several years. He explained that city
code required a clearance of 13.5’ above the roadway but there were problems with the trees on
Taylorsville Blvd in that they were not being maintained. He had recently taken it upon himself
at age 82 to begin pruning them. However, he had recently been asked not to. He assured the city
council that he would not harm the trees and asked that if he were not allowed to attend to them,
that someone else be assigned to do so. Mayor Overson explained that the City would be delighted
for him to maintain any of the trees for as long as he felt able to. He was asked to refrain from
climbing a ladder, however.

    3. REPORTS

        3.1      West Valley Animal Services – Maranda Weathermon

      Ms. Weathermon went over some basic numbers about the amazing work being done at the
animal shelter. Digital story time had been created which was geared toward children aged 3-7
years old. Story time was held in English one week followed by a Spanish version the following
week for Latinx members of the community.


A class designed to educate the public about the problems associated with dogs running at large
was now live and doing great things for both Taylorsville and West Valley. Fines were waived if
first time offenders took the class. Other types of public education opportunities were in the
planning stages.

     Ms. Weathermon then presented statistical data about the field services, return-to-owner, and
trap-neuter-return programs. She also pointed out that the shelter’s length of stay was almost down
to the national average which was a huge improvement.

Vice Chair Barbieri and Councilmember Burgess expressed appreciation to Ms. Weathermon for
her enthusiastic work and the work of her staff.

        3.2 Taylorsville Police Department Update – Jay Ziolkowski

      Mr. Ziolkowski explained that the transition team was on schedule. The sergeants had all
been hired and were conducting background checks. The City had received over 100 applications
for the front-line officer positions. By the end of the current week, sixty interviews would have
been conducted, twenty job offers would be extended and the interview and hiring process would
continue to move forward. A job posting for Evidence Manager would soon go out as well.

Regarding the TVPD charter, it was also moving ahead as scheduled. The transition team was
looking into a resiliency program (behavior, health, wellness) for officers and addressing
construction needs for the office space downstairs. Negotiations with West Valley City were
continuing regarding SWAT services and a new policy index had been forwarded to the council
just that evening. He suspected that the team would be ready to present the final version of police
policies to the council for formal approval near the end of April.

Mr. Ziolkowski and the transition team were looking for long-term storage space for vehicles—
police vehicles as well as vehicles held for evidentiary reasons. They were also addressing the
IT needs of the department (both hardware and software) and he expressed appreciation to Chief
Anjeweirden and other UPD staff who had moved Taylorsville up in the transition to the new
Computer Aided Dispatch system.

        3.3 Unified Fire Authority Report – Captain Richard Rich

     Captain Rich summarized call volume, call type and other data from the fourth quarter of
2020 as well as the first quarter of 2021. Call volume was beginning to trend upward, and units
were well within the national average of turn out and response times.

     Construction on some new fire stations was underway. The UFA was using a program from
Darkhorse Diagnostics which used information from city planning such as growth projections to
estimate what the City’s emergency response needs might be like 30 years down the road,
including what the best locations for future fire stations might be.

There had been quite a few retirements in December followed by promotions and new hires. A
class of 33 was currently in recruit school and should be on shift as of mid-June. A battalion chief
boot camp and exam were taking place and ten individuals were going through that process. Part
of the boot camp was training about “compassionate leadership” and how to interact with “those
who aren’t like you.” Forty UFA employees went through that presentation and because of that,
he felt they would make much better leaders in the future.

Captain Rich said that his department was no longer seeing the number of COVID calls that it had
been seeing for a while. 90% of the staff had been fully vaccinated.

Councilmember Burgess expressed appreciation to Captain Rich, particularly regarding his CPR

would someday save a life.

Captain Rich wrapped up his report by pointing out that drought conditions promised to create a
very challenging fire season. The wildland team was in the process of transitioning to a “Hot
Shot” crew, which typically lead to more experienced firefighters hired by the department.

        7.1 Resolution No. 21-13 – A Resolution of the City of Taylorsville consenting to the
            Reorganization of the Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District as a Local
            District - Tracy Cowdell

      Ms. Heather Grover of Cowdell & Woolley, PC provided just a brief synopsis of this agenda
item since the Council and public had been given information on the topic during the March 17
city council meeting. She explained that WFWRD was currently a Special Service District and
sought to become a Local District which would make it independent from Salt Lake County.
Approving Resolution No. 21-13 would give consent to ratify reorganization of WFWRD if it were
able to successfully negotiate the reorganization with Salt Lake County and the other member
agencies.
