yearly update on Bluffdale's progress with moderate-income housing progress. As part of the
reporting, a Moderate-Income Housing element was adopted into the General Plan in 2022. Bluffdale

was also required to submit three strategies it is utilizing to further housing affordability in the City.
One strategy the Council adopted was to utilize a set-aside from the Redevelopment Agency("RDA")
to incentivize moderate-income housing development. The City also entered into an Interlocal
Agreement with the Salt Lake County Housing Trust Fund, which requires Bluffdale to implement
an Accessory Dwelling Unit("ADU")Toolkit.


Ms.Tubbs introduced Consultant Heidi Neilsen from FFKR Architects and Josh Narvez from the Salt

Lake County Housing Trust Fund. Ms. Neilsen reported that her team has met with City Staff and
Mr. Narvez monthly since June to develop the Toolkit and Checklist. One oftheir guiding principles
was to ensure clarity for residents and City Staffby being consistent with the language already present
in City resources. They also needed to include resources for new landlords so they could easily
understand the rules and regulations.


In creating the Toolkit, they looked at ADU resources provided by other cities in the Intermountain
West and California. In doing so, they discovered that the initial outline of resources Bluffdale
included in its Request for Proposals("RFP") aligns with what other cities are doing. They built on
Bluffdale's existing Frequently Asked Questions and refined the other information.


The draft ADU Toolkit was reviewed both internally and extemally to ensure that no steps were
missed. The review group included residents who have already gone through the ADU process in
Bluffdale. Those residents provided feedback on items to clarify and additional details to include, all
of which were reflected in the most current draft.


The ADU Toolkit was in the final review process and is expected to launch in January 2025. A multi-
prong community outreach and education program was planned,including a web banner on the City's
website, social media announcements, promotion in the City newsletter, and potentially designating
January as Attainable Housing Month. Mr. Narvez also arranged to feature Bluffdale's ADU Toolkit
in the County newsletter. In response to a question from Council Member Lord, Ms.Neilsen clarified
that the Toolkit and Checklist are specific to Bluffdale but an announcement will be included in the
County newsletter to reach a broader audience.

Council Member Crockett referred to the approval process outlined in the draft and noted that it gives
a broad overview but it does not provide detailed information like how difficult it is to obtain a
Building Permit or what information needs to be provided with the application. She suggested
including more specific information about the process and potentially providing a sample application.
Ms. Tubbs clarified that the ADU Toolkit was created with longevity in mind. The approval process
may change and become simpler over time, particularly if the legislature enacts legislation to that
effect, and as written the current draft would not need to be updated to refiect those changes.

Ms. Tubbs reported that checklists were developed for both Internal and External ADUs and detailed
requirements to obtain approval and include links to the application form. The application form also
has a checklist of items that are required with the submittal. Two separate permits are required for
ADUs including a Building Permit for construction and a Planning Permit for administrative Land
Use approval. The relevant applications and submittal checklists were then reviewed.

It was noted that page 9 ofthe draft includes a QR code and link to the Building Permit application,
but not the ADU application. The ADU application is included in two other areas of the draft but
would also be added to page 9.


Ms. Tubbs clarified that the ADU could be an addition, a finished basement, or an External ADU.
The applicant would need to know the type of construction required in order to determine the
appropriate submittal requirements. Mayor Pro Tempore Aston suggested updating the website to
include definitions ofthe two types ofADUs.

Council Member Wilding thanked Ms. Neilsen for her efforts in making the City Code easily
understandable for residents and asked if there were areas in the current Code that are unique to
Bluffdale. Ms. Neilsen stated that one thing that is unique to Bluffdale is its lot sizes, which allow
for fairly large detached ADUs in relation to the size of the home. Bluffdale also does not allow
separate addressing or metering. Some cities require the architectural details of a detached ADU to
match the primary dwelling, but Bluffdale does not. It does require attached ADUs to blend with the
main dwelling, which is a consistent requirement across most cities. Parking requirements are always
city-specific.

In response to a question raised by Mayor Pro Tempore Aston, Ms. Tubbs reported that whether it
would be legal to require specific design standards for an ADU would depend on the development
stage ofthe primary home. For example,State statute prohibits cities from regulating design standards
for single-family homes. If someone proposes an internal ADU, the City cannot require specific
design standards. However, it does not currently prohibit appljdng architectural design standards to
detached buildings. Ifthere is an existing home and the owner wants to convert an existing detached
building to an ADU or build a new one, the City could include those requirements. An argument
against making such requirements would be,for example,ifa home was built in the 1950s with vinyl
siding and they want to build a brick or stucco ADU. In that case, the City may not want to require
them to instead make it look like the original home and install vinyl siding.

In response to a question from Council Member Wilding, Ms. Tubbs reported that the City elected to
include very limited design components in the most recent update to ADU requirements. Modular
and manufactured homes fall under a different Building Code, so adding architectural requirements
to those types of homes would require the application to go through an additional customization
process. The Division of Workforce Services, which oversees moderate-income housing reporting,
recommends limiting the application of design standards to ADUs because such requirements often
result in an additional cost, which can dissuade owners from building ADUs.


Council Member Lord stated that a new Bluffdale resident is in the business ofmodular homes. There

is a real cost advantage in modular homes, and that resident indicated that it is a quality product for a
much lower cost than a traditional home. He asked if they were allowed to regulate those types of
homes. Ms. Tubbs stated that both modular and manufactured dwellings are currently allowed in
Bluffdale. If the City wanted to prohibit or further regulate those types of homes. Staff would need
to research the State Code to ensure that it is allowed to do so.


In response to a question from Council Member Wilding, Ms. Tubbs reported that one-quarter of all
ADUs approved since Bluffdale began allowing them were approved after the Council adopted the
most recent ADU Ordinance. That number does not include ADUs that were established outside the

Ms. Tubbs presented the Staff Report and indicated that on July 16, 2024, the City Council directed
Staff to prepare amendments to the fencing height standards in Bluffdale Code Section 11.160.140.
The proposed modifications were reviewed by the Planning Commission at an October 2,2024,public
hearing, and the Commission forwarded a unanimous recommendation for approval to the Council.

Currently, the fencing code govems both sight-obscuring and non-sight-obscuring fencing. The
maximum fence height in a front setback is 42 inches (3.5 feet) for sight-obscuring and 60 inches(5
feet) for non-sight-obscuring fences. The unique circumstance that inspired the change was a
preexisting home on a lot that was subsequently subdivided. As a result, the lot orientation changed
fi-om the side to the fi*ont facing the new right-of-way. The proposed Text Amendment would allow
an existing home to maintain the side-yard designation so long as it predates the subdivision and has
gone through the necessary approval processes.

The Text Amendment would add the following to Bluffdale City Code Section 11.160.140.1.1:

         2.     Notwithstanding the foregoing, a parcel or lot that has been determined to have
                changed front yard orientation to a newly created right-of-way through an approved
                subdivision plat AND which had an existing dwelling at the time of subdivision may

9.      Staff Reports. Additional Council Discussion, and Calendaring Items.

Council Member Wilding requested more details on the Moderate-Income Housing Report. City
Manager, Mark Reid stated that a yearly report is required by the State. It is a challenging report, and
last year a lot ofcities did not comply in a manner that the State approved of. In its first year, Bluffdale
received a notice of acceptance followed by a letter of reprimand. Staff then brought the report into
compliance. This is the second year the report has been required, and the City must add new goals
each year.


Ms.Tubbs provided an overview ofthe Moderate-Income Housing Report and indicated that it is now
required to be filed annually. The City has received its compliance letter for 2024, which is available
with the report and relevant General Plan section on the Planning Department page at Bluffdale.gov.

Bluffdale is required to provide analysis on at least three housing menu items. Cities with fixed
guideway transit like a TRAX line or FrontRunner station are required to report on five items,so once
the FrontRunner station is open in Bluffdale that requirement will increase. The report details the
City's progress on each of its three menu items, including the number of entitled housing units, the
effect of policy decisions or procedural changes on housing development within the City, and things
they have either noticed internally or have been communicated to them by residents or applicants as
barriers to the development of more affordable housing. The most common complaint received by
Bluffdale and neighboring cities is in regard to the overall cost of construction, both materials and
labor costs. Because there is little municipalities can do to address that issue, the State provided the
additional menu items as options for cities to mitigate the problem.


City Staffhas considered providing the Council with more frequent updates on the Moderate-Income
Housing Plan so they can respond more quickly ifa menu item is not as effective as it needs to be for
the upcoming reporting year. Council Member Wilding commented that the Council ultimately
controls the success ofthe process, but their decisions can often take six months ofplanning,so more
frequent updates would be helpful.
