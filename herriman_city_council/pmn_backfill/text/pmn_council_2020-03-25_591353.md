     3.3. State of the City - Brett Wood, City Manager
Mayor Pro Tempore Smith said the past few weeks had been interesting. Not only were they facing the rapid
spread of COVID-19, they also experienced an earthquake last week. They were very fortunate to live in a
community that had always taken emergency preparedness very seriously, and the City had many plans in place
to handle these situations. They already had networks in place to check on neighbors and friends. The City
declared a State of Emergency on March 19, 2020. This declaration positioned the City to receive any State or
Federal funds to help with the aftermath of this pandemic. Staff had done a great job of keeping the City
moving. He stated the health and safety of Herriman residents had been and always would be the first priority
of the City. He asked all members of the community to heed the directions of the CDC and the Health
Department to help slow the spread of disease. This included washing hands frequently, avoiding contact with
those that were sick, staying at home as much as possible, and cleaning surfaces consistently. Staff had
witnessed an increase in residents going outside and using park facilities. While it was nice to see people
outside, it came with potential challenges. The City was doing its best to keep facilities clean, but they would
likely have to limit public access sin the future. The increased use of trails had also brought parking concerns
at trailheads.

City Manager Brett Wood provided an update to the Council and the public regarding how staff was preparing
for and responding to the pandemic. Prior to the announcement from the State about closures, Herriman City
staff was already watching the spread of COVID-19. They took proactive measures immediately, which
included taking inventory and ordering supplies, and setting up the Incident Command Structure. Once the
announcement was made by the State, they immediately started closing certain parts of City Hall, talking about
personnel and who might be high risk, cancelling all media events and meetings. They discussed how to get
employees working at home and switching to teleconference meetings. When the earthquake hit, the Incident
Command Structure knew what to do and immediately started checking infrastructure and services throughout
the City. For everyday functions, they had divided their workforce in half, with people working in shifts. This
protocol would continue until they had the assurance that there was no longer a virus threat. He specifically
thanked the IT Department for helping them transition to remote working and electronic meetings.

Police Chief Troy Carr said public safety was a critical City service. Their prime directive was to make sure
they could respond to 911 calls. It was crucial they kept their law officers from getting sick. This is why they
separated the work force, and their shifts were such that the crews were never in the building at the same time.
The Police Department was closed to the public, including family members of the officers, and they were
consistently decontaminating the building. It was key for the community to know the government was not
shut down. They needed to know that the Police Department was fully operational. They were asking people
if they were able and willing to accept contact by phone for non-priority calls to keep the officer’s contact with
the public low. There had been an increase in domestic violence over the past few weeks. One of his concerns

was having to investigate deaths in the future, as the virus progressed. Death investigations were a big
consumer of both time and equipment, and the officers would be more exposed to the virus.

Assistant City Manager Wendy Thomas reported on the status of City parks. The department was in contact
with other cities and the County to coordinate the potential closure of parks and amenities. The current plan
was to close down restroom facilities, water fountains, the skate park, basketball courts, and playgrounds. They
wouldn’t be using caution tape at their facilities, but they would be putting up signage and trusted that the
residents would follow the rules. She was concerned about trails and trailheads, and hoped that it would not
come to a point where those amenities were also closed. The big summer events had not been cancelled yet.

Councilmember Ohrn questioned how the City would patrol or enforce the closure of parks. Chief Carr said
that he was nervous about restricting people’s freedoms. He hoped they wouldn’t come to a point where the
police department was patrolling the streets making sure people were inside. He said education was key to
enforcing the closure of public amenities. He worried about exposing officers to the virus for low-priority
contacts, such as people playing on closed playgrounds. He wanted to use law enforcement sparingly in that
regard. He hoped people would regulate themselves so that law enforcement didn’t have to.

Public Works Director Justun Edwards spoke about the infrastructure of the City. Public Works was also
working with split crews to perform essential functions, but projects that required a larger crew had been
pushed off. The virus would not affect the water system at all. He described the protocol response to the
earthquake and he assured the Council the water system was safe.

Manager Wood said staff was not just thinking about today, but they were planning for long-term effects. They
were planning for the worst and hoping for the best. Staff was working on the budget, although they’ve had
adjust to consider the potential effects of the closures, including sales tax and the housing market.

Assistant City Manager Tami Moody reported the Economic Development Team had been actively working
with local businesses so they could continue to let the public know which businesses were open and what types
of services they offered. Many businesses had cut back but were still offering curb-side pickup and other
options. Economic Development had also been meeting with potential new businesses coming in the City.
The State and Federal Governments were putting together programs that would assist businesses that had
financial hardship due to this event.

Manager Wood was confident they had the right team in place to work through these difficult times.
