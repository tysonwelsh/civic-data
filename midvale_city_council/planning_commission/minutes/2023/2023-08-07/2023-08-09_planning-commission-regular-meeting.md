GENERAL SESSION

Chair Liedtke called the Planning & Zoning Commission meeting to order at 6:00 p.m. He
explained how the meeting would proceed. First, the Planning Department would brief the
Commission; then the applicant would speak to the Commission; the item will be opened to the
public for their comments on public hearing agenda items; the Midvale Deputy City Attorney
will then brief the Commission on their role on the given item; and then the Commission would
deliberate and decide.

1.   Dan Jeppson requests a Conditional Use Permit approval for a Flag Lot located at
     220 E Greenwood Ave in the Single-Family Residential Zone (SF-1).

      The applicant requests conditional use permit approval to allow for a Flag Lot located at
      220 E Greenwood Ave in the Single Family Residential-1 (SF-1) zone.

      This proposal has been reviewed by Planning Staff, the City Engineer, and the Unified
      Fire Authority for compliance with the respective guidelines, policies, standards, and
      codes. Staff finds the proposal complies with requirements outlined in Midvale City
      Municipal Code for minor subdivisions (16.04.050) and the lot development standards of

      Public notice has been sent to property owners within 500 feet of the subject parcel. No
      objections have been received as of the writing of this report.

3.      (b) Nathan Brockbank requests a Small-Scale Master Plan for a residential
        development consisting of 9 units located at 7240/7260 S 525 E in the Single-Family
        Residential Duplex Overlay Zone (SF-1 DO).

       Nathan Brockbank requests a Small-Scale Master Plan for a town home development
       that.
       consists of nine units grouped into two triplexes, one duplex and a single-family lot for
       the existing home at 7260 S 525 E. The home located at 7240 S 525 E will be replaced by
       the northeastern triplex in the development.

       Public notice has been sent to property owners within 500 feet of the subject parcel;
       additionally, a sign was placed on the property and will remain until the city council
       approves the plat. At the time of this writing no public comment has been received.

       Staff recommends approval.


                                              ***

APPLICANT:

Paul Wattson, Project Engineer, here to represent Mr. Nathan Brockbank. Mr. Wattson does
clarify that the open space in the back area of the plans will be open green space. There will be
an HOA to maintain all landscaping in that public area.

                                              ***


PUBLIC HEARING:

Chair Liedtke opened the public hearing.

Jeff Anderson, family member of nearby neighbors to the development site, is mainly concerned
with parking. Entryways for all units being on 525 E is a huge concern. The road is not well enough
maintained as it is, and the family is concerned in the winter there will not be easy access with
parking on both sides of the street. Another concern is the boundary wall being removed if another
structure is built.

Matt McNichols, southern neighbor, is concerned with the restrictions for grading on the property
then the height restrictions being added on top of that. The privacy of the residents nearby will be
disrupted.

Mark Snow, builder on the project, addresses the cinder block wall mentioned in a previous public
comment by Mr. Anderson. The cinder block wall will remain. As for the grading question
mentioned by Mr. McNichols, it will be continuous with his lot. No extra height will be added,

PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION AUGUST 9, 2023.
and all height restrictions will be met.

Mr. Davis, a local resident, addresses parking regulations and the concerns about the Utah State
legislation being applied so liberally to all municipalities. Legislation is not one-size fits all. The
quality of the development and neighborhood will be directly related to how parking issues are
resolved.

Becca Labrum, representing the residents of the condominiums across the street. The residents are
all concerned about parking and street safety. The park access is nearby, and the children cross the
street nearby the blind driveways. Storage needs to be maintained so that cars fit in their driveways.

Chair Liedtke recommends applying for the Traffic Control Device for the Engineering
Department to review.

Nate Brockbank, the applicant, and developer of the project, expresses the willingness to add four
more parking spaces if the code allows it.

Mr. Watts, Project Engineer, explains the purpose of the fill dirt to allow for sewer to function.

Mr. Brockbank, the applicant, reassures residents that parking requirements for the project are met.
The willingness to add the four additional parking spaces is clear. The addition of these spaces will
depend on the interpretation of the city code.

Mr. Wattson, Project Engineer, proposes a variance on the North side, helping to leave only 3 of
the driveways with 16 feet compared to the remaining driveways being 20 ft, which can fit two
vehicles.

                                               ***


MOTION:        Vice Chair Erickson MOVED to close the public hearing. The motion was
               SECONDED by Commissioner Anderson. Chair Liedtke called for a voice
               vote. The motion was passed unanimously with all voting in favor.


                                                 ***

DISCUSSION:

Mr. McKnight, Deputy City Attorney, clarifies that this is an administrative decision based on the
findings. If it suits the code, it must be approved.

Chair Liedtke clarifies that all 6 criteria/requirements must be met to receive the incentive. Based
on the ordinance, Mr. Knobloch verifies that is the case. Chair Liedtke mentions that the trail is
not in place yet and providing incentives before construction is complete is somewhat of a worry
for him.



PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION AUGUST 9, 2023.
Vice Chair Erickson and Chair Liedtke re-examine the plans and notice that the plans propose
triplexes which are not included in the Duplex-Overlay Zone. This is not compliant with the
changes that Midvale City Council has made.

Planning Commission asks for Legal guidance for how to proceed with the process now that they
have concluded that it does not fit the zoning ordinances.

Mr. McKnight, Deputy City Attorney, clarifies that all the findings will need to be drawn to
attention in order to deny the motion.

Adam Olsen, Community Development Director, suggests that denial of the project and
reapplication is the simplest way to move forward at this point.


                                              ***


MOTION: Vice Chair Erickson MOVED that we deny the Small-Scale Master Plan for a
        residential development consisting of nine units located at 7240/7260 S 525 E in
        the Single-Family Residential Duplex Overlay Zone (SF-1 DO) with the
        following findings: (1) Does not comply with required trail connection and (2)
        Does not adhere to the Duplex Overlay requirements and (3) Does not meet
        applicable codes and or ordinances. The motion was SECONDED by
        Commissioner Anderson. Chair Liedtke called for a roll call vote. The vote was
        as follows:

                     Chair Liedtke                         Yes
                     Vice Chair Erickson                   Yes
                     Commissioner Anderson                 Yes

                     Commissioner Tippetts                 Yes


              The motion passes unanimously.
