       Approximately 6.7 Acres, LH Perry Investments, LLC, Applicants (Application 2021-
       31)- Staff Presenter, Grant Crowell.

City Planner/Economic Development Coordinator, Grant Crowell, presented the staff report and
stated that the request is for a Mixed-Use Special District Zone at the southwest comer of Porter
Rockwell Boulevard and 14600 South. He identified the location of the subject property, which has
been owned by LH Perry Investments, LLC, for approximately seven or eight years. It is to the north
of the Marketplace neighborhood and across from the Rockwell Ridge Business Park.

The ctment General Plan designates the property as Mixed Use. The current zoning is also Mixed
Use, which allows for a variety of development options and densities. The Land Use Plan that was
included with the Development Agreement, which was created in 2014 labeled the subject property
as Light Industrial. Mr. Crowell noted that the proposed plan for the old prison site includes the
parcel near the subject property designated as Mixed Use as well. Mr. Crowell provided a historical
review of the 518 acres that were rezoned to Mixed Use for the Independence project in 2004. In
2007, the plan was reviewed and approved and has subsequently undergone substantial development
and rezoning for a wide variety of uses.

Mr. Crowell reported that to apply for a Special District (SD-X) Zone, the applicant must first
participate in a Work Session with the City Council to discuss the proposal and rationale for the SD-X
zone. That meeting took place on January 13, 2021 . Traffic, design, the number of units, and a host
of other topics were discussed at that meeting. The applicant took that feedback and prepared a
submittal to the City. The application must include deta,il on what the applicant plans to do. It was
clarified that it is not a site plan proposal at this point. The proposal from the applicant would include
a 236-unit stacked apartment complex that would include about four stories of residential units on top
of a two-level parking garage as well as potential pad sites for commercial development. Private
amenities would be included in the apartment project. The landscaping would also be presented for
approval.

It was noted that the transportation network will generate substantial feedback and concern. There
are two state highways near the subject property; therefore, the Utah Department of Transportation
("UDOT") requirements will be applied for the access points to the subject property, including traffic
light locations. The proposed parking stalls on the subject property comply with City Code. Parking
for the retail units needs to be reviewed as part of the site plan review. Mr. Crowell noted that a

Preliminary Traffic Impact Study had been conducted for the proposed project. Engineers who

Commissioner Flynn asked about the expectation of traffic flow for Rising Star Way. Mr. Crowell
stated that ultimately everyone will have to use the traffic light to go notih. Since traffic was expected

Ryan Hales from Hales Engineering gave his address as 1220 North 500 West, in Lehi, and described
how the traffic study was conducted. The first task was to study the traffic volume under the current
conditions to establish a baseline. From there, the volumes would be adjusted based on the current
conditions, including the seasonality of the traffic and the impact of COVID on traffic. The traffic
information was obtained from UDOT, including the traffic signal timing at 14600 South and Porter
Rockwell Boulevard after which a simulation model is created. The evaluation includes the traffic
volume at the intersection before the project is developed and evaluated once the project is complete.
Rising Star Way was also included in the study. Mr. Hales noted that UDOT is looking at the
intersection of Rising Star Way and Porter Rockwell Boulevard as a potential location for a traffic
signal. Mr. Hales explained the various warrants included in the UDOT manual for justifying a traffic
signal. The peak volume warrant for that intersection would be met sometime between 2021 and
2026. The real impact on the traffic volume signal warrant will occur when Porter Rockwell
Boulevard opens to the south. Current studies were being conducted to determine future locations of
traffic signals along Porter Rockwell Boulevard.

Mr. Hales acknowledged that the proposed project will bring additional traffic into the area. The key
is how the additional traffic is handled. UDOT has determined that Porter Rockwell Boulevard will
eventually be labeled as a Category 5 roadway, which will include criteria for where intersections and
access points can be placed and the mode of entry, such as right-in and right-out at the middle of the
project. At Rising Star Way, full access and movement will be possible. The parking rate for the
apartments, which are one and two-bedroom apartments, is two stalls per apartment. The demand
rate at 12 different locations in Salt Lake County is 1.52, so the proposed parking should be more
than adequate to keep parking off the roadway. The most challenging problem to be resolved is the
left-tum traffic in the mornings. The road designs are critical to ensuring tum lanes and safety.

Noting that a traffic signal had a very good chance of going in at some point, Chair Cragun asked if
installation of the traffic signal will coincide with the project or if there will be a time when there will
be increased traffic but no signal. Mr. Hales did not know the timeline but stated that the tolerances
will still be such that the intersection can function adequately. Once Porter Rockwell Boulevard is
complete, the situation will be completely different. Chair Cragun next asked how traffic flow will
be impacted if people choose to tum right rather than left and make a U-tum down the road to go
north again. Mr. Hales acknowledged that there is no easy solution. He explained that roundabouts
are sometimes used to allow people to change direction without making a U-tum in the middle of the
road. However, because this road does not have a roundabout, he hoped the traffic signal would be
installed sooner rather than later. He noted that the completion of Porter Rockwell Boulevard will
hasten the installation of a traffic signal.

The proposed project will have a high-quality aesthetic that will comply with the cun-ent City Code
in tenns of landscaping, lighting, and building materials. They want to make the area a gateway to
Bluffdale and integrate it into the trail system. They were also committed to bringing in eating

       Zoning, Staff Presenters, Grant Crowell, Zoning Administrator, and Todd Sheeran, City

Mr. Crowell discussed Form-based Codes and Mr. Sheeran discussed Conditional Use Permits to
provide training to be in compliance with HB 409.

Mr. Crowell conducted a training on Form-Based Codes, which are very complicated. They constitute
a major paradigm shift from the Legislative and Administrative paradigm because Form-Based Codes
foster predictable results and a high-quality public realm by using physical form as the organizing
principle, with less focus on land use through municipal regulations. If the proposed site and facility
meet the design preferences they can be approved regardless of how the facility is to be used. There

can be differences in expectations between a major city center and a suburb in such things such as
parking and design. Since fonn would be the guiding principle, form-based codes eliminate
prohibited uses, which will be a major leap for Utah cities. Fonn-Based Codes require extensive
work to create and administer. Such a change would make it necessary for Bluffdale to have an
Architect on staff because the architectural demands would be so intense. Bluffdale City has opted
to have a Design Code and Land Use Tables rather than a Fonn-based Code. The design and land
use standards should be reviewed periodically. Mr. Crowell stated that that Form-Based Code was
used on a fairly limited and discreet basis in general. He could not see Bluffdale going all-in with
this approach.

Mr. Sheeran made a presentation on Conditional Use Penni ts. He provided an overview of how the
law defines conditional uses and how they are to be administered. In Bluffdale, the Planning
Commission is the Land Use Authority for considering and approving conditional uses because it is
an administrative and not a legislative decision. Conditional uses are difficult to deny; however, the
Planning Commission can identify and impose reasonable conditions that in their judgment mitigate
the detrimental effects of the proposed land use. The standards of review would be evaluated by the
Hea1ing Officer, who in Bluffdale is the City Attorney. The evidence that is reviewed by the Hearing
Officer must be on the record. In the event, the Planning Commission has to make an interpretation
of the City Code and that decision is challenged, the Hearing Officer would then have to review the
evidence to detennine if the judgment was correct and defensible.

With regard to detrimental effects, Mr. Sheeran stated that there must be substantial evidence that
they were present and needed to be mitigated. He noted that there is a difference between "substantial
evidence" and "public clamor." The Planning Commission has the statutory auth01ity to weigh the
evidence while the Hearing Officer cannot weigh the evidence. If the Planning Commission
determines that the argument asserting that there is substantial evidence of a detrimental effect that
requires a reasonable condition to mitigate, the Planning Commission has the authority to make the
judgment to do so. Mr. Sheeran indicated that he could provide guidance on how a ruling might be
made in court if the Planning Commission has questions on the defensibility of their decision.

Mr. Sheeran next presented a real-life situation that took place in Moab in 201 7. Mr. Sheeran noted
that in Moab, all Conditional Use Permits are considered by the City Council. The issue revolved
around the applicant's desire to change their home-based child-care business to a bed and breakfast.
The residents were wonied about the application and the City Council denied the request on the basis
that the application did not meet the criteria of the General Plan and would "change the characteristics
of the neighborhood." The proposed business was located on a cul-de-sac, where the traffic might
be especially problematic. The District Court ruled that there was speculative evidence that did not
support a finding of undue increase to traffic because there was already a daycare operating in the
home. In addition, the assertion that there would be additional noise was also ruled as mere
speculation. Mr. Sheeran explained that in cities where the City Council makes decisions that legally
should be administrative they sometimes confuse the nature of their legislative and administrative
discretion. The Distiict Court found other faults in the judgment made by the Moab City Council.
