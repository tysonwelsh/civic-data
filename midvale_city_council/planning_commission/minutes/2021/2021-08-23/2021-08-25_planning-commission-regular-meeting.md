2.     TXT-21-08; Zoning Ordinance Text Amendment; City wide; Text Amendment
       Modifying Accessory Dwelling Units in Chapter 17 of the Midvale City Municipal
       Code; Midvale City (Applicant); Nate Rockwood, Community Development Director
       (Staff).

Mr. Rockwood stated that as part of the 2021 legislative session the State approved HB- 82 which
requires cities to allow for internal accessory dwelling units (IADU) in areas zoned as primarily
residential. The actions required by the legislation are to take effect on October 1, 202 1. He
stated that Staff has prepared the relevant sections of code to include the uses of Internal
Accessory Dwelling Units (IADU) and External Accessory Dwelling Units (EADU) in zones
that allow detached single family use. He also added that the new legislation does provide some
discretion in implementation and application which is included in the presented text amendment.

                                                ***
APPLICANT:

Mr. Rockwood asked if the Commissioners have any questions?

Vice Chair Anderson asked what the process is if you know that someone is in violation of this
ordinance? She added that there is a home that is a single family home with 4 people living in the
house, no parking and portions of the house have been walled off to make them separate units.
How would the City know that there are people renting the prope1iy?

Mr. Rockwood stated that there are code enforcement measures that the city cunently has in
place to address these situations. He added that if the owner is renting out individual units, the
owner would have to get a business license to legally do what they are doing and in that process
the city would be notified of any illegal situations taking place and could take action to stop it.
He added that it sounds like what is taking place in this instance would not become a qualifying
circumstance under this new text amendment.

Mr. Ganett Willcox stated that there are a few facts that need to be identified before the city can
make any determination on the legality of the situation. He added that if there are 4 separate

dwelling units, then that would be a violation. He added because the city does not define
" family" or maximum occupancy in a home, there is nothing that would prevent someone from
renting to roommates. This new text amendment is allowing for mother-in-law zoning.

Commissioner Erickson asked if in regards to restricting exterior modifications, could a
homeowner install an exterior entryway to a basement apartment from the front, or would that be
prohibited?

Mr. Rockwood stated that the text is not that detailed. It is very broad and just states that you
cannot modify a home in such a way that it does not look like a single family home.

Chair Tippetts asked if preexisting mother-in-law apartments will be grandfathered in, or would
owners need to go through a process to get it approved?

Mr. Rockwood stated that it depends on how the owner is using the apartment. If they are not
renting the apartment and only have family members living in it, then they do not. If they are
renting it, they will need to acquire a business license, at which point it will go through an
approval process.
