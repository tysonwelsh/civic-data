2. Public hearing and possible action regarding Newpark Terrace Plat amendment 
and a Final Site Plan amendment, 1182 Center Drive, Newpark; Rory Klungervic, 
applicant – Amir Caus, County Planner 
 
County Planner Amir Caus presented the staff report and explained that this project was 
approved in 2013, and during construction, the developer made several aesthetic changes 
that were not in accordance with the final site plan.  Once that was discovered, the 
developer submitted an application for an amendment to the plat and final site plan to 
match the as-built product.  The unit sizes and square footage have not changed, and most 
of the changes are aesthetic.  Planner Caus provided photographs showing examples of 
the changes.  He indicated some changes that were made to comply with Fire Code and 
Building Department requirements.  He stated that they immediately contacted the 
Development Review Committee (DRC) when they learned of the changes, and they 
found that no public health, safety, or other issues were involved and that the overall 
spirit of the project did not change.  Staff recommended that the Planning Commission 
forward a positive recommendation to the County Manager for the plat amendment and 
final site plan amendment.

builder makes these kinds of changes without seeking the County’s advice.  Rory

should have had Planner Caus review the project all the way through.  Commissioner 
DeFord stated that it is unacceptable and inexcusable for a professional developer to 
have to come back to the Planning Commission and ask for these major changes.  It 
seemed to him that the developer was trying to get something past the County without 
going through the Planning Department, and now there is no recourse for the Planning 
Commission.  He asked what else could be done.  Planner Caus replied that the changes

3. Public hearing and possible action regarding a proposed amendment to Canyon 
Corners Development Agreement; 6622 North Landmark Drive – Ray Milliner, 
Principal Planner 
 
Principal Planner Ray Milliner presented the staff report and explained that this 
application is for an amendment to an existing Specially Planned Area (SPA) located on 
North Landmark Drive.  The applicant proposes a 2-building development, which 
includes a 43,000-square-foot retail structure designed for a Whole Foods store and a 
19,000-square-foot building designed for retail on the bottom and 20 units of affordable 
housing on the second level.  He recalled that the Planning Commission has reviewed this 
application four times in work session and public hearings and reviewed the issues raised 
during those meetings.  The most recent issues are safety on Landmark Drive, traffic 
impacts, and the sign plan.  Staff recommended that the Planning Commission forward a 
positive recommendation to the County Council for the proposed SPA amendment with 
some additional amendments.  He explained that the applicant has worked with Staff to 
provide features that have generally not been associated with projects in this area, 
including the bike sharing program, contribution to the shuttle program, and willingness 
to comply with requests that have been asked of them, and he asked the Planning 
Commission to keep that in mind.

Alec Paddock, representing the applicant, stated that after months of working with Staff, 
they are confident they have a plan that should work for the needs of the County and the 
community.  He recalled that the biggest concern was traffic and stated that Hales 
Engineering has worked hard to study levels of service and address those concerns,

Ryan Hales with Hales Engineering reported that he visited the site with two of the 
County’s engineers and evaluated the sight distances coming out of the project.  There 
was not sufficient sight distance for the access furthest to the west, and they propose that

flashing light at the crosswalk where the pedestrian can push a button and activate a 
flashing light to alert drivers that someone is in the crosswalk.  They also propose a two-
way left turn lane in the center of the road, which will allow anyone wanting to turn left 
into the project to move out of the through traffic lane.

eastbound, a left-turn lane coming out of the hotel, and a left-turn lane coming out of the

location, and she did not understand how an additional left-hand turn lane would be an 
improvement.

improvements, which includes the two-way turn lane.  He explained that, by lining up the 
intersection, vehicles turning out of Whole Foods and vehicles turning out of the hotel 
will not cross paths.  He also noted that there would be a right-turn pocket out of the 
through traffic lane for vehicles turning right into the site.  He confirmed that they have

the area is surrounded by counties that frequently violate federal

4. Wood smoke is even more toxic than other particulate pollution and

include particulate matter, carbon monoxide, formaldehyde, sulfur

9. Wood smoke is not just an outdoor problem.  There is no practical 
way to prevent wood smoke pollution from seeping into nearby 
homes.  The extremely small size of the particles results in the 
particles remaining suspended in the atmosphere for long periods, 
making a disproportionate contribution to air shed pollution.

10. Ultrafine particles are more potent in inducing inflammatory
