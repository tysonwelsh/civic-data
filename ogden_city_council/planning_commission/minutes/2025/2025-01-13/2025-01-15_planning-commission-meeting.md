2. Zone Ogden — Review non-residential zone chapters
Consultants John Jensen and Delaney Sillman went over the next set of chapters which

were the nonresidential and downtown chapters. They gave an overview of the code structure of where
those chapters sit and how they interact with some of the other chapters (slides and drafts shown). The
chapters will start with purpose and applicability, permitted and conditional use, and long use with tables
for each chapter with spatial standards, downtown, heights, setbacks, etc. The tables are all in one place
now as opposed to being broken up into three different chapters as before and will correspond with the
applicable charts and reference individual illustrative purposes. As far as links go, the last column on all
the use tables will have some, but the City is currently using a publishing company to publish their codes
that tie their hands on how much linking and usability they can do. However, when you get to a sign
chapter, it's going to be tables that have the zone and signs and standards within it so, you will have
everything you have questions about. Spatial standards will be easier to handle with some nice use-based
design standards. They did try to standardize the regulations and work on the transitions, breaking down
what was previously clumped together, so they have their own chapters. There will be some language
clarification as well around the vertical and horizontal articulation but, the massing in general will stay very
