County Planner Tiffanie Northrup-Robinson presented the staff report and explained that 
this is a plat amendment to combine two and a half lots.  Meadow Wild was platted in 
1980 with very small lots ranging between .06 and .11 acres as a Planned Unit 
Development.  The combination of these lots will result in a lot .20 acres in size.  The lots 
were combined by deed in 1998, but no formal plat amendment was requested to 
combine the lots.  The applicant wishes to expand her deck, but because the lot lines still 
exist, she would be unable to meet the setback requirements.  Planner Northrup-Robinson 
reported that she received one inquiry from someone who was concerned that this lot was 
being combined so a very large home could be built that would be inconsistent with the 
surrounding residences, and when she explained that the applicant wants to expand her 
deck, there were no further concerns.  Staff finds that there is good cause for this 
amendment and recommends that the Planning Commission conduct a public hearing and 
approve the proposed plat amendment. 
 
Commissioner Klingenstein asked about the likelihood that a larger home could be built 
on this lot once it is combined.  Planner Northrup-Robinson replied that someone could 
rebuild on this site, but the applicant has no intention to do that.  She clarified that 
additions or new construction would have to meet the setback requirements in the Code.  
Commissioner Klingenstein expressed concern that Staff may have given the wrong 
impression to the person who expressed concern about the lot combination.  He asked 
where MW-1-39 is located, because it is not shown on the plat.  Planner Northrup-
Robinson explained that, because this was previously recorded by deed and Lot 39 was 
already divided and absorbed into Lots 38 and 40, the Recorder’s Office does not reflect 
it.  The actual lot once this is recorded will be 37-AM, and neither Lots 38 nor 39 will be 
shown on the plat. 
 
Commissioner DeFord asked why building pads are sometimes designated on the plat 
and sometimes not.  Planner Northrup-Robinson explained that it depends on what the 
circumstances were when the subdivision plat was approved. 
 
Commissioner Peck confirmed with Planner Northrup-Robinson that this subdivision 
was recorded as a Planned Unit Development.

5. Public hearing and possible action regarding a Conditional Use Permit to expand 
the existing Rocky Mountain Power Substation near the Bear Hollow 
Condominiums; near 5585 North Grizzly Way, Park City, UT; S-59; Rocky 
Mountain Power, applicant – Sean Lewis, County Planner 
 
Planner Lewis presented the staff report and recalled that the Rocky Mountain Power 
Substation has been in place since 1981.  Rocky Mountain Power submitted an 
application to expand the site by about an acre, which required land swaps between 
Rocky Mountain Power, Summit County, the Snyderville Basin Special Recreation 
District, and the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.  Those agreements are 
dependent upon approval of a Conditional Use Permit (CUP).  The intent is for Rocky 
Mountain to add transformers and equipment to upgrade their power lines from 46 kV to 
138 kV to increase capacity and reliability for customers in the Snyderville Basin and 
Park City.  The expansion would include the relocation and expansion of seven 
transmission poles.  One issue is landscaping, and the Recreation District has asked the 
applicant to leave the existing natural landscaping and not add any landscaping, which is 
supported by the Community Development Department.  Planner Lewis presented photo 
simulations of existing conditions look and how the expanded site will look from various 
vantage points.  It was Staff’s finding that the proposed conditions meet the terms of the 
Code and recommended that the Planning Commission approve the proposed CUP. 
 
Commissioner Lawson asked about the poles that would be removed.  Chad Ambrose, 
Regional Business Manager for Rocky Mountain Power, explained that the steel pole to 
the west will be replaced with a steel pole, and the poles to the south are wood.  All of the 
wood poles will be replaced with wood except for two, one inside the substation and one 
outside.  The other structures shown on the simulation inside the substation are called 
dead-end structures and are used to transform the 138 kV to 46 kV.  Commissioner 
Lawson asked if any thought was given to using Cor-Ten steel rather than galvanized, 
stating he believed it would blend into the background better.  Mr. Ambrose replied that 
they have considered it, and in the summertime it would blend in better.  However, in the 
wintertime it would stand out against the snow.  Sam Knecht, Project Manager with 
Rocky Mountain Power, explained that they would not use Cor-Ten on structures inside 
the substation.  He explained that they weigh all the different scenarios and find that 
galvanized blends into the background better than Cor-Ten, but they do have the 
flexibility to use Cor-Ten outside the substation.  Commissioner Lawson believed the 
steel poles outside the structure should be Cor-Ten.

Commissioner Barnes asked if Rocky Mountain envisions any future expansions at this 
substation.  Mr. Ambrose explained that this substation was built in 1981 to serve the 
needs of the local community.  He came in 2008 to get permits for the second expansion, 
and now he is requesting a permit for the third expansion.  This is considered a 
significant expansion, because it will provide voltage support to the greater area and 
greater reliability and redundancy in the Snyderville Basin area.  The Snyderville 
substation is served out of Salt Lake City, and if they lose that transmission line, this 
substation will be out of power.  Once they complete this expansion and another project, 
they will have a redundant power source coming out of Wyoming.  Planner Lewis 
recalled that a task force was formed in 2010 to look at a power plan for the Snyderville 
Basin, and it was determined by local participants that it would be better to expand the 
existing substation than to try to find a location for a new substation.  Mr. Ambrose 
further explained that this expansion will allow them to bring in a 138 kV source plus 
another 138 kV redundant source, which is a great opportunity to provide reliability to 
the power system in Summit County.  Commissioner Barnes asked why Cor-Ten steel 
can be used outside the substation and not inside the substation.  Kevin Freestone, 
Director of Substations for Rocky Mountain Power, explained that, as a Cor-Ten pole 
rusts, it causes operational issues with the insulators and other equipment that would be 
on the poles inside the substation. 
 
Commissioner Klingenstein stated that he was surprised that no public comment was

1. Discussion regarding Pace Meadows Development Code Amendment and Rezone – 
Jennifer Strader, County Planner 
 
County Planner Jennifer Strader described the location of the property, which contains 455.84 
acres and is zoned Rural Residential (RR).  The applicant proposes a Code amendment to add a 
Mixed-Use Zone to the Code and a rezone application to rezone a portion of the property to the 
Mixed-Use Zone.  A previous work session with the Planning Commission was held in June 
2015.  Planner Strader stated that the Mixed-Use Zone District would be built around mass 
transit and would be pedestrian oriented.  Property currently zoned Rural Residential, 
Neighborhood Commercial, Community Commercial, or Service Commercial could be rezoned.  
She noted that the property would have to be surrounded within 300 feet on at least two sides by 
existing residential development that exceeds one unit per acre or by commercial or industrial 
uses totaling at least 80,000 square feet.  There would be a minimum requirement of 75% open 
space, and if TDRs are adopted, they could be transferred into this zone and open space could be 
reduced to 70%.  No density could be transferred out of the zone. 
 
The applicant proposes to rezone 46 of the 455.84 acres to the Mixed-Use Zone, and 
approximately 409 acres would remain as open space with a conservation easement.  At the 
previous work session, the Planning Commission requested information regarding whether this 
application proposes a net increase in density.  They also asked how the transportation 
component of the development relates to the Snyderville Basin Transportation Master Plan and 
whether this development would be subject to the recently adopted General Plan.  With regard to 
the increase in density, the applicant has submitted an analysis attached to the staff report, and 
the applicant will review that information with the Planning Commission.  She reported that they 
have not had a detailed discussion about transportation, because there are not many details 
available about the proposed project.  A primary component of the Mixed-Use Zone District 
would be to create a transit-oriented development that could accommodate the impacts of the 
development as well as regional pressures.  She confirmed that this project should conform to the 
recently adopted General Plan.  She explained that the applicant and Staff are looking for input 
regarding whether the key elements of the Mixed-Use Zone are appropriate, whether the 
Planning Commission considers this project to be an increase in density, and if the proposed 
density and uses are appropriate for the site.
