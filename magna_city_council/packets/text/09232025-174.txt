EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

With Salt Lake County and Salt Lake County’s Redevelopment Agency turning over the
assets of the Downtown and Arbor Park Redevelopment Districts, Magna City has the
opportunity to direct the future of Downtown Magna itself.

Downtown Magna is on the cusp of exciting changes. With the right investment from the
Community Reinvestment Agency (CRA), we can breathe new life into the area, draw in
more community activity, and encourage private investment. This proposal deﬁnes key
projects and a staffing request for the next year, and beyond, that together represents a
meaningful step toward revitalizing the heart of our community.

The proposed budget items work together to position Downtown Magna as a place where
people want to live, visit, and invest; vibrant, well-used public spaces are safer spaces.
Each element supports one or more of the following outcomes:

   -   Activates the downtown core with events, signage, and physical improvements
   -   Builds local business capacity through marketing and economic tools
   -   Generates tax increment growth via increased foot traffic, property values, and retail
       activity
   -   Establishes a foundation for long-term revitalization through consistent leadership
   -   By drawing more residents, families, and businesses into Downtown Magna through
       events, markets, and amenities, we create a self-reinforcing cycle of visibility,
       accountability, and safety.

PURPOSE

This White Paper recommends that the CRA Board allocate funds for targeted
improvements, programs, and staffing in Downtown Magna. These investments align with
the CRA’s mission to stimulate economic development, increase property values, and
improve quality of life for residents and visitors.

PROPOSED BUDGET ITEMS

1. Marketing of Downtown Magna – $10,000

A consistent and visible marketing effort is critical to reintroduce Downtown Magna as a
vibrant, welcoming destination for residents, businesses, and visitors alike. This
investment will help to shape public perception, build local pride, and support new and
existing businesses. These activities will include:

   •   Branding and advertising campaigns
   •   Create ﬂyers, ads, and digital content
   •   Promote downtown events and businesses

2. Software – $5,000

Reliable tools make it easier to manage events, track engagement, and stay organized. This
small investment helps us modernize how we communicate and collaborate.

   •   Purchase event and communications management software
   •   Improve outreach and vendor tracking
   •   Streamline downtown coordination tasks

3. Digital Reader Sign – $60,000

A digital reader board will serve as a hub of information right on Main Street, helping
promote events, safety notices, and CRA initiatives in real time.

   •   Install programmable digital sign along 8400 West
   •   Share event notices and community alerts
   •   Provide daily visibility for CRA activities

4. Municipal Advisory Firm (Economic Development Consultant) Retainer – $12,000

Having expert ﬁnancial guidance available ensures we’re making sound, strategic
decisions. This retainer secures those services on an as-needed basis throughout the year.

   •   Access to a ﬁnancial advisory ﬁrm
   •   Get expert help with CRA ﬁnance strategies
   •   Model projects and tax increment scenarios
   •   Economic Development Studies (see item 5)

5. Economic Development Study(s) – $50,000

Strong decisions rely on solid data. These studies will help us better understand economic
trends, project feasibility, and needs within Downtown Magna.

   •   Conduct market and economic assessments
   •   Evaluate project sites and opportunities
   •   Support grant applications and private investment

6. Downtown Parking Improvements – $200,000

One of the biggest barriers to downtown growth is parking. This funding helps us move
forward on near-term solutions identiﬁed in recent planning efforts.

   •   Add or upgrade lighting and signage
   •   Improve shared-use lots
   •   Support items 7 and 8

Commissary Kitchen – $75,000

A shared-use commissary kitchen would provide a critical piece of infrastructure to
support Magna’s food economy. Local caterers and food truck operators to aspiring chefs
and small food-based businesses, this space would offer affordable, licensed access to
kitchen facilities. Located in the Webster Center, it would complement the Farmers Market
and Food Truck Court while fostering entrepreneurship and job creation.

   •   Renovate or retroﬁt a commercial kitchen space
   •   Serve as a licensed prep and storage facility for vendors
   •   Support food-based business startups and job training

Proposed Events

7. Downtown Farmers Market – $20,000
A Magna Farmers Market will serve as a cornerstone event that brings the community
together while supporting local entrepreneurs. Positioned on Main Street or in a nearby
public space, the market will promote healthy living, drive foot traffic to downtown
businesses, and create a regular rhythm of community activity. The Farmers Market would
run from Memorial Day to Halloween.

   •   Launch and manage a weekly Farmers Market

   •   Highlight local agriculture and vendors
   •   Draw people to downtown each week
   •   Youth Market

8. Food Truck Court – $10,000

The Food Truck Court could bring life to Downtown Magna during evenings and weekends
by offering diverse food options and casual gathering spaces. It adds vibrancy to
underused spaces and supports small food businesses. It is anticipated that we would
utilize some newly acquired CRA parcels along Main Street.

   •   Launch a managed food truck program
   •   Use underutilized public spaces
   •   Activate downtown after hours and on weekends

Proposed Downtown Event Coordinator

An active and well-managed downtown doesn’t happen by chance, it requires consistent
effort, coordination, and local leadership. As we invest in new events, marketing strategies,
and public spaces, it’s essential to also invest in the personnel who can bring these
initiatives to life and keep them running smoothly. Having dedicated staff time on the
ground will ensure that Downtown Magna continues to thrive, grow, and respond to the
needs of the community in real time.

9. Event Coordinator (CRA-Funded Staff Position) – $50,000 to $60,000/year

A vibrant downtown needs a dedicated person to bring events to life and keep things
running smoothly. This position would work under the direction of the Economic
Development Manager to lead the charge in promoting Downtown Magna, building out
events like the Farmers Market and Food Truck Court, and supporting large community
celebrations. With someone in place to focus on programming, coordination, and
outreach, we can ensure that momentum in the downtown continues to grow.

It is anticipated that this position would have quarterly signature events in the Downtown.

   •   Lead marketing for Downtown Magna
   •   Launch and manage the Farmers Market
   •   Oversee the Food Truck Court
   •   Help plan major events like the 4th of July or Downtown Halloween, Christmas Trees
       Avenue, etc.

This professional services and municipal advisory agreement, referred to as the "Agreement" dated _____________,
is made by and between the City of Magna located 8952 W Magna Main Street referred to as the "Client", AND
Clearwater Financial, LLC located at PO Box 505 Eagle, ID 83616, referred to as the "Consultant."

1. Professional Services. Upon request, the Client may employ the Consultant to perform the following services:
         a) Public Engagement Services: Public education services including surveys; marketing, messaging,
             branding, media engagement and press releases, social media and website marketing;
             intergovernmental relations including joint powers agreements and public private partnerships (PPP);
             stakeholder engagement.
         b) Planning and Development Services: Strategic, comprehensive, infrastructure and other long-range
             planning services; master facility planning; comprehensive financial planning; planning & zoning
             administration and best practices.
         c) Public Budgeting and Accounting Services: Budget creation assistance; audit prep and review;
             financial analysis; financial policies and best practices; demographic and financial projections; what-if
             scenarios; and project pro forma.
         d) Economic Development Services: Public private partnerships; redevelopment; development services of
             public assets; property assemblage; site selection; design team creation; highest-and-best-use, market
             and economic impact analysis; development impact fees; urban renewal TIF analysis, feasibility
             studies, and finance.
         e) Banking and Treasury Services: Banking services RFQ/RFP; latent fund analysis; process/systems
             improvements and innovations; banking and treasury policies; bond funds reinvestment.

2. Municipal Advisory and Financial Services. The Client hereby engages the Consultant as its exclusive
registered “municipal advisor” with both the Securities Exchange Commission “SEC” and the Municipal Securities
Rulemaking Board “MSRB” to perform the following services in accordance with the rules and regulations adopted
by the SEC and MSRB and with the terms and conditions set forth in this Agreement for the purpose of obtaining
financing (bonds, notes, loans, leases) for necessary projects. Municipal Advisory and Financial Services include:

         a) Assistance with the creation and coordination of the finance team including bond council,
            underwriter(s), trustees, paying agents, bond registrars, escrow agents, escrow verification agents,
            rating agency(s), bond insurers, arbitrage rebate consultants and any other parties engaged in providing
            services pertaining to the proposed financing. All other parties will be paid by the Client.
         b) Advice and assistance in selecting the method of financing; bidding of various services and financing
            packages; bond rating preparation and coordination; assistance with state-wide finance programs;
            funding options analysis and other related services as necessary.
         c) Advice related to bond structuring and issuance process such as: rate, term, covenants, finance options,
            credit analysis, and other services. This also includes, if necessary, debt incurring capacity, tax impact
            analyses and other related calculations.
         d) Producing information about the Client such as: population, assessed values, largest employers, largest
            rate payers, property/revenue diversification and other information needed for financing purposes.
         e) Provide financial advice regarding municipal credit and debt management, fund balances, debt ratios,
            and funding options policies and procedures.
         f) Create project proformas with corresponding tax increment financing (TIF) analysis, and budgets.

The Consultant will consult with the Client and its representatives as needed to fulfill the terms of this Agreement.
All services to be provided by the Consultant will be performed with promptness and diligence and at a level of
proficiency to be expected by a consultant with the background and experience that the Consultant has represented it
possesses. The Client will coordinate and communicate with the Consultant on its financings and provide accurate
information as needed to fulfill the terms of this Agreement.
