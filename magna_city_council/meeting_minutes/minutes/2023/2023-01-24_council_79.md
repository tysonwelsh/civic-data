Unified Police Department Report

               Chief Del Craig introduced Magna Precinct’s new sergeant, Sergeant John Neron,
who has been in law enforcement for about eighteen years, four of which were in the Unified
Police Department’s (UPD) Metro Gang Unit. The UPD started seeing fights at school, and
discovered it was a gang issue. It was successful at addressing that, in large part due to Sgt.
Neron’s efforts.

               Sergeant John Neron, Unified Police Department, stated he has been in law
enforcement for eighteen years, eight of which have been with the UPD. Before that, he was a
sergeant with the Sheriff’s Office. He was also an Army Veteran with the 101st Airborne Division.
He has worked as a patrol officer, a detective, and with the Metro Gang Unit, which he did from
2017 until being promoted to sergeant. For the last year-and-a-half, he has worked exclusively
for the Magna Precinct. One of his big focusses is youth violence. He not only addresses the
violence, but also advocates for juveniles who are implicated into gangs and tries to help them
find resources to get them out of it.

               Detective Russ Buhler, Unified Police Department, stated announced the UPD is
holding its Kearns and Magna Youth Citizens Academy March 1, 2023, through April 26, 2023.
The Academy takes 15 kids from each community and puts them through an eight-week course
about the UPD. It is a tool to lower the barriers between law enforcement and youth and makes
them feel more comfortable to talk to law enforcement.

               Mayor Peay asked if parents had to be involved.


                                                 2
DATE            TUESDAY                                JANUARY                               24, 2023

                 Detective Buhler stated parents must sign a permission slip. A parent meeting
will be held before the course starts so parents understand what their children will be doing. Then,
there will be an opening night at Mom’s Café in Kearns. Once the course is complete, the UPD
will hold a closing night and allow parents to come, at which it will award the kids with a certificate
of completion, present a PowerPoint with photos, and have the kids talk about their experience in
the academy.

              Council Member Hull stated the Magna Town Council has been paying for
Magna’s half of the cost for food, and Kearns pays the other half. She thought the Magna Metro
Township should start participating with that. She asked if these kids ended up becoming police
officers.

             Detective Buhler stated several of the participants went onto becoming cadets in
the UPD’s Youth Cadet Program, and later several of those cadets became police officers.

               Chief Craig stated a growing issue of concern is parking. There are a lot of cars
parked on the streets, and a lot of those streets are narrow, so service providers cannot get their
vehicles down those streets to provide services, especially under heavy snow. He suggested
putting together a focus group or committee, with input from the legislative body, the police, legal
counsel, and the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD) to address the issues,
including signage. He also suggested the Council consider addressing the parking ordinances,
as they are not clear to the public.

                Council Member Barney stated one of the problems is the development
agreements for some of the planned unit development have some unique parking requirements
and restrictions, such as not allowing parking in the driveways, with no way to enforce them.

               Mayor Peay stated he would have this placed on next week’s agenda workshop
meeting.

                Council Member Prokopis suggested looking to see what other cities have done
with their parking ordinances to see what has worked rather than reinventing the wheel.

Financial Report

                Dave Sanderson reviewed the budget for fiscal year ending December 31, 2022.
The revenue budget developed by the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD) is $8.2
million, and the Magna Metro Township brought in $10.95 million, which is 133 percent of that
budget. Revenues will still change because sales and transportation taxes lag for a couple
months, and those are projected to be $11,455,000, which will increase Magna’s revenues to 140
percent of the revenue budget. He also noted that Code Enforcement fines in Magna were about
$24,900, which was higher than previous years. On the expense side, the projected expenses
were about $457,000, which is about 75 percent of the expense budget of $615,000. Most all the
bills have been paid, so there will not be much change from what was projected.

Ordinance No. 2023-O-01

                Justin Smith, Planner, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District, reviewed the
following application for a rezone request.

                                                7
DATE           TUESDAY                               JANUARY                              24, 2023

               REZ2022-000725 – Joe Colosimo is requesting approval of a zone change from
an A-1 zone to an R-M zone to subdivide property into 16 lots for townhomes on 1.72 acres of
property located at 2802 South 7200 West

                Mr. Smith stated this rezone came before the Council last month, where the
Council voted to send it back to the Planning Commission for its recommendation on alternative
options. The Planning Commission heard the request two weeks ago and recommended
approval. The applicant is now looking to rezone the property to develop four four-plex
townhomes after getting feedback from the Planning Commission and Council. The general
opinion was a desire to have a more dense and appealing project built there than what the
applicant originally brought forth. The density for RM is limited by code, ranging from seven to
twenty units per acre – seven for single family and 20 for multi-family. The applicant is proposing
16 units.

Most of the surrounding area is zoned A1. The community to the west is about four to five units
per acre, and to the north, there are about ten units per acre. The property across 7700 is in West
Valley and it is currently undeveloped. Future land use designation calls for a mix of single family
homes and duplexes.

              Justin Smith, Planner, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD),
delivered a PowerPoint presentation on the property and surrounding properties, and what the
long-range goals were for strengthening the street to the west to connect onto Parkway Blvd.,
which would become a transit lane. Then, the 3300 South Lane could dip to the south up to Main
Street.

               Council Member Barney asked what West Valley had planned long-term for its
property. It would be helpful to know before the Council decided what should go on its side.

               Mr. Smith stated the MSD has had no communication with West Valley on that
property.

                Joe Colosimo, applicant, stated he approached the Planning Commission in
November with a concept of single family manufactured homes, but the Planning Commission
wanted a townhome concept. It was not opposed to density, but it did not think single family
homes was appropriate. The Planning Commission sent a negative recommendation to the
Council, after which he created a site plan for four fourplexes. The units would be about 2,000
square feet, or nine units per acre, with a 20-foot driveway. There would be 4.43 parking stalls
per unit – two in the garage and two in the driveway, and guest parking of about 4.43 stalls. The
other thing the Planning Commission had been concerned about was the proximity to the
intersection, so he created a fire hammerhead that comes off 2880 South, with no access out to
7200 West. He still had to get approval for a planned unit development (PUD). If this fits those
requirements, he would take it back to the Planning Commission for approval.

               Council Member Hull, seconded by Council Member Prokopis, moved to approve
the following Ordinance No. 2023-O-01. The motion passed unanimously.

Ordinance No. 2023-O-02

                Justin Smith, Planner, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District, reviewed the
following application for a rezone request.

REZ2022-000745 – Carlos Diaz is requesting approval of a zone change from an R-1-6 zone to
an R-1-5 zone on 0.15 acre of property located at 3060 South 8850 West to develop a single
family home.

                 Mr. Smith stated this parcel is located in a residential neighborhood, and all the
properties in the neighborhood are zoned R-1-6, except the one it touches, which was rezoned
R-1-5 in 2020. The property is currently vacant, but a Google Earth image shows there was a
building on it in the 1990s. The property to the north was rezoned a couple years ago, with the
intent to subdivide it and develop another home on the southern portion, but that subdivision was
never built and the property is still vacant. The setback for R-1-6 and R-1-5 are similar. The front
setback requirement is 25 feet, and rear setbacks are 15 with a garage or 30 without. The side
setbacks are 5 and 11, or 8 and 8. The reason this lot cannot be developed in an R-1-6 zone is
because it is only 50 feet wide, and the requirement in that zone is 60 feet in width. The R-1-5
requires a maximum width of 25 feet and a minimum lot size of 5,000 square feet.

               Mayor Peay asked if there was room to put a garage or driveway in.

               Mr. Smith stated the mock site plan did not include that. There is room with the
setback in the front and the rear.

             Council Member Prokopis stated allowing him to push out to the street would
compound the parking problem.

               Paul Ashton asked if there was room in front to have him conform to the
neighbors’ setbacks.

              Matt Starley, Long-Range Planner, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District
(MSD), stated the MSD has talked about creating an overlay zone, which would settle the issue
of conforming with the neighbors.

             Council Member Pierce, seconded by Council Member Hull, moved to approve
Ordinance 2023-O-02. The motion passed 4 to 1, with Council Member Barney voting “Nay.”

Changing the Form of Government

               Paul Ashton stated the statute that governs the metro townships changing their
form of government was drafted as part of SB 199 (during the 2015 legislative session), which
allowed the creation of the metro townships. The statute reads that a municipality can have a
proposition on a general election or municipal election ballot to change its form of government,
but it had to wait for another municipal election before the voters could elect the officers of the
newly created form of government, which would not be for two years. Then, the new form of
government would take effect January following the election of the officers.

He has talked with the Magna Metro Township Council’s lobbyist, Kory Holdaway, and others who
think the Magna Metro Township could put a proposition on the ballot this fall for the change of
its form of government, but it would have to get legislation passed in order to have its officers
elected earlier. They were looking to see if they could piggyback onto a bill this year. The
incorporation statute allows the election of officers at a general election, which is only a one year
wait.

               Council Member Prokopis asked if the Magna Metro Township could do that
during a special election.

               Mr. Ashton stated that cannot be done in a special election the way the statute is
written.

                Council Member Prokopis stated the new form of government might not pass
here. It could take a few attempts on a ballot to get that change. Millcreek City tried to incorporate
several times and got voted down every year for a long time.

              Council Member Barney stated he was not concerned about the gap between
voting to become a city and then choosing officers. It is a two-year gap, which would give the
Magna Metro Township time to lay some groundwork. He did not think the Magna Metro
Township should waste its resources to lobby the Legislature for a change that only impacted it.

Council Reports

Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District (WFWRD)

                Council Member Barney stated the administrative control board met yesterday
and went over the budget. It is over budget, but it has enough money to cover everything. It
saved a lot of money by not purchasing trucks it ordered when the vendor canceled its order
because it could not meet that order. The board also talked about the Seasonal Container
Reservation Program, which it wants to tweak because there has been a lot of concern with that
program. It will probably not change that program this year, but it wants to make it fair. Some
people were getting the notice and able to reserve a dumpster because they got their notice in
the mail before others who were not able to reserve a dumpster. Twelve percent of the residents
in Magna were able to access a dumpster last year, and five percent of requests were not able to
be filled. Those who did not get a dumpster were very upset. The board talked about setting a
date in the future to allow for everyone to get a notice before that reservation date. In the interim,
there are free passes to the landfill available at the library, and any resident of Magna can get
them and use them. There is a trailer rental option as well. Last year, 1,200 passes were used.
The highest users were residents in the Magna and Kearns Metro Townships and Taylorsville
City.

               Mayor Peay asked if people could use last year’s landfill passes.

               Council Member Barney stated that would depend on whoever was at the landfill
at the time they went. If someone took the pass, he imagined the landfill would get reimbursed
for that.

               Mark Elieson, resident, stated his sons called the landfill to ask if they could use
last year’s pass, and were told they could. However, when they went to the landfill, on two
different occasions, the landfill refused the passes.
