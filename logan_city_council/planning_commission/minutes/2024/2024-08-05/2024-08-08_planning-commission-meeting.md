Comments received against STRs mentioned that STRs raise the price of homes and take away
homes from families, increased noise, more activity, instability, and parking.

Commissioner Guth about data regarding actual complaints received of permitted STR’s over the
past two years. R. Holley stated that we have received just a couple of complaints about operating
STR’s. Parking is an on-going complaint with all types of uses, occupancy is a common complaint,
noise and weeds.

Commissioner Heare asked about whether these changes are the result of public clamor or actual
complaints received on STRs. R. Holly said that City Council members and the Mayor have received
complaints about STRs being allowed in residential neighborhoods. There was additional discussion
about the distinction between a formal complaint and public clamor. Commissioner Heare raised the
point about legitimate complaints of STR’s versus opinions of residents who just don’t want STR’s in
their neighborhoods, and which should be the basis for making code changes.

Commissioner Doutre asked what would happen to the existing STRs if the proposed code is
adopted. R. Holley said that legally established STRs would be considered grandfathered or legally
non-conforming uses.

Public: Sharilee Griffiths said that the rules on STRs should be based on facts and not fears. Looking
at her own STR, over the past 30 days, there have been very few cars parked per stay. most of their
guests that come to stay together come in one or two cars. Logan’s parking and occupancy
requirements are too restrictive.

Megan Schmidt, STR owner said there are several illegal STRs in Logan. The existing regulations
are sufficient if the City could figure out a way to get the illegal STRs legal.

Holly Hill spoke about an illegal STR in her neighborhood that she has reported to the City but is still
operating.

Kandi Larsen asked how occupancy and parking are enforced by the City.

Alex Watts has a STR in the basement portion of his home. He spoke about the process of getting
his STR permitted and licensed being difficult and having to notify all of his neighbors about the STR.
He also said that he wouldn’t have been able to afford his home without the STR.

Eli Hopkins said the proposed STR regulations undermine homeowner's property rights and Logan’s
economic strength. He also spoke about the small sat conference that the City lost because there
was not adequate lodging for conference attendees. The City has sufficient STR regulations in place,
enforcement is the issue.

Dan Everton said that STRs are a commercial business. They should be in designated areas, not in
residential neighborhoods.

Darlene Dily agreed that STRs should be in designated areas and said that the City should not allow
STRs if the HOA doesn’t.

Jim Chambers read an email from Frank Schofield, Logan City School District Superintendent stating
they are opposed to any efforts made to transition existing homes into STR’s. Homes should be
reserved for long-term residents. Also asked the question of who really benefits from an STR going
into a residential neighborhood?
