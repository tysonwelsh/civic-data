                              Nephi City Council Work Session
                                    September 27, 2022
                                          7:00 p.m.
                                         21 E 100 N
                                     Nephi, UT 84648

1.    Juab County Sheriff Doug Anderson addressed the council about the Juab County’s
     necessity of building a new 200 bed Public Safety Complex. He indicated that the
     current facility is not big enough and is wearing out. He stated that the county
     commissioners had agreed to fund a feasibility study of the project. Sheriff Anderson
     said that the purpose of him and the commissioners and Officer Travis Kenison visiting
     the city council was to inform them about their plans of pursuing this public safety
     complex project. He said they have hired an architect that will be doing the feasibility
     study. The council asked about location, funding, and timeline of the complex.
     Councilor Memmott asked about dispatch services. The county officials indicated that
     the funding would come from a bond. They are looking at several locations but were
     more than likely going to build next to the current facility. The timeline is 2 ½ to 4 years
     to completion. The complex would be built with options to expand it in the future.

2. Shauna Talbot, Nephi City Planner, joined the meeting via zoom. Ms. Talbot reviewed
   with the council the proposed final version of amendments to Title 10.16 - off-street
   parking and loading code. She stated since the council last discussed the amendments
   and from the feedback she received from the Planning Commission, much research had
   been conducted to try and ensure the number of spaces required are both reasonable
   and adequate. Through reading other city code and referencing the International Traffic
   Engineering (ITE) manuals (industry standard manuals for parking) the number of
   spaces required had been grouped into their industry standard land use categories, and
   our proposed number of parking spaces had been compared against their data in the
   recommended average to 85th percentile peak traffic hours as a second check that the
   spaces we have chosen are reasonable but adequate. She had also taken into account
   real world examples of businesses mainly within Utah County to make sure our
   requirements are comparable to businesses already built in place by using google earth
   and google maps, and measuring approximate square footage of buildings, and counting
   available visible parking spaces.
   Ms. Talbot also noted that an added item “D” section 10.16.8 allowed commercial and
   industrial applicants to reduce the amount of parking spaces if they conduct a parking
   study, and their findings reasonably show the commission how reduced parking would
   be an acceptable option. Landscaping requirements was also addressed for parking
   areas.
   The council directed Ms. Talbot to move forward and to bring back the Ordinance
   amending Title 10.16 for final approval at the next regular meeting.

3. Mr. Atkinson reviewed with the council the Level 2 analysis for the 138 kV Power Line
   Annexation area. The analysis included what affect the annexation would have on
        sewer and water rights using assumptions of the future use of the area. Councilor
        Memmott thanked Mr. Atkinson for the in-depth analysis. Mr Atkinson recommended
        that the council ask the planning commission to study and make recommendations of
        the type of zoning and rate of development desired for this annexation area.
          The council directed staff to move forward with the annexation process for this area by
        bringing a level change request to the next council meeting. Mr. Atkinson said that the
        city will probably need a bond rating before the city can bond for the project. Mayor
        Seely said the sooner the better and to begin the bonding process.

   4.   The council reviewed the latest updated financial reports.

   5.   Lisa Brough updated the council about the city vehicle fleet.

   6. City Attorney Kasey Wright announced that the date of the Hive open house has been
      set for November 2, 2022. He also said that the RV park owners will be cited tomorrow
      for long-term stays at their parks.

   7. The council gave the following updates:

           a.  Mayor Seely announced that Jenson Refrigeration will be holding a ground-
              breaking ceremony on October 4, 2022.
           b. Councilor Worwood stated that the council needed to arrange a time to put up
              the Halloween Town decorations.
           c. Councilor Parady asked when reservations can be made for the Hive. Mr.
              Atkinson answered that it might not be available for outside reservations until the
              first of the year but would check on that.
           d. Councilor Ostler stated that the Miss Nephi Royalty had contacted all the
              businesses about decorating for Halloween Town.

   8.    Councilor Worwood moved to convene into executive session to discuss the character
        and competence of an individual and real property. Councilor Callaway seconded the
        motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

Meeting adjourned at 8:50 p.m.
