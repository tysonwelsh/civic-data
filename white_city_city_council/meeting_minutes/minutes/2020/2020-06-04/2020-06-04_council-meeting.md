Unified Police Department (UPD)

               Officer Paula Stinson stated the statistics in White City are really good for May
and there was nothing major. The suspects involved in the homicide that took place just outside
of White City have been caught. She has worked on getting Big Bear Park closed at night. A
couple of kids were caught one night at Big Bear Park.

               Chief Randy Thomas stated Officer Nathan Lyday’s funeral will be held on
Saturday, June 6, between 11:00 am and 1:00 pm. The cemetery will be a private service with
family and the police department. Officer Paula Stinson arranged for officers to drive-by a couple
of individuals homes who were having birthdays. UPD officers responded to the riots uptown.
Several officers were assaulted and injured as well as civilians and some property damage. UPD
does proactive training for these types of events and bias training.

Resolution

             The Council reviewed the following Resolution approving a license for pipeline
agreement for a nonexclusive wireless telecommunications infrastructure franchise between
Teleport Communications America LLC and White City;

RESOLUTION NO. 20-06-01                                                    Date: June 4, 2020

      A RESOLUTION OF THE WHITE CITY METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL
      APPROVING A LICENSE FOR PIPELINE AGREEMENT FOR A
      NONEXCLUSIVE WIRELESS TELECOMMUNICATIONS INFRASTRUCTURE
      FRANCHISE BETWEEN TELEPORT COMMUNICATIONS AMERICA LLC AND
      WHITE CITY

                                         RECITALS

   A. The White City Metro Township (“White City”) is a municipality pursuant to Utah Code
      Annotated (“UCA”) §§ 10-2a-401 et seq.

   B. The White City Metro Township Council (the “Council”) is the municipal legislative body
      for White City pursuant to UCA § 10-3b-501.

   C. Teleport Communications America, LLC (the “COMPANY”) is a Delaware limited liability
      company that conducts business under Utah Law.

   D. The Company has contacted White City to seek a nonexclusive wireless
      telecommunications infrastructure franchise license agreement (the “AGREEMENT”) from
      White City.

   E. The agreement would allow the Company to install wireless telecommunications pipelines
      in White City to improve the efficiency of wireless telecommunications for the users of
      such services.

   F. Improved wireless telecommunications services are generally desired by the residents of
      White City.

   G. Entering into this agreement is to the mutual benefit of the Company and White City.

Unified Fire Authority (UFA) & Unified Fire Service Area (UFSA)

              Council Member Perry stated the board discussed expenses that have been
incurred by the COVID-19 response. Emergency operations will be reimbursed by Salt Lake

County and there is no increase to White City. Clint Mecham’s position, Emergency Operations,
was reclassified and streamlined. UFA will adopt its tentative budget on June 16, 2020. The initial
budget had a 4 percent employee increase, but because of the economy the increase will only be

Unified Police Department (UPD) & Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA)

                  Mayor Flint stated the UPD budget includes a 2.75 percent increase and a 2
percent market adjustment. Twenty-five percent of UPD is funded by Salt Lake County, it owns
all of the infrastructure, buildings, and assets; however, Salt Lake County no longer has the
population. For the last couple of years, weighted voting by population, has been discussed.
Weighted voting will come back for a decision at the next meeting. The Kearns precinct is currently
being leased and there is now an option to purchase the building. The person who owns the
building is talking about selling the building and willing to negotiate.

                                 −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
South Salt Lake Valley Mosquito Abatement District

                Council Member Price stated the district is required to have an emergency aerial
spray option. The district had a bid of $.80 per acre that has since expired. The current bid
submitted is for $.92 per acre; the board approved the bid. Millcreek is doing a project with St.
Marks Hospital and requested a fee waiver; the board approved the request. Testing for West
Nile Virus started on June 1 in its own facility. Labs were brought in house to speed up the amount
of time that test results come back. Employees have been hired for the summer. Safety training
was done online and start times will be staggered to maintain social distancing.

                                 −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (GSLMSD)

                Mayor Flint stated the GSLMSD talked about the CARES Act funding and the
White City Council will need to determine if it wants to accept its portion of the funds. One big
downfall is that White City does not have staff to administer this kind of funding. If the money is
not used, it goes back to Salt Lake County to be spent where it sees fit. The money should benefit
White City businesses, but she also does not want to get in trouble for mishandling the funds.

                 Paul Ashton stated there are a lot of strings attached when using these funds. A
lot of entities have turned it down because of the requirements.

              Mayor Flint stated businesses do not qualify for the funds if it has already received
loans. The funds are exclusively for people in need that have not had any assistance.

                  Council Member Price stated she has read through the CARES Act and there are
a lot of restrictions. There is only one business and the others are home businesses. She would

                 Council Member Little stated he has watched different trainings on the CARES

funding. Salt Lake County wants to distribute the funds with a collective agreement.

Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District (WFWRD)

                Council Member Dickerson stated Pam Roberts, Executive Director, Wasatch
Front Waste and Recycling District, asked if White City would be holding a community clean-up.
I told her probably not this year, but we would let her know.

              Council Member Price stated residents have to request a dumpster for the
assigned clean-up. The dumpster does not cost anything, and it can be shared with neighbors.
The dumpster is dropped off in the driveway.

              Mayor Flint stated that Ms. Roberts has requested to be put on the agenda for
next month to discuss the clean-up.

                Council Member Price asked to get more dump vouchers.

               Council Member Little stated he is concerned about the agreement that has to
be signed when a dumpster is rented. It states that the person who requested the dumpster is
responsible for items that are discarded that are not supposed to be dumped. If he was getting a
dumpster, he would not want to take the liability for someone else’s trash.

                Mayor Flint asked the Council to put a list of concerns together to discuss at the
next meeting.

                Council Member Dickerson stated WFWRD does not have any way to police the
dumpsters; it is up to the individual.
