Public Comment No comments MAG Transportation Improvement Program (TIP) Project Selection Process - Shawn Eliot The Project Priority List was approved by MPO Regional Planning at their May 5th meeting. MPO staff used the approved priority list to apply the available MPO and county transportation funds (approximately $41M) for the next 2 years to establish the Project Priority Funding List. Project Priority List was funded from the top ranked project down the list until funding was expended. There were 6 different federal and county funding sources used. If additional funds become available, the next project on the Priority List will be funded. The remaining $4.4 million is reserved for the next project on the list and will be used if additional funds become available. The Mountain View Corridor Southern Extension Study through Saratoga Springs received $250,000. TAC met May 23rd and voted unanimously to recommend the Project Priority Funding List to Regional Planning. Mayor J.H. Hadfield moved the MPO Regional Planning Committee approve of the Funding List. The funding list is the approved list of projects to fund in the TIP and will be used to fund unfunded projects if additional funds come available. Mayor Bert Wilson seconded the motion and the carried unanimously. MPO Federal Funds Exchange for UDOT State Funds and Joint Project Management Process - Bob Allen The MPO receives approximately $9 million in federal STP Urban and non-Urban funds annually. The federal requirements to use these funds add a significant cost and time to projects. The MPO and UDOT are working together to develop a project management and oversight process. This is the first time UDOT has offered to exchange funds with the MPO for its entire federal STP funds, in the past the exchange funds were issued for one project at a time. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning June 2, 2016 Page 3 MAG would be able to exchange federal STP dollars for state dollars at .85/1.00 ratio. Currently federal funds require and 6.77% local match and exchange state dollars would also require the some 6.77% local match. The exchange would take place annually, funds will be receive in one lump sum in FY-2017 and FY-2018 with an option to continue this exchange program in the future. TIP projects will continue to be selected through the TIP Selection Process. At the TAC meeting on May 23rd they voted unanimously to recommend to Regional Planning to enter into a MOA to exchange funds with UDOT. Mayor J.H. Hadfield moved the Regional Planning Committee to enter into the memorandum of agreement between MPO and UDOT to exchange federal funds for state funds. Mayor Bert Wilson seconded the motion and the motion carried unanimously. Joint Management Process: The MPO would be responsible for managing the TIP projects using the exchanged state funds, with an UDOT oversight. Utah County will continue to manage the TIP county funded projects. All projects funds awarded to UDOT and UTA will be managed by their respective agencies with an Interlocal Agreement. TAC met May 23rd and voted unanimously to recommend to Regional Planning to adopt the MPO/Utah Joint Project Management Process. Mayor J.H. Hadfield moved the Regional Planning Committee adopt the MPO/Utah County Joint Project Management Process. Mayor Bert Wilson seconded the motion. The motion carried with 19 yes votes and 1 no vote from Commissioner Larry Ellertson. Provo 820 North Corridor Study Findings - Chad Eccles Provo 800/820 North Corridor Study has evaluated potential improvements to the 800/820 North corridor including a possible connection to I-15. The improvements would improve the east-west mobility and access to central areas of Provo and the west side area. Goals of the study: reduce traffic congestion; accommodate bicycle and pedestrian needs; land use and economic opportunities in Provo; identify potential infrastructure improvement; evaluate how I-15 access would affect 820 North, and connectivity and regional mobility with more direct access to major employers. Concerns widening 800/820 North Concerns with New Interchange Relocation of residences & businesses Increased traffic Impacts to adjacent neighborhoods Relocation of residences & businesses Safety for pedestrians & bicyclist Impacts to adjacent neighborhoods Noise Safety for pedestrians & bicyclist Proximity to schools Impacts to schools Speed Ability to access I-15 Air Quality Noise This a planning level study with results to become part of the next RTP and updates Provo Transportation Plan. The final report will be available this fall. Questions contact Chad Eccles. Transportation Impact Fees and Municipal Utility Fees - Andrew Jackson In continuing our ongoing discussion concerning funding for maintenance for local roads, staff felt it was important to discuss the various fees and taxes:  Taxes are imposed for the primary purpose of raising revenue, with the resultant funds spent on general government services.  Fees are imposed for the primary purpose of covering the cost of providing a service, with the funds raised directly from those benefitting from a particular provided service.  Penalties are imposed for the primary purpose of penalizing or regulating behavior, generally imposed as part of judicial proceedings, with resultant revenue a secondary consideration.  Hookup fees are imposed for the primary purpose of covering the actual cost of connecting to the service. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning June 2, 2016 Page 5  Impact fees are imposed for the primary purpose of covering the proportionate cost providing new or expanded public capital facilities required to serve new development. Cannot be used to maintain a facility and the state cannot charge impact fees There are two components for taxes, a rate and a base. The rate or mil levy is set as a percentage of a purchase or value of an asset. The base is what the rate is multiplied against. Most transportation taxes exclude food purchases from the base. The state constitution prohibits motor fuel taxes to be used on transit. 18.5 cents of federal gas tax is spent on federal transit projects. Gasoline tax is an excise tax (per gallon charge). Utility fees may be used for maintenance and is typically a fee charged for the average cost of servicing the payer of the fee. Different users pay a different fee associated with the cost or potential cost of servicing the user, such as, a water utility fee. Additional information or questions contact Andrew.
