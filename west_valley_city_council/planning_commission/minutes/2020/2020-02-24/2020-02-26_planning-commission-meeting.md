ZT-2-2020
Erik Hoffman

The applicant, Erik Hoffman, has requested a proposed amendment to the West Valley City
Municipal Code to allow for Auto Emissions Centers in the RB, residential business zone.

Currently, auto emission centers are listed under Auto Service per the following definition:

       “Automobile Service” means the repair, rental, and maintenance of automobiles and
       trucks, including but not limited to body shops, transmission shops, lube and emissions
       centers, tire stores, car washes as a Primary Use, auto glass shops, and car rental.

This amendment would remove that use from this definition and a new use/definition would be
added to the City Code as follows:

        “Automobile Emissions Center” means a test only business for emissions, inspections
       and registration renewals, excluding Automobile Service.

The use table would also be amended as follows to add an Automobile Emissions Center as a
conditional use in the RB zone:

7-6-201. AGRICULTURAL AND RESIDENTIAL USE TABLE.




      Automobile                  X    X      X    X      X     X    X      X     X    C*
      Emissions Center*


The use would also be added to the Commercial and Manufacturing use table and is proposed to
be regulated as it was previously under Automobile Service.

Since the text amendment affects all properties in the RB zone, Mr. Hoffman has also
suggested the following use specific regulations for this amendment as well.

7-7-105 AUTOMOBILE EMISSIONS CENTER

(1) All Automobile Emissions, Inspections and Registration Renewals shall be conducted
within an enclosed Building.
(2) Inoperable vehicles, or service equipment shall not be stored outside.
(3) The minimum Lot size for an Automobile Emissions Center in an RB Zone shall be one
acre.

Mr. Hoffman has stated the justification for the requested text change is that “Automobile
Emission Centers (Test Only) have a lower impact than other Automobile Service businesses
or even some existing approved uses for an RB Zone. Limiting this to properties over an acre
will further limit this use. The property can still have other permitted and conditional uses.”

Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval of the proposed ordinance amendment.

   2. Continuance, to allow for the resolution of any issues raised at the public hearing or to
      allow time for the applicant to revise the proposed regulations in the ordinance
      amendment.

   3. Denial, the current Ordinance requirements are adequate, and this use is not appropriate in
      this zone.


Discussion:
Jody Knapp presented the application. She stated that Mr. Hoffman is interested in opening an
emissions business at a specific location in West Valley City. The property is located at 3540 W.
4700 S and is currently zoned RB. Jody explained that if this text amendment were approved this
use would come back to the Planning Commission as a conditional use.

Applicant: Erik Hoffman then addressed the Planning Commission. Erik stated that Jody
presented the application very well. Erik explained that his Automobile Emissions business is
currently located in Taylorsville and he is looking to relocate to West Valley City. He feels the
zone text change would be less impactful to the City than looking to change the zone of the
property he is interested in to a C2 (general commercial) zone.

Public Comment: Fred Cox addressed the Planning Commission. Mr. Cox explained that C2
zones have been explored for the business, however, there is a 5-acre minimum requirement for
the C2 zone and that will not work for the Automobile Emissions Center. He also stated that the
hours of operation would be 7 a.m. – 6 p.m. to keep the impact on the residential area’s
surrounding the land in question to a minimum. Mr. Cox stated that his thought is the Credit
Union currently located in the lot next door has more traffic in and out than the emissions center
will. Mr. Cox then explained that the Staff had added lube into the application. He explained the
business in question is not looking to add a lube service. Jody stated this was an error and she
would correct the amendment.

Commissioner Winters asked Jody if the City Council’s intent in stopping future RB zoning was
to reduce the impact on existing neighboring homes. He then asked if that was the main idea or if
there was other intent as well. Jody explained the Council was reviewing the city zoning a few
years ago when they decided to stop future RB zones, however, she was not sure what the
reasoning behind that decision was. Commissioner Winters then stated that he would like to see
the impact on the residential zones surrounding this lot be kept to a minimum, however, he does
not want that lot to remain vacant. Commissioner Fuller stated that this business would better fit
in a C2 zone as opposed to revising the RB zone. He also feels an acre is too large for just an
emissions center and more businesses would be added to that lot causing more impact to the
residential zone. Commissioner Wood agreed and stated her feelings are the RB zone was meant
to protect residential areas.

   Motion: Commissioner McEwen motioned for approval of ZT-2-2020. This motion was not
   seconded.

   Commissioner Fuller motioned for denial of ZT-2-2020. Commissioner Wood seconded this
   motion.

   Jody explained that is the application is denied the applicant will need to appeal the denial to
   the City Council. She stated if the applicant does not appeal this application will end with the
   denial of the Planning Commission.

C-2-2020
Mapleleaf Cabinets
2554 S. 3400 W.
M Zone (2.5 Acres)

The applicant, EA Architecture, representing Mapleleaf Cabinets, is requesting a conditional use
for an expansion of warehousing and manufacturing for property located at 2554 S. 3400 W.
This property is zoned M, manufacturing and the West Valley City General Plan designates this
area as Light Industrial. The surrounding zone to the north, east and southeast is M,
manufacturing and to the west and southwest the property is zoned R-1-6(7), single unit
residential. The use to the north is RSK (atv repair shop), Cuban Cigar Inc. (manufacturing) is
located to the east, Engineered Steel Structures (Metal Fabricator) is to the southeast. There are
existing residential homes to the west and southwest.

This property was originally developed in 1993 as a millwork shop and office location for
Westwood Mill. The current tenant is Mapleleaf Cabinets and they manufacture commercial,
retail and industrial fixtures and millwork for companies ranging from Banana Republic to
grocery displays. The office hours for this use are M-F from 8am - 5pm and the shop is open M-
Th from 6am - 4:30 pm.

There is currently a 15,000 sqft two-story building on site and Mapleleaf Cabinets would like to
expand their facility to add an additional 12,438 sqft of warehouse and 14,067 sqft or workshop
space. The new building additions will be pre-finished metal panels that tie into the existing
brick/block building. This use is not required to meet the Commercial Design Standards. There is
an existing dust collection system on the west side of the building that will be redone and
reconstructed within the new building addition which will significantly reduce the amount of
noise that is produced from the business.

The loading docks are located on the east side of the building away from the residential uses.
There will be overhead doors along the west side of the warehouse, but these will primarily be
for access to service the equipment inside. Due to the proximity to residential this area will not
be allowed to be used for any truck loading or unloading.

There must be a minimum of 5% landscaping on site which consists of 50% live plant material.
The minimum required setback adjacent to residential is 20’ and this setback must include a
minimum of one tree every 30’. This area of the site will not be open to or visible by the public
so they have asked that the shrub requirements for this area be relocated to the front portion of
the building that will be redeveloped and improved to create more of an entrance feature for the
site. Staff would work with the applicant on the specific design and ensure that the minimum 5%
landscaping requirement is met.

There is an existing wall adjacent to the residential properties on the west and south side of the
site, so no additional screening is required at this time. There is a section of the wall that is 10’

           5. Must meet the requirements of all affected departments and agencies.
           6. Subject to review upon valid complaint.
