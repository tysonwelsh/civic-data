COVID-19 Rescinding Action Plan and Authorizing New Plan

               Nathan Bracken stated he has put together a resolution to reflect decisions made
during the July 7, 2020, emergency Council meeting.

               Council Member Pazell, seconded by Council Member Stitzer, moved to ratify the
following resolution:

RESOLUTION NO. 20-07-01                                              Date: July 15, 2020

       A RESOLUTION OF THE COPPERTON METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL
       CLARIFYING ACTIONS AND PROCEDURES TO ADDRESS CONFIRMED
       CASES OF COVID19 WITHIN COPPERTON

       WHEREAS, on March 13, 2020, the Copperton Metro Township Council (the “Council”)
adopted an action plan and declared a public health emergency for the coronavirus disease of
2019 (COVID-19) pursuant to Chapter 2.86 of the Copperton Metro Township Code; and

       WHEREAS, the Council worked with the Salt Lake County Department of Health to
develop the action plan, which was based upon the best available information at the time;

       WHEREAS, following the Council’s adoption of the action plan, additional information and
resources have become available regarding COVID19 and the circumstances involving the virus
have also changed; and

        WHEREAS, the Council desires to rescind the action plan and develop a new plan to
better account for the current circumstances involving COVID 19 and the most recent information.

        NOW, THEREFORE, IT IS HEREBY RESOLVED, effective immediately by the Copperton
metro Township Council that the action plan adopted on March 13, 2020 is rescinded and the
Council and all applicable Copperton personnel are authorized and instructed to develop a new
action plan for the Council to consider.

       APPROVED and ADOPTED this 15th day of July, 2020.

CARES Act Funding Agreement

                Mayor Clayton stated the County has allocated $23,000 to Copperton for Covid
expenses. The money has been approved for metro townships and it must be used by October.
If not spent, the funds will be returned. Copperton would like to use it for Covid expenses other
than small businesses. The Municipal Services District should help Copperton small businesses
because the County retained $40 million for that purpose.

               Council Member Stitzer, seconded by Council Member Bailey, moved to approve
the following agreement:

RESOLUTION NO. 20-07-02                                               Date: July 15, 2020

       A RESOLUTION OF THE COPPERTON METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL
       APPROVING AN AGREEMENT BETWEEN COPPERTON AND SALT LAKE
       COUNTY TO RECEIVE $23,364.68 FROM THE SALT LAKE COUNTY CARES
       ACT FUND FOR CERTAIN COVID RELIEF ACTIVITIES

                                          RECITALS

   A. The Copperton Metro Township (“Copperton”) is a municipality pursuant to Utah Code
      Annotated (“UCA”) §§ 10-2a-401 et seq.;

   B. The Copperton Metro Township Council (the “Council”) is the municipal governing body
      for Copperton pursuant to UCA § 10-3b-501;

   C. Salt Lake County (the “County”) is a County pursuant to UCA Title 17: COUNTIES;

   D. The CARES Act is a law passed by the Federal Government that provided for an
      appropriation of funds to the States and certain Counties for certain governmental
      emergency costs and economic relief efforts related to the COVID-19 pandemic;

   E. In May 2020, the State of Utah and Salt Lake County received their CARES Act
      appropriations from the Federal Government;

   F. In late May 2020, the County, taking a cue from the State regarding the management of
      CARES Act Funds, and not compelled by the CARES Act to do so, decided it would send
      amounts of their appropriation to the municipalities of the County to fund and manage local
      COVID-19 efforts;
DATE          WEDNESDAY                                JULY                            15, 2020

   G. Pursuant to the County’s desire to promote local engagement in the COVID-19 relief effort,
      the Council and Copperton’s legal counsel have received and reviewed the attached
      agreement for consideration and possible approval of Copperton; and

   H. In addition to providing funding to municipalities, the County will retain a portion of the
      CARES Act funding it has received to operate a program that will assist businesses
      affected by COVID19, including businesses within Copperton.

                                        RESOLUTION

      THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED, by the Copperton Metro Township Council effective
immediately:

   1. The attached agreement authorizing Copperton to receive $23,364.68 of the County’s
      appropriation of the CARES Act funding is approved and Copperton assumes all of the
      Federal regulatory requirements, reporting rules, advertising costs, and possible future
      liabilities of the use of these funds as set forth in the Agreement;

   2. The Mayor and Council will coordinate with the County to educate Copperton businesses
      regarding the County’s CARES Act program and will assist businesses, as needed, in
      applying for assistance from the County;

   3. All requested expenditures of CARES Act funding that Copperton receives directly shall
      require approval of the Council prior to the distribution of the funds; and

   4. The Mayor and Council will expend the CARES Act funds that Copperton receives as
      follows:

          a. Reimbursement of Act-Qualified expenses incurred by the Unified Fire Authority
             and Unified Police Department as Copperton’s first-responder agencies;

          b. Reimbursement of Act-Qualified expenses incurred by the Greater Salt Lake
             Municipal Services District as Copperton’s municipal-type services provider; and

          c. Reimbursement of any other Act-Qualified expenses that Copperton incurs directly
             responding to the COVID-19 pandemic.

      APPROVED and ADOPTED this 15th day of July, 2020.

UPD Update

               Council Member Pazell stated Taylorsville submitted their intent to leave the
Unified Police Department (UPD). Midvale is staying for the time being. Sheriff Rosie Rivera has
started discussion on what this departure means for such things as pooled services. There is a
DATE            WEDNESDAY                                 JULY                            15, 2020

push to remove Sheriff Rivera as executive officers of UPD. He does not support that. There is
another meeting tomorrow.

                Council Member Bailey asked what will happen with pooled services and if
Taylorsville will contract with UPD for pooled services.

               Council Member Pazell stated Taylorsville may contract with UPD but it can go
anywhere to get those services. With the city leaving, Copperton will lose a lot of those services
as well. The pooled services are a bonus, not a guarantee.

                Nathan Bracken stated the UPD board voted to approve weighted voting. Under
the terms of the interlocal agreement, once the board approves an amendment, the member cities
need to ratify the amendment. There is some uncertainty on what would happen if Copperton did
not ratify the amendment. There may be some discussion about rescinding the vote because the
amendment was passed quickly to appease Taylorsville and Midvale. Weighed voting was a
symptom of other underlying concerns. Those concerns are still prompting member entities to
consider leaving. It may make sense to wait on ratifying this amendment to see what happens at
tomorrow’s meeting.

He did not know what effect Midvale and Taylorville leaving will have on Copperton. He
recommends the Council commission a feasibility study to give them some independent ideas
and other options.

He and other members are also concerned about having the Sheriff as CEO. The metro
townships think that is fine, but some cities have an issue with that. If the Sheriff is removed, the
County will pull its funding from UPD.

             Council Member Pazell stated he will be discussing with some of the smaller
communities whether they will be revisiting some issues discussed in the last meeting.

             Mayor Clayton stated he would reach out to staff and see if they can put
something together.

The matter was tabled until next month.

        Legislative Research Committee

               Nathan Bracken stated during the special legislative session, the legislature
amended the Open Public Meetings Act by amending the anchor location section. Previously, a
public meeting had to have an anchor location. But now, if an anchor location is not possible
(such as online meetings due to the pandemic), the Council Chair is required to read a statement
and sign a declaration explaining why. The Copperton Metro Township Council will be doing that
going forward for all these electronic meetings.
