DISCUSSION:       Steve P. presented an updated plan from Edge Homes that adds more parking stalls and
                  removes the single-family homes. There was discussion among the Commissioners
                  about the parking and the loss of single-family homes.

                  Jon Springmeyer, from West Valley City Economic Development, presented a
                  PowerPoint about the potential for commercial on the subject property. The ED
                  Department hasn’t received regional commercial proposals for the property that its
                  large size calls for. Most major retailers are within a 15-minute drive. There is minimal
                  potential on this property due to it being mid-block on Redwood Road. Residential
                  development will bring in increased property and sales taxes compared to a large
                  retailer in a less populated area.

                  Chair Fuller asks if the new residential property taxes offset the demands on City
                  services. Jon replies that yes, property taxes cover City services and sales tax is bonus
                  income. Chair Fuller stated that he remembers in the 1990’s the request was for more
                  rooftops but mostly apartments were built, and the retail didn’t increase. Jon confirms
                  that owner occupied housing usually has a higher discretionary spending than rental
                  housing. Steve Pastorik adds that regional players, like Home Depot, Lowes, WinCo,
                  Costco, Walmart and Target are all within 15 minutes. You can get just about anywhere
                  in the entire City in that drive time.

                  Commissioner McEwen asked when Sage Valley asked businesses to take the lots
                  around Redwood and 4100 S, if that was based on their desire to do so and did the City
                  not have any luck bringing in businesses for those lots? Is there a demand that we
                  would be able to bring in businesses for pad sites if we were to have them on this piece
                  of land upfront by Redwood Road? Jon replies there is minimal demand and it’s for
                  retail that is already in the 5 minutes’ drive time. He also points out that Sage has a
                  hard corner lot.

                  Commissioner Wood commented that she appreciates the information. She was leaning
                  toward commercial and wondered why a store like Walmart didn’t want to build there.
                  She learned from Jon that this property is better for residential. She knows the property
                  is going to be developed somehow and she doesn’t like the look of strip malls. She
                  thanked Commissioner McEwen for asking Jon to put together his presentation.

                  Commissioner Woodruff talked about the recognizable cities and development in the
                  County and doesn’t feel this project is special and memorable to honor the site. He’d
                  like to see higher-density builders and more open spaces, like Daybreak. Commissioner
                  Fuller asked Commissioner Woodruff if he’s wanting apartments, that’s the only way
                  to get higher- density homes. Commissioner Woodruff would like higher-density
                  homes that can be purchased by first-time homebuyers. Commissioner Wood replied
                  that Daybreak is nice but that’s not a 26-acre lot.

                  Commissioner McEwen thinks the Commission has requested improvements based on
                  the City’s zoning and ordinances, such as parking, aesthetically pleasing
                  amenities, and landscaping in between the driveways. He’s never looked at any
