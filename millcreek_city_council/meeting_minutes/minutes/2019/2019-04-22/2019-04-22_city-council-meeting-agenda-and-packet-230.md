1. Review Proposed Sales Tax Revenue Bonds, Series 2019 Financing Structure; Marc
Edminster, Lewis Young Robertson & Burningham, Inc.
Mayor Silvestrini said Millcreek is considering, as part of the city center master plan, putting
a park along the fault line and in order to do that, Millcreek would need to acquire money.
The conversation with Marc Edminster would be about securing a bond on sales tax revenue
in the amount of $20 million. This bond would be repaid by money generated from the
community reinvestment area as it develops. The current community reinvestment area fund
would pay on the interest until revenue is generated.

Marc Edminster said Lewis Young Robertson & Burningham, Inc. had been retained as
Millcreek’s financial advisor. They work with cities on securing bonds with the lowest
interest rate possible. He said the bonding process is interesting and educational. He said
various professionals will come together to structure the bond advantageously for the City. He

Millcreek City Council Meeting Minutes 22 April 2019 Page 2 of 10

went through a draft calendar of the bonding process. The Council starts the process by
adopting a parameters resolution which set limits of the bond per state law. There will then be
a public hearing to get citizen input. The Council will appoint a pricing committee to be
empowered to a make the final decision on the issuance of bonds. The super parameters
resolution allows more flexibility when the bonds are sold. Mayor Silvestrini said Mondays
are bad days to sell bonds, so instead of convening as a Council to make the determination,
they will delegate pulling the trigger to the pricing committee. Edminster said the committee
is limited to the approved parameters. Once that starts, a series of events take place which will
be coordinated by Lewis Young Robertson & Burningham. They will schedule a time to meet
with bond rating agencies. They anticipate the City will get an investment grade rating, the
better the rating, the lower the interest rate on bonds. The agencies will then announce the
rating. The bond underwriters will have a better idea on price and a bond sale takes place. The
pricing committee then accepts or rejects the underwriters’ offer and then the bonds close.

Mayor Silvestrini said property prices could be affected by adopting the city center master
plan. Mike Winder said he was working with appraisers on relocating Plowgian Auto to
Highland Drive, but it could not happen until it was funded. Mayor Silvestrini said developers
were waiting to see if Millcreek was going to follow through with the master plan. He said the
public comment on the city center at the Millcreek second adversary open house in January
was overwhelmingly positive. Council Member Marchant asked about the city center going
from 3300 S. to the north city boundary. Winder said the community reinvestment area
includes the entire area to the boundary on the north, but the fault line park goes north to
Miller Avenue. Mayor Silvestrini said he had talked about dividing the park into two phases,
to bond first for acquiring property south of Woodland Avenue to 3300 S. Council Member
Marchant asked about acquiring another bond for properties north of Woodland Avenue.
Winder said it depended on where the City was at at that stage. Mayor Silvestrini said there
would be a public hearing on the bond in June, but the Council would not make a final
decision until July. Edminster said it takes about 30-45 days to issue a bond.

Winder said the city center small area plan will go through the community councils and
Planning Commission in May. Staff is working hard to meet with property owners and
appraisers to figure out what it would take to pull everything together. The park space will be
a catalyst for the rest of the city center to redevelop. Mayor Silvestrini said Plowgian Auto
and other property owners in the area need to know what will happen. Council Member
Jackson asked what the cost to Millcreek would be. Edminster said the $20 million figure was
a preliminary estimate. He said the residents may think about a general obligation bond,
which requires a public vote, but this is not that. This bond is backed by a pledge of city sales
tax revenue receipts. Laurie Johnson said based on projections, up front there is not enough
tax increment to cover the debt service payments, but it could cover the interest for the first
few years. Then the tax increment would begin to pay for the debt service. The goal of the
bond is to be paid through community reinvestment area tax increment. Johnson said the City
may loan from the general fund to the community reinvestment area and it would be repaid.
Mayor Silvestrini said the condition under which lenders would loan the money to the City is
based on the fact that it will be paid by community reinvestment area tax increment, but it will
be backed up by the City’s sales tax. There is risk and the potential for making cuts on capital
improvements for a time to meet that obligation, but it is a great opportunity.

Council Member Marchant asked about the range of rating. Edminster anticipated the rating
would be AA to AA+. Council Member Marchant asked about the interest rate of a AA bond
with today’s pricing. Edminster projected a rate of 3.15%, based on the assumption that this
would be calculated on a 20-year amortization. Winder said twenty years matches the life of
the community reinvestment area. The interest rate would be locked in for the twenty years.
Winder said the State and County is rated at a AAA. Edminster said Utah is highly thought of
by rating agencies. Winder said this bond takes the potential of the community reinvestment
area to be able to enjoy the benefit today. Council Member Jackson asked about how to select
a bond council. Edminster said there are firms in Utah that specialize in work as bond
attorneys and his firm can go through an RFP process to acquire them. John Brems said for
services, the Council can pick who they want instead of going through the RFP process. He
said with the tax anticipation revenue bonds, the bond council should know everything about
the City, so it does not have to be reexplained to another firm. Mayor Silvestrini said Gilmore
Bell currently does work for the City. The Council directed Edminster to get the bond council
to start drafting documents.

2. Discussion with District 4 County Council Member Ann Granato
Mayor Silvestrini acknowledged County Council Member Shireen Ghorbani in the audience.
He said he wanted to discuss the Salt Lake County public works contract. He said he has had
meetings with Salt Lake County Mayor Jenny Wilson and other cities that are contracted with
the County for public works and there is concern about the budget for that contract service for
the next fiscal year. Millcreek wants to continue with the County’s service, but the $1.7
million price increase was not sustainable. He said he thought staff had come to an
arrangement where Millcreek will be increasing payment by about $1 million, a 24% increase,
and not a 44% increase as the County previously requested. Mayor Silvestrini said the
Municipal Services District (MSD) was paying a fixed cost and the City was paying an
incremental cost. He said the City is not trying to ride on the back of the MSD, but Millcreek
needs a contract that meets the residents’ needs and that the City can afford.

County Council Member Ann Granato said she met with Holladay and their concern was
taking the financial hit all at once instead of over time. Mayor Silvestrini concurred with
Holladay’s assessment and said because Millcreek is buying a service, the budget should be
more transparent and detailed so that Millcreek knows what it is getting. He said Millcreek
would be paying $200,000 per full-time employee to plow roads in the winter and Millcreek
should know what those employees are doing in the summer. The Council wants to know they
are getting the benefit of what is being paid for. He said the administrative overhead for the
contract was exorbitant department wide at $3.5 million and Millcreek’s portion was
$760,000. He said an element of that cost goes to the County Council and $300,000-400,000
goes to the Mayor’s administration. Millcreek wants to get what is being paid for on public
works specific things per that contract. County Council Member Granato clarified that the
Council wanted single line items in the budget broken into multiple line items. Mayor
Silvestrini concurred and said the expenses of the County Council and County Mayor should
be paid out of the general fund except for specific things relating to municipal services. He
asked what the County Council and County Mayor were contributing to the public works
department. County Council Member Granato asked about duplication of administrative
services in Millcreek’s budget. Mayor Silvestrini said he doubted there was but was willing to
have the discussion, he said Millcreek has not burdening the County Council or Mayor’s

2. Financial Matters
2.1 Presentation of FY 2019-20 General Fund & Capital Improvement Project Fund
Tentative Budget; Laurie Johnson, HR-Finance Director
Mayor Silvestrini said the Council would look at the budget that was put together by
staff. He said by the Council tentatively adopting it, the public will have a chance to look
at it and then there will be a public hearing for the public to make comment, then it will
be adopted. Mayor Silvestrini said Millcreek enacted a property tax for the first time last
fall to pay for the Unified Police Department (UPD) contract. Millcreek shifted the tax
from the former taxing district, Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area
(SLVLESA). The Council was careful to select a tax rate that would cover only the
contract and the cost of borrowing to finance the contract. He then went over the revenue
highlights of the tentative budget and said this budget contemplates no property tax
increase. Millcreek has had a 97% property tax collection rate in 2018 and the police
contract constitutes 41% of the total budget. Millcreek has collected in excess of
$860,000 from new growth and a high collection rate which will cover an increase in the
UPD contract. He said the City mainly functions on sales tax, and staff is projecting a 2%
increase over the previous year, which also constitutes 41% of the total budget. The State
ranks the collection of sales tax for various jurisdictions and Millcreek was 13" in the
State in the amount of additional sales taxes collected this year over the previous year.
The remaining 8% of revenue is fees for services, such as business license fees and
building permit fees.

Mayor Silvestrini then highlighted the expense budget. Millcreek currently has 38
fulltime employees and will not be hiring additional employees this year. The budget
allows for a 1.5% cost of living adjustment (COLA) for employees and they are eligible
for 2.5% merit increase. There will be a 6.68% increase for medical insurance costs. The
UPD contract will be covered 100% by property tax collections and that contract will
increase by about 5%. Salt Lake County requested a $1.7 million increase for the public
works contract over the current year. Millcreek negotiated that figure down and the
projected increase will be $968,750 for the same level of service. Mayor Silvestrini
expressed dissatisfaction with transparency of that budget, the amount of increase in one
year (44%), and that the department has an overhead of $3.5 million a year. He said he
has asked staff for a study to determine the best public works scenario for Millcreek. He
said he wanted to make sure Millcreek was getting the value for the County’s snow
plowing. Some noteworthy budget items are the cost to join the Central Wasatch
Commission for $75,000, the cost of shared expenses for a sustainability coordinator that
serves three cities for $15,000, municipal election expenses with Salt Lake County for
$130,000, and an event coordinator contract for $30,000. Millcreek has been able to
negotiate lower costs for legal services provided by Salt Lake County. There will be no
additional vehicles purchased. Millcreek must maintain 5% of the general fund in the
reserve account, but not more than 25%. The fund contemplates a projected 15.68% left
in fund balance, which is about $4 million. There have been unexpected expenditures
with respect to storm drain failures that have diverted money from the Capital
Improvement Project (CIP) fund. The CIP fund will have a budget of $1,751,000, which
decreased by 44% from the previous year due to the increased costs of the public works
operations contract with Salt Lake County. The CIP fund includes: $350,000 for
pavement preservation, $355,000 for sidewalk projects, $100,000 for traffic calming,
$700,000 for storm drain emergency repairs, $150,000 for transportation grant matches,
and $96,000 for lighting.

The Mayor went over the budget calendar; April 22", presentation and tentative adoption

adoption of the final budget and certified tax rate. He then went over Millcreek service

additional taxes.
