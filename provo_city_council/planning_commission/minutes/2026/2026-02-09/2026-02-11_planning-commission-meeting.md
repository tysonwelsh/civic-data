BACKGROUND
The applicant has submitted a project plan along with a rezone request for properties
located at approximately 1507 S 180 E to allow for a new data center facility to be built.
The property is currently zoned as Planned Industrial Commercial (PIC) and they are
requesting to combine it with the Data Center (DC) overlay zone. The DC overlay can
only be applied to property in the PIC of Light Manufacturing (M1) zones. This is a new
overlay zone, and this is the first property requesting this zone.

Staff have been working with the applicants to address the standards in Planned
Industrial Commercial (PIC) zone and the requirements in the proposed Data Center
(DC) overlay zone. The original plans for this site were to have a data center with onsite
power generation. The opportunity came to acquire power from the city grid instead of
generating power onsite and this has been seen as a favorable alternative because it
will not create the environmental concerns that came with generating power onsite. The
applicant has proffered a development agreement to restrict power for the data center to
only come from the grid and that the data center will utilize a closed-loop water system
for non-consumptive purposes that would not exceed 4,380,000 gallons per year.

The adjacent property to the east is currently zoned Public Facilities (PF) and the
property to the north, south and east, is zoned Planned Industrial Commercial (PIC) and
the property to the north west is Regional Shopping Center (SC3).

STAFF ANALYSIS
Sec. 14.020.020(2) establishes criteria for the amendments to the zoning title as
follows: (Staff response in bold type)
       Before recommending an amendment to this Title, the Planning
       Commission shall determine whether such amendment is in the interest of
       the public, and is consistent with the goals and policies of the Provo City
       General Plan. The following guidelines shall be used to determine
       consistency with the General Plan:
       (a) Public purpose for the amendment in question.
Staff response: The amendment would allow for a data center that would not
generate onsite power or consume large amounts of water. There is growing need
for data centers in the state.
       (b) Confirmation that the public purpose is best served by the amendment
       in question.
Staff response: Power will come from the city grid and water used for cooling will
be part of a closed-loop system. They proposed partnerships with local
universities for data center technician training and paid apprenticeship programs.
They will also hire a local workforce for as many operations and maintenance
employment positions as possible.

BACKGROUND
The applicant has submitted a project plan along with a rezone request for properties located at
approximately 1507 S 180 E. The project plan proposes removal of the existing building and the
construction of a new data center facility. The new two-story facility will have a building footprint
of 66,000 and a total of 132,000 square feet.

The attached site plan shows another building that was going to be used to house generators
for onsite production of power to run the data center. The plans for power have changed since
the time this was originally submitted, and they are now going to be acquiring all the power from
Provo City. So a building for onsite power generation is no longer needed. The applicant will
provide updated plans for staff approval, and this is included in the recommended staff
conditions.

The adjacent property to the east is currently zoned Public Facilities (PF) and the property to the
north, south and east, is zoned Planned Industrial Commercial (PIC) and the property to the
north west is Regional Shopping Center (SC3).

Staff have been working with the applicants to address the standards in Planned Industrial
Commercial (PIC) zone and the requirements in the proposed Data Center (DC) overlay zone.
Parking requirements are higher than what will be needed for a data center. The applicant has
provided a Transportation Demand Management (TDM) program (see attachment 7), and they
are asking the Planning Commission for a reduction in parking to be considered.

The proposed overlay zone does not allow data centers to be located within 200’ of a property
with a school. The property to the south of the proposed data center has a charter school. The
property with the school is owned by the same owner as the proposed data center and so they
plan to adjust the property lines on that property to be able to meet this requirement. Since the
data center will not be producing power onsite with potential environmental impacts and only
using backup generators for power outages and periodic testing, this approach seems
reasonable.

FINDINGS OF FACT
1. CRC approval is still needed for the project plan.
2. The parking requirement by code is 297 stalls (will be fewer with building square foot
   reduction in updated plans) and 72 are provided.
3. An approved TDM by the Planning Commission would allow for a parking reduction.
4. Existing parcels would need to be combined into one parcel. An application has already
   been submitted but still needs CRC approval.
5. PIC zone allows 55’ in height and the proposed building height is 55’.

STAFF ANALYSIS
Updated plans showing the removal of the onsite power generation facilities that were initially
part of the plan will be needed for staff approval. Onsite parking for the data center will be much
less than is required by code. Parking ratios are based on the closest related land use listed in
the parking chapter. Since data centers are not listed, warehouse parking ratios would be the

Since there will not be power generation onsite, and they will be relying on power from the grid,

Noise and vibration information and calculation were provided when onsite power generation
was anticipated, and they showed how they would be able to control those to meet city noise
ordinance standards. Now that onsite power generation is not happening these will be greatly
reduced. The noise ordinance allows up to 75 dBA at property line, both day and night at
property line of industrial property. They will be less than 65 dBA at property line (especially
without a power plant now). They will use acoustic louvers, generator enclosures, and noise
isolation walls to mitigate sound to meet Provo City Code. Their vibration estimates are <0.05
in/s peak particle velocity. Research showed this as a very low, typically negligible level of
vibration, well below the threshold for human annoyance or limits for structural damage. For
mitigation they will use vibration isolation pads, floating slab design, and structural dampening
systems for chillers and rotating equipment

The overlay zone requires an end of project mitigation plan for proper removal of project
equipment. They have committed to developing a plan to ensure the responsible disposal of any
equipment that may have been utilized by the project to eliminate any negative impact on Provo
City and so this has been included in the recommended conditions of approval.

Staff recommend approval of the project plan with conditions noted in this report. With the

On behalf of B+F Timpanogos Tech Center, LLC (“B+F”), we are submitting this formal outline
regarding the initial phase of the proposed data center located at 1507 South 180 East, Provo,

Power and Emissions

The current project proposal is designed to be powered exclusively by Provo Power’s municipal
electrical grid. Under standard operating conditions, the facility’s emissions profile will be
comparable to that of a standard commercial office building. The project will utilize emergency
backup generators; however, these will remain inactive except during mandatory annual
maintenance testing or in the event of a total loss of city grid power. The total emission
producing equipment is anticipated to produce nearly identical volumes of PM 2.5, PM 10, NOx,
VOC and CO2e/year as a typical 130,000 square foot office building, which would be produced
by the following equipment:

   •   Cooling Plant Modules (Chillers): ~ 3-6

   •   CRAH/CRAC / data hall air units: ~ 50-150

   •   Tankless water heaters (gas): ~ 2-8

   •   Emergency generators: ~ 4-10

Energy efficiency measures are of paramount importance to the applicant. Lighting, HVAC and
general office energy efficiencies will be implemented to allow for the maximum amount of
power to be delivered to the data center servers and graphic processing units (GPUs). The data
center components will be ultra-condensed to maximize the output of the GPUs.

As the current project proposal to be considered by Provo City relies exclusively upon Provo
Power’s municipal electrical grid, the applicant is not proposing any self-generated renewable
power source for the project.

The data center project proposed by B+F is significantly different than many of the data center
projects that are frequently portrayed in the media. Contemporary data centers are regularly
pursuing hundreds of megawatts, if not gigawatts of energy to power million square feet data
hall structures. As electrical utility grids are increasingly strained, hyperscale data centers are
pursuing “self-generation” energy projects to provide their own power. B+F’s proposal is to rely
on Provo City’s municipal electrical grid where existing electricity can be readily supplied. Thus,
by comparison, B+F’s project represents a mere fraction of the energy consumption being
demanded by contemporary hyperscale facilities across the state.

While B+F, has engaged with Provo Power and UMPA to identify additional energy generation
opportunities, any future energy project would be presented to Provo City in a separate and
distinct application from the current application. Should B+F propose a separate and distinct
proposal for a joint energy project with Provo Power and UMPA in the future, a full
environmental analysis of said project including air emissions, water consumption, waste water
impact, and all other items required by Provo City Code 14.34A.050 will be provided to Provo
City for their full review and approval. However, this application does not include or authorize
any on-site power generation, nor propose the approval of any environmental impacts that might
be created thereby.

Water Consumption and Wastewater Impact

The projected water usage for Phase I is consistent with the demands of a typical office building
of similar scale. The data center will utilize a closed-loop cooling system. We currently anticipate
a daily consumption rate of approximately 7,000 to 12,000 gallons per day. This usage
supports both the facility's cooling requirements and domestic needs. The anticipated design of
the data center will have little to no impact on the Provo City wastewater system as the closed-
loop cooling system does not require cycling. Wastewater impacts would be limited to
comparable wastewater discharge to a similar sized commercial office building.

Fortunately, data center campuses across the western United States no longer construct
evaporative cooling systems due to the introduction of closed-loop cooling systems. Due to the
size differential between B+F’s project and these other hyper-scale facilities, the proposed water
use and wastewater impact by B+F will be a mere fraction of the water and wastewater demand
from a hyper-scale data center project.

Greetings,

I am writing shortly ahead of your meeting to discuss possible plans for a data center here
in Provo.

I strongly urge you NOT to move forward with new zoning to enable this to move forward. I
know your commission members like to argue that their zoning decisions are neutral and
are only made so far as to enable Provo City Council to make decisions. In this case,
however, it is clear to me that recommending zoning changes to be put forward would
simply set our city on a disastrous path.

Data centers will either compromise residents' electricity rates (which are already climbing
steadily year after year) or air quality, if the center decides to generate its own
electricity (often done with air polluting diesel fuel). Furthermore, noise concerns and
water usage, regardless of so-called closed-looped systems, are in my opinion not at all
worth the economic "benefits" (I don't think only 20 new jobs in the long run, post
construction, are at all worth the potential impacts).

Data centers will certainly have a place in our digital lives. However, our environment (with
inversion already a perennial concern) and water needs (which we already have already
heavily exceeded) state clearly that Provo is NOT the place for these centers.

Due to these concerns, I ask that you not allow these plans to move forward another inch.
Please vote NO on recommending these changes to the City Council and save us all the
headache.

Yours respectfully,

Paul Matthews | February 11, 2026
Data center proposal in provo

Members of the council,

I would like to voice my strong opposition to the proposal to build a datacenter at 1507 S
180 E in Provo. As a resident, and as a customer of Provo City Power, I cannot see any
significant public benefit to Provo with the addition of a data center.


---

The proposal submitted asserts that they will hire local community members, and they can
partner with local universities. This sounds great - all above board. And I agree, they
probably will. From an outsider's perspective, this sounds like it could be a valuable
addition to the city. The reality of their promise is far more nuanced. I have worked my
entire career in the computer industry. I have worked inside data centers, and utilized their
services on a personal and professional basis. The reality is this: data centers do not
require large workforces. If you have ever been to a data center or worked for one, they
have very few people on staff. Maintenance folks to maintain the facility and possibly one
or two data center techs on the clock at any given time to actually run the data center.

Paul Matthews | February 11, 2026
Data center proposal in provo
Members of the council,

I would like to voice my strong opposition to the proposal to build a datacenter at 1507 S 180 E in Provo. As a resident,
and as a customer of Provo City Power, I cannot see any significant public benefit to Provo with the addition of a data
center.

The proposal submitted asserts that they will hire local community members, and they can partner with local
universities. This sounds great - all above board. And I agree, they probably will. From an outsider's perspective, this
sounds like it could be a valuable addition to the city. The reality of their promise is far more nuanced. I have worked my
entire career in the computer industry. I have worked inside data centers, and utilized their services on a personal

                                                         Page 4 of 9


---

and professional basis. The reality is this: data centers do not require large workforces. If you have ever been to a data
center or worked for one, they have very few people on staff. Maintenance folks to maintain the facility and possibly
one or two data center techs on the clock at any given time to actually run the data center. Server hardware rarely
fails, but it does happen. When they do, you need someone around that can go fix it. As someone who has worked the
lonely day or night shifts at a datacenter housing tens of thousands of servers, there's not a lot of pressing work for a
single person to do. Data centers rarely employ a significant number of staff members to do anything other than menial
hardware work. These positions are generally entry-level positions, with very little opportunity for career advancement.

Reading the company's proposal, they project they are going to use between 30-50 megawatts of power, potentially
even more. As noted in the proposal, Provo City Power supplies about 175 megawatts of power. Further, the proposal is
going to use 100% of grid power for its operation. That means an additional 17 to 28 percent greater demand on the
grid. With greater demand, this will almost certainly result in an increase in residential power costs due to the effects of
a small few. Furthermore, this will almost certainly require an additional capital expenditure to expand and upgrade grid
capacity - for this singular facility - and who is going to pay for that? Everyone. All ratepayers of Provo City Power are
going to have to subsidize the upgrade of electrical infrastructure that benefits a single entity only. And even if - as asked
in the proposal - they cover the entire 66,000 square feet of the building in solar panels, they could only generate
around 10 megawatts of power under optimal conditions during the day. No matter what, the bulk of their demand will
be a significant portion of usage on the grid.

The company claims they will have less than 65dBA of noise at the property line, which falls within the noise ordinance.
The reality is this, you don't realize how much you value silence until it's gone. Have you ever lived near a freeway or
busy road? You eventually get used to it, but you really tend to notice how annoying it is when you actually go
somewhere without the constant hum of noise. And that's the problem. This is a datacenter. It doesn't shut down. The
annoying hum from this facility will become a thorn in everyone's sides. It's designed to run 24x7x365. All. The.
Time. You will be looking at a constant, neverending hum of noise for as long as this facility is in operation. It is also
noteworthy that this facility is across the street from not one, but two hotels, and a school. What kind of impact is that
going to have on them?

Finally, datacenters tend to utilize a significant amount of water. The proposal seems not to have a clear plan for what
they are going to do on the water and cooling front. It seems to propose anything from moderate water use to a closed-
loop system. On one end, the proposal calls for fairly hefty water usage. On the other - a closed-loop system, I can only
imagine that the power demands would increase even further - there is no free lunch fighting the laws of
thermodynamics. They can either vent water, which saves cooling costs; or they have to use air conditioning to cool the
water before it can be reused.

Please, members of the planning committee, I beseech you. Reject this proposal. This company, nor any other
datacenter proposed, has Provo's best interests in mind. They are seeking one thing, and one thing only: cheap electrical
power. While true, this is no hyperscale datacenter, it proposes a 25% increase in demand to Provo City Power. This will
almost certainly impact municipal customers, for the benefit of a small few. In addition, they will use a significant
amount of water, which we need to conserve.

Thank you,

-Paul Mathews
