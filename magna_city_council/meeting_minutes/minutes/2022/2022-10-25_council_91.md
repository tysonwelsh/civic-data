ORDINANCE NO. 2022-O-09                                                      Date: October 25, 2022

       AN ORDINANCE VACATING PUBLIC INTEREST IN A PORTION OF PLATTED
       STREETS WITHIN THE GATEWAYTO LITTLE VALLEY ROAD DEDICATION
       PLAT LOCATED WITHIN MAGNA METRO TOWNSHIP

       The legislative body of Magna Metro Township, State of Utah, ordains as follows:

      Section I: (1) A portion of the platted streets, which are more fully described in Exhibit A
and Exhibit B attached hereto and incorporated herein by reference, are hereby vacated.

      (2) The purpose of the vacation is to allow D.R. Horton Inc. and Little Valley Gateway
Owners Association, Inc. to incorporate the land into their existing parcels.

        (3) This ordinance is based upon a determination by the Magna Township Legislative
Body following a public hearing on October 25, 2022 that due and proper notice of the hearing to
vacate the platted street segments were duly given according to law; that no objection was made
to the proposed vacation; that good cause exists for the vacation; and that neither the public
interest nor any person will be materially injured by the vacation; that fair and adequate
consideration shall be provided; and that Magna has no present or foreseeable future public need
for the portion of the streets being vacated, or for any other public purpose.

       (4) Pursuant to Section 14.48.030 of the Magna Township Code of Ordinances, the
vacation of these streets are adequate compensation for Magna’s interest.

       (5) All right, title, and interest in and to the specified portions of the street being vacated
on Hallsville Drive, more fully described in Exhibit A, are to revert or otherwise be conveyed, by
operation of state law, Magna ordinances, to the abutting property owner, D.R. Horton, Inc.


                                                  4
DATE            TUESDAY                                   OCTOBER                           25, 2022

       (6) All right, title, and interest in and to the specified portions of the street being vacated
on Bowie Drive, more fully described in Exhibit B, are to revert or otherwise be conveyed, by
operation of state law, Magna ordinances, to the abutting property owner, Little Valley Gateway
Owners Association, Inc.

        (7) This ordinance shall have no force or effect to impair any other existing easements or
rights-of-way for public utilities, public franchises, irrigation, storm drainage, or other such
easements, as presently exist under, over, or upon the vacated portions of said streets, or as are
or may be shown on the official plats and records of the County.

       (8) The Salt Lake County Recorder is hereby directed to record this ordinance and
accompanying exhibits, and to make the necessary changes on the official plats and records of
the County to reflect said ordinance.

       SECTION II: This ordinance is effective upon posting as required by law.

       IN WITNESS WHEREOF, the Magna Council has approved, passed and adopted this
ordinance this 25th day of October, 2022.

            THIS BEING THE TIME heretofore set for a public hearing to receive public
comment regarding the following request:

File #OAM2022-000721 – Parker Rose is requesting a partial alley vacation near 2981 S 9200
W.

                Shad Cook, Planner, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District, reviewed the
application to vacate a portion of the alley near 2981 S 9200 W. The alley is not being used and
parts of it have already been fenced in by other property owners. In the Magna Metro Township
ordinance on conditions for vacation, it states that if the vacation of an alley, walkway, or trail
relieves the municipality from present or future obligation to maintain it, then that benefit is
adequate compensation for the property.

Intersection Realignment Project and Allocation of ARPA Funds

                Steven Kuhlmeier, Engineer, Engineering and Flood Control, stated the
intersection realignment at 2700 S 8000 W is a project that was included in the Master
Transportation Plan. Magna received $3,419,005 in federal funds from Wasatch Front Regional
Council’s (WFRC) Surface Transportation Program to complete this project. An environmental
study is required before a Federal Aid Agreement can be entered into for the grant funds. WFRC
does not cover the cost of the environmental study and it will cost Magna Metro Township
$100,000 if it chooses to proceed. The intersection realignment will require the condemnation of
three homes.

             Mayor Peay asked if Magna Metro Township will have to pay for costs above the
amount granted for if the project goes over.

              Mr. Kuhlmeier stated if the total amount is greater than the grant, the cost will
need to be paid for by Magna Metro Township.

Arbor Park

                Jevon Gibb, Salt Lake County Redevelopment Agency, reviewed the Arbor Park
apartment complex development. The developer is requesting an allocation from Magna Metro
Township of $5.9 million which would be a 13.5% return. The developer would absorb the risk of
collecting the amount of increment they are requesting, Magna Metro Township nor the RDA
would be responsible if they do not collect that amount. Increment will be collected through 2029.
If the Council decides to do a developer reimbursement agreement, regardless of what the cap is
set at, the reimbursement should not exceed what the project area collects. There should not be
an expectation of extension or modification of the project area.

                Dale Christiansen, Developer, stated he is committed to this project and the ability
to perform is there. He is asking for $5.9 million which would be the max and he is taking the risk
of that being generated by the RDA. The sooner the development is built, the sooner the tax
increment kicks in. He is not asking for an extension of the RDA or for affordable housing funds
that are derived from the development. He wants to bring a development to Magna that it is proud
of. If he does not get the assistance the project will have to be put on hold until circumstances
change, the RDA would end, and the development would not happen.

                Michael Jensen stated a CDA for Arbor Park was done at the end of 2014 and
still has some store front that are not leased out. Part of the reason why the amount went from
$4.8 million to $5.9 million is because of the two available commercial pads. There are two
businesses interested in the location. If those businesses come online, it will bring in more
assessed valuation. If the development does not get built to the same level, the assessed
valuation will not be there, and that risk goes to the developer.

                Council Member Barney asked is the intent for the requested funds is to be used
to build out the apartment area and sell the two commercial areas.

                Mr. Christiansen stated the requested funds is going to be used for the multi-
family project. There are some retail developers that have national retailers that they can bring in.
Those retail developers are only interested in the commercial pads if there is multi-family
development going in. The funds will not be going directly to the retail but would benefit it.

              Council Member Barney stated he is concern with long term maintenance and
value as a community. There have been developments built, sold, and then the maintenance
declines. What is the quality and longevity of the materials being used?

               Mr. Christiansen stated they would own and operate the complex. Materials that
are aesthetically pleasing and last a long time are used.
