   a. Request for Encroachment Permit, for stone wall at 2172 Tyler Avenue. (Attachment A)
      Ms. Olson reported this request is to allow a sloped rock landscape retaining wall which would
      encroach 19’ into the public right-of-way which would replace an existing cement retaining wall
      which was in the same location which had previously been approved. The proposal would also
      relocate the walkway and stairs in order to allow expansion of the existing driveway, where a
      parking stall would also encroach 3’ into the public right-of-way, In considering an encroachment
      permit, the Commission is to determine there is a good cause for the encroachment and it will
      not interfere with vehicular and pedestrian traffic.

      She reviewed the proposal, stating the right-of-way on Tyler is 80’ in width, but it has been
      constructed as a local street, leaving right-of-way to extend 19’ 6” east of the curb. Because of
      the slope and in order to maximize yard space, many homes in this area have installed retaining
      walls. Staff feels the existing slope and the excessive right-of-way creates a good cause for the
      encroachment, and traffic will not be affected. Approval of the parking would require a variance
      to be approved. Staff recommendation is for approval subject to all staff review comments and
      ordinance requirements being satisfied, and the applicant obtain a variance for the extension of

   front yard parking proposed.
   MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Garner to recommend approval of the
             encroachment permit as proposed based on the findings there is good cause for the

5. Request for Encroachment Permit, to allow awning at the Monarch Building,455 25th Street.
   (Attachment G)

  As the applicant was not yet present, Mr. Spencer described the project, indicating a gate has been
  installed on the Monarch Building without permits, and also was not shown in any of the
  construction drawings for the project. Because it extends over the public right-of-way when it is
  open, an encroachment permit is required. The Commission is to consider whether there is good
  cause for the encroachment, as well as to determine it does not interfere with pedestrian or
  vehicular traffic. He stated as part of the plan, Staff requested an operating plan for the gate, stating
  the operation is manual and during its opening and closing, two cones with caution tape will be
  placed in front of the gate. He also stated a $2million insurance liability policy is required, and while
  there is one on file, it has expired and needs to be updated. He stated the gate acts as an awning
  when open, giving a 10’ clearance from the sidewalk and will not interrupt pedestrian traffic. The
  gate is attractive and fits with the character of the building, providing needed security at this entry
  point. Staff recommendation is for approval, subject to a building permit being acquired and
  complied with, an updated insurance policy be provided and all other staff recommendations being
  satisfied.

  Commissioner Stoker asked about access the building at this location due to its width. Mr. Spencer
  responded a man door exists near the gate, and the open gate is anticipated for events.
  Commissioner Boykin asked about the approval process. Mr. Spencer indicated it was installed
  without specific approval for the gate, while the overall plan for the building had been reviewed and
  inspected. Commissioner Garner clarified the review is only due to its extension into the public
  right-of-way.

  Mr. Thaine Fischer, 2444 Washington stated the building was accepted to the National Register in
  2012 and all work, including the gate has complied with the Secretary of Interior standards. He
  stated a gate had been in place at this location at that time, and it was always anticipated it would
  be replaced as part of the construction. While the original intent was for the gate to swing inward, a
  structural wall had been removed and existing windows exposed, and if it were to swing inward,
  those windows would be blocked. He stated the design and operation had been approved by the
  National Park Service, and he had not anticipated the overhead gate would require an encroachment
  permit. He stated he had previously obtained an encroachment permit for the patio, and it was an
  oversight that the gate had not previously been approved.

  Commissioner Safsten asked about the opening mechanism, and whether it was typical of the time
  period of the building. Mr. Fischer stated it was typical of that era, and felt the manual operation is
  exposed allowing it to be viewed. Chair Southwick felt the gate is an accent to the building and the
  encroachment to the airspace does not obstruct the public sidewalk.

  Commissioner Schade expressed concern the pylons may not provide adequate security,
  suggesting a person be stationed to control people from entering the building during its operation.

6. Request for 12th Street Overlay Exception, to allow 48 units at 1770 Canyon Road. (Attachment H)
7. Request for 12th Street Overlay Exception, to allow 138 units at 1770 Canyon Road. (Attachment I)

  Mr. Chris Haertel stated he is seeking approval for a multi-family project, taking advantage of the
  12th Street Corridor Overlay Zone regulations.

  Mr. Spencer stated the Commission previously approved the Canyon Way Subdivision which
  dedicates Maxfield Drive to connect to Canyon Road, splitting the property so it has now become
  two projects. The subdivision also vacates the old Canyon Road right-of-way which was recorded
  but not developed. This subdivision is required to be recorded prior to the site plan being approved.
  He reviewed the initial project which is on the west side of Maxfield, consisting of two residential
  buildings and a clubhouse. The area east of Maxfield includes six building containing 138 units and
  a tot lot.

  He reviewed the history of the property, stating it was added to the 12th Street Overlay zone in 2017,
  which allows higher density options than the underlying R-1-10 zone so long as specific design
  criteria are complied with. The Horace Mann Community Plan was adopted 2003 and gave direction
  for the development of a 12th Street Corridor Plan as well as extending Maxfield Drive to connect to

                                                                                           Page 6 of 20
Canyon Road. The 12th Street Corridor Plan then triggered a specific ordinance for properties facing
12th Street which provides options for future development so long as design standards are satisfied.
The Ordinance indicates any residential project consisting of more than 30 units requires Planning
Commission approval, at which time the Commission is to determine whether the proposed project
creates any impacts to the surrounding area and whether those impacts can be mitigated.

He then reviewed details of the plan, stating the ordinance limits building height to 38’ while the
elevations show the residential buildings at 42’ 6”. Entry doors are shown on both sides of the
buildings but there is no floor plan and it is unclear whether the minimum unit size of 800 sq. ft. is
satisfied. The Ordinance also indicates buildings are to be closer to the street, with a 20’ build-to
line, but an existing canal easement limits the placement of buildings and would be required at 30’
on that side, while buildings fronting Maxfield should be moved to the 20’ build-to line. He stated all
mechanical equipment is to be screened, as well as parking areas where vehicle lights would shine
into adjacent property. He stated the ordinance also requires a specific amount of trees and shrubs
per unit as well as a tree preservation plan. The species of both trees and shrubs must be shown on
the landscaping plan and a low growth ground cover will be needed to replace proposed rock on the
north side. He stated while this project is divided by the Maxfield right-of-way, it is to function as a
single project, with amenities provided for both sides. He stated assurances need to be in place that
residents of each side of the project continue to have access to all amenities.

He then reviewed specific items for the buildings to the east of Maxfield, indicating buildings are
shown at a 36.5’ setback where the ordinance requires a build-to line at 20’. He stated it is
anticipated that higher density residential projects would be on major traffic corridors such as 12th
Street, indicating an adequate buffer exists between the project and homes to the north, stating
there are 260’ between the first building and the existing home to the north. He stated connectivity
needs to be addressed with additional sidewalk for pedestrian access. Proposed fire hydrants need
to be relocated to be in the public right-of-way He suggested an additional 6’ paved trail be added as
an amenity to make the space more usable, and that the tot lot be relocated to be more centrally
located within the project. He listed conditions identified by Staff which need to be corrected and
recommended approval so long as these conditions can be satisfied. He further emphasized some
type of deed restriction or covenant should be in place to assure it remains as one project due to the
shared amenities.

Mr. Haertel stated this would have been one project if he were not required by the City to connect
Maxfield Drive to Canyon Road. He expressed a willingness to comply with Staff’s conditions of
approval for both projects.

8. Public Hearing, Petition to Amend Zoning Ordinance, to increase building height maximum in the
   DDR zone.

  Mr. Cameron Cook, representing Boyer, 1150 Depot Drive stated the industrial market is evolving
  and some users are desiring higher buildings than is now allowed in the DDR zone. He stated while
  the MRD has no building height limit, due to the number of existing employees, Boyer has not been
  comfortable marketing taller buildings in that area. The DDR zone currently limits building height to
  50’, and Boyer feels the best area for taller buildings is at the far northeast corner of the BDO and is
  requesting building height limits be revised for that area. He stated this area is somewhat isolated
  from the rest of the industrial park and screening is available. It also has less traffic and fewer
  employees.

  Mr. Simpson stated the request is to amend the height of buildings in the DDR zone, limited to the
  area east of Depot Drive and north of 400 North. He agreed this area is somewhat isolated from
  other existing uses, with no nearby residential uses. He reviewed the DDR District Plan, stating
  much of the Industrial Park is bounded by residential uses and the Ogden Nature Center. He also
  reviewed existing regulations in other industrial zones, stating many have no height limit. As much
  of the surrounding area is vacant, the height of a 90’ building would have little effect and would
  accommodate potential user of the industrial area. This area is isolated from the Ogden Nature
  Center and existing residential uses. The General Plan indicates both existing buildings and
  accommodating new development within the BDO and allow for growth of the industrial area. Staff
  recommendation is for approval of the amendment as proposed.

  Commissioner Schade noted adjacent property is in Harrisville City and asked if contact had been
  made relating to the relationship of the amendment to their future development plans. Mr. Simpson
  responded he had not contacted Harrisville City.

  future demand. He stated the existing development ha all been on the east end of the airport and
  the FAA approves terminal area forecast based on anticipated growth. He stated the long-term
  development anticipates potential development on the west end. While there are no existing utilities
  or road access from the west, it is a potential growth area. There are some existing land uses
  including a mobile home park adjacent to the airport which are affected by the noise of aircraft and

  alternative has been identified. He described the color coding on the preferred alternative map

  west. He stated the FAA would not provide funding until ridership increases anticipating this could
  occur within 5-6 years. Other areas are defined for maintenance, repair and overhaul for existing
  aircraft, which do work for HAFB. Potential connection to Frontrunner is also anticipated as well as
  other aircraft related industries in the private sector.
