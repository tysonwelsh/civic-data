         The purpose of the Joint Work Session was to discuss proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 budget amendments for the
City and Redevelopment Agency; proposed FY 2021–2025 Consolidated Plan Amendment #2 – CDBG–CV Funds; proposed FY
2020–2021 Annual Action Plan Amendment #2; proposed FY 2026–2030 Capital Improvement Plan; Council/Board business;
and hear Council/Board comments.

Agenda Review
       Chair White reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council meeting scheduled to begin at 5:00 p.m.

Proposed City Fiscal Year 2024–2025 Budget Amendment
          Finance Director Sorensen explained that the proposed FY2024-2025 budget amendment recognizes revenues and
appropriates funds totaling 118,395,800. A significant portion of the revenues comes from excess fund balance ($4,000,000),
interest income ($3,000,000), state and federal grants ($21,317,075), and bond proceeds ($77,000,000). The remaining revenues
come from donations, RAMP grants, loan proceeds, equipment replacement funds, and balances from various funds or retained
earnings ($13,078,725). Funding is proposed for Airport projects ($11,415,000), Union Station ($3,517,300), the canyon waterline
project ($87,549,375), Golf capital improvements ($750,000), and refuse trucks and other equipment ($4,934,175). The remaining
appropriations are for other projects and regular operational costs ($10,229,950). Mr. Sorensen used the aid of a PowerPoint
presentation to expound on the various funds in the City budget impacted by the budget opening as well as the source of funding
and the projects/programs for which the funds are being appropriated. He engaged in discussion with the Council throughout his
presentation to answer questions about various projects included in the budget opening. For a copy of the presentation in its
entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.

Proposed Redevelopment Agency (RDA) Fiscal Year 2024–2025 Budget
Amendment
        Finance Director Sorensen explained that Administration is proposing a budget amendment of $1,033,000 to increase the
FY2025 RDA budgets for the Airport ($480,000), Kiesel ($63,000), Adams ($140,000) and the RDA Housing Fund ($350,000).
Appropriations are for contractual services, corridor and environmental studies, arts programming, and the Quality Neighborhood
program, respectively.
        Board Administrator Eller-Smith indicated public hearings for both the City and RDA budget amendments will be
scheduled for May 20, 2025.
Proposed Fiscal Year 2021–2025 Consolidated Plan Amendment #2 –
CDBG–CV Funds; and
Proposed Fiscal Year 2020–2021 Annual Action Plan Amendment #2
        Community Development Director Smith explained as a requirement to receive federal funding from the U.S.
Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD), the City’s Community and Economic Development Department prepares
and submits to the Council what is known as a five-year Consolidated Plan (ConPlan) and an Annual Action Plan (AAP). The
AAP is used to outline how federal Community Development Block Grant funds, HOME funds and other federal and non-federal
program funds will be used by the City in the upcoming fiscal year. City Administration is proposing to amend both the Five-year
Consolidated Plan and the FY21 Annual Action Plan. The two are proposed to be amended together because they were originally
approved in 2020 as a combined document. Mr. Smith used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to identify the amendments
proposed with this update:
                  •        Modify the “Target Areas Public Improvements” project in the FY21-25 Five-Year Consolidated Plan.
                           o        Add language to the project scope that indicates the city will use HUD funds to “create,
                                expand, or enhance infrastructure and public facilities to help prepare for future Coronavirus
                                outbreaks.”
                           o        Re-allocate $500,000 CDBG-CV (covid) funding to Target Areas Public Improvements.
                           o        Increase goals to one project to be completed in FY2026.
                  •        The re-allocation of CDGB CV funding is proposed for Target Areas Public Improvements.
                       Administration has indicated that the intended use of these unused emergency funds will be for recreation
                       and park improvement projects, and that the proposed amendment is needed for the city to retain the
                       funding.

Proposed Fiscal Year 2026–2030 Capital Improvement Plan
          Council Senior Policy Analyst Burton explained that the Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) is reviewed and adopted each
year as a planning tool for major capital investments in the City’s physical plant over a rolling five-year period. Each year’s plan
is updated based on the changing needs of the City, funding opportunities, projects completed and other similar factors. During
this year’s budget process, proposed capital projects will be reviewed alongside each Department’s proposed budget and in
context with the overall needs and resources available to the City through the budget process. Mr. Burton used the aid of a
PowerPoint presentation to provide Council staff’s analysis of the proposed CIP; he defined a capital improvement project as
follows:
              • Projects exceeding $50,000 that add to the city’s physical plant:
                        o Includes design and/or construction
                        o Includes public buildings, streets, utility lines, parks, and other public facilities
              • Projects exceeding $50,000 for major rehabilitation or reconstruction of the physical plant.
              • Projects resulting in the acquisition or long-term lease of land.
              • Projects which require debt financing or borrowing.
              • Public facilities that may be financed in whole or in part by the imposition of impact fees.
          Funding sources for CIP projects include the General Fund, Federal and State grants, B&C Road Funds/Active
Transportation Funds, Enterprise Funds, Bonds, and other miscellaneous grant programs, partnerships, fundraisers, and
Redevelopment Agency Funds. He presented the proposed CIP review schedule, with adoption of the CIP possibly occurring on
June 17, 2025 following a public hearing. The Planning Commission has reviewed the proposed CIP and has forwarded a
determination of compliance; projects that were included in the proposed CIP that were determined not to be compliant were:
              • Project EN017 – 1100 North and Monroe Boulevard Traffic Signal. Mt. Lewis Plan calls for a roundabout at
                  this intersection. The Commission is recommending an evaluation and if a traffic signal is preferred that the
                  plans be updated.
              • Project RG010 – Community Field House. No location has been determined so General Plan consistency
                  cannot be determined at this time.
          Mr. Burton noted there are 92 projects included in the five-year CIP, 27 of which are new, for a total funding proposal of
$249,362,741. He noted 42 of the projects are perpetual projects, 45 are one-time projects, and five are Recreation, Arts, Museum,
Parks (RAMP) grant projects. The CIP includes a funding proposal of $13,620,300 for the General Fund in the FY26 Budget.
Funding proposals for all other funds for FY26 total $29,722,450. He presented a spreadsheet that provided a more detailed
review of the projects included in the proposed CIP; the spreadsheet included:
              • Total cost by Department.
              • Project Number.
              • Administrative Ranking.
              • Strategic Plan Directive.
              • Total Project Cost.
              • FY26-30 CIP Plan Proposed First Year Funding.
              • Proposed Funding in FY26 Budget.
              • Future Funding Planned.
          For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
