2.  Consent Agenda
     a.  Conditional Use Permit, to allow cell tower amendment at 2828 Harrison Boulevard. 
	Ms. Hale reported this request is to add cell antennas to the existing 83’ tower on the southeast side of the Ogden High football stadium in order to improve service to customers in the area.  The addition will increase the width of the array by one foot, creating a diameter of 15’5” while the original conditional use allowed a 13’6” diameter.  She reviewed conditional use standards and indicated while the size of the array will increase, the replacement of the new sectors will likely not be noticeable as the antennas are similar in size to existing antennas.  Utilizing existing towers is preferable to constructing new towers and will create the least impact to the community while allowing the needed service to be provided.  Staff recommends approval subject to the antennas being painted white to match the existing antennas and all departmental staff comments being satisfied.
MOTION:	A motion was made by Commissioner Schade to approve the conditional use permit based on the findings the proposed use and design will be compatible with surrounding development, and will not be detrimental to any person or surrounding property.  Motion was seconded by Commissioner Safsten and passed unanimously with Commissioners Blaisdell, Safsten, Schade, Stoker and Sandau voting aye.

     b.  Conditional Use Permit, to allow generator addition for existing cell tower at 205  W. 17th Street.
	Ms. Hale reported the applicant is proposing to install an outdoor generator for the existing cell tower.  She stated a question arose at the previous meeting about the location of the fuel for the generator due to the potential of residential development in this area.  As the applicant was not present, the item was tabled at that time.  She described the location and potential noise anticipated by the proposed generator and indicated the diesel tank will be attached to the generator.  She reviewed conditional use standards and indicated the generator will not be visible to the public as it will be inside the screening fence.  The generator will improve the service of the cell tower and will not affect any existing uses.  Staff recommends approval subject to the additional land needed for the generator be added to the lease agreement and all departmental review comments be satisfied.
MOTOIN:	A motion was made by Commissioner Schade to approve the proposed generator addition based on the findings the proposed use and design align with the present facility and is compatible with the surrounding development and the installation of the seven foot screening fence will protect the use and not be detrimental to any person or surrounding properties.  Approval is subject to additional land for the generator being added to the lease and that all departmental review comments are received and satisfied.  Motion was seconded by Commissioner Safsten and passed unanimously, with Commissioners Blaisdell, Safsten, Schade, Stoker and Sandau voting aye.

     c.  Conditional Use Permit, to allow cell tower expansion at 325 Gramercy Avenue. 
	Ms. Hale reported the proposal is to install an additional array of antennas on the existing monopole which will increase its height by 20’ as well as installing a new diesel generator and walk-in cabinet to accommodate the new equipment.  The service area will be screened with a 7’ chain link fence with vinyl slats and native shrubs.  She reviewed the conditional use standards indicating while the height will be increased, it will have less effect on the surrounding neighborhood than adding additional towers, and existing mature trees to the west provide a buffer to the residential area.  The addition will improve service to the surrounding community and ground equipment will be screened by a 7’ privacy fence and native shrubs.  If lighting is to be installed, it will need to be fully shielded, with its design to be approved by Staff.  The new generator will be inside the enclosure with its fuel tank attached.  It is anticipated the generator will only be used in the event of power failure in the area and for routine maintenance.  Staff recommends approval subject to the following conditions:
1. 	Install ten (10) native shrubs along the east and south fence line around the existing cell tower and new lease area; 
2. 	A financial guarantee for the approved landscaping is received before any permits are issued;
3. 	New lighting fixtures are fully shielded from the sky and residential properties to the north and the west; and
4. 	Comply with all department review comments 
MOTION:	A motion was made by Commissioner Schade to approve the conditional use permit for the additional equipment based on the findings the proposed use and design will be compatible with the surrounding area and the installation of the required screening plant material will protect the area and not be detrimental to any person or surrounding properties.  Approval is subject to Staff recommendations as follows:
	•	Ten native shrubs be installed along the east and south fence lines around the existing cell tower and new lease area;
	•	A financial guarantee be provided for approved landscaping prior to permits being issued;
	•	New lighting fixtures are to be fully shielded from the sky and residential properties to the north and west; and
	•	All departmental review comments be satisfied.  
Motion was seconded by Commissioner Safsten and passed unanimously with Commissioners Blaisdell, Safsten, Schade, Stoker and Sandau voting aye.

Because of this loss of right, the use must meet the standards of the CBD zone and other development standards.  The applicant is creating some on-site parking with four angled parking stalls behind the building, while the ordinance requirement for four units is eight parking spaces.  The applicant desires to repair damaged features such as stucco, plaster, windows and wood siding.  Existing wood lap siding will be replaced with cement fiberboard lap siding, and the building is to be repainted matching existing white and reddish-brown colors.  No other changes are proposed for the building façade and no landscaping or sidewalk changes are proposed.  While a garage had previously provided two parking spaces, the applicant is now proposing four angled parking spaces in the existing paved area.  As this design limits access to one-way, Staff recommends parking be changed to 90 degree, which will still allow four parking spaces.  Due to limited space on the site, the request includes a parking reduction be approved by the Planning Commission.  He stated utilities and lighting must be screened and Staff recommends installation of a dumpster enclosure as well as a 6’ screening fence.  He noted the property is near the downtown and the bus rapid transit route and a reduction in parking could be warranted.  Staff recommends approval subject to the following conditions:

3. A 6’ tall masonry screening wall or wrought-iron fence with 6’ tall evergreen hedge/trees be installed along the south and east property lines along drives and parking stalls with the design, materials, colors, and locations approved by planning staff. 
4. Exterior lighting be directed downward and 
shielded so all light is contained on the site. 
5. Utilities be fully contained and screened from public view. 
6. The landscaping on the site be brough into compliance with current standards by implementing water-wise landscaping and reducing turf grass to 35% of the landscaped area – landscaping being reviewed and approved by planning staff. 
7. Professionally drawn (architect or engineer) site, landscaping, lighting, and fencing plans be provided to planning staff for review and approval. 
8. Ogden City permits be obtained for all work on the building and site and all code enforcement cases be resolved on the property. 
9. All department staff review comments be satisfied 
MOTION:	A motion was made by Commissioner Schade to approve the site plan and reduced parking as requested based on the findings the colors and materials will be compatible with the site, landscaping and sidewalk are compatible with the site, traffic circulation will not create negative impacts, the architectural lines of the building are compatible, the site will provide adequate screening and standards to allow reduced parking are met.  Approval is subject to Staff recommendations as follows:

3.  Public Hearing, Petition to Rezone property at 305 Harrison Boulevard from R-1-6 to R-2
  
	As the applicant was not present, Mr. Simpson stated this is a request to rezone existing property from R-1-6 and R-1-8 to R-2 in order to allow a duplex townhome project.  The property is currently vacant, surrounded by single-family residential zoning with a charter school to the west and a small commercial center to the north.  The proposal is for 28-units to be constructed as a duplex row-house group dwelling.  While the applicant has provided a concept plan, it is not being reviewed at this time, and consideration is only whether the property should be rezoned.   He reminded the Commission a previous action to rezone the property to R-3 had been recently denied by the Commission and indicated the applicant has now revised the petition rather than pursuing its course to the City Council.

	He stated the property is in the Hillcrest-Bonneville Community, and reviewed the recommendations of that community plan, stating it suggests a slightly higher density than the existing single-family could be compatible so long as developed as row houses with an individualistic appearance.  He also acknowledged since that time a portion of the property has been developed as a charter school, which is a permitted use in all residential zones.  The proposed concept plan shows a two-story row house design.  Staff feels the proposed zone is compliant with the Community Plan, as well as the Community Policies as an edge can be developed, the use will reflect the prevailing character of the neighborhood, would not isolate existing neighborhoods and provides a transition between the surrounding single-family area and the school property to the west and commercial development to the north.  Staff recommends approval of the rezoning, noting the Commission will later review the specific design of the group dwelling project.  He also noted the tiny parcel within the site which is under separate ownership should be included as part of the zoning amendment.

	Mr. Roy Harris stated the project will limit access onto Harrison Boulevard and will be developed as attractive duplex units, stating the property has been vacant for some time and the proposed plan limits traffic onto Harrison and provides individualistic style housing for the community.

	Commissioner Safsten asked if the location of access onto Harrison has been identified.  Mr. Richins indicated the applicant should work with the City traffic engineer as well as UDOT in identifying the appropriate access location.  

As there were no comments from neighboring residents, a motion was made by Commissioner Schade to close the public hearing.  Motion was seconded by Commissioner Safsten and passed unanimously.
	
MOTION:	A motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to recommend approval of the rezoning request based on the findings it is consistent with the Community Policies of the General Plan as well as the direction of the Hillcrest-Bonneville Community Plan.  Motion was seconded by Commissioner Blaisdell and passed unanimously with Commissioners Blaisdell, Safsten, Schade, Stoker and Sandau voting aye.

4.   Conditional Use Permit, to allow Casa Castillo Event Center, 1602 Washington Boulevard. 

	As the applicant was not present, Ms. Patrick presented the application indicating the request is for a small events center on the southeast corner of 16th Street and Washington Boulevard.  While commercial zoning is to the north, south and west, property to the east is a well-developed single-family neighborhood.  The vacant building is proposed to have two entryways and 26 parking spaces, including one ADA space on site.  The applicant has requested hours of operation be from 10am until 11pm Monday thru Fridays and 9am until midnight on Saturdays, with no indication of its use on Sundays.  She reviewed conditional use standards indicating if well-managed, an event center could be compatible with the residential area to the east.  She suggested the hours of operation should be limited and that all events be held  indoors in order to reduce the potential impact of the use to the surrounding neighborhood.  The use is consistent with the General Plan in that it provides a reuse of an existing vacant building.  Staff recommends approval of the conditional use permit subject to hours of operation being limited from 10am until 10pm, the parking lot being re-striped with improved landscaping, an ADA accessible parking space with access aisle be provided close to the building entrance, all events are to be held indoors, trash receptacles must be screened from the view and placed away from the residential area, and all staff corrections and requirements be satisfied.

	Commission discussed the recommended hours of operation, indicating while 10pm may be too early for the applicant, it may be too late for surrounding residential uses.  Commissioner Stoker asked if the applicant had expressed support of the limited hours, stating 10pm seems restrictive for typical events.  Ms. Patrick indicated she had informed the applicant about the meeting and had anticipated their attendance to answer questions.  She also stated area residents also had been invited.

MOTION:	A motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to approve the conditional use permit based on the findings limiting the hours of operation will assure the proposed use is not detrimental to persons or property, the use is consistent with the General Plan, is compatible with the character of the site, adjacent property and the surrounding neighborhood, and will comply with the regulations of the Zoning Ordinance.  Approval is subject to the following conditions:
	•	The hours of operation, Monday through Saturday, will be limited to10 am to 10 pm.
	•	The parking lot must be remarked, and landscaping improved.
	•	Parking for one handicap stall with access aisle be provided close to the entrance of the building. 
	•	All event activities must be held indoors.
	•	Garbage receptacles must be screened from view and placed away from the single-family homes to the east.

6.  Public Hearing, Petition to Rezone, property at 712 7th Street

	Mr. Zack Spencer stated he owns three parcels in this area which contain homes, one of which had been destroyed by fire.  An additional four parcels are included in the petition which are under separate ownership.  He stated the two remaining homes are in poor condition and the intent is to rezone the area to redevelop the lots and beautify the area.

	Mr. Brierley stated the petition includes seven parcels consisting of 1.55 acres signed by four property owners.  The request is to change the zoning from R-1-6 to R-3.  In considering a zone change, the Commission is to determine whether it is compliant with the General Plan.  While the Hillcrest-Bonneville Plan indicates single-family residential neighborhoods should be strengthened, it also indicates muti-family should be limited to existing developed areas within the community.  The seven parcels are surrounded on three sides by existing R-3 zoning with multi-family townhomes and the amendment would fit with existing development, filling in a peninsula of existing R-1 with multi-family residential project which would fit in with the existing development pattern of the area.  He reviewed the Community Policies indicating the proposed zoning would create a better definite edge than the current conditions and would not isolate neighborhoods.  The 7th Street right-of-way can serve as a barrier between the single-family and multi-family zones.  Staff recommends approval of the rezoning as requested.  He acknowledged no development plan is proposed at this time and existing single-family homes may remain in their current use as long as they desire.  Staff feels development of a multi-family project should incorporate the entire inner peninsula of property.

	Mr. Darryl Warner, resident at 673 7th Street, stated he has become a neighborhood activist promoting safety along 7th Street  He expressed concern with adding another multi-family development would affect pedestrian safety, particularly of children walking to and from schools in the immediate area.  He stated crossing 7th Street is already a safety concern due to the speed of traffic created by the steepness of the road.  He stated there are storm water drainage concerns as many properties to the east were developed without the requirement of installing curb and gutter, with gravel from these sites trailing down into the residential neighborhood.  He stated sidewalks are lacking in the area and students are required to make multiple crossings to get to school.

	Mr. Richins indicated it has long been the policy of Ogden City that properties install improvements as developments occur but older projects often were developed prior to this standard.  He stated the width of 7th Street should accommodate potential traffic created by increased residential units and 7th Street would continue as it currently exists.  He stated the Traffic Engineer coul,d determine whether there is a need to prohibit on-street parking based on the road width and other factors, and that parking is restricted near designated crosswalks.  Discussion continued relating to the steepness of the hill and safety to both pedestrians and drivers.  Mr. Richins responded the road was installed as development occurred and is difficult to change to meet current standards.  New development will be required to install curb, gutter and sidewalk in front of their property as well as handle storm water runoff.  

	Chair Sandau asked how many units could be allowed if the rezoning were to be approved.  Mr. Brierley responded the three lots owned by the main petitioner might allow up to 12 units, with the entire infill area allowing up to 20 units on the seven lots.  

	Ms. Kathie Darby, 5188 Sunset Drive, stated this is a prime area that needs to be redeveloped and felt the entire seven lots should be included in an overall project of the area.  It was noted while the Shoshone Hills project is zoned R-3, the homes are mainly single-family.

As there were no additional public comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to close the public hearing.  Motion was seconded by Commissioner Schade and passed unanimously.

MOTION:	A motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to recommend approval of the rezoning of the seven parcels as proposed based on the findings a definite edge can be created, zoning would reflect the overall character of the area, 7th Street can serve as a buffer to warrant different zones, zoning boundaries would not cut across individual lots, concepts for development can reflect the surrounding neighborhoods and would not isolate neighborhoods.  Motion was seconded by Commissioner Blaisdell and passed 3-2 with Commissioners Blaisdell, Safsten and Stoker voting aye and Commissioners Schade and Sandau voting no.
