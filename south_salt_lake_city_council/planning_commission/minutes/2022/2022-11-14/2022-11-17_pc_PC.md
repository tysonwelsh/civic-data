3.     Public Hearing – GP-22-001 – Consideration of a Moderate-Income Housing Plat
       Report Incorporated into the General Plan.

Action Item

Applicant:     South Salt Lake City

Ms. Ungricht presented the staff report and stated that every year the City updates its Moderate-
Income Housing Plan. This year the State put forward HB-462, which requires municipalities to




                                               T
take additional steps to ensure that they are planning for and reducing barriers to moderate-income
housing. There are 24 strategies to choose from with action items showing how the strategies will
be met. The intent was to approve the Plan prior to the end of the year to meet the State deadline.
Ms. Ungricht reported that the State defines moderate-income housing as affordable to those with
household incomes of less than 80% of the Area Median Income (“AMI”). As part of HB-462, the
City is required to include the strategies in the General Plan and amend it each year. As a result,
         AF
staff is proposing to amend the appendix to the General Plan. Currently, it includes the Moderate-
Income Housing Plan from 2020. It was proposed to be added to the end of the General Plan. It
would show the Resolution that would be voted on by the City Council.

The City is required to implement at least five strategies. Those chosen by staff were as follows:

1.     (E) Strategy 1: Create, or allow for, and reduce regulations related to, internal or detached
       accessory dwelling units in residential zones.

2.     (F) Strategy 2: Zone or rezone for higher density or moderate-income residential
       development in commercial or mixed-use zones near major transit investment corridors,
       commercial centers, or employment centers.
R
       Commissioner Carter asked about Transit-Oriented Development (“TOD”) when it was last
       looked at, and the City’s policy in reviewing the boundary. Ms. Ungricht stated that it has
       not been revisited since she has been employed by the City. It was, however, on staff’s
       docket because it is quite large. The preliminary conversations will likely be at staff level.
       If changes were proposed, it will have to be reviewed by the Planning Commission and City
       Council because it is a land use ordinance that is regulated by the City Council. Public
       hearings would also be held.
D

3.     (H) Strategy 3: Amend land use regulations to eliminate or reduce parking requirements of
       residential development where a resident is less likely to rely on the resident’s own vehicle,
       such as residential development near major transit investment corridors or senior living
       facilities.

4.     (Q) Strategy 4: Create a housing and transit reinvestment zone pursuant to Title 63N,
       Chapter 3, Part 6, Housing and Transit Reinvestment Zone (“HTRZ”).
South Salt Lake City Planning Commission Meeting                                                  6

        Ms. Ungricht explained that the HTRZ is a way to get tax funding from the County. This is
        the first year it has been in place by the State and is fairly new. It must be within a certain
        proximity of a TRAX station and the County is only accepting eight applications. Chair
        Vernon’s understanding was that if there is a tax reduction, more affordable housing will be
        created and result in less dependence on developers.

        Commissioner Pechmann noted that the Central Point TRAX stop abuts major commercial
        development. Ms. Ungricht stated that there is a project going in nearby as well as other
        projects that staff is currently working on. Commissioner Carter stated that the other stop is
        on 3900 South and is nearly built out with high-density residential. This was likely the only




                                                T
        one that could be chosen. Ms. Ungricht agreed. She stated that with this being required
        every year they can always go back and amend it. Commissioner Carter asked if stops on
        the S-line could be chosen. Ms. Ungricht was unsure.

5.      (V) Strategy 5: Develop and adopt a Station Area Plan in accordance with Section 10-9a-
        403.1.
          AF
        Chair Vernon questioned how adopting a plan is going to help with moderate-income
        housing. Ms. Ungricht explained that as part of the plan, a municipality can focus on the
        housing around it. The plan will help cities know what they want in an area and drive
        development that is more transient-oriented. Commissioner Carter commented that the stops
        are walkable and the City is ahead of the game. Commissioner Pechmann stated that it makes
        sense that it is in the Master Plan. Ms. Ungricht remarked that tied to the report is funding
        for roads. In response to a question raised by Chair Vernon, Ms. Ungricht stated that the
        State has set the moderate-income housing standard at 30%, 50%, and 80% of AMI.

Chair Vernon opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public hearing was
closed.
