36
37   Senior City Planner, Andrew Hulka, reported that the above item is a request for a Subdivision
38   Amendment for proposed changes to Lot 76 of the existing Steffensen Heights Subdivision. The
39   matter was carried over from the April 7, 2021, Planning Commission Meeting. The property was
40   divided several years prior by deed without going through the subdivision process. In order to
41   designate the lots as buildable lots, the subdivision has to be approved by the City. It would be
42   necessary to amend Lot 76 of Steffensen Heights creating two lots, lot 76a and 76b. A rendering was
43   displayed. Measurements were verified by the City Engineer and corrected for accuracy’s sake. Both
44   lots meet the 8,000 square-foot minimum lot size and zoning requirements. Staff recommended
45   approval with the condition that prior to final plat approval, all subdivision requirements and
46   department comments be met in full. Technical revisions will be completed to ensure that everything
47   is in compliance with the Code to staff’s satisfaction prior to the recordation of the Final Plat.

36          3.1       (Project SUB-21-001) – Discussion and Possible Action on a Request from
37                    Tamara Elsberry and Darin Tea for a Subdivision Amendment for Proposed
38                    Changes to Lot 76 of the Existing Steffensen Heights Subdivision. Continued from
39                    the April 7, 2021, Planning Commission Meeting.
40
41   Senior City Planner, Andrew Hulka presented the staff report and stated that the request is for a
42   subdivision amendment to Lot 76 of the Steffensen Heights Subdivision. The matter was heard at the
43   last Planning Commission Meeting and continued to allow staff time to verify the measurements and
44   technical details on the preliminary plat. The City Engineer verified that both proposed lots meet the
45   minimum 8,000 square-foot lot size. Because it is an amendment to an existing subdivision, Mr.
46   Hulka read from the Subdivision Code regarding grounds for vacating or changing a lot. A
47   determination should be made as to whether the request causes material injury or if there is good
48   cause for the proposed alteration. Staff verified some of the details since the last meeting and felt that

     Cottonwood Heights Planning Commission Meeting – 04/21/2021                                             4
 1   the application was ready for approval. Staff recommended approval with one condition set forth in
 2   the staff report.
 3
 4   The applicant, Tamara Elsberry, reported that her father has owned Lot 76 since 1955. Around 1999
 5   he wished to divide the back half to allow her brother to live there. He began the process before the
 6   City was incorporated and spent a great deal of time preparing for that. When Ms. Elsberry inherited
 7   the property, she assumed that it was ready to be developed. The present intent was to sell the
 8   property.
 9
10   Chair Coutts opened the public hearing.
11
12   Benjamin Reece thanked the Commission Members for their professionalism and was pleased to see
13   that he will not be removed from the protections afforded by the Covenants, Codes, and Restrictions
14   (“CC&Rs”). He learned about the plans to build when the public notice was posted. He wanted to
15   make sure it was being done legally as the lot is very close to his property. Since looking into the
16   legality of the CC&Rs, he discovered that 96 of the lots have been subdivided within the Steffensen
17   Heights Subdivision since 1954, with the exception of one on which a neighbor built a tennis court.
18   Mr. Reece stated that selling the property with the intent to build was not disclosed to him before he
19   purchased his property. At the April 7 meeting, staff recommended approval with the condition that
20   a consent form be submitted by him. Mr. Reece asked if that condition still applies.
21
22   City Attorney, Shane Topham, explained that the packet was prepared about the time he became
23   involved. At that time, he looked at whether consent of all landowners was required. He determined
24   that it is not mandatory. Since the April 7 meeting, staff has backed away from obtaining consent
25   from the adjoining landowners. That condition should be waived based on the fact that there is
26   nothing in the Code that requires it. Mr. Topham stated that if the concern pertains to the loss of
27   CC&R protections, the two landowners should rerecord those protections in connection with the
28   subdivision. Mr. Reece was concerned about what happens on the subject property and wanted to
29   ensure that nothing is proposed that is not permitted. Mr. Reece confirmed that he does not mind if
30   the pending application proceeds as long as it is done legally; however, it conflicts with the CC&Rs,
31   which are outside the purview of this meeting. His understanding was that the CC&Rs can be revised
32   but doing so requires the consent of half of the property owners in the subdivision.
33
34   Bradley Tilt identified himself as Mr. Reece’s legal counsel. He disagreed with Mr. Topham’s
35   assertion that there is nothing in City Code requiring Mr. Reece to give consent to the subdivision.
36   He stated that City Code is very clear that any land that is divided, resubdivided, or proposed to be
37   divided into two or more lots or parcels meet Section 12.12.010, which specifies that the preliminary
38   plat shall contain information and show that it complies with the various requirements. Mr. Tilt stated
39   that one requirement specifies that all property to be subdivided is under the control of the subdividers,
40   which requires that the subdivider own both parcels at issue or own the one and get the consent and
41   approval from his client. He stated that the City should not put itself in the position of unilaterally,
42   and against his client’s will and stated objections, carve his property out of the protections of the
43   CC&Rs that have been in place since 1954. The private contractual obligations of the property owners
44   under the existing CC&Rs cannot be affected by this body and would materially injure his client. He
45   stated that there is no good cause to allow a subdivision change to go forward because they do not
46   have the consent of all of the owners of the impacted property.

27   8,000 square foot lot, is not creating another similar situation. Mr. Hulka responded that it would be
28   similar to a lot line adjustment that would not create a new parcel or lot. As a result, it does not have
29   to go through the same subdivision process. Mr. Topham agreed and explained that the church next
30   door deeded property to the then-owner of the lot to make the lot larger. It involved a lot line
31   adjustment, which has relaxed standards and is not required to go through a major process like a
32   subdivision. He explained that the CC&Rs are a private contract between the owners of the real estate
33   that is encumbered by the CC&Rs. There are issues regarding CC&Rs that are this old with respect
34   to enforceability. Ultimately, the City cannot get involved in private contractual relationships. Mr.
35   Topham indicated that the CC&R issue needs to be set aside by the City and the Commission should
36   focus on whether the proposed subdivision is legal under City and State law. If so, it can be approved.

 1          3.2     (Project ZMA-21-001) – A Public Hearing to Receive Comments and Take
 2                  Possible Action on a Request from Lance Paulson for a Zoning Map Amendment
 3                  to Rezone 0.3 Acres of Property at 2751 East Fort Union Boulevard from R-1-8
 4                  (Residential Single-Family) to RO (Residential Office).
 5
 6   Mr. Hulka presented that staff report and stated that the current zoning of the property is R-1-8. The
 7   request is to rezone the property to RO. Renderings of the zoning and land use maps were displayed.
 8   The existing use is residential single-family. The proposed use would create RO for this lot only. It
 9   is consistent with the long-range vision of having residential office for properties in the middle of this
10   section of Fort Union Boulevard. Permitted and conditional uses in the proposed RO zone include
11   single-family dwellings as a permitted use. There is a list of conditional uses, which includes offices,
12   mixed residential housing, studios, child care, and bed and breakfasts. Staff recommended the
13   Commission forward a recommendation of approval to the City Council.
14
15   The applicant, Lance Paulson, thanked the Commission for their consideration. He stated that he
16   would like to have the option to relocate from the property in the future and continue to run his office
17   or rent the upstairs unit. It would also make obtaining a loan easier for himself and future owners.
18   Potential benefits to the City were identified. Mr. Paulson stated that those with commercial uses are
19   held to high standards. He urged the Commission to recommend approval of his request.
20
21   Chair Coutts opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public hearing was
22   closed.
23
24   Chair Coutts commented that there has been a lot of back and forth regarding this portion of Fort
25   Union Boulevard. Most assume that Fort Union Boulevard is very commercial, which is the case for
26   most of it. East of 2700 East is very residential with very little commercial other than in-home
27   business, which are appropriate in the area. It seemed to her that the zone allows the applicant to
28   continue to operate his business as he has. She struggled to allow RO zoning in a single-family
29   section of the City.
30
31   Mr. Hulka explained that in 2018 the long-range plan for this segment was designated for mixed-use.
32   Based on previous concerns raised with other projects, perhaps the previous plans of having mixed-
33   use development up and down the corridor were not appropriate. It was noted that this segment of
34   the street is much quieter, narrower, and more residential. The solution at the time was to change the
35   map and as a result, the selected parcels were changed. The intent was to find a way to address the
36   concerns regarding development on the east side of Fort Union Boulevard while finding something
37   appropriate for more commercial pockets. Mr. Hulka reported that the RO zone has built-in
38   parameters for issues such as setbacks and screening as well. In most cases on smaller parcels, the
39   setback requirements prohibit new buildings from coming in.
40
41   Commissioner Anderson shared Chair Coutts’ concerns and stated that this is definitely a residential
42   neighborhood even though it faces Fort Union Boulevard. Commissioner Wilde did not think the
43   applicant should suffer because the City cannot make up its mind. He felt that the proposed use makes
44   sense and under normal circumstances would be approved out of hand. Because the City fluctuates
45   should not result in the applicant missing out on the benefit of the use of his property. Chair Coutts
46   clarified that it is a zoning request and not a use request and Mr. Paulson’s business is operating
47   legally in the residential zone. The will not change regardless of the action taken by the Commission.
