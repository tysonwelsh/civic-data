Development (HUD) funding from the federal government. The City receives annual CDBG and HOME grant funding and if an
activity is not included in the ConPlan, HUD funds cannot be used to fund it. Con Plan documents include the Five-Year plan

programming is limited to 30 percent of CDBG funding over a stated period. The CDBG-CV is also a new source of HUD funds
and is allocated through the Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security Act (CARES Act). This is one-time funding for
community development activities and Ogden City has been awarded $609,198. The funding offers the City additional flexibility
to address ConPlan activities; it must also be used to prevent, prepare for, and respond to COVID with CDBG regularly
requirements applied to funding considerations. A duplication of benefits analysis is required to ensure best practices are
implemented.

Proposed Ordinance 2020-18 amending the Ogden Municipal Code to
increase the maximum building height in the DOR Zone
         A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding an amendment to building height
regulations in the Defense Depot Reuse (DDR) zone came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated a petition has
been filed by Boyer Company to increase the maximum height limit for buildings north of 400 North and east of Depot Drive
from 50 feet to 90 feet. The area proposed for increased height is located with the DDR zone, which is classified as one of the
City's manufacturing and industrial zones. The petitioner has indicated an increase in demand for taller buildings within the
Business Ogden Depot (BDO).
         Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
implications of the proposed ordinance amendments. He presented an aerial map to orient the Council to the area ofBDO that is
subject to the proposed amendment; it is located in the northeast quadrant of the installation. At present, the current maximum
building height in the DDR zone is 50 feet. The petitioner would like to increase the maximum building height to 90 feet in the
area north of 400 East and east of Depot Drive; this area is bordered by agricultural/wetland property to the east and the Weber
County Fairgrounds to the north. Planning Commission considerations included the following:
              •    Some manufacturing uses require additional building height.
              •    The area is far from residential properties and the Ogden Nature Center.
              •    Other manufacturing zones in similar situations have no height limit.
              • Unlike most of the BDO, this area has historically been vacant property.
              •    The change will accommodate and support growth in the BDO, which is consistent with the General Plan
                   (amusement ride manufacture company needs additional height to develop prototypes and fill orders).
         Mr. Montgomery concluded the Commission voted unanimously to recommend approval of the proposed ordinance
amendment to increase building heights from 50 feet to 90 feet in the area of the DDRzone located north of 400 North Street and
east of Depot Drive.

Central Business District Community Plan - MAKE Ogden
          Community and Economic Development Deputy Director Cooper explained in Spring 2019, Ogden City and Design
Workshop initiated the planning process to create a new downtown master plan that will significantly change portions of the
City's General Plan and the Central Business District (CBD) Community Plan. This process--brandedMAKE Ogden-is an effort
to position the City of Ogden as a competitive location for businesses to thrive. This plan will ultimately guide future development
of the downtown through the next two decades. The MAKE Ogden initiative was also created- in part- to facilitate transparent
communication to the neighborhoods about new development coming to the area. Mr. Cooper introduced Robb Berg of Design
Workshop, who used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide a preview of the MAKE Ogden draft plan; the presentation
focused on the following main points:
               • Project overview;
               • Project approach;
               • Episodic plan;
               •    General Fund projections; and
               • Design standards.
          It has been over 10 years since a plan has been created specifically for downtown Ogden; the MAKE Ogden plan is
intended to guide strategic decision making to balance assets and build momentum toward a prosperous future. Tenets of the
project include land use, preservation to historic assets, mobility/transportation, open space, equity, and branding. A border of the
downtown area has been identified and the total area is divided into five proposed districts, including the Downtown District,
Union Station District, River East District, Nine Rails Arts District, and the Marshall White District. Residents and stakeholders
have been given the opportunity to participate in a public process to provide their feedback regarding the types of projects and
improvements they would like to see in each of the five districts. Now that these priorities have been identified, it is time to
consider smart growth in the project area. The following 10 principles are considered the foundation of a smart growth approach.
                    1. Mix of land uses.
                   2. Take advantage of compact design.
                   3. Create a range of housing opportunities and choices.
                   4. Create walkable neighborhoods.
                    5. Foster distinctive, attractive communities with a strong sense of place.
                   6. Preserve open space, farmland, natural beauty, and critical environmental areas.
