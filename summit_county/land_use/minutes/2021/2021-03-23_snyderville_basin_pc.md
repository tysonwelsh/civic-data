Ms. Rodriguez said there is a new established transit district, named High Valley 
Transit.  The boundaries include all of the Park City School District boundary and a 
piece of Silver Creek.  It excludes Park City proper boundaries.   
 
Ms. Rodriguez said this is governed by a Board of Trustees.  They are appointed by the 
County Council.  Currently, there are seven Board Members; five are existing members, 
and two are appointed members.  
 
Ms. Rodriguez shared her screen of the fixed route transit and the micro-transit.  She 
began with describing how micro-transit functions.  The micro-transit system will 
determine where the best place is to pick up and deliver a rider based on a host of 
different factors.  Things that will be considered will be the weather, other riders in the 
area, and the current traffic conditions.    
 
Ms. Rodriguez said that based on the outcome of this data, a rider will likely have a 
smaller vehicle pick them up at one of 1,000 virtual bus stops.  This vehicle will then 
deliver the rider to a fixed route stop, or even possibly to the rider's destination.  This is 
determined by the computer algorithm.  The likelihood is that over 90% of customers 
within the orange boundaries will wait less than 10 minutes from the time a ride is 
requested to the time a vehicle picks them up.  Currently, in some of the areas, if a bus is 
missed, the rider will have to wait another 30 minutes for the next bus.  This system is 
more flexible and efficient.  Micro-transit is also known as "on-demand."  This will 
expand transit to many more people.   
 
A slide was shared showing the proposed mix of the micro-transit and two fixed route 
services that will overlay the micro-transit zone.  One is the existing Kimball Junction 
circulator.  It will operate the same way it does now, which is 17 hours a day and every 
15 minutes.  Adding to that route is a Bitner Road connector.  This will give the people 
who live there frequent and reliable service.

Ms. Rodriguez said the current "pink" and "brown" routes are being combined to one

Regulations, Chapter 10-11 Definitions and Chapter 10-2-10 Use Table as it

Development Code-  Ray Milliner, Principal Planner 
 
Planner Milliner said that tonight they are prepared to have a public hearing and 
possible action for amendments to the Development Code as it relates to the creation 
and construction of accessory buildings.   
 
He said that last week, the County Council placed a six-month moratorium on accessory 
buildings in order to address a number of public concerns with regards to the 
proliferation, the size, and the placement of accessory buildings throughout the County.  
The two areas of greatest concern are the Silver Creek and Old Ranch Road areas.  He

said they would like to get this language at least 80% of the way to completion and then

Planner Milliner said the way the current language reads is that a person is allowed to 
build an accessory building less than 2,000 square feet.  An accessory building between 
2,000 and 10,000 square feet is processes as an administrative review.  Any building 
over 10,000 square feet will come to the Planning Commission as a Conditional Use.  
The proposed regulations will divide accessory buildings into three categories.     
 
• It will be established that an accessory building needs to be incidental and 
supportive of the main use.  What that means is if the land is a residential lot, 
residential will need to be the focus of the property.  Planner Milliner described 
some of the requirements.   
1. An accessory building would need to be smaller in size than the primary 
structure.  The height of both buildings would be limited.   
2. The lot would be limited to one accessory and one primary structure.   
3. The square footage of the major and minor accessory could not be combined.   
4. The accessory building must be under the same ownership or control of the 
principal structure. 
5. The accessory building cannot be constructed before the accessory building 
is in operation or under construction.   
6. No signs are allowed for any commercial use. 
 
Minor Accessory Building: 
1. This is based on the coverage of the lot.  The maximum coverage of a single 
minor accessory building shall not exceed 75% of the primary building's 
footprint, up to 2,000 square feet.  He gave examples of how this would work.      
2. A secondary structure is allowed with an additional 400 square feet.  This is in 
addition to the 75% coverage.  This would allow for something similar to a "Tuff 
Snyderville Basin Planning Commission 
March 23, 2021 
Page 15 of 22 
 
Shed" or a small greenhouse.  These two structures cannot exceed 90% of the 
primary structure's footprint.  
3. There is a 26' height limit.   
 
Major Accessory Building 
1. This structure would typically be used as horse stables or training on larger lots.   
2. These will be prohibited on any lot that is less than one acre.   
3. The structure size is based on the lot size. 
 1 to 2 acres could have a 2,500 square foot building 
 2 to 3 acres could have a 5,000 square foot building 
 5 or more acres- There is a maximum of 12,500 square feet 
4.   Other requirements were read, such as setbacks.  The Planning Commission will 
have some discretion depending on the situation.   
5. Only one per lot 
6. Steel shipping containers are prohibited as an accessory building.  If someone wants 
to be creative and use a steel container as a single family home, it would have to 
meet architectural guidelines.  
 
Planner Milliner said the definitions are included of the three types of buildings.  He

to take care of the moratorium they have in place.

INDINGS OF FACT: 
1. In the Snyderville Basin Development Code, Accessory Buildings are listed as 
Permitted uses if they are less than 2,000 square feet in size, Low Impact uses if 
between 2,000 square feet and 10,000 square feet in size and a conditional use permit if 
greater than 10,000 square feet in size.  
2. The Stated Goal of Chapter 5 of the Snyderville Basin General Plan is to identify 
natural resources and ensure that all development undertaken is compatible with an in 
Code Amendments harmony with the surrounding mountain environment while 
maintaining ecological balance and protecting the scenic and historic qualities of the 
basin.  
3. There may be several Accessory Buildings legally constructed under the current 
regulations that are not in harmony with the goals of the Snyderville Basin 
Development Code regulations.  
4. Over the past few years, the public has asked that the Planning Commission do 
something to limit the impact of accessory buildings on adjacent properties.  
5. On March 10, 2021, the Summit County Council adopted a 6-month moratorium on 
the construction of all accessory buildings in the Snyderville Basin Planning Area.  
6. The purpose of the temporary zoning ordinance is to give the Planning Commission 
and County Council time to create regulations for Accessory Buildings.  
7. The draft regulations divide Accessory buildings into three (3) categories;  
 1. Minor Accessory Buildings  
 2. Major Accessory Buildings    
 3. Steel Shipping Containers

8. The purpose of the new regulations is to limit the size of accessory buildings and to

Conclusions of Law: 
1. The amendment is consistent with the goals, objectives, and policies of the General 
Plan.  
2. The amendment will not permit the use of land that is not consistent with the uses of 
properties nearby.  
3. The amendment will not permit suitability of the properties affected by the proposed 
amendment for the uses to which they have been restricted.  
4. The amendment will not permit the removal of the then existing restrictions which will 
unduly affect nearby property.  
5. The amendment will not grant special favors or circumstances solely for one property 
owner or developer.  
6. The amendment will promote the public health, safety, and welfare better than the 
existing regulations for which the amendment is intended to change.
