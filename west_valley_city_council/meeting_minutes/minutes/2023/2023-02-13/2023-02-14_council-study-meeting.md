RESOLUTION 23-14: AUTHORIZE THE PURCHASE OF A CHALLENGE
COURSE FROM GREAT WESTERN RECREATION FOR WEST VIEW PARK
Nancy Day, Parks and Recreation, presented proposed Resolution 23-14 that would
authorize the Purchase of a Challenge Course from Great Western Recreation for West View
Park.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:
   MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – FEBRUARY 14, 2023

       With the purchase of the properties located at 5980 W 4100 South and 4059 South
       6000 West which are located next to West View Park, the Utah and Salt Lake Canal
       Trail and the Mountain View Corridor Field and Trail; the Parks and Recreation
       Department applied for and received a Utah Outdoor Recreation Grant for
       $191,565.00 to help with the purchase and installation of a Challenge Course to be
       utilized by trail and park users. This amount covers the purchase of the challenge
       course, sports turf impact material, curbing and timers. Great Western Recreation
       holds a national OMNIA Partners contract, number 2017001134, so it is not
       necessary to bid the project. Parks and Recreation is in the process of design for the
       future park which will incorporate the trail, field, and Challenge Course.

Mayor Lang asked if the parking lot will be constructed in Phase 1. Nancy replied that is
the goal but funding/costs will determine if that can be done. Councilmember Harmon
asked why this park was put before Newton Farms. Nancy replied that the City received
grant money for this project and it needs to be completed by spring of next year. Mayor
Lang stated that she would like to ensure that the parking lot is constructed in Phase 1 and
would rather not do the project in pieces. Nancy replied that the total cost of construction
for the parking lot won’t be known until the project is bid but noted preliminary numbers
are 1.3 million. Councilmember Huynh asked when Phase 1 will be complete. Nancy
replied before the fall.

The City Council will consider Resolution 23-14 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
February 21, 2023 at 6:30 P.M

CONSIDER APPLICATION SMI-10-2022, FILED BY JOHN BAIRD,
REQUESTING FINAL PLAT APPROVAL FOR THE PSB INVESTMENTS
SUBDIVISION LOCATED AT 3909 SOUTH 2200 WEST
Steve Lehman, CED, presented proposed application SMI-10-2022, Filed by John Baird,
Requesting Final Plat Approval for the PSB Investments Subdivision Located at 3909 South
2200 West

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       John Baird, is requesting final plat approval for a two-lot subdivision. The subject
       property is located at 3909 South 2200 West and was recently rezoned from the A
       zone to the R-1-8 zone. An existing dwelling occupies the front portion of the
       property and will remain as part of the subdivision.

       Lot 1 will be approximately 8,350 square feet while lot 2 will be approximately
       12,003 square feet. Access to the subdivision will be gained from 2200 West.
       Public improvements including curb, gutter, sidewalk and parkstrip will be
       required. The applicant will also be dedicating a half width of 33 feet.

       Mr. Baird, is proposing to construct a new drive approach to the flag lot. The
       driveway location for the existing single-family dwelling will remain but will be
       replaced as a requirement of the development agreement.

       The existing dwelling will remain on what will become lot 1. During the rezoning
       process, Mr. Baird agreed to make certain improvements to the existing structure.
       The wood portions of the exterior will be painted, and all landscaping will be
       replaced per city standards. The detached garage will be removed and a new two
       car garage will be constructed for the existing dwelling.

Councilmember Huynh asked if there will be basements. Steve replied yes.
Councilmember Huynh suggested that staff look into the surrounding area before
construction because there is a street near this location that experienced flooding issues.
Steve stated he would relay that information to Building Inspections.

The City Council will consider Application SMI-10-2022 at the Regular Council Meeting
scheduled February 21, 2023 at 6:30 P.M

CROSSING SUBDIVISION LOCATED AT 6383 WEST 3500 SOUTH
Steve Lehman, CED, presented proposed Application SMI-13-2022, Filed by Ryan Halder,
Requesting Final Plat Approval for the 3500 South Krause Crossing Subdivision Located at
6383 West 3500 South

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       Ryan Halder is requesting final approval for the 3500 South Krause Crossing
       Subdivision. The proposed subdivision is located on the southeast corner of 3500
       South and 6400 West. The subdivision will consolidate two parcels to create 1 lot
       of approximately 1 acre in size. The subdivision will also dedicate right-of-way to
       West Valley City, UTA and UDOT.

       The existing hardware store will be replaced with a convenience store and fueling
       station. A conditional use application was reviewed and approved by the Planning
       Commission in September 2022. All of the site issues including access,
       landscaping, fencing etc., were addressed as part of that application.

       Access to this new lot will be gained from 3500 South and 6400 West. An area
       along 3500 South is being dedicated to UTA for a new bus stop. The applicant will
       also be dedicating 7 additional feet along a portion of 3500 South to complete the
       right-of-way requirements along said street.

       In summary, the proposed subdivision will consolidate parcels, dedicate right-of-
       way, and will establish easements and other information applicable to the division
       of property.

The City Council will consider Application SMI-13-2022 at the Regular Council Meeting
scheduled February 21, 2023 at 6:30 P.M

COMMUNICATIONS
A.  LEGISLATIVE UPDATE
    Mayor Lang noted that Lincoln Shurtz was attending a meeting regarding the photo
    radar bill and would provide the legislative update next week.

B.     CAPITAL EXPENDITURE PLAN
       Wayne Pyle, City Manager, indicated that Dan Johnson, Public Works Director,
       was working a capital expenditure plan that would note projects by list, timing,
       funding, and a timeline proposal. He added that Dan was also working with other
       departments on a similar plan. Councilmember Harmon stated that he would like to
       see a schedule of improvements that the City will want or need in the next 5-10
       years. He indicated that he would like a better understanding and a plan put in place
       for any projects that would need to be replaced/repaired in the coming 5-10 years.
       Wayne discussed various funding sources, fund balance, and noted that many items
       are planned and budgeted but not everything has an earmarked budget that can be
       put aside. Councilmember Harmon stated that he would like to see funds set aside
       for things like HVAC repairs, deck repairs at the golf courses, playground
       equipment repair, etc. Mayor Lang suggested that staff provide two lists, one for
       new projects and one for existing projects that need repair or maintenance. Wayne
       replied that most of these have a budget source already in place but not all projects
       have the budgetary means to allocate funds. He indicated that there are repair and
     MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – FEBRUARY 14, 2023
                             -7-


       replacement funds set aside each year that help cover these things. He indicated he
       would suggest increasing this to $400,000 to help increase coverage for projects
       that need ongoing repair or maintenance. Wayne stated that there are items that City
       has to lease for because of expense, rotation, etc. He indicated that the City has
       been fortunate enough to increase the CIP fund over the years. Councilmember
       Harmon stated that he would like to start with a schedule, review the balance of the
       CIP funds and what they can and could be used for. Mayor Lang suggested
       including anything over $100,000 and something that can easily be defined as a
       capital improvement.

C.     BUDGET REDUCTION PROPOSALS
       Councilmember Harmon requested that staff propose budget reduction options.
       Wayne replied that staff does this internally each year before the budget moves to
       the Council and indicated he could share that information. He indicated that about
       87 million dollars in the budget is personnel, 10 million is debt service in payments,
       and the rest is for utilities, etc. Councilmember Harmon stated that he is looking for
       significant budget reductions and understands that is looking at personnel. He
       indicated that he would like to see what services the City offers and what may need
       to be cut or reduced. Mayor Lang stated that she has seen where programs could
       become more cost effective if managed and merged in different ways.
       Councilmember Harmon stated that the City can’t keep increasing property taxes
       every couple years and noted he would like to see other ways of reducing the
       budget. Councilmember Huynh suggested hiring a consultant. Councilmember
       Nordfelt stated that 1/3 of revenue comes from property taxes and that revenue
       won’t increase if the Council doesn’t periodically increase it due to increased
       property values, etc. Mayor Lang stated that new growth will also increase property
       taxes. Councilmember Nordfelt indicated that with new growth comes the need for
       additional services. He indicated that he is willing to look at reduction proposals
       but saying the City has to stop increasing property taxes when it’s needed is not a
       good strategy. Councilmember Nordfelt added that cutting services is not an option.
       Councilmember Harmon stated that he would like to be more thoughtful on what
       services are provided so if property taxes need to be raised, the Council can feel
       good about that decision.
