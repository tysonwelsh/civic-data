3. Update       on the Comprehensive                        Code    Update;          Francis       Lffly,       Assistant        City     Manager
    Francis     Lilly     gave an overview             of the comprehensive                    code update           timeline,       which        started in
    2022 with         code assessment,            initial     interviews,          and survey.          In 2022-2023            there were
    stakeholder         committee        evaluations.          In 2023-2024              individual         chapters      were reviewed                by public
    bodies.     In 2023 there was neighborhood                         outreach          on R-1 and R-2/RCOZ                     updates.         In 2025
    there was neighborhood                  outreach         on recodification.            He reported            that 18 code chapters                     were
    reviewed       individually         by community               councils,       the planning            commission,           and the city council.
    Early     adoption       of landscaping,           parking,        subdivisions,           R-1/RCOZ              updates,       Forestry       Recreation
    Estate Zone,         signs, and Village            Center       Special        Districts      had already            taken place.        Most
    property      owners       received       at least two mailed               notices        (about      43,000      pieces       of mail total).              The

    code update          was published           in five printed            city newsletter           articles.      Online      there was a link                    to

    each proposed            chapter     which      included        a summary,            the proposed            text, and a link          to the staff
    report.


    The new code amendments                      for Millcreek          bring       significant         improvements             to zoning,
    application         procedures,         and plaru'iing         standards.        The number             of commercial             zones will                be
    reduced      firom three to two,            and the number               of FR zones will               decrease      from       five to one. The
Millcreek         City     Council          Meeting       Minutes                                    13 January         2025        Page 3 of 6



    RCOZ         and FCOZ           zones are being             eliminated,      although        their standards         will    remain      in place,
    and the complexities                   of overlay     zones, including           the airport       overlay        zone, are being        removed.
    A major        change includes                the clarification       of application         procedures,          creating      consistent            and
    user-friendly          guidance          for 24 different         planning     application         types,     supported        by easy-to-
    understand           tables.    Additionally,            every use referenced            in the code is now clearly                  defined          as
    either proibited               or permitted,         a previously         absent feature.         The building         envelope
    requirements           for R-I,         AG,     and R-2 zones have been made more adaptable,                                 considering
    different      lot sizes and circumstances,                    addressing        feedback         from the community.                Changes            to
    the landscape           ordinance         also help residents             take advantage          of the Utah Water             Savers        Grant
    Program.        In the Forestry            Recreation         Estate Zone,        a new five-acre           lot size minimum             will
    protect      buildable         sites. The Village            Center    Special     District       ordinance        has advanced          the city's
    economic         development             goals, while         improved       sign standards           may reduce            the number           of
    billboards.       Subdivision            standards         have been updated             to ensure compliance                with    state statutes,
    and parking           standards         have been revised            to support     affordable         housing       development.                These
    standards        include       a new parking             category     for affordable         housing       and specific         requirements                 for
    studies      and plans.         The code has been modernized                      with     clearer     tenninology           and more
    accessible       tables,       ensuring        greater     transparency,       and efficiency           in planning          and development.

            Mayor        Silvestrini        said Western            Governors         University          (WGU)       is a leader       in teacher

            Utah,      with    a median          student      age of 32, highlighting               its role in supporting              those balancing
