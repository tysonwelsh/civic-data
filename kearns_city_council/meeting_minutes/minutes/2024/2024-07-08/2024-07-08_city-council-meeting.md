5. Stakeholder Updates/Information
a. Kearns Improvement District
Greg Anderson, General Manager of the Kearns Improvement District,
reported that the 4320 West Waterline Project has been completed, resulting in
improved water quality and enhanced fire protection services for residents in the
affected area. He noted that the federally mandated lead and copper investigation has
been concluded, with no lead detected in the water. Additionally, 12,000 out of 15,000
water meters have been upgraded to smart meters, and an informational video is being
prepared to guide residents on how to access and monitor their water usage using the
new smart meters. The winners of the water conservation art contest will be featured on
the Kearns Improvement District float during Kearns Days. Mr. Anderson reported that
the legislature has allocated $3 per square foot for sod removal and $100 per tree for up
to five trees planted per household, and residents can apply for these rebates on the
district's website, www.kidwater4ut.gov
b. Library
Lee Whiting, Librarian, announced that the free summer reading program
will continue until July 31st. He mentioned that story time is offered at the library three
times per week, with two morning sessions and one evening session. On Tuesday
evenings, the library hosts Reading Buddies, where children can read one-on-one with
a mentor to build confidence and receive assistance with book selection. Mr. Whiting
also encouraged residents to take advantage of the 15-book per person check-out limit.

c. MyKearns

Britta Watts, Community Impact Manager of MyKearns Community
Coalition, announced that the organization is applying for the Parents Empowered
Grant, which aims to empower parents to talk with their children about underage
drinking. She stated they are currently reviewing their budget goals.

Charles Henderson, Healthy Kearns workgroup member, presented a
proposal for a new community garden project which would be managed by Wasatch
Community Gardens. He stated that they have been working on this initiative for about 4
years and have secured a $5,000 grant from Get Healthy Utah to start the process. Mr.
Anderson stated the estimated total startup costs to be around $120,000 to $140,000.
They are seeking a piece of land, approximately 1/4 acre in size, to establish the
community garden, and they have identified a potential location near the library and city
center. He further stated they estimate an annual maintenance cost of around $10,000,
which they plan to cover through grants and other funding sources rather than relying
on city funds. The city council expressed interest in the project and agreed to work with
them to identify a suitable location and provide any necessary support. The next steps
will involve the group formalizing an agreement with the city and securing the necessary
funding and resources to move the project forward.

6. Legal Updates/Information
a. Discussion on the Process to Impose Impact Fees

Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, provided an overview of the impact fee
process. He explained that one way to incentivize affordable housing in Kearns is by
waiving or reducing impact fees for developments that include moderate-income
housing. He noted that Kearns currently does not impose impact fees because the
County has not done so. He further explained that, at present, impact fees can only be
imposed for system improvements that maintain the current level of service for the
infrastructure provided.

Mr. Bracken also mentioned that, now that Kearns has become a city,
costs have increased, and imposing impact fees is one method to generate additional
revenue for the city. He stated that the impact fee process could take up to a year and
involves several steps including: publishing a notice of intent, holding a public meeting
to act on that intent, adopting a resolution, creating an impact fee facilities plan,
determining whether an impact fee analysis is needed to establish the maximum fees
that can be charged for the services provided, and finally adopting an ordinance to
implement the impact fees.

Mayor Bush stated that the process of imposing impact fees is important for
the progression of the Moderate Housing Plan.

Mayor Bush emphasized that the impact fee facilities study would yield
valuable information that could be utilized for various other purposes. Mr. Bracken
further explained that the study would identify all potential sources from which
additional revenue could be generated. The Council granted Mr. Bracken the authority
to move forward with the impact fee facility plan.
