Presentations, Proclamations, and Awards

1.          A recognition of Provo City’s August 2024 Employee of the Month 0:11:35

Mayor Kaufusi expressed the privilege of honoring an employee who has shown outstanding dedication
to the community. She introduced Bill Peperone, Development Service Director, who presented the
August 2024 Employee of the Month award to a distinguished member of the Development Services
Department.

Bill Peperone highlighted Hana Mauga as a standout employee in the Development Services
Department, emphasizing her role as a certified Building Inspector. He said Hana is involved in every
building permit issued by the city and is known for her exceptional customer service, often going beyond
her regular duties to ensure customer satisfaction and project progress. She is also a department expert
in the CityView program and is recognized for her holistic understanding of various departmental
processes, which allows her to effectively solve problems. Hana frequently assists her colleagues, covers
Planning Commission meetings, and mentors others. Her dedication is evident as she often works on
weekends, embodying exceptional care for the community.

Public Comment 0:23:43

Chair MacKay read the public comment preamble and opened the public comment period.

Brandon Jackson, of Provo, expressed frustration with being harassed by police in various public places,
despite being aware of and following the rules, such as curfew hours. He emphasized the need for a
safety barrier that ensures both the safety of the community and police while allowing individuals like
himself to maintain their freedom.

Jason Wilson, of Provo, shared his experience of becoming homeless after a series of events led him
from Southern California to Provo. He questioned how many of the Council members had been
repeatedly stopped by police and asked invasive questions about their activities and identity. He
highlighted the disparity between his experiences and those of more affluent individuals, noting that he
has been stopped by the police over 20 times in the two years he has been homeless.

Brandy Michelle Elston, of Provo, shared her experience of being homeless for about three years,
expressing frustration with frequent police harassment. She mentioned that her cars had been
impounded by the police, leaving her and her boyfriend without transportation or a place to stay. While
she understands the concerns about sleeping in parks or on church property, she expressed a desire to
find a place to rest without being harassed by the police.

Roy Eaton, of Provo, shared his difficult journey from being a professional in healthcare and counseling
to becoming homeless after his mother's death. Injured and unable to work, he applied for disability,
but he now faces constant harassment from the police, making it impossible to rest or find a stable place
to stay. He described the challenges of constantly moving to avoid trespassing tickets and the
overwhelming difficulty of managing daily life while homeless. Mr. Eaton expressed his hope for a
solution that balances the needs of both the homeless and the police, as he feels targeted by law
enforcement.

Caleb Strandleir, of Provo, acknowledged that there are countless stories of struggle within the
community and emphasized the importance of understanding and change. He expressed his love for the
city and mentioned his plans to attend UVU for game development and to open a restaurant. He said he
has written a grant proposal aimed at alleviating homelessness in the community and urged the council
to review it, believing their input could enhance the plan. He emphasized the need for collaboration to
address the issues facing society and expressed his appreciation for the intelligence and compassion
within the council.

Bonnie Schiffler-Olsen, of Provo, thanked the council for passing the library tax increase and for the
upcoming warming centers.

Action Agenda

3.         An ordinance amending Provo City Code regarding permitted locations for on-premises
           short hold time electronic display signs. Citywide Application. (PLOTA20240164) 0:32:00

     Motion:       An implied motion to approve an Ordinance, as currently constituted, has been made
                   by council rule.

Aaron Ardmore, Planning Supervisor, presented. He reminded the council that the motion from the last
meeting was to continue the item and update the amendment. Initially, the ordinance specified that the
location would extend from Cougar Boulevard northward to University Parkway, but after their previous
discussion, it was revised to 1400 North.

Councilor Bogdin confirmed that if the ordinance failed, the property could still add an electronic sign,
but the images would be limited to rotating once every hour. However, if the ordinance passed, the
images could rotate every minute.

Chair MacKay opened the item for public comment. With no public comments, and no council
discussion, she called for a vote.

     Vote:         The motion failed 4:3 with Councilors Bogdin, Christensen, Handley, and MacKay
                   opposed. Councilors Garrett, Hoban, and Whipple in favor.

4.         An ordinance adopting a budget for Provo City Corporation for the fiscal year beginning
           July 1, 2024 and ending June 30, 2025, in the amount of $292,212,512. (24-007) 0:34:14

     Motion:       An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2024-43, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

John Borget, Director of Administrative Services, presented and noted that the budget had been
thoroughly reviewed by the council in May and June. He explained that the final budget needed to be
adopted to incorporate the library tax increase, which was approved during the truth in taxation hearing
on August 13.

Chair MacKay opened the public comment period. With no public comments, she invited a council
discussion.

Councilor Hoban praised John, Kelsey, and all the departments for their excellent work on the balanced
budget. Although he said he would be voting no due to his opposition to the library tax increase, he
emphasized that his vote does not reflect any criticism of the city's efforts in managing the budget.

With no other comments, Chair MacKay called for a vote.

     Vote:         The motion passed 6:1 with Councilors Bogdin, Christensen, Garrett, Handley,
                   MacKay, and Whipple in favor. Councilor Hoban opposed.
