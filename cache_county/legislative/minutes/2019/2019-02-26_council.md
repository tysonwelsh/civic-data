The Cache County Council convened in a regular session on February 26, 2019 at 5:00 p.m.
in the Cache County Council Chamber at 199 North Main, Logan, Utah.

ITEMS OF SPECIAL INTEREST

Nibley City Request for Change in RAPZ Allocation Schedule — Nibley City Manager

David Zook thanked the county for the property conversion process connected with the

sale of county-owned North Logan property which allowed Nibley to develop the Firefly

Park. The first phase of the Park is almost done and Zook is asking that the

approximate $80,000.00 of funding to be paid over the next five years be awarded as a
total lump sum instead.

Executive Buttars said there is funding available due to the reimbursement for the
purchase of land at the Heritage Center. Council member White expressed concern that

the lump sum payment will take dollars away from the total RAPZ available for other
projects. Executive Buttars favors support of the request and he will get better financial
information for the Council’s consideration.

PUBLIC HEARING: February 26, 2019 at 5:30 p.m. — Resolution No. 2019-04-Declaring Cache
County interest in Real Property located In Logan Canyon including Parcel Number
148-065-0006 as Surplus Property — Executive Buttars explained this is .17 acres that the
adjoining property owner thought was included in their purchase in 1998. Buttars believes the
only access to the property is through private property.

Executive Buttars opened the Public Hearing and invited public comment.

Mike Nyman, adjoining property owner, said he didn’t know this parcel was not part of his original
purchase and said the legal description and the overlay are contradictory. He would like to have the
matter rectified.

Executive Buttars observed the legal description needs to be cleared up. Director Runhaar
noted a survey will cost five times the value of the property and there is nothing for the county to
tie it to.

There was no other public comment.

ACTION: Motion by Council member Zilles to close the Public Hearing. Worthen
seconded the motion. The vote was unanimous, 7-0.

PUBLIC HEARING: February 26, 2019 at 5:40 p.m. — Resolution No. 2019-05-Declaring Cache

County interest in Real Property located In Scare Canyon including Parcel Number
16-110-0000 as Surplus Property — Executive Buttars indicated some interested persons have

initiated this. Attorney Edwards stated a quit claim deed from The Hideout, LLC, transferred
ownership of the parcel to Cache County to be used as a public park providing unrestricted
public access thereto and a developed trail for public usage. There is no reversion clause.

Director Runhaar remarked the 12-acre parcel was left over from The Hideout subdivision and is
not buildable.

Executive Buttars said it appears this parcel has the only access to a privately owned parcel.
Chairman Ward opened the Public Hearing and invited public comment.

Mark Ross, owner of adjacent property, is interested in purchasing the property and believes it came to
the county for access to The Hideout property. Scare Canyon possesses deeded right-of-way on the
main roads, but not on the smaller roads.

There was no other public comment.

ACTION: Motion by Council member Zilles to close the Public Hearing. White seconded
the motion. The vote was unanimous, 7-0.

COUNCIL MEMBER REPORTS

Barbara Tidwell will be doing some ride-alongs with the Sheriff's office this Friday and a jail
tour is being set up for interested Council members. Tidwell also met with Roger Jones
concerning BRAG’s Human Resources Board.

Tidwell asked who is on the RAPZ Committee and Executive Buttars said that will be presented
at the next meeting.

Gina Worthen reminded Council members to let her know of any interest or concerns with
current legislation. Council member White suggested the state inmate pay for stay issue.
North Logan is trying to figure out funding for a new museum. An online survey produced
significant interest in the idea. Worthen feels this is a lot for a single modest-sized city to take
on and asked the Council to consider ways to assist in the project.

Dave Erickson reminded the Council that this Friday is the deadline for RAPZ requests and
asked about the status of the Chamber building. Executive Buttars said an offer has been
received and the acceptance is contingent upon the Council’s acceptance which will be on a
future agenda.

Paul Borup attended the Richmond Council meeting’s discussion on the creation of a metro fire
district in the north part of the county. Richmond’s mayor is very interested in the possibility.

Gordon Zilles reminded the Council that the Cache Valley Rendezvous is this weekend.
Executive Buttars said there are a lot of projects coming to the county currently.

Karl Ward stated a need to work on the Davenport Road issue to bring it to finalization and
requested an Executive Session for the March 12, 2019 Council meeting.

ADJOURNMENT

The Council meeting adjourned at 6:05 p.m.

CACHE COUNTY
RESOLUTION 2019 - 04

RESOLUTION DECLARING COUNTY INTEREST IN REAL PROPERTY LOCATED IN
LOGAN CANYON INCLUDING PARCEL NUMBER 18-065-0006 AS SURPLUS PROPERTY

WHEREAS, Cache County intends to sell the County interest in real property described in
Exhibit “A”; and

WHEREAS, Utah Code Ann. Section 17-50-312 provides that the county legislative body shall
provide by ordinance, resolution, rule or regulation for the manner in which property shall be acquired,
managed, and disposed of; and

WHEREAS, prior to the sale of said Property, Cache County Ordinance Section 3.40.010
requires the Cache County Council to declare said property as “Surplus Property”; and

WHEREAS Cache County Ordinances Sections 3.40.020 and 3.40.040 establish the procedures
and protocols that must be followed prior to declaring the property as “Surplus Property” before it can
be conveyed to a purchaser; and.

WHEREAS the Cache County Council must hold a public hearing pursuant to Cache County
Ordinance Section 3.40.040(B) before, by motion and vote, the Cache County Council can declare the
Property as “Surplus Property” so that it can be conveyed to a purchaser; now

THEREFORE, the Cache County Council finds as follows:

1. That it is in the public interest that the subject real property be disposed of as-surplus pioperty.
In determining whether the property shall be declared surplus, the County Council has taken into
consideration:

a. Whether the county has, or anticipates that it will have, no practical, economical,
efficient or appropriate use for the property currently or in the reasonably foreseeable
future.

b. Whether the purpose served by the property can be better accomplished by other
alternatives or property.

c. Whether the purpose served by the property or its use cither no longer exists or has
significantly changed because of the needs and demands of the county or as may be
determined by a change of policy evidenced by an ordinance or resolution of the county
council. .

d. Whether the property is so damaged, depreciated or worn that it is inoperable or limited
in operation without repairs and the cost of such repairs is unreasonable, excessive or
impractical.

e. Whether the purposes and interests of the county would be better served by the
declaration of the property as surplus and the disposition of that property.


THEREFORE, the Cache County Council, after holding a public hearing, resolves that the
Property, better described in “Exhibit A” shall be declared surplus, and that such surplus property may
be disposed of by the County Executive subject to the provisions of Cache County Code Chapter 3.40.

This resolution takes effect immediately upon adoption.
