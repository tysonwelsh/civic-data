        The purpose of the joint work session was to review the agenda for the City Council and Special Redevelopment Agency
(RDA) meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.; discuss the Plan Ogden General Plan update; discuss proposed Fiscal Year (FY)
2024-2025 fee amendments for the Police Department, Public Services Department – Utilities, Public Services Department –
Cemetery, and Public Services Department – Golf; discuss a proposed FY 2024-2025 budget amendment related to American
Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds; discuss Council/Board business; and hear Council/Board comments.

Plan Ogden General Plan Update
          Senior Planner Rypien introduced Christy Brown, representing the consulting firm Logan Simpson. She used the aid of a
PowerPoint presentation to provide a visioning update for the Plan Ogden General Plan update project. She noted the importance
of the public engagement element of this project includes the following:
                1. Provides research and information to the public.
                2. Seeks broad public input.
                3. Builds vision directly from public feedback.
                4. Uses transparent methods throughout.
                5. Builds momentum for implementation.
          Ms. Brown also touched on the scenarios process and public visioning process, which yielded 39 maps created by the
public. These maps were narrowed down to three different scenarios that had an emphasis on livable neighborhoods, community
corridors, and a vibrant downtown. As of November 13, 2024, there have been 307 participants participating in the scenario
evaluation. These participants were given a survey regarding their demographics, preferred housing strategies, economic issues,
job creation, transportation, recreation offerings, and the appropriation location for a transit hub for Weber State University
(WSU) transportation options. The potential vision map incorporates the feedback provided through the survey, addresses land use
priorities, transportation, job centers, open space and recreation, and Ogden Express (OGX) areas. Ninety percent of those who
responded to the survey have indicated support for:
              1.   Preserving/enhancing open spaces and natural lands;
              2.   Maintaining and enhancing a strong and vibrant downtown;
              3.   Creating more household-sustaining jobs in the City; fostering local independent businesses;
              4.   Planning so the city's children, if they choose, can afford to live and work here when they grow up;
              5.   Maintaining and strengthening existing neighborhoods; and
              6.   Growing efficiently with climate resilience and careful resource use in mind (e.g. water, energy, public
                   infrastructure).
         The following draft vision statement has been created based upon the feedback received to this point: “Ogden City, with
a stunning mountain backdrop and a downtown renown as a cultural and entertainment destination, is at heart a place of people
committed to caring for one another and the city that we all love. We are a varied group, but we share a common purpose.
Together…Ogden City celebrates diversity, progress and possibility, creating space for individuals to feel valued, connected, and
empowered to reach their fullest potential today; and plans so that our children and grandchildren will also thrive as they live,
work, learn and play here in the future. To that end, Ogden City…
              • Nurtures, grows, and strengthens our neighborhoods for the well-being of all residents
              • Supports downtown, cultivating cultural, entertainment, business, and residential opportunities
              • Enhances centers of business and education for household-sustaining jobs
              • Preserves and improves our recreational and natural lands for all to enjoy
              • Provides access to opportunities, prioritizing active transportation and transportation choices
              • Grows responsibly with mindful use of resources, including water, air, energy, and public infrastructure
              • Together, we will create a community of opportunity, Ogden’s Way!”

Proposed Fiscal year 2024-2025 Fee Amendments – Police Department
         Deputy Police Chief Ziegler discussed the proposed mid-year fee amendments for the Police Department, which are
intended to align the fee schedule with current operations in the department. The proposed amendments eliminate fees for crime
scene photos, photographs for licenses, fees for a landlord training program, and fees for the towing rotation program. The
proposal includes the addition of fees for solicitor background checks and badges and adds accident reports to the police report
fee. There was brief discussion among the Council and Mr. Ziegler regarding the changes to the Good Landlord Program that led
to removal of the training fee for the program, as well as any adjustments to the vehicle towing program that influenced the
proposed fee amendments related to towing.

Public Services Department – Utilities
          Public Works Operations Director Ramos discussed the proposed mid-year fee amendments for the Public Services
related to recycling carts and tailings, or crushed asphalt, as green waste materials. The proposal would allow residents to pay for
a second recycling cart without the need to pay for a second refuse cart. The proposal would also allow tailings from road milling
to be purchased at the green waste site. Other code changes include updating the rates to reflect the most recent Consumer Price
Index (CPI) changes and other minor grammatical and formatting changes. Discussion among the Council and Mr. Ramos
centered on the location at the green waste site where tailings will be stored.

Public Services Department – Cemetery
          Parks, Cemetery, and Trails Director Stewart discussed the proposed mid-year fee amendments for the Public Services
related to cemetery operations. The proposed amendments increase cemetery rates for grave spaces, interments, overtime fees,
disinterment fees, and monument inspection fees. Administration staff have compared Ogden City’s fees to those of surrounding
cities with City owned cemeteries and have found Ogden City’s fees to have fallen below the midpoint. Brief discussion among
the Council and Mr. Stewart centered on the current capacity of the cemetery, as well as some of the improvement and
maintenance projects planned for the cemetery.

Public Services Department – Golf
          Golf Professional Brenkman reported on a proposal to increase cart fees for El Monte and Mt. Ogden courses, increase
certain green fees, and eliminate a discounted rate Monday-Thursday for 18-hole play. The last fee increase for green fees, season
passes, and punch tickets took effect on January 1, 2023. Historically, green fees are adjusted every two years in the local market.
With an increase in rounds played, many customers have expressed support for periodic fee adjustments, provided the quality of
the golf experience continues to improve. Significant investments, such as 62 new carts in January 2024 and major projects at Mt.
Ogden Golf Course and El Monte Golf Course, demonstrate the department's commitment to course improvements. Given the
competitive nature of the golf industry, it is sometimes necessary to raise fees to maintain a superior product while still offering
discounted rates for seniors, juniors, and military customers. He concluded by emphasizing that patrons support fee increases
when they see the revenues generated by the fees being reinvested into the golf courses, which has been the case in recent history.
There was brief discussion among the Council and Mr. Brenkman regarding the number of 9-hole rounds played in comparison to
18-hole rounds; removal of phragmite from the ponds at the courses; and other opportunities for discounted cart rates depending
on rounds played.

Proposed Fiscal Year 2024-2025 Budget Amendment – ARPA Funds
          Finance Director Sorensen reported Administration is proposing the reappropriation of funds from the American Rescue
Plan Act (ARPA). The City received $25,612,825 in federal ARPA funds and an additional $4,000,000 in state ARPA funds for a
total of $29,612,825. The Council has previously allocated ARPA funds for public safety radios, community grants, community
cleanup, Marshall White Center construction, the 36” canyon waterline replacement, and 4th Street Park netting. The remaining
funds were allocated to general government operations—primarily police and fire wages. As a policy, the city does not use one-
time funds to cover ongoing expenses such as wages, so the funds allocated to general government operations are available for
reappropriation to other projects. The Administration is recommending that the funds be reappropriated to the following:
              • Lester Park Improvements
              • Union Station Museum/Campus
              • Acquisition and Demolition of County Jail (Municipal Block)
              • General Park Improvements (All Abilities Park)
              • 4th Street Park Netting
          Additional funds from General Fund fund balance are proposed for transfer to the Fleet and Facilities equipment
replacement reserve. The total proposed budget amendment is $18,530,850. All ARPA Funds must be encumbered by December
31, 2024, and expended by December 31, 2026.
          The Council concluded they need more time to consider the proposed budget amendments, with Chair Richey directing
Council staff to include the matter on the December 10 work session agenda for continued discussion.
