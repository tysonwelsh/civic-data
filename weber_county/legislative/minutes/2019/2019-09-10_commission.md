F. PRESENTATION: UPDATE ON HUNTER AND COMPANY CONTRACT.
      Steve Hunter, of Hunter and Company, county lobbyist, gave highlights of the 2019 legislature and potential items
      for the 2020 session. For 2019 there were discussions on 911 issues to ensure a sustainable future for 911 Dispatch
      facilities across the State and especially in Weber County, about locating the inland port in Weber County as well
      as tax reform for the State. For 2020 he believes that there will be some type of infrastructure bonding package
      involving hundreds of millions if not billions of dollars, and he will make sure the county is involved as there are
      projects that deserve State attention. Over the years the county has focused the legislators’ attention on northern
      Utah infrastructure needs. The county has missed out on some things in the past and is now better positioned to get
      needed improvements. This is a key time for the county to move the ball on northern Utah road needs, particularly
      the I-84 interchange and the 5600 interchange by Hill Air Force Base. He said there would be bills about property
      taxes increasing based on inflation rather than truth in taxation hearings, on personal property for government use,
      on allowing more flexibility on how our transient room tax dollars are spent and perhaps lowering the 47%
      threshold, on public safety retirement adjustments, and potential talk of having the use of a portion of our 4th quarter
      cent sales tax qualifying for State matching funds. Commissioner Froerer had discussion with Senator Jerry
      Stevenson last week who indicated that the revenue prospects were looking good and Mr. Hunter also heard that
      revenues are expected positive again this year and there is the idea that they have been socking money away for a
      long time and may not need to put away as much. The Senator had indicated that the county may have a good
      opportunity to take the EDCUtah funds allocated last year and make them ongoing and Commissioner Froerer asked
      Mr. Hunter to stay on top of this item and to convey that this is as important as infrastructure for northern Utah.
      Commissioner Harvey noted that currently the I-84/89 interchange was not funded beyond the Weber River. Daily
      he sees the Davis, Weber and Morgan traffic severely backed up on Highway 89 coming into Weber County, an
      addition of 2,800 new jobs impact the 5600 interchange, etc., and infrastructure is needed. Chair Jenkins informed
      Mr. Hunter that he feels strongly about putting the sales tax back on food. Mr. Hunter recognized that several
      people in the county do a lot of work at UAC, such as Ricky Hatch, County Clerk/Auditor, and John Ulibarri,
      County Assessor, stating that our county does a great job of maintaining legislative relationships and doing the
      necessary work.

    2. CONTRACT WITH MATTHEW GOFF, MELISA HARRISON, DALE SATTERTHWAITE, AND KERRY WAHLEN
       TO ASSIST IN PAVING 7900 EAST, (BURLY LANE).
         Sean Wilkinson, County Community Development Director, stated that these four citizens approached the
         county about paving the road and are contributing generously to that cost. Chair Jenkins explained that the
         citizens were pushing to get the county to asphalt the road but there was no money available. These residents
         came up with their money to help seed the project to allow the county to do the work. Chair Jenkins stated that
         Commissioner Froerer has a conflict as he resides there, but he had not participated in this and did not
         necessarily support it. Commissioner Harvey noted that this is only for existing substandard roads and Mr.
         Wilkinson concurred. Commissioner Froerer said that some speed and safety mitigations will be done, that the
         road is plowed by the county and that this item makes economic sense for the county. Mr. Wilkinson stated
         that the county was fortunate to work with the property owners but did not expand the road to make it
         conforming, etc.; it is still a substandard road and the county is just improving the surface and safety issues.
         Commissioner Harvey moved to approve contract with Matthew Goff, Melisa Harrison, Dale Satterthwaite, and
         Kerry Wahlen to assist in paving 7900 East, (Burly Lane); Chair Jenkins seconded.
         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Froerer – abstained; Chair Jenkins – aye
