and

according to the laws of the State of Utah;

      COVID-19 and the consequential risks to the health, safety, and welfare of Bluffdale
      residents.

   3. The area affected or threatened is the entire City of Bluffdale.

   4. The condition which caused the emergency is COVID-19.

   5. This proclamation shall take effect immediately and be declared to be ongoing for a
      period of thirty (30) days, or until revoked, whichever is earlier.

       Policies and Procedures shall be ratified by a majority vote of the City Council at its next
       public meeting. If the City Council does not ratify these Electronic Meeting Policies and

                  ELECTRONIC MEETING POLICIES AND PROCEDURES
(



    I.    Policy:       This shall be known as the City of Bluffdale (the " City") Electronic
          Meeting Policy (the "Policy" ).

    II.   Electronic Meetings:

    A.    General:      A City public meeting (whether City Council, Planning Commission,
          Appeals and Variance Hearing Officer, Historical Preservation Commission, Bluffdale
          Arts Advisory Board, or other public body of the City) may be convened and conducted
          by means of telephonic, telecommunications, or computer conference by satisfying the
          requirements of Utah Code Ann. § 52-4-207.

    B.    Participation: The primary purpose for holding electronic meetings is to enable
          members of the public body to participate in the meeting electronically. Nevertheless,
          provision may be made for a member of the public to monitor an open meeting of the
          public body through electronic means provided that the member of the public so requests
          in writing at least three days prior to the meeting, and further provided that the City will
          not be required to acquire any equipment, facilities or expertise which the City does not
          already possess in order to accommodate the request. Notwithstanding anything to the
          contrary in this Policy, with the exception of a public hearing, the general public and
          other interested persons need not be provided an opportunity to participate in, as opposed
          to attend and monitor, an electronic meeting.

    C.    Anchor Location:       One or more anchor locations must be established for all electronic
          meetings. The anchor location is the physical location from which the electronic meeting
          originates or from which the participants are connected. At least one anchor location for
          an electronic meeting must be in the building where the public body would normally
          meet if not holding an electronic meeting. A quorum of the public body need not be
          present at a single anchor location for an electronic meeting to be held. As few as one
          member of the public body may be present at the anchor location, as long as all other
          requirements of this Policy and of Utah Code Ann. § 52-4-207 are satisfied for a meeting
          to be held as an electronic meeting, provided that the member of the public body who
          chairs the meeting is physically present at the anchor location. Space and facilities must
          be provided at the anchor location(s) so that all interested persons may attend and
          monitor the open portions of the meeting. In addition, if the meeting is a public hearing,
          space and facilities must be provided at the anchor location(s) so that interested persons
          and the public may attend, monitor and participate in the hearing. The City may limit the
          number of attendees of the public at the anchor location during a local, state, or national
          emergency.

    D.    Notice: Not less than 24 hours' advance public notice, including the agenda, date, time,
          location, and a description of how the Board Members will be connected to the electronic
          meeting, will be given for each electronic meeting of the Board by posting a written
          notice at the principal office of the City (or at the building where the meeting is to be held
         if no principal office exists) and providing written or electronic notice to at least one
         newspaper of general circulation in the City and to a local media correspondent, and by
         posting the notice on the Utah Public Notice Website created under Utah Code Ann. §
         63 F-1-70 I. In addition, the notice must be posted at the anchor location (which may be
         the principal office of the City) and must be provided to all members of the public body
         at least 24 hours before the meeting. These notice requirements are minimum
         requirements and are not to be construed as precluding such additional postings and
         notifications as may be directed by the Board.

E.       Budget or Logistical Considerations: The Chair, or the Vice-Chair in the Chair's
         absence, may determine, based upon budget or logistical considerations, that it is not in
         the best interest of the City to hold an electronic meeting, in which event the meeting will
         not be held as an electronic meeting. The Chair, or the Vice-Chair in the Chair's
         absence, may also restrict the number of separate electronic connections that are allowed
         for an electronic meeting based on available equipment capacity. The request from a
         member of the public to participate in a meeting electronically may be denied by the
         Chair, or Vice-Chair in the Chair's absence, based on budget, public policy or logistical
         considerations deemed sufficient by the Chair or Vice-Chair.

F.       Conduct of Meeting: No action may be taken and no business may be conducted at a
         meeting of the public body unless a quorum, consisting of a simple majority of the voting
         members of the public body, is present. A member of the public body who is not
         physically present may nevertheless participate in the meeting through electronic means
         and be counted toward the required quorum in accordance with Utah Code Ann. § 52-4-
         207. Any member of the public body participating via electronic means may make,
         second and vote on all motions and participate in the discussion as though present, except
         that the member who chairs the meeting must be present at the anchor location. If neither
         the Chair nor the Vice Chair is physically present at the anchor location (but there is still
         a quorum) a member of the public body who is physically present at the anchor location
         will preside over the meeting.

RECOMMENDATION: To approve the General Plan Land Use Map Amendment Application (2020-11) for 4.50
acres as recommended by the Planning Commission on April 15, 2020.

BACKGROUND: The property which is the subject of this application is currently in a predominantly
residential area and identified as Very Low Density Residential in the General Plan. This land use designation
provides large lot and agriculture development. The applicants are requesting to change the land use
designation to Multi-Family Residential which allows a range of density from 4 to 20 units per acre. This land
use would provide the opportunity for future developments to varied residential units including single-family,
multi-family townhomes, condominiums or apartments, or smaller units for senior housing. These higher
density areas may be located adjacent to commercial land uses and areas where adequate infrastructure
exists or can be provided to support the residents.

Although the applicant has provided information to the City of their intent to seek for senior housing for this
property, the City’s current zoning structure does not provide for these types of residential land uses within
the code. The multi-family residential land use designation would provide for all future opportunities for
growth within the density and land use description. If an amendment to the General Plan Land Use map for
this application is approved to Multi-Family Residential, the City would need to consider adopting a new
zoning designation with accompanying development standards and requirements. The City Council should
consider all the impacts of potentially changing the land use policy in this area and not specific development
plans.

FINDINGS FOR APPROVAL:
The Planning Commission recommendation for approval of the General Plan Land Use Map Amendment
Application is based on the following findings:
   1. That the General Plan identifies as a goal to create more zoning or development tools to provide for
       other types of multi-family residential options which may include senior housing.
   2. That providing the land use designation of Multi-Family Residential would provide a transitional
       buffer from low density residential to future commercial uses.
   3. That providing the opportunity for multi-family residential to this area of the community for senior
       housing would still maintain the character of the area and is located close to civic and commercial
       uses supported by a higher traffic corridor.

SUMMARY
The City of Bluffdale’s General Plan (Adopted March 25, 2014) provides a Land Use Map illustrating areas
for current and possible future growth opportunities for all land use types desired by the City. The purpose
of the General Plan is to establish policies, goals and objectives in balancing future residential growth and
economic opportunities while maintaining the overall unique attributes the City desires for the residents.
The City of Bluffdale General Plan should be a guideline in making land use decisions by the Land Use
Authority which is the City Council for this application following a recommendation by the Planning
Commission.

ANALYSIS
General Plan: The property which is the subject of this application is currently in a predominantly
residential area and identified as Very Low Density Residential in the General Plan. This land use
designation provides large lot and agriculture development. The applicants are requesting to change the
land use designation to Multi-Family Residential which allows a range of density from 4 to 20 units per
acre. This land use would provide the opportunity for future developments to varied residential units
including single-family, multi-family townhomes, condominiums or apartments, or smaller units for senior
housing. These higher density areas may be located adjacent to commercial land uses and areas where
adequate infrastructure exists or can be provided to support the residents.

Land uses within the corridor from Bangerter Highway to 14000 South have recently been changed from
residential to commercial uses in anticipation of future economic growth for the City. Future changes are
also anticipated for the Bangerter Highway intersection at 2700 west including a reconstructed highway

General Plan Land Use Map amendments are a legislative decision of the City Council, after receiving a

property owners, adequate infrastructure, and meeting the overall needs of the community for future
residential and economic growth are all valid considerations when making land use decisions. The
Planning Commission should establish adequate findings in support of a positive or negative outcome and

This legal description was prepared using a Record of Survey done by Prism Land Surveying
for the Midvalley Bible Church in September of 2018. The basis of bearing for this survey was

        Re: Springview Farms – Sage Estates Phase III

Dear Planning Commissioners,

Stonefly Development Corporation (a related entity to GMMN and CW Management Corporation) are
purchasing the Gerald C. Parry property located at 14135 South Loumis Parkway consisting of 7.77 acres.
That parcel together with our .90 parcel (owned by GMMN) of property, located adjacent to Sage
Estates Phase I is being developed into single family homesite. The details of the project are as follows:

    1. The project name shall be Sage Estates Phase III (Sage III) and will be annexed into the
       Springview Farms community by our company as we are the original Declarant/Developer of
       Springview Farms.

    2. Stonefly Development will annex Sage Estates Phase III into the Springview Farms Homeowners
       Association requiring them to meet or exceed the same exact standards associated with
       Springview Farms Sage Estates Phase I (with a couple modifications).

    3. As mentioned, Sage III shall be part of the existing Springview Farms Homeowners Association
       and therefore be subjected to the annual dues and potential future assessments as found in the
       existing CCR’s that can be found at www.springviewfarms.com.

    4. Sage III will landscape the park strip along Loumis Parkway which when completed will be
       managed by the HOA.

    5. A summary of the homes MODIFIED design guidelines is:
           a. 1700 square feet above grade for a single‐story home and 2050 for two story homes
              (above grade).
           b. Homes may not exceed 38 feet in height from the top back of curbing on the street.
           c. Setbacks will be the same as required by the CCR’s for Sage Estates Phases I/II (25’ front,
              10’ sides and 20’ rear).
           d. Home designs must comply with all other CCR’s and Design Guidelines for Springview
              Farms.

                                                    1
    6. In our preliminary meeting with the Bluffdale City staff, they commented that the present
       design makes it so pedestrian traffic would need to go out to the north or east to walk to the a
       greater distance to the neighboring areas such as Sage Estates I and II. To resolve the
       pedestrian issue, they suggested a thru street be constructed over the Rose crest drainage ditch.

        Stonefly looked at this option and quite frankly it’s a bit redundant with the original master
        planned street connections from Sage I and II that will need to be connected when the Johnson
        property (not part of this application) to the south is developed. However, we believe the
        pedestrian routing without a connection would be a bit cumbersome and Stonefly has re‐
        drafted the concept plan to stub a trail between lots 14 and 15 to the County Rose Creek
        drainage property for future completion by others.

    7. Even though the lots in Sage Estates have, for the most part frontages that are less than the
       subject property (at or around 85’) the average frontages on the Sage Estates Phase III lots are
       at or around 97‐ 100 feet which will provide a more spacious atmosphere in this phase.

    8. Storm water management shall be provided through the detention basins provided in Sage
       Estates Phase Ic. These storm water detention basins are oversized and should provide enough
       area to accommodate the Sage III need. We will provide an engineered certificate showing this
       to be the case during the approval process or design a separate parcel for detention basins on
       site.

At the conclusion of the lot development process, Alpine Homes will be the purchaser of the building
lots. We are pleased to be partnering with such a fine homebuilder in Springview Farms. After the
conclusion of the construction process of the lots and the homes, we are positive that Sage Estates
Phase III will be a great addition to the Springview Farms community.

Lastly, this addition into the community will provide an estimated $11,000 per year in HOA dues
providing financial relief to the HOA annual costs associated with managing the private portions of the
141 acres of open space provide to all Bluffdale City residents through the Springview Farms
homeowner’s management of the parks, trails and other aesthetic improvements.
