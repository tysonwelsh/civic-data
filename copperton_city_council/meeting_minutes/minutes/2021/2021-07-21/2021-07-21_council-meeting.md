Rio Tinto Dust Mitigation

               Council Member Stitzer stated Rio Tinto is working on a dust report. She
introduced Brendan Murphy with Rio Tinto who is working on the report. Typically, the report is
given to the Community Council, but Rio Tinto will give an update to the Metro Council today.

                Brendan Murphy, General Manager for Minerals, stated he has been in Salt Lake
City for three months but with Rio Tinto for three years. He introduced Tyler Jolley who has been
with Rio Tinto for 15 years.

               Tyler Jolley reviewed Kennecott’s Dust Management Strategy. He discussed dust
management and the three components to ensure Kennecott meets it health, safety, and
environmental goals. He continued with the watering strategy, chemical dust suppressions,
locations of the mines, dust spotting and documentation, dust mitigation process, and the
escalation process for complaints received outside the mine.

Change to Copperton Code

               Mayor Clayton stated there would be a change to Copperton code for Accessory
Dwelling Units (ADU). Currently, an outbuilding is a maximum of 800 square feet, cannot be more
than 25 percent of the yard, and requires a conditional use permit with a lot of fees. Some
residents have asked the Council to look at this, although the issue needs to go to the planning
commission first.

He would like the planning commission to look into changing the ordinance to allow for more
outbuildings, perhaps using a percentage of the total lot. Specifically, he would like the Council
to provide direction to the Planning Commission to look at Section 14-2 to remove the limitations
on the square footage but keep the height requirements. He would like something for adoption at
the August meeting.

              Nathan Bracken stated he will put together a draft revision for the planning
commission to review. There must be a ten-day public notice before the planning commission
can hold a hearing. Then they can make a recommendation to the Council.

 Council Member Clayton

   Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD)

                   Mayor Clayton stated the MSD has met twice since the last Council meeting.
   They are working on budgets, policy changes, capital projects and a strategic plan. As head of
   the audit committee, he was concerned about how the financials are being reported; the metros
   are reported in a “blended” way as if the metros are a “child” to the MSD “parent.” This will change
   next year. Copperton needs to have its own financial statements rather than being blended into
   the MSD’s finances.
