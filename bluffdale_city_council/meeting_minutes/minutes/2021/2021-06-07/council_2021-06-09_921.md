Mr. Olsen explained that this is a bi.g shift in the JVWCD policy. The Board of Trustees and staff
have worked for years to work out how to proceed with the water efficiency standards. They
observed the situation of other western water providers such as drought responses in California
and Nevada where water providers have been forced to take expensive emergency measures
because they did not plan ahead for their development. He explained that many of the new
measures by NWCD will not only keep costs of water down but makes them more drought

Mr. Olsen described some of the benefits of the new standards. He explained that sunm1er peak
use will reduce and result in lower infrastructure costs. There are also lower conservation
expenditure costs such as having new construction plan appropriate landscaping rather than having
to pay for turf removal later. He explained that the cost of removing landscaping is five times
more than the initial correct installation. The component for indoor standards exists but they do
not have the jurisdiction for changing plumbing codes. At some point in the future, Utah State
Code may develop better standards for water-efficient fixtures .

Mr. Olsen explained that there is a new ordinance for residential landscaping. Front and side yards
will follow the Localscapes method, which is a design methodology for water-efficient
landscaping. It includes a 35% maximum for turf. Mr. Olsen rep01ied that they want grass lawns
in areas where it is functional and not the default cover. He outlined several scenarios and issues
such as drip irrigation, planting beds, small lots, and the City's right to make exceptions in small
lot cases. Commercial landscaping will follow many of the same standards. Active recreation
areas such as parks are exempt. There is a 20% turf maximum outside of an active recreation area.

Mr. Olsen explained that the JVWCD acknowledged that the new standards may impact cities
looking to adopt the new standards and so there are two member agency grants available. JVWCD
is offering the ability for them to pull in $52,500 per year for about three years to go toward
training, consultation services, software updates, and other transition issues. Herriman and South
Jordan have both adopted the standards. West Jordan was considering it.

4.      Consideration and Vote on Resolution No. 2021-29, Adopting Certain Restrictions on
        the Use of Fireworks within Certain Areas of the City, Staff Presenter, Fire Chief
        Warren James.

Fire Chief, Warren James, repo1ied that the City was supposed to have fireworks restrictions
announced by June 1. Chief James explained that he does not take the issue lightly. In his capacity
as Fire Chief, he would prefer a City-wide mass restriction but knows that is not realistic or fair.
He sent out 12 crews and identifi ed areas the Fire Department is most concerned about. The things
they looked at were fuel height and level, dryness, access, proximity to homes, and evacuation.
Chief James emailed a map to the City Council showing that Salt Lake County is in the exceptional
drought stage. The email also included the Division of Natural Resources Fire Prediction Map. It
was noted that Bluffdale is in the highest category for brushfires. Most of the concern petiained
to the lack of rain.

meter readings were conducted and were concerning. He displayed photos of the effects of fire on
different areas and homes. He addressed the threat of Scrub Oak and how Porter Rockwell
Boulevard is not an adequate fire break in the Parry Farms area. Restricted areas in Bluffdale were
identified as well as in Draper, Herriman, and Riverton.

Chief James was to be given more authority in the resolution to add more restrictions if needed.
He clarified that if the Fire Department notices an area of high ri sk or if conditions get worse, they
could add additional areas to the restrictions. He explained that firefighters will go door-to-door
to explain the situation to the residents east of Porter Rockwell Boulevard. The Fire Department
also plans to provide information on social media and online. It was recommended that they do
door-to-door outreach in Bluffdale Heights. Chief James stated that the intent was to perfonn
educational outreach everywhere. Mayor Timothy supported the idea of giving Chief James the
authority to place restrictions.

critical drought situations addressed concerning indoor and outdoor residential, irrigation for the
City, industrial, and agricultural. The rate of water increase as the water supply becomes closer to
critical. In a moderate year, there is a non-enforced reduction in irri gation and watering of grass.
