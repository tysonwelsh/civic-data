Unified Fire Authority (UFA)

                Chief Dominic Burchett introduced himself stating he started his career in fire in
1997 as a wildland fire fighter. Since then, he has been a paramedic, Captain, Division Chief, and
Assistant Chief. When appointed as the fire Chief, he developed a 100 day plan. His first 100
days in office, he met with employees at each fire station during each of their shifts. He is also
meeting with every municipality and its council. The plan is to prioritize the information he received
from those meetings. The critical area that needs to be focused on is the health and well-being of
the staff. UFA is in a really good position because of the work Chief Peterson accomplished.
There is not anything that needs a major overhaul, but continuous improvement is the goal moving
forward. UFA is a complex organization that serves areas in Emigration Canyon to Eagle
Mountain. As a fire department, UFA needs to be here to give municipalities what it needs and
deserves as a community. Chief Prokopis is Magna’s liaison, he acts as Magna’s voice.

Pleasant Green Cemetery Caretake Services

              Paul Ashton stated an agreement with N&W Enterprises LLC was entered into a
year ago to provide caretaking services at Pleasant Green Cemetery. Originally the agreement
was to be for multiple years; however, when the selection group met with Nunny Nichols, it

decided to go with a one-year contract. This gave Mr. Nichols the opportunity to see what the
costs would be and if he wanted to continue to do this type of work. Mr. Nichols has sent out

Copper Park Lease

                Council Member Barney stated in previous meetings, discussions about the
ownership of Copper Park were brought up. He reached out to Kennecott and located the
agreement that was approved in 2019. The agreement that was approved and signed is a five-
year lease with a one-year renewal. Magna Metro Township does not own any of the property.
There are two parcels that exist next to each other. The property being leased to Magna Metro
Township encompasses part of one parcel and part of the other one. The Council and Kennecott
has the right to terminate the lease with a written notice. At the time of termination, Kennecott will
retain all improvements or additions made to the park.

In addition, the Utah Inland Port Authority maps have included all of Kennecott owned property
within Magna Metro Township, which includes vacant properties, golf course, Copper Park, and
all agricultural properties south of SR 201 into its jurisdictional maps. Any of that property could
be developed at anytime by the Utah Inland Port Authority, Magna Metro Township has no
authority over it. He reached out to Kennecott to see what its intention is with the park, and they
said the property is used as a buffer between the tailings pond and the community and have no
intention of doing anything different. Sales tax from any potential commercial development within
Utah Inland Port Authority’s jurisdiction will be given to them, Magna Metro Township would not
be able to collect those funds.

              Paul Ashton stated it would make sense to go back to Kennecott and ask for a
longer lease agreement.

               Council Member Barney stated while discussing the lease agreement with
Kennecott, he learned that because of prior incidents, they are not interested in long term leases.

              Mayor Peay stated since this is a community park, Magna Metro Township would
be responsible for putting in the playgrounds.

             Council Member Barney stated maintenance, upkeep, additions, and
improvements would all need to be funded by the Magna Metro Township.

                 Clare Collard, Utah State Representative, stated with new leadership coming into
the legislature, she thinks representation on the Utah Inland Port Authority Board is worth
revisiting at the next legislative session. Originally, it was determined that Magna Metro Township
did not have enough land mass to be included on the board.

               Council Member Prokopis stated there is about 2,000 acres.

               Ms. Collard stated that is not an insignificant amount.

               Council Member Prokopis stated with this information, he does not think that
should hold the Council back from developing Copper Park for the community.

              Council Member Hull stated she obtained a $4,000 grant for fencing that needs
to be used by December 2022. What are the next steps.

Amendments to the Code Enforcement Penalties

                Paul Ashton stated under Title 12 of the Magna Metro Township Code, the
Council has the authority to amend code enforcement penalties. The Council needs to notify the
Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD) Code Enforcement that it has authority to
exercise Title 12 including enforcement of repeat offenses.

               Alicia Gonzalez, Code Enforcement Supervisor, Greater Salt Lake Municipal
Services District (MSD), stated she wants to make sure the Council authorizes code enforcement
to proceed with repeat offense citations. The change will require a lot of support from the Council
and code enforcement as there will be a lot of push back from residents.

               Council Member Barney stated from his understanding, a resident with a first
time offense that receives a citation will have 30 days before they can be charged a penalty. If
the resident is a repeat offender, a 30-day notice is not necessary because they have already
received a prior notice.

              Mr. Ashton stated the Council will need to review the 2023 fee schedule and
include the amended code enforcement penalty fees. Bryan Hartsell, Associate General Manager,
MSD, suggested the Council wait until January to implement the change.

                Ms. Gonzalez stated waiting until January will also allow enough time to work with
Nathan Bracken, Smith Hartvigsen, to develop the citation notice. Code enforcement will also
need to know how long a resident should be given to correct the offense and how many times it
is allowed to issue a citation within a period of time.

             Mayor Peay stated he does not want to wait until January, he would like to begin
enforcing immediately.
