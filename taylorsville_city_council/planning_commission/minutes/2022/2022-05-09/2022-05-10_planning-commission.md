no site plan or development concerns with this request.

4.1 Mr. Spung presented this item. He said that today’s review is to introduce the proposal. He then
did a project overview of the project. John Buckel (Baidee Development) is requesting Planning Commission
review of a preliminary non-administrative conditional use permit and site plan to allow the construction
of an industrial office warehouse project at 4202 South Riverboat Road, Taylorsville, Utah the development
proposal includes five buildings that would create 58,250 square feet of office space and 244,851 square
feet of warehouse space on the currently undeveloped 24.41 acre site. Given the size and complexity of
the application, the Planning Staff does not recommend the Planning Commission take action during this
meeting; however, it is intended that this item will be presented to the Planning Commission for formal
consideration and action at a later date. The intent of the meeting this evening is to: (1) Introduce the
application and concept development plan to the Planning Commission; (2) Hold a public hearing; (3)
Review the Planning Commission’s decision-making authority; (4) Allow the applicant to engage with the
Planning Commission; and (5) Solicit feedback from the Planning Commission to inform design of the
development proposal.

4.1.1 Site Description: The property is approximately 24 acres of land near the Jordan River. The project
area is currently vacant except for a high-transmission powerline which runs north/south through
the site along the western property line and the Jordan River Parkway Trail which also runs
north/south along the east boundary adjacent to the river. Salt Lake County-owned and operated
Meadow Brook Golf Course is adjacent to the property on the west. This development would
expand the current developed area to the south which has a mix of businesses (Sorensen Research
Park) and multi-unit residential properties (Maison’s Landing). The point of access is the existing
Riverboat Road, which is currently used to access the surrounding businesses. The project is
primarily flat open land with mixed vegetation. There are several constraints affecting the
property that will influence development potential and design. These constraints include but are
not limited to: flood hazards; wetlands; geological/geotechnical hazards; limited site access;
existing easements; and established recreation amenities. He then went on to discuss each issue.

4.1.2 Flood Hazards. The property has a FEMA Special Flood Hazard Area (SFHA) of Zone AE with a
floodway and is located in the Jordan River meander corridor. Since the project is in a Zone AE
with a floodway, fill will need to be placed and any buildings will be required to be above the Base
Elevation (BFE) and no impacts on the floodway. This will also require either a letter of Map
Revision based on Fill (LOMR-F) or a standard Letter of Map Revision (LOMR). If the proposed
project will cause the water surface to increase by more than one foo t or impact the floodway, a
Conditional Letter of Map Revision (CLOMR) would be required. The applicant is working with a
consulting firm to obtain a Conditional Letter of Map Revision based on Fill (CLOmR-F) be
completed to obtain assurances that the project will be approved by FEMA before proceeding with
final design, construction, and a LOMR-F. As the project is in the Jordan River meander corridor,
lateral migration erosion protection will be required to project future buildings from lateral erosion
hazards. This could include adding protection to the banks of the river or other method such as
buried riprap trench away from the banks that would stop the banks from eroding once it reached
the trench. The applicant’s consultant believes that adding a riprap trench adjacent to the Jordan
River Trail would be the best option as it would protect the property and trail from erosion.
4.1.2.1 In addition to the FEMA requirements, a Stream Alteration Permit, Flood Control Permit,

Floodplain Development Permit, Endangered Species Act (ESA) compliance, and other

4.1.3. Wetlands:
4.1.3.1 Due to the property’s FEMA floodplain designation and its proximity to the Jordan River,
the City required the applicant to provide a wetlands study/report to identify potential
impacts to existing wetland areas. The applicant’s consultant Bowen, Collins &
Associates, prepared an “Aquatic Resources Delineation Report” for the site. The purpose
of which was to identify channel boundaries of the Jordan River and any potential
wetlands in preparation for permitting and construction on the property.
4.1.3.2 Aquatic resources were observed along the north and eastern edges of the project area.
This is where the project area boundary based on parcel lines crossed into the Jordan
River and the wetland fringe along parts of its banks. The portions of the Jordan River
identified within the project area total 1,038 linear feet or 0.37 acres. This channel has
hydrology year-round which was present at the time of the delineation field inspection.
The emergent wetlands identified within the project area total 0.12 acres. These
wetlands are along the bank of the Jordan River, from which they source hydrology.
Based on current information, no wetlands exist west of the Jordan River Parkway Trail.
4.1.4 Geological/Geotechnical Hazards:
4.1.4.1 The property is within a “High Liquefaction Area”, meaning there is a 50 percent
probability of having an earthquake within a 100-year period that will be strong enough
to cause liquefaction. Liquefaction takes place when loosely packed, water-logged
sediments at or near the ground surface lose their strength in response to strong ground
shaking, resulting | material that is ordinarily solid behave like a liquid.
4.1.4.2 Due to this site condition, the applicant has submitted a Geotechnical Study to evaluate
the subsurface soil and groundwater conditions across the site. The results of this study
inform appropriate foundation, earthwork, pavement, and _— geo-seismic
recommendations for the design and construction of the proposed development. Boring
samples were taken at 27 locations on the site and show liquefiable soils present across
the site. To best mitigate the potentially liquefiable soil conditions, the geotechnical
consultant (GSH Geotechnical, Inc) recommends that the proposed structures be
supported upon soil reinforcement methods such as a grid of rammed-aggregate piers. A
more detailed technical review ensuring compliance with all applicable building codes
would take place prior to issuance of any development permits on the site.
4.1.4.3. The High liquefaction designation of the property means it is subject to the Sensitive
Area Overlay Zone.

4.1.5 Limited Site Access: The subject property has one means of ingress and egress via Riverboat Road,
which terminates as a stub street at the southern edge of the property. The proposed
development plan includes the addition of a cul-de-sac to provide a proper terminus to the existing
street. The cul-de-sac would be dedicated as a public street through the subdivision platting
process. There are few opportunities to improve connectivity to the site because the property is
bounded by the Jordan River to the north and east and a Salt Lake County-owned golf course to
the west. Pedestrians and cyclists may access the property via Riverboat Road or the Jordan River
Parkway.
4.1.6 Amenities: The Jordan River Trail runs parallel to the Jordan River and runs north/south along the
eastern portion of the subject property. Portions of the Jordan River and nearly all of the Jordan
River Parkway traversing the subject property are within the property boundaries The Jordan
River and Jordan River Parkway make up approximately 2.38 acres (103,628 square feet) of the
subject property. The Jordan River parkway is the centerpiece of an extensive trail system in the
City and also serves as the primary backbone of a regional transportation system, spanning
approximately 44 miles from Utah Lake to the Great Salt Lake.
