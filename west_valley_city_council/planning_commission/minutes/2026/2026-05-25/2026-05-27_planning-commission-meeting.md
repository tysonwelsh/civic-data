BACKGROUND:

106, 7-5-101, 7-6-201, 7-12-105, and 7-7-115.5 and add Section 7-7-107.5 to comply with

Each of these changes is covered below by topic. The proposed ordinance changes for topics
1-4 are found in the attached document entitled “SB 284 Changes”. The proposed ordinance
language for topic 5 is found in the attached document entitled “Detached Accessory Dwelling

Topic 1: New or Unlisted Uses
This change allows applicants to bypass the Planning Commission and go straight to the City
Council when requesting that a new or unlisted use be added to the Zoning Ordinance.

Topic 2: Removal of Planning Commissioners
This change describes the causes for which a Planning Commission member may be removed
from the Planning Commission. These causes are:
    • using public funds for a political purpose;
    • violating the Municipal Officers’ and Employees’ Ethics Act; and
    • acting with the intent to influence a land use decision in a manner that creates
       impermissible bias.
Topic 3: Zoning Amendments
This change no longer requires applicants of zoning amendments who have received a denial
recommendation from the Planning Commission to submit an appeal to the City Council. All
zoning amendments, regardless of the Commission’s recommendation, would automatically go
to the City Council like General Plan/zone changes.

Topic 4: Appeals
This change would vary the standards of review for appeals based on whether the applicant is
a land use applicant or an adversely affected party. The change also limits who can speak
during an appeal hearing.

Topic 5: Detached Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs)
Of all the proposed changes, these are the most impactful. Currently, the City only allows
internal ADUs under certain conditions (see Section 7-7-115.5) and detached ADUs are
prohibited. Effective October 1, 2026, cities must allow detached ADUs on single family home

lots that are 11,000 square feet or larger and where the zoning allows single family homes.
While cities can regulate things like placement, height, area, and parking, State code limits
cities’ ability to regulate. For example, cities can require additional parking for a detached
ADU; however, cities can’t require more than one space for a detached ADU that is smaller
than 650 square feet and no more than two spaces for a detached ADU that is 650 square feet
or larger.

To help the Commission understand the impact of this change, staff used the City’s GIS to
identify lots that would be eligible for a detached ADU following the amendment. Of the 27,296
parcels with single family homes, 3,766 (13.8%) are 11,000 square feet or larger and are
within a zone that allows single family homes.

As allowed by State code, staff is proposing regulations to govern:
   • the minimum lot size required;
   • the type of zoning required;
   • the number of ADUs allowed on a parcel;
   • the provision of adequate utility access and capacity;
   • compliance with building, health, and fire codes;
   • placement, height, and area;
   • design consistency with the home on the same parcel;
   • owner occupancy of either the detached ADU or the home;
   • the conversion of a legally constructed accessory building into a detached ADU;
   • parking; and
   • licensing for rented, detached ADUs and the minimum rental period.

the current owner originally wanted to build the project based on the previous approval. Due to
an earthquake fault line that runs through the site, that is no longer possible. This request is to
relocate the buildings away from the fault line and outside of the required fault line setbacks.

PROJECT OVERVIEW:

The proposal includes 18, 3-story townhome units, divided between three buildings. There will
be a 5-unit building, a 6-unit building, and a 7-unit building. Access to the property will be
gained from 4100 South and from Redwood Road. The original subdivision plat contained
cross access easements for both the 4100 South and Redwood Road connections.

Buildings

Per the development agreement, units are required to have at least 2,400 sq. ft. of finished
floor area. The development agreement requires that the building elevations be built
substantially like the elevations shown in Exhibit I. The building elevations also need to comply
with all materials, building relief, building design, window treatments, and roof treatments
outlined in what was section 7-11-400 of the City Code at the time of the development
agreement. Staff has reviewed the proposed building elevations, and several modifications are
required to meet both the development agreement and the multi-unit dwelling design
standards. These are briefly outlined below:

       •    Brick or stone is required on the entire first level of each building (front, back and
            sides).

       •    A cap/cornice is required along the top of the roof line.

The code requires that when placing a building next to a residential property line, the maximum
height at the minimum building setback is 25’. Where the building is over 25’ in height, the
building must be set back one additional foot for each foot in height (7-6-210(6)). All the
buildings meet this requirement, except for Building 3 at the north-west corner of the building.
The building at that corner requires a 26-foot setback from the residential property line. The
building in the current proposal is roughly 24’ from property line to the foundation, and only 19’
to the cantilevered deck of the building. The building and/or site layout need to be adjusted so
that the setback requirement in the code is met to all portions of the building.

Landscaping

The development agreement states that the landscaping on the site needs to be substantially
like what was shown in Exhibit C of the agreement. The proposed plan generally meets that
requirement. Some additional trees need to be added along the west property line so that they
are spaced 20’ apart. The current proposal has 33.3% landscaping on the site.

Parking

Each unit will have a garage of at least 400 sq. ft. At the time the property was rezoned,
townhomes only required two parking spaces per unit. The apartment project provided 8 more
parking stalls than were required in their conditional use proposal, so some of those stalls
could be shared with the townhomes. The applicant has also added 16 additional parking stalls
on the townhome parcel.
