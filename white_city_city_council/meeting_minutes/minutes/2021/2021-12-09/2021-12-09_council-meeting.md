    Engineering Update

                Shane Ellis, Engineer, Salt Lake County Engineering and Flood Control, stated
Sandy City has declined to move forward with the White City Canal Trail. Salt Lake County Parks
and Recreation is still working on it and will continue to work with the Council to determine what
direction it wants to go.
DATE             THURSDAY                                 DECEMBER                              9, 2021

              Mayor Flint asked for an update on the light that is supposed to be installed at the
lower end of Larkspur, near 700 East.

               Mr. Ellis stated Kenny Barrier, Salt Lake County Public Works Operations, has
tried contacting the owner of the home to discuss the installation of the light and has only been
able to leave a message. At the direction of the Council, Mr. Barrier is waiting to speak to the
owner of the property before installing the light.

              Mayor Flint stated a pole needs to be installed for the light on Larkspur, it is close
to the corner but in the right-of-way. She suggested the owner be contacted because of the
proximity.

                Paul Ashton stated since the light will be in the right-of-way, engineering does not
need permission to install it. He suggested that if Mr. Barrier cannot get a hold of the owner to
send a certified letter as a courtesy.

                 Mayor Flint stated if there is not a response to the letter, move forward with the
installation of the light. She also has concerns about traffic coming from Alta View Elementary.
When parents pick up their children, they are creating 2 lanes of traffic on the one-lane road.
Individuals going east do not have much room to get by. How can that issue be fixed, can lines
be painted? It needs to be looked at for safety purposes.

                Council Member Little stated painted lines would be good.

               Mr. Ellis stated there has not been any progress made on the 9400 south Ski
Connect or the 730 east sidewalks. Both projects are almost ready to go out to bid and work can
begin in Spring. The sidewalk master plan inventory was completed, and the engineers will draft
a preliminary concept design to get a better cost estimate. He submitted a grant to WFRC for
sidewalks on Onyx Lane.

                Council Member Perry asked for an update on the 10600 crosswalk near the
canal trail.

              Mr. Ellis stated he did not think they would be able to proceed with the light at the
crosswalk. Sandy City will not help because it believes the light will be too close to the traffic light.
The Walk White City committee will hold a kickoff meeting on Saturday.

Unified Police Department Report
DATE            THURSDAY                                 DECEMBER                             9, 2021


                  Chief Randy Thomas stated Detective Jeff Fenton is working with Altaview
Elementary on the school zone and parking issues. More officers are patrolling the school zones.
Fraud crime is up in White City and that includes packages being stolen from porches. There
have been numerous complaints on flags that are being flown and there is not a lot UPD can do
as it is a constitutional right to be able to display those flags. He has reviewed his precincts budget
including operational expenses. The precincts contract janitorial and maintenance services, when
the contract expired, he went out for an RFP. The RFPs came back with a 300 percent increase
in fees. Instead of hiring full-time services, he hired a part-time individual and only incurred an 80
percent increase. Domestic violence cases where strangulation is involved requires a forensic kit
for the District Attorney to review the case. The forensic kits cost $600 each and must be paid for
by the precinct. He has talked to legislators about getting a grant to cover these costs.

Resolution No. 21-12-01 – Google Fiber License Agreement

               Paul Ashton stated the Google Fiber License Agreement is almost complete.
Jacob Brace, Government Affairs, Google Fiber, is here to answer questions regarding some of
the language in the agreement. One of the concerns with the language is references to ariel poles.
DATE            THURSDAY                                DECEMBER                             9, 2021

                Council Member Little stated he will have a hard time supporting the agreement
with Google Fiber having the option to install on poles. He would like a change in the language to
state that if Google Fiber must use a pole, it will come back to the Council for approval.

                 Jacob Brace, Government Affairs, Google Fiber, stated it is the goal of Google
Fiber to have 100 percent of its fiber optics installed underground. If a situation comes up where
fiber optics are unable to be installed underground, there is an option to use poles so that
residents will still have internet access. When doing this type of work, it is better to have all the
tools available and that includes installation on poles, but it is the last resort. Before ariel
installation is started, Google Fiber must get approval through the permitting process. If the pole
belongs to another municipality or utility provider, it will have to get approved through the provider
and the engineering office could also stop the permit.

               Mr. Ashton asked if the language could be changed to address the procedure for
using poles.

               Council Member Cardenaz asked how many times a pole has had to be used in
a community.

               Mr. Brace stated Google Fiber has not had to attach to a pole, it is the last case
scenario.

                Council Member Little stated there was a discussion about utility boxes being
installed in parking strips and the Council was told that there would not be utility boxes installed.
Can language reflecting that information be added to the contract?

             Mr. Brace stated in the most recent deployments, the only thing that has been
above ground are the Google Fiber Huts.

               Paul Ashton stated the next concern is regarding 2.15 – Access to Services. The
provision gives Google Fiber the right to give the scope of construction and timing. The language
could be interpreted as giving Google Fiber the right to serve only portions of White City and leave
out other areas. There needs to be some assurance that the entire metro township will be
serviced.

               Mr. Brace stated 100 percent of White City has been designed and it is the
intention of Google Fiber to build that out 100 percent. On occasion there are properties like
HOAs, that do not allow access to its properties; therefore, it cannot be guaranteed that services
can be built out in those areas. They do work with private streets to gain access.

                Council Member Little asked if it can be assumed that if Google Fiber has a public
right-of-way, the property will be served.

               Mr. Brace stated that is the intent.
DATE            THURSDAY                                DECEMBER                             9, 2021

               Mr. Ashton stated he would like to add language to that paragraph to better
explain that situation. He asked if the Council had any other concerns that needed to be
addressed.

                Council Member Little stated he would like to see the finished agreement before
voting on it.

                Mr. Ashton stated this agreement is very common with utility companies.

                Council Member Little moved to defer the decision to accept the agreement until
the contract is finished. The motion failed due to a lack of a second.

                  Council Member Cardenaz stated Google Fiber is working across the United
States. There might be an unusual situation where they need these tools to complete the
installation. If the tools are not available, there may be areas that cannot be served.

                Council Member Little stated Mr. Brace stated that Google Fiber would use their
best efforts, that should be added to the agreement.

              Council Member Perry, seconded by Council Member Cardenaz, moved to pass
Resolution No. 21-12-01, approving and authorizing the execution of a license agreement with
Google Fiber. The motion passed 3 to 1, showing Council Member Little voted in opposition.

                Council Member Little stated he is not opposed to the agreement; he would just
like to have a finished agreement before voting for it.

                Mr. Ashton stated once the agreement is finalized, he will forward it to the Council.

               Mayor Flint stated there are some issues on the table that need to be considered.
Please consider the concerns that Mr. Ashton and Council Member Little brought forward and
finalize a document that reflects those concerns.
