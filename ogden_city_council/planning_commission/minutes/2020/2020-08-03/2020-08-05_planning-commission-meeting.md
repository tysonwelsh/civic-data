2. Public Hearing, Preliminary Subdivision Amendment, for Old Post Estates, approximately
   5300 Old Post Road. (Attachment A)
  Mr. Alex Hurtado stated a property line had been moved and recorded in the 1970’s but the
  easement remains. This request is to move the easement so he can construct a new garage
  on the property.
  Mr. Simpson stated this lot is part of an older platted subdivision and the request is to amend
  the plat to show the correct location of the property line. The Commission is to determine
  whether there is good cause for the amendment and then determine whether the amendment
  will comply with City codes. He showed a property plat which identifies Lots 7 and 11, with a
  portion of Lot 11 now being associated with Lot 7 While the property line had been moved
  many years ago, the plat was not amended, and the intent of this action is to shift the property
  line on the plat so it matches the existing conditions. The lot line and the easement would be
  shifted to match the existing property line and legalize the moving of the property line. He
  stated the lot area requirements would still be satisfied for both lots, and while some language
  on the submitted plat needs to be revised, the lots meet ordinance requirements and Staff
  recommendation is for approval subject to details on the plat being corrected and all
  departmental and utility company requirements being satisfied.
  Commissioner Southwick asked if a new lot is being created. Mr. Simpson responded that
  the proposal is to move a property line between two existing lots and there will be no new lots.
  Commissioner Safsten asked about the easements and whether there are any underground
  utilities, Mr. Simpson indicated there are no City utilities, and that the plat had been sent to
  utility companies and no comments have yet been received, and Staff is of the understanding
  that there are no utilities. He stated it is common to require public utility easements around
  each lot in a subdivision whether or not any utilities are anticipated. He clarified the
  easement will be relocated along the new property line and if there are any utilities, they also
  will be relocated to be contained in the new easement, allowing the construction of a new
  garage where the existing easement exists.
As there were no public comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Schade to close the
  public hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Garner and passed unanimously.
  MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Blaisdell to recommend approval of the
               subdivision amendment as requested based on the findings there is good cause
               to amend the plat and the new lot lines are compliant with City ordinances.
               Approval is subject to the following conditions:
               1. Clearly identifying the title, new lot lines, easements signature blocks,
                   deductions on the plat, and
               2. Obtaining and satisfying all departmental and private utility company
                   requirements.
               Motion was seconded by Commissioner Southwick and passed unanimously with
               Commissioners Blaisdell, Boykin, Garner, Safsten, Schade, Southwick, Stoker
               and Graf voting aye.

3. Change of Nonconforming Use to allow exterior changes in order to convert nonconforming
   commercial building to permitted use as single family home in the R-2EC zone at 939 25th
   Street. (Attachment B)
  Mr. Alan Winkel stated he desires to convert this building as a single-family home. He stated
  he ha acquired property to the west which has now been combined in order to create a
  garage and yard space. He also has obtained a variance allowing parking to be 20’ in the
  side yard setback facing a street in order to maintain some yard space behind the building.
  Mr. Spencer explained because this building has been a nonconforming commercial use in
  the R-2 zone, Planning Commission approval is required in order to allow the use to change.
  In considering a reuse, the Commission is to determine the use and building material
  complies with the regulations of the R-2EC regulations and the building will reflect much of its
  original appearance. He stated the proposed use as a single-family home is a permitted use
  in the zone and will be a benefit to the surrounding community. The owner has removed
  building material and exposed original brick on the building, restoring much of the original
  appearance, as well as replacing windows and kickplates to match the original appearance.
  He has completed the process of combining the lots and obtained a variance for the location
  of parking. As the use and appearance of the building will be compliant with the standards of
  the East Central zone, clarifying brick cannot be painted in this area, Staff recommendation is
  for approval subject to finish work being completed as proposed and that no brick on the
  building may be painted.
  Commissioner Safsten asked about the building material for the proposed garage, indicating
  it should be brick to match the main building. Mr. Winkel indicated he felt the original brick
  would be difficult to match and his intent is to finish the garage with fiberboard. Mr. Spencer
  reviewed material options for the East Central Community, including fiberboard, stone, brick
  stucco, wood-lap or hardiboard, indicating brick is not required.. He indicated the building
  materials will be reviewed as part of the permit process at which time Staff will determine its
  compliance with the EC Ordinances.
  MOTION:     A motion was made by Commissioner Southwick to approve the change of use
              as proposed based on the finings the use will meet the zoning ordinances of the
              East Central community and the original appearance of the building is preserved
              as much as possible. Approval is subject to completion of the lot split and
              variance processes and that there be no painted brick. Motion was seconded by
              Commissioner Blaisdell and passed unanimously, with Commissioners Blaisdell,
              Boykin, Garner, Safsten, Schade, Southwick, Stoker and Graf voting aye.

4. Petition to allow Honorary Street Name as Bingham Fort Lane for 2nd Street between UPRR
   and Washington Boulevard. (Attachment C)
  Ms. Anna Keogh expressed support of Staff recommendation for the length of the honorary
  name to be limited to the Fort’s original boundaries from Century Drive to Wall Avenue,
  indicating it would require the installation of three additional street signs and would make
  people more aware of the history of the area.
  Mr. Spencer stated the Commission is to determine whether the honorary name serves a
  public purpose and whether there will be any negative effect on emergency services. He
  stated while the petition was to extend the honorary name from the UPRR tracks east to
  Washington Boulevard, Staff is suggesting the name be limited to the area where the fort was
  located. He stated there is evidence that in 1850 the road was named Bingham Fort Lane,
  and then when streets were numbered, the street became known as 2nd Street. He stated

                                                                                            pg. 3
  including the honorary name in addition to the existing name as 2nd Street will not affect
  emergency services but will serv as a reminder of the history of the area, In researching the
  fort’s boundaries, Staff has found the west wall was at about 317 West on 2nd Street, with the
  east wall just to the east of Wall Avenue. Staff recommendation is for approval of the
  honorary name to extend from Century Drive to Wall Avenue.
  Chair Graf noted the Commission had received several emails which had been sent to the
  Planning Office and accepted for the record. There were comments both in favor of the
  honorary name as well as some opposed, suggesting that not only the pioneers in the fort be
  recognized, but also the Native Americans who had lived in this area.
  MOTION:          A motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to recommend approval of the
                   honorary name to be added to 2nd Street between Century Drive and Wall
                   Avenue based on the findings there is a public purpose to recognize the history of
                   Bingham Fort and will not create problems with emergency services as the
                   original name of 2nd Street will still be eliminated. Motion was seconded by
                   Commissioner Blaisdell and passed unanimously, with Commissioners Blaisdell,
                   Boykin, Garner, Safsten, Schade, Southwick and Stoker voting aye.

5. MU Revised Building Elevation and Site Plan, for the development at 350 Park Boulevard.
  (Attachment D)

  Mr. Joe Torman stated Lotus is anticipating opening the Ogden’s Brewery in September, and
  this proposal is a new building which will be attached to that building, extending the use to
  include display area for brewery items as well as retail space and rooms for events and
  meetings. Two additional retail pads are included in the new construction and anticipated to
  house two restaurants. The intent is to tie into the theme of Industrial/Modern architecture of
  the existing brewery building. He stated the proposal is compliant with both the MU
  Ordinance and the Ogden River Master Plan.
  Mr. Montgomery stated the Commission had approved a building at this location in October,
  and this proposal is to change the location and design to blend with the new building now
  under construction. The colors and materials will be similar, and will work together as one
  project, creating a campus appearance.
  He reviewed the details of the site plan, stating while the MU zone requires buildings to be 5’
  from the property line, an existing utility easement and the need for outdoor dining, allows the
  Commission to increase the setback from the property line, He stated a landscaping plan has
  now been submitted and the required 20’ landscaped setback for parking is not shown, but
  can be achieved with the removal of one parking space. With this addition, it appears to meet
  the requirements of both the MU zone and the River Master Plan. Staff recommendation is
  for approval, subject to the Commission allowing the front setback to be extended to 20’ in
  order to allow for outdoor dining and accommodate the utility easement, adding landscaping
  to be 20’ between the street and parking lot, and subject to all departmental requirements
  being satisfied.
  Mr. Torman stated parking is limited for this area and expressed concern about the loss of a
  parking space to accommodate landscaping as recommended by Staff.
  MOTION:          A motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to recommend approval of the
                   new site plan, building elevation and materials based on the findings it can
                   comply with the transitional/modern River Bend Design Guidelines and building
                   types as identified in the MU Ordinance for commercial buildings. Approval is
                   subject to installation of a 20’ setback between the property line to the first

6. Public Hearing to consider adoption of Make Ogden Master Plan. (Attachment E)
  Mr. Montgomery stated the Make Ogden Master Plan has been completed and is ready for
  adoption by Ogden City. He stated the process began in 2019 with many City staff members
  visiting Denver after which several open houses were held, a public survey conducted, and
  then findings presented in February 2020. Since that time, a public comment period was
  initiated on the website, the Planning Commission has held several work sessions in an effort
  to review the plan’s policies and design guidelines. This action is to determine whether the
  proposed plan is consistent with the Involve Ogden General Plan.
  He identified the five elements of the plan:        Real Estate and Land Use
                                              Historic and Cultural Assets
                                              Transportation and Utility
                                              Parks & Open Space
                                              Social Equity and Amenities
  Each of these has specific goals and strategies, and then an episode model was created as a
  means where these become a part of the downtown, including the design standards for the
  downtown. He stated the recommendations of the Commission have now been included in
  the plan and it is intended to become a guide for the future of the downtown for the next 20
  years. Staff recommendation is for approval.
  Commissioner Safsten asked how the proposed plan would be implemented. Mr.
  Montgomery responded once adopted, concepts and standards can be later added to the
  zoning regulations of the downtown and the standards would then become regulations. He
  stated funding could happen through CIP, tax increment zones and enterprise zone funding.
  Commissioner Stoker asked if there is any incentive or direction to utilize local developers.
  Mr. Montgomery stated while there are no incentives, the intent is to find developers with the
  same goals who would buy into the standards of the plan. He also stated the Commission
  had received feedback from the public via social media and the City’s website.
  Mr. Robb Berg state this has been a focused process with local engagement, stating they had
  received good input from the community, and indicated the plan reflects the fingerprints of the
  community.
  Mr. Chris Geddes commended City Staff and their ability to assist with the direction of the
  plan. He felt the proposed plan is authentic to the Ogden community.
  As there were no additional comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Garner to close
  the public hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Southwick and passed
  unanimously.
  MOTION:     A motion was made by Commissioner Schade to recommend approval of the
              Make Ogden Master Plan with revisions that Staff and the Commission had
              addressed as changes needed to the Goals and Strategies as well as the Design
              Standards based on the findings it would then be consistent with the General
              Plan. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Blaisdell and passed
              unanimously, with Commissioners Blaisdell, Boykin, Garner, Safsten, Schade,
              Southwick and Stoker voting aye.

Review of Meeting
Commissioner Southwick expressed concern that there are a number of painted brick buildings
in the East Central community where this is prohibited. Mr. Montgomery stated Code
Enforcement has stopped work on some buildings and continue to work with owners to remove
the brick and restore the original appearance, as well as issuing citations if the owner is not
cooperative. Discussion continued relating to how the community could be better informed.

Mr. Stratford reminded Commission members they are to avoid any ex parte communications
and inform residents discussions should occur in open public meetings. The intent is for the
Commission to act fairly and avoid prejudice. He stated these are explained in the Rules of
Procedure.

Mr. Montgomery stated the August work session will continue discussion on apartments in the
commercial zones.

Commissioner Blaisdell asked if there is any consideration for holding live meetings, particularly
for work sessions. Discussion continued among Commission and Staff, with some indicating
they prefer continuing with the Zoom format for the time being and others feeling a social
distance can be created in the Council Work Room. It also was suggested some could
participate via Zoom and others could attend a meeting depending on their comfort level in
doing so.
