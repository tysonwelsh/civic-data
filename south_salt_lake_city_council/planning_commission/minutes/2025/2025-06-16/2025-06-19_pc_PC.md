1.       PUBLIC HEARING
         An Application for Preliminary Subdivision Plat for a One-Lot Plat, located at 120 East
         Spencer Avenue.
         ACTION ITEM
         Applicant: Jian Zheng
         Address: 120 East Spencer Avenue

City Planner, Jed Shum, presented the Staff Report and indicated that the application was for a
Preliminary Subdivision Plat at 120 East Spencer Avenue. The landowner intends to build a new
office building on the subject property, which is currently zoned Commercial Corridor (“CC”).
Photographs of the existing undeveloped site were displayed, and the survey, proposed Site Plan,
elevations, and Landscaping Plan were reviewed. The office building will be 2,500 square feet
with 10 parking stalls and will meet all City design standards.

Staff determined that the Preliminary Subdivision Plat meets Economic Development Goal 3 of
the General Plan:

     •   Economic Development Goal 3: Continue to support existing and future businesses
         through the City’s partnership with the South Salt Lake Chamber of Commerce and
         comprehensive business retention and recruitment program.
             o The application allows the landowner of 120 East Spencer Avenue to develop the
                current empty land parcel to increase location opportunities, development density,
                and local destinations.

Based upon the information submitted in the application, supporting documentation, and analysis
of the relevant code sections and General Plan goals, Staff recommended the Planning
Commission move to approve the Preliminary Subdivision Plat Amendment to plat one parcel into
one lot located at 120 East Spencer Avenue based on the Findings of Fact, Conclusions of Law,
and Conditions of Approval in the Staff Report. Mr. Shum explained that the Planning
Commission had the option to approve, deny, or continue the item.

Chair Carter asked for more information on the variance request and how it related to a variance
request that was recently denied. Mr. Shum reported that the CC Zone requires a minimum one-
acre lot size, but the subject parcel is 0.21 acres, so a variance is required. The property is in the
same zone as the Wendy’s for which a variance was denied, but that property had an existing
business and use, whereas the subject property is currently vacant. Ms. Ungricht added that at the
previous meeting, they reviewed an appeal of a variance denial. The current variance was not
denied and is a one-lot variance, whereas the previous Applicant sought to combine multiple lots.

Chair Carter opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public hearing was
closed.

2.       PUBLIC MEETING
         An Application to Amend a Conditional Use Permit, C-16-040, for a Multi-Family
         Residential Building (the HUB), located at 3848 South West Temple.
         ACTION ITEM
         Applicant: Mike Kienast, representing Columbus Foundation Inc., along with the Housing
         Authority of the County of Salt Lake
         Address: 3848 South West Temple

Ms. Ungricht presented the Staff Report and indicated that the application was to amend a
Conditional Use Permit (“CUP”) that was first approved in 2016 and subsequently amended in
2018 for the HUB located at 3848 South West Temple.

The Applicant requested the removal of the following condition:

     •   Subject to the duty to provide reasonable accommodation under state and federal law, the
         project shall restrict at least 50 apartments for “non-driving residents” to effect the
         promised reduction in project parking demand.

Ms. Ungricht reported that no other substantial changes were proposed. Some minor grammatical
changes were included, as well as six new Findings of Fact that address the removal of the
condition and how City parking standards would be met. The Applicant is required to have 777.7
parking stalls on-site. The site will have 778 stalls and meet all City parking requirements. Staff
was comfortable with the standard being removed from the CUP.

Staff determined that the amended CUP meets the following General Plan considerations:

     •   Housing Goals 1: Encourage the equitable development of diverse, safe, affordable, and
         attractive housing that is accessible and appropriate for residents of all incomes, needs,
         ages, backgrounds, and familial status.
             o The amended CUP will allow a broader type of resident to live in the 50 units than
                 was previously allowed per the condition.
South Salt Lake City Planning Commission Regular Meeting                                           7
June 19, 2025

   •   Economic Development Goal 3: Continue to support existing and future businesses
       through the City’s partnership with the South Salt Lake Chamber of Commerce and
       comprehensive business retention and recruitment program.

Staff recommended that the Planning Commission approve the amendment of Conditional Use
Permit C-16-040 for a Multi-Family Residential Building located at 3848 South West Temple,
with the Conditions stated in the Staff Report and enumerated on the record.

Chair Carter asked if reducing the number of units available to those without vehicles would make
it harder for those families to rent the units. Ms. Ungricht deferred to the Applicant on that matter.

Kristy Chambers, Columbus Community Center CEO, spoke on behalf of the Applicant and
reported that under the current CUP, they are disqualifying people from living at the HUB if they
are drivers. The Applicant was seeking to remove the condition because restricting units to non-
drivers is somewhat discriminatory; many of the people they are disqualifying would otherwise
qualify for their autism program. An objective of that program is to help clients achieve as much
independence as possible, and the current restriction would prohibit them from driving. She
believes there was some overreach in that if a person had a driver’s license, they were ineligible
to occupy certain units in the HUB, and the condition violates Fair Housing Standards because it
discriminates against people who drive.

In response to a question from Chair Carter, Ms. Chambers confirmed that rents would not change.
They do not charge a premium for drivers. The units were limited to non-drivers in order to meet
City parking requirements. Now that they have confirmed there is an adequate number of parking
stalls, they no longer have to distinguish between drivers and non-drivers. Traditionally, non-
drivers sometimes do not have the capacity to live at the HUB.

In response to a question from Commissioner Slifka, Ms. Chambers stated that the HUB also has
commercial parking that could be used for residential overflow if necessary. It is located near the
Meadowbrook TRAX Station, which also has ample parking. However, there are always open
parking stalls at the HUB.

Chair Carter stated that members of his family have utilized the Columbus Center’s services, and
he appreciates the work they do.

7.       PUBLIC HEARING
         A Petition to Forward a Recommendation to the City Council to Amend Title 17 to Reduce
         the Minimum Lot Size in the Commercial Neighborhood (CN) Land Use District from 0.5
         acre to 0.25 acre.
         ACTION ITEM
         Applicant: South Salt Lake

Ms. Ungricht reported that the Staff analysis determined that only four of the 105 parcels in the
Commercial Neighborhood Land Use District meet the current 0.5-acre standard. The zone is
spread throughout the City, including areas on 2100 East, along Main Street, and 2700 South, and
further east on 2700 South. The average lot size is 0.2 acres. The minimum was reduced from one
acre to 0.5 acres in 2022, but there had been no new construction in the district since 2007. The
intention of the amendment is to encourage redevelopment and bring more properties into
compliance.

Staff determined that the amendment would meet the following General Plan considerations:

     •   Land Use and Neighborhood Strategy 8: Appropriately seek the redevelopment of legal
         non-conforming uses, properties, and structures to be more conforming over the long term.
     •   Economic Development Strategy 5: Maintain and enhance neighborhood health, vitality,
         and integrity.
South Salt Lake City Planning Commission Regular Meeting                                          34
June 19, 2025

   •   Economic Development Goal 3: Continue to support existing and future businesses
       through the City’s partnership with the South Salt Lake Chamber of Commerce and
       comprehensive business retention and recruitment program.

Ms. Ungricht reported that redevelopment would encourage small-scale development and require
unplatted properties to be platted, which would add more sidewalks to the area. For example, the
Town and Country Market on Main Street is on 0.2 acres and is a good use that supports the
neighborhood. The change would not negatively impact existing property owners and would make
expansion easier by lowering the acreage requirements.

Staff recommended that the Planning Commission forward a positive recommendation to the City
Council to approve a petition to amend Title 17 to reduce the minimum lot size in the CN Land
Use District from 0.5 acres to 0.25 acres, with the Findings of Fact as stated in the Staff Report
and enumerated on the record.

Chair Carter asked if the minimum should be reduced to 0.2 acres to reflect the average lot size.
Ms. Ungricht stated that Staff discussed the matter and decided on 0.25 acres to ensure adequate
space for required parking stalls. The Planning Commission could recommend a smaller minimum,
but she cautioned that it might give the impression that a site can be developed when in fact it
would not meet design and development standards. The City wants to encourage more small
businesses in the zone, and currently, there are a lot of vacant properties. Reducing the requirement
could drive small businesses to come in and redevelop those properties.

Chair Carter opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public hearing was
closed.

Commissioner Southey stated that she liked that the City was encouraging large developments in
some neighborhoods and smaller businesses in others.

Chair Carter stated that there is a popular small business on his street, and its customer’s park on
the street. The scale fits his neighborhood. His neighborhood has changed in the past few years
due to all the pedestrians who are spending money and buying homes there. He appreciates the
balance between large box stores and small boutique shops.

Motion to forward a recommendation of APPROVAL to the City to Amend Title 17 to
Reduce the Minimum Lot Size in the Commercial Neighborhood (CN) Land Use District
from 0.5 acre to 0.25 acre, based on the following:

Findings of Fact:

       1.      The Commercial Neighborhood (CN) land use district requires 0.5 acres for
               any new Lot.

       2.      The proposed change would amend the new minimum standard to 0.25 acre
               for any new Lot.
