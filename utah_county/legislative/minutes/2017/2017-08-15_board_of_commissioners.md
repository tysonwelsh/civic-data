2.      APPROVE A NO-COST CHANGE ORDER TO THE MERCER CONTRACT (COUNTY
        AGREEMENT 2017-249), MODIFYING THE WORKDAY TIME TRACKING MODULE FROM A
        GO-LIVE DATE OF OCTOBER 7, 2017 TO NOVEMBER 4, 2017
(Track A, 05:00)
Commissioner Graves stated that while this item is listed as a no-cost change order, there will be costs to the county.
He opposes the potential approval of this item because WorkDay indicated to Utah County they could provide the
services and meet county requirements, but are now going back on their word. Commissioner Graves explained
there will be additional expenses due to delayed trainings and additional hours invested; it is not good business to
stall for an update. Utah County Human Resources Director Cammie Taylor pointed out the county has signed an
agreement, and has been clear about county service expectations – there are currently no breaches of contract and
negotiation discussions did include various ‘go-live’ deployment dates. Commissioner Graves noted WorkDay
would be out of compliance if this change is not approved. Ms. Taylor countered the issue is not that WorkDay
cannot be ready in time, but instead that the additional requested month would allow a more efficient and concise
platform for employees to use.

Cammie Taylor outlined the proposed WorkDay schedule, which includes a ‘soft’ deployment and training to allow
county employees to become comfortable using the system. Commissioner Graves reiterated his concerns with
delaying implementation, including the potential funds in lost productivity, and stated he would not vote in favor of
this change. Commissioner Ivie asked whether employees would need additional training because of the delay, and
Ms. Taylor stated no. Commissioner Lee noted his general disapproval of change orders, but mentioned
amendments can sometimes serve to better fulfill a need. He asked Ms. Taylor whether she perceives that is the
case in this request, and Ms. Taylor responded in the affirmative. Ms. Taylor described the work tags, modules,
customizable project codes, payroll, and time tracking updates.

(Track A, 15:25)
Commissioner Graves asked whether the county, or the contractor, requested this change order. Cammie Taylor
explained she saw a potential problem with the work tags, and this is the solution WorkDay proposed in response.
Utah County Clerk/Auditor Bryan E. Thompson expressed respect for the issues raised, and mentioned his own
professional experience to advocate in favor of the change order. Commissioner Graves wanted to know who will
be held accountable if the time tracking does not work – is it Human Resources, the County Attorney’s Office, the
Commission Office, or WorkDay? He stated, “If we bought something that does not work for the county, then
shame on us for selling ourselves snake oil.” Mr. Thompson replied as the County Auditor, he understands the
importance of carefully safeguarding assets, but also sees value in allowing flexibility in these complex processes
and trusting the project managers to accomplish county goals.

Commissioner Lee asked Cammie Taylor to keep the contractors updated about the county concerns as voiced today
if this change order is approved. Commissioner Ivie added he does not want this to become a pattern. Ms. Taylor
noted the County Attorney’s Office could follow-up with a letter notifying WorkDay the county reserves all rights in
case of any contract breach.

                  Commissioner Ivie made the motion to approve a no-cost change order to
                  County Agreement No. 2017-249 with Mercer as outlined in Consent Agenda
                  Item No. 2. The motion was seconded by Commissioner Graves and carried
                  with the following vote:

the county multiple times. Commissioner Ivie summarized the figures presented: NFP includes twelve trainings in
their contract fee of $59,950, and Soltis has a flat cost of $80,000 with additional visits billed at $750 per day. He
noted his preference for NFP as the best dollar value, and reviewed some numbers for Commissioner Lee with input

twelve days of training; the $750 is an additional cost if extra sessions are requested. Commissioner Lee stated he is
on the other side, strongly preferring the quality of service Soltis has proposed in going beyond county requirements
as well as the trust level from Soltis; this is an opportunity for the county to “start fresh.” Commissioner Graves
deferred to his comments on this subject during the previous Commission meeting (reference Regular Agenda Item
No. 10, page 5 of the August 8, 2017 Utah County Board of Commissioners Minutes), summarizing these funds
belong to the Utah County employees; those employees have the right to know what is happening with their money,
and he is very concerned that may not be happening.

Commissioner Lee explained state legislature recently amended laws in an effort to encourage accountability
regarding forfeiture proceedings. Utah County Attorney Jeffrey R. Buhman clarified the county is already in
compliance with the state laws, as most of the changes were related to civil forfeitures and Utah County does not

mimic. Mr. Buhman and the commissioners discussed drug-distribution offenses, cash and vehicle forfeitures,
firearms, verifiable legal costs compared to public salaries, grant funding, and transparency.
