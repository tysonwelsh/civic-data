Wasatch Front Regional Council("WFRC")Transportation Planner,Lauren Victor introduced herself
and stated that she is a Project Manager for the Transit Fresh Look Study. UTA's Long-Range and
Strategic Planning Manager, Alex Beim, also introduced himself and was available to answer
questions.


Ms. Victor acknowledged that there have been a lot ofstudies in the area with the Transit Fresh Look
Study being the last before implementation begins. They want it to be a distinctive process. They
convened a partnership ofall communities in southwest Salt Lake County and northwest Utah County,
as well as all the transportation partners and stakeholders in the area, and the consultant has met with
each city to understand their growth assumptions. They heard feedback that some ofthe assumptions
used in the previous planning process were incorrect, so they met with cities to ensure that they are

accurate before moving into the scenario processes. During this process,they focus on regional transit
connections and high-capacity transit, such as light rail and bus rapid transit. They are also evaluating
potential improvements based on partnership goals.

The WFRC has met with the Technical Committee and Partnership Committee to understand mobility
needs, what communities want to get out of high-capacity transit, and set goals based on that
information. They are not just evaluating based on other studies and are looking at what the cities
want to get out of transit. They are also taking a "What would it take?" approach. If a city wants
light rail but the ridership does not meet the threshold identified in previous studies, they will look at
other things, like adjusting Land Use and political will. They have coordinated with all the affected
cities and transportation agencies to ensure that at the end of the process, everyone has a common
vision and can move toward implementation.

       7.1     Discussion of Traffic Manual. (StaffPresenter^ Addison Mittonh

Assistant City Engineer, Addison Mitton, presented the Staff Report and stated that several years ago,
a Traffic Calming Manual was prepared by capstone students for a college project, which Bluffdale
adopted. A lot of work went into it and they had some great ideas but Staffrealized that it needed to
be updated.


The updates include:

       Renamed "Traffic Manual" to broaden its scope;
       Reorganized for clarity;
       Updated process for studies;
       Added speed reduction measures;
       Speed bump discussion;
       Created project prioritization system;
       Updated flowchart; and
       Updated Manual of Uniform Traffic Control Devices("MUTCD")references.

Items that were reorganized for clarity included moving the traffic calming information sheets to the
appendix, removing outdated case studies, and changing some costs to be relative so they do not
become outdated.
                                  BLUFFDALE CITY COUNCIL

The process for studies was refined to be clearer and more streamlined, and the timing of citizen and
City Council involvement was added. The process was as follows:

   • Initiate study;
   • Identify the issues;
   • Develop a plan; and
   • Implement the plan.

Speed reduction measures that were added include temporary measures like the one that was recently
installed on Freedom Point Way. Those include rubber curbs, which are to be used for delineation
only. They can be used to create chicanes, bulb-outs, and traffic circles. Sand barrels are included as
another altemative to curbs, as well as rubber speed humps, which are being considered for trail
crossings only. Non-construction measures include speed safety cameras and roadway narrowing
using paint.


References were added to studies regarding the expected effects of speed bumps. Mr. Mitton noted
that the language is around speed bumps, not speed humps, which are gentler and more forgiving.

Included studies discuss:


       Discomfort for vehicle occupants;
       Vehicle damage;
       Noise pollution;
       Reduced fuel efficiency;
       Difficult for emergency vehicles;
       Drivers take unintended routes;

       Uneven wear ofroads;

       Limited effectiveness; and

       Damage to snowplows.


A project prioritization system was created to score issues as they come up. The scoring system
included in the appendix provides different metrics to help prioritize projects.

Council Member Wilding asked about the methodology shown in the Executive Summary and for a
summary of the Supreme Court case with Pleasant Grove City that is mentioned there. Mr. Reid
stated that the case was in regard to a transportation fee. Cities began implementing transportation
fees to cover road repair costs, and the 13*^ entity that implemented the fee was Pleasant Grove.
Outside challenges came in and the court case was the result. They used a study to show how their
fee was determined and that study was the evidence that caused the Supreme Court to decide that a
transportation fee could legally be assessed. That was the test case for transportation fees.

City Attorney, Fred Donaldson, added that the allegation was made that it was not a fee, but a tax that
looked like a fee. The Supreme Court ruled that it is a fee. He noted that Bluffdale's Public Service

data was not included so the fee will remain in line with what Staff expects the revised law will
require.


Mr. Reid stated that the study indicated that the City could charge three to four times more than the
proposed fee but Staff based it on the dollar amount necessary to handle the public safety increases
identified in the budget. Ifthey wanted to fully fund the increased needs of both the Police and Fire

Mr. Kartchner was grateful to ChiefBurton's Assistant at the Saratoga Springs Police Department for
