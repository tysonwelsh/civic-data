based on population. He then listed the appropriations, which were: $6,000 to the Canyon

2.2 Discussion and Consideration of Ordinance 19-34, Approving a Monetary
Contribution of $6,000 to the Canyon Rim Citizens Association, $5,000 to the East
Mill Creek Community Council, $3,000 to the Mt. Olympus Community Council,
$15,000 to the Millcreek Community Council, $125,000 for events (Utah Venture

Council Member Uipi said she met with the community councils and the Venture Out
folks about city funding. Mayor Silvestrini said the community councils submit budgets
to the Council every year justifying what they expended the prior year and making
requests for the coming year. He said Venture Out does the same thing. He mentioned

2.3 Introduction of Jerry Nowlin, Representative of KeyBanc Capital Markets —
Underwriter for the Series 2019 Bonds

Jason Burningham, Lewis Young Robertson and Burningham, said the Council adopted a
parameters resolution to issue up to $20 million for the purpose of the Millcreek center
park project which was a catalyst from the General Plan and City Center Plan. He said
that he, the Mayor, and the City Manager went to San Francisco to get a bond rating for
Millcreek. Millcreek received a AA+ bond rating. He said Millcreek has a lot of history,
commercial establishments, and a good governance structure. He offered congratulations
to the Council. He said they selected an underwriting firm from a request for proposals
based on market data, experience, and proposals. They recommended KeyBanc Capital
Market to the Council. He then introduced Jerry Nowlin and Tom Coverick with
KeyBanc. Jerry Nowlin congratulated the Council on the bond rating and mentioned his
background. Tom Coverick then went over his career experience. He said they were
committed to do well on the transaction and to establish a relationship with Millcreek.

Mayor Silvestrini said the underwriter will market the bonds to be sold and purchased.
He agreed with Burningham’s recommendation in selecting KeyBanc because of their
experience with Utah cities. Council Member Uipi appreciated being informed about the
selection and said she supported it.

Council Member Marchant asked if the other agencies had the same bond rating for
Millcreek. Mayor Silvestrini said they met with bond insurers in case the rating was low.
Burningham said the other firm, Fitch Ratings, did not publish a rating. Fitch wanted 20
years-worth of financial history and Millcreek did not have that. Burningham said factors
that accounted for the good rating were that Millcreek has great socioeconomics and the
way the sales tax is distributed, not all population based or point of sale based. He said
that 25-30% of the rating is the agency’s opinion of Millcreek’s financial management
practices. He said having multiple ratings on this size of a bond was not cost effective.
Mayor Silvestrini said some other factors in obtaining the rating were that the top ten tax
outlets in Millcreek have been in business for over a decade, Millcreek has a diverse
economy, the sales tax coverage is almost 6 times more than necessary to service the debt
on the bond, Millcreek does not have a franchise tax, Millcreek has a stable property tax
valuation, and the Council has been able to build and sustain a fund balance of over 15%
in the City’s first two years which was a credit to their fiscal discipline. Nowlin said the
status of the bond market is favorable and rates are at all-time lows. Burningham said
they would accelerate the timeline in anticipation of the Federal Reserve’s meeting on
July 30-31. He said the bond would be in the market by July 18" and close at the end of
the month.

Mayor Silvestrini said the economic development team has been in negotiations for
property in Mill Park in the City Center. Plowgians Auto will be relocated to Highland
Drive. Mike Winder said they are working with commercial and residential property
owners on property negotiations. They recently signed an agreement with Serv-a-Cup
Coffee. He said many property owners are looking to close on their property in August.

3. Business Matters
3.1 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution 19-22, Approving an Agreement for
Park Services with Salt Lake County
Mayor Silvestrini said this was a renewal of the existing contract for maintenance of the
parks in Millcreek. Kurt Hansen said Millcreek has four parks and recommended a parks
needs assessment in the future. The contract price is the same as last year, $388,187.
Council Member Jackson asked about tree maintenance in Canyon Rim Park. She said
that what killed the trees there could spread to others in the park and she would like it
addressed. Mayor Silvestrini said the County lost their arborist and he would like
someone with that expertise working in the parks. He also posed the question if the
County could provide urban forestry services. Hansen said he would look into it.

Council Member Catten moved to approve Resolution 19-22. Council Member Jackson
seconded. The City Recorder called for the vote. The motion passed unanimously.

3.2 Discussion and Consideration of Ordinance 19-35, Regulating, Preventing, and
Banning the Discharge of Fireworks or Use of Any Ignition Source Including

Lighters, Matches, Sky Lanterns, and Smoking Materials Within Certain Areas of

Chief Duane Woolsey said he discussed with Rita Lund about the designated areas in the
city where fireworks would not be allowed. Mayor Silvestrini said the map was the same
as last year in restricting fireworks in the existing areas where there is wildfire risk, but
the ordinance was being approved to be in accordance with state law. Council Member

fireworks and other dangerous things that can occur at this season of the year. Council

of the Millcreek Employee Policy and Procedure Manual

Laurie Johnson said she revised the existing employee policy handbook to make it more

4. Reports
4.1 Mayor’s Report
Mayor Silvestrini thanked the Unified Fire Authority (UFA) and Unified Police
Department (UPD) for participating in the July 4'" parade. He announced that UPD came
to an agreement on their budget. Sheriff Rivera made cuts to the regional services which
allowed Midvale to stay with the department. He said they would relook at the budget to
identify more cost savings. They authorized an 8.75% increase to officers; 2% COLA,
4% market adjustment, and 2.75% merit. He thanked Council Member Uipi for chairing
the last Council meeting. He said he went to the Summer Jam series put on by the Arts
Council and it was a great event.

graffiti at Scott Avenue Park on his own time and thanked him for that. He also brought
up that property tax notices came in the mail that day. He said Millcreek’s property taxes
have not gone up, they were transferred from SLVLESA last year.

Chief Steve DeBry said Unified Police has been patrolling Parleys Historic Nature Park
for Salt Lake City and they are have not paid for those services; $35,000 annually. He
said some officers have retired early through the offered buy-out, so UPD is refilling
positions. Council Member Marchant asked about staffing. Chief DeBry said they are
down one officer who is on administrative leave and down one patrol officer. Council
Member Jackson thanked him for the 4"" of July parade support.

Chief Duane Woolsey said UFA has one battalion chief opening and they interviewed for
the position today. He said there were 6 recruits who were taking the EMT class and 5 of
them passed. The sixth will retake the test.

John Miller brought up that a six-month term was negotiated for the County Public
Works contract. He said the pavement preservation program is healthy and Highland
Drive will be able to be repaved this year by county crews from 3900 S. to Murray
Holladay Road in partnership with Holladay. Mayor Silvestrini said Millcreek was able
to do emergency pavement repairs to 1300 E. around 3900 S. Miller said the current
project bid for streetlights on 300 East Gordon to 4200 South came in higher than
anticipated. It would be $84,000 instead of $64,000. Council Member Jackson asked why
the pricing was high. Miller said boring costs were up and it accounted for half the total
cost. He said wood poles would be cheaper to install, though they are contrary to city
standards. Council Member Marchant said he would like to keep with the standards.
Miller said there are other streetlight priorities in the City. The Council supported
spending the extra $20,000 on the project. Miller said Salt Lake City Transportation is
installing bike lanes on 2000 East between 2700 South and the city border (about 3000
South). He said it would make sense for Millcreek to mirror the cross-section Salt Lake
City does. Flyers will be going out to residents to inform them of the project. The
irrigation ditch there will be buried on Salt Lake City’s side. Mayor Silvestrini said he
was supportive of bikes lanes on 2000 East. Miller said storm drain repair projects cost
estimates were coming in high. He recommended going forward with the projects that are
emergency situations. He announced that the 3900 South Federal Aid Agreement was
signed for $8.7 million at zero cost to Millcreek. Transportation Choice Funds will be
used for the match on the grant. The project is two years ahead of schedule. Miller
mentioned that copper wire was stolen from streetlights on 3900 S. The City is launching
a new map program on the website for capital improvements projects. The Jupiter Jump
fix is in design right now, staff is working on the specs. Council Member Uipi asked to
wait to open the bidding on the Jupiter Jump project until the timing was right to keep
expenses down.

Rita Lund said she received information from the State Emergency Division about the
FEMA floodplain maps. The flood insurance rate maps will become effective July 12".
There will be a 30-day review period with meetings and there will be a public open
house. Then there will be a 90-day appeal period. She said it would wrap up at the end of
year unless there are appeals. Lund said she sent out notices to businesses in Millcreek
about the new insurance rates. Council Member Uipi requested soliciting homeowners
about their insurance agents so the City could also notify them.
