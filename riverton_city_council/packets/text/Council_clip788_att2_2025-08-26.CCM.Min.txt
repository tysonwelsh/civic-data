38          Code Enforcement Presentation
39
40   Gwen Sayre explained that an Administrative Law Judge (“ALJ”) could help reduce pressure on
41   the City’s current criminal court by handling code enforcement and minor cases, potentially
42   lowering costs since ALJs can be contracted and scaled according to caseload. Examples from
43   Taylorsville showed that hearings grew from three cases per month at adoption to as many as
44   18 cases, with outcomes perceived as more educational and compliance-driven rather than
45   punitive. Positives included efficiency, scalability, and more tailored consequences for
46   violations, while concerns included additional costs, questions of necessity given the current
47   part-time court judge, and potential challenges with impartiality.

 3            250th Celebration Discussion
 4
 5   Sheril Garn, Events and Operations Director, presented about Riverton’s possible participation
 6   in the Utah 250 Sesquicentennial Celebration, which would require forming a community
 7   committee, passing a resolution of support, and submitting a commemoration idea. The State
 8   has suggested service hours, walking challenges, potlucks, and a traveling art show, while City
 9   staff proposed incorporating patriotic themes into existing events with banners, concerts, chalk
10   art, and Youth Council projects, as well as new ideas such as a commemorative coin, a barn
11   dance, a 1.776-mile run and 17.76-mile bike ride, a flag-retirement ceremony, a possible
12   production of 1776, a symbolic torch run, and patriotic school assemblies with coloring books.
13   Most costs could be absorbed into the current budget, though some items may require a budget
14   amendment, and the City would be eligible for a $1,500 state grant and a $3,000 Rocky
15   Mountain Power grant.
16
17   Mayor Pro Tempore Buroker asked the Council for input and feedback to decide whether to
18   move forward with funding or not.
19
20   Councilmember Haymond said he would be comfortable moving forward with the plans and
21   seek out grants and other funding to support additional activities.
22
23   Councilmember McCay proposed setting a funding cap, such as $20,000, with the option to
24   revisit the amount if needed. She also recommended moving the carnival to the week before or
25   after the Fourth of July to keep the holiday focused on celebrating the country’s 250th
26   anniversary, noting that some residents had expressed similar concerns about the carnival’s
27   audience and impact.
28
29   Councilmember Pierucci emphasized the importance of seeking business sponsorships before
30   spending taxpayer dollars, noting that local businesses would likely be eager to contribute to
31   celebrating the nation’s 250th anniversary. He volunteered to help with outreach efforts and
32   suggested offering businesses sponsorship opportunities for multiple events.
33
34   Councilmember McDougal reiterated support for the plan and encouraged seeking
35   sponsorships to reduce taxpayer expenses.
36
37   To summarize, Mayor Pro Tempore Buroker said that the Council appeared willing to support
38   spending for the celebration but wanted staff to first explore community and business
39   sponsorship opportunities before making expenditures.

 4   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, presented a draft AI policy intended to guide employee use, noting

18            Ordinance No. 25-21 – RM-6 Amendment, Proposed Updates to the RM-6 Zone to
19            include provisions for using private roads in specific circumstances and clarify
20            Development Standards
21
22   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that the RM-6 zone originally
23   functioned as a multifamily zone but has evolved to apply to individual lots with detached units,
24   prompting updates to better reflect current use. Key changes include incorporating private lane
25   standards, such as required widths, parking limitations, and possible road or sidewalk
26   connections, into RM-6, similar to single-family zones, while clarifying that private road areas
27   cannot be counted within lot sizes to preserve buildable space and driveway depth. The
28   Planning Commission also recommended modifying the rear setback, changing the current
29   requirement of 25 feet when adjacent to single-family zoning to a consistent 15 feet, citing
30   challenges with smaller lot sizes and the lack of significant public concerns at the 15-foot
31   standard.
32
33   Councilmember McDougal asked whether adopting a consistent 15-foot rear setback in the RM-
34   6 zone would still allow the Council to regulate individual developments, particularly infill
35   projects adjacent to homes. Mr. Lethbridge confirmed that the Council could still address such
36   situations through the development approval process.
37
38   Councilmember McDougal then raised concerns about parking overflow in small lot
39   subdivisions, noting that narrow lots often force residents to use nearby public streets. Mr.
40   Lethbridge explained that zoning changes would require private lanes serving three or more lots
41   to allow parking on at least one side, and that the City retained authority to require wider roads
42   to accommodate additional parking. He also confirmed that the Council could require project-
43   specific parking solutions to reduce overflow onto public streets.
44
45   Mayor Staggs called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Public Hearing.

48   Amendments to the RM-6 zone in Section 18.45 of the Riverton City Code as shown in

50   discussion on the motion.

29            New Skate Park Discussion
30
31   Cary Necaise, Public Works Director, reported that an RFP had been issued for a new skate
32   park at Centennial, requesting at least two design concepts in two potential locations: one
33   near a parking lot with no homes facing it, and another near a parking lot with some homes
34   nearby. The timeline for initial designs was estimated at 13–14 weeks, after which the
35   concepts would be reviewed through an open house or council session, and possibly with
36   local skaters. The current skate park was over 20 years old and in disrepair, with liability
37   concerns preventing piecemeal fixes. The City has applied for TRCC funding for the concept
38   drawings, with a September presentation scheduled, and hoped to seek additional TRCC
39   funding next year for construction, aiming for completion in 2026.
40
41   Councilmember Pierucci asked for clarification on whether Centennial Park had been formally
42   selected for the skate park, noting that other locations had been proposed. Mr. Necaise
43   explained that while no formal decision had been made, Centennial Park was identified as the
44   most suitable location after reviewing the City and nearby communities, including Bluffdale,
45   Herriman, South Jordan, and Draper, to ensure a central location relative to existing skate
46   parks. The site at Centennial was deemed optimal due to available land, minimal impact on
47   nearby homes, proximity to a fire station, flat terrain, and no drainage issues. He noted that
48   Centennial Park was currently the only site with a large enough footprint for the skate park.
49
50   Mayor Staggs added that while the new regional park could eventually provide space, the
51   timeline and exact footprint for that park had not yet been confirmed. He indicated that aside
     Page | 7 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1   from Centennial and the future regional park, no other suitable locations had been identified.
