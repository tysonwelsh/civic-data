The CAISO Extended Day-Ahead Marked (EDAM)
The CAISO market covers part of the Western states. EDAM mandates that each utility
has dispatchable power resources that can be called upon when needed. Failure to meet
the requirements results in penalties and forced purchases of expensive power.

Logan will be required to have available capacity equal to 115% of our forecasted
demand available to bid into the market or be subject to financial penalties.

PROS:
● Lower overall power costs: Optimizing resources across a wider geographic area
should lead to more efficient, and thus cheaper, electricity generation.
● Easier integration of renewable energy: The larger market makes it easier to use wind
and solar power from different locations.
● Improved grid reliability: A wider pool of resources helps maintain stability

CONS:
● Less control: Logan gives up some direct control over its power supply decisions.
● Price volatility: Can lead to price swings depending on local conditions.
● Complexity: EDAM is a complex market, requiring Logan to adapt to new rules and
procedures.
● Penalties: While penalties for not meeting capacity requirements are not set RA
Contracts to meet requirements currently go for $60,000 to $180,000 per MW of capacity
per year.

Current Sources (2026):
Fossil Fuel: 64%
Renewable: 22% [37 MW in Summer & 45 MW in Winter]
(Strict Accounting) Market Purchases: 14%

Key Metrics (2026):
Peak load of 103 MW
Minimum base load of 56 MW
Peak load growth of 1.75% per year
Base load growth of 2.25% per year

Challenges:
Two resources are retiring in the next 7 years and need to be replaced while meeting
growing demand
● Sunnyside-Coal fired: ~5 MW, end in 2028
 ● Hunter- Coal fired: 13 MW, end in 2032
● 32% of our baseload
● Nebo - Natural Gas : 17MW, end 2044

Logan's growing population and economic development will drive increased energy
demand.
● Assumed Base Load Growth 2.25%
● Assumed Peak Load Growth 1.75%
● UAMPs Assumed Load Growth 1.5%

Issue
In 2024, Logan City Business Licensing received an application for an itinerant
merchant license to operate a dumpling restaurant called Wong's out of a trailer at 96
E Center.

Currently, itinerant merchants are prohibited in the historic downtown area (200 N to
100 S and 100 E to 100 W), unless they receive written permission from the mayor.
Mayoral approval is not a typical requirement for licensing of land use
determinations, as we try to adopt licensing and zoning codes that are clear and can
be equally applied to all applicants. Typically, when there is discretionary language
regarding land use, permitting, or licensing, those decisions are made by quasi-
judicial bodies, such as the Planning Commission, or at times, particularly for
temporary uses, by the Director.

The current version of the code was adopted in 2017. Prior to its adoption, itinerant
merchants were prohibited from operating in the historic downtown area. The 2017
amendment provided a pathway for itinerant merchant approval through mayoral
approval (Ord 17-10).

Wong’s received mayoral approval for 2024, but staff was directed to consider changes
to the code to eliminate mayoral approval from the license process to reduce the
perception of political bias. The following draft code revisions examine
eliminating mayoral approval and consider the appropriate location and
reasonable restriction of itinerant merchants in the downtown area.

Option 1 - Remove Mayor Approval
This simple revision makes the code more restrictive as there would no longer
be an option for mayoral approval of an itinerant merchant. This essentially
reverts the code section back to the pre- 2017 language. This amendment
would require businesses such as Wong's to find a location outside the historic
downtown area specified in the code.

Option 2 - Remove Historic District Restriction
Another simple revision would be to remove the downtown area restriction.
Currently, itinerant merchants are permitted to operate in the Town Center
zones, so there are no restrictions on them operating very near the designated
historic downtown area. There are also limited areas in the historic downtown
area that could reasonably accommodate a transient merchant, as adequate
parking would have to be provided. Per the Land Development Code
§17.31.120.C.4, 95% of the required on-site parking would have to be
maintained. Many of the current private parking lots are at capacity, are legally
existing nonconforming, or are shared parking, and would not be approved for
an itinerant merchant. Wong's was approved as the on-site flower shop use has
minimal parking requirements and the business is closed in the evening. Other
locations in the downtown area that are restaurants or higher traffic businesses
would have difficulty coming up with the required parking.

Staff Recommendation
After reviewing the potential options, staff's recommendation is to allow itinerant
merchants in the historic downtown and remove mayoral approval (Option 2). The
following considerations have led staff to this determination:

1. Limited permissible areas - With most of the parking in downtown being
   publicly owned, and the private parking lots being at capacity and likely not
   eligible, there is little available space for an itinerant merchant to locate in this
   area.
2. Mobile food vendors are permitted - Currently, mobile food vendors can operate
   in and have a commissary in the downtown area. Just as itinerant merchants,
   mobile vendors are required to have permission from the property owner. This
   has allowed businesses to host mobile food vendors for staff lunches with
   minimal impacts on the character of downtown. It is anticipated that itinerant
   merchants would operate much the same, with property owners looking after any
   negative impacts from the itinerant merchants operating on their property.

3. Chance to evaluate impacts - Itinerant merchant licenses expire after 180 days.
   These licenses are temporary in nature and if the City Council finds that the
   expansion of this license type into the historic downtown area has produced
   negative impacts, the code could be evaluated and adjusted. Evaluation of the
   initial season of Wong's operation downtown has been primarily positive, with
   the business complementing the daytime use of the site and expanding its
   footprint after the primary business closes. This type of collaborative site use is
   supportive of a vibrant social and economic condition downtown.
