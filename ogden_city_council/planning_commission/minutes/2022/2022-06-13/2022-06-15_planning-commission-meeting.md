1.   Planning Commission Training
     Mr. Montgomery supplied each of the members a copy of the book “Ground Rules” which has been
     published by Utah Land Institute and given to cities for their Commission and Council members, written
     by Craig Call, who has previously served as the State Planning Ombudsman. He stated he has picked
     one of the rules to discuss this evening as “public clamor” due to some comments that had been made
     at the previous meeting.

     He stated the City invites residents to participate in the public process as there may be information
     unknown to the City that nearby residents know about and particular issues can be rectified prior to
     construction. However, it sometimes sends a message that residents feel they can stop a project from
     happening. The conditional use process allows the Commission only to identify impacts created by a
     particular use and determine whether or not these impacts can be mitigated, and only if the Commission
     determines they cannot be mitigated, can they deny the request. He stated the Commission deals with
     both administrative and legislative items. Administrative items are required to be approved if they
     satisfy the standards of the ordinances while legislative items typically are rezonings or ordinance
     amendments and these may be denied and are considered based on whether they comply with the
     General Plan, Community Plan or overall character of the surrounding community. The Commission
     may find information presented at the meeting has not been considered by Staff, and indicated it is
     appropriate for the Commission to table action if they feel they need additional information. Decisions
     should be based on evidence presented at the meeting, and not pressure of residents or their opinions.
     He stated some residents voice concern because their view is lost because of a particular development,
     but the Commission cannot regulate what is done on private property so long as it meets ordinance
     regulations.

2.   Proposed Amendments for multi-family in residential and commercial zones

     Mr. Montgomery explained the Commission had raised some questions relating to this proposed
     ordinance amendment during its public hearing, and this discussion is to clarify its intent and determine
     appropriate language to be included as part of the Ordinance. He reviewed the recent history relating

     to multi-family dwellings, stating while some standards had been adopted for these in commercial
     zones, Staff feels they should also be included in multi-family residential zones. One concern has been
     the small properties which could create what has been termed the missing middle housing market.
     Staff suggests the regulations be determined by proposed building types, stating buildings need to fit
     within the context of the surrounding neighborhood. There also are some standards in the commercial
     chapters Staff desires to clarify in order to make the meaning more clear. He reviewed existing
     standards as well as items which have been learned from past developments relating to size, massing
     and scale with the surrounding neighborhood. While current standard require 50% of the entry features
     to face the street, there are some building styles such as a courtyard or great house where consideration
     might be made to allow the doors to face the courtyard or allow placement of a door at the side.

     The proposed language would adopt the same building material regulations, with a solid material for
     the ground floor, and limiting the number of different materials used to two for smaller buildings, with
     the size of the building determining the number of materials used. A single-family, duplex or small
     apartment should be limited to one main material with accent materials allowed. Commission asked if
     the solid base material is required to extend to the full first floor, or whether it could be limited to the
     window level. After some discussion, Commission felt it should be required to the top of the first story
     of the building. Commission also discussed new methods of creating stucco that deteriorate quickly,
     and suggested it be restricted. Building material for single-family row houses or townhomes would
     allow one main building material and accents, or each unit could have a different material. Larger
     apartment buildings would be allowed two main materials and an accent.

      He then discussed window styles, stating window placement should be symmetrical in order to fit with
     the character of Ogden’s neighborhoods. Buildings should still face the street except for courtyard
     styles. Building size would be limited to a 70-75’ depth. Discussion then centered on entryways,
     indicating it is important to have a defined entrance with a covered porch for townhomes, and a main
     entryway for apartment buildings. There is concern with the open entry features which are common
     create security problems as they can become a place for the homeless to rest, and that entry doors
     often are not always entry doors and do not provide proper insulation for the tenant and both weather
     and noise are a concern. In addition, these areas become a place where wind-blown trash and leaves
     can accumulate, and could provide an unsafe place for tenants to have a grill.

     Open space amenities are required for projects of more than 17 units. Height limits also are added to
     both the R-3 and R-4 zones so new construction fits with its surrounding community. The ordinance
     also would require a larger setback for projects in the C-3 zone as these are not always in walkable
     communities. Discussion then centered on ADA regulations, indicating it is easier to accommodate
     wheelchair users during the initial construction rather than trying to remodel at a later date. These
     standards are regulated by the building code, and not the land use code.

3.   Proposed R-9 Zone

     Mr. Brierley reviewed the intent of the R-9 Creative District which had been identified as part of the 9-
     Rails Plan previously adopted by the City. This effort is to allow uses proposed by that plan, which
     identified its intent is to create a creative center for the community. The new zone would increase
     residential density and allow smaller cottage lots and language would allow art related uses such as
     art studios, galleries, and sale of art products. While the Commission approved language relating to
     uses in the R-9 zone at its previous meeting, there was no action to identify areas where the zone is
     appropriate. While much of the identified “creative district” is in the CBD and outside the East Central
     Community the area currently zoned R-3EC would not allow many of the identified uses. This
     proposal is to identify where the boundaries of the R-9 zoning should be established. He presented
