1.   Planning projects and priorities for 2023
     1. Zoning Ordinance Update
        Mr. Rypien explained the City has obtained a grant from Wasatch Front Regional Council
        to update the Zoning Ordinance. Staff has prepared an RFP which has been sent to
        various companies specializing in this type of project, and the deadline for submittal to
        the City is January 27th. The RFP includes consolidating the Zoning Ordinance, the
        Subdivision Ordinance and the Sign Ordinance into one Development Code, which will
        eliminate duplication or misunderstanding of definitions and standards. The intent is to
        create both a searchable and easy to understand land use code to assist both Staff and
        the development community.

        Commission asked if there are any local companies who understand what Ogden is
        about and whether the process is required to accept the lowest bid, as well as whether a
        representative of the Planning Commission would be involved in the selection process.
        Mr. Rypien indicated the RFP was sent to several companies in Utah, and some which
        are out-of-state, but is unaware of any companies with expertise in creating a unified
        code in the Ogden area. He stated the grant amount is $350,000 and the City may choose
        any consultant they feel will meet the needs desired.

        Mr. Simpson indicated there also are National standards through APA which identify
        definitions and standards. Mr. Rypien indicated the process would likely include bringing
        topical sections of the proposed code to the Planning Commission in a work session
        setting for comment and review, and once the Commission is comfortable will schedule
        a public hearing for is review. He also desires to include the development community

2. Subdivision Amendments
   Mr. Rypien indicated while the full subdivision ordinance will be reviewed as part of the
   Ordinance update previously discussed, Staff feels there is an immediate need to clarify
   language relating to subdivision amendments, lot line adjustments, lot combinations and
   parcel boundary adjustments in order to meet State law, eliminate inconsistencies and
   improve the recording process. Mr. Simpson explained while the City has had a history
   of approving these with a deed to be recorded, the County Recorder is now requiring a
   mylar plat for each of these which is a financial burden on citizens. While State law
   allows both processes, Weber County has determined that because the current code for
   Ogden City does not identify a process, the mylar is required. The proposal will add the
   needed language to allow the recording without a mylar plat to be created.

3. Lynn Community Plan
   Mr. Rypien indicated the City Council has requested the Planning Commission review the
   Lynn Community Plan in an effort to better identify the historic character of the Fort
   Bingham area and to preserve the heritage of both pioneer and Shoshone history of the
   area. While some work has been done in designating particular homes to the local
   historic register and creating monuments identifying the Bingham Fort, additional work
   can be done to preserve this heritage for future generations. He stated he is in the
   process of creating a Plan amendment and will present it to the Landmarks Commission
   on January 26th and later to the Weber Heritage Foundation. After these reviews, a public
   open house for the community will be scheduled. Discussion continued relating to the
   limited historic buildings left on West 2nd Street, as many are in disrepair or have had
   major additions and the historic building is no longer visible. Once the open house has
   occurred the proposal will be presented to the Planning Commission for their review and
   a public hearing scheduled.

4. Marilyn Drive Historic District
   Mr. Rypien stated the City is recognize as a Certified Local Government for historic
   preservation, and as such, receives a CLG grant administered by the State of Utah. The
   grant runs in 18-month cycles and provides opportunities for research and preservation
   of historic resources. The last grant funded a reconnaissance study of the Marilyn Drive
   area between 27th and 29th Street and Buchanan east to Polk Avenue and identified 221
   homes as either historic or contributory and should be preserved. Staff is suggesting
   the next grant provide funding for a consultant to compile documentation to allow the
   establishment of both a local and national historic district for the area. He noted as this
   research is completed, the boundaries may be changed. The intent is to include the
   request as part of the grant application and if approved, an RFP can be sent to
   consultants and a selection process completed so work can begin. Mr. Simpson stated
   as part of the process, particular design guidelines for the neighborhood may be adopted,
   similar to those for Historic 25th Street.

   Commission asked about citizen involvement and whether there may be some push-
   back. It was indicated the Landmarks Ordinance requires certified mail to be sent to
   each property owner and a public meeting held by the Landmarks Commission prior to
   its consideration and final voting by the City Council. While there may be concerned
   citizens relating to the loss of property rights, many are anxious for properties to be
   identified as historic. The public meeting is often an education process. It has been
   found much of the time, as districts have been created in the past, it has generated an
   interest in historic preservation from citizens of the community and many have become
   more involved in City planning.

   Commission suggested particular design guidelines for this area may be difficult to
   establish as part of its charm is its eclectic nature and there is no common theme. Mr.
   Simpson stated he felt common elements could be identified and these could become
   an expectation for future development in the area. Commission noted there are existing
   vacation rentals in the community and asked if this use would be affected. Mr. Simpson
   state these are regulated by the Zoning Ordinance and designation of the area as a
   historic resource would not affect land uses.

5. Housing Plan
   Mr. Rypien indicated the adopted General Plan contains a housing element which is
   outdated as projections were for the year 2020 when it was adopted in 2002. While there
   has been much discussion relating to affordable housing, other housing also needs to
   be considered. Staff is suggesting a new housing element of the General Plan be created
   which will include housing needs by type, forecast of land needs and an inventory of
   developable land as well as the typical goals, policies and implementation process. Staff
   feels there is a need to balance different housing types and include the missing middle
   housing. The intent is to create a vision for a 20-30 year time period.

   Commission suggested as multi-family residential uses are allowed in zones not
   identified as residential, these areas should be included in the research as areas

   available for future residential development. They also recognized while density is often

   often difficult, they often become a liability to the developer.

6. Station Area Plans
   Mr. Rypien indicated the City is required to create station plans for any transit stop where
   mass transit travels in its own dedicated lane as well as for the main FrontRunner
   station. He stated much of the research and planning for the FrontRunner Station was
   accomplished with the Make Ogden Plan as it includes the required ½ mile radius around
   the Station. Staff has continued to collect data for the area and anticipates sending a
   plan to the State for certification in the near future. The remaining stations requiring
   plans are on Harrison Boulevard, which require a plan for properties within ¼ mile from
   each stop. The study would include a vision, a map and implementation plan. Staff has
   been working with the Geography Department at Weber State University and senior urban
   planning students have agreed to do much of this research as three of these stops are
   within the university boundaries. It is anticipated their research would identify existing
   land uses as well as what is lacking within the community to create walkable, transit
   friendly neighborhoods.

7. General Plan Water Element
   The Utah State legislature has recently required certain classes of cities to create a water
   preservation plan as part of its General Plan in an effort to reduce the demand for water
   in our communities. Standards are to be adopted for new uses as well as identifying
   ways to reduce water usage for existing uses. Staff is recommending this be added to
   the work plan for the next few years so the deadline of December 2025 can be satisfied.
   Commissioner Shale asked if the City has a sustainability plan, indicating sustainability
   is important. She identified elements which should be included in such a plan, and Mr.
   Simpson commented many of these are contained in various sections of the General
   Plan, but also felt a sustainability plan should be created. He felt many of these elements
   could be consolidated and created as a sustainability plan as suggested. Commission
