RESOLUTION 22-35: AUTHORIZE THE CITY TO ENTER INTO AN
AGREEMENT WITH SUSTAINABLE REAL ESTATE SOLUTIONS, INC. TO
PROVIDE ADMINISTRATIVE AND PROFESSIONAL SERVICES FOR C-PACE
PROJECTS
Nicole Cottle discussed proposed Resolution 22-35 that would authorize the City to Enter
Into an Agreement With Sustainable Real Estate Solutions, Inc. to Provide Administrative and
Professional Services for C-Pace Projects.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       The City previously entered into an agreement with the Governor’s Office of
       Energy Development to administer C-PACE projects. Since the execution of that
       agreement, the C-PACE statute has been updated to allow for third party lenders to
       offer capital through the C-PACE process without the issuance of bonds. GOED
       has transitioned those administrative services to SRS and this agreement would
       permit the City to continue to partner with SRS.

       C-PACE (Commercial Property Assessed Clean Energy) is a program enacted in
       Utah in 2013 that authorizes local governments to offer commercial property
       owners a unique mechanism for financing energy efficient, renewable energy, and
       water conservation improvements to their buildings.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Lang there were no further questions from members of the City
Council, and she called for a motion.

RESOLUTION 22-36: AUTHORIZE THE CITY TO ENTER INTO AMENDMENT
NO. 1 TO AN INTERLOCAL COOPERATION AGREEMENT BY AND
BETWEEN THE CITY AND THE GREATER SALT LAKE MUNICIPAL
SERVICES DISTRICT
Nicole Cottle discussed proposed Resolution 22-36 that would authorize the City to Enter
Into Amendment No. 1 to an Interlocal Cooperation Agreement By and Between the City and
the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District.
Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       In 2019, West Valley City and the MSD executed an interlocal agreement defining
       responsibilities and cost sharing associated with the 4700 South Reconstruction
       project. At that time, it was assumed that the entire project would be constructed
       under one construction contract administered by UDOT, which is a requirement
       when using federal transportation funds. However, to facilitate timely bidding and
       construction, the project has been divided into the following three projects:

              Project 1:     Box Culvert for the Utah and Salt Lake Canal Crossing
              Project 2:     Reconstruction of 4700 South from 5450 West to 4000 West
              Project 3:     Reconstruction of 4700 South from 5600 West to 5450 West
              – including the railroad crossing

       There is not enough federal funding to complete all three projects. As a result,
       Projects 1 and 3 will be funded and administered by the MSD and WVC. The
       federal funds will be utilized on Project 2 which will be administered by UDOT.
       The purpose of this amendment is to clarify that the previously agreed to
       responsibilities and cost sharing arrangements are applicable to all three projects.
  MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING FEBRUARY 15, 2022

      Approximately 30% of the entire project area falls within the jurisdiction of West
      Valley City. Through this agreement, the City and MSD agree to share the federal
      funds proportionally, and to pay matching funds and all additional funds
      proportionally (29.2% / 70.8%). Under this agreement, the city agrees to pay up to
      a total of $5,000,000.

A.  RESOLUTION 22-37: AUTHORIZE THE CITY TO APPROVE A DELAY
    AGREEMENT WITH BACK DEVELOPMENT, LLC TO DELAY
    CERTAIN IMPROVEMENTS AT 3725 WEST 4100 SOUTH
    Wayne Pyle discussed proposed Resolution 22-37 that would authorize the City to
    Approve a Delay Agreement with Back Development, LLC to Delay Certain
    Improvements at 3725 West 4100 South.

      Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
      information as follows:

             The building on the subject property formerly housed Granger Medical.
             The new owner of the property, Back Development, LLC., recently
             finalized an office condominium plat to divide the building up for future
             medical and office users. On January 3, 2022, Genesis Dental submitted a
             building permit application for a tenant improvement in the building. The
 MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING FEBRUARY 15, 2022
                         -9-

            estimated construction costs for this project are approximately $113,000.
            It is anticipated that there will be additional tenant improvement permits
            for the building submitted in the future. The property has frontages along
            Bangerter Highway and 4100 South, which are both considered High-
            Image Arterial Streets in the City. These frontages have specific
            landscaping requirements set forth in Chapter 7-10 of the West Valley
            City Municipal Code. The requirements take effect when any substantial
            modification to an existing site or structure has estimated construction
            costs that exceed $50,000. The standards call for a 20’ landscaped area
            between the sidewalk and parking area with one tree required every 30’.
            The site currently has 20’ of landscaping along Bangerter Highway but
            does not have the full landscaped area adjacent to 4100 South.

            This property is in a UDOT noticing area for high priority transportation
            corridors, and they received notice of this proposal. UDOT has indicated
            that there may be a potential impact to this property in the future when
            Bangerter Highway improvements are made at the 4100 South
            intersection. However, the design is still in the preliminary stages, so the
            full impact, if any, is unknown currently. Since the landscaping that needs
            to be installed along the 4100 South frontage of this property may be
            impacted by UDOT in the next few years, staff recommends that the
            improvements be delayed for now as described in the following delay
            agreement.
