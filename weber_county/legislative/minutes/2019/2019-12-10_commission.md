    2. PUBLIC HEARING TO DISCUSS AMENDMENTS TO THE OPERATING AND CAPITAL BUDGETS OF WEBER
       COUNTY FOR THE 2019 CALENDAR YEAR.
         Scott Parke, County Comptroller, presented housekeeping items to conclude the 2019 budget adjustments.
         Highlights, which many were being reallocated from previous projects, included budgeting just under $620,000
         to purchase various new equipment. Other expenditures included contracted services for Building Inspections,
         increased expenses for Pathways for sewer and weeds and transfers from Fund Balance to the Weber-Morgan
         Health Department to help them finish a carpet project, and additional roadway projects (some of which the
         county was able to bill out). There was about $577,000 from new grants. Commissioner Harvey noted that the
         county was taking the money from savings, pointing out that the county was not borrowing it. He stated that
         the county is in a good financial position. Chair Jenkins invited public comments and none were offered.

    3. PUBLIC HEARING TO PROVIDE THE OPPORTUNITY FOR REPRESENTATIVES FROM NON-PROFIT ORGANIZATIONS AS
       WELL AS OTHERS TO DISCUSS POTENTIAL 2020 COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT BLOCK GRANT (CDBG) PROJECTS.
          Chad Meyerhoffer, of County Engineering, stated that this hearing is to consider potential projects for which
          funding may be applied for under the 2020 Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) program. The
          national objective is for grant money to primarily be spent to benefit low to moderate-income persons but other
          national objectives include using funds to aid in the prevention or elimination of slums or blight and to meet
          urgent needs because existing conditions pose a serious and immediate threat to health and welfare. Grant
          money must be spent on projects benefiting primarily low and moderate-income persons. The Wasatch Front
          Regional Council, of which Weber County is a member, is expecting to receive approximately $870,000 in this
          new program year. All eligible activities that can be accomplished under this program are identified in the
          CDBG Application Policies and Procedures Manual and interested persons can review it at any time. Mr.
          Meyerhoffer read several of the listed eligible activities including construction of public works and facilities
          (e.g., water/sewer lines), fire stations, provision of public services (e.g., food banks or homeless shelters). Loan
          programs for private businesses which would then hire low income persons and homeowners are also eligible.
          The unincorporated county does not have any active grant projects. In the past, Weber County as a whole
          sponsored Weber Housing Authority’s project that offers incentives for first time home buyers, received grants
          for Weber County Library’s restrooms modification to bring them more in compliance with ADA standards,
          developed ADA-compliant campsites installing door hardware at the fairgrounds, an emergency housing
          rehabilitation project administered through Weber County Housing Authority, installed storm drains in low to
          moderate-income communities to reduce flooding, installed wheelchair ramps at street intersections to make
          pedestrian sidewalks more accessible, and sponsored non-profit agencies (Your Community Connection,
          Ogden Weber Community Action Agency, Catholic Community Services).
          The County has handed out its capital investment plan as part of the regional "Consolidated Plan." This list
          shows which projects the county has identified as being needed in the community. Mr. Meyerhoffer was not
          contacted by any non-profit agencies who were at the CDBG “How to Apply Workshop” that would like the
          county to sponsor them and wanted to submit a pre-application for a grant, however, Andi Beadles, Weber
          Housing Authority Director, was represented at the workshop, and today she discussed their interest in
          purchasing and minimally rehabbing a single family property, and renting or selling it to a low-income family.
          Chair Jenkins invited public comments and none were offered.
