He asked who would be responsible for nuisance animals and hoped the City would address the
custody/responsibility issue.

Becky Williams, West Jordan resident, prepared a handout for the Council regarding the current
West Jordan Tree Clearance Code compared to tree clearance codes in nearby cities. She expressed
concern that West Jordan’s tree clearance was one foot higher than that of nearby cities and noted
that recent changes to code would require huge cuts on mature trees. Ms. Williams recommended
changing the tree clearance to 8 feet over walkways and 14 feet over the street. She also requested
the Council train code enforcement officers to avoid unreasonable enforcement.

Karen Hyatt, West Jordan resident, agreed with Becky Williams’ comments. She expressed the
opinion that the change from 10 to 15 feet was unrealistic for mature trees. Ms. Hyatt proposed a
grace period of at least five years and requested that enforcement address specific complaints and
issues rather than making blanket changes to code that affect the entire City rather than the
complaint at hand.

Dave Holmes, West Jordan resident, had attended planning meeting discussion regarding nuisance
animals. He felt the concern seemed to be more for safety of cats. Mr. Holmes likes animals but did
not like having to remove dirt, full of cat feces, from his garden boxes. He requested that Council
focus more on the safety and welfare of humans. He also commented that cats were able to escape
the traps that were set. He felt there was a lot more needed to solve the problem.

 8. BUSINESS ITEMS
       a. Ordinance No. 22-46 Setting the 2023 City Council Annual Meeting Calendar and
          other City Council Boards and Committees
          Council Office Director Alan R. Anderson oriented the Council with three options for the
          2023 annual meeting calendar.
               •   Option A – typical calendar as done in the past for COTW meeting and Council
                   meeting on the second and fourth Wednesday of every month with consideration
                   for various events included.


City Council Meeting Minutes              Wednesday, October 26, 2022                        Page 3
               •   Option B – included COTW meetings at 5:30 pm every week in May and June for
                   budget purposes, with regular City Council meetings at 7:00 pm on the second and
                   fourth Wednesdays of every month.
               •   Option C – included COTW meetings on the third Wednesday of each month at 5:30
                   pm and Council meetings on the second and fourth Wednesdays of each month at
                   6:00 pm.

           Council Member Bloom expressed support for Option C. Council Member Worthen
           suggested combining Option B and Option C to include extra meetings in May and June
           for budget discussions. Council Member McConnehey commented that the position of City
           Council Member was not a full-time position, and most Council Members had other full-
           time employment. He expressed the opinion that meeting every week in May was a lot
           and suggested going with Option A and scheduling extra meetings on a different nights of
           the same weeks as regularly scheduled Council meetings.

           Council Member Pack preferred meeting longer on scheduled Council meeting evenings
           rather than adding additional meeting dates. He acknowledged that additional meetings
           would be needed for the budget. Vice Chair Green suggested the Council meet in the
           afternoons rather than in the evenings. He felt that the Council too often glossed over
           agenda items and did not focus enough on the details. He stated that making policy
           decisions late at night should not be standard operating procedure.

           Responding to a question regarding increasing staff time for the additional meetings in
           Option C, Mr. Anderson explained that non-exempt employees took time off during the
           week to balance time spent at Council meetings in order to not exceed 40 hours in a week.
           Council Member Bloom was in favor of spreading meetings out on different weeks rather
           than meeting longer during regular meetings to allow staff time to get done what they
           needed to during the week.

           Council Member McConnehey liked the idea of starting earlier on Wednesdays and
           suggested starting at 4:00 pm instead of 5:30 pm. He felt a lot of time was spent in
           meetings going through slides and information provided to the Council ahead of time in
           the packet, and suggested time could be saved if everyone came prepared, having already
           studied the information provided. He said he would prefer to stick to meeting two days
           per month, starting earlier in the day.

           Council Member Worthen said she would not be able to meet earlier in the day because
           of work commitments. She would be fine meeting more often in the evenings. She said
           she liked Option B, with the addition of COTW meetings on open Wednesdays when
           necessary. She suggested limiting Council Member comments during Council meetings to
           two or three minutes.

           Chair Whitelock felt the Council’s role was to represent residents with good dialogue and
           good debate to make the best decisions for the City. She felt that sometimes the Council
           did not have enough dialogue and emphasized the importance of sharing information
           with residents during Council meetings and listening to resident comments. She
           expressed the opinion that the Council needed to put more time into serving the City.

           Chair Whitelock suggested that a change in the Council meeting time to before 5:00 pm
           would need to be done during an election cycle. She liked Option C but would want to add
           additional meetings for discussion of the budget.

           Council Member McConnehey said meeting every week would have a bigger impact on
           his job than meeting earlier in the day. Vice Chair Green suggested May 3rd should be a
           regular Council meeting regardless of the option selected, so that the Mayor would be
           required to provide the budget to the Council on May 3rd.

           MOTION: Council Member McConnehey made a motion to TABLE discussion until
                   all seven Council Members were present to have less chance of a
                   stalemate. The motion failed for lack of a second.

           Responding to a question from Council Member Pack regarding which option staff would
           prefer, Mr. Anderson and the Mayor both stated that staff were happy to work with
           whatever meeting schedule the Council desired.

           MOTION: Council Member Worthen moved to APPROVE Ordinance No. 22-46
                   adopting the 2023 City Council meeting annual calendar presented as
                   Option B. Council Member Pack seconded the motion.

           Chair Whitelock said she felt strongly that the Council needed to put in more time, and
           suggested adopting Option C with alterations. Council Member Pack suggested beginning
           work sessions at 5:30 pm and Council meetings at 7:30 pm to allow more time for work
           session discussions.

           The vote was recorded as follows:
           Yes: Melissa Worthen, David Pack
           No: Chris McConnehey, Kelvin Green, Pamela Bloom, Kayleen Whitelock
           Absent: Zach Jacob
           The motion failed 2-4.

           MOTION: Council Member Bloom moved to APPROVE Ordinance No. 22-46
           adopting the 2023 City Council meeting annual calendar presented as Option
           C, with an additional meeting on May 9, 2023, for discussion of the budget. Chair
           Whitelock seconded the motion.

           The vote was recorded as follows:
           Yes: Melissa Worthen, Kelvin Green, Pamela Bloom, Kayleen Whitelock
           No: Chris McConnehey, David Pack
           Absent: Zach Jacob
           The motion passed 4-2.
