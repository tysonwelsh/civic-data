1. Discussion of Plan Ogden update

Mr. Rypien gave a preview of what will be shared with the public at the workshop on October 23" and
the spanish speaking workshop on October 24" at the Union Station pizza and refreshment nights. This
gives the public an opportunity to support which scenario they feel should be a part of the Plan Ogden vision
(slides shown). Staff will go through the presentation, and following that, they'll invite everyone that's present
using a worksheet to evaluate the scenarios and answer questions such as, which scenario do you feel like
best meets the transportation needs or which scenario do you think addresses housing. So, it'll be an
opportunity for the public to provide input on what they like about each scenario. The current general plan
was adopted in 2002. They'll! go over how things, preferences and needs have evolved and changed over
time, including us as individuals, and where they see themselves and the city by 2050. They'll talk about the
purpose of the general plan, which is to guide growth, set priorities, future opportunities and making sure we
become what we want to be. We're evaluating the vision that will allow us to evaluate long term
consequences of the choices we make now and in the future. We will be using maps to develop different
scenarios that the public can interact with. So, after they've gone through the brainstorming workshops, we'll
have an online survey that the public can access in multiple ways including a QR code, and that will help us
develop the preferred scenario, which will become the vision. Then they will develop a vision statement as
well as vision principles, and those vision principles will help us develop the general plan. As far a public
outreach, we have held multiple workshops and events, reached out to local schools, phone polling, etc. The
city did a survey and pulled out the things that really stood out. The top 6 that basically 90% of the people
that took the survey selected, is one, to preserve or enhance open space and natural lands, two, maintain
and enhance a strong, vibrant downtown, three, create more household sustaining Jobs in the city and foster
local independent businesses, four, plan so our city's children, if they choose, can afford to live and work here
when they grow up, five, maintain and strengthen existing neighborhoods, and six, grow efficiently with
climate resilience and careful resource use in mind. Three scenarios have been developed. You have livable
neighborhoods for scenario one, community corridors for scenario two, and then vibrant downtown for
Scenario three (slides shown). One of the things with getting the publics feedback, they're not asking them
to pick their favorite scenario, they're asking, how do you think each of the scenarios perform, and they will
