3. Regular Agenda

3.1) Public hearing and recommendation of Cole Peck's request for review of the Lakeview
Land and Rock General Plan amendment on 12.81-acres of property located at
approximately 600 East 600 South, changing the land use designation from LDR (low density
residential) to Heavy Commercial and Commercial.
Commissioner Jackson reviewed instructions for decorum and public comment.

Katie Bussell presented information on the agenda item.

The applicant, Tony Trane, was present. He stated the development agreement was in place so
they could start planning in detail for the site concept. At this point, the request is to give
recommendations of what needs to be in the development agreement and to make the building
look like a barn-type building. Mr. Trane clarified he will make feasible changes from an
engineering standpoint.

Commission Chair Jackson opened the public hearing.

Keith Southard questioned whether Peck's property belongs to the county and how the city can
legally make decisions regarding it. He also wanted to know where the tax money from these
contracts would go. Additionally, Southard expressed worries about the extension of 570 South,
especially regarding traffic and businesses. He suggested considering safety by routing 570
South through the county instead.

During the meeting, Alexa Jarrett expressed her worries about the proposed route of 570 South,
which would pass through a neighborhood and cross a school boundary where children walk to
school. She pointed out that the county lists the area as agricultural land. When she and her
family moved into their home, they checked the General Plan Amendment (GPA) and saw that it
had to be compatible with the zoning regulations. She mentioned that the Development Review
Committee (DRC) initially opposed the recommendation, and she believes more residents would
be affected by the change. Lastly, she mentioned that the Peck family feels they are not
complying with the law.

Liz Anderson mentioned that several properties nearby had ongoing heavy equipment
construction, which affected their property. She expressed her support for the proposed zone
change. Additionally, she noted that the road was originally intended to have a dead-end. Liz
pointed out that the Peck property existed before the surrounding properties were developed. She
emphasized the importance of property rights and stated her preference for maintaining the
current level of development rather than seeing more homes built, as she has grown accustomed
to the presence of heavy equipment in the area.

Becca Fordham shared that she and her family purchased their home 5.5 years ago and enjoy
living in the industrial area. She mentioned that the truck drivers using the narrow road near their
home have been respectful and haven't caused any issues, as they are mindful of the limited
space and slow down. However, she expressed concern about other drivers who need to drive
more cautiously. Becca mentioned that she has had to confront these drivers and ask them to
slow down, but she hasn't had any issues with the truck drivers. She voiced her opposition to the
idea of opening 570 South.

Skylar Thomas expressed concern about the rapid growth of Lehi, particularly the increasing
number of commercial developments. He mentioned a preference for more residential
development. Skylar's house is situated near the property line, and he is worried about potential
noise disturbances. He firmly opposes the proposed development.

Darryl Fordham mentioned that he doesn't see traffic along 600 East as a problem; instead, he
believes that cars just need to reduce their speed. He stated that the Peck family opposes the
construction of the 570 South road, as it would limit their usage of the area. Darryl highlighted
that residential housing was approved after commercial establishments were already in place. He
expressed his view that having housing and public access poses more risks than industrial usage.

Megan Butler mentioned that she owns an empty lot and doesn't oppose the plan being
discussed. She believes that resistance to change is a common issue among people. Although she
would prefer the road not to pass by her lot, she acknowledges that the decision is out of her
hands. Megan understands that things will inevitably change with the zone alterations.

(inaudible) She does not feel putting heavy commercial will be useful, especially with the
housing shortage in Lehi. She feels like it is a walkable distance to Main Street and would help.
They did their due diligence when they moved to Lehi to see what it would like when they
moved into their home knowing what they were moving next to, so she is opposed to the zone
change.

Emily Grace Beck expressed gratitude towards the Pecks and their truck drivers for their
respectful behavior. However, she voiced concern that not all truck drivers from new companies
may act the same. Additionally, she requested that the Commissioners review the notes from the
last meeting where this issue was discussed.

Chase Kirkham is representing Robert Kirkham. He was raised in the fields when there weren't
any houses as when it was when he was a kid and change is hard. As a City, we must learn to get
along, and he would ask that the Commissioners let the Pecks own their property and let them do
what they need to do and take what they are planning over what is there now.

During the meeting, Cole Peck clarified that their previous request for light industrial zoning was
mainly due to the necessity for outside storage, as it was the only zone permitting it. He believes
that Lehi is deviating from its focus on light industrial development and disagrees with the staff's
assessment on this matter. When Tony Peck bought the property, it was specifically for the yard.
Cole acknowledges concerns about safety but questions the notion that one neighborhood should
be prioritized over another in terms of safety measures. While he would prefer not to have 570
South connected, he recognizes the potential benefit in rerouting in case of a train accident.
Ultimately, he seeks to operate his business and is currently in the process of annexing into Lehi
City.

Katie Bussell acknowledged and summarized the online comments (included below).

Leslie Cuppett (online comment) I am against the rezoning of Lakeview Land and propose it
stays zoned in the originally agreed upon way. The Pecks can still develop their land and benefit
from the value without a rezone. It is unfair to the surrounding area who bought their land before
a proposal for commercial development. Commercial doesn’t fit the area. We’ve already been
through this process and the city council agreed that it was best to leave the land residential. Why
would 2 years change their mind? Why do we need to keep revisiting this? The original zone
should stick because as already talked about 2 years ago, it makes the most sense. Thanks Leslie
Cuppett

Benjamin Lee (online comment)
This comment is in response to the planning agenda item 3.1 (March 14 - Lehi city Planning).
I am a property owner that directly borders the Peck property. I live at 419 E 570 S Lehi, UT
84043. We submitted our concerns to the planning commission in 2021 when a similar rezoning
was up for a vote. The Peck’s have not shown a responsibility for their land even within the
current designation. They have parked commercial vehicles, storage, and dumping on their land.
I would like to detail the concerns that Heavy Commercial will create. See below for details on a
negative property value effect, harms and other dangers. Loss of value Per re-zoning statutes,
rezoning should avoid rezoning when property values are significantly affected. A rezoning to
heavy commercial would devalue over 10 properties with the building of warehouses, heavy
machinery, and other office space the Peck's would seek to build. Bright lights, crime, and other
factors contribute. In my research, I would lose up to 10% of my property value instantly if re-
zoned from low density residential to heavy commercial. Safety of children The connection of
570 south would increase traffic on the low density residential streets and the 300 east outlet
does not have sidewalks (county land not held to city standard). Kids are on foot to school
without sidewalks (no buses are committed to the area within within the Meadow Elementary
area) and that is not acceptable. Heavy and large machinery not seeing or being able to stop for
children could increase the liabilty of city and county. Crime increase With business, expensive
equipment, and additional traffic comes an increase in crime. Besides physical harm, possessions
are at risk. The security that the surrounding neighborhoods have will be in jeopardy.
Chemical exposure Warehouses, repair shops, and similar businesses carry chemicals and
substances that can be harmful to immediate and surrounding areas. Some of these risks are not
realized until a disaster or exposure are experienced. Noise pollution The difference in noise
between low residential and Heavy Commercial is significant. Depending on the working hours
of the businesses, this could be throughout the night with 24 hour shifts being the norm. This
changes the dynamic of Lehi city and significantly effects quality of life. Light pollution In order
to service the buildings under a heavy commercial zoning, lights are used that will affect the
livelihoods of residents. Quality of life is compromised when neighborhoods are placed close to
commercial zones. I’m happy to answer questions but overall Peck’s haven’t shown to be
responsible neighbors that will take in the concerns of surrounding property owners.

Stephanie Moore (online comment) Hi. My name is Stephanie Moore. I live in Valley Point
neighborhood, adjacent to the Peck property. I have major concerns with making this property
commercial. We did our due diligence before buying in the neighborhood. It’s important to us
that we live in neighborhoods, not a commercial area. Two years ago the Pecks unsuccessfully
tried to convert the property into light industrial. The city council denied their request. They did
have suggestion on what they could do. Instead of listening to their thoughts and suggestions,
they came back with an even worse suggestion. Their property will directly connect with ours. It
could mean a huge increase in traffic coming through our neighborhood. With a commercial
designation they could have auto sales, car wash, rec center, parking lot, pawn shop, medical
offices, heliport, storage units, restaurants, and so much more. Those are not buildings that
belong in the middle of a neighborhood. Exiting our neighborhood, there are whole stretches on
the way to the elementary school that doesn’t have sidewalks. That would lead to a very
dangerous situation of the traffic increased through our neighborhood. Please don’t approve the
switch to a commercial property. Thank you for your consideration.

Kamille Lionetti (online comment) I am a homeowner in the neighborhood next to this land.
Before we moved into this charming oasis, we did research. We found out that the land next to us
was zoned for low density residential. We would not have bought a house next to heavy
commercial! That would severely worsen our property values and lifestyle. Please do not vote to
change the land to heavy residential!! Thank you!

Commissioner Chair closed the public hearing.

Tony Trane stated the intent to get the agreement where it needs to be to submit the annexation it
needs to be submitted before the Planning Commission before it can be submitted to the County
for annexation. He stated they need direction for the General Plan for the development
agreement.

The road must be connected to adhere to block length requirements; it's essential for
undeveloped properties to have connectivity. According to the code, connecting roads provide
crucial access for traffic and emergency services. Moreover, connectivity standards necessitate
this connection. Currently, the Peck property is isolated within the county. Future plans designate
the area for Low-Density Residential (LDR) zoning within the city limits, requiring annexation.
To determine the annexation's specifics, the city examines the General Plan. This is why they're
seeking a change. Additionally, he discussed the impact of the annexation on neighboring
properties.

Tony Trane stated while they were going through the process, the City advised to hold off while
they were adopting some processes. They want to get involved and get it finished, they just want
to make sure it is going in the right direction, there has just been a time delay.

The Commissioners want to make it clear they respect the applicant's property rights. They also
want the applicant to understand others in the community who surround the area. Zoning plays a
lot in the development. The two issues are the right to petition to zone, the other issue is to
preserve what the other property owners have considered.

        Motion: Commissioner Eyre moved to send a positive recommendation of Cole Peck's
        request for review of the Lakeview Land and Rock General Plan amendment on
        12.81-acres of property located at approximately 600 East 600 South, changing the
        land use designation from LDR (low density residential) to Heavy Commercial and
        Commercial; including all DRC comments, and findings that the general plan amendment
        is not detrimental to the health welfare and safety to the surrounding area, the proposed
        General Plan Amendment allows the forward motion at the recommendation of the City
        Attorney to move forward with a plan that the City Council can craft a development
        agreement with the developer that will have limitations on location, use, and adjoining
        connectivity to the surrounding area, including the impacts that will come with the
        development. Commissioner Everett clarified a development agreement would be in place
        before the City Council would make a General Plan Amendment. Commissioner Everett
        seconded.

        Vote: Commissioner Jackson, no; Commissioner Everett, no; Commissioner Eyre, yes;
        Commissioner Lockhart, no; Commissioner Kunze, no. The motion failed.

        Motion: Commissioner Lockhart moved to send a negative recommendation of Cole
        Peck's request for review of the Lakeview Land and Rock General Plan amendment
        on 12.81-acres of property located at approximately 600 East 600 South, changing the
        land use designation from LDR (low density residential) to Heavy Commercial and
        Commercial; including all DRC comments. Commissioner Everett seconded.

        Vote: Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Everett, yes; Commissioner Eyre, no;
        Commissioner Lockhart, yes; Commissioner Kunze, yes. The motion passed 4 - 1.

        Commissioner Eyre moved to recess for five minutes. Commissioner Jackson seconded.
