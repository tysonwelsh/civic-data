power resources at this time. The project has some problems from a financial standpoint,
and the City was not sure how we were going to do the financing. There were also water
issues and from her understanding those in charge of the project have still not secured the
water rights. Nuclear power needs a lot of water and the rights to obtain water rights from
the Snake River have not been secured at this time. There were serious concerns in the
minds of herself and the Council about the viability of the project.
Chair Simmonds stated that we were also exposing ourselves financially.
Mayor Daines said she hopes the project is successful down the road and if so, it can be
replicated because there are modular reactors that can be produced in a factory. This project
was the first of its kind and she does not know if it will ever materialize. She and the
Council will be watching and waiting to see if anything changes in the future and if the
water rights are secured. The City is not opposed to nuclear power but she and others did
not feel that it was a viable project for a variety of reasons.

PUBLIC HEARING - CODE AMENDMENT - Consideration of a proposed
amendment to Section 2.19.010 of the Logan Municipal Code regarding Police and
Fire Residency Requirements – Ordinance 22-18 – Police Chief Gary Jensen

At the September 6, 2022, Council meeting, Police Chief Gary Jensen addressed the
Council regarding the proposed ordinance. Ten years ago, an ordinance was passed
determining what is a reasonable distance for police officers and fire department
personnel to live away from the City. Recruitment and retention affect this notion today.
Part of the drive is the competitive nature of trying to reach out and acquire staff and
retain employees. One of the factors to consider is what other communities are doing to
address similar situations and be mindful of competitors as well.

The potential change to residency requirements of Police requirements of staff. (Fire will
not be changing their residency requirements.) The major competitor of Logan City
officers is the Cache County Sheriff's Office. The Sheriff's department allows the same
area radius of residence that he is proposing. Another aspect to consider is housing
affordability. The potential to find a home outside of the near Logan vicinity is much
easier.

At present, there is a 17-mile radius of residency for Police and Fire. The radius for
Police will be moved out three miles to create a 20-mile radius of recruitment. This will
open recruitment options and give housing options to potential employees as well.

The change to a 20-mile radius will include new areas of Cache County but also slightly
extend outside areas of Cache County barely into Idaho. The Mayor and Chief Jensen
were not comfortable extending the radius through Sardine Canyon toward Brigham City.
The map is slightly skewed North as a result.

Principles of Successful Mixed-Use Developments:
• Greater housing variety and density
• Reduces distances between housing, workplaces, retail, and other destinations• Compact
development
• Strengthens neighborhood character
• Promotes pedestrian and bicycle friendly environments

Logan has Several “Mixed-Use” Zones: Neighborhood Center, Community
Commercial, Campus Residential, Mixed Use, Commercial, and Town Center I & II.

Local Examples of Mixed-Use Projects:
• Horizontal Mixed Use – mixture of uses spread out over a project
site, e.g., Riverwoods (residential, commercial, office, hotels)
Homestead (residential emphasis).

• Vertical Mixed Use – mixture of uses arranged vertically within a
building, e.g., Morty’s, Blue Square, Renewal, Westfield Lofts, Solo
Vista.

• Hybrid – mixture of uses both arranged within a building and
spread out over a project site, e.g., Park Avenue (residential,
commercial live/work type of units, commercial pad sites), Brickyard
(Residential, commercial), Borden Lofts (residential, commercial
live/work).
Proposed Changes Mixed Use Development Standards:
• Added MU Design Characteristics to Intent language

• Complete streets with an emphasis on bike & pedestrian mobility

• Thoughtful and integrated landscaping and green areas
• Community Gathering Spaces
• Shared Parking & Integrated Public Transit
• Innovative and distinctive building architecture
• Variety of commercial uses & activities
• Thoughtful and integrated mixture of housing types
