PUBLIC COMMENT PERIOD
A resident addressed the Council regarding broader community health concerns related to food
safety and dietary habits. The speaker referenced reports of testing on common candy brands and
expressed concern about repeated exposure to additives and chemicals in foods frequently consumed
by children and adults. The speaker contrasted food practices in the United States with experiences
overseas and raised concerns about chronic illness, medication use, and overall public health
outcomes. The speaker also cited national food industry examples, including reports of discarded
food products and concerns about industrial additives in pizza products. The comments focused on
perceived links between processed foods, chemical exposure, and public health impacts.

Speaker 2 continued expressing concerns regarding processed foods, chemical additives, and their
perceived connection to chronic disease in the United States. Comments included references to
glyphosate, food manufacturing practices, prescription drug usage rates, and rising rates of obesity,
depression, anxiety, and ADHD. The speaker compared the U.S. food and healthcare systems to
those in other countries, citing examples of smaller-scale food production and lower healthcare costs
abroad. The speaker urged the City to consider promoting healthier food systems and community
wellness initiatives, encouraging a shift toward locally sourced, minimally processed foods. The
remarks concluded with a call for civic leadership and community collaboration to strengthen public
health, resilience, and overall well-being within West Valley City.

Jim Vesock reported participating in the Youth in Action event with the Family Fitness Network,
noting that more than 60 youth attended—one of the largest groups to date. He shared that City
directors and staff were present, providing an opportunity for youth participants to engage directly
with City leadership, discuss projects and programs, and build meaningful connections. Jim also
referenced attending a recent Chamber event where the City Manager spoke. He described the
remarks as inspiring and expressed confidence in the City’s direction under current leadership. He
highlighted the City Manager’s focus on collaboration, long-term progress, and community
investment. While acknowledging ongoing challenges and costs facing the City, Jim expressed

CONSENT AGENDA

A. RESOLUTION 26-19: APPROVE AN EXECUTIVE ORDER TO AMEND THE
   PERSONNEL POLICIES AND PROCEDURES MANUAL
   Mayor Lang, presented proposed resolution 26-19 that would approve an Executive Order to
   Amend the Personnel Policies and Procedures Manual.

   Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as follows:

       A PTO Cash-In Schedule was implemented in November 2024 and applies to both the City’s
       PTO Cash-In Program and termination payouts. Under that schedule, accrued PTO is paid
       out at a percentage based on an employee’s years of service.

       This update revises the policy so that employees who resign in good standing are eligible
       to receive payment for all accrued PTO, up to the maximum allowable 320 hours.


B. RESOLUTION 26-20: APPROVE THE PURCHASE OF EMAIL SECURITY
   SOFTWARE AND SERVICES
   Mayor Lang presented proposed resolution 26-20 that would approve the Purchase of Email
   Security Software and Services.

   Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as follows:

       DarkTrace provides an email security solution that applies self-learning AI to analyze intent,
       content, and context across thousands of data points per message, determining whether a
       communication “belongs” in a given environment. It detects subtle anomalies and novel
       attack patterns that traditional secure email gateways may miss. In addition, DarkTrace
       provides detailed, context-rich explanations and investigations through features such as
       analyst outputs and user-facing analysis tools, enabling security teams and end users to
       understand why an email was flagged as suspicious and respond appropriately.

       The organization currently uses ProofPoint as its primary email security solution to protect
       against spam, phishing, malware, and other email-borne threats. While this platform has
       provided effective filtering and broad threat coverage, administrators and support staff have
       found that the product often lacks sufficient contextual information explaining why specific
       messages are blocked or flagged. This limited transparency complicates troubleshooting,
       increases time spent investigating user-reported issues, and can reduce confidence in

C. RESOLUTION 26-21: APPROVE A FRANCHISE AGREEMENT BETWEEN EMERY
   TELCOM AND WEST VALLEY CITY FOR A TELECOMMUNICATIONS
   NETWORK IN THE CITY
   Mayor Lang presented proposed resolution 26-21 that would approve a Franchise Agreement
   Between Emery Telcom and West Valley City for a Telecommunications Network in the City.

   Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as follows:


      Applications for telecommunications networks in West Valley City are governed by Chapter
      20-5 of the City Code. The franchise granted by this Agreement is for a 10 year period, with
      the option to renew for an additional 10 years with the same terms and conditions. Chapter
      20-6 of the City Code permits the City to require all telecommunications providers to collect
      taxes from their customers and deposit them with the Utah State Tax Commission. This
      agreement memorializes this provision as well as acknowledging Emery’s duty to secure
      permits from Public Works for any excavation or construction.
