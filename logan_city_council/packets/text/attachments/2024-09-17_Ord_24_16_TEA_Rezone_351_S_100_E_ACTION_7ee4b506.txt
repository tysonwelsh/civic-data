PLANNING COMMISSION MINUTES
Meeting of Aug 22th, 2024

Commissioners Present: Sara Doutre, Jordy Guth, Ken Heare, Jessica Lucero, David Lewis, Eldon
Peterson, Sarah McNamara

Commissioners Excused:

Staff Present: Russ Holley, Aimee Egbert, Mike DeSimone, Mohamed Abdullahi, Amanda Pearce, Ben
Anderson, Darren Farar, Jeannie Simmonds (City Council). Teresa Harris, Logan City Recorder, swore in
new Planning Commissioner, Sarah McNamara.
PC 24-033 TEA Rezone [Zone Change] Erik C. Ashcroft and Thomas Lee Hale are requesting the rezone
of three properties located at 351 South 100 East from Traditional Neighborhood Residential (NR-6) to
Commercial (COM); TIN 02-053-0026; -0027; -0028 (Wilson Neighborhood).

Staff: Russ Holley, City Planner reviewed the request for a rezone of three properties, .92 acres from
Traditional Neighborhood Residential (NR-6) to Commercial (COM) zoning.  The largest property, located
in the rear, is vacant, with the two smaller properties along 100 East both currently having single family
homes on them.  This block is split zoned, with the west half being COM and the east half being NR-6.
With The Future Land Use Plan (FLUP) identifying these properties as Commercial, and the four vacant
properties to the north being planned for future commercial development, staff is recommending approval
of the proposed zone change.

Commissioner Guth asked about the vacant properties to the north of the project site that the City owns.
R. Holley said it is still to be determined, the city has no plans to put a city or public facility there.  More
than likely it would be sold for commercial development.

Proponent: Tom Hale spoke about their previous request proposing a rezone to TC-1 to put townhomes
on the property and the Commission suggesting that they go another direction. They decided to follow the
FLUP and request commercial.

Erik Ashcroft said they want to have the zoning done before they spend the money to have plans drawn.

Commissioner Guth asked what their vision is for the property if it is rezoned to commercial.  Mr. Ashcroft
said they have discussed a development with commercial on the ground floor and apartments above.

Public: Michael Anthony said that the United States Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) is looking for a
location in the Logan area for a new medical clinic.

Jared Nelson who lives south of the project site asked what the rezoning of these properties will do to the
adjoining property values in the future.

Ryan Merrick who lives to the north of the project site said he and the other neighbors would like the area
to stay residential, they do not want to live next to commercial.

Commission: Commissioner Doutre spoke about the transition from residential to commercial and trying
to allow growth for the needed commercial while still maintaining the feel of the neighborhood.

R. Holley said that many zone changes occur at a street, and that there are transition requirements in the
Land Development Code regarding building and setbacks to try to achieve better compatibility.

[page 5]

Commercial development next to residential is required to match the 25 foot residential setback and has a
two story height restriction at the setback.

R. Holley also explained that when a property is up-zoned the value typically increases because the
property now has more development potential.

Commissioner Peterson said the transition has to happen somewhere.  It makes sense to rezone this
area to address commercial needs and is in favor of approving the rezone.

Commissioner Guth agreed with commissioner Peterson and said that consistently rezone requests that
support the FLUP are typically approved.

Commissioner Lewis said that moving towards commercial is a good thing but is not in favor of creating a
commercial island between residential.  Commercial use would allow an auto service station, fast food,
event center, commercial parking, etc.

Commissioner Heare said he is not comfortable rezoning to commercial until there can be a better
transition

Commissioner Lucero said that transition has to start somewhere.  The plan is for this area to be
commercial and eventually the remaining homes on the block will be commercial as well.  She is in favor
of the rezone.

Commissioner Doutre said the City has committed to a future Commercial zone so the property owners
should be able to benefit from that.

Commissioner McNamara said the middle of the block makes the rezone a little more difficult but agrees
that it has to start somewhere and is in support of the rezone.

Motion: Commissioner Peterson made a motion to forward a recommendation of approval to the
Municipal Council on a zone change for PC 24-033 with the findings for approval as listed below.
Commissioner McNamara seconded the motion

FINDINGS FOR APPROVAL
1. The Logan City FLUP identifies the area as COM which is consistent with this request.
2. The area is contiguous with other COM zoned properties on the same block and along high-capacity
    streets and corridors as prescribed in the LDC.
3. The COM zone allows for a range of commercial and mixed-use developments, which are similar to
    some of the surrounding developments.
4. The transitional development standards in the LDC will ensure compatibility with NR-6 zoned
    properties located east of the subject area.

PROJECT
The proponent is requesting to rezone three properties for a total of 0.92 acres of property from
NR-6 to COM. The largest property, located in the rear, is vacant with the two smaller properties
along 100 East both currently having single family homes on them. This block between Main
Street and 100 East is currently split zoned, with the west half being COM and the east half
being NR-6. Most of the existing homes along 100 East were built in the early to mid-1900's.

GENERAL PLAN
The Future Land Use Plan (FLUP), adopted in 2008, identifies these properties as Commercial
(COM) The General Plan, a nonregulatory visioning plan, describes COM areas as being
intended for retail, service and hospitality businesses that provide employment centers and
serve city-wide and regional populations. Most COM designations inside Logan City are located
near the Main Street/Highway corridor north and south of downtown.

[Figure 2: Shows the site and the surrounding existing zoning. — zoning map graphic; labels PUB, TC2, COM, NR-6, TC-1, MU; "Project Location / 351 South 100 East / Zoning = NR-6"; street labels 200 S, 300 S, 400 S, MAIN, 100 E, 200 E, 300 E, 350 S]

ZONING HISTORY
Logan City adopted its first zoning map in 1950. From 1950-1972 these properties were zoned
R2, a low-density residential zone. From 1972-1976 the property was zoned R1, a low-density
residential zone. From 1976-2000, the property was zoned R3, a medium density residential
zone. From 2000-2011 the properties were zoned SFR, a low-density single-family residential
zone. From 2011-2013, the properties were zoned COM, a general commercial zone allowing a
wide-ranging of commercial uses. From 2013-2023, the properties were zoned NR-6 a low-
density single family residential zone. The downzone in 2013, was part of the 2012 citywide
outreach effort, in which numerous properties along 100 East were downzoned back to NR-6.

LAND DEVELOPMENT CODE
The Land Development Code (LDC) 17.10.080 states the COM is intended for retail, service
and hospitality businesses that serve city-wide and regional populations. COM land will be
located near high-capacity streets and served by mass transit. COM land may also support
vertical mixed use building with commercial on ground floors and residential on upper floors.
COM lands adjacent to NR zones are subject to transitional development requirements that may
require lower building heights and larger setbacks near or adjacent to NR zoned properties.

STAFF SUMMARY
The FLUP and General Plan show this entire block, along with blocks to the north, as future
COM areas. The Ville 364 project and associated TC-1 zoning is an anomaly and currently
under a down-zoning process back to COM to match the area. Staff would encourage that as
part of the forthcoming new General Plan project, some focused thought and future
consideration be put into these fringe areas along 100 East and 100 West. Thoughtful
