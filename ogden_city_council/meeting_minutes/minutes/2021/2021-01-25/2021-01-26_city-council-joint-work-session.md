Proposed Development Agreement Amendment- Mountain America Credit
Union at 1825 Washington Boulevard
         In October 2018, the Council approved an amendment to the Mountain View Community Plan and a rezone of property
with a conditional overlay at 1825 Washington Boulevard for the development of a Mountain America Credit Union. The overall
project included the demolition of the River Inn on the property as well as the relocation of a billboard. The development
agreement set forth a timeline for construction of two (2) years from the approval date and memorialized the design of the
proposed credit union with architectural exhibits. The proposed amendments include an extension of the timeline for project
completion and an update to the proposed design of the building. The extension is needed due the additional time it has taken to
fmalize the relocation of the billboard and the architectural changes are due to a rebranding by the credit union.
         Planning Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the proposal; he reviewed the
changes that have occurred at the site since the 2018 Council approval of the Mountain View Community Plan and zone change.
The proposed amendments would extend the time line for an additional two (2) years and would update the concept plan for the
building with a new design. The proposed design changes must still meet the standards included in the conditional overlay as well
as section 5.04 of the development agreement. The proposed building designs are a change from what was approved with the
original development agreement; however, the Planning staff and the Planning Commission have indicated that the new design
sufficiently meets the standards approved for the project. The updated design maintains the use of the original or similar materials
which include natural stone, metal, and cement fiberboard siding. The color scheme is generally the same as well using dark
browns and creme colors. Mr. Montgomery presented a rendering ofhow the building may look if the proposed amendments are
approved. He concluded The Planning Commission reviewed the proposal at its December 2, 2020 meeting and forwarded a

        Fiscal Year 2021 Budget Amendment- Arts Plaza
                  Council member Choberka reiterated her personal conflict of interest in regard to this project and recused herself from
        discussion of this item as well as the next agenda item.
                  City Comptroller Stout explained City Administration is proposing a Fiscal Year (FY) 2021 budget amendment to
        appropriate $4,250,000 from grant funds received from Recreation, Arts, Museums, and Parks (RAMP) grant and the Dumke
        Foundation, and Business Depot Ogden (BDO) Lease Revenue fund balance to fund construction ofthe Arts Plaza. The proposed
        project will transform the site of the former Courtyard Inn into a public plaza that will help activate 25th Street, create a stronger
        connection between the Central Business District and East Central neighborhoods, and catalyze revitalization in the Nine Rails
        Creative District. The Courtyard Inn was in dilapidated condition and contributed to blight, vandalism, and other crime in the area.
        Planning for the art plaza to be constructed on this site is underway, the estimated construction costs for the project are estimated
        to be $4,000,000. Several project partners have committed funding to construct the plaza, including the Dr. Ezekiel Rand Edna
        Wattis Dumke Foundation, in the amount of $2,200,000 and Ogden First aka Ogden Contemporary Arts in the amount of
        $450,000. The Dumke Foundation has a sponsorship naming agreement that will be transmitted to Council in connections with
        this budget action. Ogden First will pass their RAMP grant from Weber County through to Ogden City for the Arts Plaza. The
        current request for City funding is also to support the remaining construction costs, in the amount of $800,000. The plaza will
        include public art elements and has been identified as a key project to activate the Nine Rails Creative District. The estimated
        remaining construction costs of$550,000 are also intended to come from Ogden First through a second RAMP award from Weber
        County. That application will be reviewed by the County and is anticipated will be awarded in March, if approved. Community
        and Economic Development has been working with partners on this project and has plans to fund an endowment with funds from
        Weber State University to help support the programming costs for the plaza in future years, for the City to fund $600,000 in the
        future to a reserve for equipment and capital replacement, and to fund the annual maintenance of the plaza through funding from
        tax increment revenue collected by the Redevelopment Agency (RDA) in the Adams Community Reinvestment Area increment
        area. As the project progresses, the City will need take additional action in the future to recognize the additional $550,000 in
        construction funds when those are awarded and committed, fund the reserve in the amount of $600,000 using City funds, and
        appropriate an annual maintenance budget for the plaza through the RDA. She concluded the total fmancial commitment for the
        project to date is $5.5 million.
                  Council member Stephens asked for more infonnation about the Ogden First entity that will be passing their RAMP grant
        to the City. Ogden City Arts Coordinator Buckley stated Ogden First is an Ogden-based non-profit entity; their original name was
        Ogden Contemporary Arts. Council Executive Director Eller-Smith inquired as to the principals of the non-profit. Ms. Buckley
        stated Venessa Castagnoli is the Executive Director and Cori Anderson is the President ofthe Board.

        Sponsorship Naming Agreement- Arts Plaza
                 Business Development Manager Meess explained Administration is requesting the Council adopt a joint resolution
        authorizing the City to enter into sponsorship naming agreement with the Dr. Ezekiel R. and Edna Wattis Dumke Foundation (the
        Foundation). Key elements of the sponsorship naming agreement are as follows:
                      •   The Foundation will grant $2.2 million for construction of the Arts Plaza, subject to the tenns and conditions of
                          the agreement. Any unused funds must be returned by December 31, 2021.
                      •   Ofthe $2.2 million grant, $200,000 must be used for improvements to Ogden Avenue adjacent to the Plaza and
                          $250,000 must be used for design, fabrication, and installation of the "Beacon," an art sculpture to mark the
                          Plaza.
                      •   The arts plaza shall be called the "Dr. Ezekiel R. and Edna Wattis Dumke Foundation Arts Plaza" or "Dmnke
                          Arts Plaza" which name shall be placed and pennanently displayed on several prominent markers in the plaza.
                          Recognition shall also appear in publications, maps and signage, and for events at the Plaza.
                      •   The City is responsible for construction, installation, and maintenance of the name per the consultant's design.
                      •   Construction of the Arts Plaza must be completed by October 15, 2021.

Council Business
          Audit Committee: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith reminded the Council of the creation of an audit committee
last year, the membership of which includes two Council members: Council member Hyer and Vice Chair White. The City's
independent auditor has recommended a fmancial expert be appointed to that committee as well and it is necessary to replace the
City's Financial Advisor, Laura Lewis, on the committee. She has approached Tracy Probert, who formerly worked for Ogden
City and is now the Finance Director for Layton City. She asked if the Council is comfortable considering him being assigned to
that role. Council member Choberka wondered if the position could be opened to the public. Ms. Eller-Smith stated that when
discussing the position in the past, the Council had expressed hesitance to opening the position to the public because they wanted
a very specific set of requirements including governmental accounting experience. Council member Hyer agreed and noted that
Ms. Lewis's input was very helpful to the committee; if the position is opened to the public, it is important that the notice clearly
communicate that the committee needs an individual with governmental accounting experience. Ms. Eller-Smith stated she will
proceed with advertising the position and will report back to the Council with a fmal recommendation.
          Leadership Meeting: Chair Blair stated that Council member Stephens recently recommended that Council leadership
allow involvement from individual Council members during regularly scheduled leadership meetings; leadership has taken this
under advisement and has determined to allow participation by one Council member on the last Thursday of each month. He asked
Council members to notify Council staff if they are interested in participating and if there is a demand, leadership will set up a
rotating calendar.
