2. APPROVAL OF RESOLUTION 39-2021 MAKING APPOINTMENTS TO THE PUBLIC
DEFENDER BOARD.

Bryan Baron (Attorney) proposes Mark Decaria and Richard Mauro be appointed to the Public Defender
Board; plus one Commissioner needed. Commissioner Froerer proposed Scott Jenkins; one year term
proposed. Board member terms are typically three years; however, the first term must be staggered. Scott
Jenkins shall serve for one year, Rich Mauro for two years, and Mark DeCaria for three years.

Commissioner Froerer moved to approve Resolution 39-2021 of the County Commissioners of Weber County
making the following appointments to the Public Defender Board: Mark DeCaria (three year term, December
31, 2024), Richard Mauro (two year term, December 31 2023), and Commissioner Scott Jenkins (one year term,
December 31, 2022). Commissioner Jenkins seconded.

Roll Call Vote: Commissioner Froerer — aye; Commissioner Jenkins — aye; Chair Harvey — aye

APPROVAL OF A CONTRACT WITH UTAH DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH FOR THE INMATE
CONTRACEPTION PILOT PROGRAM.

Chief Phillip Reese (Chief Deputy, Corrections) explained this is a state-driven pilot program driven from
House Bill 102. Weber County participating along with three other counties. Provides continuity of
contraceptive care for inmates who received such medication prior to incarceration.

Commissioner Froerer moved to approve a contract with Utah Department of Health for the Inmate
Contraception Pilot Program; Commissioner Jenkins seconded with comment: under normal circumstances I
would not approve but it is funded by the state.

Commissioner Froerer — aye; Commissioner Jenkins — aye; Chair Harvey — aye

APPROVAL OF RESOLUTION 41-2021 DECLARING THE COUNTY’S INTENT TO CREATE A
PARTNERSHIP WITH WESTERN GOVERNORS UNIVERSITY (WGU).

Melissa Freigang (Director, Weber Prosperity Center of Excellence) recognized her staff for all of the work
they do. WGU Academy prepares students for success in WGU; credit hours are transferable to WGU degrees.
WGU approached Weber County to be a part of solution to intergenerational poverty (IGP). IGP statistics in
the county:

e 10% (approximately 7200) of children are experiencing IGP in Weber County;

e 23% are in poverty, at risk of remaining in poverty.

Educational attainment in Weber County IGP population

28% of county do not have high school diplomas

62% of county have high school diploma or GED only
10% have some small amount of post-secondary work
0% with bachelor degrees

Wes Smith (Senior Vice President, WGU) shared WGU history and influence; focus on internet access to allow
learning to happen anywhere and competency-based learning. Founded on belief “Talent is universal,
opportunity is not.” Aiming to help individuals stuck in intergenerational poverty. Weber County is a great
place to pilot injecting education into intergenerational poverty cycles to help solve these cycles. Looking
forward to scaling and proving out interventions to a state and national scale.

Jim Taggart (President, Ogden-Weber Technical College) excited in removing financial aid barrier.

Commissioner Jenkins: Impressed with WGU with the fact they jumped on this program the way they did.
Commissioner Froerer: Great to see collaboration.

Chairman Harvey: This represents a significant financial commitment to Weber County from WGU.

Mrs Freigang: Yes, WGU has contributed $100K per year for the next five years that allows hiring a resource
integration coach who helps Weber Prosperity Center of Excellence families get access into post-secondary
pathways.

Commissioner Jenkins moved to approve Resolution 41-2021 declaring the county’s intent to create a
partnership with Western Governors University; Commissioner Froerer seconded.
Roll Call Vote: Commissioner Froerer — aye; Commissioner Jenkins — aye; Chair Harvey — aye
