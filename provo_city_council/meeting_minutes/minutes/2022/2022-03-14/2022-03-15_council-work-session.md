2.      A presentation regarding the FY2023 Budget- Public Works (including
        Administration, Streets, Engineering, Water, Wastewater, etc.) (22-016) (0:36:14)

Dave Decker, Public Works Director, presented. Mr. Decker reviewed sanitation operations and
services. He noted some of the increasing costs and how these will impact their operations, as
well as some changes to their staffing level to meet ongoing demand. Mr. Decker then reviewed
fleet operations and increasing costs and staffing needs for this area. He noted that fleet is an
internal revenue account; the fleet essentially charges other departments for their use of fleet
services (including mechanics’ time and the cost for parts).

Streets is part of the General Fund and includes major services such as snowplowing, street
sweeping, street repairs and maintenance, signs, paint, and weed abatement. Streets and
stormwater are operated together in Public Works as there are some related items between these
two functions, although they operate from different funds. Councilor Rachel Whipple asked
about weed abatement (especially for goatheads). Mr. Decker explained if there are weeds in the
public right-of-way, including the park strip, the Streets division will handle that. Mr. Decker
reviewed the revenue sources and funds for streets, including the B&C road tax revenue through
Utah County. Mr. Decker then reviewed the stormwater operations. Commercial stormwater fees
are based on the hard surfaces of a particular property. Ms. Whipple asked if businesses can
reduce their fees by replacing hard surfaces with permeable surfaces. Mr. Decker explained that
was an option for many businesses. He noted that the stormwater revenue outpaced expenses
because the extra revenue was utilized toward capital improvement projects. Mr. Decker shared
the five-year CIP projects and funding sources. The plan includes a focus on stormwater in the
Franklin Neighborhood, a result of the recent improvements to 500 West. Another focus point of
the coming fiscal year is a retention basin at the forthcoming park on Canyon Road.

Mr. Decker reviewed Engineering operations and services, including development, permitting,
maintenance, transportation, and traffic. They requested several supplemental items including
software maintenance, uniforms, safety equipment, and a variable message board (used to keep
the traveling public aware of messages regarding traffic improvements). The variable message
board was a key component of their public relations and keeping the community informed.
Engineering is one of the general fund components of Public Works and their budget must be
supplemented by general fund dollars. The B&C Road Funds pays for some Engineering CIP

this. Engineering has taken their directive from the Council’s vision statement regarding Center
Street in order to prioritize some of the improvements. Mr. Decker displayed a map showing
traffic volumes on Center Street and surrounding corridors. He reviewed a list of improvement
projects and the rankings assigned by the Transportation and Mobility Advisory Committee. Mr.
Decker reviewed the recommendations from Engineering, but he reiterated that these items fell
into the unfunded category. Engineering suggests testing some options through use of temporary
measures during the summer months in order to test the effectiveness of certain changes before
investing significant funds for permanent solutions.

Mr. Decker reviewed a summary of options and hoped to help the Council understand the need
for experimental measures to be undertaken first. Option A are small minor improvements such
as bulb-outs and expanded dining areas. Option B would result in physical changes to curb,
gutter, and parking layouts; this does not include removal of the center median and parking on
Center Street. Option B would also take out one lane of traffic and convert it to a bicycle and
scooter corridor—in the experimental stage they would change to one lane with temporary
striping to help the City decide whether to pursue permanent changes or not. Option C would be
a complete reconstruction of Center Street, which would impact businesses and be highly
disruptive to the area. Mr. Handley asked whether options B or C would involve redirecting
traffic. Mr. Decker said one of the options would include rerouting much of the vehicular traffic
to 100 North. If the Council is interested in pursuing this, Mr. Decker indicated he and his staff
would be happy to present in more detail in a future presentation.

Mr. Decker highlighted some of the items for Slate Canyon Drive—Engineering recommends
funding at least $500,000 to do two intersections, but the best option would be to do at least three
intersections on Slate Canyon Drive. Mr. Decker also highlighted a section of Canyon Road that
is missing sidewalks. These items rise higher for Public Works because both of these corridors
are on safe routes to school and young children are directed to walk in these locations. The
unfunded CIP for Engineering also includes an intersection at 5600 North and University
Avenue. A signal needs to be placed there; UDOT is funding the signal placement, but Canyon
Road needs to be realigned to meet the engineering requirements for an intersection at this

3.      A discussion regarding the rezone of Provo City property from Single Family
        Residential (R1.8) to Open Space (OSPR). (22-029) (1:44:40)

John Magness, Interim Council Executive Director, presented. The Lakewood Park is city
property currently zoned as R1.8 with power distribution sites nearby. Even if the property were
to be sold at a future time, the City would need to maintain easements for the power facilities.
Even if the Council did not want to rezone to open space, it might be more appropriate to rezone
to Public Facilities. Councilor George Handley asked about the parcels in the park—Mr.
Magness confirmed that the park is owned by the power company so it was not rezoned at the
same time as other city properties. Councilor Katrice MacKay noted there was no bathroom at
this park and with lots of young children playing there, it really limits the use of that area. There
is a lot of development going in that area, so increasing the size of the park in that area would be
appropriate. Ms. MacKay spoke to Travis Ball, Provo Power Director, who said they are
concerned with the possibility that the property could become a development in the future.
Councilor David Shipley asked about the use of the open space versus public facilities zone. Mr.
Handley said Open Space was preferred for parks. Councilors were supportive of the proposal
and staff would prepare an application for future approval. Presentation only.

4.      An ordinance amending the Provo City General Plan regarding Southwest Area
        development policies. Citywide (PLGPA20210242) (2:06:31)

Hannah Salzl, Planner, presented. She explained where this component fit into the General Plan.
The revised policy would address the need for a variety of lot sizes. Councilor Shannon
Ellsworth asked if medium-sized housing could be located with medium-sized under this
policy—Ms. Salzl clarified that the policy suggests locating small and medium together, and
medium and large housing together. Data showed that small and large housing in close proximity
tended to introduce a disconnect into the area and developers found that type of product did not

required—it should simply be an indication to developers of an ideal type of project.

Ms. Salzl noted the need to clarify the 4 units per acre figure and whether it was a net or gross
figure. The land use map in Appendix J of the General Plan only says 4 units per acre but it
needs to be revised to specify gross. Ms. Salzl shared some illustrations of different models of

not a complex formula or matrix of ideas. She thought it captured the intent of the Council; she
