RESOLUTION 21-08: ADOPT THE LEGISLATIVE AGENDA OF WEST
VALLEY CITY FOR THE 2021 UTAH STATE LEGISLATIVE SESSION
Mayor Bigelow discussed proposed Resolution 21-08 that would adopt the Legislative
Agenda of West Valley City for the 2021 Utah State Legislative Session.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       As the second largest city in the state it is imperative that the City has a significant
       presence at the state legislature. The City enjoys a particularly strong presence and
       maintains that presence each year through the process of setting guidelines for
       representation and executing on those guidelines.
   MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING JANUARY 19, 2021
                          -3-

       Due to the city’s unique position, it has been essential to maintain this presence and
       to proactively work on legislative issues throughout the year. Each January, the
       Council sets forth the guidelines for this work. These guidelines are found in the
       attached resolution. The guidelines allow staff and contractors to adequately
       represent the City on any topic in a constantly changing environment and provide
       protection to the City from any misunderstanding of the City’s position.

       Due to the extremely dynamic nature of the legislative session, staff must be clear
       about the direction of the City Council. This resolution assists in that effort.

       Of the various issues that will be discussed during this legislative session, of
       particular interest are policing, land use, emergency operations, taxation, funding,
       local control, economic development matters, and transportation funding.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Bigelow there were no further questions from members of the City
Council, and he called for a motion.

       Councilmember Buhler moved to approve Resolution 21-08.

CONSIDER APPLICATION SMI-11-2020, FILED BY STEVEN FLORES,
REQUESTING FINAL PLAT APPROVAL FOR THE STEVEN FLORES
SUBDIVISION LOCATED AT 2283 WEST 3800 SOUTH
Mayor Bigelow discussed application SMI-11-2020, filed by Steven Flores, requesting
   MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING JANUARY 19, 2021
                          -5-

Final Plat Approval for the Steven Flores Subdivision located at 2283 West 3800 South.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       Steven Flores, is requesting final plat approval for a two lot subdivision in the R-
       1-8 Zone. The subject property is located at 2283 West 3800 South. An existing
       dwelling occupies the front portion of the property and will remain as part of the
       subdivision.

       The applicant is proposing a subdivision consisting of 2 lots. Lot 1 will be
       approximately 10,900 square feet while lot 2 will be approximately 30,900 square
       feet which excludes the stem portion to the new lot. All frontage and area
       requirements relating to both lots will be satisfied as part of the R-1-8 zone.

       Access to the subdivision will be gained from 3800 South. All public
       improvements such as curb, gutter, sidewalk and stamped colored parkstrip are
       currently in place. The applicant is proposing to use a single drive approach to the
       new lot. The driveway for the existing single family dwelling will remain. A new
       water and sewer line will need to be installed for lot 2. The applicant and/or builder
       will need to coordinate with the City Engineering Division the replacement of
       existing infrastructure when the water and sewer line is installed.

       The existing dwelling will remain on what will become lot 1. The existing free
       standing outbuildings will be removed to make way for the new lot. It is anticipated
       that the new home would be located behind the existing dwelling. The remainder
       of lot 2 could be used for a detached garage or garden space as it is rather large.
       Another option could be that the far south portion of lot 2 could be incorporated
       into a future subdivision should property to the south ever develop.

       As with all new subdivision development, there is a concern with the potential of
       ground water impacts. A soils report is needed to determine water table depth. For
       the Northridge Subdivision to the east, the basement depth was approximately 4
       feet below existing top back of curb. If Engineering and the Building Official are
       comfortable using the report that was prepared for that subdivision, a new report
       would not be required. However, the applicant would be relegated to follow the
       recommendations outlined in that report.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Bigelow there were no further questions from members of the City
Council, and he called for a motion.

CONSIDER APPLICATION SMI-12-2020, FILED BY TYLER BODRERO,
REQUESTING FINAL PLAT APPROVAL FOR THE WALLACE STEGNER
ACADEMY SUBDIVISION LOCATED AT 5990 WEST PARKWAY
BOULEVARD
Mayor Bigelow discussed application SMI-12-2020, filed by Tyler Bodrero, requesting
Final Plat Approval for the Wallace Stegner Academy Subdivision located at 5990 West
Parkway Boulevard.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       Tyler Bodrero, representing the property owner, is requesting final subdivision
       approval for the Wallace Stegner Academy Subdivision. The subject property is
       bordered on the north and west by agriculturally zoned property, the east by Rocky
       Mountain Power and the Mountain View Corridor and Parkway Boulevard to the
       south.

       The proposed subdivision has been submitted to divide an existing 42 acre site into
       one 6 acre lot and a remainder parcel of approximately 36 acres. The 6 acre lot or
       lot 1 as noted on the plat, will be used for a new Charter School. The remainder
       parcel will be developed at a later date.

       As a requirement of the subdivision, the property owner will be dedicating right-
       of-way along Parkway Boulevard. Improvements will be installed along this right-
       of-way consisting of curb, gutter, sidewalk and asphalt. The right-of-way
       improvements along this section of Parkway Boulevard will be constructed by the
       City as a City project.

       To provide access to the school itself, a new public road will be installed along the
       west boundary of the subdivision running north from Parkway Boulevard. This
       road will be a 54-foot right-of-way and will also be constructed with curb, gutter,
       and sidewalk. This road will be built by the applicant. The new road will stub to
       the north providing a connection to the remainder parcel for when it develops.
       Access to the school will be via the new road with two points of ingress/egress.
       Staff has included a preliminary site plan for the School and will include that in the
       power point presentation.

       The final plat identifies two parcels along the west boundary of the plat. The
       alignment of the new road is taking into consideration improvements from the
       adjacent land owner that encroach the proposed subdivision. Anticipating a
       resolution with the property owner to the west, the road has been designed to
       accommodate those improvements along with a new drive access to the single
       family dwelling.

       A permitted use application will be reviewed for the school itself. Site
       considerations regarding parking, landscaping, storm water etc. will be handled
       with that application. Therefore, staff and agency comments will be better
       addressed through that process which is already on-going. The subdivision will
       create the new lot and will dedicate Parkway Boulevard and the new right-of-way
       for future access to the remainder parcel.

Councilmember Fitisemanu verified that this is just the subdivision plat. Mayor Bigelow
replied yes.

A.   ACCEPT PUBLIC INPUT REGARDING APPLICATION ZT-9-2020,
     FILED BY RANDY MOORE, REQUESTING A ZONE TEXT CHANGE TO
     ALLOW PROPERTIES WITH ACCESS AND FRONTAGE ON A STREET
     WITH A PLANNED RIGHT-OF-WAY WIDTH OF AT LEAST 80 FEET
     WHERE AN EXEMPT HOUSING DEVELOPMENT IS PROPOSED TO
     APPLY FOR THE RM (MULTIPLE UNIT DWELLING RESIDENTIAL)
     ZONE
     Mayor Bigelow informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular
     Council Meeting scheduled January 12, 2021, in order for the City Council to hear
     and consider public comments regarding Application ZT-9-2020, filed by Randy
     Moore, requesting a Zone Text Change to allow properties with access and frontage on
     a street with a planned Right-of-Way width of at least 80 feet where an exempt housing
     development is proposed to apply for the RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling Residential)
     Zone. This item was continued from the January 12, 2021 Regular Meeting to the
     Regular Meeting of January 19, 2021.

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

               Randy Moore’s original application defined qualified 55 or older
               development and allowed properties with access and frontage on a street
               with a planned right-of-way width of at least 80 feet where a qualified 55 or
               older development in which the density must not exceed 8 units per acre is
               proposed to apply for the RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling Residential) zone.
               The Planning Commission recommended approval subject to the density
               being capped at 7 units per acre.

               After reviewing Mr. Moore’s proposal, the City’s Legal Department
               modified the language to not include the federally defined term “qualified
               55 or older development” which could change. Despite the revision, the end
               result is the same because qualified 55 or older developments are the only
               form of exempt housing.

               Included with this issue paper is a map that shows remaining vacant land
               within the City as well as roads designated on the Major Street Plan as an
               80’ right-of-way and roads designated as 100’ right-of-way or more
               excluding nonaccess highways and freeways like I-215 and Bangerter
MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING JANUARY 19, 2021
                       -9-

         Highway. The map also highlights the one-half mile areas within eligible
         light rail stations. The purpose of this map is to illustrate where rezone
         requests to the RM zone are currently allowed and where they would be
         allowed if this application is approved.

         Also included in this report is a graph showing the population of the City
         by age. As of 2018, 17% of the City’s population was aged 55 or older. Over
         the next 10 years, just over 15,000 more residents will fall into the 55 or
         older cohort.

  Mayor Bigelow stated that he visited various potential sites over the last week. He
  indicated that the existing senior living facilities he visited are very different than
  regular multi-unit projects. Mayor Bigelow stated that he felt more comfortable
  about traffic and access. He indicated that there are 12 locations that could be
  eligible for senior housing if this ordinance is changed. Mayor Bigelow stated he is
  favorable to this proposal after visiting the locations. He wanted to verify that units
  must be majority occupied by residents 55 and older due to exempt housing. Steve
  Pastorik stated yes and noted that this is the only type of housing that can be
  discriminated based on age. Councilmember Lang asked if this is federal law. Steve
  replied yes. Councilmember Lang asked if the City can tie this age limit to the text
  change. Steve replied that the City can never be more restrictive than federal law.
  Councilmember Lang asked if the City can require that the only 12 properties
  allowed to be rezoned are those eligible now. Councilmember Buhler stated that he
  wouldn’t be supportive of that because it would put a burden on staff. Mayor and
  Council discussed properties and what could be considered vacant or not and how
  this could be interpreted in the code.

  Mayor Bigelow opened the Public Hearing.

  Randy Moore thanked the Council for considering the application. He indicated
  that these projects are meant for seniors. Mr. Moore stated that federal law changing
  from 55 and up is unlikely to change and he noted that CCR’s are also included in
  these projects that bind them to 55 up. Mr. Moore stated that he is not concerned
  about vacant properties. He noted that he doesn’t see a rush of applicants petitioning
  for 12 properties to develop as senior developments. Mr. Moore stated that the land
  must be vacant to come forward. He indicated this will provide a great service to
  the community.

  Mayor Bigelow closed the Public Hearing.
