PUBLIC HEARING:

PC 25-031 Young Automotive Sales and Service -Continued from the May 22nd meeting.
[Design Review and Conditional Use Permit] Kenneth Stuart/YPPVRE LLC, authorized agent/owner,
is requesting a design review and conditional use permit for a new 44,453 square foot auto dealership
and service department located at approximately 1900 South 800 West in the Commercial (COM)
zone; TIN 03-006-0006 (Woodruff Neighborhood).

Staff: Russ Holley, Logan City Planner, reviewed the request for a Conditional Use Permit and a
Design Review Permit for a new 44,453 square foot automotive sales building on a 4.84 acre project
site. R. Holley explained that the site is located within an area that is slowly being developed and the
proposal is for the sales/service of utility trailers, reviewed the Future Land Use Plan and the current
zoning, and outlined the phasing plan, site conditions, existing points of vehicular access, roadways,
and surrounding land uses.

R. Holley discussed the current lot configuration and stated that a boundary line adjustment will be
required to match the proponents phasing plan. This specific permit decision is only related to Lot 5.
The setbacks and site planning were discussed. The building design requirements in the Commercial
zone were discussed especially as it relates to commercial architecture near the street, with the
exception that an auto sales lot is different with its parking & vehicle display in the front of the building.
R. Holley pointed out the Commission’s discretion in permitting a larger building setback in the
Commercial zone for auto sales. The site layout with its large drive aisles and larger parking stalls
was discussed, all of which relate to the proposed use type.

The need for additional landscape screening was discussed. Building design with the building being
orientated to the street, while transparency/fenestration/material mix were discussed. R. Holley
pointed out that the building is not necessarily oriented to the street and the south façade needs
additional elements to match the east side, or rotate the building. Additional architectural elements
on the south building elevation were recommended by staff.

Building height and numbers of parking stalls are compliant with LDC requirements, while the project
needs some type of pedestrian connection to the adjoining public street. Staff is recommending that

additional landscape materials be moved into the southern landscape area to help buffer the building
from the street. R. Holley discussed recommended changes to the list of Conditions of Approval to
address project deficiencies.

Commission Chair Peterson asked about the suggested changes to the conditions to address the
trailer parking. There was additional discussion about the enhanced landscaping along the south side
of the project.

Proponent: Kenneth Stuart, proponent, concurred with staff’s presentation. K. Stuart responded to
a question about trailer sales and confirmed that was their intent.

Public: No public comment was made.

Commission: Commissioner Heare discussed adding a condition to require additional building
design elements be added to the street facing south building elevation to match the east elevation.
The Commission discussed building design as it relates to the street frontage and defining the front
of the building and modifying the four sided architecture requirement. Commissioner Doutre asked
about fenestration. R. Holley explained the south side was deficient in meeting the fenestration
requirement; however, the doors could be modified with opaque materials to meet the minimum
requirements, or by bringing in the architectural elements from the east side, they would be able to
meet the minimum requirements. Additional landscape was also discussed. S. Doutre discussed
replacing fenestration with additional architectural elements and landscaping and not requiring four
sided architecture. The Commission discussed modifying Condition #3 to include additional
architectural elements on the southern elevation to make the building design more uniform, modifying
Condition #5 to strike language regarding parking stalls & include a pedestrian connection to the
street, and modify Condition #6 to include additional landscaping and a berm along the south side of
the parking lot.

PC 25-032 Logan Flex Spaces [Design Review Permit] Jarod Hall/Logan Secure Storage LLC,
authorized agent/owner, is requesting a design review permit for four new warehouse buildings that
will be divided up into 85 flex units to accommodate a variety of uses, located at 1950 North 1000
West in the Industrial (IP) zone; TIN 04-076-0015 (Bridger Neighborhood).

Staff: Russ Holley, Logan City Planner, reviewed the request for a Design Review Permit for four
new flex-space industrial buildings totaling approximately 73,500 SF with 85 possible units. R. Holley


covered the Future Land Use Plan designation and the current zoning, both of which are the Industrial
Park. R. Holley discussed the range of potential uses such as small warehousing, contractor service,
light manufacturing, food manufacturing, and other similar businesses that may occupy these
buildings. He stated that each individual business will be evaluated through the business licensing
and Tenant Improvement (TI) processes for compliance with land use and parking requirements.

R. Holley explained the different parking requirements dependent upon the type of use that may be
anticipated to occupy the space which makes it difficult to assess a minimum parking standard at this
point in the project. Staff recommended a stall count of 205 as sufficient to accommodate most uses
throughout the entire project. The proponent has proposed a total of 209 parking stalls.

R. Holley discussed the building design, height, materials, layout, placement, and setbacks and
indicated the proposal meets minimum code. The single issue that is non-compliant concerns the
amount of landscaping provided in the parking areas. The LDC requires a landscape island for every
20 stalls which, in this specific case, conflicts with the overall functional design of the buildings, e.g.,
roll up & ped doors. Staff recommends that the Commission take into consideration the unique design
of the building and allow for a relocation of landscaping to other areas on the site.

The Commission and staff discussed where the landscape issue was located on the project site and
how Condition #3 applies, i.e., directly adjacent to the building versus not adjacent to the building,
and what type of discretion the Commission has in this specific issue.

Proponent: Jarod Hall, project architect, explained that the project will probably cater to small
contractors, and has included enhanced landscaping in areas outside of the parking areas.

Public: No public comment was made.

Commission: The Commission discussed the required parking lot landscaping on the site. R. Holley
pointed out that the overall project is required to provide 39,726 square feet of open space and
landscaping and clarified that the only deficiency is not actual landscape square footage, but the
project is lacking four specific landscaped parking lot islands. This specific deficiency is due to the
unique design of each of the four buildings with roll up and pedestrian doorways, and that landscape
islands placed every 20 feet could block access to some of the potential units. The Commission
discussed the requirement to require a landscape island every 20 stalls, discussed moving the
required landscape elsewhere, discussed landscaping flexibility, and discussed modifying Condition
#3 to require additional landscaping along the southernmost parking area. As part of the discussion
and deliberation, the Commission approved the preliminary project landscape design as submitted
showing the specific landscaped islands adjacent to the buildings in locations that did not interfere
with potential units, and S. Doutre further proposed modifying Condition #3.b to require an additional
landscape island along the southern row of parking.
