## C. CONDITIONAL USE APPLICATION(S)

### 1. Mosida Energy - Application #CU2025-02 – Request to allow for alternative fuel sources to a previously approved conditional use for a natural gas electric power generation facility in the Mining and Grazing (M&G-1) Zone, located in Section 25, T7S R2W, approximately 34-acre project site, Cedar Valley/Fairfield area of unincorporated Utah County

Greg Robinson clarified that the item under discussion was a new conditional use request specifically for the use of alternative fuels in an already approved power generation facility. He emphasized that this was not an amendment, but a separate request related to the prior permit. The applicant sought to use alternative fuels, such as diesel or liquefied natural gas (LNG), only during interruptions in the natural gas supply, enhancing the facility's energy resilience. He noted the presence of multiple conditional uses for the overall site and described the tank locations, noting their unspecified dimensions. He stated that if tanks exceed 40 feet in height, a separate conditional use would be needed. He also mentioned the request for an expiration extension aligning with prior conditional use and the fire marshal's condition for hydrants.

Shayne Pierce asked a clarifying question about distributed materials.

Greg Robinson confirmed that he provided an updated recommendation page to clarify the request's distinction from the previous one.

Karen Ellingson asked about water pressure for fire hydrants.

Greg Robinson explained that the applicant discussed drawing water from Eagle Mountain and use an onsite fire tank to meet fire code standards.

Greg Probst, the applicant, reiterated that the use of alternative fuels was intended strictly for emergency situations. He preferred LNG over diesel due to its cleaner emissions and stated that customer requirements for high reliability drove their inclusion of backup fuel. He detailed the technical setup of LNG storage, explained the design and scale of tanks, and affirmed that they would only select either LNG or diesel. He emphasized their focus on safety, air quality, and minimal emissions. He also highlighted their solar and battery backup systems, noting the challenge of integrating backup fuel for continuous service.

Sullivan Love asked about negotiations with Eagle Mountain for utilities.

Greg Probst confirmed they had a recent development agreement but were still evaluating whether to drill a well or extend a pipeline.

Robert McMullin inquired about how LNG tanks are kept at -260°F.

Greg Probst explained that it involved specialized cooling systems similar to air conditioning.

Karen Ellingson asked what would happen if power were disrupted.

Greg Probst said they had a diesel-powered backup generator to initiate operations and keep tanks cool.

Lorraine Davis questioned the timing and necessity of the backup fuel plan.

Greg Probst said the need became clear through further engagement with the pipeline provider, Kern River, and was driven by customer assurance needs. He emphasized that it was always meant as an emergency measure, not part of a phased approach. He also addressed traffic concerns, stating that truck traffic would only increase briefly when filling or refreshing the tanks and would comply with county road requirements.

Lorraine Davis expressed concern about the fire risks associated with LNG.

Greg Probst agreed and said they would work with the fire marshal on all safety measures. He explained that diesel and LNG would require similar space, about 1.5 acres, and the tank setup would depend on seasonal modeling for reliability.

Lorraine Davis asked for precedence.

Greg Probst said this was a first for their company, which mainly focused on renewable projects, but they were working with professionals experienced in LNG.

Shayne Pierce confirmed there were no further questions and invited public comment.

Holly McKinney, Mayor of Fairfield Town, stated she had spoken previously and raised concerns that the project site was within Fairfield's annexation policy area, not Eagle Mountain's. She questioned why Eagle Mountain was involved when Fairfield was closer and better positioned to provide services. She criticized Eagle Mountain's over-allocation of water and suggested that the conditional use permit should explicitly list approved fuels rather than use the broad term "alternative fuels." She also raised concerns about notification compliance and urged consideration of impacts on Fairfield's roads and infrastructure.

Sullivan Love asked Holly McKinney to clarify her earlier reference to methane gas.

Holly McKinney explained that the bright pink square on the map represented a municipal landfill with 17 wells actively burning methane gas, capable of powering up to 8,000 homes. She emphasized that this location bordered Fairfield more than Eagle Mountain and questioned Eagle Mountain's development agreement, given their water limitations. She maintained that Fairfield had never denied annexation and was better positioned geographically and infrastructurally to support the project.

Shayne Pierce responded that the commission did not involve itself in annexation decisions.

Holly McKinney reiterated that Fairfield was never approached for annexation, even though its roads and water lines were closer, and suggested that Eagle Mountain's involvement was motivated by revenue.

Lorraine Davis asked why Fairfield would be more affected.

Holly McKinney pointed out past damages to Fairfield's roads during solar panel construction and the lack of compensation. She reiterated that the project clearly bordered Fairfield and not Eagle Mountain and raised concerns about notification practices.

Greg Robinson confirmed that notices were mailed to adjacent property owners, and that Fairfield was also notified.

Shayne Pierce asked staff about road maintenance requirements.

Greg Robinson explained that conditions from the original conditional use, including road improvements and coordination with Public Works, would still apply and be enforced, although this new conditional use was separate and contingent on the first.

Shayne Pierce asked whether restrictions could be placed on using roads through Fairfield or Eagle Mountain.

Greg Robinson and Dale Eyre clarified that the county could not regulate road usage within other jurisdictions.

Dale Eyre added that cities have sole jurisdiction over their roads, and the county's only role is to notify them of nearby developments.

Shayne Pierce asked if both cities could have annexation plans for the same area.

Greg Robinson stated that Fairfield recently updated its annexation plan, which does include the project site.

Dale Eyre emphasized that annexation decisions rest with property owners and cities, not county.

Sullivan Love questioned whether county approval might strengthen a developer's case to seek services from Eagle Mountain.

Greg Robinson said that while developers might try to coordinate with cities for practical reasons, county approval alone does not give them leverage over municipalities.

Shayne Pierce asked under what conditions the commission could deny a conditional use.

Dale Eyre explained that denial must be based on a detrimental impact that cannot be reasonably mitigated and that staff typically reviews those issues in advance.

Lorraine Davis expressed discomfort with Fairfield bearing most of the project's impact while receiving no benefit. She questioned whether communication was adequate and suggested that more residents might have attended if given more time.

Greg Robinson acknowledged the concern but noted that the county followed its 10-day notice requirement and faced tight deadlines for processing applications.

Greg Robinson reiterated that road impacts were addressed in Condition 6 of the original conditional use, and roads near the site would be improved to county standards. He explained that while earlier solar applications allowed access via gravel roads, the current project's occupancy required paved roads.

Shayne Pierce asked if the staff report identified any unmitigated impacts.

Greg Robinson confirmed that while concerns existed, especially about traffic, they were addressable through coordination. He also clarified that the amended approval language specified that either liquefied natural gas or diesel could be used, with all applicable state and federal regulations required.

Lorraine Davis encouraged improved communication between the developer and Fairfield.

Adrienne Bell, representing the developer Enyo and Mosada Energy, responded that they had worked with Fairfield for over five years, maintained regular communication, and planned for road access exclusively through Eagle Mountain, not Fairfield.

Lorraine Davis thanked Adrienne Bell for the clarification.

Motion: Robert McMullin    Second: Sullivan Love

Motion to approve the conditional use permit application #CU2025-02, including nine conditions in staff recommendation. The motion passed with the following vote: "Aye" Shayne Pierce, Robert McMullin, Karen Ellingson, Sullivan Love, Lorraine Davis. "Nay" none.

### 2. Mosida Energy, LLC - Application #CU2025-03 - Request for conditional use for a proposed covered water tank which exceeds 20 feet in height and proposed exhaust stacks which exceed 40 feet in height as part of a natural gas electric power generation facility in the Mining and Grazing (M&G-1) Zone, located in Section 25, T7S R2W, approximately 34-acre project site, Cedar Valley/Fairfield area of unincorporated Utah County

Shayne Pierce introduced the next agenda item, confirming it was a conditional use request from the same applicant.

Bryce Armstrong stated that the process was the same and that the review would focus on any anticipated detrimental effects and how they could be mitigated.

Greg Robinson presented the staff report, explaining that this application was for exhaust stacks up to 80 feet tall and a fire suppression water tank up to 40 feet tall. The proposed structures exceeded the standard 40-foot maximum but were permissible through the conditional use process. He stated that the increased height was necessary to meet air quality standards and reduce noise. He clarified that while the facility was approved for 12 generators, each exhaust stack required separate approval to exceed the standard height.

Karen Ellingson noted the inconsistency in the documentation regarding previously approved stack heights.

Greg Robinson clarified the grammar issue, explaining that the facility was approved for 12 generators but not for the increased stack heights, which this request was meant to cover.

Shayne Pierce asked about the types of detrimental impacts.

Greg Robinson confirmed that aesthetics, air quality, and noise were the main considerations. He noted that the review applied only to the portion of the structures above the base height allowed by ordinance.

Karen Ellingson pointed out that while the maximum height for stacks was stated, the water tank's maximum height was not included.

Greg Robinson acknowledged the oversight and agreed that 40 feet should be specified.

Sullivan Love raised a concern that a 40-foot water tank would not meet fire flow requirements alone.

Bryce Armstrong and Greg Robinson both responded that those issues would be addressed independently through fire marshal review and engineering standards, and pumps could be used to meet pressure needs.

Robert McMullin asked how tall other power plant stacks might be, but no one had a precise answer.

Shayne Pierce invited the applicant to speak.

Greg Probst explained that the request for 80-foot stacks stemmed from a manufacturer's standard design by General Electric. The height supported emissions compliance and noise reduction, with built-in equipment for monitoring and mitigation. He clarified that the original application included this height request, but it was not included in the public notice due to a timing oversight. Regarding the 40-foot water tank, he confirmed it would be engineered in coordination with the fire marshal, using pumps to ensure adequate pressure and flow.

Greg Probst provided visual simulations showing the relative visual impact of the stacks from different distances, comparing them to existing 125-foot power line towers. He emphasized that the stacks would be far less visually prominent and reiterated the remote location of the facility.

Karen Ellingson asked again about the adequacy of a 40-foot tank.

Greg Probst stated they would not rely on height but rather pump-driven pressure. He noted that total water use would be far below 1000 acre-feet, with the fire water being just one component.

Shayne Pierce asked if all 12 units would be identical.

Greg Probst confirmed all would be the same General Electric LM6000 PF+ models and reiterated the efficiency and low water usage of the dry low combustion design.

Motion: Sullivan Love    Second: Karen Ellingson

Motion to **approve** conditional use permit #CU2025-03 request for the height of proposed exhaust stacks to exceed 40 ft and water tank to exceed 20 ft as part of an electrical power generation facility subject to staff findings included in the staff report and the conditions items 1 through 9. With an amendment to condition 3, to include that the height of the water tank does not exceed 40 ft. The motion **passed** with the following vote: "Aye" Shayne Pierce, Robert McMullin, Karen Ellingson, Sullivan Love, Lorraine Davis. "Nay" none.

## D. AGRICULTURE PROTECTION AREA

### 1. The Relief Mine Company - Proposed agriculture protection area in the Residential Agricultural (RA-5) and Critical Environment (CE-1) Zones, Sections 17, 18, and 19, T9S R3E, Salem area of unincorporated Utah County, approximately 175 acres

Greg Robinson explained that agricultural protection areas help shield agricultural operations from nuisance complaints and zoning changes and provide some protection from eminent domain. He noted that property owners can opt-out at any time and that the protection runs with the land, not the individual. He outlined the state criteria for granting such a designation, including current agricultural use, zoning compatibility, viability for agricultural production, and trends supporting continued agricultural use. The 175-acre area in question is used for growing alfalfa and grazing cattle and lies within Salem City's annexation plan area. Approximately 10% of the land is classified as prime farmland. The zoning is RA-5 and CE-1, both permitting agricultural activities.

Shayne Pierce asked whether the designation offered protection from eminent domain.

Greg Robinson confirmed that it added a layer of complexity for those seeking to exercise it. He also explained that zoning would still allow any use currently permitted in the zone, though only listed agricultural activities would be protected under the designation.

Sullivan Love asked about apparent development in one of the presentation images.

Greg Robinson replied that the visible activity was outside the designated area.

Karen Ellingson asked how a mining company became involved in agriculture.

Greg Robinson clarified that the Relief Mine Company owns multiple properties and that the parcel in question has not been used for mining.

Robert McMullin commented on nearby residential development, noting that the agricultural protection request likely responded to encroaching urbanization. He supported the designation to protect existing agricultural use.

Kurt Beecher, representing Central Utah Water Conservancy District, stated that the district owned adjacent land and planned a water treatment facility there. He affirmed that the district supported the designation and also used nearby land for grazing.

Sullivan Love raised concerns about how utility infrastructure can be affected by agricultural protection zones and noted that no requests to remove or adjust such zones have succeeded historically.

Kurt Beecher acknowledged the potential difficulties but reiterated the district's support.

Shayne Pierce asked what justifications the commission might have for denying the request.

Bryce Armstrong and Greg Robinson explained that the decision should be based on whether the request met the state-defined criteria. They noted that the Agricultural Advisory Board had already reviewed and unanimously recommended approval.

Greg Robinson clarified that nearby residential parcels were not approved building lots.

Bryce Armstrong added that operators often seek this protection when they anticipate new developments.

Greg Robinson confirmed that subdivisions within 300 feet must notify future property owners of the proximity to an agricultural protection area.

Sullivan Love and Robert McMullin affirmed that adequate notification had been given.

Motion: Robert McMullin    Second: Karen Ellingson

Motion to recommend **approval** to the Utah County Commission of the proposed creation of the Relief Mine Company 2025 Agriculture Protection Area for agriculture protection, which includes approximately 175 acres, based on the staff report and findings listed in the report. The motion **passed** with the following vote: "Aye" Shayne Pierce, Robert McMullin, Karen Ellingson, Sullivan Love, Lorraine Davis. "Nay" none.

## E. OTHER BUSINESS

### 1. Discussion on updating Utah County Planning Commission Resolution No. 2013-01 for Rules of Conduct for Public Hearings and Meetings

Shayne Pierce opened a discussion about the commission's rules of conduct for public hearings, referencing a resolution previously distributed and printed for reference. He questioned whether the current procedures were too permissive in allowing public comment, which might be unnecessarily lengthening meetings. He suggested an edit to the rules where individuals would need to request to speak on a specific agenda item at least 48 hours before the meeting in order to be allowed to comment.

Karen Ellingson asked whether state law permits requiring individuals to sign up beforehand or whether meetings must remain open to anyone wishing to speak.

Bryce Armstrong clarified that while public meetings must be open for observation, only public hearings are legally required to include public comment. He explained that an older resolution blurred the distinction between public meetings and public hearings, requiring comment at both. He supported adding clearer language that would give the commission discretion over allowing comments in public meetings.

Karen Ellingson voiced concern that requiring a signup sheet might unintentionally restrict participation.

Bryce Armstrong responded that while procedures like time limits and signup sheets are allowed, everyone who wants to speak during a public hearing must be permitted to do so.

Shayne Pierce interpreted the rules as allowing people to sign up to speak at any point before others had been invited.

Karen Ellingson questioned what would happen if someone simply didn't want to walk to the back of the room to sign up.

Dale Eyre confirmed that public hearings must permit everyone to speak under orderly procedures and that public comment outside of a public hearing is entirely optional. He explained the legal distinction and emphasized the importance of maintaining order without limiting lawful participation.

Karen Ellingson suggested they should include language that makes this distinction clear—specifying that public hearings allow for comment and outlining procedures without appearing to restrict participation.

Bryce Armstrong mentioned that the evening's meeting had no public hearings, so there was no legal requirement to allow any public comment. He added that, based on Dale Eyre's point, there needs to be a mechanism to allow comment only when it brings material and factual clarity.

Lorraine Davis asked what the commission would do if many members of the public came to a meeting where the comment wasn't legally required and were denied the opportunity to speak, leading to disorder.

Bryce Armstrong explained that mechanisms should exist to prevent the process from being overwhelmed by public clamor and to allow comment only when it's relevant to the commission's role.

Dale Eyre explained how the Utah County Commission handles this issue by allowing public comment at the very end of the meeting.

Lorraine Davis expressed surprise that they would permit comments after decisions had already been made.

Dale Eyre stated that it was effective, and people still appreciated the opportunity to be heard.

Lorraine Davis asked whether that model would be more honest with the public by not giving the impression that their input could change decisions.

Bryce Armstrong and Dale Eyre confirmed that public comment can legally endanger decisions if they are perceived to be influenced by it.

Bryce Armstrong recalled instances where public input swayed decisions improperly, resulting in lawsuits and required reversals.

Sullivan Love said he supports allowing public comment, even if it doesn't change outcomes, because people feel acknowledged.

Lorraine Davis agreed with the importance of people feeling heard but said she was uncomfortable when people shared personal stories, believing it would impact decisions when it couldn't. She said the public should be made aware when their input has no bearing.

Karen Ellingson shared how Springville handled the issue by reading a disclosure at the beginning of meetings, clarifying whether items were open for comment and what the procedures were.

Lorraine Davis supported the idea, agreeing that people should be informed about when and how they could participate.

Bryce Armstrong noted that staff had been working to reduce the number of items that require conditional use permits by changing repetitive use cases to permitted uses with standard conditions.

Greg Robinson supported this, saying it reduced unnecessary applications and repeated discussions.

Dale Eyre emphasized that the trend in property law is to give property owners clearer expectations without requiring appearances before a commission.

Shayne Pierce stated that he liked that the commission had structured procedures rather than a free-for-all approach.

Lorraine Davis stressed that public clamor is likely to increase in the future and that it's important to update the rules accordingly.

Bryce Armstrong confirmed this would be the fourth version of the resolution since 1992 and said it's due for a refresh.

Karen Ellingson pointed out that the last update was in 2013.

Shayne Pierce concluded the discussion by confirming that staff would bring back a revised version of the resolution for further review.

Lorraine Davis encouraged everyone to brainstorm and come prepared with suggestions.
