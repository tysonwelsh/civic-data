ITEMS OF SPECIAL INTEREST

o Request for Support for Goring Ranch Forest Legacy Project — Casey Snider,
Executive Director for Bear River Land Conservancy, is working on a property above

Bug Lake — half of the property is in Cache County and half is in Rich County. It is called
the Forest Legacy Project. The program allows landowners to be compensated in part
for having land that provides public access and has critical natural values. In exchange
the landowner agrees to not develop the property. Snider represents Junior Goring who
has 2,800 acres he would like protected through this program. There is wide support for
the project. Snider wants to let the County know about the project; an application will be
submitted sometime in February to the Utah Division of State Forestry, Fire and State
Lands. Snider is asking for the Council’s support of the project.

Council member White noted this is funded by off-shore drilling and not by public money,

The Council favored the project and Executive Buttars recommended that when the
project is finalized, Snider send the County a letter requesting formal support.

Buttars invited those present for the Public Hearing to sign a roll and explained the purpose of
the hearing is to gain citizens’ input as to their needs and consider potential projects for which
funding may be applied under the 2019 Community Development Block Grant program. The
grant money must be spent on projects benefitting primarily low to moderate income individuals.
Approximately $450,000.00 is expected to be available in the Bear River Region for CDBG
projects in this new program year. Information on eligible activities that can be accomplished
under this program and a schedule of the 2019 application process can be found on the Bear
River Association of Governments’ website — https://www.brag.utah.gov We cannot accept
applications for projects located within the Logan City limits. Logan City has its own CDBG
program.

PUBLIC HEARING: November 27, 2018 — 6:00 P.M. — 2019 Budget and/or Tax Increase
Hearing — Chairman Erickson read the statement regarding the proposed tax increase and
opened the Public Hearing.

Jeff Young, Richmond Mayor, said Richmond held the rate this last tax session and he recommends the
County holds the rate. An analysis in Richmond indicated that because Richmond had not held the tax
rate from the 1970's to the 1990's the increase was equal to what the rate would have been if it had been
the tax rate had been held as needed over the years. Young expressed appreciation for the County
considering hiring additional firemen with the revenue generated by holding the tax rate. This has
spurred additional discussion among mayors and cities concerning fire services. He has met with four
other cities and discussed the possibility of joining in local agreements for metro style fire service.

Gary Foster, asked that more roads and bike paths be built in Cache County to have better connectivity.

Alton Hebdon said there are alternatives to raising taxes — development such as recreational/resort.

There was no other public comment.

ACTION: Motion by Council member Zilles to close the Public Hearing. White seconded
the motion. The vote was unanimous, 6-0. Tidwell absent.

Council member Merrill told Mr. Foster the county is trying to address roads and SR30 is an
example. UDOT is going improve the road and put in a bike path, but it has taken how many
years of prodding from the county? A county bike trail coordinator has been hired and opened
up scores of miles of new paths. The county is trying to do it in a systematic, fiscally
responsible way.

Director Jensen explained that cities run on a fiscal July to June year and know the values of
their properties before they set their tax rates. Counties run on a calendar year and have to
estimate what values will be in 2019 and base a rate from that which we want to equal the
budgeted revenue. But when values are calculated next year, our rate may change on what the
value actually is. Holding the rate is more of an art than a science, but we do our best to
forecast. Cache County's composite rate is 54% of what is allowed by the State.

INITIAL PROPOSAL FOR CONSIDERATION OF ACTION

e Discussion — Reconsideration of Council Action taken August 28, 2018 regarding
Ivin’s Hollow Road — Executive Buttars said on August 28", three options were

presented and Option 3 was chosen which was to close the road, stabilize the drainage,
build a channel and simplify slope excavation. It would cost about $270,000.00 and
federal dollars would cover 75% of that amount. Property owners gave their input and
they have concerns with the road closure.

Director Runhaar said the work program was cancelled for the project as a result of
dialogue with the property owners so there will not be any federal monies for the project.

Buttars recommends Option 2 — rebuild the road to a smaller one-lane road. Property
owners in the area have offered gravel and labor to help with the road. This road existed
for fifty years and the standard has been pretty low. Runhaar said the county will have to
do work on the road eventually and suggested taking it back to a gravel surface which is
less costly and easier to repair. It is used for farm equipment because Walker Hill farther
up the road is problematic for farm equipment.

Chris and Clair Allen spoke in favor of keeping the road. Clair noted that it worked great until
Richmond Irrigation Company closed their canal; however, there is an equalizing pond now which
may eliminate the water problem. He suggests giving it a try for a few years.

Council members suggested a gravel road with winter closure as a possible solution.

Chairman Erickson said the County should have had a better discussion with the property
owners before making the decision to support Option 3.

ACTION: Motion by Council member Zilles to explore the cost and effort for a gravel
road with winter closure and gating at which time the Council will have more information
to make a better decision. Worthen seconded the motion. The vote was unanimous, 6-0.
Tidwell absent.

FEDERAL ROADLESS RULE DESIGNATION: Nov. 27™, 2018

The State of Utah is developing a petition regarding the Federal Roadless Rule (FRR) and has asked that all
Utah counties review this policy. Enacted in 2001, this policy restricted the development of additional
roads through 49% of the US Forest Service (USFS) areas within Utah. Specifically, the County has been
asked to:

1) Review the current inventoried roadless areas within the County

2) Coordinate public meetings with PLCPO to receive feedback

3) Assist PLPCO with designing management regimes to better manage forests, protect property,

and ensure healthy and sustainable watersheds and wildlife habitat.

The Federal Roadless Rule specifically categories USFS lands into Inventoried Roadless Areas (IRAs) with
three classifications:
1B: IRAs where road construction and reconstruction is prohibited
1B-1: IRAs that are recommended for wilderness designation in the forest plan and where road
construction and reconstruction is prohibited.
1C: IRAs where road construction and reconstruction is not prohibited.

While Federal map data is currently incorrect, a verbal confirmation has affirmed that all of the USFS areas
in Cache County affected by the FRR are class 1B. The mapping provided on www.cachecounty.org/gis has
been updated from the Federal maps to reflect this verbal confirmation. Note: Wilderness areas are not
designated as roadless in Cache County, but fall under different regulations. They are not being included by
the State in this petition.

The State has asked that the County provide comment on the FRR classifications by indicating a preferred
management type as follows:
1) Primitive Areas — little to no evidence of human use
2) Forest Restoration Areas — managed areas that still retain their roadless character
3) Active Management Areas — Display increased levels of human use (roads, facilities, vegetation
manipulation)
4) Areas Recommended for Re-inventory or Boundary Adjustments — areas that should be changed
from a roadless status.

Staff has completed an analysis of the issues, communicated repeatedly with the USFS, and understands
that the primary result of changing the FRR classification will provide the USFS with additional tools to
help with forest management, specifically the building of temporary roads. In looking at the available data,
staff is recommending that a few areas within the County have their FRR classification changed from 1B to
1C, allowing for additional temporary road building to assist with vegetation, watershed, and wildlife
management. These areas are locations that have slopes that will accommodate road building, tree stands
that are sizeable enough to manage, and already have existing built roads through the area including IRAs:

4440 Mt. Naomi 5989 Mt. Naomi

Description
A request has been made by Bruce Leishman for a Design Exception to extend the county road and
maintenance on a substandard county road, 8100 South (Exhibit A).

Requirements and Resolution 2015-20
The Cache County Manual of Roadway Design and Construction Standards (Road Manual) section
1.8 Authority and Design Exceptions, specifies that:
“Full justification and documentation must be provided explaining the reasoning as to why the
roadway standards cannot be met, why an alternative design or construction method can meet the
intent of the roadway standards, and including any other relevant information.”

Resolution 2015-20 specifies that the expansion of road services on substandard roads that do not
already serve existing homes is not permitted. However, the Council may grant an exception if
proposed road improvements improve the health and/or safety of existing subdivisions, homes, or
businesses.

Resolution 2015-20 also states that:

“...within the existing county budget constraints funding is not sufficient for the existing
network of roadways to be properly maintained and that additional road length is a net cost to the
county even considering existing Class B allocations from the State and property tax rates...”

“...it is not in the best interest for the safety and/or welfare of existing or future residents of
Cache County to diminish services on existing roadways, to maintain new roads, or to provide
substandard service on new roadways...”

“..there are nearly 200 miles of existing paved road in Cache County that can accommodate
development with little to no impact on the County’s ability to serve said development.”

Proponent Justification and Staff Response

Justification 1:
A hammer head turn-around at the west end of 8100 South will improve access for service and
emergency vehicles. The proposed turn-around would be built by the proponent.

Staff Response to Justification 1:

A. While a turn-around may improve access for service and emergency vehicles, the long-term cost
and diminishing level of county wide service associated with the additional maintenance of the
associated roadway is not in the best interest of the county at this time.

MOUNTAIN VIEW MEADOWS REZONE

AN ORDINANCE AMENDING THE COUNTY ZONING MAP

WHEREAS, the “County Land Use Development and Management Act,” Utah Code Ann.
§17-27a-101 ef seq., as amended (the “Act”), provides that each county may enact a land
use ordinance and a zoning map establishing regulations for land use and development;
and

WHEREAS, pursuant to the Act, the County’s Planning Commission (the “Planning
Commission”) shall prepare and recommend to the county’s legislative body, following a
public hearing, a proposed land use ordinance and a zoning map, or amendments thereto,
that represent the Planning Commission’s recommendations for zoning the area within the
county, and;

WuerEAS, the Planning Commission caused notice of the hearing to be advertised at least
ten (10) days before the date of the public hearing in The Herald Journal, a newspaper of
general circulation in Cache County, and;

WHEREAS, on November 1, 2018, 5:35 p.m., the Planning Commission held a public
hearing for a rezone from the Agricultural (A10) Zone to the Rural 2 (RU2) Zone, which
meeting was preceded by all required legal notice and at which time all interested parties
were given the opportunity to provide written or oral comment concerning the proposed
rezone, and;

WHEREAS, on November 1, 2018, the Planning Commission recommended the approval of
said rezone and forwarded such recommendation to the County Council for final action,
and;

WuerEAS, the Act also provides certain procedures for the county legislative body to
adopt or amend the land use ordinance and zoning map for the county, and;

WHEREAS, the County Council caused notice of the hearing to be advertised .at least ten
(10) days before the date of the public hearing in The Herald Journal, a newspaper of
general circulation in Cache County, and;

WHEREAS, on November 27, 2018, 5:30 p.m., the County Council held a public hearing to
consider any comments regarding the proposed rezone. The County Council accepted all
comments, and;

WHEREAS, after careful consideration of the recommendation of the Planning
Commission, comments at the public hearing and other public meetings where such
proposed rezone was discussed, recommendation of county staff, and the findings of fact
identified in the staff report, the Council has determined that it is in the best interest of the
health, safety and welfare of the citizens of Cache County to approve such rezone;
