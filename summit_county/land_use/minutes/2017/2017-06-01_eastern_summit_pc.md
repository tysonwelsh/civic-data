Director Putt stated the background. In Eastern Summit County, there are several Zoning 
Districts which identify which types of uses can occur, how much of that use can take place 
(density), and standards for height and setbacks, which are very specific. In Eastern 
Summit County, a SPA is a Zone. There is a process associated with it, but a SPA is a Zone. A 
SPA is different from other Zones because the Standards are determined through the 
review process, itself, including the mix of uses. A SPA is intended to give flexibility and 
mixing of uses. SPA processes go through the Planning Commission first for a 
recommendation to the County Council. The County Council will be the final body that will 
make the decision for a SPA project. One of the Standards that must be reviewed before a 
recommendation is given is if this project provides a benefit to the residents of Eastern 
Summit County. It is the responsibility of the Master Developer to help demonstrate the 
project is in the best interest of the residents’ health, safety and welfare. There are certain 
projects that would benefit having a mix of uses, such as the existing Promontory 
Residential Development. A SPA allows creative solutions with the design. 
The requirements for approving a SPA are: there are legitimate, tangible public benefits, 
other than just building the project. There will also need to be a determination that there

are unique sets of circumstances that the SPA is the right tool to use, rather than another 
Zone available. There is an existing rural, small town, agricultural character in Eastern 
Summit County that changes to the SPA must remain compatible with. The changes must 
not have a negative effect on the social, cultural, rural institutional values in Eastern 
Summit County. 
There is an existing SPA in the proposed area now that is memorialized through contract of 
a Development Agreement. To change that, the area must meet these criteria for 
modifications:  
1. The circumstances surrounding this request have changed, unknown at the time the 
original SPA was approved. 
2. This modification is consistent with efficient, smart development that does not 
adversely affect the surrounding lands and community.

Attorney Strachan stated that in 2015, Promontory Investments, LLC applied for an 
amendment to the Promontory SPA, seeking an Administrative Amendment under the 
original SPA. There are two ways to amend the Development Agreement: An 
Administrative Amendment or a Substantial Amendment. A Substantial Amendment is 
when uses, density, configuration, etc. is changed. Anything else is considered an 
Administrative Amendment. When the application for an amendment was submitted, it was 
studied in detail to determine if there were significant enough changes to bump into a 
Substantial Amendment. Staff determined that the application showed the uses, density, 
etc. were the same as the broader Promontory area and did not qualify for a Substantial 
Amendment. South Point just wants to have its own sub-development agreement under the 
broader Promontory umbrella. This did not go through the Planning Commission because it 
was treated as an Administrative Amendment, approved by the Community Development 
Director. In March 2017, South Point, LLC became the owner of the South Point 
Development. The new proposed SPA Amendment is a Substantial Amendment.
