         3.1.1     Samantha Price is requesting a permit to operate a daycare out of her residence She has applied
                   for a Home Occupation – Family Childcare Class D3, defined as a Class D home occupation that
                   provides caregiver services for nine to twelve children with sufficient staffing required by the State
                   of Utah Health Department. That number includes Ms. Price’s own children under the age of six
                   who are not yet in full day school. She has five children of her own under age six and not attending
                   school all day, allowing her to request to provide service for up to seven additional children with a
                   second provider present or provide services for three if there is not a second provider.

         3.1.2     A Class D Home Occupation is generally reviewed by City Staff but during the public notices being
                   mailed to entities and property owners within 300 feet of the property, Staff received seven
                   comments expressing concern or opposition to the proposed daycare use. A common concern
                   was the existing home daycare at 5778 South Player Ridge, and this now being the second
                   application for a home daycare in the cul-de-sac. Residents expressed that they feel accessory
                   dwelling units and home occupations being allowed in the area is changing the residential nature
                   of their neighborhood. Residents also expressed concern that an additional home daycare would
                   increase traffic and noise and frequently mentioned the capacity for traffic on the narrow Jordan
                   Canal Road as well as in Player Ridge Circle. Therefore, the Director delegated this particular
                   proposal to the Planning Commission for review and decision pursuant to the powers and duties
                   identified in City Code.

         3.1.3     The property is described as Lot 1 of the Player Cove Subdivision, located on Player Ridge Circle, a
                   cul-de-sac off Jordan Canal Road. The only access is via a bridge that crosses the South Jordan
                   Canal. Lot 1 has the first front property line upon entering Player Ridge Circle and contains
                   approximately 16,117 square feet. The property is a single-family home owned by Rachel Young.
                   The home has an interior accessory dwelling unit (ADU), occupied by the applicant, Samantha
                   Price.

         3.1.4     There is an existing Code Enforcement Department complaint for this property which shows
                   fencing issues that need correction. There must be a 6’ high fence for the whole back yard. There
                   is also a problem with the back yard not being properly landscaped and maintained.

3.2      FINDINGS:
         3.2.1  This application was initiated by Samantha Price on April 12, 2022.
         3.2.2  The subject property is in the R-1-15 zoning district.
         3.2.3  The subject property is in the “Estate Residential” designation in the City’s General Plan.
         3.2.4  This conditional use permit request is consistent with current zoning and General Plan
                designations.
         3.2.5  Samantha Price resides in the internal accessory dwelling unit (ADU) on the property and has
                submitted a notarized Property Owner Affidavit acknowledging the home occupation use.
         3.2.6  As of the date of the Staff Report, the property owner has not secured necessary permits/licenses
                for the internal accessory dwelling unit. The driveway is 1,950 square feet and can accommodate
                all requested uses of the building.
         3.2.7  The proposed daycare use has passed all State inspections at this location.
         3.2.8  City Staff received seven responses to the mailed notice.
         3.2.9  Staff and review agencies have identified conditions of approval to be considered by the Planning
                Commission prior to preliminary approval.
         3.2.10 The Director has deferred decision-making authority for this administrative conditional use permit

Zoning Text Amendment

 6.        4Z22/DCA-000224-2022 – Public Hearing and Recommendation to the City Council for a Zoning Text
           Amendment to Taylorsville Municipal Code Section 13.25.010, Related to Park Strip Landscaping.
           (Terryne Bergeson/Planning intern)

6.1      This item was presented by Terryne Bergeson/Planning Intern.             She stated that conditions in the
         State and throughout the Country have led to efforts to reduce water consumption. Park strips are an easy
         opportunity for property owners to reduce water use through landscaping design. City Staff has reviewed
         Section 13.25.010 of the City’s Code to ensure that there are no barriers to water-wise landscaping. She
         reviewed the proposed update to park strip standards which is intended to provide clarity while remaining
         conscientious of effects on pedestrian safety, water use, urban heat island effect, storm water
         management, and sense-of place. Other cities throughout the valley have enacted ordinances that limit
         lawn in yard and prohibit lawn in park strips. Taylorsville’s proposed text amendment does not prohibit
         lawn but does provide clear standards.

         6.1.1     The City of Taylorsville is proposing to amend the General Landscape Standards found in the Land
                   Development Code, specifically the standards for park strips in 13.25.010 (C) and (D). Landscaping
                   throughout the State of Utah is becoming a visible area for improvement in water conservation
                   practices. Taylorsville City wants to ensure that the City Code is not a barrier for property owners
                   who wish to update their landscaping in the interest of decreasing water demand, and the Code
                   provides guidance and standards to maintain aesthetics and safety.

         6.1.2     Staff has reviewed the current landscape standards in Chapter 13.25 for language that would
                   inhibit property owners from updating to more water efficient designs. After reviewing Chapter
                   13.25, City Staff determined that while there is nothing that requires or prohibits lawn, it is not
                   easy to decipher the landscaping standards for park strips. In some zoning districts, the Code
                   requires 100 percent landscaping materials but in other zones park strips could be 100 percent
                   hardscape materials. This proposed amendment helps to clarify what types of landscape
                   treatments are appropriate and in what areas.
