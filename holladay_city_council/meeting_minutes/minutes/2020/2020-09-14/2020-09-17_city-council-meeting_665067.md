I.     Welcome – Mayor Dahle.
Mayor Dahle called the meeting to order at approximately 6:00 p.m. He read into the record the
Order on Public Meetings of the City Council of the City of Holladay.

II.      Public Comments
Mayor Dahle updated the Council on the officer involved shooting that happened earlier in the day.
It started with a crash at the intersection of 4500 S and 2300 E and a Millcreek officer was at the gas
station and went to help. There was a foot pursuit and altercation that involved three officers.
Witnesses are still being interviewed, the suspect is in critical condition and no officers were injured


III.   Public Hearing on Proposed Amendment to the Zoning Ordinance Creating a New
       Residential Infill (RI) Zone.
Community Development Director, Paul Allred reported that for many years the City Council has
expressed a disinterest in zoning anything R-1-4. The result is three zones that are close to what he
wants to do. The RI zoning would be more flexible, however, than any of the three. The zone would
have to be located in an area with equal or greater density.

Mayor Dahle opened the public hearing at 6:15pm. The Council has received written comments from
Steve Doxey, Ron Hilton, Ryan Steele, Thomas Leishman, Alex & Mandana Ghaffarian, Joe &
Maryann Rosenthal, Holley Richards, Steve Peterson and Thomas Nebeker that will be included in
the public record.

Steven Doxey -2430 Kentucky Ave. He, mentioned that he had been speaking with staff about how the
proposal might pick up some of the “sliver properties” throughout the City. He supports the proposal
by Hilton and is in support of the ordinance.

Melissa Hilton - she wants to stay in Holladay but wants to downsize. The RI zone would close to
the city center and would help keep elevations lower.

style living, lower height, smaller landscape area. It is something that Holladay does not have but

Cynthia Collier- 1886 Osage Orange Ave. She supports the proposal. She explained that it would

IV.    Continued Public Hearing on Proposed General Plan Amendment for the Area Located at
       Approximately 2450 East and 4680 South, Currently Designated as MDR-S (Medium-
       Density Residential Stable) to LDR-S (Low-Density Residential Stable).
Mayor Dahle continued the public hearing on the matter and reported that the City has received a lot
of public comment. There were no members of the public wishing to speak. Mayor Dahle closed
the public hearing at 6:37 pm.
The matter was to be discussed during the Work Session and return at the October 1 meeting for vote.

V.     City Manager Report – Manager Chamness.
Manager Chamness updated the Council on recent rainstorm damage in the City. An emergency was
declared and clean-up efforts occurred quickly. The City was asking the public to report property
damage in the City as well as all volunteer efforts and hours for debris removal to support emergency
funding. Council Member Quinn clarified that the definition of “property damage” includes fallen
trees. Ms. Chamness expressed her sincere gratitude to City staff and the City of Holladay’s partner
organizations.

Ms. Chamness reported that Salt Lake County has informed the City that it will be receiving a second
distribution of Coronavirus Aid, Relief and Economic Security (“CARES”) Act funds for COVID-19
response. The funds will be used specifically for public safety response efforts.

Ms. Chamness noted that the State of Utah and the City of Holladay are experiencing a spike in
COVID-19 cases. She urged all to continue to be vigilant.

VI.     Council Reports and District Issues.
Council Member Quinn echoed Ms. Chamness’ gratitude for neighborly assistance during the recent
storm. She asked Ms. Chamness for updates on the Wander Lane traffic impacts. Ms. Chamness
noted that there have been meetings and updates should be forthcoming in the next several weeks.

Council Member Fotheringham also expressed gratitude for neighbor assistance during the recent
storm. He mentioned that the Arts Council held a meeting over the weekend and acknowledged the
upcoming chalk-art contest. The Council also recently received Zoo Arts and Parks (“ZAP”) funding.

VII.    Recess City Council to a Work Meeting.
        a.      Discussion on Proposed Storm Water Ordinance.
Mayor Dahle introduced the Storm Water Ordinance discussion and Ms. Smith to provide an
overview of the ordinance to the Council. Ms. Smith indicated that the proposed ordinance reflects
previous policy discussions with the Council. Essentially, the fee will be restructured according to
residential A and B areas as well as non-residential areas. The ordinance defines how the fee is
determined and sets forth an appeal process. The ordinance also establishes a fund for collection
purposes. Staff would like Council to review the ordinance more thoroughly on October 1. A public
hearing was scheduled for October 15. Staff planned to host a Facebook Live Event Q & A event
prior to the public hearing. She asked for approval to publish the public hearing date in the newsletter.

Council Member Gibbons suggested two public hearings be conducted to provide the community
with more background on the need for the ordinance. Mayor Dahle stated that it was possible to
simply keep the public hearing open rather than close it on the 15th. He agreed that there should be
ample opportunity for the public to comment. City Attorney, Todd Godfrey agreed that this approach
would be acceptable.

Ms. Smith reported that staff is working on a complementary administrative document for the
ordinance that will be shared with the Council.

The Council supported publishing an October 15 public hearing date.

        b.      Discussion on Proposed Holladay Crossroads Zone.
Council Member Gibbons reported that the Holladay Crossroads Zone is a zone that was approved
by the Planning Commission and warrants a hearing before the Council. He identified concerns with
strip malls in the area and felt that the Holladay Crossroads Zone may provide a better solution for
residents in the long run.

Mr. Allred added that the Small Area Master Plan was adopted less than one year after the General
Plan update was adopted. The Small Area Master Plan is the precursor to the Holladay Crossroads
Zone. The Holladay Crossroads Zone provides an opportunity to create a gateway to the City. The
area produces around 15% of the City’s tax base. Mr. Allred gave an overview of the benefits of the
Holladay Crossroads Zone and explained that it also assists with community transition.

Council Member Fotheringham asked about the area boundaries and the proposed plans for abutting
lots. Mr. Allred explained that the abutting lots can apply for different zoning in the future.
Council Member Gibbons emailed comments to the Council that were to be attached to the record.

The Council and the Mayor discussed next steps on how to move the matter toward resolution.
Ms. Chamness suggested that staff prepare a timeline for the Council to consider at the next meeting.
Mr. Allred added that the proposal could result in joint development projects that will be beneficial
to the City. The Council engaged in discussion on possible future projects.

        c.      Discussion on Proposed General Plan Map Amendment.
Mayor Dahle asked for clarification on whether duplexes are a part of the application. Mr. Allred
indicated that they are not allowed in the HV Zone.

Mayor Dahle raised concerns about the incongruity of the Planning Commission’s unanimous
recommendation in relation to staff’s suggestion that the MDR-S designation is a long-term vision
that considers more density as redevelopment occurs. He was compelled to side with the Planning
Commission on the proposal.

Council Member Fotheringham was concerned about getting back to LDR-S in the buffer-zone. He
remarked that MDR-T may be more appropriate on the east side of the City around the Village.
Council Member Gibbons stated that the discussion would be different if the subject area had not
been historically built up. He was opposed to allowing build-up in legacy neighborhoods.

Council Member Quinn raised concerns with increased traffic that accompanies increased density and
potential strain on infrastructure. Council Member Durham recognized the concern with increased
density but considered it to be a close call due to the proximity to the Village. He questioned where
the MDR designation would be allowed.

        d.      Discussion on Proposed Residential Infill Zone.
Council Member Gibbons stated the Richards’ application seemed straight forward, however, the
Hilton parcel raises procedural and substantive issues. He raised concerns that the Hilton application
was withdrawn from the Planning Commission and revised. He asked if the Council was considering
a revised draft that has not been reviewed or considered by the Planning Commission.

City Recorder, Stephanie Carlson reported that the document in the Council packet was what was
considered by the Planning Commission prior to changes being made. Mr. Godfrey added that
Mr. Hilton submitted two separate applications. One was for a General Plan Amendment that would
have accommodated the RI Zone and the other was the text amendment to create the RI Zone.
Mr. Hilton withdrew the General Plan Amendment application, which was not before the Council.
Procedurally, the City was in a good position to make a decision on the RI Zone text amendment.

Mayor Dahle asked if there was a nexus between both applications. Mr. Godfrey indicated that there
was not. Mr. Allred explained that the RI Zone text amendment sat for a long time prior to coming
to the Council in part due to COVID-19. There was discussion regarding the processing of the
request. Mayor Dahle reminded the Council that it is a City-wide application and not specific to Mr.
Hilton’s property.

Mayor Dahle asked for clarification regarding what the proposed RI Zone text amendment would
provide that does not already exist. Mr. Allred explained that the new RI Zone is more restrictive in
terms of height but less restrictive in terms of landscaping required. It is more flexible with setbacks
allowing for a greater portion of the lot to be used for the floor area of the home. It also restricts any
structure on a lot of that size to one story. Mayor Dahle asked for a review based on the Planning
Commission denial. Mr. Hobbs explained that the Commission felt that the application was
premature and they were concerned about how the proposal would apply to Mr. Hilton’s property.

Council Member Gibbons was reluctant to approve the application since the proposal is premature
for the entire City. He was grateful to Mr. Hilton but stated that this is an unusual situation because
it is a citizen-proposed text amendment that is not completely divorced from Mr. Hilton’s property.

Council Member Quinn voiced concerns about water absorption in the ordinance allowing for water
to cover up to 80% of impervious surfaces. She explained that the rest of the City is limited to 60%
coverage, particularly because the Council is considering a Stormwater Fee. She suggested meeting
with the Planning Commission to discuss zoning ramifications at some point in the future.

Council Member Durham asked if approval of the application would result in language that is just
available to be utilized in future zoning matters, not necessarily that it would be applied to any specific
matter. Mr. Allred confirmed that that was the case. He agreed that further discussion on zoning
ramifications with the Planning Commission would be beneficial.

Council Member Fotheringham did not object to the idea of the RI Zone but had concerns about its
compatibility. He explained that the long-term plan is to add density to the downtown area.
Discussion ensued regarding the various zoning ramifications. Council Member Fotheringham did
not support approving the proposal at this time. Mayor Dahle agreed with Council Member Gibbons
that the proposal may be premature. Mr. Hobbs stated that staff is in favor of the general concept.

Mayor Dahle reminded the Council that they could address any questions with staff prior to the vote
scheduled for October 1.
