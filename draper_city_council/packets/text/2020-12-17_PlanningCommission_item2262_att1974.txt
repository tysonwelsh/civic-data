BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY
This application is a request for approval of a Site Plan for approximately 1.75 acres located on the east
side of Lone Peak Parkway and South of Ikea Way, at approximately 123 W. Ikea Way (Exhibit C). The
property is currently zoned CSD-DRC. The applicant is requesting that a Site Plan be approved to allow
for the development of the currently vacant site as an office building with a furniture showroom and
storage space.

As part of the CSD-DRC, the proposed building is subject to the specific requirements of that zone,
including but not limited to parking, required landscaping, and landscape buffers. The applicant is
requesting a reduction to parking requirements as well as review and approval from the Planning
Commission that the proposed site plan is substantially in accordance with the schematic development
plan provided in ordinance. The applicant also seeks approval from the Planning Commission for the Site
Plan prior to submittal or review of a site specific geotechnical report in compliance with current
Geologic Hazards ordinance. Such a report is required as part of the Site Plan application per Draper City
Municipal Code (DCMC) Section 9-5-090(D)(1)(h) and the Geologic Hazards code requirements found in
DCMC Sections 9-19-090 and 9-19-100(A).

Site Plan Layout.
The applicant is proposing to develop the site with a two-story commercial building having a 19,880 ft²
footprint and 30,554 ft² of gross floor space (Exhibit F). Access will come from a shared internal access
road with connections to Lone Peak Parkway (200 West) and Ikea Way. The building is proposed to be
located to the North on the property and is setback from the northern property line between 23.75 and
28.08 feet which exceeds the minimum ordinance requirements of 20 feet. Automobile parking is
proposed to be located on the East, South and West sides.
Landscaping
The CSD-DRC zone requires development to be in substantial compliance with a schematic development
plan per DCMC 9-18B-030, included in ordinance as Exhibit A in DCMC 9-18B-110 (Exhibit K). The
schematic development plan depicts a building being located to the west with a large landscaped buffer
between the building and the western property line. The area of the buffer shown on the schematic plan
has been approximated to be between 8,900 ft² and 10,865 ft². The schematic plan was developed and
codified in ordinance prior to a redesign of the intersection of Ikea Way and Lone Peak Parkway/200
West. The result of the intersection redesign added approximately an additional 2,727 ft² of property to
the Northwest corner of the subject property.

The proposed plans (Exhibits F and G) provide a landscape buffer of approximately only 4,684 ft²
between the western property line and the nearest edge of the western parking lot. This is significantly
smaller than the buffer depicted in the schematic development plan. The applicant is requesting that
approximately 1,271 ft² of additional landscape area on the north of the western parking lot and an
approximately 1,307 ft² on the South of the western parking lot be counted towards the overall buffer area
required on the west. The combined total would be approximately 7,262 ft² of landscaped area between
the western property line and the face of the proposed building. The applicant is requesting that the
Planning Commission accept the proposed layout as being in substantial compliance with the landscape
buffer requirements of the schematic development plan.

Landscaping in the CSD-DRC zone requires sensitivity to the low precipitation environment and the
amount of parking required in large scale commercial developments. A critical component of the
proposed landscaping plan is parking lot landscaping, with the goal of shade and refuge from the heat
islands of large parking lots. The landscaping plan for the two parking lot islands proposed comply with
the typical parking area landscape detail of ordinance.

Each phase of development is also required to meet the minimum fifteen percent (15%) overall
landscaping percentage. The provided landscape plans (Exhibit G) show a net landscape coverage
calculated by the applicant of approximately 25%, this calculation however includes areas within the
public right of way that are expressly not permitted to be included in the landscape coverage calculations
per DCMC 9-18B-050(B). Staff estimates that the correct calculation, removing the landscaped area lying
within the public right of way, is approximately 22%.

Screening of parking areas from adjacent public streets shall include two foot (2') high shrubs or berming,
which may be substituted by a screen wall of equal height. The proposed plans comply with this
requirement.

Circulation.
The site layout as proposed provides adequate circulation for private vehicles and provides emergency
vehicle access acceptable to the Fire Marshal.

Parking.
According to the CSD-DRC zone, required parking for all uses less than 75,000 ft² is 5 spaces per 1,000
square feet of commercial use. Based on the plans provided, 153 total parking stalls are required. The
applicant has provided a parking study (Exhibit J) in support of their request to reduce the number of
parking stalls down to 82, a rate equivalent to approximately 2.68 spaces per 1,000 square feet of
commercial use. The requested number is comparable to the normal parking code requirement of 2.5
spaces per 1,000 square feet of retail space. The zone allows for modifications to the standards to be
authorized if justified by a parking study and approved by the planning commission in accordance with
DCMC section 9-25-090. Staff has reviewed the parking study and has found a minor discrepancy where
the calculations were short by 875 ft² versus the building area reported in the civil plans. This missing
square footage should have been included in the calculations for parking counts associated with the
storage area of the building. The net difference would be the need for one additional parking stall over the
56 minimum spaces required as calculated by the report. This minor discrepancy however does not
materially alter the findings of the report that the 82 spaces provided would support the specific mix of
uses proposed within the building.

The first floor of the building is predominantly office space, with a furniture showroom located towards
the front doors on the south side of the building, and a storage area towards the west. The entire second
floor of the building has been proposed as storage space only. The parking study relies on the specific
breakout of these uses to justify the reduction in parking, including the use of the second story of the
building as storage space. If the second floor were to be utilized as retail, showroom, or office space, the
proposed 82 stalls would not be sufficient. The reduction in parking if approved would necessitate that the
internal usage of these spaces not change, and could impact future potential uses for the building. Staff
has included as a condition of approval, if the Site Plan is approved, that that the second floor be used
only for storage and that no retail, offices, desks, showroom, or other workspace be added without a new
review and approval from the Planning Commission.

Architecture.
The proposed building will contain 2 stories and will be 25-feet tall which is in compliance with the
height requirements of DCMC 9-18B-040(C) (Exhibit H). The building will be constructed of tilt up
concrete and will be painted black and white. The dumpster enclosure will be constructed of masonry and
will be painted the same colors as the building in compliance with ordinance requirements of DCMC 9-
18B-040(E). A covered entry canopy is located at each entry doorway and white awnings are included
over the first story windows.

Lighting.
The outdoor lighting plan (Exhibit I) complies with the lighting requirements of the CSD-DRC listed in
DCMC 9-18B-070. Parking lot light fixtures will be mounted on 18’ tall poles anchored to a concrete
base that extends 2 feet above the finish grade. Wall mounted lights on the building exterior, and those
mounted under the entryway canopies will be recessed and pointed downwards.

Parking Modification.
Chapter 25 of the Draper City Municipal Code makes provisions for the Planning Commission to
authorize a deviation to the strict compliance with the terms of the Code regarding the calculation of the
minimum number of required parking spaces. DCMC Section 9-25-090 states that the number of parking
spaces may be modified to provide flexibility beyond natural adjustments in order to adapt to specific
circumstances, reduce potential environmental impacts, and conserve resources. The deviation to strict
compliance is justified based upon a parking study submitted by the applicant. In order to approve a
deviation to strict compliance with the parking requirements of the DCMC, the Planning Commission
must make findings based on the type of use under application.

STAFF RECOMMENDATION
Staff finds that the application does not comply with DCMC and recommends that the Planning
Commission review the request, receive public comment, and continue the application to allow the
applicant additional time to comply with ordinance requirements, or alternatively deny the application
based on the findings listed below and the criteria for denial, as listed within the staff report.

Parking Deviation:
If the Planning Commission decides to approve the request, staff recommends they include the following
conditions of approval:
         1.      That the second floor of the building be limited to storage only as detailed on the
                 proposed plans. No retail, showroom, offices, desks, or other workspace is permitted.
                 This condition of approval shall be recorded on the property title in a form approved by
                 the Draper City legal attorney.
         2.      That any proposed change to the layout or use of the building requires a new review and
                 approval by the Planning Commission including provision of a new parking study.
         3.      That the number and type of ADA spaces not be adjusted lower to match the reduced
                 parking stall count but be provided based on the higher number of overall stalls required
                 in ordinance.


Parking Deviation:
The findings for denial are as follows:
        1.      Adequate parking will not be provided.
        2.      The total number of spaces that would otherwise be required for the establishment are not
                overly burdensome for the site specific factors of the application.
        3.      There are no other businesses which are open when others are closed that would create
                trade offs between and reduce burdens to the parking provided.
        4.      There is not an adequate availability of approved on street or shared parking for all
                associated uses.
        5.      Transportation alternatives, including proximity to transit stations, do not provide
                adequate transportation to offset any reduction in parking for this site.
        6.      Any potential for future expansion or addition to the development may not be able to
                provide adequate parking for that expansion or addition.
        7.      ADA compliant parking requirements are proposed for adjustment.

Subject:       Draper Alice Lane Parking Study

This memorandum discusses the parking study completed for the proposed Alice Lane furniture
store located in Draper, Utah. The study identifies the City parking supply rates, and parking
demand rates identified by the Institute of Transportation Engineers (ITE).

Project Description

The proposed Alice Lane project is located on the southeast corner of the Ikea Way / 135 West
intersection in Draper, Utah. A vicinity map of the project site is shown in Figure 1, and a full site
plan is found in Appendix A. The building will have approximately 8,719 square feet of office
space, 7,709 square feet of showroom space, and 1,989 square feet of warehouse space on the
main floor with 11,264 square feet of storage on the upper floor. The proposed site plan calls for
82 parking stalls to be on site. Based on conversations with the developer, it is anticipated that
in peak periods, there will be up to 20 employees and a maximum of 10 patrons being helped at
one time at the store. Additional employees will be housed in the office space portion of the
building. The store operates Monday through Saturday from 10:00 AM to 7:00 PM. Saturday has
been reported as the day with lowest number of customers due to the design center portion of the
store being closed over the weekend. Alice Lane as a company primarily services customers
online or in private consultation visits and anticipates minimal customer traffic to their store front
location.

Draper City Parking Code

The site lies within a Commercial Special District. As outlined in section 9-18B-060 of the Draper
City code, 5 parking stalls per 1,000 square feet of development of commercial use within the
Commercial Special District will be required. Based on this parking rate and the proposed 29,679
square feet of space, the City would require the project to have a minimum of 149 parking stalls.

ITE Parking Demand Rates

Based on the multi-use nature of this office space, three different land-use datasets were
implemented from the Institute of Transportation Engineers (ITE), Parking Generation, 5th Edition,
2019 manual. Both weekday and Saturday 85th percentile parking rates were compared to find
the time with the anticipated maximum parking generation to remain conservative in this analysis.
The number of stalls needed based on each land use and their associated 85th percentile rates
for the proposed project are shown in Table 1. It was determined that the maximum parking
demand of 51 stalls is generated on weekdays.

Comparison

The discussed parking rates were compared to determine the number of stalls that should be
constructed at the proposed Alice Lane store in Draper. The rates were applied to the proposed
29,679 square-foot building size to calculate the number of stalls that would be required. The
comparison of the rates and number of stalls between various parking rate sources are shown in
Table 2.

It is important to note that the ITE parking rates represent the actual parking demand rate, as
opposed to supply. Typically, the parking supply should be at least 10% higher than the parking
demand to accommodate customers entering / exiting the lot to find a parking space and to
account for possible bumps in demand. The number of stalls in Table 2 reflects the calculated
parking supply with the 10% increase added to the ITE parking demand.

The estimated parking supply based on the national ITE parking generation data (56 stalls
including the 10% increase to the parking demand) is significantly lower than both the Draper City
required parking supply (149 stalls) and the parking stall supply shown in the current site plan (82
stalls).

parking at the proposed Alice Lane store in Draper, Utah:
