PRESENTATIONS:

1) GP-4-2025 Petition by West Valley City to amend the City’s General Plan by adopting station
area plans for the ½ mile areas surrounding the River Trail, Redwood Junction, and Decker Lake
light rail stations. Staff – Steve Pastorik at 801-963-3545 or steve.pastorik@wvc-ut.gov

Discussion: Steve Pastorik reintroduced Christian from WSP, who prepared revisions to the stationary
plans for three light rail station areas in West Valley City. Wasatch Regional Council (WFRC)
administers the funding for these studies and will certify the adopted plans to ensure compliance with
state code requirements. Christian explained that these plans are mandated by a 2022 state law requiring
a station area plan for the half-mile radius around each light rail station. West Valley City has an
existing plan for Fairbourne Station, and these revisions address the remaining three. The revisions
incorporated feedback received from previous Planning Commission meetings and City Council
meetings. The Station Area Plans need to be adopted by the City and certified by WFRC by December
31, 2025.

River Trail: The revised plan emphasizes preserving the area's unique character, opting for small-lot
single-family homes and townhomes at the Roots High Farm site instead of high-density residential.
Flex industrial was removed from riverside locations but remains elsewhere. Key improvements include
adding street connections and improving bike and pedestrian links to the Jordan River Trail along the
Jordan River.
Redwood Junction: The plan maintains Redwood Road as a commercial corridor but would convert
underutilized parking with new flex developments. East of Redwood Road small-lot single-family
homes are proposed to protect existing neighborhoods. On the Redwood Elementary site, contingent on
relocation, townhomes with retail are proposed. In addition, the plan proposes street grid connectivity
and adding bike/pedestrian paths throughout the area.

Decker Lake: The plan focuses on creating an entertainment district centered around the Maverick
Center. Revisions include converting existing parking lots into structured parking, adding mixed-use
developments with retail and residential components, a hotel, and improving street connections and
bike/pedestrian paths. Commissioner Drozdek suggested implementing free-fare transit zones and
improving pedestrian accessibility between Decker Lake and the mall area, as well as adding
educational opportunities, such as a Salt Lake Community College presence, and extending business
hours in the district. Chair Lovato expressed a desire for the area to be revitalized beyond event days.


2) ZT-5-2025 Petition by West Valley City requesting to amend Sections 7-1-103, 7-6-203, 7-9-114,
7-11-200P of the West Valley City Municipal Code to modify surfacing standards as well as clarify
and consolidate the residential landscape standards. Staff – Jody Knapp at 801-963-3497 or
jody.knapp@wvc-ut.gov.

Discussion: Jody Knapp presented proposed amendments to the landscaping code specifically for
residential properties. The proposed changes aim to clarify issues related to driveway paving and
decorative dirt yards, which contribute to stormwater runoff. The proposed amendments include:
    • 7-9-114: Increasing the maximum allowable driveway coverage to 50% of the front yard area,
       with a potential increase to 60% for properties on cul-de-sacs.
    • 7-11-205: Reducing the minimum live plant material requirement from 30% to 25% of the front
       yard area.
    • 7-1-103: Removing “decorative dirt” as an allowable landscaping material.

Jody indicated that these proposed changes are designed to maintain the enforceability of the code and
ensure compatibility with other existing code sections. The Planning Commission will consider these
proposals to be recommended to City Council.

3) Planning Commission Business: Procedure Training

Brandon Hill, the Assistant City Attorney, conducted a training on procedural guidelines for Planning
Commission meetings. The Commissioners had questions relating to public input, alternate voting, and
rules of order. Key points that Brandon emphasized were:

   ▪   Have clear intent when making their motions.
   ▪   Ensure fair opportunities for public comment.
   ▪   Prioritizing calm and respectful discussions, even in contentious situations, over strict adherence
       to Robert's Rules of Order.
   ▪   Allowing applicants to respond to public concerns before public comment closes.
   ▪   Reopening public hearings when new information is presented.
