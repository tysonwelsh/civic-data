1. Discussion regarding a Low Impact Permit and Plat Amendment for Park City 
Valet Storage on Lots 8 and 9 of the Park City Business Center – Jennifer Strader, 
County Planner 
 
County Planner Jennifer Strader presented the staff report and explained that the applicant is 
requesting to combine two lots into one parcel to accommodate a 40,000-square-foot warehouse 
intended for RV, boat, and vehicle storage and a ski and snowboard rental and delivery service.  
The zoning is Service Commercial (SC).  She explained that two findings need to be made when 
reviewing a plat amendment—that there will be  no increase in density, and that there is good 
cause for the plat amendment.  In the SC Zone density is determined by the ability of the project 
to meet the applicable development standards, and good cause is determined on a case-by-case 
basis.  As part of the plat amendment, the Planning Commission looks at the site design to see if 
the application meets the requirements, and in this case, they are dealing with a development 
agreement that has specific design guidelines.  Planner Strader reviewed the site layout and 
indicated setback area between the two buildings if individual buildings were constructed on the 
two lots as they currently exist.  She indicated that the setback areas will be covered with the 
building if the lots are combined, which has a domino effect on the rest of the site design and 
architectural standards.  One issue is that most of the site would be asphalt and parking, but the 
design standards state that the parking lot and cars should not be the dominant visual element on 
the site and that large, expansive parking areas should be avoided in favor of smaller multiple 
lots.  The design standards also talk about a rural mountain style of architecture, but this building 
appears to be a large warehouse structure with a large footprint.  She noted that the roof will be 
flat and highly visible from Highway 40, but the design guidelines state that flat roofs will be

the applicant can bring it out and have it ready to hook up when the person gets there.  He 
explained that the pavement area would be their staging area.

2. Legislative Update – Jami Brackin, Deputy County Attorney 
 
Deputy County Attorney Jami Brackin explained that the Planning Commission asked her in 
December to provide an update of the legislative session.  She provided a packet of information 
listing potential land use and planning bills and recommendations made by the Land Use Task 
Force (LUTF) which have not yet found a sponsor.  She described the makeup of the LUTF and 
explained that it is sponsored by the League of Cities of Towns and is comprised primarily of 
representatives from municipalities.  She is basically the only county representative on the Task 
Force.  She explained that they work hard to come up with legislation consensus bills that make 
sense in terms of policy.  She explained that the infrastructure improvements bill was a request 
of the property rights coalition representatives.  It would identify what they can and cannot bond 
for as part of a development improvement agreement, and it takes away the 10% contingency 
Summit County builds into its DIAs, so the 10% will have to be built into the actual costs.  They 
also included a dispute resolution process if there is a discrepancy in what the parties to the 
agreement believe the costs are.  The bill does not yet have a sponsor, but she believed the 
property rights coalition would work hard to get a sponsor. 
 
HB0042 was a request from Cedar City to allow peninsulas and islands to be annexed into cities 
if they are within the city’s annexation declaration area.  Members of the Task Force feel it is 
bad policy, and a compromise bill is proposed that will allow the annexation of peninsulas and 
islands if both the city and the county involved agree and there is an agreement to provide 
services.  The counties would have veto power, but this may not be what the bill will look like 
when it is adopted, and she was concerned about the consequences that could result, especially in 
Summit County.  She stated that the Utah Association of Counties (UAC) has taken a position in 
opposition to this bill and is adamant that the bill not change from its current status of counties 
having veto power.

SB0029 did not come to the LUTF, but it is helpful, because it would require schools that want 
to purchase new school sites to come to the Planning Commission for input before they are 
allowed to build.  The Planning Commission’s power would be limited, but they would have 
more power than they have now. 
 
Ms. Brackin explained that the LUTF utilities exemption would ask that utilities be able to divide 
off a small parcel to put in a pump station or other structure without violating the zoning and 
having to go through a lot of hoops to divide off the parcel.  It would allow them to subdivide at 
less than the minimum zoning for public utility purposes.  The LUTF request regarding third-
party exactions is a request of the property rights coalition, because exactions were being 
imposed for people other than the government entity.  Unless the entity that approves the 
exaction will use the exaction, they cannot impose it.  The exaction has to be related to the 
impacts of the development and roughly proportional to the impacts.  Another LUTF proposal  
by Salt Lake County relates to zoning districts and landscaping.  She recalled that last year 
Representative Mel Brown filed a last-minute bill that does not allow government entities to 
demand that people who live in single-family properties restore or landscape their property if 
they dig it up.  Salt Lake County wants to propose legislation that says Representative Brown’s 
bill does not apply to first-class counties, and Salt Lake County is the only first-class county.  
She asked Salt Lake County to either get rid of it for all the counties or to repeal the bill.  She 
was not sure if this bill would be able to get a sponsor. 
 
SB0068 is this year’s version of the eminent domain amendments and only addresses giving first 
rights to the original owner if a government entity condemns property and ends up not using it. 
 
Ms. Brackin stated that there is a boxcar bill regarding municipal and county enforcement, and 
she is waiting to see what it will look like.  She explained that a boxcar bill opens a file and gives 
general information about the subject matter, but no other information, which is done to stave off 
opposition until the last minute.  There is also a boxcar bill regarding building code amendments, 
and she has no information about that.
