Study Session

      Discussion: Short-Term Rentals
      Todd Taylor, Planner, reported that staff had been directed to develop
      proposed standards for short-term rentals. He presented a draft of the
      process and standards created by staff after reviewing practices implemented

     Jennifer Jastremsky, responded to questions from the Council.

     The Council discussed potential parking and occupancy standards. Mr. Taylor
     spoke of linking parking requirements to occupancy, and providing a matrix
     based on size of bedrooms to determine maximum occupancy. He said some

     Report: Major Road Capital Improvement Projects
     City Engineer and Public Works Director Scott Cooley updated the Council on
     major road capital improvement projects in 2025. He explained difficulties
     with Federal grant funding for projects due to the rising cost of materials, and
     said staff suggested asking the Wasatch Front Regional Council (WFRC) for the
     ability to combine Federal funding granted for two projects to just one project,
     so that one project would be Federally funded and one would not. Mr. Cooley
     noted that it was uncertain whether the WFRC would approve the request.

     The remainder of the Study Session agenda was presented following the
     conclusion of the Business Session agenda.

     interests at the Capitol.

6.a   Public Hearing: Ordinance #1639 amending the text of the Land Use and
      Development Code of the Draper City Municipal Code relating to
      deviations for government buildings
      Community Development Director Jennifer Jastremsky presented proposed
      amendments to the Land Use and Development Code. She said the goal of the
      proposed text was to create exceptions in the Code for government buildings.

      Mayor Walker opened a public hearing, and closed the public hearing
      seeing no one come forward.

      Responding to a question from Councilmember Vawdrey, Ms. Jastremsky said
      the proposed amendments would allow government buildings, such as
      schools, more flexibility with height. City Manager Mike Barker said staff were
      interested in proposing amendments regarding City buildings and establishing

      Report: Parks Capital Improvement Projects
      Parks and Recreation Director Rhett Ogden provided an update on parks
      capital improvement projects, and answered questions from the Council. The
      Council requested additional Study Session discussion regarding the Phebe
      Brown Trail.

      Discussion: Verizon Wireless Cell Tower at Southfork Park
      Assistant City Manager Kellie Challburg presented options for a proposed
      Verizon cell tower at Southfork Park. Ms. Challburg stated that Verizon
      proposed to pay the City a monthly rent of $2,800 ($33,600 annually with a
      2% annual increase) with a 5-year lease term and options to renew.
      Councilmember Johnson said the proposed coverage area would not reach a
      known dead spot, and said she was interested in seeing the analysis behind
      the proposed location. Mayor Walker said he preferred the water tower
      option over the silo option, and suggested putting the city logo on the side. A
      majority of the Council indicated support for the 80-foot water tower option.

     Council/Manager Reports
     City Manager Mike Barker reported that the proposed cost for Salt Lake
     County to administer the 2025 municipal election was approximately double
     the cost incurred in 2023. City Recorder Nicole Smedley explained that the
     increased cost was presented as a range, depending on the number of
     municipalities participating in the traditional election method versus those
     opting for ranked choice voting, as well as the number of races requiring a
     primary election. She noted that several municipalities had already opted to
     use ranked choice voting.
