3. Text Amendment — Tree Canopies; Amend the 2009 West Jordan City Code Sections 13-

13-10 and 13-18-10 regarding tree canopy height requirements; City-wide applicability;
City of West Jordan (applicant)

[A discussion by the Commission and staff was held on this subject during the pre-meeting. The

following points were made:

e The amendment is at the request of City Council after receiving complaints from residents receiving

notices of violation.

e The tallest city-owned vehicle is the police command center at 15 feet. The tallest fire truck is

thirteen feet.

e Tree branches can become even lower when laden with precipitation.

e Most vehicle damage occurs at the eight to ten-foot mark.

e Most violations of this code are sent for enforcement as city crews work on road projects.

e The Commission did not think that one foot justifies a text amendment.

Larry Gardner explained that the ordinance amendment will change the minimum tree canopy height

from 15 feet to 14 feet.

Alan Anderson responded to the questions that were raised in the pre-meeting. He said in the minutes

from the December 21, 2022 Committee of the Whole meeting, Council Members Worthen, Bloom,

Pack, Whitelock, and Green expressed support of the amendment and Council Member McConnehey

expressed opposition because he preferred the 15-foot UDOT clearance standard. When the City

Attorney suggested that the measurement applied to any portion of the pavement, there was no

objection from the Council to include that change for clarity.

George Winn asked Mr. Anderson to explain what problems would be solved by amending the code.

Alan Anderson explained that two residents brought the issue to the City Council as a legislative body

who oversees the code, and at least five council members supported the change. The amendment still

provides opportunities for code enforcement and gives opportunities for compliance. The city could
promote the types of trees that are more columnar in shape, which don’t need trimming. It was not
known how much lower than 14 feet the trees were of those who complained.

Duncan Murray referred to an informal study that showed West Jordan was on the high end of the scale

and that 14 feet was more of a uniform standard.

Ammon Allen opened the public hearing.

Paul Brockbank, West Jordan Fire Marshal, said that the code has a provision that says any canopies or

overhangs, which trees are included, cannot be any lower than 13 feet 6 inches, which is in line with the

proposed amendment. The standard was much lower when he started at the city and vehicle damage and
scratches were common. He was glad that the amendment would remain taller than the fire apparatus.
Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

Ammon Allen thought the amendment was silly and that the height should remain at 15 feet, because

this standard has been working for us. The tallest piece of city equipment is 15 feet and with potential

weight from rain and snow the trees could easily get to the 13 % foot level.

Matt Quinney agreed with Commissioner Allen. He referred to Criteria 2 and said he did not feel there

was sufficient justification, mostly due to Criteria 4 not being met because the modification is based on

the request of two individuals who received citations.

Duncan Murray said it is good that the Commission is using the criteria in their deliberation. If the

Commission still wants to make a recommendation to insert clarifying language, he recommended

using ‘gravitationally vertical’ regarding how to measure. He also recommended that they keep the

recommended changes as to the street surface even if they choose to not recommend a change in height.

Trish Hatch said the amendment seems very counterintuitive and it is not needed.

Jay Thomas agreed that the height should remain at 15 feet, but he would like the clarifying language to

go forward.

Scott Langford said he had attended many of the council discussions on this matter. He pointed out on

the exhibit that by moving the minimum height up to fifteen feet it would clip more branches. By

lowering the minimum height, it reduces the number of potential violations and fewer complaints.

Matt Quinney said on the other side of that, if two people complain about the speed limit and the limit

is raised, there are fewer speeding tickets but that doesn’t make it a better situation. He did not like the

idea of lowering our expectation just to make people happy.

MOTION: Matt Quinney moved to forward a positive recommendation to the City Council for
the Text Amendment to Sections 13-13-10 and 13-18-10 regarding tree canopies
except leaving the height at 15 feet and inserting ‘gravitationally vertical’ after 15
feet. The motion was seconded by Jay Thomas and passed 6-0 in favor.

MOTION: Matt Quinney moved to adjourn.
