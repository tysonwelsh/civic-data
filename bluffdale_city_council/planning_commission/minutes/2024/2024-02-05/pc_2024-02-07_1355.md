4.     CONSIDERATION AND VOTE on Proposed Covent Garden Amended Plat to
       Eliminate a Lot Line for Two Units located in the Heavy Commercial Zone at 14655
       and 14659 South 855 West, Jerris Koplin, Applicant, (Application 2024-02) – Staff
       Presenter, Jennifer Robison.

Senior City Planner, Jennifer Robison, presented the Staff Report and stated that the Planning
Commission is the Land Use Authority that approves plat amendments. The property location was
identified on a map displayed. The two units are on the north end and are occupied by Kopsen
Property Management, LLC who owns both units at 14655 and 14659 South 855 West. In August
of 2021, the plat was recorded for all of the condominium units. Because it is a condominium plat,
there is some common open space that is managed by a Condominium Association that has its own
Bylaws. The applicant, Jerris Koplin, was required to get signatures from other owners in the
building to make the change. Staff will ensure that the plat is signed by the responsible parties.

Mrs. Robison identified the two units on a rendering and stated that the intent is to eliminate the lot
line between the two. The applicant has indicated that it is an ownership and financial decision for
loan purposes. He owns both units but they need to be combined to obtain financing. The final plat
will show the two units as one property. Staff spoke to the Surveyor earlier in the day and will
work with the County to determine which is the proper address to use. It will most likely be
assigned the address of one of the two units. The proposal does not affect the building itself and
simply combines the two parcels. It was noted that no alterations are needed to link the two.

In response to a question raised by Commissioner Flynn, Mrs. Robison stated that the developers
constructed the shells of the buildings and each of the tenants made their own improvements.
Mr. Koplin gave his address as 14655 South 855 East in Bluffdale and stated that the units were
already separated when they purchased the property.

Commissioner Luker moved to APPROVE the proposed Covent Garden Plat Amendment

5.     Training on Open and Public Meetings Act – Fred Donaldson, City Attorney.

City Attorney, Fred Donaldson, presented the Training on the Open and Public Meeting Act and
requested that staff forward the presentation to the Commissioners after the meeting. The intention
of the State Legislature is that political subdivisions take action and conduct deliberations openly.
Meetings of a public body are to be open to the public unless they are properly closed. Meetings
can only be closed for specific reasons.

A meeting is defined as the meeting of a public body with a quorum present. It includes a
workshop or executive session and is to discuss and receive comments from the public and act on
any matter over which the public body has jurisdiction or advisory power. Meetings can be in
person, electronic, or hybrid and do not include a chance meeting or social gathering, even if a
quorum is present.

A public body includes an advisory, executive, or legislative body that was created by the
Constitution, statute, rule, ordinance, or resolution, has two or more members, spends, distributes,
or is supported by taxpayer dollars, and has the authority to make decisions about the public's
business. Some public bodies in the City include the City Council, the Planning Commission, the
Redevelopment Agency (“RDA”), and others. There must be 24 hours’ notice of a public meeting
and the notice must include the agenda, date, time, and place of the meeting. It needs to be
reasonably specific. Matters that are not found on the agenda can be discussed in the meeting, but
they cannot be put to a final vote. Notice is posted at City Hall, on the Utah Public Notice Website,
and the City’s website.

Emergency meetings of the Planning Commission are exceedingly rare. There are written minutes
kept and meetings are recorded. The City has a transcriptionist who produces minutes from the
recording. The minutes must be detailed and include the names of members present, and absent, the
substance of the matters discussed and decided, specify who is speaking, and other necessary
information. The written minutes are the official record and the court does not look at anything else
unless it is ambiguous in the written minutes. The minutes requirement can be satisfied by having a
link to the recording of the matters that are discussed. It is classified as public. Open meetings
must be recorded completely unedited. It can also be recorded by members of the public. The
recording is a public record and must be made available on the Utah Public Notice website and the
City website within three days of holding a meeting.

Electronic meetings are authorized and the notice requirements still apply. There must be an anchor
location. Typically City Hall would be the anchor location except during an emergency when a
meeting may be held electronically only. Members of the public must have a means to attend and
participate in the meeting even if it is held electronically. Members of the public body should not
transmit electronic messages to each other during an open meeting. It was stressed that decisions
and deliberations must be made in the open. A person can be removed if they are disruptive. In
response to a question raised, Mr. Donaldson reported that the City has adopted in its Personnel
Policies and Procedures Manual some Conduct Rules. They specify that a member should not text
other participants in the meeting even if they are not members of the public body.

Mr. Donaldson reported that meetings are closed only for specific reasons and are not legally
required to be closed. It is left up to the discretion of the public body. To close a public meeting, a
quorum must be present and two-thirds of the members present must vote to go into a Closed
Meeting. In a City Council Meeting, there are five members and the Mayor, who votes in the case
of a tie. In that case, they must have four present to vote to close a meeting. The public meeting
must first be opened and then closed. Reasons for conducting a Closed Meeting must be stated as
well as the location and the vote by name of each member for or against entering into the Closed
Meeting. Closed Meetings are recorded except for specific situations. Some of the reasons for
closing a meeting include the following:

   •   To discuss the character, professional competence, or physical or mental health of a person;
   •   Collective bargaining;
   •   Litigation;
   •   Security personnel, devices, or systems;
   •   Investigative proceedings regarding allegations of criminal misconduct;
   •   Issues related to procurements and trade secrets;
   •   A loan application if the discussion would disclose certain information; and
   •   For the City Council to discuss the sale of real property, water rights, and shares if the
       discussion would disclose the appraisal or value of the property.

During a Closed Meeting, the public body may NOT approve the following:

   •   An ordinance, resolution, rule, regulation, contract, or appointment;
   •   Interview a person to fill an elected position;
   •   Discuss the professional competence or physical or mental health of an applicant to fill a
       mid-term vacancy; and
   •   Take final action on a matter.


Violations are criminal and can also be enforced by members of the public. A violation can be
cured in a subsequent meeting, however. It can be enforced again by the Attorney General, the
County Attorney, or a private citizen.

Commissioner Flynn asked if Robert's Rules of Order apply to City meetings. Mr. Donaldson
stated that the City Code specifies that Robert’s Rules of Order and Parliamentary Procedures are to
be used as a guide. In City Council Meetings public comments are timed. The same can be done
by the Planning Commission.

Community and Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell, suggested that open public
comment be added back onto Planning Commission agendas. Mr. Donaldson commented that the
City Council has a public comment period set aside for matters that are not scheduled for public
hearing. It allows citizens to make their voices heard. With the support of the Commission,
Mr. Crowell agreed to add it in right after approval of the minutes. Mr. Donaldson recommended
that the Chair who opens the public comment make an introduction and specify that comments will
not be taken on items that are to be later in the meeting as public hearing items. The public should
also be asked to keep their comments brief, on topic, and limited to three minutes. The intent was
not to use a timer unless necessary. Mr. Donaldson stated that it may be useful in situations where
they are likely to receive a lot of public comment.
