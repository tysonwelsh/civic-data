Proposed Resolution 2025–6 determining the adequacy of consideration to
be received under a lease agreement with MWCC NMTC SPE, Inc., a Utah
nonprofit corporation, as required by Utah Code 10–8–2 and Ogden
Municipal Code Section 4–3A–5, to facilitate a new market tax credit
transaction that will provide additional funding for the Marshall White
Community Center reconstruction and programming
         A memo from the Management Services Department regarding a proposed resolution authorizing the lease of the
Marshall N. White Community Center to MWCC NMTC SPE, Inc. came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated
the resolution that outlines public policy considerations that justify a no-cost lease of the Marshall N. White Community Center
(MWCC) to MWCC NMTC SPE, Inc, a special purpose entity created to facilitate the New Markets Tax Credit (NMTC)
transactions. The Administration is proposing that the City take advantage of the federal New Markets Tax Credit Program to
receive approximately $2.1 Million in investor funding. This funding will be used to complete elements of the Marshall White
Community Center project that were removed from the scope of work due to budgetary constraints. Use of the NMTCs for the
MWCC project requires a series of complex business transactions among multiple parties. Adoption of the proposed resolution is
one aspect of the process.
          Cody Rogers, an attorney from Stinson LLP, summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
provide background information regarding the NMTC program. He presented a diagram to provide an overview of the structure of
the NMTC Program, which highlighted the role of private investors, Ogden City, and the Municipal Building Authority in the
Program. Through the proposed resolution and lease agreement, the City will receive $2.1 million in private investor funding that
will be used to complete elements of the Marshall N. White Community Center construction plans that were removed from the
scope of work due to budgetary constraints—namely lighting for outdoor pickleball courts, fencing, landscaping, and pool and
weight equipment. The City is working with the Community Development Finance Alliance (CDFA), a Utah nonprofit
organization and a qualified Community Development Entity (CDE). CDFA was awarded $40 Million in federal funding for
investment in qualifying projects. CDFA has invested $30 Million--$11.7 Million in tax credits-- in other projects. CDFA is
seeking to invest the remaining $10 Million in the Marshall N. White Community Center project. CDFA and its investors are
using a Leverage Model for the transaction on the MWCC. For the NMTC process to move forward, the City Council must find
that the public policy factors associated with the no-cost lease to MWCC NMTC SPE, Inc. provide adequate consideration to
justify the conveyance. The public policy factors served by the lease include fostering a sense of belonging, providing educational
and recreational opportunities, promoting health and wellness, and supporting economic development. Community centers
provide a space to gather, interact, build relationships, and create a sense of community, all crucial components in combating
social isolation.

Proposed Joint Resolution 2025–1 of the Ogden City Council and Mayor
approving the naming of Nathan James Lyday Fieldhouse
          A memo from the Public Services Department regarding the proposed name for the Marshall N. White Community
Center (MWCC) Fieldhouse came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Administration is recommending that
the fieldhouse located within the new MWCC be named the “Nathan James Lyday Fieldhouse” in honor of Officer Lyday’s
sacrifice for and devotion to his community. Nate’s family has given their approval for this naming and Ronald White, son of the
late Marshall N. White, is also very supportive. The Administration believes that naming the fieldhouse in honor of Officer Lyday
will serve not only as a tribute to him, but also as a powerful reminder of the bravery and dedication of all the City’s first
responders.
          Recreation Manager Bridge summarized the memo and stated Nate was a life-long Ogden resident. He was born in
Ogden, attended Taylor Elementary, Mound Fort Jr. High, and graduated from Ogden High School. He graduated Summa Cum
Laude from Weber State University with a bachelor’s degree in criminal justice. Nate fulfilled his lifelong dream when he was
sworn in as an Ogden City police officer on May 28, 2019.
          Ronald White also addressed the Council; he expressed his support for naming the Fieldhouse for Officer Lyday. He
considers Officer Lyday to be family, and he is also part of a unique group of people who have sacrificed their life in the line of
duty; these heroes should be placed on a pedestal as a role model for the community. Now is the time to work to unite the
community and he hopes naming the Fieldhouse for Officer Lyday will be a signal to the community that police officers can be
viewed as role models and mentors for the young people in Ogden.
          The Council discussed past actions in the community to name City facilities for individuals; they also clarified that the
broader facility will still be called the Marshall N. White Community Center, but that the fieldhouse amenity within the Center
will be named for Officer Lyday.
           Chair White introduced in writing proposed Joint Resolution 2025-1, entitled:
                   “A joint resolution of the Ogden City Council and Mayor approving the naming of Nathan James Lyday
                   Fieldhouse.”
          Chair White then called for public input regarding the proposal.

White’s son regarding his support for naming the Fieldhouse for Officer Lyday. It was touching to hear Mr. White refer to Officer

community they serve. She stated she is currently in Washington, D.C. and can see the Martin Luther King, Jr. and Abraham
Lincoln memorials from her current location; these are two men from different backgrounds and races, and different times in the
nation’s history. They both were willing to die for different things and she sees a similar parallel between Marshall N. White and
Nate Lyday, both of which were willing to give their life for the safety and freedom of the community they served. They both
called Ogden home and loved their community. She thinks the proposal to name the Fieldhouse for Officer Lyday is beautiful and

situation, which he feels is a reflection of the City’s outdated land use policies. He drives by the Adam & Eve business daily and

that allowed for the business to be permitted in its current location; he does not think it is appropriate and does not want similar
