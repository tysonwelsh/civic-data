3.5) Public hearing and consideration of Mammoth Holdings request for approval of the
Wiggy Wash Concept Plan located at 26 South 2300 West, requesting exceptions to the
Development Code.
Jacob Curtis presented information on the agenda item.

The applicant, Bret Wignall, was present. The wash and subdivision approval will be put in
simultaneously. They are divided into 4 parcels total that total 4 acres. This is zoned commercial.
Mr. Wiggins is asking for a setback due to an ordinance change from Main Street and the
landscaping buffer requirement. Kim Struthers clarified there is a maximum setback that is set.

Commission Chair Jackson opened the public hearing.

Jacob Curtis acknowledged the online comments.

Jared Hansen is concerned about the noise and traffic as well as the amount of car washes that are
already available.

Katelyn Kinney mentioned she resided in Lehi and noted that the area constituted a busy
intersection. The congestion near that intersection was already pronounced and raised concerns.
Within a three-mile radius, there were six car washes. Despite Lehi's incentives to become a more
walkable community and enhance its overall quality, it appeared not to be serving the interests of
Lehi or its citizens. Kately Kinney also appeared at the meeting to give public comment and wanted
to clarify that 2300 West to Main Street is congested and adding a turn will cause concern.

Kirk Evans wanted to echo Katelyn Kinney’s comments and the traffic congestion that will be
certain to arise with another car wash being built. He stated 2300 West and Main Street is a
complete nightmare, especially during rush hour traffic in the morning and the evening. The high
density will not help the situation.

Commission Chair Jackson closed the public hearing.

The applicant, Bret Wignall, addressed the public comments that were heard.

Commissioner Jackson wished to emphasize that there were established zones and a general plan
dictating future zoning changes, which governed property management. He observed that
comments were focused on the number of car washes in Lehi but stressed that it was within the
rights of private property owners, who assume the risk by adhering to property zones. The car
wash in question does comply with the zoning outlined in the general plan.

        Motion: Commissioner Eyre moved to grant approval of Mammoth Holdings request
        for approval of the Wiggy Wash Concept Plan located at 26 South 2300 West,
        requesting exceptions to the Development Code; including the exception of the setback
        and drive aisles, all DRC comments and findings stated in the report. Commissioner Kunze
        seconded.

        Vote: Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Everett, yes; Commissioner Eyre, yes;
        Commissioner Lockhart, yes; Commissioner Kunze, yes.

3.7) Public Hearing and consideration of Ivory Innovation’s request for approval of a plat
amendment and revised architectural elevations to the Innovation Park subdivision located
at approximately 2700 North Redwood Road.
Jacob Curtis presented information on the agenda item.

The applicant representing Ivory Homes was present. They had decided to revise the design to
create a more consistent product and cost for the customer. The original design included the offset,
but the new design delayed the process and was not efficient for building townhomes. They opted
for straight lines for the interior to optimize the space. The price point was for rent because there
was no profit involved. They needed to be able to explain to the IRS that this property was viable
and sustainable. The City code increased construction costs.
Commission Chair Jackson opened and closed the public hearing.
     Motion: Commissioner Lockhart moved to grant approval of Ivory Innovation’s
     request for approval of a plat amendment and revised architectural elevations to the
     Innovation Park subdivision located at approximately 2700 North Redwood Road;
     including all DRC comments and proposed revisions, with findings that excepted the
     stucco and balcony, as they met the development code. These findings were tied to the
     exceptions being considered due to the affordability housing crisis and the nonprofit
     nature of the project. Commissioner Lockhart seconded.

        Vote: Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Everett, yes; Commissioner Eyre, yes;
        Commissioner Lockhart, yes; Commissioner Kunze, yes.
