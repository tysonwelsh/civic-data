1. Discussion of 30553, Adoption of the RM Residential Multifamily Zone (discussion

only)

John Janson said the ordinance has been through the Planning Commission. He went through
the provided staff report. He mentioned that they have deleted some of the typical uses, like
an airport, and the height previously in the zone was 75 ft. and it has been reduced to 40 ft.
The Mayor asked him to discuss the background for the parking regulations. Janson brought
up a chart in the ordinance (pg. 10 of 19 on the staff report). The chart shows the addition of
parking standards for studio units. The Mayor said the City has received several applications
for multi-residential developments and there have been questions about the parking being
adequate. The main concern was that if the housing has more rooms per unit, should it also
have more parking. There was conversation that there are more people and families that live
in these units now than there used to be. J anson said it is an increase in parking requirements,
but he believes it is reasonable.

Janson said the mixed-use nature of the ordinance was retained because there are currently a
lot of businesses in this zone,

The Mayor asked about how many single-family dwellings there are in the RM Zone now.
Janson said very few, and most of the properties that are vacant in the zone are less than one
acre.

The Mayor asked if there were discussions about height in the potential town centers. Janson
said the footnotes say that a greater height could be done with a development agreement
(table 10.30-2).

Janson said there is a new section about development requirements. He said one of the issues
that came up is open space with larger parcels versus smaller ones; to reduce the amount of
open space required for smaller parcels. He went over the chart allowing for residential
parking reductions for bicycle areas, being close to transit, and for senior housing. He said
the exterior requirements list better materials, fencing requirements, landscaping, lot lighting,
and signs. Amenities are now required onsite, going with the county policy, which includes
adding a leisure outdoor space requirement. The Mayor said there was discussion with the
Planning Commission about giving the developer a bonus for making an amenity larger than
is required, but that is not being proposed.

Janson said some questions were asked about the difference between a duplex and a twin

home, and it comes down to ownership. He talked about rear yard requirements going to 20
ft. Garages are required to include 220 power for electric vehicles. Major streets in the City
are 66 ft. or wider, but staff doesn’t want cars backing on to the street so properties fronting
on them will need k-shaped or circular driveways. Yard lights are required. There cannot be

any buildings longer than 200 ft. The Mayor mentioned that having an onsite manager with
larger properties would help the City with code enforcement issues. This would be required
for projects that have more than 20 units. The Mayor questioned the procedural process with
the ordinance. Janson said it has been to the community councils and Planning Commission,
except for the portion on the studio parking ratio as previously mentioned and that residential
facilities for handicapped persons should be a permitted use. Mayor Silvestrini asked about
group homes in residential zones. John Brems said they must be allowed in residential zones,
He then said the Council can amend the ordinance to make these changes without the

Council Member Marchant asked if the Planning Commission was unanimous in their

He said there was debate about having the applicant provide it instead of having a third-party,
unbiased study done. Mayor Silvestrini asked if there would be an evaluation of the parking
study. Francis Lilly said it is unclear in the ordinance what action to take if the staff disagrees
with the findings of the parking study. The Mayor gave context to this concern with a current
development that provided a study that was based off of Salt Lake City standards, not
Millcreek code. There was back and forth discussion about finding a remedy to the situation.

The direction for this item by the Council is to come back on a future agenda.

2. Discussion of 30459, Adoption of the M - Manufacturing Zone (discussion only)

The Mayor prefaced the discussion to the public, that the zone discussion was noticed
properly and is to see if staff incorporated public input into the ordinance.

Janson went through the alternate language in the ordinance outlined in red; some changes
that he mentioned are with definitions, and the list of uses was removed. Mayor Silvestrini
said he likes to have broad definitions instead of trying to list all activities. Janson said
recreational vehicle campgrounds are only allowed west of State St. The Mayor doesn’t think
there should be a 40 ft. limitation on height. Francis Lilly said the mixed-use zone height is
75 ft. Council Member Marchant thinks the City should accommodate existing 80 ft.
structures. The Council expressed that 80 ft. was an appropriate height.

Mayor Silvestrini asked about the workability of 4. (16.66.050), Janson iss unsure about the
practicality of it. Janson talked about the design standards with a minimum window
percentage on the first floor of street-facing sides. Council Member Uipi asked if the change
came from comments about vandalism. Janson said it came from letters. Mayor Silvestrini
asked what the goal of the window revision is. It was decided that 5. and 6. in that section

3. Discussion of Crosswalk Flag Program

Mayor Silvestrini said some residents have contacted him about doing something to help
pedestrians on dangerous intersections (Richmond St. and Elgin Ave.). Geilmann said he had a
meeting with the County about their crosswalk flag program. He said it works by having a
sponsor takes on the crosswalk, and if the flags fall apart, the sponsors notify the County. He
thinks the City needs to recruit a sponsor for the crosswalk. Council Member Jackson asked who
pays for the flags. Geilmann said either the City or the sponsor. The Mayor thinks the City
should provide the flags and flag holders, but it is a great idea to have a sponsor. Council
Member Jackson asked if corporations could sponsor it; yes. She asked if the current flag stations
are sponsored, for instance on the roundabout on Heritage Way. Rita Lund said they have been in
the past but some need to be renewed. Council Member Uipi asked if staff could map out where
these crosswalks are and where they need to be. John Miller suggested that there should be a
policy of when these crosswalks are allowed and stated that the County has an existing policy.
The Council expressed that they would like to review it. Council Member Uipi would like to
look at this program as a city-wide initiative instead of piece-milling as they are needed. Miller
said these give a false sense of security on high speed roads (35+ mph). The Mayor would like to
review the County policy and the City’s current locations before Starting anything.

4. Discussion of City Credit Cards

Geilmann talked with the HR-Finance Coordinator and Treasurer, and they have found that the
City should change their credit card system. He said there are currently 2 credit cards for staff,
but he would like one in the recorder’s office, the planning department, the community relations
department, and have one for administration. Council Member Uipi asked if new departments
would get a card as the City grew; Geilmann said yes. The Council gave direction to staff to
move forward with the proposal.

4.1 Discussion and Consideration of RES 17-80, A Resolution Approving an
Interlocal Cooperative Agreement between Salt Lake County and Millcreek with
Respect to Transfer of up to $1,200,0000 of County Transportation Funds to
Millcreek for the 3900 South Project

The Mayor prefaced the discussion by stating that this tax is called the quarter of the
quarter, and that citizens of Utah authorized it. The money comes through the
Legislature to the County. Cities are required to make an application to the County
for the funds for projects that repair roads of regional significance. 3900 S. is in bad
repair. As mentioned before, the Mayor said he is working with all cities that touch
this road, which includes West Valley, Taylorsville, South Salt Lake, Millcreek and
Holladay. The Council decided to repair the section between 700 W. and 700 E. by
doing an overlay and some sidewalk work through a grant. This agreement is to be
able to get those monies from the County.

John Miller said this is $1.2 of 47 million dollars. The City is putting in for a grant to
the Wasatch Front Regional Council to repair the full section of 3900 S. If the
Regional Council grants the request, then these funds will be used as the City’s
match. If it’s not, then it will be used for the section between 700 E. and 700 W.
Millcreek has met with Holladay and there will be agreements in the near future for
repairs, but South Salt Lake is able to participate in funding for this section now.

Council Member Jackson moved to approve the Interlocal Cooperative Agreement,
Resolution 17-82, between Salt Lake County and Millcreek with Respect to Transfer of up
to $1.2 million of County Transportation Funds to Millcreek for the 3900 South Project.
Council Member Catten seconded. Mayor Silvestrini called for the vote. The motion
passed unanimously.

4.2 Discussion and Consideration of RES 17-81, A Resolution Approving an Interlocal
Cooperative Agreement between the Metropolitan Water District of Salt Lake & Sandy
and Millcreek for Non-District Use of District Lands and Interest in Lands

Mayor Silvestrini said this agreement is to permit the improvement of the piping that leads
from the terminal reservoir to the Salt Lake City system. It will benefit Millcreek residents by
improving water delivery to them, it also benefits Salt Lake City and the Metropolitan Water
District, which owns the terminal reservoir and the aqueduct. The agreement gives the
District permission to use Millcreek’s right-of-way’s and streets to improve the piping.

John Miller said there is currently a failing storm drain that crosses the aqueduct and it is part
of the 3 storm drain projects that the Council identified as needing to be worked on. The
agreement also states that if the District needs to do repairs on their line, that the storm drain
may be destroyed. The agreement protects the District in the event it gets damaged. Council
Member Marchant asked if there is a cost that the Council should be aware of with this
project. Miller said there is no cost, however the expense to repair the storm drain would be
on the City.

Council Member Uipi moved to approve item 4.2. Council Member Jackson seconded.
Mayor Silvestrini called for the vote. The motion passed unanimously.
