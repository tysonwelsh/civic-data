WORK SESSION
Chair Jan Bradshaw called the meeting to order at 5:33 p.m. The agenda items were reviewed and
discussed.

Planning Manager, Jonathan Teerlink provided an overview of the Kershaw Office Building
matter, which was continued from the previous meeting. The Planning Commission asked the
applicant to come back with a more detailed proposal of what the materials will be for the office
building in the NC Zone. The applicant was proposing a material that was not on the pre-approved
materials list for buildings in the NC zone. An applicant can propose a material that is not on the
list and the Planning Commission can approve it if the applicant can prove that the use of the
material is a major part of the theme of the architecture.

of the opinion that the design and the materials being suggested appeared to be better.

review and reapply for the R-2-10 zone. Staff recommended approval as the R-2 zone is
recommended by the Medium-Density Two-Family Residential Transitional (“MDR-T”) Zone
transitional corridor for 2300 East and 4500 South.

With regard to the Hulton Subdivision request, Mr. Teerlink stated that the applicant, Ron Hilton,
is requesting a two-lot split for his property on Murray-Holladay Road. The front lot will be
slightly smaller than the rear lot but both lots will be serviced by a private driveway. The Technical

The proposed ordinance amendment to Title 13 of the Holladay City Code was next addressed.
Mr. Teerlink stated that the matter is in response to a City Council moratorium that was enacted
to curtail enforcement issues and concerns regarding sport ramps in backyards. The intent was to
establish a moratorium period to give staff time to assess the situation and propose options to the
City Council. The concern was that in Chapter 9 of the City of Holladay Ordinance, the Noise
Ordinance specifies that under sport ramps no division of the Community and Economic
Development Department may allow a sports ramp to be built. The ordinance originated in the
County in 1999 and has not been changed or enforced. The original ordinance came from the Salt
Lake County Health Department and that specific limitation was eliminated in 2012, however, it
remained in the City’s ordinance. The three options put forward by staff were as follows:

       1.      Deregulate sport ramps altogether.

       2.      Provide a definition for “accessory structures” that sport ramps would fall into and
               regulate hours of use.

       3.      Require Conditional Use approval for skateboard ramps to be built in backyards
               and reviewed by the Planning Commission based on a set of standards.

5.     Ordinance Amendment – Definition of Terms – (13.04.040).            Review and
       Recommendation on Proposed Amendments to Title 13 of the Holladay City Code,
       Regarding Temporary Zoning Regulation 2020-15 Relating to the Use and Regulation
       of Accessory Sporting Ramps Structures. File #20-4-04-1

Mr. Teerlink presented the staff report and stated that the proposal from staff was in response to a
request to study noise regulations as they relate to sport ramps in backyards. The City Council
asked staff to study how sport ramps can be regulated in residential zones and City-wide. Staff
referred to the Salt Lake County Health Department regulations and other city ordinances as they
pertain to sport ramps. Staff presented a staff proposal for the Commission to consider and discuss.
The proposal was to amend Section 9.4A dealing with noise regulations that was rolled over from
the County in 1999 when the City incorporated. It included a set of revisions that was established
by the Salt Lake Valley Health Department and addressed nuisance abatement. It addressed
sporting ramps and restricted them within 300 feet of a residential property. The specific ordinance
was stricken in 2012, however, the City of Holladay still has the regulation on the books and there
have been requests to enforce it. In this section, the limitation of sport ramps was eliminated and
a short sentence was added regulating sporting equipment.

Mr. Teerlink explained that someone is allowed to have a sport/skateboard ramp in a backyard and
it can be used between the hours of 8:00 a.m. and 10:00 p.m. The noise regulation goes into effect
at 10:00 p.m. The second portion involves creating a definition for how to regulate skateboard
ramps as an accessory structure that must be built in accordance with the setbacks of the underlying
zone. Previously, there was a definition for accessory buildings but structures such as swimming
pool slides, stake board ramps, faux rock landscape features, or playground features are still
defined as structures and should be required to have a setback from property lines. Staff proposed
a new definition for “accessory structure” being a permanent or semi-permanent detached structure
that is intended to provide ancillary support for a primary or accessory building or land use. When
an accessory structure is attached to the main building or an accessory building it shall be
considered part of the main building and must meet the setback requirement. The third part is that
