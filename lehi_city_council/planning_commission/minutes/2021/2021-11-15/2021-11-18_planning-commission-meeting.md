3.2) Consideration of PODS Enterprises request for conditional use approval of the PODS’
outdoor storage use, located at 5025 West Hudson Way, Building 2. (Tabled from the
October 28th meeting)
Brittney Harris presented information on the agenda item. The Planning Commission and Staff
discussed screening of the PODS.

The applicant, Kyle Hunt, was present. He stated for the POD's to be efficient, they need to be
stacked. He stated they cannot be seen from the public roads.

Craig Chambers, legal, clarified code and asked if they were temporary or permanent. Craig
Chambers stated there was a difference of the product being used as a storage container and the
container being the actual product. Kim Struthers stated the zone allows the POD's as a product.
Commissioner Everett stated the code probably needs to be rewritten for clarification purposes and
consistency. Craig Chambers recommended the motion contain language to include reviewing
clarifying the table of uses.

    Motion: Commissioner Ellis moved to approve PODS Enterprises request for conditional
    use approval of the PODS’ outdoor storage use, located at 5025 West Hudson Way,
    Building 2, with the condition the storage containers can only be stacked three high and must
    be maintained in the truck court, finding that the area is out of view of the public street and
    will be screened on three sides with trees, noting the storage containers are a product of sell;
    including the use does meet code and will not be detrimental or injurious to property in the
    vicinity, will comply with the goals and policies of the Lehi City General Plan, and will not be
    detrimental to adjoining properties; including all DRC comments. Commissioner Ellis also
    included to have Staff review and clarify the table of uses. Commissioner Eyre seconded.

    Vote: Commissioner Ellis, yes; Commissioner Hereth, yes; Commissioner Everett, yes;
    Commissioner Nielsen, yes; Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Eyre, yes;
    Commissioner Newall, yes. The motion passed unanimously.

3.4) Public hearing and recommendation of Slopes Residential’s request for review of the
Thanksgiving Station concept, proposing new land uses for a mixed-used development
including approximately 5,300 new residential units within the Thanksgiving Point Area
Plan located at Executive Parkway and Ashton Boulevard.
Brittney Harris presented information on the agenda item.

Commissioner Eyre asked how many units were currently built. Kim Struthers stated there are no
units of the 109 acres as of right now. The Commissioners stated they would like a total number
of units for the entirety of the Thanksgiving Point area. Kim Struthers clarified how the value of
the units are being measured and counted by the City. Commissioner Ellis asked Kim to explain
Senate Bill 217 which allows for HTRZ in the area. Commissioner Nielsen asked Luke Seegmiller
about the traffic study that Hales Engineering conducted. Luke updated the Commissioners with
UDOTS improvement plan which has been programmed up to the year 2029. Luke discussed the
regional improvement that includes UDOT and stated 2100 North is estimated to be updated
between 2035 – 2040, the North interchange that runs between Traverse Mtn Blvd which would
include an extension of the frontage road system with the timeframe of 2035 – 2040, the Clubhouse
Drive extension, where MAG funding was previously awarded, but not utilized, and the 3600 West
extension to the north which would tie in with the North interchange. Luke asked Chairman
Nielsen to include Hales Engineering study spreadsheet as a threshold base. Commissioner Nielsen
asked if the infrastructure could support the development. Brad Kenison briefed the
Commissioners on the infrastructure and how there can be some difficulties in determining the
requested information without a determinate number. Brad recommended the Commissioners
listen to the developer and the public and forward a number of units to the Council so Staff can
have an accurate number to make better determination. Commissioner Ellis asked how those
improvements are paid for. Brad stated impact fees cover the majority of those improvements.
Commissioner Nielsen asked if the area was TOD. Brittney Harris stated it could be added as a
TOD. Kim Struthers stated he wanted to make it clear there is a draft general plan that has not been
adopted yet where some analysis has been conducted. Kim wanted to make is whatever density is
or is not approved some property is reserved for UTA development.

The applicant, Nathan Ricks with STACK Real Estate and Gardner Real Estate, acting as Slopes
Residential, and Chris Perezo with (inaudible). Nathan and Chris gave a Power Point presentation
on the proposal. Nathan discussed the lack of affordable housing and the transportation issues the
City is facing. Chris discussed the advantages and visions of the concept plan and transportation
benefits of the TOD. McKay Christensen, CEO of Thanksgiving Point, also talked about the
concept and benefit of the development. He expressed the improvement and help it would bring to
Thanksgiving Point. John Bankhead with Gardner Company they would be happy to comply with
recommendations and work in phases.

Commissioner Nielsen opened the public hearing.

Senator Jake Anderegg stated he supports the plan and was not there to discuss specifics of the
plan, but the reality of the growth the City is exhibiting. Several communities have areas that are
feeling the impact and stated transportation investment funds are being spent and studies are being
done. Affordable housing isn't sustainable right now. Home values will not be impacted like they
used to be and the studies that have been completed show so. Senator Anderegg is thrilled with the
needs that will be addressed in five to ten years on how the development will help the community.
Density doesn't mean affordable housing, but when implementable properly, can be beneficial for
affordable housing. He stated there are aspects that are great about the project.

(inaudible) stated he has lived in a TOD before and has seen them work and fail, and the reason
they fail is because there isn't enough schooling or church space. He thinks there needs to be a fire
department and that the development is falling short of its goals.

Jared Asbury stated STACK created the overdevelopment near Thanksgiving Point. He expressed
the certain zoning fails if the development is approved. He feels the development should be driven
by the City, not the development. He stated the amenities aren't available for the development.

Larry LaCroix stated 1,000 units would mean approximately 1,500 vehicles coming into the area.
When 5,400 unit are allowed into the area, approximately 8,000 vehicles are coming into the area.
He stated about 3% of that new population would be riding Trax. He would like to focus on
bringing people into the resort community, instead of driving the people out of the area.

Rudabaugh stated there is no trust with the developer and understands the Commissioners don't
have complete control over that. He stated he understands that we need to build TOD, but we aren't
prepared and are essentially going backwards if the concept is approved. He also stated this is the


Senator that approved HB 82. The biggest problem is trusting people to do it right and have plans
in place.

Jared Watson stated he has an interesting perspective on TOD and where they fail and where they
are successful. Unless you have the transportation infrastructure in place it will be a nightmare
with that amount on density.

Rowland Brown stated he was reading the section of the packet with the traffic study that was
conducted by Hales Engineering. He stated the developer and CEO of Thanksgiving Point need be
held accountable. He stated reality dictates the time of day when he can and cannot come home
and that is not okay.

JoAnn Thomas stated she hoped she is considered as voicing her own opinion and would like to
keep open space and quality of life. She stated at one time the Ashton's had a vision and she feels
they and those around he would like to keep that vision and that would dramatically affect her.

Lorie Gilbert stated five years ago a Skate Park was built in her neighborhood and considers it a
unique feature. Lorie stated Thanksgiving Point is a unique feature and feels if the area is
developed, we will lose what makes Lehi unique.

Cynthia Glover stated when she moved to Thanksgiving Point it was a dairy farm and would
always remain a partial dairy farm, and there is still a little farm, but not what was promised. She
stated this is a business to the developers. She asked the Commissioners to see though the
developers and to listen to the community.

Rachel Asbury wanted to remind the Commissioners there was a vision for the area and the
developer wants to completely undo this vision because it doesn't suite the developer’s vision. She
also wants to bring up that the developer thinks that anyone that lives in this area doesn't drive

McKay Christensen clarified the improvements they plan to continue to invest in Thanksgiving

    plan is being adapted due to external forces, and recognizing Thanksgiving Points vision to
