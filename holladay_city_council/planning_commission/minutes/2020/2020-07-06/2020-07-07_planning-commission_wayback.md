CONVENE ELECTRONIC MEETING – Public Welcome and Chair Opening Statement.
Chair Marianne Ricks called the meeting to order at 6:30 p.m. and read a statement regarding the
reasoning behind holding an electronic meeting as well as the Commission statement.

PUBLIC HEARINGS
1.     General Plan Amendment – Future Land Use Map 2.3, MDR-S to LDR-S.
City Planner, Jonathan Teerlink reported that the current General Plan was adopted in 2016 and
the current zoning has been in place since around 2000. When the City incorporated it was
necessary to have a General Plan in place. In 2016, several new land use districts were proposed.
The City hired a consultant who created a list of new land use districts throughout the City.

Chair Ricks recalled that in updating Chapter 2 they identified three ways to increase moderate-
income housing in the City, one of which was identified as Accessory Dwelling Units (“ADU”).

In response to a question raised, Mr. Teerlink stated that if a duplex in the R-1-10 zone is
determined legal, non-conforming, and is demolished, only a single-family home can be built in
its place. It was noted that the desire to change to lower density was to ensure that higher density
does not encroach on the single-family residents that already exists.

The applicant, Holley Richards stated that there was some confusion with the district and the
zoning. She had spoken to each of the applicants personally and they are proceeding as a group.
She reported that she had not contacted the owners of the duplexes or triplexes. Her understanding
was that they were grandfathered in and they did not want to create confusion. They did not object
to them remaining as they exist and the residents are not property owners. She noted that 70% of
those that own property are in support of the proposed change.

Mrs. Richards was present speaking on behalf of all of the applicants. She reported that the
application was brought forth because the property owners and staff have philosophical differences
about how development should move forward in the neighborhood. Prior to 2016, they were in
the Low-Density Residential district. The current residents do not want to change the character of

their single-family residential neighborhood and are united and passionate about their request.
They do not mind some increase in density but are at odds with the appropriate way to do so. They
also want to declare that new development and increased density is not the same as affordable
housing. In researching other MDR-S districts she found that they are all apartment and
condominium units. The MDR-S district is intended to accommodate traditional multi-family
development where increased density has been previously established or where single-family
development is no longer viable. Mrs. Richards further stated that the LDR-S district is almost
completely congruent with single-family homes zoned R-1-10. That proved to her that their
request has merit.

Chair Ricks opened the public hearing.

Kevin Anderson identified himself as the co-applicant and explained that the area to the north on
Murray Holladay Road is already zoned R-2-10 and nearly all are duplexes. They have only
included in the application areas that are currently zoned R-1-10. He also clarified that no zoning
is being changed and will not have an impact on the current duplex owners in the area.
Mr. Anderson stated that the area residents are at odds with planning staff who wants the character
of the neighborhood to change. That, however, is not the objective of the General Plan. He
commented that the character of the neighborhood is nearly identical to the neighborhood across
the street east of Clearview and should be so designated in the General Plan.

Steve Doxey gave his address as 2430 East Kentucky Avenue and asked that he not be limited to
three minutes in order to have a full and fair hearing. The residents were not aware of the General
Plan update and the area is being redesignated and targeted for multi-family development, which
is incompatible with the character of the neighborhood. Mr. Doxey pointed out that the duplexes
on Kentucky Avenue are in the HV/MU designated area. For that reason, they were not included
in the application and do not need to be revised. He explained that the only suggested rezone for
a duplex that is torn down is RM, which has a density of 16 units per acre.

Melissa Hilton gave her address as 2394 East Murray Holladay Road. She recently met with her
neighbors to the east who own duplexes and they had no idea they had been incorporated as R-1-
10. They were also surprised that they had not been contacted. Mrs. Hilton considered this to be
an appropriate area for slightly higher density and stated that they are surrounded by duplexes.
They don’t prefer a density that is similar to what exists on Kentucky Avenue.

Robin Hough who owns property at 2465 East Kentucky Avenue, submitted comments in writing
expressing her support for changing the district to low-density residential, which is what the
existing land use is.

Mr. Allred pointed out that the apartments on the lower end of Kentucky are not in the Village
Zone and are in the R-1-10 zone. Mr. Doxey agreed but stated that they are not in the MDR-S
zone and are in the HV/MU designation in the General Plan. Duplexes and triplexes in the area
were identified. It was reported that the duplex at 2416 East Kentucky Avenue is in the Holladay
Village Zone. There are three duplexes in the project area on the west side of Clearview Street,
east of Kentucky Ave.

Steve Doxey pointed out that the Future Land Use Map is not precise while the previous map is
very clear and shows that the duplexes are in the HV/MU designated area.

Holley Richards commented that there was a clear boundary to keep the R-1-10 zoned properties
together rather than pull out Mr. Hilton’s property, which is zoned R-1-10. The other properties
are zoned R-2-10. She commented that it seemed logical to do so.

There were no further public comments.

Commissioner Bradshaw agreed with what the residents are doing.

Mr. Allred clarified that the proposed change will not affect Mr. Hilton’s existing zoning.

Commissioner Mackin was not certain that every property owner is on board. She would want
verification from each of the 20 property owners to ensure that they are aware of what is proposed
and verify whether they support it. Mr. Anderson urged the Commission to not impose rules on
this request that are not imposed on others. Chair Ricks pointed out that not all of the residents
were informed when the change was made to Medium-Density Residential. Commissioner Lloyd
considered this situation to be different since the proposal involves only a few property owners
and changes are being advocated on behalf of people who do not want that to occur. She agreed
and to be comfortable considering the request, she would want to ensure that all of the residents
are aware of what is proposed.

Commissioner Bradshaw agreed with Mr. Anderson and stated that the City did not provide notice
of the request because they are not required to. The only change proposed was a change to the
designation and does not involve a zone change. Commissioner Mackin stated that the neighbors
are petitioning the City on behalf of 20 property owners, however, not all are aware of it. She was
not comfortable moving forward without knowing that all 20 property owners have been notified.
Only a portion of the property owners have responded to the request.

Chair Ricks stressed the importance of respecting the rules regarding changing the General Plan.
The job of the Planning Commission in this case was to forward a recommendation to the City
Council who will make the final decision.

Chair Ricks agreed that this area conflicts with the Planning Department because they continue to
get requests that are in concert with the General Plan. They are being put in an awkward situation
by appearing to not support the neighbors when they are working in the best interest of the City as
a whole. She also felt for people who live in the area who do not feel support from staff. She was
in favor of forwarding a positive recommendation to the City Council and encouraging the
applicants to contact all of the property owners.

Procedural issues were discussed. Mr. Allred confirmed that what is proposed is not a rezone. He
explained that the General Plan is a long-term vision. Because it is not a rezone, direct notice
would not be sent.

Chair Ricks proposed that the matter be continued to the next meeting and request that the City
send notice to all of the property owners. The public hearing was to remain open to allow for
additional public comment. The notice should provide the specifics of what is being proposed.
Chair Ricks suggested that the notice be clear that the proposed change is to the General Plan
districts.

Commissioner Mackin moved to continue the matter to the next meeting where they can get
feedback from all 20 impacted property owners. The motion was seconded by Commissioner
Lloyd. Vote on motion: Ann Mackin-Aye, Jan Bradshaw-Nay, Alyssa Lloyd-Aye, Chair
Marianne Ricks-Aye. The motion passed 3-to-1.

The applicants were thanked for all of their work on the proposal.
