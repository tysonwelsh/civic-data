 Presentations, Proclamations, and Awards

 1.       Provo City Employee of the Month - October 2024 0:09:02

Mayor Kaufusi shared how privileged she was to recognize and honor an employee who has
demonstrated exceptional service for an exceptional community. She invited Chief Headman to present
the employee of the month from the Fire Department.

Chief Headman expressed his honor in presenting the October Employee of the Month Award to Carrie
Hill, the fire department’s executive assistant for the past 14 years. He highlighted her exceptional
foresight in identifying the office’s needs, often staying ahead of tasks without waiting for instructions.
He shared an example of Carrie stepping up to manage the billing program for wildland firefighter
deployments, which helps fund the department’s Wildland Urban Interface (WUI) program. Chief
Headman praised her collaboration with other office assistants, calling them the backbone of the fire
department, and emphasized Carrie’s humble, approachable, and friendly demeanor, concluding that it
is not only a pleasure to work with her but also to call her a friend.

Councilor Handley asked about the sales tax distribution and why Provo is fairing more poorly compared

 Public Comment 0:27:30

Chair MacKay read the public comment preamble and opened the public comment period.

Bonnie Schiffler-Olsen, of Provo, expressed her deepest gratitude to the Mayor, City Council, and city
employees for their support in addressing homelessness. She thanked them for sharing meals in her
home to discuss solutions, for their correspondence, and for volunteering at the Food and Care
Coalition. She also appreciated the introduction to local legislators and the kindness shown by the police
department towards the homeless community. Bonnie recognized the collaborative efforts of various
organizations, including the Continuum of Care, the Winter Task Force, Provo City Housing, and local
churches, for providing warming centers and essential support to those in need. She continued to share
personal experiences from her life even after her 2-minute time limit had expired. When she continued
after being asked to step aside to allow others to comment, Chair MacKay called for a brief recess.

The council reconvened and continued with the public comment period.

Stephanie Smith, of El Paso, Texas, shared her desire to initiate a conversation about hosting her
property as a short-term rental. As a military family nearing retirement, they are purchasing a home in
Provo to be closer to their older children and plan to rent it out when they are not living there. With
experience as an Airbnb super host in San Antonio, Stephanie wants to share her insights on managing
short-term rentals. She highlighted her careful guest screening process, utilizing Airbnb’s identity check
features to ensure valid identification and background checks. Ms. Smith mentioned that she manages
her San Antonio property closely, maintaining cleanliness and staging it between guests, and that her
oldest daughter will be the primary manager of the Provo home, with support from her other children
who live locally. She outlined house rules, including quiet hours and a no-party policy, and emphasized
that any violations would result in guests being asked to leave without a refund.

Chair MacKay suggested that Ms. Smith send an email to the council with any other information she
wanted to pass on, as her time limit expired before she finished her comments.

With no other public comments, Chair MacKay closed the public comment period.

 3.       A resolution approving the appointment of individuals to various boards and commissions.
          (24-005) 0:35:33

       Motion:      An implied motion to approve Resolution 2024-48, as currently constituted, has been
                    made by council rule.

Mayor Kaufusi recommended the appointment of Michael Anne Gardner to the Housing Authority
Board for the vacant term left by Christian Faulkner, which ends on June 30, 2025. She highlighted
Gardner's extensive experience as a director at United Way of Utah, where she gained a deep
understanding of the community's housing needs, along with her skills in board management, fiscal
oversight, and public relations.

Chair MacKay called for a vote.

       Vote:        The motion passed 7:0 with Councilors Bogdin, Christensen, Garrett, Handley, Hoban,
                    MacKay, and Whipple in favor.

 4.       An ordinance amending the Hillsides and Canyons Plan to include a new appendix. Citywide
          application. (PLGPA20240230) 0:36:55

       Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2024-51, as currently constituted, has been
                    made by council rule.

Michael Sanders, Council Policy Analyst, presented. He provided an overview of the timeline for the
plan, which was requested by the council in December of last year. He noted that it took staff some time
to draft the language. The updated version was presented at a work meeting in July, where some
suggestions were made. He confirmed that the latest version, included in the council's packet, received
unanimous approval from the planning commission a couple of weeks ago.

Chair MacKay opened the item for public comment. With no comments and no council discussion, she
called for a vote.

       Vote:        The motion passed 7:0 with Councilors Bogdin, Christensen, Garrett, Handley, Hoban,
                    MacKay, and Whipple in favor.

 Adjournment

The meeting was adjourned by unanimous consent at approximately 6:03 PM.
