       8.1     Resolution 2024-50-Adopting Final Budget Adjustment for the 2024 Fiscal Year

Administrative Services Director, Bruce Kartchner, reported that this should be the last adjustment
for FY2024. Additional modifications were required after the initial Budget Adjustment was
prepared,including one that day.


City Manager, Mark Reid, stated that the typical process is to approve the yearly Budget as was done
in the previous City Council Meeting. Between December and February, a Budget Adjustment is
adopted for any new items that come up. At the end ofthe year, a final Budget Adjustment is made.
No department can be over budget at the end ofthe fiscal year on June 30. Bluffdale does this on a
line-item basis. No departments are currently over budget but a Budget Adjustment is needed for the
line-item modifications.


Finance Director, Stephanie Thayer, provided an overview of the adjustments that were made after
the distribution ofthe Staff Report were as follows.


   • Capital Projects: Funding for new police vehicles was added, which will be purchased using
       American Rescue Plan Act funds. Mr. Kartchner noted that the vehicles were previously
       budgeted for FY2025, but there are vehicles available now. If they aren't purchased when
       available, the City could face a six-month delay in delivery, as well as a potential price
       increase.

   • East Bluffdale EDA: Increment increase.

   • Gateway RDA: Infrastructure and incentives reimbursement agreement.

Ms. Thayer next reviewed adjustments that were included in the Staff Report.

   • General Fund: Transfer to fund future Capital Projects and ensure that, per state law, the
       accumulated Fund balance is between 5% minimum and 35% maximum allowed.

Mr. Kartchner noted that the exact amount contributed to the Capital Projects Fund for FY2024 is still
unknown as two months' sales tax revenue is still outstanding. It has been estimated, but the offset
cannot be determined until the exact revenue is known. The offset is the reappropriated Fund balance.
From a budgeting standpoint, because the budget must be balanced, offsetting revenue is required to
cover any expenditure or transfer.

Ms. Thayer clarified that some line items have come in over or under budget, so adjustments were
made to balance them. The items were estimated a year ago, and it is policy to be conservative on
revenue and as precise as practical on expenditures. Mr. Reid added that this document is used in
preparing the following year's Budget, so their goal is to be as accurate as possible.

   • Capital Projects: Some expenditures, such as the Public Works utility truck, came in higher
       than budgeted. Some additional revenue was generated by surplussing City-owned property
       and project-related funds received from the County. The end-of-year transfer to the Capital
       Projects Fund and accompanying offsetting reappropriated Fund balance were also included.
   • Arts Council: The adjustment reflects additional costs related to some plays, as well as
       additional revenue.

   • LBA: The Water Fimd's portion of the downpayment for the Public Works Building was
       transferred into the LBA from the Water Fund. Mr. Kartchner noted that a portion of the
       amount will be paid back when the bonds are issued so that the down payments from the Water
       Fund and General Fund are equal.
   • Eastern Bluffdale EDA: An adjustment of$5,000 for community garden improvements in the
       Affordable Housing category.
   • Impact Fees for Roads and Bridges: An additional transportation reimbursement of$40,000.
   • Water Fund: The $7 million transfer to the LBA was included, as noted earlier.
   • Fire and Police Protection Fund: Adjustments were made to reflect expenditures and
       insurance reimbursements for accidents, as well as additional revenue for the school resource
       officer and reallocating costs within the police department to cover equipment purchases.

Mr. Kartchner presented the Staff Report and stated that due to new State requirements, prior to
issuing Lease Revenue Bonds, the City is required to pass this specific resolution related to the lease
between the LBA and the City. Currently, the City has a Lease Agreement with the LBA for City
Hall. The amendment is necessary to change the Master Lease to include the Public Works Building.
The Lease Adjustment will not be created yet but is required for bond issuance to ensure that the lease
payments are covered by the City's General Fund and Water Fund.


Matt Dugdale from Stifel, Nicolaus & Company was asked to describe the bond process. He thanked
Mayor Hall and the City Council for going through the additional steps that were made necessary by
recent legislative changes. He noted that, with a Lease Revenue Bond,the City creates collateral for
investors. The City Council is limited in what it can bind future Councils to. With General Obligation

                NOTICE OF PUBLIC HEARING AND BONDS TO BE ISSUED

        PUBLIC NOTICE IS HEREBY GIVEN pursuant to the provisions of the Local Building
Authority Act, Title 17D, Chapter 2, Utah Code Annotated 1953, as amended, and the Local
Government Bonding Act, Title 11, Chapter 14, Utah Code,as amended (together, the "Act"),that
on June 12, 2024, the Governing Board (the "Governing Board")of the Local Building Authority
of the City of Bluffdale, Utah (the "Authority") expects to consider for adoption a resolution (the
"Resolution") authorizing the issuance of the Authority's Lease Revenue Bonds, Series 2024(the
"Bonds"),and calling a public hearing to receive input from the public with respect to the issuance
of the Bonds.

                   TIME,PLACE AND LOCATION OF PUBLIC HEARING
        The Authority shall hold a public hearing on Thursday, June 26, 2024 at the hour of 6:00
p.m. The location of the public hearing is at the City offices of Bluffdale, Utah (the "District")
located at 2222 West 14400 South, Bluffdale, Utah. The purpose ofthe hearing is to receive input
from the public with respect to:(a)the proposed Bonds and(b)any potential economic impact that
the improvements,facility or property financed in whole or in part with the proceeds of the Bonds
may have on the private sector. All members of the public are invited to attend and participate.
                               PURPOSE FOR ISSUING BONDS

       The Authority intends to issue the Bonds to provide funds to (a) finance all or portion of
the costs of acquisition and construction of a new public works building and all related
improvements (the "Project");(b) fund any required deposits to a debt service reserve fund; and
(c) pay costs associated with the issuance ofthe Bonds.
                               PARAMETERS OF THE BONDS

       The Authority intends to issue the Bonds in a principal amount of not to exceed Sixteen
Million Dollars ($16,000,000), to bear interest at a rate or rates of not to exceed six and one half
percent (6.5%) per annum, to mature in not more than twenty-six (26) years from their date or
dates, and to be sold at a price not less than ninety-eight percent(98%)ofthe total principal amount
thereof, plus accrued interest, if any, to the date of delivery of the Bonds.
       The Bonds are to be issued and sold by the Authority pursuant to the Resolution, including
as attachments to said Resolution forms ofa Second Supplemental Indenture ofTrust(the "Second
Supplemental Indenture") amending and supplementing a General Indenture of Trust dated as of
January 1, 2016, as previously amended and supplemented (collectively, the "Indenture") and a
First Amendment to Master Lease Agreement(the "First Amendment to Master Lease")amending
the Master Lease Agreement dated as of January 1,2016, as previously amended (collectively, the
"Lease"), which Second Supplemental Indenture and First Amendment to Master Lease were
before the Governing Board at the time of the adoption of the Resolution. The Second
Supplemental Indenture and First Amendment to Master Lease are to be executed by the Authority
and/or the Board with such terms and provisions and any changes thereto as authorized by the
Resolution.

                                 SECURITY FOR THE BONDS

       The Bonds are payable solely from the rents, revenues and other income received by the
Authority from the leasing of the Project to the Board on an annually renewable basis (the "Lease
Revenues").
