    4. APPROVE THE WEBER COUNTY 2017 TENTATIVE BUDGET
       Scott Parke, County Comptroller, stated that the county proposed a property tax increase in August 2016
       of $1.62 million (25%) to help increase compensation for law enforcement and other county employees
       and to increase the county’s ability to fund critical infrastructure and capital projects. During the public
       hearing the commissioners committed to look for ways to reduce that tax increase. As part of that effort,
       the county has significantly altered its employee compensation package with the goal of saving the county
       taxpayers nearly $27 million over the next 25 years with savings starting to be realized in about year
       seven. The changes include capping the county’s contribution to employee health insurance at
       $350/month/individual and $1,000/month/family, phasing out the five years of health insurance offered to
       retiring employees, capping sick leave accruals and combining sick and vacation into a single paid time-
       off category. Proposed pay increases for sworn officers and most support staff in the Sheriff’s Office to
       bring them up to market value has an average increase of about 13.3%. Other county employees will
       receive market adjustments of 2%, 7% or 10% depending on how far below market their positions are.
       These adjustments will bring other employees closer to competitive market wages but many will still be
       below market rate. These changes along with other cost cutting efforts have allowed the county to reduce
       the proposed tax increase from 25% to 22%, saving taxpayers about $800,000/year. The tentative budget
       is available on the county’s website and a public hearing on the tentative budget is scheduled for
       11/29/2016.
       Commissioner Gibson applauded the work done by staff and others and stated that the commissioners
       used the constructive public comments as they worked to reduce the tax increase. Every position was
       reviewed on its own rather than by blanket review. Commissioner Ebert noted that there were spirited
       debates and a lot of give and take and said that the Commission is committed to reach a final budget that
       pushes the county forward. He added that this is only one small part of the county’s plan to move
       forward. One focus is to drive economic development and bring new growth to our community—new
       money alleviates the tax burden on taxpayers.
       Commissioner Gibson moved to approve the 2017 Tentative Budget; Commissioner Ebert seconded.
       Commissioner Gibson – aye; Commissioner Ebert – aye
