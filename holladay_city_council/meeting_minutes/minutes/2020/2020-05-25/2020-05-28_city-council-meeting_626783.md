III.    Consideration of Ordinance 2020-08 Amending the Zoning Map for Property Located at
2417 E Butternut Road from R-1-10 to R-1-8 for approximately .66 Acres.
Paul Allred Community Development Dir reported that the duplex is legal and was approved about 50
years earlier but is non-conforming. The County indicating that the duplex is legal.

Council Member Fotheringham asked if grandfathering the duplex would continue if the use
changes. Mr. Allred stated that once the duplex is demolished and the use changes, the duplex will
no longer be legal.

Council Member Gibbons clarified that the proposal is for three lots with three dwelling units. He
was concerned about increasing from two permitted dwellings to three but now has a better
understanding of the situation.

Council Member Fotheringham asked for confirmation that the Planning Commission and staff are
in agreement with respect to how to proceed on the matter. Mr. Allred confirmed they were in
agreement.

The applicant, Mr. Mingle was present remotely. Council Member Durham asked Mr. Mingle if he
would agree to place the driveways on the properties to address traffic concerns on Butternut.
Mr. Mingle agreed that could be done but clarified that it would make sense for the home to face
Butternut Road. Mr. Allred stated that staff would work with the applicant on the issue.

Mayor Dahle asked about the mechanics regarding approval of the rezone. Mr. Allred’s preference
was for the Council to include a specific condition requiring the developer to enter into an
agreement with the City to restrict the location of two of the driveways to Butternut and one to the

Council Member Durham moved to adopt Ordinance 2020-08 subject to the following condition:

   1. The developer shall enter into an agreement with the City to restrict the location of two of
      the driveways onto Butternut and the southern driveway onto 4370 South.

Council Member Petersen seconded the motion.

Mayor Dahle asked to confirm 4370 South is the name of the outlet road for the southern home.

Council Member Fotheringham expressed concern with the consistent application of rezone
principles with the Planning Commission and use of the General Plan.

Council Member Quinn recalled that the Hilton property was a flag lot situation with only one
driveway and little road frontage. She did not think the problems with the Hilton property were
comparable to the issues with this project.

Council Member Petersen stated that the General Plan is not a binding document but agreed that the
Planning Commission could be more consistent in terms of project approvals. With the Hilton
property, the Hiltons were able to get more density on their property without a rezone, which is
consistent with the General Plan. She would support the motion because the correct density already
exists on the property. She clarified that she was not voting in favor of the motion to make the
numbers work for the developer.

Council Member Durham thanked staff for their help on the matter.

The Council roll call vote was as follows: Council Members Durham, Fotheringham, Quinn,
Gibbons, Petersen, and Mayor Dahle in favor. Ordinance 2020-08 was adopted by a unanimous
vote.

IV.    City Manager Report – Manager Chamness.
Manager Chamness reported that Salt Lake County will be sharing funding it received under the
Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security (“CARES”) Act with the City. The City of
Holladay should receive around $900,000, which can be used for qualifying expenses such as
technology for remote meetings and cleaning required for public use. Additional information for
the coming fiscal year would be provided as it is received.

Ms. Chamness discussed summer events and reported that staff determined there is no feasible way
for the City to move forward with the Fourth of July festivities so they will be canceled.

The Arts Council has been looking at other scenarios for summer concerts. Information would be
forthcoming. Council Member Fotheringham asked if live attendance was still being pursued. He
would like to help but only if the COVID-19 case count numbers decrease, which was not currently

the case. Ms. Chamness stated that there is a call for volunteers but only if it is comfortable. Staff
was also looking for ways to meet other than virtually. More information was to be provided.

Mayor Dahle asked for updates on the 3900 South project. Ms. Chamness reported that right-of-
way finalization was being completed and advertising would begin next. Any work being done was
likely utility work needing to be completed.

UPD Chief Justin Hoyle provided updates on workplace logistics and reported that things are
currently back to normal. He also reported that the Fire Department assisted with a recent
graduation at Olympus High School. The Department also assisted a local neighborhood with
general complaints prior to COVID-19, which were suspended. The department was now able to

Mayor Dahle asked if it would be helpful for the Council to use their media connections to notify
residents that speeding enforcement will be increasing. Chief Hoyle agreed that would be helpful.
There was general discussion about speeding and addressing concerns on social media. Council
Member Gibbons and Mayor Dahle thanked Chief Hoyle and his staff for their efforts.

V. Council Reports and District Issues.
Council Member Fotheringham reported that he received a call from a constituent about an
abandoned property with a weed and rat infestation problem. He asked if there was any assistance
that could be provided by the City. Mr. Godfrey stated that there is action the City can take and he
would look into the background of the property and report back. He also asked if there was any
progress with concerns raised about bike lanes near Harmons. Ms. Chamness offered to look into
the issue and report back.

Council Member Gibbons reported that he received complaints on short-term rentals and
appreciated how staff was addressing the concerns.

Council Member Petersen thanked staff for their assistance with the recent graduations.

Council Member Quinn commented that short-term rental issues had quieted down in her area. She
received an email following the last Council Meeting from a possible new resident who was very
impressed with the City.

Council Member Durham received a call from a constituent regarding road conditions in his area.
He planned to meet with the individual the following week to follow up. He had noticed a lot of
golf carts and dune buggies driving around the City and asked about enforcement. Council Member
Petersen had the same concerns. Mr. Godfrey stated that the State Legislature recently passed a bill
prohibiting golf carts from being driven on streets unless permitted by City ordinance. Council
Member Quinn had received emails regarding the legality of golf carts on streets. There was
discussion concerning the merits of regulating golf carts on streets.

Mayor Dahle reported that he received data regarding the long-term impacts of COVID-19 for the
future, in anticipation of what the new normal will be. He suggested that it may be beneficial for
the Council to review the information.

VI.    Recess City Council to a Work Meeting.
Council Member Quinn moved to recess into a work meeting. Council Member Fotheringham
seconded the motion. The motion passed with the unanimous consent of the Council.

        a. Discussion on Proposed Development Agreement with Holladay Hotel, LLC.
Mr. Godfrey reported that the rezoning ordinance for the parcel east of Highland Drive was
approved by the Council with the condition that the developer enter into an agreement with the City
setting development requirements to address height and setback issues. The agreement was now
before the Council for review. Mayor Dahle confirmed that no public hearing was required.

Mayor Dahle asked for clarification of Paragraph E and specifically the reference to 53’ and 49’
referenced in the agreement. Staff agreed to investigate and report back to the Council. The Mayor
also asked about the interpretation of Paragraph F. Mr. Godfrey did not consider the specific
language to be problematic.

Council Member Gibbons asked for additional clarification on height and setback issues. There was
further discussion between the Council and staff. Council Member Gibbons agreed to follow-up
with Mr. Godfrey by telephone. Ms. Chamness stated that a draft of the agreement would be
prepared for posting on the website.

       b. Discussion on Holladay Foundation – Search for Chair.
Mayor Dahle asked the Council to put forward the names of any individuals who may be interested
in chairing the Holladay Foundation. The ideal candidate would be someone with time and
organization skills to form a board and the ability to provide vision and leadership.

      c. Discussion on Helping Hands Awards.
Mayor Dahle reported that four individuals were identified to receive the Helping Hands Awards.
A formal presentation, when appropriate will be done before to the Council

       d. Discussion on Tentative Budgets.
Mayor Dahle suggested bifurcating the discussion and pointed out that the budget issues should be
minimal. The larger issue was the proposed presentation from the Citizen Advisory Group
regarding the proposed tax increase. Mayor Dahle reviewed the previous discussion about the three
options for the Council to select from and the timing of presenting the information to the public. He
asked the Council to come to a final decision at this meeting.

Council Member Gibbons applauded the work of the Citizen Advisory Group and was convinced
that the City of Holladay needs to raise more money. He was, however, ambivalent about the
timing due to COVID-19. Because this is not a typical event, the public is entitled to transparency.
His constituency comprises an older generation and may not have the opportunity to fully
participate in a public meeting setting based on technology or feel safe attending meetings in
person. He would support a Stormwater Fee because it entails less process. Otherwise, he would
support preparing for a process at the end of COVID-19 and a Phase 3 survey.

Council Member Petersen was not opposed to a survey but believed it would show that people do
not want a tax increase. She raised concerns about the timing of the City receiving the tax increase
revenue and a potential delay.

Council Member Fotheringham supported moving forward immediately. He recognized that the
public process is important but stated that it needs to be done regardless of a delay. He stated that
the City’s budget situation is very dire and the process regarding the increase had already been open
to the public.

Ms. Chamness expressed concerns about the precariousness of the situation and the magnitude of
the change being proposed. She thought that a decision to move forward would result in significant
pushback leaving the City unable to make changes in the future. She was concerned that the City
will not benefit if it pushes the increase through this year. She supported waiting. Council Member
Fotheringham clarified her comments to mean that if the City pushes too hard, it could result in
Holladay not being able to collect what is needed down the road.

Council Member Durham asked about the timing of the Citizen Advisory Group’s recommendation
being relayed to the public. Ms. Chamness stated that if the Council decides to start the process
next year, public outreach could begin in January depending on the pandemic.

Mayor Dahle asked Assistant to the City Manager, Holly Smith about the proposed process for the
current year and her perspective on the Truth in Taxation process. She stated that originally, the
group planned to make a presentation to the Council in early April. The group lost six to eight
weeks due to COVID-19. She stated that moving through the Truth in Taxation hearing process is
under a very tight timeline now. No other cities in Utah are planning to take similar action at this
time. She understood the concerns being raised. She cautioned that if the City proceeds this year
and is unsuccessful, it will be very difficult to move forward next year. In the meantime, the roads
will continue to get worse. The Council engaged in a discussion regarding the timing of the
process.

Council Member Quinn asked Ms. Chamness about the Council agreeing to pursue the Stormwater
Fee in January. She was concerned about delaying the tax increase but also about the impact on the
infrastructure. She asked if a mid-point approach (treading water) would be better. Ms. Chamness
considered that to be a difficult question but felt there would still be concerns about public
pushback.

Mayor Dahle stated that the good news is that there is a consensus that the most aggressive
approach recommended by the Citizen Advisory Group is necessary. With the delays already
incurred and the minimal ability to use a robust public process, he was concerned that the optics to
the public will be very poor. He was committed to the action but felt the need to delay the process
for the time being. Council Member Petersen supported a delay as long as there is a commitment
to take action in January. Mayor Dahle suggested a public statement be issued in August or
September. The Council discussed the content and format of the statement.

Ms. Chamness addressed a Cost of Living Allowance (“COLA”) for staff and stated that a 2%
increase would cost approximately $35,000. She reported that Utah’s March sales tax revenues
decreased by approximately 1%. The City showed an increase in sales tax revenue in March.
Council Members Gibbons and Petersen expressed support for a COLA increase for staff. Council
Member Durham asked what other jurisdictions are doing and would support a COLA if it is before
January when the revenue proposal is sent out.
