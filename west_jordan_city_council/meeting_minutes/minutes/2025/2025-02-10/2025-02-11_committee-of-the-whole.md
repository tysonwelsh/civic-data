 2. DISCUSSION TOPICS
       a. Potential amendments to West Jordan City Code Section 14-5-5 connecting stub
          streets to adjacent subdivision
          Council Member Shelton said the Council adopted an ordinance that would close off a stub
          street for GEM Apartments in November of 2024, subsequently vetoed by the Mayor. The
          Council overrode the veto with a 6-1 vote at the following Council meeting.

          Council Member Bloom joined remotely at 4:02 pm.

          Council Member Shelton believed residents had not wanted a road that would connect
          existing neighborhoods with the planned apartment complex. He thought residents
          would have been comfortable with the road connecting the neighborhoods with another
          similar single-family neighborhood. He felt the existing 2001 Code was intended to help
          similar subdivisions connect with each other, and said the Council had been comfortable
          not connecting two such different residential uses when the ordinance was adopted near
          the end of 2024. Chair Lamb commented that parking concerns were also a consideration
          when the ordinance was adopted in November.

          Council Member Shelton believed land use has drastically changed in the last 20 years. He
          and Council Member Green met recently met with staff to discuss the Code in depth and
          draft possible amendments. Community Development Director Scott Langford said that
          he did not believe the proposed Code amendments would preclude the use of existing
          stub streets if deemed appropriate. Assistant City Attorney Duncan Murray said one of

          the main Constitutional concerns when it came to stub streets was making sure they were
          required of developments for the purposes of connectivity, public utilities, and public
          safety. Mr. Murray believed the proposed language would make the Code more legally
          defensible.

          Council Member Green noted that the proposed amendments included a lot of clean up in
          Section 14-5-5 that made the Code easier to read. He referred to Brinkerhoff Bluffs as an
          example of a senior housing overlay and PRD with a required trail that ended at a
          boundary wall because the City did not have an ordinance to require a connection. Council
          Member Green noted that with the proposed amendments, neighborhood connections
          would be required, without necessarily needing to be road connections. He pointed out
          wording in the proposed language that needed to be capitalized.

          Council Member Jacob referred to Section 14-5-5a-1 that stated stub streets were
          required at reasonable locations, and did not want to require stub streets at all. He wanted
          to require the concept of connectivity, but not necessarily stub streets because residents
          tended to view empty stub streets as their personal space over time and became mad
          when roads eventually went through. Council Member Jacob suggested leaving space for
          a street undeveloped, with funds left in escrow by a developer to finish the street when
          development came along.

          Council Member Shelton did not like the idea of leaving the space open as a weed patch.
          Council Member Green felt neighboring residents would still do what they always did. He
          pointed out the city would lose money on every eventual street connection if developers
          were required to put money in escrow because of ever rising construction costs. Council
          Member Shelton commented that city resources would be drained if the spaces were
          grassed as temporary park space.

          Chair Lamb asked if the city currently had a lot of stub streets. Mr. Murray responded that
          the Wasatch Electric project would have some stub streets. Mr. Langford said a stub street
          signaled a future road connection to residents. Council Member Jacob noted that some
          stub streets became dark corners for nefarious activity. City Administrator Korban Lee
          suggested using jersey barricades or fencing in the darker areas to eliminate the ability
          to hang out. Council Member Green suggested installing a curb across the middle of stub
          streets to prevent vehicle traffic. Mr. Lee said he would work with staff to put something
          together. The Council discussed possibilities. Council Member Bloom wanted to make
          sure stub street areas met emergency vehicle access requirements.

          Vice Chair Whitelock suggested changing the word ‘will’ to ‘may’ on line 18 of the redlined
          document. Mr. Murray said using ‘may’ at that location would be fine. Council Member
          Jacob suggested the word “shall” should be removed from the first line. Council Member
          Green agreed. The Council and staff discussed the GEM Apartment Development
          Agreement. Mr. Murray believed the proposed code amendments addressed issues of
          concern. Council Members Green, Whitelock, Lamb, Bedore, and Shelton indicated
          support for sending the proposed amendments to the Planning Commission with changes
          discussed.

       b. Discussion of amendments to Council Policies and Procedures
          Policy Analyst & Public Liaison Warren Hallmark presented proposed amendments to
          Council Policies and Procedures to establish a procedure and process to hold Town Hall
          Meetings. Council Member Jacob suggested the first line stating that a Town Hall Meeting
          would not be representative of the Council as a whole should be deleted. Council Members
          Green and Shelton expressed agreement.

          Regarding distribution of flyers for a Town Hall Meeting, Council Member Green
          suggested the language should reflect that Council Members were responsible for
          coordination of any distribution of flyers. Vice Chair Whitelock said the intent of the
          proposed language had been to indicate to Council Members that flyer distribution for a
          Town Hall Meeting would be their responsibility, not the responsibility of Council Office
          Staff. Council Member Jacob would prefer the language not to refer specifically to flyers.
          The Council discussed possible wording, including ‘communication’ and ‘community
          notification’ and asked staff to make recommendations.

          Under Section 6.6 regarding establishment of subcommittees, Mr. Hallmark proposed
          removing reference to subcommittees for topics the Council had decided to address as a
          Committee of the Whole or in regular Council meetings. The remaining subcommittees
          would be: Budget and Audit Committee, General Plan Committee, and Youth Council
          Committee. Council Member Jacob liked the proposed change. Council Member Green
          suggested the language could be shortened further by simply stating standing committees
          were established.

          Referring to the section regarding electronic participation, Mr. Hallmark asked if the
          Council wanted to continue allowing remote electronic participation by Council Members
          in Council meetings. Council Member Bloom appreciated the opportunity to participate
          remotely and asked if there was a reason to remove the opportunity. Council Member
          Shelton expressed the opinion that remote participation was less effective. However,
          referring to Council Member Bloom he said he would not want individual Council
          Members to be excluded from meetings due to changes in work situations. Vice Chair
          Whitelock said she wanted to keep the option of remote electronic participation but
          wanted to require that the camera be turned on. Chair Lamb agreed but pointed out that
          even with remote cameras turned on, those participating remotely were not visible on
          the live stream. Mr. Hallmark said it would be possible to show the camera view of Council
          Members participating remotely during Council meetings. Council Member Bloom
          expressed a desire for the policy to be respectful and sensitive to potential future Council
          Members for whom appearing on camera would be undesirable because of their beliefs.
          Council Member Shelton felt that it should be made clear that in-person attendance was
          preferred.

          Mr. Hallmark said the Council Chair currently had the option to grant five minutes instead
          of three minutes to speak during the public comment period to individuals representing
          a group. He said there had been some discussion about the possibility of removing the
          five-minute option. Council Members Whitelock, Shelton, and Bedore said they would not
          support removing the five-minute group representative option. Council Member Green
          suggested individuals representing a group and requesting five minutes instead of three

          minutes should provide a list of the individuals they represented. The Council discussed
          the discretion of the Chair. Vice Chair Whitelock believed most people would not abuse
          the system. Council Member Jacob expressed support for being consistent and allowing
          everyone the same amount of time. Council Member Green spoke in favor of requiring
          individuals who spoke during Council meetings to fill out a card with their name, address,
          and names of individuals whom they represented, which would provide contact
          information for possible follow-up, and provide an accurate spelling of names for meeting

          Vice Chair Whitelock pointed out the Council already had the right in any meeting to limit

          to represent a group to identify the group.

          Council Member Green commented that Planning Commission Rules did not include a
          five-minute option for individuals representing a group, which surprised some residents
          who attended expecting to have five minutes to speak. Council Member Green spoke in
          favor of working with the Planning Commission to establish a consistent, Citywide
          standard. Council Member Shelton responded that the Planning Commission had latitude
          to set its own rules, and he would hesitate to dictate public comment rules for the
          Planning Commission.

          Vice Chair Whitelock said she did not see that there was a problem that needed to be
          solved. Council Member Green said the current policy would allow individuals to talk
          about whatever they wanted during public comments, and to speak again during public
          hearings. Council Member Green expressed a desire to have a legally defensible policy
          clearly stated. He would like the City Attorney’s Office to look at and recommend the best
          practices for Council policy. City Attorney Josh Chandler said his office could put together
          what they viewed to be best practices for Council review and consideration. Mr. Hallmark
          said other cities in the State were having similar discussions about proactive practices.
          Mr. Chandler said he agreed with the desire to be prepared.

          Mr. Hallmark asked if the Council wanted to establish a defined time limit for public
          comments during meetings in Council Policies and Procedures. Council Member Green
          suggested setting a one-hour limit for the public comment period, with the option to
          extend if the Council desired. Council Members Lamb, Jacob, and Shelton expressed
          agreement.

          Mr. Hallmark asked if the Council wanted to permit only in-person public comment
          during Council meetings, which had been a trend among other cities. Vice Chair Whitelock
          said the State Legislature allowed online/remote comments with a camera turned on, and
          said she did not want to be more restrictive than the State. Council Member Shelton said
          he wanted public comments to be in-person only. Council Member Bloom said she wanted
          Council meetings to be accessible to the public and wanted to welcome online/remote
          comments. Council Member Shelton expressed the opinion that the option had been
          abused. Council Member Bloom disagreed and wanted to keep the doors open. Vice Chair

       c. Discussion of the Upcoming 2025 Resident Survey (via Y2 Analytics)
          Policy Analyst & Public Liaison Warren Hallmark said the Council had expressed an
          interest in being involved earlier in the annual survey process. Mr. Hallmark spoke of one-
          time questions that had been included in the survey in the last two years, as well as
          questions repeated year after year for data-gathering purposes. He reviewed 2024 survey
          questions with the Council and asked for feedback.

          Vice Chair Whitelock referred to the survey question asking if West Jordan was heading
          in the right direction, and said many residents had responded they did not know. She
          requested a follow-up question or statement to help the Council understand why they felt
          they did not know. Referring to questions about the quality of services, Vice Chair
          Whitelock would like to know the reasons if respondents gave any City services a score
          lower than a specific threshold, perhaps 70%. She said the purpose was to improve City
          services, and specific data was needed to improve. She suggested including the City
          website address with the question asking where residents obtained information.
          Referring to the question about satisfaction from interaction with City personnel, Council
          Member Green suggested asking for a reason if the satisfaction level was below a certain
          threshold.

          Council Member Green suggested asking if respondents would like the city to follow up
          with them, with an option to leave a personal email address, or a link that would take
          respondents to a different page after completing the anonymous survey where they could
          email any concerns. Vice Chair Whitelock responded the City would need to have
          personnel able to follow up with the contact. City Administrator Korban Lee said he would
          like to have the ability to follow up with and address resident frustrations. Council
          Member Jacob liked the suggestion to ask if respondents wanted to be contacted by City
          personnel. Council Members Shelton and Whitelock agreed.

          Chair Lamb said he did not like the question, “is housing affordable in West Jordan?” Staff
          responded that the question would be worded differently on the actual survey if the

           Council wanted to include questions about housing affordability issues. Council Members
           Bloom and Whitelock said they would like to include a question about housing
           affordability, to be able to share the feedback with State Legislators. Vice Chair Whitelock
           suggested asking if residents supported use of tax dollars for City involvement in
           affordable housing.

           Council Member Green suggested including a question about business license rules, and a
           question about childcare accessibility. Vice Chair Whitelock suggested a question about
           business license rules would be better sent specifically to businesses. Council Member
           Green suggested asking if respondents operated a home-based business in West Jordan,
           with a follow-up question about business licenses.

           Council Member Shelton suggested asking if the city provided adequate facilities and trails
           for recreation. Council Member Green suggested asking if respondents favored the city
           pursuing a recreation center in Ron Wood Park and emphasized that the question would
           need to be worded correctly. Mr. Lee felt the question should only be asked if the Council
           were prepared to pivot based on the response. Vice Chair Whitelock said the previous
           bond vote already asked that question. Mr. Lee suggested asking if residents supported
           the city partnering with the County for a County-owned and operated recreation center
           in Ron Wood Park as a different question from the previous bond question.

           Mr. Lee asked if the Council wanted to ask respondents to rank priority of aesthetic issues.
           A majority of the Council indicated support. Vice Chair Whitelock would be interested in
           a question about resident experiences calling City offices. A majority of the Council
           indicated a preference for interacting with a person instead of an automatic system when
           they called the office.

        d. Informal Discussion with Ryan Holtan, Mayor’s Appointee for West Jordan Justice

           Jordan Justice Court Judge. Mr. Holtan introduced himself and his wife, spoke of his
           professional experience, and answered questions from the Council. Council Member
           Green encouraged Mr. Holtan to consider implementing a judge pro-tem program.

           Council Member Bloom left the meeting at 6:20 pm.
