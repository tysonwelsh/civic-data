Chair’s Report [00:04:28]
2a. Federal Transportation Reauthorization Principles
Andrew Gruber, WFRC, Carlton Christensen, UTA Trustee, and Linda Hull, UDOT, reviewed a
draft of Utah’s federal transportation reauthorization principles, seeking the Council’s feedback.
These principles have been prepared with consideration of AASHTO, APTA, and
NARC reauthorization principles, as well as an eye towards Utah's unique needs and has been
discussed at Utah's Joint Policy Advisory Committee (JPAC) meeting. The draft principles are:
1.
Long-term and timely reauthorization
2.
Sustainable funding mechanisms
3.
Utilize existing formula-based programs with enhanced flexibility and multimodal
funding
4.
Streamline processes
5.
Support and ensure state, regional, and local transportation agencies have the
ability to harness innovation, data, and technology
2b. Legislative Preview / Tax Reform Update [00:25:56]
WFRC Executive Director Andrew Gruber requested that Item 2b be tabled until Representative
Robert Spendlove, a member of the Utah House of Representatives, arrives.
[01:15:10] LaNiece Davenport, WFRC, provided a brief legislative preview. Some key legislative
items for 2020 are:
1. Statewide transportation bond bill
2. UTA Transit Oriented Development (TOD) sites bill
3. Transportation catch-all bill
4. Commission on Housing Affordability bill
5. Tax reform (Tax Restructuring and Equalization Task Force)
The overview of this list of topics was followed by a more in-depth discussion on Utah’s tax reform,
led by Representative Spendlove.

4a. Wasatch Choice 2050 Vision Implementation
Ted Knowlton and Julie Bjornstad, both with WFRC, provided an update on the Wasatch Choice
Implementation workshops. The implementation workshops are designed to detail the adopted
Wasatch Choice Vision, explore implementation challenges in each area, and share information
on key implementation opportunities that relate to recent state legislation. Half of the ten
implementation workshops have been held so far. Each workshop involves local leaders and staff
from a small group of cities and townships, including County representatives. The input gathered
has been distinctive in each meeting. After the update, the members of Council were asked for
their input on the implementation challenges that the region as a whole seems to be facing.

5c. Air Quality Update [01:07:08]
Kip Billings, WFRC, provided an update on mobile source emissions and air quality conformity.
WFRC analyzes present and future traffic and the corresponding emissions to verify that the goals
of the State Implementation Plan (SIP) are met before the transportation plan can be approved.
This emissions analysis process is known as “conformity”. Portions of the WFRC region have
been designated by EPA as maintenance areas for carbon monoxide (CO), non-attainment for
coarse particulate matter (PM10), and non-attainment for ozone (O3). The Utah Division of Air
Quality, with support from WFRC, is in the process of seeking from EPA a designation as a
maintenance area for fine particulate matter (PM2.5).

Commissioner Shawn Milne, Budget Committee Chair, reported on the items that were discussed

6a. PUBLIC HEARING AND ACTION: Approve FY20 Budget Amendments
Commissioner Milne requested a motion to open the public hearing on the FY20 Budget
amendments. Mayor Jeff Silvestrini made a motion and Mayor Jenny Wilson seconded, and the
vote was unanimous. Andrew Gruber, WFRC, informed the Council of the proposed amendments
to the draft budget as outlined in the memo and documents included in the packet. As WFRC
completed FY19 and final expenditures were reviewed, the actual spending came in lower than
budgeted. This resulted mainly from spending on some contractual items that occurred more
slowly than was anticipated or from items that were added to the FY19 budget in the later months
but did not begin work until FY20. As a result, the amount of funds that carried forward from FY19
into FY20 is higher than was anticipated, resulting in an increase in the FY20 budget as some
spending shifts from FY19 to FY20. The majority of the funds and projects carried forward are
for Transportation and Land Use Connection program projects. There are also several new
joint planning projects and an adjustment to the Supplies/Software/Sponsorship line item.
