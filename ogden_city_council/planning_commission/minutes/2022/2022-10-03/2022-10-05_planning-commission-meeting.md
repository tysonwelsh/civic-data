5. Subdivision Amendment, Spring Valley 4275 Spring Drive

   Mr. Chad Olson, 4274 Spring Drive, indicated this is a request to amend the subdivision so the remnant parcel
   is again part of its original lot.

   Ms. Patrick explained this subdivision is in the R-1-10/SA zone and had previously been reviewed in January,
   but was tabled pending completion of a new geotechnical study as well as analysis of the existing rock wall.
   The subdivision was recorded in 1988 at which time a restriction was placed that no structures are allowed
   beyond 150’ from Spring Drive. Since that time, the previous owner has recorded and sold a remnant parcel
   without City approval creating two illegal lots, and this action is to remove the isolated parcel and reconnect it
   with its original lot. A new geotechnical report has been completed as well as analysis of the existing rock
   wall. She indicated a majority of Lot 1 is above the allowed 30% slope allowed by Ordinance and is
   unbuildable unless a variance to this standard is approved by the Board of Zoning Adjustment. She clarified
   the combination of the lot and parcel as proposed does not guarantee approval of such a variance, but brings
   the lot into conformance with the R-1-10/SA zone regulations. She stated evidence indicates the rock wall
   was constructed in 1989 and its recent analysis indicates it is in stable condition. According to the
   geotechnical study, the existing sheds should be removed and no structures should be allowed within 20’ of
   the rock wall.

                                                          4
In considering whether the amendment should be approved, the Commission is to determine there is good
cause for the proposed amendment and whether lots will conform with Zoning and Subdivision Ordinance
requirements. She indicated the cause to eliminate an illegally created parcel is valid, and the new lot will

currently exists is unbuildable. Staff recommends a note being placed on the plat which indicates the lot is in
the Sensitive Area and any grading, construction or other development must comply with the standards of
that zone and there is not sufficient buildable land on the lot to permit construction of a single-family home
without variance to these standards being granted. Staff recommends approval of the proposed amendment
subject to this note being placed on the plat prior to it being recorded and all other conditions of City Staff be
adhered to.

Commissioner Graf expressed concern the lot would be unbuildable as it currently exists and suggested it
might be added to an adjacent lot as open space, stating there is no guarantee a variance could be granted.
Ms. Patrick answered the action is to merge the lot with the remnant parcel as it was originally recorded and

Ms. Kelly Lowrey, 4260 Spring Drive, expressed concern relating to the stability of the rock wall and asked if

7. Restoration of Nonconforming Use, 186 North Harrison Boulevard.

   Mr. Brierley explained this was a project consisting of three duplexes which were approved with a conditional

                                                         6
  use permit in 1983 at which time the property was zoned R- 2. In 2001, the City rezoned this area to R-1-6 at
  which time the units became nonconforming. City water was turned off in 2007 and between 2006 and the
  present, there have been numerous code violations and the buildings have become deteriorated and become
  uninhabitable. He cited examples of code violations and citations, indicating the City has boarded the
  buildings to reduce trespassing and a September 9th notice and order for demolition was approved by the
  Mayor.

  The applicant is now requesting the Planning Commission approve the restoration of the use as three duplex
  buildings as a nonconforming use. While the Ordinance allows the Planning Commission to re-establish a
  nonconforming use, certain standards must be satisfied when doing so.
  1. Re-use to the original number of units as originally constructed would enhance the neighborhood by
       providing improved housing conditions and reduce nuisance potential; and
  2. Building upgrades are made in areas defined in a building report and concurred with by the Building
       Official to make the building safe for habitation utilizing current codes. The building report is to be
       prepared by a licensed architect, engineer or contractor and contain specific items to qualify for human
       habitation.
  Mr. Brierley stated no building report has been received by the building official and as the use is surrounded by
  single-family homes, the reuse as duplexes would not enhance the surrounding neighborhood. Staff
  recommends the request be denied, stating the removal of the buildings would eliminate a nuisance to the
  neighborhood and allow construction of a new swingle-family home which would enhance the neighborhood.

  Commissioner Safsten asked what would happen to the property if the buildings were to be demolished. Mr.
  Tanner responded if the City demolishes the buildings, a lien is recorded on the property requiring the owner to
  cover the cost of the demolition. The property is then certified with the County Recorder but is not eligible for
  tax sale for four years after that certification. Code Enforcement would continue to monitor the site and
  continue the citation process if not maintained, and may require the property to be fenced to provide better
  security.

  MOTION:        A motion was made by Commissioner Graf to deny the request to restore the nonconforming
                 use based on the findings the proposed use would not enhance the neighborhood, would not
                 remove nuisance potential and no building report made by a qualified professional and
                 concurred with by the building official has been submitted. Motion was seconded by
                 Commissioner Schade and passed unanimously with Commissioners Aaberg, Blaisdell, Graf,
                 Safsten, Schade, Shale, Shinoda, Southwick and Sandau voting aye.

9. Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance Amendment, Residential Vacation Rentals

  Mr. Brierley stated Staff has received some complaints relating to residential vacation rentals, and following a
  previous work session with the Commission, has developed some proposed language to clarify and assist in
  enforcement. The first revision is to change the name to short-term rentals rather than residential vacation
  rentals. He indicated Code Enforcement has had difficulty enforcing owner-occupied vacation rentals and has
  also requested some revisions as it is difficult to determine the owner lives there eight months during a
  calendar year. Most complaints are related to parking and noise in the neighborhood. Staff is suggesting the
  owner must be present at all times the owner-occupied vacation rental is being used as the eight-month time
  period is unenforceable. Staff also is suggesting a third proof of residency be required upon application for an
  owner-occupied vacation rental which may include a driver’s license or State ID card, utility bill, tax return etc.
  On-street parking would also not be allowed, and the use of recreational vehicles as part of the vacation rental
  is not to be permitted.

  Commissioner Southwick felt the proposed ordinance would not be more enforceable as there is no means to
  assure the owner is present 24/7, which also is impractical. Commissioner Safsten suggested it would be as

                                                          9
  effective if the owner were able to return within a specified time period rather than requiring their complete
  presence during the rental period. Commissioner Shinoda asked about the purpose of owners being present,
  asking if the intent is to address problems and whether this is an impractical solution. Commissioner Safsten
  stated if the owner is present, people behave better. Discussion continued relating to allowing vacation rentals
  as a privilege and the owner being present often reduces code violations. Mr. Brierley explained the proposed
  revisions to the Code is to respond to current complaints and assist in enforcement of the Code. He stated
  while the City cannot cite violations based on advertising, they are able to issue citations when a vacation
  rental is visited and there are violations. He indicated there are currently 20 licensed vacation rentals and likely
  around 250 operating without licenses, but the City is working with a contracted company to find these and
  request they either license or desist the use.

As there were no public comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Blaisdell to close the public hearing.
  Motion was seconded by Commissioner Aaberg and passed unanimously.

  Mr. Brierley addressed the details of the ordinance language proposed and indicated if a home is being used as
  a vacation rental, they are unable to store an RV on site as it invites the potential of visitors staying in these as
  well as the home. Commissioner Southwick expressed concern with this restriction indicating it is
  micromanagement and is unnecessary as there already are codes which prohibit living in a recreational vehicle.
  Mr. Tanner indicated the illegal use of recreational vehicles as living quarters is an ongoing concern with Code
  Enforcement. Commissioner Southwick felt this restriction should be eliminated, and if an RV can be legally
  parked on the property, it should be allowed. Discussion also centered on whether the restriction of on-street
  parking is enforceable as the streets are public. Commissioner Shale indicated she has used a vacation rental
  and part of the agreement for occupancy indicated where parking is and is not available, and may restrict on-
  street parking as well as the prohibition of additional guests. Commissioner Southwick felt these should not be
  over-regulated, and that the number of bedrooms limits the occupancy so there is no need to restrict visitors.

  MOTION:         A motion was made by Commissioner Southwick to change the name to short-term rentals as
                  proposed by Staff, the owner be required to present the three forms to prove residency and
                  meet the same requirements as a non-owner-occupied short-term rental in terms of either being
                  present or available within 30 minutes as well as the requirement to notify neighbors of the use
                  and identify any responsible party who may respond to complaints based on the findings the
                  amendments are consistent with the General Plan, the purpose of the vacation rental ordinance
                  and State law. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Schade and failed by a 4-4 tie vote with
                  Commissioners Schade, Shinoda, Southwick and Sandau voting aye and Commissioners
                  Aaberg, Blaisdell, Safsten and Shale voting no.

  allowed on the premises when a vacation rental use is occupied.

  MOTION:         A motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to rename these as short-term rentals, to require
