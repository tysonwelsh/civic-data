3.3. Mayor
Ms. Dina Blaes , Director, Office of Regional Development, stated the Mayor ’s 
Office was caught off guard with the ordinance regarding outdoor weddings as a 
conditional use in a Foothill Residential (FR) Zone and has concerns with it .  
Council staff assumed this would be a change in an FR Zone with some proximity 
or relation to a lawful restaurant operation in a Commercial Visitor (CV) Zone, but 
that is not the case.  This applies to outdoor weddings, but it is unclear whether that 
includes rehearsals, dinners, parties, etc.   In addition, the scope is enormous; the 
FR Zone covers well more than 125,000 acres in Salt Lake County, and that will all 
be subject to this ordinance.  Land use implications were not vetted and planners 
were not consulted.   This will likely come back to the Council in February after it 
goes to the community councils and planning commissions.

Council Member Snelgrove  stated this has been worked on for months and the 
administration has been aware of it and had meetings on it.  Then, there is zero 
percent chance this will have an impact on the FR Zone because outdoor weddings 
will require a conditional use permit and many hoops to jump through.  What is 100 
percent certain is that restaurants will no longer have this burdensome 
administration issue hanging over them.  Had appropriate steps been taken earlier, 
the Council would not have needed to take action on this.
Ms. Catherine Kanter , Deputy Mayor of Regional Operations, stated the 
administration has been working on a special exception application submitted by 
Log Haven.  This ordinance is completely different than that.    
Mayor Wilson  stated a better public process would have been to directly engage 
the Mayor ’s Office with something as sweeping as this rather than forcing her team 
to scramble to come before the Council with what they believed were significant 
concerns.  When any ordinance is proposed, the subject experts should be allowed 
time to review it, whether they are people from her portfolio, the MSD, or 
elsewhere.   It concerned her there was no window to engage in discussion.

RESOLUTION NO. 5920
A RESOLUTION OF THE SALT LAKE COUNTY COUNCIL 
REDISTRICTING THE BOARD DISTRICTS OF LOCAL BOARDS OF 
EDUCATION AS REQUIRED BY UTAH STATUTE
WHEREAS, Utah Code Ann. § 20-A-14-202, provides for the election of 
members of local boards of education (alternatively referred to as “local 
school boards”) as established under Utah law; and
WHEREAS, Utah Code Ann. §20-A-14-201, establishes that “the county 
legislative body, for local school districts whose boundaries encompass 
Page 4 of 13
County Council Meeting Minutes December 7, 2021
more than a single municipality, … shall divide the local school district into 
local school board districts;” and
WHEREAS, Utah Code Ann. § 20A-14-201, further establishes that, among 
other criteria, a county legislative body “shall redistrict local school board 
districts to meet the population, compactness, and contiguity requirements ” 
… “at least once every 10 years;” and
WHEREAS, on or about April 26, 2021, the United States Census Bureau 
published the most recent decennial census report; and
WHEREAS, Chapter 2.71 of the Salt Lake County Code of Ordinances, 
2001, provides for the establishment of a County redistricting commission to 
act in an advisory capacity to the Salt Lake County Council for the purpose 
of reapportioning local school districts; and 
WHEREAS, on September 15, 2020, the Council appointed seven members 
of the Salt Lake County Redistricting Commission to serve a term 
commencing on January 1, 2020, and terminating on December 31, 2028; 
WHEREAS, the Salt Lake County Redistricting Commission has met in 
open and public meetings throughout 2021, and has faithfully discharged its 
duties under Section 2.71.040 of the Salt Lake County Code of Ordinances; 
and
WHEREAS, the Salt Lake County Redistricting Commission, after soliciting 
input from affected school districts located within Salt Lake County and 
from the public, has provided recommendations for redistricted local school 
board districts to the Council, and has also provided maps of suggested 
boundary changes to the Council for its consideration; and
WHEREAS, the Salt Lake County Council, acting in its capacity as county 
legislative body for Salt Lake County, now intends to adopt plans 
redistricting local school districts located within Salt Lake County whose 
boundaries encompass more than a single municipality as required by and in 
a manner consistent with the applicable legal requirements of federal and state 
law;
NOW, THEREFORE, be it resolved by the Salt Lake County Council that:

1) The Salt Lake County Council hereby adopts a redistricting plan for 
each of the four local boards of education located within Salt Lake County 
whose school district boundaries encompass more than a single municipality 
as further provided for, described, and incorporated by reference in Exhibits 
A, B, C, and D, each of which also includes a map of the reapportioned local 
school district boundaries and their respective voting precincts.
2) The Salt Lake County Council certifies that each of these redistricting 
plans satisfies the population, compactness, and contiguity requirements of 
Utah statute, and achieves substantial equality of population among the board 
of education districts contained within each of the respective local school 
districts.
3) The Salt Lake County Council commends the Salt Lake County 
Redistricting Commission, the Salt Lake County Clerk, and the Salt Lake 
County Surveyor, for their professionalism, expertise, and assistance 
throughout the redistricting process.

RESOLUTION NO. 5921
RESOLUTION APPROVING AN INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT THAT 
PROVIDES NOTICE OF SALT LAKE COUNTY ’S WITHDRAWAL 
FROM THE CENTRAL WASATCH COMMISSION AND PROVIDES 
FUNDING TO THE CENTRAL WASATCH COMMISSION
WHEREAS, pursuant to Utah Code Annotated Section 11-13-202.5, an 
interlocal agreement that provides for a public agency to share taxes or other 
revenue must be approved by the public agency’s legislative body; and
WHEREAS, Salt Lake County (“County”) and the Central Wasatch 
Commission (“CWC”) are both public agencies under the Interlocal 
Cooperation Act; and
WHEREAS, County has served as a “Member” of the CWC since its 
inception and has provided funding (at its election) to the CWC to assist the 
CWC’s operations during the time period that County has served on the 
CWC; and
WHEREAS, County has decided to withdraw from its membership in the 
CWC effective as of June 30, 2022, and the agreement attached as “Exhibit 
Page 8 of 13
County Council Meeting Minutes December 7, 2021
1” is being proposed that will accomplish these desires and will confirm the 
County and CWC ’s understanding of circumstances related to certain 
payments made (and to be made) by County to the CWC; and
WHEREAS, in 2020, County paid the CWC $ 192,000 for the time period 
June 2020-June 2021.  This payment was made pursuant to an appropriation 
of $ 200,000 approved by the Salt Lake County Council (the “Council”) in 
December 2019 in connection with the County ’s 2020 budget (which was 
later reduced by $8,000 due to COVID budget cuts); and 
WHEREAS, an additional $ 200,000 was appropriated by the Council in 
December 2020 in connection with the County ’s 2021 budget, for the time 
period June 2021-June 2022.  County has not yet paid to CWC any of this 
$200,000 amount that was appropriated in County’s 2021 budget; and 
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED that the Salt Lake County 
Council hereby approves the attached Interlocal Cooperation Agreement, the 
effective date being the date it is filed with the keeper of records of each of 
the Parties to the Agreement.
