Copperton Code Rewrite

               Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, stated the Municipal Services District (MSD) has
completed the second tranche of the Title 19 rewrite. He reviewed the rewrite with the MSD, and
they had discussed holding a work session to make the changes Copperton-specific. Under the
current code inherited from Salt Lake County, appeals go through a Land Use Hearing Officer,
which Copperton does not currently have. Another option would be to have appeals go to a
trained Administrative Law Judge (ALJ).

              Mayor Clayton stated he would prefer an ALJ because if Copperton were to ever
have a City Manager, they might be biased to want to please the Council. An ALJ would probably
be fairer.

                The Council decided to hold a special meeting at 6:00 on October 19th, 2022, a
half hour prior to the regular meeting, to discuss the Copperton code re-write.

Copperton Storm Drain Master Plan

               Mayor Clayton stated after a Copperton resident sustained extensive flooding
storm drain damage to his home a couple years prior, the Council commissioned a study with a
consultant from Boeing Collins and Salt Lake County Engineering.

                Steven Kuhlmeier, Salt Lake County Engineering and Flood Control, stated the
objective of the Copperton Storm Drain Master Plan was to identify all the known storm drains in
Copperton and then, working with Boeing Collins, create a report that complies a list of capital
improvement.

               Greg Loscher, Principal, Boeing Collins, presented the Copperton Storm Drain
Master Plan dated September 2022. As Copperton land is developed, the impervious areas
contribute to more runoff than the natural landscape would. This master plan focuses on areas

DATE            WEDNESDAY                                 SEPTEMBER                         21, 2022

where storm drains are insufficient/missing, identifies maintenance and replacement needs, and,
where possible, moves storm drains into the public right-of-way.

The study area consists of 465 acres. Only 15% is developed and the rest is private land
belonging to Rio Tinto. Everything flows from the mountains and into Bingham Creek. There are
two main pipes; one runs through the state highway and the other through Park Street. Both
empty into the wash. There is some infrastructure in the west part of town that is not connected.
The water bubbles up and eventually will find a pipe. There are twenty-seven subbasins in the
study area. The pipes are in reasonably good condition considering their age. Some areas are
missing pipes, causing water to run into the street before it can be picked up again.
The priority is the area of known flooding on Apex Road and Park Street, and the recommendation
is to bring in more pipe. This would cost about $3 million and would be done in six projects which
can be completed over time. As some of the drains are located on private property, Copperton
will need to figure out how to access the drains legally.

              Council Member Olsen, seconded by Council Member Severson, moved to
approve the plan. The motion passed unanimously.

       Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD)
       Council of Governments (COG)

               Mayor Clayton stated the MSD has a new Director of Finance. It is currently going
through the budgeting process. The MSD must have a Justice Court and is currently contracting
with Salt Lake County, but there are concerns because the Salt Lake County Court is currently
being audited by the Salt Lake County Auditor due to their billing practices and excessive fund

City, along with Kearns and Magna. He intends to ask for a budget item for the first storm drain
project.

    Council Member Olsen

       Unified Police Department (UPD)
       Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA)

             Council Member Olsen stated UPD formed a new committee to be chaired by
Midvale Mayor Marcus Stevenson. This committee will work on operations study and funding
review.

               The SLVLESA Board voted against allowing a further tax hike. They are looking
for other sources of revenue. One consideration is using the MET tax, of which Copperton’s
portion would be $42,000. All the Metro Townships would need to be on board. Without property
tax or MET revenue, SLVLESA would need to cut services.

            Mayor Clayton, Council Member Stitzer, Council Member Olsen, and Council
Member Severson spoke in opposition to using the tax.
