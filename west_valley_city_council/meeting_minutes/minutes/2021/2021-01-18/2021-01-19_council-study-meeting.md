     FILED BY WEST VALLEY CITY, REQUESTING A ZONE TEXT
     CHANGE TO ADD SPECIFIC FENCE REGULATIONS TO PROPERTIES
     ALONG 4100 SOUTH
     Mayor Bigelow informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular
     Council Meeting scheduled January 26, 2021, in order for the City Council to hear
     and consider public comments regarding Application ZT-8-2020, filed by West
     Valley City, requesting a Zone Text Change to add specific fence regulations to
     properties along 4100 South.

       Proposed Ordinance 21-03 related to the proposal to be considered by the City
       Council subsequent to the public hearing, was discussed as follows:

       ACTION: ORDINANCE NO. 21-03, AMEND SECTION 7-2-114 OF THE
       WEST VALLEY CITY MUNICIPAL CODE TO AMEND CERTAIN
       REQUIREMENTS APPLICABLE TO FENCES ON 4100 SOUTH
       Steve Pastorik, CED, discussed proposed Ordinance 21-03 that would amend
       Section 7-2-114 of the West Valley City Municipal Code to amend certain
       requirements applicable to fences on 4100 South.

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

               The City’s Public Works Department is requesting a zone text change to
               Sections 7-2-114, which is the Section of the Zoning Ordinance that
               addresses fence regulations. The proposed change would add specific
               fence regulations to properties along 4100 South.

               The following justification for this change was provided by the Public
               Works Department:

                      “The City has invested millions of dollars to the reconstruction of
    MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JANUARY 19, 2021
                           -3-


                     the 4100 South roadway. Inside the project, it is planned to replace
                     current fencing with a wrought iron (steel/aluminum) fence. As a
                     main artery through the City, it would be valuable to have uniform
                     fencing along the entire corridor to continue the aesthetic of the
                     roadway. Within the 4100 South Reconstruction Project, all fences
                     removed will be replaced with the new style of fence. The City is
                     offering to replace all existing fences not included within the
                     project scope with the new style of fence. The new ordinance will
                     require all NEW fences to be in the same style/specification.”

              By way of background, the 4100 South Reconstruction Project extends
              from Bangerter Highway to 5460 West and is nearing completion. Fencing
              is currently being installed.

RESOLUTION 21-10: AWARD A CONTRACT TO EVANS WEST, INC. FOR THE
INSTALLATION OF FENCING ALONG 4100 SOUTH
Dan Johnson, Public Works Director, presented proposed Resolution 21-10 that would
award a Contract to Evans West, Inc. for the installation of fencing along 4100 South.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       As previously discussed with the City Council, the City seeks to enhance the 4100
       South corridor between Bangerter Highway and 5600 West by replacing existing
       chain link fences with ornamental iron fence. During the reconstruction project,
       chain link fences impacted by construction work were upgraded to ornamental
       fence with the project. This project provides an opportunity to upgrade the
       remaining chain link fences along the project corridor.

       The Public Works Department issued a Request for Proposals to identify the
       fencing contractor that would provide the best solution for the various fence and
       gate scenarios that would be encountered on the project. The selection criteria
       included price, solution flexibility, company experience, materials warranty, and
       installation warranty. Two contractors submitted proposals and Evans West, Inc.
       was chosen based on the specified selection criteria.
    MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JANUARY 19, 2021
                           -4-


       The Public Works Department is preparing agreements for the individual property
       owners to sign giving the city consent to replace their chain link fences. The
       quantity of fence shown in the RFP was an estimated quantity, and when applied to
       the prices submitted by Evans West Inc, total $386,601. The actual quantity of
       fence placed will be based on the number of residents who consent to the fence
       upgrade. The public works department is requesting spending authorization up to
       $450,000 to cover potential fencing scope increases above the estimated scope.

Councilmember Buhler asked if any property owners have opposed to this. Dan replied yes
but not many. Councilmember Buhler asked what would happen if the property owner
doesn’t agree. Dan replied that the City isn’t forcing anyone to remove their fence but hope
most residents will be amenable. Councilmember Lang asked whose responsibility fence
damage would be. Dan replied the property owners. He noted that they sign an agreement
when the fence is installed and are aware that the fence becomes their property.

The City Council will consider Resolution 21-10 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
January 26, 2021 at 6:30 P.M.

       -   Definition of Human Trafficking
              o Modern slavery” and “human trafficking” are umbrella terms for
                   the act of recruiting, harboring, transporting, providing, or
                   obtaining a person for compelled labor or commercial sex acts
                   through the use of force, fraud, or coercion.
       -   What is force, fraud, and coercion
              o Force: the use of physical harm or violence
              o Fraud: Deception for financial gain
              o Coercion: Persuading someone to do something by using force
                   or threats
                        COERCION is when someone is persuaded - or better
                           coerced - to do something not by free will, but through
                           the use of force or threats. Being commanded to do as
                           told to avoid harm to a loved one is an example of
                           coercion. Sometimes only one tactic is used, but often
                           times it is a combination of all three.
                        An example of labor trafficking using force, fraud and
                           coercion is a U.S. Department of Justice case from 2009,
                           when a woman from Peru was offered a nanny job in a
                           western state with a wealthy family and was told that it
                           would include a fair wage, benefits, and room and board.
                           Instead, she was forced to sleep on the living room floor,
                           with little to no food and was threatened that if she left,
                           she would go to prison or be deported. The family who
                           employed her confiscated her passport and all other
                           documentation she had in her possession, and she did not
                           actually receive any payment for her services. As the
                           children’s nanny she would walk the three kids back and
                           forth to school, and other parents at the school started to
                           notice that the woman was disheveled and had no change
                           of clothing. They reported the situation to the principal,
                           who was then able to build rapport with the Peruvian
                           woman and eventually found out she had been a victim
                           of human trafficking.
       -   Signs of online grooming
              o Children are more vulnerable to potential grooming and

       -   Familial Trafficking
              o Another common form of exploitation is called Familial
                  Trafficking, which is when there is a relative involved in
                  recruiting, harboring, transporting, providing or obtaining a
                  child for labor or commercial sex. Let me give you a recent
                  example: In March of 2019, a 16-year-old in Salt Lake City
                  shared how she was victimized by a father that had used his own
                  daughter to befriend her and then he trafficked. She testified that
                  she was befriended by the man's adult daughter, who then
                  introduced her to her father. He proposed that the two girls could
                  make money through commercial sex work and helped them set
                  up advertising online, transported them, and collected up to half
                  of the profits made. The father recruited, solicited, and
                  transported the 16-year-old child for commercial sex and was
                  sentenced to two terms of five years up to life in prison for sex
                  trafficking.                                             (Source:
