        1.2      Discussion of draft General Plan update – Michael Maloy, City Planner & Susie
                 Petheram, FFRK
City Planner Michael Maloy explained staff will look for feedback from the City Council and Planning
Commission on the next steps for the General Plan. He explained a link to the first draft of the General
Plan was provided, and questioned the level of engagement from the public was desired. He explained a
good amount of feedback had been received in the past; however, recently had not been obtained due to
COVID. He advised the City would be required to have at least one public hearing. Planner Michael
Maloy turned the time over to Consultant Susie Petheram to offer an update of the General Plan.
Consultant Petheram offered a short presentation of the General Plan. She explained this was the
beginning of the end of the process before going to the Council for final approval. The Council had the
option to hold a public hearing prior to final adoption, if desired. She explained if a public hearing was
conducted, they would seek to have a work meeting discussion to review the final draft plan and the
comments offered by the Planning Commission.
It was explained after the public hearing, it could be possible to make minor adjustments to the plan.
Consultant Petheram proposed hosting a series of “town hall” open house style meetings. She noted
there were two different ways to conduct the meetings, one being structured by key topics and chapters
or host a separate meeting for each topic and chapter. The second suggestion would ideally be
something to cover in each council district with a Council Member and Planning Commissioner being
present. Prior to the commencement of these meetings, it would be helpful to push this communication
out to the public to help them understand what is trying to be accomplished. The goal of the General
Plan is to help the City Council along with the Planning Commission with their land use decision
making process.
Chair Chris Berbert solicited feedback on what the City Council and Planning Commission would like to
do in regard to the town hall meeting series. He solicited advice on essentially each district having one or
more meetings which could ultimately present different action plans throughout the City. Consultant
Petheram mentioned the Community Outreach Facilitators would be a good resource to use as well.


Planner Maloy clarified the first public hearing would be held with the Planning Commission. After
which, the Planning Commission would offer a recommendation to the Council. A second hearing
could be conducted during a City Council meeting; however, was not required. Councilmember Ohrn
opined the public should have as many opportunities as possible to offer input on the General Plan.
Mayor Watts agreed and explained this has been an ongoing process and needed to have public
participation. He also opined it was unnecessary to host “town hall” meetings, and recommended the
General Plan be available on the City’s website and allow for input. Consultant Petheram explained the
team had been working closely with the GIS Department to offer potential tools for the public moving

       1.3     Discussion of City Code Enforcement Approach and process – Michael Maloy,
               City Planner and Rockne Crisp, Code Enforcement Officer
Planner Michael Maloy introduced the new Code Enforcement Officer Rocky Crisp. He observed the
purposes of Code Enforcement including: protect the health and safety of the public, preserve
appearance and beauty of the neighborhood, encourage community pride and preserve the value of
property. Planner Maloy reviewed items encountered with regards code enforcement. Planner Maloy
turned the time over to Planner Sheldon Howa.
Planner Howa offered an oversight of the enforcement process and explained approaches do have to be
adjusted according to the violations observed. He noted difficulties due to the COVID-19 pandemic,
and ultimately reached out to surrounding jurisdictions to gain alternative perspectives. It was
determined the majority of cities had stopped being proactive to limit exposure. Planner Howa
recommended to postpone all of the administrative law judge hearings and address violations through
extension letters requesting compliance during this time. He observed an increase of minor infractions
which caused momentum loss with other violations. He presented the caseloads from 2020 which
included the closure of 278 cases and currently have 25 active cases.
City Planner Maloy explained his hopefulness and encouragement 2021 being a better year. Planner
Maloy expressed his excitement regarding the new addition to the department as an experienced officer
would help the City get back on tract and make improvements to the process. Commissioner Jackson
Ferguson asked how many type of violations were directly related to land use or building. City Planner
Maloy responded there were no building or land use related violations.
Mayor Watts wanted to know if something could be done with signage placed by developers and opined
the regulations were being blatantly ignored. Planner Howa responded a fine of $25 could be assessed to
the developer to have the signage removed.
Commissioner Brody Rypien asked if the majority of enforcement was done on a proactive or reactive
approach. He expressed concern of the size of the city having adequate time being proactive on minor
violations like weed mitigation. Planner Howa noted the approach needed to be balanced in the current
workload. Commissioner Rypien asked if the department was sufficiently staffed. Planner Maloy opined
the department was understaffed and explained the number of cases would continue to increase as the
City ages.
Councilmember Ohrn questioned the point when parking a large vehicle on personal property is the
City infringing on personal property rights. She recommended being cautious with regulations as there
are personal property rights. Commissioner Adam Jacobson agreed with Councilmember Ohrn, but was
concerned about the large vehicles deteriorating infrastructure and how it affects the community in
general.
City Attorney Chase Andrizzi explained from a statutory perspective enforcement is easier if a common

        1.4     Discussion of the Herriman Towne Center Commercial Property – Heather
                Upshaw, Economic Development Manager
Economic Development Manager Heather Upshaw explained the City owns four commercial pads
surrounding Crane Park in the Towne Center and noted there has been several businesses interested in
occupying those sites over the last few years. She observed options would include to either sell or lease
the property. Chair Berbert asked if the businesses which have approached the City were interested in
owning or leasing the property. Manager Upshaw responded those individuals were not necessarily
wanting to construct the building, but were more interested in leasing the property. Councilmember
Ohrn asked what the original intent and vision was for this area as it was important to view and utilize
these pods as assets to the City.
Assistant City Manager Tami Moody explained there was no consensuses of how to use the Towne
Center property, and staff would like to solicit direction on how to proceed. She acknowledged the
amount of interest received from small businesses owners who do not have the means to construct
premises.
Councilmember Jared Henderson felt this was a terrible location for an investment property, and
suggested some studies need to be conducted prior to determining the best use. He opined there were
currently two different options, first, to sit on the land and sell it when it would be more valuable or
secondly, turn it into a ball field. Councilmember Henderson reiterated the need to conduct a study to
determine if it would be considered a viable location. Councilmember Ohrn felt something needed to be
done with the properties, rather than just leave them empty. Mayor Watts suggested this could be a
long-term solution for future as the City continues to grow and additional building space would be
necessary.
Commissioner Brody Rypien explained there was already a good market study done from Edge Homes
which recommended not having commercial uses on those properties due to commercial property being
available across the street. He explained this should be more flexible. Councilmember Henderson
reiterated a market analysis would be good for this area to help determine if it was truly viable, and did
not want to waste time hoping something could work.
Manager Upshaw explained previous conversations outlined businesses which would not be viable in
the area due to the lack of foot traffic. She noted requiring a building to be constructed with 60-percent
brick or stone would be too expensive for a business to consider purchasing the property for a business.

        1.6     Discussion pertaining to Flag Lot Amendments – Michael Maloy, City Planner
Planner Maloy reviewed the current ordinance for flag lot development. He explained the ordinance
which added several new standards was adopted in 2017 and it was intended to increase restrictions on
flag lot development. Staff looked at several other surrounding cities and found flag lot developments
were allowed; however, each jurisdiction had different standards. When the current ordinance was
adopted, it was determined flag lots could change the look and feel of a neighborhood. He explained it
could be possible to add some clarifying language to the ordinance, if desired.
Planner Maloy reviewed the current ordinance and offered some basic changes which could broaden the

Councilmember Ohrn felt the most important thing was to keep the integrity and safety of the
