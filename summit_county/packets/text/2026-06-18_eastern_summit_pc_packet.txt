Proposal 
The applicants, Garff Rogers Ranches LLC and Ivory Development, have applied to amend  
the Eastern Summit County Development Code to create a new zon e called the Lost Creek 
Community Zone (LCCZ).  The zone would enable the creation of public/private 
infrastructure, multiple types of residential uses, and neighborhood commercial uses.  
 
Background 
The applicant is the owner of approximately 407  developable acres in the Brown’s Canyon 
area. Currently the property is zoned AG -80, which means one unit of density for every 80 
acres of land. Access to the property would be from Brown’s Canyon Road.  
 
The applicant stated: 
 
“The intent of the LCCZ is to allow for a flexible, master -planned approach to land 
use, infrastructure, and design that responds to site -specific conditions, protects 
environmental resources, and advances long -term community objectives in a 
predictable and transparent manner.” 
 
The creation of the LCCZ would be the first step in the process. If the application to amend 
the Development Code is successful, the applicant would then apply for a Master Planned 
Development/Rezone process that would get into the specifics of the project (project layout, 
utilities, number and type of units etc.).   
 
Applicable Review Process 
 
The process for modifying the Eastern Summit County Development Code is established in 
Section 11-5-3. The Code outlines two distinct but related processes: (1) amendments to 
the text of the Development Code, and (2) amendments to the Official Zone Distric t Map 
(rezoning). 
17
2 
 
 
A. Development Code Text Amendments 
 
Amendments to the text of the Development Code involve changes to the written regulations 
and standards. The review process includes the following steps: 
 
1. Planning Commission Review: The Planning Commission conducts a public hearing 
and forwards a recommendation. 
2. County Council Action: The County Council conducts a public hearing and takes final 
action to approve or deny the proposed amendment. 
 
The current request is an application to amend the text of the Development Code to 
establish the proposed Lost Creek Zone.  If the text amendment is approved, 
implementation of the zone would require a separate application to amend the Zone District 
Map (rezone) for any specific property.  
 
Analysis 
 
During prior work sessions, much of the Commission discussion focused on potential 
development outcomes and infrastructure considerations associated with the proposed 
zone. While those topics are important, the current application is a legislative request to 
amend the Development Code text. The primary question before the Commission is whether 
the proposed language establishes an appropriate framework, standards, and review 
criteria for future applications. The following questions are intended to help focus  
discussion on the adequacy and implementation of the proposed ordinance language. 
 
Discussion Questions 
 
1. Does the proposed Lost Creek Zone clearly define the vision, character, type, and 
scale of development intended for the area?  
2. Does the proposed code language contain sufficient objective standards to provide 
predictability for applicants, neighboring property owners, staff, and future decision-
makers? If not, what additional standards should be included in the ordinance rather 
than addressed through future negotiations or applications?  
3. Does the proposed applicability area—including the one-mile radius from Lost Creek 
Road and the allowance for non-contiguous parcels —appropriately define where 
and how the zone may be used?  
4. Does the ordinance adequately address infrastructure considerations, including 
water, sewer, transportation, public services, and the timing or phasing of 
development relative to infrastructure availability?  
5. Are the proposed open space and site design requirements sufficient to achieve the 
goals of the zone, including clustering, connectivity, resource protection, and the 
desired development pattern?

3 
 
6. Does the affordable housing component provide sufficient clarity regarding 
implementation, long-term expectations, and the relationship between housing and 
transportation access? 
 
Recommendation 
 
Staff recommend that the Eastern Summit County Planning Commission review the 
proposed amendment to the Eastern Summit County Development Code and provide 
direction to the applicant and staff.

11-3-__: LOST CREEK COMMUNITY ZONE (LCCZ):  
 
A. District Intent: The Lost Creek Community Zone establishes a collaborative and flexible 
approach to creating a community in Lost Creek  that is capable of deliver ing public 
benefits such as increasing attainable housing supply, open space , and employment. It 
provides an adaptable, large- scale framework that coordinates land use, infrastructure, 
housing, and open space to guide future growth. This approach supports integrated, well -
designed communities and avoids reliance on rigid traditional zoning, ensuring orderly 
development, long- term sustainability, and consistency with Summit County’s overall 
goals.  
1. The Lost Creek Community Zone will enable the creation of a broad strategy that 
will result in a comprehensive, community- specific land use and design strategy 
that will address community specific needs . The needs that will be addressed , 
include, but are not limited to:  
a. Construction of cost-efficient public and/or private infrastructure (streets, 
water and wastewater).  
b. A range of housing opportunities. 
c. Space for neighborhood businesses and live-work space.  
2. Definitions: 
a. Lost Creek  Community Zone  means a zoning district applied to real 
property where a property owner or group of property owners has 
submitted a petition to amend the county’s zoning map. 
b. Community Plan means a depiction of proposed uses and other elements 
within an area qualifying as a LCCZ  area, which plan is approved as a 
zoning map amendment at the time the LCCZ  is applied to a qualifying 
area by the County Council. 
B. The Lost Creek Community Zone Location Requirements: 
1. In order for a property to be rezoned to the LCCZ, the property must be located within 
one (1) mile of Lost Creek, and within Township 1 South, Range 4 East, Section 25 or 
Township 1 South, Range 5 East, Sections 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 29, 30, 31, not 
within an existing Special Planned Area (SPA), as generally depicted in subsection H.

Community Plan Design Narrative 
 
This Community Plan reflects a deliberate, flexible and context-sensitive approach to growth, 
shaped first and foremost by the site’s existing natural systems, topography, and access 
constraints. Rather than imposing a rigid development pattern, the plan organizes land uses, 
circulation, and open space in direct response to the land, reinforcing environmental stewardship 
while creating a coherent and functional community framework. 
 
The overall layout is structured around the site’s natural drainage corridors, ridgelines, and 
slopes. Hillside preservation areas are strategically designated to protect steeper terrain and 
visually prominent landforms, maintaining the natural character of the landscape and minimizing 
disturbance. These preserved hillsides also function as natural buffers between development 
areas, reinforcing ecological continuity and long-term slope stability. 
 
Open space is not treated as residual land, but as an organizing element of the plan. A continuous 
network of community and neighborhood parks is woven throughout the site, aligned with 
riparian corridors and low-lying areas that already support natural drainage and vegetation. This 
approach enhances habitat value, supports stormwater management, and creates meaningful 
recreational and visual amenities that are accessible from surrounding neighborhoods. The 
central community park will serve as a focal point for gathering and recreation, while a robust 
trail system and smaller neighborhood parks will provide localized open space integrated into 
daily life. 
 
Circulation is carefully designed to respect existing grades and minimize earthwork. Road 
alignments follow natural contours, reducing visual impacts and infrastructure costs while 
improving safety and drivability. Primary access points are limited and strategically located 
along Lost Creek Road, providing efficient connectivity while avoiding unnecessary 
fragmentation of the site. Internal streets are designed to balance mobility with neighborhood 
character, supporting walkability and intuitive wayfinding. 
 
Development areas of the project are clustered in the locations that are best suited for 
construction. These areas are generally on gentler slopes and outside of sensitive environmental 
corridors. This clustering allows for more efficient infrastructure extension, preserves larger 
contiguous open spaces, and reinforces a clear transition between developed and natural areas. 
The result is a pattern of growth that feels embedded in the landscape rather than superimposed 
upon it. 
 
Overall, the Community Plan demonstrates a thoughtful integration of land use, infrastructure, 
and open space that responds directly to existing site conditions. By aligning development with 
natural systems and topography, the plan achieves a balanced framework that supports long-term 
sustainability, visual quality, and community livability, while remaining flexible enough to adapt 
over time as individual phases are implemented.

Chapter 10: Public Facilities and Services 
 
Introduction 
 
The Public Facilities and Services Element establishes  a framework for coordinating public 
infrastructure, facilities, and essential services in Eastern Summit County. These facilities—
including water and wastewater systems, emergency services, schools, parks, libraries, 
solid waste facilities, and communications infrastructure —support public health, safety, 
and quality of life. 
 
This chapter promotes efficient , ﬁscally responsible service delivery by directing growth to 
locations that can be served by existing or planned infrastructure. It also supports 
coordination among Summit County, service providers, municipalities, special districts, and 
other public agencies so  facilities and services remain aligned with community needs and 
long-term growth patterns. 
 
Context 
 
Eastern Summit County’s  rural geography creates  unique service-delivery challenges. 
Development is dispersed across a large area, making infrastructure extensions and 
emergency response more costly and complex than in more urbanized areas. 
 
Many essential services—including healthcare, childcare, community centers, libraries, and 
major commercial services —are concentrated in  incorporated municipalities or the 
Snyderville Basin. As a result, many residents travel signiﬁcant distances for everyday 
needs. 
 
Community engagement during this General Plan update showed  strong support for 
directing growth toward existing communities and areas with established infrastructure. 
Residents favored development patterns that make  efficient use of public investments , 
preserve rural landscapes, and limit costly infrastructure expansion. 
 
As Eastern Summit County grows , maintaining service levels will require continued 
coordination among public agencies, utility providers, school districts, emergency service 
providers, and private infrastructure operators. Long-term facility planning will help ensure  
growth is ﬁnancially sustainable and consistent with community values. 
 
Existing Conditions 
 
Eastern Summit County is served by public agencies, special service districts, utilities, and 
private providers that collectively deliver essential services to residents and businesses.

Works Facility, Animal Control Facility, Henefer Landﬁll, and Three Mile Canyon Landﬁll. 
Additional public facilities and service providers are shown on Maps X and X. 
 
Water and wastewater service is provided through private water companies, special service 
districts, and individual well and septic systems. Because of the area’s rural character, many 
properties rely on private wells and onsite wastewater systems rather than centralized 
utilities. 
 
Fire protection is primarily provided by the North Summit Fire District and South Summit Fire 
District. Emergency medical services are provided through regional partnerships and 
contracted providers. Law enforcement services are provided by the Summit County 
Sheriff's Office. 
 
Schools, parks, libraries, recreation facilities, and community services are generally 
concentrated in incorporated communities, where infrastructure and population centers 
can most efficiently support public investment. 
 
Infrastructure and public service  availability varies across Eastern Summit County. While 
many community areas have access to essential services, remote locations often face 
longer emergency response times, limited utility availability, and higher long-term 
maintenance costs. 
 
Community Engagement Feedback 
 
Public outreach emphasized the importance of aligning growth with infrastructure and 
public services. Residents supported directing development toward existing communities 
and locations where facilities, utilities, trails, transit, and public services are already 
available. 
 
Key themes included: 
 
• Nearly 65% of respondents indicated that redevelopment near existing public 
facilities, transit, trails, and services was an appropriate or very appropriate strategy. 
 
• Residents supported growth patterns that use existing infrastructure efficiently and 
reduce the need for costly public service expansion. 
 
• Community members emphasized the need to maintain reliable emergency services, 
utility systems, and transportation infrastructure as growth occurs. 
 
• Participants supported planning future public facilities in ways  that preserve rural 
character while meeting current and future needs.

GOAL: Provide public facilities and services that protect  public health and safety, support  
rural communities, promote  ﬁscal responsibility, and align with Eastern Summit County's 
long-term growth patterns. 
 
OBJECTIVE A : Maintain adequate public facilities and service levels throughout Eastern 
Summit County. 
 
Policy 10.1: Coordinate with public agencies, special districts, utility providers, and 
service organizations to maintain adequate levels of service for residents and 
businesses. 
 
Strategy 10.1.1: Meet regularly with service providers to identify facility needs, 
service limitations, and long-term capital improvement priorities. 
 
Policy 10.2: Support emergency services, public safety facilities, healthcare access, 
and other essential services that protect public health and safety. 
 
Strategy 10.2.1: Consider emergency response capabilities, service 
coverage, and facility capacity when evaluating future growth patterns. 
 
Policy 10.3: Encourage expanded and modernized communications infrastructure 
and broadband service throughout Eastern Summit County. 
 
Strategy 10.3.1: Coordinate with telecommunications providers to improve  
broadband access in underserved areas. 
 
OBJECTIVE B: Align growth with infrastructure capacity and available public services. 
 
Policy 10.4: Ensure new development can be  served by adequate infrastructure, 
utilities, and public services. 
 
Strategy 10.4.1: Require development proposals to demonstrate adequate  
water, wastewater treatment, transportation access, emergency services, and 
other necessary infrastructure. 
 
Policy 10.5: Promote development patterns that make efficient use of existing public 
investments and infrastructure. 
 
Strategy 10.5.1: Encourage inﬁll, redevelopment, and growth in existing 
community areas where infrastructure and services are available. 
 
Policy 10.6: Ensure new development contributes a  proportionate share toward 
infrastructure and facilities needed to serve growth.
