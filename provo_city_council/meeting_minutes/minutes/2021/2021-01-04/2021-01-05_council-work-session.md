also covered what was required to be included in official meeting minutes, noting that the City
Council records its broadcasts online to cover many such requirements.

Management Act (GRAMA), which outlines in statute the practices for management of records

2.       A discussion about the Elected Officials Compensation Commission. (21-007) (0:36:59)

Cliff Strachan, Council Executive Director, presented. He outlined the process for selecting
members of the elected officials compensation committee, which convenes during municipal
election years. Councilor David Sewell expressed that this system was a significant improvement
over the previous process; he invited Wayne Parker, CAO, to share more background on the
formation of the commission. Mr. Parker indicated that the commission was originally formed
under Mayor John Curtis to examine mayoral compensation. Mr. Strachan added that councilor
compensation was added to the scope of the commission and was enacted in city code. The
Mayor and Council would each nominate three members, after which those six individuals would
select a seventh and final member of the commission to study the issue. Presentation only.

3.       A presentation from the Provo Housing Authority. (21-002) (0:46:36)

Robert Vernon, Provo Housing Authority CEO, presented. Mr. Vernon reviewed the Provo City
Housing Authority, as well as the Utah Regional Housing Corporation and Utah Community
Land Trust. He highlighted the work done by each organization, as well as some of their past and
upcoming projects. Throughout the presentation, he responded to questions and clarifications
from Councilors. Several questions related to the PCHA and how vouchers are allocated within
the city using a waiting list system. Councilor Bill Fillmore asked for clarification on how the
property tax credit program worked; Mr. Vernon illustrated with a recent example, noting the
program allotted money to each state each year and remained a competitive and effective tool.

One of Provo’s most significant challenges is the dearth of starter homes and the lack of options
which remain affordable to different segments of the population. Mr. Vernon reviewed data from
the federal Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) which outlined area median
incomes and housing costs, compared with HUD’s fair-market-rate levels. Several Councilors
offered their insight and feedback as to the nature of the housing issues facing Provo. Councilor
Shannon Ellsworth also noted that housing affordability was also a challenge to renters, in
addition to homeowners. Renters were often left out of the conversation but make up a
significant portion of households in Provo (approximately 60% by some counts). Councilors
supported getting more area-specific data on housing and housing costs. Presentation only.

5.       A presentation from the Community and Neighborhood Services Department
         regarding the Housing Element of the General Plan. (21-004) (3:07:03)

Melissa McNalley, Management Analyst, presented. She highlighted 2019 data on housing and
demographics. About 39% of Provo’s population is engaged in post-secondary education. Of the
approximately 26% of households in the defined level of poverty, over half of those households
are enrolled in post-secondary education. Several Councilors commented on this phenomenon,
noting that graduate students may currently rely on student loans and therefore their income
levels would place them in this bracket, but were probably on a trajectory to more economic
prosperity in the future after completing their education. Ms. McNalley also highlighted a study
on the impediments for different populations in obtaining housing. In response to a question
from Councilor Shannon Ellsworth about why Provo’s Hispanic population was often being
denied housing; Ms. McNalley explained that in many cases, it was because of income levels.

Ms. Ellsworth also expressed interest in getting visual data, such as a heatmap, illustrating
housing types and density overlaid with amenities in the city. She wondered whether long-range
planning was adequately locating complementary uses in proximity, rather than concentrating
them in a disparate manner. Several Councilors shared comments and suggested using a
comprehensive approach for the General Plan and housing planning. Councilors also discussed
the possibilities of a housing audit and what it might entail. The discussion turned to
neighborhood plans and whether or not these were an effective and sustainable tool. Robert
Mills, Planner, shared a staff perspective from the Community and Neighborhood Services
Department, noting that area plans (which combined several proximal neighborhoods) may be a
more productive and sustainable approach, while still allowing some area-specific long-range
planning throughout the City. Presentation only.

6.       A presentation from the Utah League of Cities and Towns (ULCT) regarding
         proposed legislation regarding housing issues for the 2021 Legislative Session.
         (21-005) (2:29:20)

Victoria Ashby, ULCT Director of Government Relations, presented. She previewed several
issues relevant for the upcoming state legislative session. She highlighted several changes to the
usual session as pandemic precautions are in place and many opportunities for public and
municipal involvement are being moved online. Ms. Ashby specifically noted several issues
relating to cities which ULCT would monitor throughout the course of the session. She
highlighted a bill from Representative Ward addressing accessory dwelling units and the related
building codes as a way to facilitate adding affordable housing in cities. In response to several
questions and comments by Councilors, Brian Jones, Council Attorney, clarified the possible
implications of a state code change under the bill. Ms. Ashby invited the Council’s continued
participation with ULCT and during the upcoming session. Presentation only.

Administration: Mayor's Update

7.       A Franchise Agreement (Amended) between Provo City and Google Fiber. (21-008)
         (4:08:03)

Wayne Parker, CAO, introduced the discussion. With the advent of streaming platforms, he
explained that many customers are leaving traditional cable and VOIP platforms, a phenomenon
known as “cord-cutting.” When Google Fiber was initially instated in Provo, their business
model was comprised of a package option including bandwidth, video, and telephone services. In
order to continue to evolve and best meet the needs of their customers, Google Fiber and the City
have discussed and negotiated new terms for their legal agreements going forward.

Dan Follett, Treasurer, highlighted specific changes in the proposed agreement and Brian Jones,
City Attorney, offered further clarification where needed. Mr. Parker, Mr. Follett, and Mr. Jones
responded to Councilors’ questions during the presentation. Councilor David Sewell noted that
Google Fiber has been a great community asset in its free service to many low-income residents
of Provo. He mentioned his hope that Google Fiber would continue to offer competitive pricing
and services that would reach and benefit as many citizens as possible. Presentation only.
