38          Trails Discussion
39
40   Cary Necaise, Public Works Director, presented on City trail development and improvement
41   initiatives. He provided an overview of a City map that highlighted current and potential future
42   trails, distinguishing between types such as existing, planned, sidewalk, and paved trails
43   through color coding. He noted that Midas Creek and Rose Creek trails had been identified as
44   priority areas, with estimated costs of $150,000 and $250,000, respectively, for full completion.
45
46   Mr. Necaise proposed securing approximately $100,000 annually for trail maintenance and
47   development, suggesting that consistent funding could enable trail connections and
48   improvements in phases rather than scattered across various sites. He recommended creating
49   a rating system, similar to street evaluations, to prioritize future trail maintenance and
50   development needs.

32            Architectural Code Changes
33
34   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, presented updates on proposed revisions to
35   architectural standards and underlying codes to provide clearer direction while allowing more
36   flexibility in design. He noted that current standards are rigid, requiring specific elements, which
37   can limit creativity and flexibility. Proposed changes would enforce "four-sided architecture,"
38   where all sides of buildings are equally treated, rather than focusing solely on the street-facing
39   sides. Additionally, he suggested setting limits on materials per building—no more than four
40   types and no less than two—aiming to avoid excessive variation.
41
42   Mr. Lethbridge referenced past Council feedback, proposing a restriction of 75% for any single
43   material on a building’s façade, with potential exceptions in the downtown area where brick is a
44   dominant theme. He proposed reducing the specific requirements in the downtown ordinance
45   from five specific design elements to a more manageable number and replacing rigid elements
46   (such as clock towers) with more adaptable features like towers or vertical accents. For older
47   buildings undergoing renovation, new code requirements would ensure cohesion with
48   surrounding structures by identifying two primary architectural elements that align with existing
49   themes. This would allow Planning Commission staff to refer to original approvals to maintain
50   consistency while offering flexibility to adapt to market trends.

 1   Mayor Staggs expressed frustration regarding the allowance of infill developments to have
 2   finished grades significantly higher than the surrounding built environment. In response, Mr.
 3   Lethbridge explained that in some cases, developers choose to leave the dirt on-site rather than
 4   remove it for cost reasons. He emphasized the need to balance various site requirements
 5   during the review process, including engineering, storm drainage, and access.
 6
 7   Mr. Lethbridge discussed ongoing work regarding residential conversions to commercial
 8   buildings, noting the challenges these cases present. Currently, each conversion has been
 9   evaluated on an individual basis, but staff anticipates an increase in proposals and aims to
10   establish minimum standards to ensure a proportionate change in the building's structure.
11   Additionally, he addressed the need for clarity regarding renovation triggers. He emphasized the
12   importance of distinguishing between minor changes, such as repainting, and more significant
13   renovations that might require adherence to updated standards.
14
15   Councilmember McCay proposed adding stone as an acceptable primary material alongside
16   brick for commercial buildings in the downtown zoning area, emphasizing that modern designs
17   can include various materials. Mr. Lethbridge clarified that while the current proposal includes a
18   minimum 30% brick requirement for downtown buildings, there is room for adjustment. He also
19   acknowledged that while the downtown zone may have more specific material requirements,
20   other zones would have different criteria focusing on the variety of materials.
21
22   Councilmember McCay also inquired about the current minimum size requirement for
23   commercial lots, currently set at half an acre. Mr. Lethbridge explained that while this size is
24   necessary to accommodate various commercial elements, there is interest in exploring potential
25   adjustments to better facilitate smaller businesses, like Swig.
26
27   Councilmember McDougal expressed concern that the proposed flexibility in architectural
28   standards for the downtown area could undermine the thematic consistency the City has been
29   working to maintain. He emphasized the importance of adhering to established styles, such as
30   the use of brick and arches, to preserve the character of the downtown district.
31
32   Addressing the Mayor's comments, Councilmember McDougal highlighted ongoing frustrations
33   with how developments impact residents, particularly in relation to height variances in fencing
34   and structures. He cited a specific instance involving a development on Water Street, where the
35   anticipated outcome differed significantly from what was delivered. This discrepancy led to
36   residents feeling dissatisfied, especially when they were faced with towering walls that blocked
37   views or created privacy issues.
38
39   Mayor Staggs and Councilmember McDougal suggested that the Council should have more
40   authority to influence final site plans, particularly in commercial developments, to prevent
41   discrepancies between community expectations and what ultimately gets built. They
42   emphasized the importance of ensuring that developments align with residents' visions,
43   particularly regarding height and design features.
44
45   Mr. Lethbridge acknowledged that previous practices had not adequately clarified how
46   developments would affect the surrounding areas, especially concerning grade differentials. He
47   noted that often, engineering adjustments made during the review process were not effectively
48   communicated to the public or the Planning Commission. He recommended implementing a
49   conditional use permit for projects with a significant grade differential (over 18 inches) between
50   properties. This approach would provide an additional layer of review and ensure that residents
51   were informed about potential impacts on their views and privacy.

 2   Councilmember Buroker expressed skepticism about the Council approving site plans, arguing
 3   that it wouldn't effectively address ongoing issues due to potential changes in Council

 6   code that specifies allowable grade differences, she believes that many of the problems

 9   Councilmember Haymond expressed concern that the City's requirements for aesthetics might
10   hinder the development of functional buildings that don't need to be prominently featured or
11   "fancy." In response, Mr. Lethbridge clarified that it's common for cities to implement varying
12   standards based on the type of building. While public-facing structures often adhere to higher
13   aesthetic standards, more industrial buildings, like workshops or maintenance facilities, can
14   have more lenient requirements.
