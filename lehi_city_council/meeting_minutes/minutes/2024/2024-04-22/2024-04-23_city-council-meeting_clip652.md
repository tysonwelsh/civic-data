2.1 Update on Alpine School District Split
Stacy Bateman, Alpine School District Board Representative, gave the Council an update on the
study commissioned by ASD regarding the feasibility of a school district split, emphasizing its
large geographical area and high enrollment. Ms. Bateman noted that the survey had mixed
opinions from patrons regarding tax increases. She presented the various options that were
proposed. One option being to remain as a 1 school district. Other options included a 2 district
split, with Lehi going either east or west; and a 3 district split with a central district that would
include Lehi, American Fork, Highland, Alpine, Cedar Hills, and a small section of Draper.

The Council discussed the voting and approval process for a school district split, noting that any
chosen option must be approved by a majority vote on an election ballot. It was discussed that
cities could create an interlocal for a school district split and could then submit for a ballot
proposition. Ms. Bateman stated that the School District is likely to submit their own ballot
proposition.

2.2 Presentation from Utah Association of Municipal Power System (UAMPS)
Mason Baker and Jackie Coombs presented an update to the Council. He also discussed market
changes and new energy resource projects. He outlined UAMPS's efforts to adapt to changes in
the wholesale electricity market and discussed the organization's membership options to address
local distribution system needs. He reviewed UAMPS's diverse resource projects and
emphasized the importance of member autonomy in selecting participation in these projects.

Baker provided an overview of UAMPS's current energy mix, noting a significant reliance on
hydroelectric generation, natural gas, and coal, while acknowledging the challenges and
volatility faced in the wholesale market in 2023. He detailed UAMPS's transition towards
renewable energy sources, such as solar and wind, and the incorporation of battery storage to
enhance reliability and cost-effectiveness.

Baker also discussed the broader energy trends in the U.S., including the shift from coal to
natural gas and the increasing importance of renewable energy and battery storage. He addressed
the regulatory and market-driven factors influencing these changes and UAMPS's strategic
approach to resource planning, emphasizing the need for a balanced and reliable energy supply.

During the Q&A session, Baker responded to questions about current battery technologies,
potential legislation affecting nuclear energy, and the barriers to geothermal energy
development. He highlighted the advancements in geothermal technology driven by former
fracking experts and the potential for closed-loop systems to reduce operational costs.

13. Consideration of Ordinance #24-2024 approval of the BCP Property General Plan
    Amendment on 14.67 acres of property located at approximately 1200 West 1450 South,
    changing the land use designation from VLDRA (very low density residential) with the
    Pioneer Village Mixed Use Overlay to LDR (low density residential).
    Tony Trane was representing the applicant.

    There was discussion regarding the overlay. Concerns included whether piecemeal
    development could undermine the village overlay concept, which envisioned a more cohesive
    plan with mixed-use elements. Challenges to create a cohesive vision include various
    property owners with different development timelines.

    Mayor Johnson asked for public comments.

    Larry Handfield expressed concerns that the area could become too dense and lose its relaxed
    neighborhood feel.

    Kenneth Glade expressed concerns with connectivity and asked that consideration be given
    to agriculture uses in the area. He was opposed to the overlay designation.

    Trent Toughride expressed concerns with traffic and the overlay designation.

    Jessica Topper stated that an additional road connection is needed.

    Michelle Christensen stated that she was opposed to an increase in density.

    Randy Mellow was opposed to the overlay. He suggested maintaining the VLDRA zone. He
    also noted that they need help addressing the traffic issues on 1100 West.
