          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-
2025 Annual Action Plan Amendment #1 and an associated budget amendment came before the Council for consideration. The
memo stated the Annual Action Plan (AAP) for FY 2025 was approved and adopted by the City Council on May 7, 2024. The
plan is used to outline how federal Community Development Block Grant funds, HOME funds and other federal and non-federal
program funds will be used by the City in the upcoming fiscal year. At the time the plan was adopted, the administration estimated
carryover funds (from FY24 to FY25) and program income because the fiscal year had two months remaining.
          Community Development Director Smith summarized the memo and stated the purpose of the AAP Amendment #1 is to
update the AAP Budget and Goals based on the following:
               • A decrease in actual carryover funds. The amendment shows that carryover funds decreased by $394,955 to
                    reflect actual funds available on July 1, 2024. Carryover funds are unspent funds from the previous year’s AAP
                    budget.
               • An increase in program income funds. The amendment also shows that Program Income increased by
                    $1,006,015 from the City receiving proceeds due to the completion and sale of Quality Neighborhoods projects.
               • An increase in program income carryover funds. The amendment shows that Carryover Program Income
                    increased by $63,040. Carryover PI are funds received from loan repayments that are not spent in the fiscal year
                    that the funds were received. Carryover PI funds are budgeted to complete projects in FY25.
          The amendments proposed with this mid-year update address the following:
              •    Increase the Own–In–Ogden HOME budget by $250,000 with an increase to the goal for down payment
                   assistance from 25 to 50 for the year.
              • An overall proposed increase of $674,100 to the Annual Action Plan HUD Budget. This is due to actual carry-
                   over amounts, updated program income estimates, and allocation of the corresponding budget adjustments.
          Finance Director Sorensen added the proposed budget action on this item is to amend the City’s FY 2025 budget to
reflect the changes included with the Annual Action Plan Amendment #1.
           Chair Richey introduced in writing the proposed Resolution 2024-15, entitled:

          Memos from the Finance Director regarding proposed fee amendments across several Departments came before the
Council for consideration. The memos explained the Ogden City Budget Guidelines and Financial Principles state the following
regarding fee changes: “Proposed fee changes are to be adopted as part of the budget process. City Financial Principles related to
fees are to be followed. The Council recognizes that some fees are impacted by the calendar year, by seasonal activity, or by the
calendars or scheduling constraints of other governmental agencies or businesses. Certain fees may need to be amended during the
fiscal year and therefore cannot be amended with the annual budget process. Mid-year fee increase requests are due October 1,
accompanied by sufficient rationale and justification. Fees to be implemented in January (excluding utilities) will be considered
by the Council in December if all applicable information is received.”
          Deputy Police Chief Ziegler summarized the memo regarding proposed changes to Police Department fees and stated the
proposal includes the elimination of specific fees for crime scene photos, photographs for licenses, fees for a landlord training
program, and the towing rotation program. The proposal adds a fee for a solicitor background check and a solicitor badge at $10
each and adds police accident reports to the police report fee of $25. He emphasized that no fees are being increased and no new
fees are being created.
          Public Services Executive Director Anderson summarized the memos regarding the proposed changes to golf fees and
utility fees. He stated for golf, the proposed amendments would increase cart fees for El Monte and Mt. Ogden courses and
increase certain green fees. The changes would also eliminate a discounted rate Monday-Thursday for 18-hole play. Relative to
utilities, the proposed amendments are related to recycling carts and tailings, or crushed asphalt, as green waste materials. The
proposal would allow residents to pay for a second recycling cart without the need to pay for a second refuse cart. The proposal
would also allow tailings from road milling to be purchased at the green waste site. Other code changes include updating the rates
to reflect the most recent Consumer Price Index (CPI) changes and other minor grammatical and formatting changes.
           Parks, Cemetery, and Trails Director Stewart summarized the memo regarding proposed changes to cemetery fees; the
proposed amendments increase cemetery rates for grave spaces, interments, overtime fees, disinterment fees, and monument
inspection fees. Administrative staff compared Ogden City’s fees to those of surrounding cities with city-owned cemeteries and
found Ogden City’s fees to have fallen below the midpoint. He concluded that cemetery fees have not been updated since 2019.

Proposed Ordinance 2024–36 amending the budget for the Fiscal Year July
1, 2024 to June 30, 2025 by increasing the anticipated revenues and
appropriations for a gross increase of $18,530,850; or
Proposed Fiscal Year 2024-2025 Budget Amendment – ARPA Funds –
Alternate Schedule B. Proposed Ordinance 2024–36 amending the budget
for the Fiscal Year July 1, 2024 to June 30, 2025 by increasing the
anticipated revenues and appropriations for a gross increase of
$18,530,850.
         A memo from the City Finance Director regarding a proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 budget amendment dealing
with the allocation of American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated
Administration is proposing the reappropriation of funds from the American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA). The City received
$25,612,825 in federal ARPA funds and an additional $4,000,000 in state ARPA funds for a total of $29,612,825. The Council has
previously allocated ARPA funds for public safety radios ($1.5M), community grants, ($1.3M), community cleanup ($300K),
Marshall White Center construction ($5.4M), the 36” canyon waterline replacement ($12M), and 4th Street Park netting ($70K).
The remaining funds ($9,042,825) were allocated to general government operations—primarily police and fire wages. As a policy,
the City does not use one-time funds to cover ongoing expenses such as wages, so the funds allocated to general government
operations are available for reappropriation to other projects.
          Finance Director Sorensen summarized the memo and stated Administration is recommending that the funds be
reappropriated as follows:
               • Lester Park Improvements - $1,042,825
               • Union Station Museum/Campus - $2,500,000
               • Acquisition and Demolition of County Jail (Municipal Block) - $5,000,000
               • General Park Improvements (All Abilities Park) - $500,000
               • 4th Street Park Netting - $70,000
          Additional funds from General Fund fund balance are proposed for transfer to the Fleet and Facilities equipment
replacement reserve ($152,600). The total proposed budget amendment is $18,530,850. At the December 10, 2024 work session,
the Council gave direction to prepare an alternative Appropriations Schedule B to direct the proposed funding for Union Station
Museum and Campus ($2,500,000), acquisition and demolition of the county jail ($5,000,000) and general park improvements
($500,000) to a non-project specific CIP account for reappropriation at a later date. All ARPA Funds must be encumbered by
December 31, 2024, and expended by December 31, 2026.
          Vice Chair White inquired as to the administration of the non-project specific account that the money could be placed
into as discussed. Mr. Sorensen stated that account would be under the control of the City Council and once the appropriate use of
the funds is determined, Council action to allocate the funding will be required.
           Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2024-36, entitled:
                   “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the budget for the Fiscal Year July 1, 2024 to June 30, 2025 by
                   increasing the anticipated revenues for a gross increase of $18,530,850 from sources as detailed in the body of
                   this ordinance; and increasing the appropriations for a gross increase of $18,530,850 as detailed in the body of
                   this ordinance; and by providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final
                   passage.”
          Chair Richey then opened the public hearing.
          Laura Lewis stated it is her understanding the City has extra money, but she does not see any of that money being
allocated to the Ogden Canyon Waterline project. She suggested that some of the funding be dedicated to that project to reduce the
financial burden on taxpayers.
          Heath Satow noted that a number of years ago the Marshall White Center project was being discussed and there was a
request to use ARPA funds for that project. The Chief Administrative Officer at the time responded that the ARPA money could
not be used for that type of project. He is pleased that current Administration has reinvestigated qualified uses of the funding and
has recommended the allocation of the funding in a manner that will save taxpayers $5.4 million.
          There being no further persons appearing to be heard, COUNCIL MEMBER HYER MOVED THE PUBLIC
HEARING BE CLOSED. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER CHOBERKA, ALL VOTING AYE.
          Chair Richey referred to Ms. Lewis’s question about funding for the Ogden Canyon waterline project; he noted that
approximately $12 million in ARPA funding has already been allocated to that project. He noted that an additional $8 million will
be placed in a non-specific CIP account for reappropriation at a later date and the Council can discuss the Ogden Canyon
Waterline project as an appropriate project for that funding in the future. Vice Chair White added that the Council has carefully
considered other funding options for that project, and they decided not to completely deplete the ARPA funds for that purpose
when other funding sources may be available. Mayor Nadolski agreed and added that the City has access to low interest funding
mechanisms that can be repaid over the course of several years into the future, which will reduce the burden on the current
generation of the City and pass some of that burden on to future generations.

Public Comments
          Marie Hogge addressed the 20th Street construction project; the residents were sold on a nice, wider road but residents are
now dealing with negative consequences associated with elimination of their on-street parking spots in front of their homes. She
does not have adequate space for off-street parking for more than one vehicle on her property. The residents were not properly
informed of this element of the project. She cannot find another street throughout the City that is wide as 20th Street where on-
street parking has been eliminated. She invited the Council to visit her street and see what residents are experiencing and then get
to work to fix the problem.
          Heath Satow addressed the Council’s work session tonight and discussion of the completion of a townhome project on
24th Street by J. Fisher Company. He is concerned about the subsidy the developer received from the City, which guaranteed them
an 11 percent profit on the project. The Council’s vote on the project last year was split, and today the Council learned that the
homes in the project area were sold above market prices and those that had been presented to the City when the agreement was
initially approved. He asked if the development contract included a provision for profit sharing between the developer and the
City. He requested the City prepare a publicly available report detailing the profit margins for the project and how much of that
profit will be returned to the City. The City needs to examine past projects and patterns and make contractual adjustments for
future project in order to ensure the City realizes the best possible return on investment.
           Laura Lewis stated she attended a meeting regarding the 24th Street interchange project with Intersate-15 but found that
most of the discussion was about development of West Ogden near the interchange. She would like to know when there will be
more discussion about the actual interchange project. She added that she learned of plans for a roundabout in the middle of West
Ogden, but it goes nowhere. However, it will eliminate four homes, and she would like an explanation of that element of the
project. She then referenced the Council’s discussion of the paid parking program during tonight’s work session. She asked if
residents planning to attend future City Council meetings will be charged to park downtown. She concluded by addressing the
relocation of the Northern Utah Wildlife Rehabilitation Center and asked for an update on the status of building and the property
that was vacated.
           Teresa Bramwell commented on plans for a homeless shelter on 23rd Street and Madison avenue; there were many people
in attendance at the December 3 meeting to comment on their support for the project, but many of those people stand to benefit
financially due to the project. She has learned that ‘the wool has been pulled over her eyes’ for the last several years as she has
contacted so many different people throughout the City and State due to her being the victim of crime nearly every day. She has
had an idea of where the criminals were coming from, and she attempted to find out who was responsible for putting them there.
She approached Weber Housing Authority and asked if they were housing people in her neighborhood and they claimed they were
not. She also scheduled an appointment with Mr. Niederhauser regarding the homeless population in the community and he
seemed to be completely baffled as to why there could be crime occurring in her neighborhood. No one admitted that homeless
people were being housed in her neighborhood, but she has since discovered that is not true and the Housing Authority now wants
to put more homeless people in her neighborhood. She asked if this would add to the crime problem in her neighborhood.
           Chair Richey noted that the Council will meet in a work session on January 7 to research and discuss the Weber Housing
Authority project further, with possible action on their proposal scheduled for January 14. He also addressed Ms. Lewis’s question
about the Wildlife Rehabilitation Center; the building they formerly occupied was demolished within two weeks of them vacating
the building. Walls are being constructed for the new hatchery building and that project will continue to move forward. He
deferred to the Mayor to address Ms. Lewis’s question regarding the paid parking program.
           Council member Choberka referenced Ms. Lewis’s questions about the meeting to discuss the 24th Street interchange
project; the project is being managed by the Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT) and any feedback they received during
that meeting will be used to inform the design process of the project. She added there will be another meeting in January to
discuss the project further. Many residents indicated they were not supportive of the roundabout element of the project.
           Chair Richey then addressed Mr. Satow’s comments about the 24th Street townhome project; he noted that
Administration did not report the sales prices of those homes, but they did discuss prices for units in other project areas. He stated
that the Council can ask for sales prices for the 24th Street project specifically. He then addressed Ms. Hogge’s comments about
the 20th Street project and noted Administration has assigned the Engineering Division to research the matter and provide a
detailed report back to the Council.
