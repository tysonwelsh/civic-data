     3.1 DISCUSSION AND ACTION – Center Street Overpass Agreement with Union

         City Manager Jacob McHargue will present the agreement with Union Pacific for the air
         rights on the Center Street Overpass. The City Council will vote to approve (or deny) the
         agreement.

Mayor Fullmer called for questions from the council.
Councilmember Judd asked about the perpetual easement costs and the $225.000 cost in the
contract. Mr. McHargue replied that the $225,000 was because Union Pacific (UP) had to have
people on site for access across the line. He added that this was just an estimate of what it would
cost. Mr. Overson explained that they had to have two (2) flaggers on site for 114 days.

Councilmember Welsh asked if there was a cap on how much UP could charge the city. Mayor
Fullmer replied that there was no cap. Councilmember Judd asked if it was UP’s flaggers that
were used. Mr. McHargue replied that it was UP’s flaggers.

Councilmember Welsh asked if there was a way to cap it. Mr. McHargue explained that there
was an hourly rate in the contract so it would depend on how many days it took to complete the
project.

Mayor Fullmer felt that the good news was that UP was finally working with the city to get
things moving. Mr. McHargue explained that this was the last thing the city was waiting for so
they could connect the overpass. Mr. Church said that when they looked at the total cost of the
project this was in line with what they would get for inspections, controls, etc.

Motion: COUNCILMEMBER JUDD MOVED APPROVE THE UNION PACIFIC
RAILROAD PUBLIC HIGHWAY OVERPASS AGREEMENT AND ALLOW STAFF TO
SIGN THE AGREEMENT. COUNCILMEMBER EARNEST SECONDED THE MOTION.
MAYOR FULLMER, COUNCILMEMBERS EARNEST, FLAKE, JUDD, AND WELSH

    3.2        DISCUSSION AND ACTION – Adoption of the Vineyard Voter Participation
          Area Map
          City Recorder Pamela Spencer will present the Vineyard Voter Participation Area Map.
          The mayor and City Council will act to approve the map by resolution.

Mayor Fullmer turned the time over to City Recorder Pamela Spencer.

Ms. Spencer explained that the Utah Legislature amended the state code to require that each city
over 10,000 divide their city into quadrants. She further explained that this was strictly for
citizen initiatives and referenda. They would be required to get signatures from each quadrant in
the city so that they had a good representation from the entire city. She mentioned that Utah
County had offered to produce the maps for the cities. She added that she had received that map
in October but it was recommended by other cities that it be adopted by resolution.

Councilmember Judd asked how the county split up the quadrants. Ms. Spencer explained that it
was by registered voters and population. Mr. Church explained that the way the legislation was
written, the city had an obligation to create the maps. He added that this was at the behest of the
county election officers, since they had to certify the signatures, and that the cities needed to
work closely with the counties. He said that they tried to make the quadrants correspond as
closely to the voting precincts as possible.

Mayor Fullmer called for a motion.
