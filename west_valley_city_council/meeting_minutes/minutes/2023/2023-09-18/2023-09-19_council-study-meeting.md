      FROM AMERICAN MECHANICAL SYSTEMS SERVICE, LLC
      Jake Arslanian, Facilities, presented proposed Resolution 23-141 that would authorize the
      Purchase of a Boiler System from American Mechanical Systems Service, LLC

      Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
      follows:

         From Three State of Utah purchasing pre-qualified bidders, the lowest bid for the
         Project was received from American Mechanical Systems Service LLC. A total of
         Three (3) bids were received:

                    American Mechanical Systems Service, LLC SOU Contract ID. MA2606
                     $145,047.00
                    American Chiller Mechanical Service SOU Contract ID. MA3361
                     $153,900.00
                      CCI Mechanical Service SOU Contract ID. MA3518 $219,260.00

             anticipated life expectancy. The Facilities HVAC Division has upheld the system
             to the best of its ability using outdated components and a worn-out system. For this
             reason, we recommend replacing the system with a more energy-efficient boiler
             and modern operating system to ensure proper heating operation of the Performing
             Arts Center.

   Mayor Lang asked how much speed bumps cost. Dan replied approximately
   $3,000. He noted that while the cost isn’t an issue, the City doesn’t have the crews
   to do more than a few a year. Councilmember Harmon verified that the cost is about
   $3,000 in house and about $7,000 if contracted out. Dan replied yes.
   Councilmember Harmon stated that there are speed bumps on Benview and noted
   that Benview hasn’t been redone in many years. Councilmember Huynh stated that
   people in the area know their streets the best. He indicated that people see speed
   bumps and instinctively slow down.

   Councilmember Nordfelt asked how the study is conducted. He indicated that the
   location of the radar would be very important. Erik Brondum, Public Works, stated
   that radar devices are mounted on lights or signposts. He indicated that he tries to
MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – SEPTEMBER 19, 2023
                        -7-


   place them between intersections, not close to a dip or curve, and on straight aways
   where vehicles tend to pick up speed. Erik stated that the radar devices are in place
   for a full week (typically a Monday-Monday) and are posted on weeks that avoid
   holidays or snow. Councilmember Harmon asked if there is a map that indicates
   where radars were placed in specific studies. Erik replied yes. Mayor Lang asked
   what the purpose of the flashing signs that indicate speed is. Erik replied that these
   are primarily used by the Police Department but added that some signs do have the
   ability to track as well.

   Councilmember Harmon asked what staff would suggest to change the criteria to
   increase speed bumps. Dan replied that he would suggest starting slow. He noted
   that if the Council felt the 100-point threshold was too high, they could take that
   down a bit. Councilmember Harmon asked if residents could install their own speed
   bumps if they chose to pay for it. Dan replied that he would not suggest this and
   noted that speed bumps should be installed based on need with data that is
   consistent and applicable citywide. He noted that cost is not the limiting factor.
   Councilmember Harmon asked how many studies can be done on a particular street
   per year. Dan replied that the policy has been one study per street per year. Erik
   stated that the signature petition requirement was added because the City used to
   do a study based on a single complaint which wasn’t necessarily representative of
   the entire neighborhood and therefore was a drain on resources. Councilmember
   Whetstone asked if the Council could make an exception under specific
   circumstances instead of changing the criteria threshold. Dan replied that they could
   but cautioned that this would be the new way that everyone would request speed
   bumps.

   Councilmember Huynh stated that he supports the need for speed bumps on Palmer
   Drive based on the residents who have expressed concern multiple times. Wayne
   noted that the accident totals on Benview (with speed bumps) and on Palmer
   (without speed bumps) are very similar and asked if more accidents would be
   expected on Benview if the speed bumps were removed. Dan replied that crashes
   in neighborhoods most often happen because the driver is distracted and most
   accidents involve parked vehicles. He noted that in most cases, speed is not a factor.
   Wayne asked why West Jordan is moving away from speed bumps. Dan replied he
   believes it’s the current political climate but added that the speed bumps in West
   Jordan are high and uncomfortable so that could be a factor as well. Wayne asked
   where speed bumps will be installed this year in the City. Dan replied on Oquirrh
   Mesa Drive in Sunset Hills. He added that the City is doing a new test on Beagley
   Lane with “speed cushions” in response to racing concerns expressed by the Police
   Department. Mayor Lang asked if staff communicates with the residents to ensure
   they are aware of school routes. Erik replied yes. Mayor Lang encouraged open
     MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – SEPTEMBER 19, 2023
                             -8-


        communication between staff and the residents requesting the study through the
        process. Erik noted that he will often go out with residents and use a radar gun to
        see what the speeds actually are. He stated that perception and actual speed can be
        very different.

        Councilmember Harmon stated that he would be in favor of reducing the point
        threshold and also doubling points for proximity to schools. Councilmember
        Nordfelt suggested adding points as well for vicinity to trail systems where
        pedestrians are more likely to walk. Councilmember Harmon stated that safety
        should be highly focused on in addition to speed. He noted that he has a hard time
        understanding the difference between Benview and Palmer. Dan replied that the
        speed bumps were installed on Benview before the traffic study program was in
        place. Wayne indicated that the purpose of the traffic study is to provide a consistent
        threshold backed by data and numbers as opposed to installing speed bumps when
        they are requested. Mayor Lang agreed that consistency is best she wouldn’t be
        comfortable allowing the Council to make exceptions on a case-by-case basis.
        Councilmember Whetstone stated that he would be concerned about making too
        significant of a change that would swing the pendulum too far in the other direction.
        Mayor Lang suggested having Police Officers do their reports on known streets
        with speeding concerns, like Palmer. She noted that just the presence of a Police
        vehicle would slow many people down.

        Wayne asked how many speed bumps can be installed in a year with current
        resources. Dan replied 4-6. Councilmember Nordfelt stated that Park City recently
        decreased the speed limit in neighborhoods. Dan replied that Salt Lake City recently
        did that as well. Councilmember Nordfelt asked if data shows whether this has
        made a difference. Dan replied that information is not available yet.

        Councilmember Fitisemanu stated that budget is not a limiting factor, but the
        Council should decide whether speed bumps are effective at mitigating speed and
        tragedies while also responding to resident concerns. He stated that his two
        considerations are whether the Council is willing to reduce the threshold to allow
        more speed bumps and whether to add more flexibility/allowance for points as they
        relate to various factors, such as trail systems and proximity to schools.
        Councilmember Fitisemanu stated this his preference is to have a numerical score
        threshold and not allow Council leeway to override data. After further discussion,
        the Council requested that staff return with a recommendation in a month.

        The Mayor and Council had no further questions or concerns.

   Mayor Lang verified that this is all bonded debt. Wayne replied yes. Mayor Lang
   asked if the City is still paying the debt for Embassy Suites. Wayne replied that the
   City holds the debt but it is being completely paid by the owner as agreed by
   contract when it was sold.

   Councilmember Harmon verified that the Cultural Center bond is the one that
   started at 15 million and will be 50 million by the time that is paid off. Wayne
   replied yes and noted that this is the 2010 bond series.

   Mayor Lang asked if the RDA makes their money from the Maverik Center and the
   Fire Stations by the City making payments. Wayne replied money is also accrued
   by tax increment generated by the property taxes in the RDA project areas. Mayor
   Lang asked what revenue refunding for the mall is. Wayne replied that this would
   be a refinance of an earlier debt. He detailed how tax increment works for payments.

   Wayne stated that most of the City debt is very straight forward but noted that the
   Cultural Center bond is unique. He indicated that the legislature enacted a SAR
   funding source that created this different amortization schedule. Wayne stated that
   RDA doesn’t believe it will happen as it was originally anticipated due to legislative
   changes that could occur. He indicated that staff will continue lobbying at the
   legislative level to create a better situation and noted that things change, leaderships
   change, and decisions regarding RDA funding happen every year. Wayne detailed
   other options the Council can consider to approach this debt in the coming years.

   Councilmember Whetstone asked if Fitch considers RDA debt as well as the hotel
   debt when assigning ratings. Wayne replied yes. Jim Welch, Finance Director,
   added that they rate the bonds and look at how things are being paid separately and
   together.

   Wayne detailed how non-callable debt can be adjusted as time passes.
   Councilmember Harmon asked if extra principal payments can be made on non-
   callable debts. Jim replied no and detailed how the bonds are paid.

   Councilmember Harmon stated that the parking structure has an early pay off date
   of 2027. He indicated that the City should be thinking about how to restructure or
   pay that down. Wayne agreed and added that staff is always looking at these trying
   to find ways to approach the bonds and get them paid in the best manner possible.
   Councilmember Harmon stated that the overall debt is reasonable with the
   exception of the Cultural Center. Councilmember Huynh requested a
   communication update once a year.
