28   1.0    Form-Based Code Discussion.
29
30   City Consultant, Mark Morris from VODA Landscape + Planning, updated the Commission on
31   the work performed as well as the purpose and scope. He explained that Form-Based Code is a
32   lengthy document and the existing Code can sometimes be difficult to interpret. The intent is to
33   ensure that the Form-Based Code has clarity and will accomplish what is envisioned. The Form-
34   Based Code Update was funded as part of the City’s General Plan Update. It allows them to target
35   redevelopment areas in the City, primarily along Fort Union Boulevard. Currently, there is a
36   mixed-use zoning designation but the same Code applies to property regardless of size. The Form-
37   Based Code provides more control and context sensitivity to establish the vision for the look and
38   feel of buildings based on the area of the City and the surrounding development. It focuses more
39   on the look, feel, design, and aesthetics than strict regulation on uses and setbacks. The goal is to
40   tailor redevelopment in a way that makes sense in any area of the City.
41
42   Currently, the Code focuses more on the purposes and what people do inside a property and not
43   necessarily how it looks. The Form-Based Code will flip that and focus on what it looks like and
44   less on what is being done in the building. It was noted that Form-Based Codes are used across
45   the nation as well as across the Wasatch Front. They are very context-sensitive and must go
46   through a calibration process.

 2   Mr. Morris reported that the structure of Form-Based Code contains different sections that address
 3   the various requirements. There are already Code requirements and the Form-Based Code focuses
 4   on where a building is located on a site, how it contributes to the public realm, and certain design
 5   elements that are more difficult to change. Staff spent time going through the draft General Plan
 6   to establish goals and priorities. The intent is to look at the priorities and vision to determine how
 7   to update the Code to reflect that. The development is a reflection of the Code that requires it. If
 8   the development is not accomplishing what they want it to, it is merely a sign that the Code is not
 9   requiring what the City desires. Mr. Morris explained that the context is what drives the
10   conversation. The establishment of a discernible Cottonwood Heights Downtown City Center
11   involves the development of key nodes along Fort Union Boulevard in particular. All of the areas
12   under consideration for the Form-Based Code are on Fort Union Boulevard and it looks at specific
13   nodes. If in the future the City wants to apply the Form-Based Code to other areas of the City, it
14   is easy to expand.

24   Mr. Morris explained that other goals include economic development and housing, which will be
25   addressed in the Form-Based Code. They are looking at three nodes in the City where
26   redevelopment is highly likely including non-commercial areas. The priority is to look at the
27   primary commercial route through the City where there are nodes of opportunity consisting of the
28   Town Center and Union Park and make sure that the Code keeps up with the goals of the City.
29   They organized the considerations and Form-Based Code into tiers for form districts, building
30   type, and street type. Use is also part of the equation and is a much broader category. This
31   approach allows for different options in terms of use with more of the focus being on site design
32   and how the building will contribute to the community. Parking is focused on location on the
33   property. Tertiary considerations include landscaping, open space, and signage which are
34   important to implementation but they do not usually drive significant development decisions.

39   district, which will determine the maximum building height and setback requirements. Four form
40   districts have been established. If the Code expands, new form districts will be established.

 4   Mr. Morris discussed the process and stated that the first step would be a Pre-Submission Meeting
 5   with staff where a property owner will review the requirements before making a formal application
 6   with the City. If the project meets certain criteria and the requirements of the Form-Based Code,

 8   than 20,000 square feet, and developments in all Residential Transition Zones could qualify for an

12   Community and Economic Development Director, Michael Johnson, presented the Staff Report
13   and stated that the request is for both land use and zoning map amendments at 6851 Big
14   Cottonwood Canyon Road. The address corresponds with a parcel that is nearly 16 acres in size.
15   The actual request, however, is being run concurrently with a boundary line adjustment. As a
16   result, the rezone request only applies to approximately three acres of the 16-acre parcel. The three
17   acres surround the constructed cul-de-sac with access through the Canyon Creek Estates
18   Subdivision. The boundary of the three-acre parcel is approximately the alignment of Big
19   Cottonwood Creek. The first request is for a Land Use Map Amendment, which is a policy
20   recommendation out of the City’s General Plan that helps guide the City when making future land
21   use decisions. Every parcel in the City is given a land use designation, which specifies what is
22   desired there in the future. The zoning of a property includes the laws, restrictions, and use
23   requirements as they apply today.
24
25   When considering changes, the intent was to ensure that a new zone corresponds with the General
26   Plan and the two should be as compatible as possible. When a zoning request comes in to change
27   the zoning of a parcel, staff first looks at the General Plan. If the General Plan matches the
28   requested zoning, oftentimes the City will support the zoning change. There are circumstances
29   such as this one, however, where it does not make sense to preserve the land use designation. The
30   current future land use designation is mixed-use, which allows for a wide range of residential and
31   commercial development. The current zoning is Foothill Residential, which is a single-family
32   zone typically reserved for foothill areas. The applicant would like to change the zoning from
33   Foothill Residential to Residential Single-Family (“R1-8”). Uses allowed in the R1-8 zone are
34   predominantly residential single-family detached homes on lots that are a minimum of 8,000
35   square feet in size. For the future land use map to be compatible with the zoning requested, the
36   applicant has also requested a change to the land use designation from mixed-use to residential
37   low-density.
38
39   The property owner is Walker Development Partnership, LLC and the applicant that applied on
40   the owner's behalf is Gilson Engineering. Concurrent with the rezone request and the land use map
41   amendment request, is a lot line adjustment. The proposed three-acre parcel does not currently
42   exist. One of Staff’s recommendations was if approved, that the lot line adjustment be completed,
43   finalized, and recorded prior to applying that zone change as the intent is not to rezone the entire
44   parcel. The original Canyon Creek Estates Subdivision Plat was recorded and approved in 1993.
45   The subject parcel is listed as future Phase 2. This shows that it was always the intention to develop
46   the property as a two-phase subdivision.
