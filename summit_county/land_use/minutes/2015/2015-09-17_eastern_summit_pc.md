3. Public hearing and possible action regarding a proposed two lot minor 
subdivision; 1515 West Bradbury Canyon Lane; NS-604-I; Neil Rossmiller, 
applicant – Jennifer Strader, Senior Planner  
 
Planner Strader said the parcel in question is located in Bradbury Canyon.  It has 20 
acres and is located in the agricultural protection (AP) zone.  This zone allows 
properties of 10 acres or more  to be eligible for two lots.  A vicinity map was shown of 
the property.  The existing house on Lot 2 was pointed out.  Next, a copy of the 
subdivision plat was shown.  Lot 1 is proposed to be 11.5 acres and Lot 2 will be 8.47 
acres.  An existing road cuts through Lot 1 and may be the access road for that lot.

1. Discussion  regarding Stewart Ranches 19 Lot Major Subdivision, located on 
Highway 35 in Woodland; Parcel CD-2123 Rex Campbell, applicant – Jennifer 
Strader, Senior Planner   
 
Planner Strader said this is a Work Session for the Steward Ranch Subdivision.  She 
reminded the Commission they have seen this application previously.  In 2008, an 
agricultural preservation subdivision of 10 lots was approved.   The cluster bonus plat 
was never recorded and the approval expired.   
 
In May of this year, the applicant submitted another cluster bonus plat for the same 10 
lot agricultural preservation subdivision.  In July, the Planning Commission held a work 
session regarding the application.  At that time, there was discussion regarding the 
proposed density potential of the lots based on zoning.  She said that based on the 
current zoning (but taking out the cluster bonus) the property is eligible for 19 lots.   
 
Planner Strader said in August, the applicant submitted a revised application for a 19-
lot major subdivision.  None of the responding service providers have had a problem 
with the creation of the subdivision.  The property is zoned highway corridor (HC), AP-
40, and AG-100.  Under the HC zone, there is a provision (Section 11-3-5) that states: 
“For the purpose of locating development, density can be transferred between commonly 
owned property in the highway corridor and abutting zone district to protect agricultural 
lands and open space based upon the findings of a site specific agricultural plan.”    
 
The location of Highway 35 was pointed out on the vicinity map.  Planner Strader said 
during the time of the agricultural preservation application, the applicants had to 
submit a site specific agricultural plan.  The current plan proposes to place Lots 9, 10, 
and 13-19 in the location that the former plan had designated as prime agricultural 
land.  Staff requests a discussion with the Planning Commission to see what their 
thoughts are on the provision that discusses moving the density from the HC zone to the 
abutting zone district.

Commissioner Vernon asked if all counties are under the same Fire Code.  He said the 
Fire Codes in Summit County seem to be different than those in Wasatch County.  Chair 
Ure said the State Code is a minimum and the Counties have the right to be stricter.   
 
Commissioner Henrie said the International Fire Code can change overnight without 
informing the Planning Commission.  Likewise, it may not be possible to have one Code 
with the Engineering Department.  The Commission’s responsibility is to their own 
Code, even though the applicant has to comply with whatever Engineering tells them.

Code as implemented by the State, they have the right to allow for lesser standards.  An 
option is to add mitigating requirements such as sprinklers, more available water, or 
more defensible space.   
 
Commissioner Vernon said some laws are subjective.  They can be applied in some 
places, but not in others.  This should be fixed.  Commissioner Clyde said that a 
development agreement takes precedence over the Code.  That is why ridgelining can 
be applied in some places and not in others, such as in Promontory.  Commissioner 
Vernon said another issue he has is the measuring of slopes.  Is it measured just where
