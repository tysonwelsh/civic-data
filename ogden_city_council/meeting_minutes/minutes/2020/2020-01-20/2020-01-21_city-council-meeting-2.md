        Ogden City Water Utility and Rocky Mountain Power - Strategic Energy
        Management
                  Water Utility Division Manager Herd introduced Matt Jensen of Cascade Energy, noting Cascade Energy is a consultant
        for Rocky Mountain Power and they facilitate the Watt Smart Rebate Program.
                  Mr. Jensen used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss Strategic Energy Management (SEM) practices
        employed in Ogden City. Two years ago, Cascade Energy partnered with a defined group within Ogden City to identify energy
        saving opportunities within the operations of the City's water system. He noted SEM programs are intended to implement low or
        no-cost changes to system operations; the focus in Ogden City was how to supply water to residents using reduced energy support.
        The executive sponsor of this effort was Kenton Moffett, previous Ogden City Water Utility Manager, who also had support from
        several employees in the City's Water Utility Division. They developed and used an energy model to identify operations and
        maintenance savings opportunities. The savings period was defined as January 16, 2018 to November 29, 2019. The actions taken
        in that period included an energy analysis of the Eden wells, increasing the use of the Weber Basin water allotment,
        reprogramming of booster pumps to run at night, lowering temperature set points in PRV vaults, conducting regular air leak audits
        at the Treatment Plant, and refurbishing the Taylor Canyon well. These efforts resulted in an 18 .2 percent energy savings, which
        equated to $27,257 in reduced costs. Mr. Jensen then presented plaques to the Ogden City employees involved in this energy
        saving effort.
                  Council member Stephens asked ifthere were other cities that participated in this type of program. Mr. Jensen confirmed
        and noted the other participants, which included a special service district: Layton, Roy, Clearfield, and Jordanelle Special Service
        District. Council member Stephens commended Ogden City's Water Utility Division for their dedication to this initiative.
                  Acting Chair Blair asked ifthere will be future similar programs in which the City can participate. Mr. Jensen explained
        this operational changes experience is a one-time program, but Rocky Mountain Power does wish to participate with the City on
        all future capital improvements that save energy. If the City intends to pursue a capital project that will result in energy savings,
        Rocky Mountain Power has funding available to assist in those projects and would like to partner with the City.
                  Council member Nadolski asked if this program identified all energy savings opportunities in existing systems or if there
        are other opportunities for reducing energy use. Mr. Jensen stated that the working group identified most of the energy savings
        opportunities; however, there may be other opportunities to reduce energy in the provision of water to City residents. Council
        member Nadolski referenced the reliance upon Weber Basin water and asked if that only shifted the energy usage from Ogden
        City to Weber Basin. Mr. Jensen stated that Weber Basin is a low energy intensive operation as much of their water supply is
        gravity fed. Relying upon Weber Basin did not create new energy usage for either entity.
                  Council member White commended the City's effort at being proactive to identify opportunities to reduce costs and save
        energy. She thanked Mr. Jensen for the information provided tonight.

159 West 2nd Street Local Historic Resource Designation
         A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed historical resource
designation came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated petitioners Richard and Tammy Creeger are proposing
the home located at 159 West 2nd Street, known as the Stone/Brown/James home, be listed as a local historic resource on the
Ogden City Register of Historic Places. The designation would require any significant changes proposed for the building,
including demolition, to be approved by the Landmarks Commission through the approval of a Certificate of Historic
Appropriateness. The designation would help ensure the preservation of the building and would help to ensure that any changes
proposed would maintain the character and historicity of the building. The City's Landmarks Commission has reviewed the
proposed designation and has forwarded a recommendation of approval finding that the building meets both the historical and
architectural criteria listed in the City's code for local designation.
