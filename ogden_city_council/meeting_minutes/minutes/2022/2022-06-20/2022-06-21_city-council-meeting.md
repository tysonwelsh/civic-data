Recognizing retiring Ogden City Planning Manager Greg Montgomery for
40 years of service to the community

A memo from Council staff regarding recognition of Ogden City Planning Manager Greg Montgomery for 40 years of
service to the community came before the Council for consideration. Council member White read the certificate of recognition
issued by the Ogden City Council and Mayor Caldwell and presented Mr. Montgomery with the certificate.

Mr. Montgomery thanked the Mayor and Council for recognizing him; he reflected on his introduction to employment
with the City, the projects he has worked on over his 40 years, service under several different Mayors and City Councils, and two
different forms of government; he stated he has loved his time with Ogden and watching it change and evolve over the years. He
thanked his family for their support over the years, they have made many sacrifices in support of his position with the City, which
often required after hours work attending City Council and Planning Commission meetings. He was asked to communicate what
his greatest accomplishment has been, but he views all his work and accomplishments as team efforts. He has made many friends
throughout his career and will miss the people he works closely with. He thanked the Mayor and Council for this recognition and
indicated his retirement from the City is bittersweet.

Council members thanked Mr. Montgomery for his patience and for his quick wit; they spoke to personal experiences
they have had with Mr. Montgomery and indicated they will miss his wisdom and service to the citizens of the community. Chief
Administrative Officer Johnson spoke to Mr. Montgomery’s deep knowledge of his field of expertise, his kindness, and his work
ethic. He considers Mr. Montgomery to be a good friend and he will miss him.

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2022
Annual Action Plan (AAP) amendment #3 came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Administration is
proposing the Council approve the third amendment to the FY2022 Annual Action Plan and budget. The proposed amendment
specifically addresses the allocation plan for $1,652,923 of grant funds related to the HOME Investment Partnerships American
Rescue Plan Program (HOME-ARP). The funding related to this grant has significant restrictions placed on it and may only be
used for specific purposes related to addressing homelessness. As per the City’s Five-year Consolidated Plan, the City itself does
not administer any programs related to homelessness or homelessness prevention. Ogden City, however, is a partner with Weber
County and other agencies that do deal specifically with homelessness issues. Additionally, Administration is proposing minor
amendments to the Business Recovery Fund (BRF) program guidelines in order to create two programs aimed at meeting two
different CDBG-CV national objectives. Objective One is Job Creation and Retention and Objective Two is Limited Clientele
Activities (related to microenterprises). The programs will be BRF-J which will meet Objective One and BRF-M which will meet
Objective Two. Funding will be split between the two programs.
Community Development Manager Ogden summarized the memo and stated HOME-ARP funding can only be used to
serve individuals that meet one of the following definitions as defined by HUD:
e Homeless
e Atrisk of Homelessness
e Fleeing, or attempting to flee, domestic violence, dating violence, sexual assault, stalking, or human trafficking
e Other populations: specifically, where providing supportive services or assistance under Section 212(a) of
NAHA (42 U.S.C. 12742(a)) would prevent the family’s homelessness or would serve those with the greatest
risk of housing instability
When considering eligible services for the HOME-ARP funding, the following activities may be undertaken and must
benefit the qualifying populations as described:
e Rental Housing
e Tenant Based Rental Assistance
e Supportive Services
e Non-Congregate Shelter

Public hearing to allow input regarding transfers from the Water, Sanitary
Sewer, Storm Sewer, and Refuse Funds to the City’s General Fund

A memo from Council staff came before the Council to consider an enterprise fund transfer. The memo stated as
required by Utah State Code §10-6-135.5, a City that is proposing to transfer funds from an enterprise fund to another fund for a
purpose not directly related to the goods or services provided by the enterprise fund must provide notice of the transfers to the
users of the enterprise fund. The City must also hold a public hearing on the transfers prior to adoption or the amendment of the
city’s budget. In Ogden, a budgetary transfer from the City’s Water, Sanitary Sewer, Storm Sewer, and Refuse Utility enterprise
funds to the general fund has been done for many years; however, the requirement to provide notice of the transfers and to hold a
separate public hearing is a recent change to state law. These utility funds are accounted for as enterprise funds in Ogden and are
operated with user fees charged for goods and services. In the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2023 budget, the City is proposing a
transfer from these utility funds into the City’s general fund to act as the collection of franchise and property tax. In order to
comply with state law requirements, the public hearing on the proposed transfers was scheduled for June 21, 2022.

Council Deputy Director Symes summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound upon
the purpose of the transfer. Other utilities like Dominion Energy and Rocky Mountain Power pay franchise tax and property tax to
the City; the intent is to operate City utilities as separate utilities and the transfer is intended to act as the City’s collection of those
franchise and property taxes equal to six percent for franchise tax and six percent for property tax. The proposed transfer for
FY 2023 is $6 million maximum. There is no action to be taken after the public hearing; the transfers are incorporated into the
proposed budget and approval or denial for the transfer will occur with adoption of the final FY2023 budget. The City will send
follow-up notices to residents once the budget is adopted.

Comptroller Stout presented a chart illustrating the amount of each utility fund transfer: the City has made a wise choice
in defining the total revenue percentage that will make up the annual transfer. She concluded that if the City were to eliminate the
practice of assessing taxes to each City utility, it would be necessary to raise property taxes by 37.6 percent to receive the same
amount of revenue.

Acting Chair Blair then called for a public hearing on the proposed enterprise fund transfer.

The Chief Deputy City Recorder presented written notice of said hearing and affidavit of posting on the 8th day of June
2022, specifying the time and place of this meeting as the time and place when and where the proposed resolution would be given
a public hearing and be considered for final passage. Notice was also given to residents through their Public Utility billing.

Angel Castillo stated that Mr. Symes did a great job of explaining the purpose and importance of the enterprise fund
transfer. She suggested that Council staff create an additional illustration or video that can be shared with the public to explain the

A memo from Community and Economic Development Department came before the Council to consider a resolution
awarding arts grants for Fiscal Year (FY) 2022-2023. The memo stated each year, the Ogden City Council appropriates grant
funding to support various local arts organizations and programs. The Ogden Arts Advisory Committee and Administration
review each application and provide recommendations to the Council. The FY2023 budget includes $150,000, which is $50,000
higher than last year.

Arts Coordinator Buckley summarized the memo and stated $150,000 has been allocated for arts grants in FY23 and the
City Council’s role in the grant process is to make the determination that the value of the projects and programming that the public
receives from the proposed arts grants are equal to or exceed the monetary value of the grants. She noted that 24 grant application
were received, and the Arts Grants Review Sub-Committee recommended 21 grant applications merit funding; this is broken
down as six general support grants and 15 project support grants. Total funding in FY23 is $139,989. Grants are reviewed with a
rigorous scoring process including the following:

e¢ Quality of the artistic mission and programs offered

e Number of creatives and community members served

e Access for underserved populations. Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion
e Applicant shows a strong ability to implement project

e Applicant shows a strong marketing plan and a solid budget

e Project aligns with goals and objectives from the Arts Master Plan.

Proposed Ordinance 2022-23 adopting the 2023-2027 Capital Improvement
Plan for major capital investments in the City

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the Fiscal Year (FY) 2023-2027
Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the CIP is reviewed and adopted
each year as a planning tool for major capital investments in the city’s physical plant over a rolling five-year period. Each year’s
plan is updated based on the changing needs of the City, funding opportunities, projects completed and other similar factors.
During this year’s budget process, proposed capital projects will be reviewed alongside each department’s proposed budget and in
context with the overall needs and resources available to the city through the budget process.

Council Deputy Director Symes summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
background and objectives of the City’s CIP. Funding sources for the CIP include General Fund, Federal and State Grants, B&C
Road Funds and Active Transportation Funds, Enterprise Funds, Bonds, and other miscellaneous grant and partnership funding.
He provided an overview of the FY2023-2027 CIP, which includes 58 total projects, 17 new projects, and 22 amended projects.
Total funding proposed for the five-year Plan period is $399,710,405. He provided a breakdown of the projects by
Fund/Department and highlighted three major projects included in the Plan:

e Marshall White Center (MWC) Project - $23 million
e 36” Canyon Waterline Project - $80 million
e Downtown Parking Lots Project - $34 million
