SUMMARY AND BACKGROUND
This application is a request for approval of a site plan for approximately 2.38 acres located
on the east side of 700 East, at approximately 11757 S. 700 East (Exhibits B and C). The
property is currently zoned CN. The applicant is requesting that a site plan be approved to
allow for the development of the currently vacant site with an office building.

The property was rezoned in 2020 with the intent of building an office building on site. The
building was never built and in 2023 there was a request to amend the land use and zoning
maps in order to allow for a self-storage facility. That application was withdrawn before
the City Council hearing. The property was then sold and the new owner is proposing to
build a two-story office building with this application.

The applicant has provided a site layout that considers the residential areas of the south
and east. The building is located closer to the north property line where it abuts other
commercial zoning. A landscaped buffer has been provided along the southern and
eastern property lines with the required retention basin located in the southeast corner of
the site. The project has one access point from 700 East near the southern boundary of
the property. The applicant has received approval from the Utah Department of
Transportation (UDOT) for the access as that portion of 700 East is controlled by UDOT.

Utility equipment will primarily be placed on the roof and will be screened by a parapet
wall. There will be some ground mounted utility equipment adjacent to the north property
line. The applicant has designed a portion of the building to screen and enclose this
equipment. The trash enclosure is on the east side of the building and is not visible from
the street. The landscape plans included the required landscape plantings around the
trash enclosure.

To lessen the impact on the neighborhoods to the south and east an existing six-foot
decorative masonry wall on the south will remain and a new wall architecturally similar to
the wall on the south will be constructed along the east property line.

DCMC 9-23-110(A)
A. Land Use Buffer Requirements: The amount of landscaping and type of fencing
   required for a land use buffer shall be as follows:
   1. A landscape buffer of twenty feet (20') shall be provided between all commercial
      and office uses when located adjacent to a residential zone and when a multi-family
      use is located adjacent to a single-family zone.
   3. Trees within the buffer area described in 9-23-110(A)(1) and (2) above shall be
      planted at one tree per four hundred (400) square feet of buffer area.
      a. Trees may be grouped.
      b. At least one buffer tree shall be planted between commercial, office, multi-family
          and industrial uses and each single-family residential lot. Trees shall be arranged
          to provide linear coverage between commercial, office and industrial uses and
          adjacent residential units.

The landscape architect indicated in their calculations that a land use buffer varies from
twenty-seven feet (27’) to thirty-eight feet (38’) in width along the south and east property
boundaries. This area has a total square footage of approximately 26,900. If there is one
tree planted for every 400 square feet of the buffer area, a total of 68 trees are required.
The applicant has shown 34 trees within this area. The applicant will need to show the
additional trees in the landscape buffer area on the south and east sides of the property.
The remainder of the land use buffer areas will be planted with ground covers, shrubs, and
planting beds.

Trees along the frontage are provided, however the proposed park strip will be less than
five feet (5’) in width. Trees are not to be planted in park strips less than five feet wide.
Street trees will need to be relocated on revised plans to be shown as being planted on the
applicants property and within fifteen feet (15’) of the front property line to qualify as the
required street trees per the landscape ordinance.

Staff has included in the conditions for approval that new revised landscape and irrigation
plans be submitted at the time of the building permit for review and approval by planning
staff that show the additional buffer trees and updated street trees along 700 East.

Outdoor amenities are required for buildings that are 20,000 square feet or more. One of
the amenities that can be included are seating areas including tables and chairs. The
applicant has chosen to provide this along the rear or eastern side of the building. A
second amenity of natural open space has been included in this area also.

Lighting. The lighting plan (Exhibit I) shows how new lighting fixtures will illuminate the site
during business hours. After hours, the lighting will be dimmed per the after-hours lighting
plan also provided in Exhibit I. Most of the proposed lighting meets the standards of DCMC
Section 9-20. However, the proposed plans exceed the maximum number of light poles
allowed for the site. A total of 10 are allowed and there are 12 proposed. Staff
recommends that as a condition of approval revised lighting plans, in compliance with
DCMC Section 9-20 for outdoor lighting design and illumination standards, be provided to
staff for review and approval prior to receiving land use approval for a building permit. The
revised plans would be required to reduce the number of light poles, and require an
additional reduction in the number of exterior lighting sources in the after-hours plan by
1/3 of the number included on the business hours lighting plan. Additional modifications

reduction in the number of light poles, wanted to propose an after-hours site plan with

The criteria for review and potential approval of a Site Plan request is found in Sections 9-5-
090(E) of the DCMC. This section depicts the standard of review for such requests as:

E. Standards For Approval: The following standards shall apply to the approval of a site
   plan:
   1. The entire site shall be developed at one time unless a phased development plan is
      approved.
   2. A site plan shall conform to applicable standards set forth in this title, including but
      not limited to, building heights, setbacks, access points, parking, landscaping, and
      building materials.
   3. The proposed development plans meet the intent, goals, and objectives of the
      general plan and the purpose of the zone district in which the site is located.

      development, as required by engineering standards and specifications.

    better in Auto Sprinkler Layout. There needs to be a hydrant within a 100’ of
    the FDC. FDC is required to have KNOX Locking Caps. ALL FIRE PROTECTION
    PLANS REQUIRE 3rd PARTY REVIEW PRIOR TO BE SUBMITTED TO THE DRAPER
    FIRE DEPARTMENT.
11. Fire Department Connection (FDC): The FDC is required as part of a water-
    based suppression system as the auxiliary water supply. The FDC will give the
    fire department the capability to supply additional pressure necessary to the
    automatic fire sprinkler system and the standpipe system. The FDC will be
    located at the entrance to the building and will be within 100’ of the fire
    hydrant. The FDC will be located to ensure connected hoses which supply the
    system will not obstruct access to the buildings for other fire apparatus. The
    final location of FDC will be approved by the Fire Marshal.
12. Fire Alarm Required. A deferred submittal for fire alarm shop drawings are to
    be sent via email to the following to: Don Buckley at
    fire.permits@draperutah.gov. A complete set of plans, with manufacturer cut
    sheets, and battery calculations. Plans must be stamped by a NICET level III or
    better in Fire Alarm Systems. ALL FIRE ALARM PLANS REQUIRE 3rd PARTY
    REVIEW PRIOR TO BE SUBMITTED TO THE DRAPER FIRE DEPARTMENT.
13. Knox Boxes Required. Fire Department “Knox Brand” lock box to be mounted
    to exterior walls, near the main entrance and/or nearest the door serving the
    exterior access to the fire sprinkler riser room at a height of 5 feet to the top of
    the box. Lock box purchase can be arranged by the General Contractor.
14. 2A-10BC Fire Extinguishers required. The extinguisher needs to be a
    serviceable type meaning metal head and metal neck. Extinguishers need to
    be located in a conspicuous location where they will be readily accessible and
    immediately available for use. Placed every 75’ of travel. If in cabinet or not
    the extinguisher or cabinet needs to be mounted so that the top is not more
    than 5’ above the floor.
15. Portable Fire Extinguishers. Not less than one approved portable fire
    extinguisher in accordance with IFC Section 906 and sized for not less than
    ordinary hazard as follows:
              (1) At each stairway on all floor levels where combustible materials have
                   accumulated.
               (2) In every storage and construction shed.
               (3) Additional portable fire extinguishers shall be provided where special
                   hazards exist including, but not limited to , the storage and use of
                   flammable and combustible liquids.
   16. Visible Addressing Required. New and existing buildings shall have approved
