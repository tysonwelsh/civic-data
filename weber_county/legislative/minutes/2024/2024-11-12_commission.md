D. THOUGHT OF THE DAY: Commissioner Bolos: One of my favorite things to think about is the sacrifices that
   these men and women make for their families and that their families make while their loved ones serve. I have
   my father-in-law who was serving in Vietnam and didn't meet his oldest daughter until she was about six months
   old. He actually thought it was a son because the mail was so slow and he just expected it would be a son; he
   started calling her Gus but she turned out to be a Patty. My two grandpas were both serving in the war when
   their oldest were born. My mom didn't meet her dad till she was about 9 months old and my dad didn't meet his
   dad till he was two and a half. One of my favorite pictures of my grandpa Arrington is when he was at the war
   and he had fallen asleep on a couch and he was holding a picture of his wife and my dad as a little boy; and I
   think, “You know, these men and women sacrifice so much and their families give so much.” I just want to
   express my gratitude for that. Thank you so much. Thank you Legionnaire for doing the Honor Guard today. I
   really appreciate your service today and what you've done in the past. Thank you so much.

    2. APPROVAL TO ABATE TAXES FOR DIFFERENCE IN WHAT WAS PAID TO OWING, PUT
       BACK ON GREENBELT AND ABATE ROLLBACK TAXES, FOR YEARS 2020-2024, OWNER
       DIXIE BUNOT, PARCEL NUMBERS:
                             10-020-0001 10-020-0019
                             10-020-002  10-020-0027
                             10-020-003  10-020-0030
                             10-020-004  10-020-0031
                             10-020-0010 10-020-0032
                             10-020-0011

         DeeDee Kimber(Assessor’s Office): We received an email from Dixie Bunot requesting help due to family
         difficulties she had in 2019, 2020, and, 2021. We presented her request to the Tax Review Committee; all
         parcels had a roll back tax charged in either 2020 or 2021 to roll them out of Green Belt status. She is
         requesting for that change to be waived due to the land still being in production. She did pay the taxes in an
         amount in her best interest of what she thought it would have been if they stayed in Green Belt. The County
         did a random audit in 2019 that captured the first set of parcels; the second set were discovered due to
         ownership changes on some of the parcels. The County sent notices via certified letters with no response.
         She did speak with Angela (Assessor’s Office) twice and to our attorney once. She applied last week to put
         the parcels back in Green Belt which is active for 2025. The Tax Review Committee recommendation, due
         to Utah Code 59-2-1347.1(a), where in the best human interest is served, is to abate the taxes and the roll
         back from 2020 and 2021. The balance owed will be $64,830.93. This will abate $79,908.46, which
         includes the roll back and tax difference from 2020 and 2021.

         Commissioner Froerer: Why did these parcels fall out of greenbelt originally?

         Angela Hill (Assessor’s Office): The parcel was audited in 2019 at which point we like a two year history
         either in the form of farm receipts or a schedule F for farming. At that point we send out two roll back
         notices, one of which is sent certified. No response was received and parcels were removed from Green Belt.
         For 2020, there was an ownership change. Two notices for application were sent followed by two notices for
         roll back since no response was received to the application notices. The owner had 120 days to respond and
         no response was received.

         Dixie Bunot: I just wanted to address in 2019 and 2020 I did receive notices. I was the caretaker of my mother-
         in-law, my time wasn’t mine. By the time I had my head on straight, we owed over $100k. The land was
         never out of greenbelt. Davis County did not roll back greenbelt. I did the worst thing I could have done
         which was nothing. I got in touch with John Ulibarri who said I could go before the Tax Review Committee
         and then here with the Commission. I am trying to make it straight. I just hope the green belt purpose can
         override the non-primary accounting.

         Commissioner Bolos: The abatement amount was for which years?
         Ms. Kimber: That is for 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, and 2024. 2022, 2023, and 2024 are not assessed at Green
         Belt but at market rate.
         Chair Harvey: I wouldn’t mind sitting on this for one week.
         Commissioner Bolos: I would prefer to have a conversation with you, Commissioners, about the balance.

         Commissioner Froerer: I just want to verify the ground was ever taken out of production or not?
         Ms. Kimber: No.
         Commissioner Froerer: So the ground was always in production which qualifies for Green Belt. I think we
         have the ability to look into this a little deeper. In my opinion this was not a case when someone tried to go
         outside the statue. I am happy to wait a week.
         Commissioner Bolos moved to table this item time certain for one week; Commissioner Froerer seconded.
         Chair Harvey – aye; Commissioner Bolos – aye; Commissioner Froerer – aye

H. COMMISSIONER COMMENTS:
   1. Chair Harvey: I was in a situation here not long ago where a friend of mine who I've known since high
      school came to me and said his mind is starting to fail. I've known some things about him in his life.
      Because he's starting to fail, he come up he said, “I want you to know when I was out of high school a girl
      and I did some things we shouldn't have done.” This is back In 1980 this is 44 years ago. He said, “Instead
      of having her go to her parents, I thought the man thing for me to do would be for me to go talk to her dad.”
      That's how he chose to handle it. He went and talked to her dad who was an old military disciplinarian,
      really a sports guy. Instead of getting angry, the dad said, “We'll work this out. Things will be okay.” This
      now son-in-law turned from bitterness and fear to love admiration and appreciation. It helped shape his life
      for the rest of his life. He was kind of a wild hellion and now he's a giver; he gives back in every area of his
      life. He spends his life giving and being kind. Just today, I love the sacrifice that we saw firsthand from the
      American Legion Post 9 posting the colors. And I was moved by someone who comes up and takes
      responsibility for their actions even when they didn't do the right thing. There's a lot of power in taking
      responsibility for one's action I sure think it's good. That's a wonderful example for the next generation.
