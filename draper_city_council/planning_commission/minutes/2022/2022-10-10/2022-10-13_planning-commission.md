2. Public Hearing: Beacon Crest Cottages Landscape Deviation (Administrative Action)
On the request of David Meredith, representing Beacon Crest of Draper, LLC and Beacon Crest
Heritage, LLC a request for approval of a Landscape Deviation from the requirement to provide a
minimum of 10 feet (10’) of landscaping around the property perimeter, related to potential future
expansion of the Beacon Crest Assisted Living Facility onto an adjacent property. The combined
properties are located at 12374, 12382, & 12392 S. 600 E. Known as application VAR-185-2022,
Staff contact: Todd A. Draper, (801) 576-6335, todd.draper@draperutah.gov.

Mr. Todd Draper, Planner III presented the project. This is a request for landscape deviation. Three
individual properties make up the site. The discussion opened and the question of clarification was
asked by Vice Chairman Hawker if there would be other potential deviations that would come
once the site plan is drawn up and submitted. Mr. Todd Draper informed the Commissioners there
could potentially be more deviations that come up when the site plan is submitted. The applicant
thought it best to request this deviation first. Mr. Todd Draper noted this request was for the
applicant to change the ten-foot landscaping around the property and do less. The request included
the provision of 21.7% overall landscaping.

A question was asked if the existing site was in compliance with the parking rules of the city. Ms.
Jennifer Jastremsky stated the site was in compliance with parking. Mr. Todd Draper shared in
other similar projects the site plan will exceed parking to take into consideration, of employees,
and patrons visiting.

Mr. David Meredith managing partner of Beacon Crest, purchased property to the north to build a
bigger area for senior living and a place where the senior community can be there and utilize the
entities but not necessarily need to have nurses visiting. The water table is too high, in that spot
and the worry was flooding in basements. They decided to raise the building and parking up to
have more safety. Deviation is more for safety. Between properties there would be slight incline.

Vice Chairman Hawker asked if there would be any seniors at any time walking over to Beacon
Crest, and if so, would there be a ramp. Mr. David Meredith stated in the final plan he plans to
have a ramp between the properties rather than stairs. Ramps are safer for seniors, than stairs.

Vice Chairman Hawker opened the public hearing.
No public comments were made.
Vice Chairman Hawker closed the public hearing.

Motion: Commissioner Susan Nixon made a motion to approve the landscape deviation as
requested by David Meredith, representing Beacon Crest of Draper, LLC and Beacon Crest
Heritage, LLC. Application VAR-185-2022. Based on the findings and subject to the
conditions listed in the staff report dated October 3, 2022.

Second: Commissioner Tab Bingham

Vote: A roll call was taken with the Commissioners voting 5-0 in favor of the motion.
Fowler, Tonks, Bingham, Nixon and Ogden voted, “Aye”.

7. Training: Origins of American City Planning Part 2
Fred Aegerter, Community Development Director, provided the training for this meeting.

Civic improvement and planning became important during the progressive era. Civic Leagues were
found in 1894. The leagues promoted professional management of local government. They wanted
to have a hands-on mayor and nonpartisan elections, looking to get away from politics. Some
examples of Civic improvements league results include the 1914 Salt Lake City Civic Arts and
Planning Commission establishment.




                                                5
The next few things we see happening in planning are grand plans and standard enabling acts. The
Burnham's plan for Chicago 1909 advocated fora different type of lake front, where there are parks
to draw people to visits rather than railways

In 1928 there was the Standard City Planning Enabling Act, which allowed cities to establish
zoning laws.

The New York City Zoning ordinance was adopted in 1916 and created height and setback
standards for buildings allowing them to have a pyramid roofing and floor system to providing the
sunlight to come down in the cities.

Village of Euclid v. Ambler Realty in 1926 established that zoning did not infringe on rights. The
last part is the planning process looks at topical, geographic and implementation lists. It involves
creating master development plans that are more specific. The implementation of planning had
much to do with the annual budget, and from there would turn the words into actions of what each
city could do depending on budgets. Which is very much like today.
