1.    HOME OCCUPATION, STEPHEN SOTER IS REQUESTING A CONDITIONAL USE
      PERMIT FOR A HOME OCCUPATION, “SIDE YARD”, TO BE LOCATED AT 1201
      WEST SUN RIVER DRIVE.

Zoning Administrator, Tim Prestwich, presented the staff report and stated that the above
request is for a Home Occupation Conditional Use Permit for a side yard located at 1201
West Sun River Drive. The property is 0.70 acres in size and in the RR-22 (Rural
Residential) Zone. Surrounding properties are also in the RR-22 (Rural Residential) Zone.
The General Plan for the area is Estate Density Residential, which fits in with the current
zoning. The applicant, Stephen Soter, has requested a Conditional Use Home Occupation
Permit for a business where he plans to store trailers outside of his home in a fenced-in
area. Mr. Soter expects less than one visitor per day and visitors will come by appointment.
The business plan was modified to meet the standards and qualifications of the Home
Occupation Ordinance. There will be no trailers longer than 24 feet and visitors will come
only between the hours of 7:00 a.m. and 7:00 p.m. There were no other home occupations
on site. Mr. Soter made an effort to read the ordinance and conform the business to the
rules and regulations.

The proposed storage area is approximately 39 feet wide, 143 feet deep, and is surrounded
by fencing on nearly all sides. There is a section near a shed where the fencing opens up
into the backyard. The stored trailers are out of the way but there is minimal visibility from
the street. Mr. Prestwich reported that a neighbor complained about a business being run
without a license. Ordinance Enforcement Officers spoke to Mr. Soter, who is now taking
steps to obtain the necessary permit.

Mr. Prestwich reported that when a Home Occupation request is received by staff, it is
evaluated based on several criteria including the following:

   •   Is the use prohibited in the list of prohibited uses?
   •   Can it be done in a way that follows all fixed standards?
   •   Can staff approve the request or does it need to go to the Planning Commission for
       approval?
   •   Is the proposed use listed as a conditional use?
   •   Is the proposed use compatible with the neighborhood?

Mr. Prestwich read from Section 18.190.090 of the Home Occupations Ordinance. Listed
under prohibited home occupations were the following:

   •   Storage;
   •   Service;
   •   Repair;
   •   Sales or rental of ambulance;
   •   Tow truck;
   •   Recreational vehicle;
   •   Watercraft;
   •   Automobiles;
   •   ATV; or
   •   Other motorized vehicles.

Mr. Prestwich felt that the question was whether trailers fall into the category of prohibited
uses or if only motorized vehicles are considered prohibited. Deputy City Attorney, Craig
Bott stated that an analysis was conducted on the ordinance. He believed the ordinance
refers only to motorized vehicles and not to unmotorized vehicles. Mr. Prestwich noted that
the applicant was not present. Several public comments were submitted and provided in
the Planning Commission packet.

Commissioner James felt it was important to be respectful of what had been approved in the
past. He believed this was the first time the Planning Commission had seen a Home
Occupation request that did not include a building such as a garage or the inside of a
residential home. Commissioner James commented that flatbed trailers parked for storage
raise some concerns. He asked Mr. Prestwich if he wanted the Planning Commission to
determine whether a flatbed trailer should be permitted or if it should be added to the list of
prohibited vehicles. Mr. Prestwich stated that the Planning Commission could not alter the

prohibited list because it would require an ordinance change. He stated that the purpose of
the Home Occupations Ordinance is to:

   •   Provide an opportunity for home occupations as an accessory use, when they are
       compatible with the neighborhoods in which they are located;
   •   Guide business activities that are not compatible with neighborhoods to appropriate
       commercial zones; and
   •   Safeguard peace, quiet, and domestic tranquility within all residential neighborhoods.

Commissioner Hansen asked why the Legal Department felt that the ordinance relates only
to motorized vehicles. Mr. Bott explained that this was due to the way the ordinance was
worded. It included the phrase, “or other motorized vehicles.” This indicates that the
ordinance describes only motorized vehicles. Commissioner Hansen believed this was a
flaw in the way the ordinance was written. Commissioner Rushton respected the
assessment from the Legal Department but felt that the ordinance language needed to be
cleaned up. He believed the intent was clear and that it included non-motorized vehicles as
well.

Commissioner Lefgren brought up previous discussions related to Airbnb rentals and stated
that storage falls under the same category. He did not like the idea of people coming in and
out of a residential area with trailers and noted that zoning is already in place for storage
areas. Commissioner Lefgren believed that if people are coming and going regularly, it
would create similar issues as an Airbnb rental.

Chair Cannon opened the public hearing.

Rusty McBride reported that he lives just south of Mr. Soter’s residence. When he walks
out his back door, the trailers on Mr. Soter’s property are visible. Mr. McBride noted that
there are over 20 children in the neighborhood that are outside regularly. Since the trailer
storage began, there had been an increase in traffic. Mr. McBride shared concerns about
the safety of the neighborhood and the children who live there. He was also concerned
about the height of the trailers and noted that fifth-wheel trailers can be as tall as 14-feet.
Mr. McBride felt that since the ordinance specifies, “storage, service, repair….” all storage
will be included on the list of prohibited uses. He believed that outside yard storage of trailers
did not make sense for the residential area and that the application should be denied.

Charlene McBride stated that there had already been noise pollution and dust due to trailers
being backed into the yard. She stated that she was unable to stay outside during those
times.

Mr. Prestwich noted that there had been seven additional public comments objecting to the
Home Occupation Conditional Use Permit.

There were no further public comments. Chair Cannon closed the public hearing.

•     DISCUSSION ITEMS

1.    REVIEW OF CURRENT ORDINANCES FOR LANDSCAPING REQUIREMENTS

      a.     RCC 18.135.080

Mr. Prestwich reported that it is important to review City Ordinances periodically. According
to the Front Yard Landscaping Ordinance whenever a front yard is required by this title, said
yard shall be planted and maintained in the form of lawn, trees, or other plantings, or
landscape features, except for areas covered by walls, driveways, and structures. Such
landscaping shall be completed within one year of the date of occupancy.

Mr. Prestwich felt that the ordinance gives people a lot of freedom. He believed that the
Secondary Water Metering Project would inform residents about their water usage and
encourage extreme water users to alter their habits. There was, however, nothing currently
in the ordinance to offer direction. Mr. Prestwich was looking for feedback and guidance
from the Planning Commission about potential updates or changes that could be made to
the Landscaping Requirements.

Commissioner Rushton previously shared information with the Planning Commission from
Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District along with a sample ordinance. He wondered if
communities could have more on-site parking if there were fewer requirements for grass and
landscaping in developments.         The Commission Members felt that more relaxed
landscaping requirements would encourage developers to construct in additional units
instead. Chair Cannon stated that the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District made a
presentation a year and a half ago related to water conservation and xeriscaped yards. He
believed it was important to look into the issue of water consumption and to tighten up
regulations related to rear and side yards.

Mr. Prestwich noted that Riverton City was entering into an interesting phase. Previously,
the main focus had been on new development but now things were shifting toward
maintenance. Mr. Prestwich felt that the Maintenance ordinance section needed additions.
He reported that staff often receives calls from residents seeking guidance on xeriscaping
or various landscaping options. More guidance from the City could help residents make
positive changes.

Commissioner James suggested looking at relevant research related to low water use and
potentially adopting something as a standard. Commissioner Rushton reported that he had
reached out to both Utah State University and Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District for
more information. The Conversation Programs Manager at Jordan Valley Water
Conservancy District, Courtney Brown, recognized that the Secondary Water Metering
Project would help residents become more aware of their water usage.

Commissioner Hansen felt that if the City dictates what residents can and cannot do with
their landscaping, it would remove variation and variety.         Commissioner Hartley
recommended the Localscapes website as a resource to minimize irrigation and promote
better usage of landscaping materials. Commissioner Rushton suggested forming an entity
focused on helping residents plan new landscaping or redo existing landscaped areas.
Commissioner Hansen asked if this would be enforced or if those with existing landscaped
areas would be grandfathered in. Commissioner Rushton did not believe the entity would
pay for yards to be redone but it could be a useful way for landscaped areas to be
coordinated and consistent without looking identical.

      b.     RCC 18.215.030 (3)

Mr. Prestwich reported that commercial landscaping ordinances have tighter guidelines but
still offer a lot of freedom. According to the Commercial Landscaping Ordinance: all yards
or lots in commercial or manufacturing zones shall be planted and maintained in lawns,
trees, or other plantings or landscaped features, except for yard areas covered by walls,
driveways, parking lots and structures, and working areas. Such landscaping shall be done
within one year from the date of occupancy of any building.

The benefit of commercial landscaping is that when a site plan is approved by the Planning
Commission, the site plan has already been designed by a landscape architect.
Mr. Prestwich noted that normally the Landscape Architect includes areas for conservation
and mixed ground cover, rather than just sod.

Mr. Prestwich discussed Park Strip Landscaping, which specifies that the area of the street
right-of-way between the curb line, or the proposed curb lines as established by the City
street standard, and the property line (park strip) shall remain free of weeds and maintained
by the abutting property owner and be landscaped with sod, shrubs, trees, ground cover,
bark mulch, and decorative rock cobble. Materials that minimize water use are encouraged.

Commissioner Hansen believed there should be guidelines related to the park strip in the
City right-of-way. He felt there should be guidance to reduce the growth of weeds and make
it attractive in the City street.
