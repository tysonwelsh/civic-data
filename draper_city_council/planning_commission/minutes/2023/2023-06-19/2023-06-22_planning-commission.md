1.     Public Hearing: X Development Office Building Site Plan and Deviation (Administrative
       Action)
       On the request of Glen Goins, representing Steve Hutchings for a Site Plan and Deviation
       request for a new office building on 0.99 acres of property located at 699 East Pioneer Road.
       Applications SPR-016-2023 and VAR-064-2023. Staff contact is Tevai Haunga, 801-576-
       6522, tevai.haunga@draperutah.gov.

City Planner, Tevai Haunga presented the Staff Report and stated that the request is for the
X Development Office Building Site Plan and Deviation. The subject property is located on the
corner of 700 East and Pioneer Road and is currently zoned Neighborhood Commercial (“CN”). The
proposed office building sits on a 0.99-acre site. The proposed use will be a 19,453-square-foot two-
story office building. There will be two access points with one off 700 East and another off Pioneer
Road. A Development Agreement is associated with the property. The Site Plan Design and parking
requirements have been met. There are 71 parking stalls proposed, which comply with the ordinance.

The proposed landscaped area of 20% and the interior parking landscaped area of 8% comply with
the minimum landscape requirements. The proposed design style, materials, and heights all meet the
Code requirements and the Development Agreement. Eighty percent brick is proposed on each façade
and 50% transparency on each façade. The applicant has applied for two deviations, one for cross-
access and the other for landscaping. For the cross-access deviation, the Code requires cross-access
between non-residential and mixed-use developments. The applicant is requesting a deviation for
cross access to the north property, adjacent to another commercial property, stating that existing site
plan improvements on the other property physically impede the ability to create cross access.

The applicant is also requesting a reduced perimeter landscape from 10 feet to 6.5 feet. They claim
that the adjacent commercial property to the north contains a 24-foot buffer that also serves as a storm
drain easement, which creates a 30-foot landscaped area between both properties. Ms. Haunga
reported that she received one email asking for a negative recommendation for the proposed office
building. The resident claimed that there are already too many commercial spaces around this
residential area. That was the only public comment received.

A Commissioner recalled that they denied a development plan on this site previously. Community
Development Director, Jennifer Jastremsky, stated that there was a previous rezone request for CN to
build a residential facility for individuals with disabilities. The neighborhood was opposed to the
proposal and the potential buyer went away. The current applicant has since acquired the property
and successfully had the zoning changed to CN. A Development Agreement was also approved as
part of the project. With that agreement, the City Council required that they comply with the
architectural standards in the Town Center (“TC”) zone since they are adjacent to Town Center.

Chair Adams asked why cross-access requirements are included in the Code. Ms. Jastremsky
explained that the purpose of cross-access is to reduce the number of street entrances by having shared
access points into adjacent commercial properties. It also provides access between adjacent
businesses to eliminate traffic on the streets. It allows for emergency service access as well. The
Code acknowledges that it does not work in every situation. In this case, the cross-access area with
the adjacent property is a large detention pond. The applicant is asking to do less landscaping because
of the retention pond to the north.

Commissioner Hawker asked if there will be any site problems with the trees getting too large. Senior
Engineer Manager Brien Maxfield, stated that some trimming may be required but eventually, the
trees will be tall enough that there will be a line of sight beneath them.

A question was raised regarding access for emergency vehicles as it is difficult to tell if there is
enough access to get through the egress on each side. Mr. Maxfield explained that the accesses meet
the Code for width and spacing. The access on 700 East on the northeast corner is a right-in right-
out but will only be signed because the fire trucks have to swing so wide through there. There will
be no pork chops or concrete barriers there. It will be sign-controlled because of the stacking. He
noted that the Fire Department signed off on the Site Plan.

The applicant, Glen Goins from X Development was present representing the property owners. With
respect to the question raised, he stated that the Fire Department signed off on the entrance but
indicated that they do not plan to use it and will service the area from Pioneer Road or 700 East.
Therefore, it is not a consideration. They did have to relocate some trees near the southwest corner
to allow the Fire Department to have room to get to the corner. He stated that the current rendition is
the result of collaboration with staff.

Ms. Jastremsky reported that the property was zoned CN a few years ago, which is the least intense
commercial zone. It is designed to be closer to residential neighborhoods as compared to other
commercial zones. The CN zone allows an array of commercial uses, such as offices and retail. They
are now in the Site Plan phase and the applicant has requested to develop the property under the CN
zoning regulations. With the application, the City's standards and requirements are to verify that the
proposal complies with the Zoning Code regulations. If it does, it has to be approved. In this instance,
it is a two-story office building although they could potentially build a three-story office building in
the CN zone. The request was found to comply with the parking and architectural standards. There
are a few deviations and it is up to the Planning Commission to evaluate the standards and determine
whether the applicant meets them. If the Planning Commission cannot find that they have met those
standards, they have to deny the deviation. In that case, she recommended they continue the Site Plan
and allow the applicant to amend it to comply with the Code. If the deviations are approved, the Site
Plan can be approved.

Ms. Jastremsky reported that a few years ago the cross-access standards were modified to provide
better access through properties. Specific standards were created regarding when a cross-access may
not be desirable or feasible. Mr. Maxfield stated that Cubes Self Storage met the cross-access
requirements. At the time they came in, the subject site was residential, which did not require cross-
access to the subject property. Cubes has cross-access to both the bank and KFC and it was noted
that KFC has cross-access to the gas station. Chair Adams commented that once the City Council
changed the zoning for a property from residential there was a new list of rules they needed to meet,
and they meet all but three of them which are the deviations they are requesting.

Commissioner Hawker commented that when looking at the Development Agreement they are
improving the exterior, which is very attractive and has only two stores. With an office use, it was
not expected that they will have to deal with traffic issues. He expressed support. In looking at the
deviations, the Planning Commission should determine if they are being used. He saw no reason for
cross-access or landscaping there.

Commissioner Fowler made a comment that when the zone question came up a few years ago and

2.     Discussion Item: 2023 Moderate-Income Housing Report (Work Session).
       Presentation and work session regarding the annual Moderate Income Housing Plan and
       required reporting to the State. Staff Contact: Todd A. Draper, todd.draper@draperutah.gov,
       (801) 576-6335.

Mr. Draper presented the item and reported that every year the State Legislature changes something
regarding the Moderate-Income Housing Plan, the reporting, or the General Plan. This year’s report
includes what the city has done with the plan that was just adopted late last year. The change to the
State law this year involves reporting on all of last year's changes and taking tangible action on them
this reporting period. The report is due on August 1.

Mr. Draper reviewed the following potential responses as follows:

   •   Strategy F – Zone or rezone for higher-density or moderate-income residential development
       in commercial or mixed-use zones in major transit investment corridors, commercial centers,
       or employment centers.

Staff anticipated that Strategy F would be a few years out. The Town Center Station Area Plan is
underway with the intent being that it would be accomplished by implementing portions of that plan.
Staff will report that the plan is underway and that action is being taken. Staff will also conduct
additional research and document that.

Ms. Jastremsky commented that as the Station Area Plan for Draper Town Center is underway and
discussed how they will address the zoning after its completion. By State law, the city will have to
change the Zoning for the Station Area Plans to develop. In response to a question raised by Chair
Adams, Ms. Jastremsky stated that there are housing components in the plan. Currently, the steering
committee is looking at putting together a Preferred Plan. An Open House was held recently with
four alternatives that public feedback was received on. Those comments were now being used to
formulate a Preferred Plan. She stressed that there will be housing on the Utah Transit Authority
(“UTA”) property among others.

A question was raised about how other cities are approaching the issue. Ms. Jastremsky had not
looked at the Moderate-Income Housing Plans for other cities but stated that any city with a
FrontRunner or TRAX station is required to do a Station Area Plan.

A Commissioner asked if there was anything else the Commission should be doing to create forward
momentum or tangible action on this strategy. Chair Adams did not know enough about how
moderate-income housing can work. He recalled the staggering number showing that the median
income in Salt Lake County is $60,000. Being in the real estate industry, he recognized that those
earning less than $100,000 per year make buying housing difficult. Ms. Jastremsky stated that there
are various mechanisms for affordable housing. For a smaller city like Draper, they may consider
reducing impact fees for affordable units to reduce the actual development costs for developers.
Larger cities like Salt Lake City have funds to develop housing and provide assistance.

Chair Adams asked how they can force developers to sell at a fair market price. Ms. Jastremsky
stated that rentals are typically Housing of Urban Development (“HUD”) approved, income-restricted
units. This requires them to apply with HUD every year and comply. With for-sale units, there are
deed restrictions that limit the increase in sales price each year so that it remains affordable.
Commissioner Nixon reported that in Murray there is a Down Payment Assistance Program where
the City contributes. The owner is required to live in the home for a minimum of five to 10 years.
Once that is accomplished, the grant is forgiven. Mr. Draper stated that it was a strategy that was not
selected, but could be added to the Moderate Income Housing Plan in the future.

Mr. Draper reported that the difference between Strategies F and G was that one changes the zone
and the other changes the regulations. The Third District Court recently clarified that a zoning map
is not a land use regulation. Staff will evaluate various options.

   •   Strategy J – Implement zoning incentives for moderate-income units in new developments.

This would be akin to bonus density. Staff would like to solicit input from developers to see what
they think might have the most impact. Chair Adams thought this was a great idea. He was concerned
about units being sold. If buyers are restricted to something like only 1% appreciation it takes the
incentive away. He felt that the benefit still goes to the developer rather than the homeowner. Mr.
Draper stated that the City has similar concerns and they want to make sure that things are balanced.
Additional issues regarding housing affordability were discussed.

The comment was made that developers need to be incentivized but she liked things that benefit
homebuyers and commented that appreciation limits do not benefit homeowners. Chair Adams
commented that an owner-occupant does not necessarily equate to a low-income buyer. Appreciation
is the most important and not just for the first buyer. Chair Adams pointed out that those earning
more than $20,000 per year cannot obtain the grant and someone at that income level will not qualify
for a home. He urged the City to come up with a grant that will work. The need for balance was
stressed.

Chair Adams asked if any moderate-income housing units are found in the City. Ms. Jastremsky
confirmed that there is affordable and moderate-income housing in Draper. Every affordable housing
project so far has been done by developers without direct City involvement. She reported that the
Veranda West Apartments are income-restricted as are the Heritage Apartments. There are other
triplexes and fourplexes in the City that are older that might also be in the affordable range.

Mr. Draper stated that even though six strategies were selected, the minimum is five. If they meet
five, they will still be considered to be in compliance.

   •   Strategy V – Develop and adopt a Station Area Plan in accordance with [Utah State Code]
       Section 10-9a-403.1

The strategy specifies that they must adopt a Station Area Plan; however, the City must adopt all of
the Station Area Plans over five years. The Town Center plan is further along. Staff met with the
Wasatch Front Regional Council (“WFRC”) to vet the existing Vista Station Area Plan. They also
discussed the other station areas and the process to complete them or request a waiver. Some of those,
such as the 11400 South station area, is mostly built out. There is not much room for change because
there is already single-family residential there and any redevelopment will be limited. The Kimball's
Lane Station Area is the same except for the farm. It was not expected that the city would be willing
to rezone the property due to impacts that increased taxes would have on farming. A waiver may be
considered there also.

The State asks that the City report on Accessory Dwelling Units (“ADU”). It was noted that there
was an increase of eight during the reporting period. The City has approved a total of 21 ADUs. Of
the ADU Permits submitted, four were denied for not meeting the requirements. Six were withdrawn
for various reasons. Mr. Draper reported that the City ordinances meet State Code and allow for
ADUs. Chair Adams expressed his support of ADUs and how it makes housing more affordable.
Ms. Jastremsky reported that there have been a few Building Permits submitted for the construction
of new ADU’s. In that case, the applicant must obtain an ADU permit before the Building Permit for
the home can be issued. Chair Adams commented that one strategy can be that the City does what it
can to support ADUs. Ms. Jastremsky commented that the ADU Code that was adopted a few years
ago allows for Internal ADUs. It was noted that ADUs must be allowed in most single-family
residential areas of the City.

Mr. Draper commented that State law and the State definition indicate that an I-ADU must be created
out of an existing space. Staff has looked at how to work around that for new construction depending
on what is being proposed and desired by the applicant and has tried to allow for them to be created
in most situations. There are 20 applications currently in the review process. Mr. Draper reported
that the last part of the Moderate-Income Housing Report asks for feedback and recommendations.
They want to know what types of support would be helpful to the community in supporting the
implementation of moderate-income housing strategies.
