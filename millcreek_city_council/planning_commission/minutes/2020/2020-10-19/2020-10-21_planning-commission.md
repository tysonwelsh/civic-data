Millcreek Code Pertaining to Hotels and Motels Planner: Samah Safiullah

Samah Safiullah said the City Council had adopted a pending ordinance that instructed staff

to review standards for hotels. Staff was proposing amendments to the definition of hotel
under Millcreek Code Chapter 19.04 Definitions. The proposed amendments were intended
to better accomplish the intent of the General Plan to promote quality lodging and to
encourage development to take place in areas which would encourage economic

Millcreek Planning Commission Meeting Minutes 21 October 2020 Page 8 of 10
development. Staff wanted to avoid a monotonous and unattractive urban design, typical
freeway or highway interchange off-the-shelf design, and unwelcoming urban space.
Safiullah reviewed the proposed text amendments, She highlighted the Roadway Functional
Classification Map from the General Plan and said that properties along the green, blue, and
orange roads in the C-2- or C-3 zone could have hotels constructed. She reviewed the
commercial design standards for the C-2 and C-3 Zones. Safiullah said the proposed changes
were intended to promote a higher standard for the design and aesthetic of future hotel
projects within Millcreek, as well as a higher quality of environment for all staff and
occupants of the hotel. Given that the current code was vague, it lacked clear standards for a
comfortable and attractive entity. All four community councils were given the opportunity to
consider the proposed changes to the Commercial Zone. The Mount Olympus Community
Council endorsed the changes without any opposition. Members asked for the design
standards to include green or sustainable architecture. The Canyon Rim Citizens Association,

changes as well. She pointed out that letters from the East Mill Creek Community Council
were attached to the staff report and said they had asked about motels/tourist courts and if

2.3 Discussion on RM and Planned Unit Development (PUD) Updates
Francis Lilly said staff wanted to update the RM and PUD codes because they conflicted
and were difficult to implement. He said feedback he had received from the community
councils and Planning Commission on recently approved projects in these zones had the
following general consensus: side setbacks in the RM zone were too narrow, private lanes
could be too narrow, ugly utilities like an A/C compressor could be visible from the street,
there were not enough trees, design standards needed work, and amenities and open spaces
may not be adequate. The areas of disagreement were on front yard setbacks and preferences
toward modern or traditional architecture. He said some potential fixes could be to align and
enhance open space requirements in PUD and RM projects. He did not want to force
overprogramming of these spaces, but allow for good, flat space for programmed activities
or spontaneous interactions. He recommended the Planning Commission go to parks to
determine what they liked. Another fix was to align design standards by building type:
single-family, twin home, live/work units, and stacked flats. He said increasing side yard
setbacks for RM projects to 15 feet, the same as PUD perimeter setbacks, could be another
fix. The other two fixes would be to simplify PUD design standards and make clear that the
base zone governed projects, at least for the RM zone, and to allow for RM PUDs on local
roads. He said in current code to qualify for a PUD, there needed to be at least three acres of

Millcreek Planning Commission Meeting Minutes 21 October 2020 Page 10 of 10
property and a certain amount of frontage or the property needed to be on a major or arterial
road. He said there were no major road concepts in the Roadway Classification Map in the
General Plan.

ordinance. Commissioner Stephens said allowing PUDs on local roads was fine, but it could
