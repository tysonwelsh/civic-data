It has been determined that the AFO (Animal Feeding Operation) and CAFO
(Concentrated Animal Feeding Operation) portions of the Ordinance Amendments will
not be reviewed or considered at the February 8, 2022 Planning Commission meeting.
No public comment will be taken on AFO or CAFO topics. Only the other proposed

ordinance changes will come forward at a Jater date, and will be subject to notice and
hearing requirements in the future.

Mr. Messel explained that a public hearing is required any time there are proposed
changes to County zoning ordinances. The public hearing was properly noticed and
scheduled for today. The proposed changes are to Title 10 Zoning Regulations,
Definitions, Hearing Officers, STR appeals, STR fixes, and other miscellaneous fixes.

Ms. Hales proceeded to present on the proposed ordinance changes. She explained the
roll of the proposed hearing officers, which will be experts in different areas for appeals
coming before them. The Attorney’s Office would assign an appropriate hearing officer
to each hearing appeal. The proposed ordinance sets forth appeal deadlines, hearing
processes, new provisions, general provisions for all hearing officers and sets forth
additional specifications for land use decisions, various appeals, and building department
appeals. She went on to explain the proposed hearing process, the review body, the
recommending body and the roll of the Land Use Authority.

Commissioner Anderson asked about how the hearing officer would be appointed. Ms.
Hales explained that it would be through a professional services agreement with various
individuals with the necessary expertise. The County Commission would take
applications, and no conflict of interest, or county employees would be accepted. The
Hearing Officer would be trained in law, public administration, or building requirements,
and vetted for their qualifications. They will be paid, not voluntary. An electronic written
and audio record of each hearing, and a written decision with findings of fact and
conclusions of law, would be created, and would be the record on appeal, if the non-
prevailing party appeals the decision to the District Court.

Commissioner Hughes asked what the estimated cost is to the county and whether it
makes sense to add a hearing officer. Mr. Messel explained that the appeal application fee
can partially cover the cost, but that there are different ways to approach that. Ms. Hales
explained that this process generally saves jurisdictions money and time because they
have a defensible record, and the real cost is having an appeal, but not having a defensible
record if a lawsuit follows. A bad appeals process costs time and money. There is a cost
to the hearing officer process, but the benefit is worth it in the long run by reducing the

realities of infrastructure and water limits.

Ms. Hales moved on to the proposed Planned Development changes and adding the PD-
STR zone. The proposed zone would not require owner occupancy for Short Term
Rentals. This would create a STR Planned Development zone where one or more
individual lots can be platted and owned as Short Term Rentals. The units may be
maintained by a rental management company. Required development amenities kick in
if the PD is over 50 units. The ordinance expands the size limitations and puts in a square
footage ceiling on units to keep them residential-like at 7,000 square feet per unit or less.
The property owner would propose the PD-STR zone change, show lot sizes, and
development details in the application process. She went on to explain that this is a zone
change only for new developments and does not apply to existing developments. Ms.
Hales also explained that some corrections need to be made to Title 9 as well, because of
proposed changes to Title 10.
