      a.     HOME OCCUPATION: SADIE’S DAYCARE TO BE LOCATED AT 2029
             WEST 11800 SOUTH, SADIE JENKINS, APPLICANT.

Chair Rushton opened the public hearing.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the above request is
for Sadie’s Daycare home occupation located at 2029 West 11800 South. The property has
been purchased or is under a Purchase Contract. The new owner would like to make it their
primary residence with a daycare. A property rendering was displayed. Mr. Prestwich
reported that he spoke with the applicant who confirmed that there will be no non-resident
employees. The business will be run by herself and her husband. There will be few vehicle
trips with parking access in the driveway. The applicant plans to have nine children per day.
Because that number exceeds the ordinance allowance of six children, it requires Planning
Commission approval. The applicant will be caring for the children who are family members
and they will be dropped off and picked up together. Hours of operation were anticipated to
be from 6:00 a.m. to 9:00 p.m. The proposed conditions included the hours of operation
being 24 hours per day due to the potential for the mother’s shift change.

Commissioner Brown asked what would happen if the pickup and drop off exceed the hours
of operation and questioned if the 6:00 a.m. time is needed. Mr. Prestwich stated that if it
was a concern for neighbors, staff could revisit the approval. If the Commission was
agreeable, the hours of limitations could be eliminated.

Commissioner Cluff asked if shift times could be altered so that the drop-off and pick-up
times could fall outside the allotted window. Mr. Prestwich confirmed that that was a
possibility.

The applicant, Sadie Jenkins, reported that the mother would be providing childcare for
works long shifts two to three days per week. She confirmed that she would be caring for
her own children and those of the one mother.

Commissioner Cluff asked if the drop-off and pick-up times will differ. Ms. Jenkins stated

Commissioner Russell stated that based on staff’s reading of the application, he believed it
contradicts the condition requiring the outside play area to be fenced and gates to be self-
locking. He questioned the location of the outdoor play areas. Mr. Prestwich explained that
those are standard conditions included with daycare or preschool uses. Ms. Jenkins
confirmed that the children will be utilizing the front property for outdoor play. Drop-offs will
take place through the front door.

Commissioner Cluff suggested limiting the use to a single client.

Commissioner Cannon hoped to see more of a finished product and a detailed drop-off.

City Attorney, Craig Bott explained that the City cannot limit the request to one client. If
approval is granted for the daycare facility, it will be open to the allowable number of children.
He stated that if the turnaround creates an issue for the neighbors, it would be appropriate
to include a condition to mitigate the problem. Because the turnaround is solely on the
applicant’s property and impacts no one other than the applicant, there is nothing to provide
mitigation so a condition would not be necessary or appropriate. He stated that a site plan
is not necessary as there are no existing conditions that would impact the neighborhood or
require a condition.

Commissioner Lefgren was of the understanding that the Health Department or other
organization would require such site planning. Mr. Bott explained that if the Health
Department has an issue, it would be their obligation to address those concerns. The
request before the Commission is to a Conditional Use Permit and under law, it has the
expected right to be approved unless the Council can identify conditions that may negatively
impact the neighborhood or the area at large. If there is no negative impact identified and
documented, the condition cannot be imposed. He reiterated that unless evidence is

      b.     CONDITIONAL USE: 7 TREE FARMS, A DOG DAYCARE, AND BOARDING
             FACILITY PROPOSED AT 4150 WEST 12600 SOUTH, IN THE
             COMMERCIAL REGIONAL ZONE, SHARI JOHNSON, APPLICANT.

Chair Rushton opened the public hearing.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the request is for conditional use
approval of 7 Tree Farms, a dog daycare, and boarding facility located at 4150 West 12600
South. The adjacent Holiday Oil contains 2.7-acres on the entire parcel with a .50-acre pad
left site behind the building for future use. The property is zoned Commercial Regional Zone.
He explained that this is a more permissive zone, however, dog daycare is still a conditional
use in the zone. A property rendering was displayed. The applicant provided a concept
plan with a small building in the middle with shared access located on the south end. The
building will include four-room areas, a lobby, and several offices with doors leading to the
exterior. He confirmed that a site plan will follow and provide more specific details.

Mr. Prestwich commented that services will include the kenneling or boarding of household
pets only. Animals will be kept inside overnight, supervised when in outdoor areas, and
outdoor areas designated for the animals must be fenced with solid fencing. Staff had not
received any emails or phone calls concerning the proposed project.

Commissioner Breinholt questioned the ability to enforce the conditions. Mr. Prestwich
stated that there are two ways to provide enforcement. The first would be with the business
license by ensuring that there are no outside kennel areas provided in the site plan. He
confirmed that boarding triggers a conditional use.

Commissioner Cluff asked if measures can be taken to mitigate barking. Mr. Breinholt stated
that there is a 10-day barking log where the complainant must demonstrate that a dog has
been barking continuously in order to be considered a nuisance. It is then turned over to
the Prosecutor to pursue possible charges. Mr. Prestwich confirmed that commercial will be
treated the same as residential. Commissioner Breinholt asked that the address be updated.

The applicant, Shari Johnson identified herself as the owner of 7 Tree Farms and reported
that she began boarding and grooming out of her home as a necessity to provide for her
family. Over the past four years, they have perfected a system to meet the needs of the
dogs so that they are not communicating in ways that are a nuisance to her current
neighbors. The dogs have indoor space to play and are let outside to use the bathroom,
much like they would be cared for at home. She explained that dogs are interviewed prior
to boarding to determine their level of obedience and ensure that they are well socialized
with other dogs.

       c.     ZONE TEXT CHANGE: RIVERTON CITY IS PROPOSING CHANGES TO
              SECTION 18.155, FENCING, REGARDING ALLOWED FENCING TYPES
              AND MATERIALS.

Chair Rushton opened the public hearing.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the request involves a staff
application to change to the fencing ordinance. A small subdivision application was
previously reviewed for Aspen Court where the developer requested a change to the six-
foot solid masonry fencing requirement along the canal to a hollow foam core fence. He
reported that the argument for approving the change hinged on a phrase in the Code that
states that the Planning Commission may approve a different style fence if it is not
detrimental to the health, safety, and welfare of the community. Mr. Prestwich reported that
the Commission asked for clarification, which was clarified by the City Council and taken
care of by the developer. He reported that what is proposed is an ordinance change to
address that language and provide clarity to the ordinance. He believed this resonated with
the Council as it comes at a time in the market where both supplies and cost are creating
difficulty with the solid masonry walls.

A rendering of the hollow foam core fencing was displayed. Mr. Prestwich stated that the
changes eliminate criteria and allow for use of the pre-cast product if desired. This
eliminates the need to establish criteria and will become part of the normal routine. He
confirmed that the fencing adjacent to major roadways is required herein or by approval and
shall not be altered or amended by conditional use. The matter was noticed as a public
hearing and no comments were received.

Commissioner Cannon appreciated the City’s willingness to look at the economics of the
solid masonry fencing and adapt as there is a considerable cost difference between the two
products. He stated that the additional weight put on the bank of the canals may pose a
challenge in the future. It was noted that solid masonry fencing is not always the best option.

       a.     SLR-BLOCK SCALE PLAN: THE PARK AND MOUNTAIN VIEW
              RESIDENTIAL DEVELOPMENT TO BE LOCATED NEAR 13100 SOUTH
              EAGLES FLIGHT ROAD.      BENSON WHITNEY, REPRESENTING
              PROPERTY RESERVE, INC. APPLICANT.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the above item involves the SLR-
Block Scale Plan for the park and Mountain View development located near 13100 South
Eagles Flight Road. The SLR Development has its own zoning through the Master
Development Agreement (“MDA”) with the landowner where the City adopted its own
approval process. All other projects in the City go through a one-step review process where
the SLR requires a three-step process narrowing focus with each review. He reported that
they reviewed and approved Step 1 as the Community Scale Plan and will be reviewing the
Block Scale Plan, which encompasses the same area as the community scale plan. The
final review would include more focused detail.

The block scale plan ensures that the Community Scale Plan recommendations are
incorporated and the increasing level of detail is acceptable. Mr. Prestwich reported that the
applicant did an exceptional job of relaying the information and has progressed on some
fronts further than needed. A map rendering was displayed. Mr. Prestwich confirmed that
Phase Two includes large buildings, theaters, and a Costco which has received a permit
and is moving ahead. The Cottages at Western Springs were reviewed. He noted that
permitted uses in the CPA within 250 feet of the north property lines should be limited to
single-family detached residential.

Mr. Prestwich reported that during the Community Scale Plan meeting, the motion included
asking the City Council if the Sunday Drive connection should come through or not as closing
Sunday Drive would take a City action. He confirmed that the City Council supported all
streets being private and treated like an apartment complex that looks like a mix of
residential types and that are individually owned. They asked that the connection be closed
and the applicant adjusted their plans.

The new Civil Plan (“CPA”) was displayed. The layout has been altered to mitigate the deep
lots and driveway areas have been added. The plan includes community open space, a
community center with pool, amenities, pickleball courts, a detention pond, and dog park.
Entry features include access gates that help distinguish the project as private rather than a
continuation of a public street. Mr. Prestwich noted that six-foot masonry fencing has been
proposed as well as along the canal. The existing vinyl fencing along The Cottages of
Western Springs will remain.

Mr. Prestwich introduced New City Engineer, Gordon Miner. While preparing the staff report
for the project, Mr. Miner observed that the gates were missing from the traffic study. The
applicant was contacted and a memo was delivered just prior to the meeting, which had yet
to be reviewed.

Part of the Block Scale Plan included looking ahead as to how it may phase. A Phasing
Plan was displayed. Phases 1 and 1B will come first with Phase 2 including most of the
single-family. Phase 3 will come last. Mr. Prestwich next reviewed public versus private
roads. The applicant included large parcels between the roads that will not be individually
parceled at this time. Utilities will drop a master meter in the right-of-way or easement and
the applicant intends to individually meter the units, particularly the single-family detached
units. He confirmed that everything behind the right-of-way will be privately maintained.

The landscaping was next described. Street trees will be planted along the edge as required
with multiple interior tree plans and individual trees planted in the yards. Two large entry
monuments will be located at the central entry off of Eagles Flight Road. Mr. Prestwich
reported that the building elevations fall within the guidelines of the SLR and SDD zoning.
The clubhouse with typical single-family homes will be located along the north property lines
with cluster homes, front-loaded townhomes, and rear-loaded townhomes being located
throughout the property. Each of the four different product types have a slightly different
design with four different color schemes to add variety. Motion language was discussed.
He reported that the matter was noticed as a decision item and previously noticed for the
public hearing Community Scale Plan. Subsequent plans will be decisions items.

Commissioner Brown expressed concern with insufficient parking and the dead ends along
the west side of the property. Mr. Prestwich stated that the homes include a two-car garage
with different units having a driveway with additional stalls located throughout. The cluster
homes include an extra deep driveway with the E unit having extra parking where the B units
do not. Interior roads will be wider widths that will provide additional parking. He indicated
that the SDD does not provide a ratio.

Commissioner Breinholt reported that the project is more dense than previously thought and
asked if there is a green space requirement per unit. Mr. Prestwich indicated the green
space requirement is over 10% of the total area or if over a certain acreage, at least a 1.0-
acre contiguous plus a percentage. The SLR, SDD, and MDA designated a certain amount
of public green space and then granted the density. He explained it does not specify that
development within the overall project area must provide a certain amount of green space
for that Block Scale Plan or Community Scale Plan. The master developer is still obligated
to give the City a certain number of acreages of parks.
