27   1.   Healthy Conflict
28        This item was withdrawn from the agenda.
29
30   2.   Financial Updates
31        Dean Lundell, Finance Director, gave an update on the city’s funds that also included operational
32        costs. The recent small utility increases have helped with the fund balance. Mr. Lundell distributed
33        the financial principles and policies to be discussed. He stated that this document is approved each
34        year with the budget, so the Council can make adjustments. The document stated that the policy is
35        to have 20-25% in the general fund reserves; however, the State recently adjusted the percentage
36        maximum to 35%. The Council suggested that they look at increasing the percentage in the
37        upcoming budget cycle.
38
39   3.   Y2 Survey Results
40        Kyrene Gibb, from Y2 Analytics, presented the annual survey results. She said between the
41        pandemic fatigue and election cycle, there is a quality of life deterioration but it’s not
42        overwhelmingly negative. She said that this is consistent with what they see in other cities. The
43        impacts of growth and development, housing, traffic, and infrastructure continue to be major
44        concerns for residents. Other things to note are that ¼ of residents would be interested in glass
45        recycle pickup, and most residents feel that there is not enough green space.
46
47   4.   Lunch and Miscellaneous Updates
48        Shaye Ruitenbeek gave an update on the Legislative Session and bills being proposed. She said
49        that water and housing continue to be priorities. Bills that were concerning to cities included
50        disallowing incentives for retail sales, removing impact fees for ADUs, IPA oversight, and the
51        Utah Lake Authority. Mayor Johnson stated that it is concerning when the State takes away land
52        use authority from local government.
53
54   5.   Council Assignments
55        Councilor Condie led the discussion. He suggested that due to the terms on some of the Boards,
56        that those positions not change every year, such as TSSD and the Hutchings Museum. It was
57        decided not to make any changes this year. The Utah Lake Commission will be dissolved if the
58        Utah Lake Authority is created.
59
60   6.   Parks Projects
61        Steve Marchbanks gave an update on budgets for the 3 major regional parks which include Mellor
62        Rhodes, Family Park, and the Dry Creek Lake Park. He said that the grant money they secured for
63        the Dry Creek Lake Park is set to expire soon, so they need to either abandoned the grant or move
64        forward now on the park. They still have some questions about the water that need to be answered.
65        They are close to having the final construction documents for Mellow Rhodes Park that will
66        include phasing. Mr. Marchbanks showed the final concept design for Family Park that includes an
67        all-abilities playground that could be constructed in the first phase. The Council will need to
68        decide which park they would like to focus on the most.
69
70   7.   Goal Setting / Campus Plan Update
71        Jason Walker, City Administrator, updated the Council on the Campus Master Plan and
72        recommended that they begin moving forward on the first phase of the plan which would include
73        the construction of a new City Hall/Library building and demolition of the current Library. He said
74        that the process for drawing up plans could take a year or more.
75
76        The Council participated in an activity that helped them identify some of their needs and priorities
77        for the community.
78
79   8.   Courageous Leadership
80        Chief Jeremy Craft gave a training/presentation on leadership and making tough decisions as
81        leaders.
82
83   9.   Capital Projects
84        Lorin Powell, City Engineer, showed the Council all the streets and water storage locations along
85        with upcoming projects. He said that they will construct a new culinary water tank and irrigation
86        reservoir to support the west side. 2300 West is being designed for build out, they plan to install a
87        light signal at 1100 West and Main Street, and the 700 South cycle track should be completed this
88        year. Mr. Powell stated that he has had some discussions with the Pecks and had some ideas with
89        developing the Mill Pond area which could include recreation, retail and restaurants. He would
90        like to make some beautification improvements to the area this year.
 91        Luke Seegmiller, with the Engineering Department, presented on what a Traffic Control Center is
 92        and how it can help the City. He said that it would give them the ability to monitor intersections or
 93        high traffic areas and make adjustments to signals if needed. He said that they would like to fund
 94        this in the upcoming budget.
 95
 96   10. Dinner
 97       Dinner was served after item #11.

 99   11. PARC Tax and Policy
100       Mrs. Ruitenbeek presented on the PARC tax. She said that they are estimating that there would be
101       about $1 million for parks and recreation and $400K for cultural facilities and cultural
102       organizations annually. She stated that organizations could apply for PARC tax funds with an
103       application process. They also plan to establish an advisory committee to review applications, then
104       the final decision will be made by the Council.
