CONSENT AGENDA APPROVED:

  Councilor Ostler moved to approve the consent agenda which included the approval of the minutes

PUBLIC COMMENT:

   Mr. Ken Zabriskie addressed the council about his concern with the increase in property taxes and
how this increase would affect the senior citizens in the county. He stated that he had been away from
Nephi for 50 years and had moved back three and half years ago. He stated that senior citizens live on a
fixed income, and some live off as little as $300 per week. He recognized and appreciated the fact that
the city had not raised property taxes in many years. But due to the school district and other entities
raising taxes the seniors had been hit hard. He stated, “schools are using our senior citizens as ATMs.”
He said he was there to inform the council that he is trying to organize the senior citizen and give them a
voice. He stated that his kids went to private schools in Kentucky, and they were educated in not the
nicest buildings, but they were all able to go onto college. He had an idea of a Senior Week and have
citizens and church groups help and serve the senior citizens.

  Mayor Seely said that the Six County Association of Government has programs to aide seniors. One of
those programs was the H.E.A.T program.

REED RIDGE FINAL PLAT APPROVED:

  The council reviewed the final plat submission for a six-lot, commercial subdivision in the HC-1 zone
for the area just to the north of the Maverik gas station. This subdivision would leave a lot to the south
of the Maverik as a remainder parcel that would need to be subdivided in the future. Mr. Atkinson
informed the council that it is unknown at this time what businesses may purchase lots within the
subdivision. Easements had been granted to supply utilities and future utility maintenance. He
indicated that the easements would serve as crossover access easements between the lots.
   Councilor Worwood moved to approve the final plat for the Reed Ridge 6- Lot Commercial
Subdivision. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

JIM OCKEY WATER AGREEMENT APPROVED:

  Mr. Atkinson explained that several months ago, the city entered into an agreement with Jim Ockey to
sell raw water to his property. The city was not responsible to treat any sold water. At the time, Sunrise
Engineering helped to estimate the amount of water that would be needed for the existing homes, the
campground, and the potential for two other residential properties. Since that point in time, a water
meter has been installed to measure the amount of water being used by these properties.
Complications with consistently reading the meter, along with some leaks in the lines on the Ockey
property have led to some difficulties arriving at an accurate measurement.
  Additional reads have been taken and infrastructure is being installed to read the meter each month
remotely. Also, the leaks have been fixed. This allows the city to more accurately measure the amount
of water needed on the property. Mr. Ockey is anxious to amend the agreement with the city to reflect
these measurements. Some revisions were made to decrease the penalty to $50 dollars a month if the
Ockeys go over 600,000 gallons of usage in a month. Another revision was that we would provide the
labor to maintain the meter and they would pay for any parts. Mr. Ockey wanted change paragraph 1.7
from 3 times to 5 times in a ten-year period if they went over the amount of usage allowed that the
agreement could be terminated. The lease is a 99-year lease.
   Councilor Memmott moved to accept the Ockey Agreement with the revision in section 1.7 from 3
times to 5 times. Councilor Callaway seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

WORK SESSION CONVENED AT 8:00 p.m.

    1.  Mr. Atkinson presented the 138 kV Project Plan draft. The project would include a substation
       to the north of the City that would connect to the Clover Creek Substation. This new line would
       provide a more reliable supply of power and more capacity that would help with the new
       businesses going in and that would possibly come in the future. He stated that it is not a true
       second connection but would help with a major outage. The easement acquisitions needed for
       the project would require a large area to be annexed into the city. Councilor Memmott
       expressed concern about annexation and suggested that Mr. Atkinson try to re-negotiate a price
       for the easements with the property owners.
    2. The Mayor updated the council about the meeting he attended with the Governor’s office
       about a new business that is interested in coming to the area. The business is more of a concept
       business instead of a business with a history. The state was not interested in providing
       economic incentives, because of the lack of a business history.

    3.     The council discussed the current blight ordinance. The council directed staff to re-write the
          ordinance adding more incentives and possibly more control by the council to determine what
          type of development was put in the blighted designated areas.

    4. Councilor Ostler moved to adjourn the meeting. Councilor Parady seconded the motion

    5. Meeting adjourned at 8:40 p.m.
