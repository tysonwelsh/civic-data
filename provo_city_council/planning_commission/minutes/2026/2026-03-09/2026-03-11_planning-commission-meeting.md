BACKGROUND
The applicant for this item is the Provo Airport Services Department. The request is for
the next phase of the airport terminal that will add three new gates, bringing the total to
seven gates in the terminal.

Phase III will add 50,000 square feet of gate hold room, future baggage handling space,
and airport tenant lease space to the north side of the existing airport terminal.

The Coordinator Review Committee reviewed the project and has approved the project
plan. Some existing parking will be displaced by the terminal expansion, but there is
additional parking capacity in the long-term and overflow parking areas. The Airport
Director has approved parking for this project as required by code.

FINDINGS OF FACT
   1. The property is located in the Airport (A) zone.
   2. Building height allowed in code is 65’ and the proposed building height is 36’.
      (14.17D.060)
   3. Airport terminals are permitted in the zone.
   4. Parking is to be approved by the airport director per 14.17D.070.
   5. The airport director has approved the parking for this project.

STAFF ANALYSIS
The location of the airport terminal is setback quite far from the parcel lines and
Lakeview Parkway. The building height complies with the zone requirements and will
not have an impact on adjacent properties.

The code requires that the Airport Director determine the number of parking spaces
required. The Airport Director has determined that the parking for the expansion is
sufficient to meet the needs of the airport. The overall parking supply available to
passengers, employees, and visitors will continue to meet anticipated peak demand.

OVERVIEW
The redevelopment team for the mall, including the applicants, PEG and Brixton, have
proposed a series of text amendments for the ITOD zone that would enable them to
build the products and concepts shown in the mall redevelopment plans. However,
these amendments would have citywide impact on any property currently in the ITOD
zone or with future application of the ITOD zone.

The specific amendments are proposed as follows:

PCC 14.23.020: add Standard Land Use (SLU) code 1112, single-family dwelling,
attached (townhome) to the list of uses permitted by-right in the ITOD Zone;

PCC 14.23.020: increase the square footage standard from 2,500 square feet to 10,000
square feet for permitted uses that become conditional uses if they are larger than the
stated number (meaning any use listed below that paragraph currently becomes a
conditional use if it is in a space larger than the 2,500 sq. ft.);

PCC 14.23.030: provide an exception to lot area for “dwelling units intended for owner-
occupancy”;

PCC 14.23.040: provide an exception to lot width for “dwelling units intended for owner-
occupancy”;

PCC 14.23.050: provide an exception to lot frontage for “dwelling units intended for
owner-occupancy”, remove the 100-foot distance standard for lot frontage, and allow
private street frontage to count;

PCC 14.23.070: remove the six-foot maximum front yard requirement (leaving no
minimum or maximum setback);

PCC 14.23.070: remove the six-foot maximum side yard requirement (leaving no
minimum or maximum setback);

PCC 14.23.070: add a “transitional yard requirement” of one foot for each two feet of
building height, when adjacent to a residential zone;

PCC 14.23.095: remove entire Transitional Building Height section (replaced with above
proposal for “transitional yard requirement”);

PCC 14.23.140: reduce the minimum habitable main floor depth requirement on a
primary street from forty feet to thirty feet;

PCC 14.23.140: reduce the minimum habitable main floor depth requirement on a
secondary street from thirty feet to twenty-five feet;

PCC 14.23.150: remove prohibition of slider doors and slider windows for street
frontage facades; and

STAFF ANALYSIS
Staff analysis for this number of amendments can get convoluted. Therefore, staff have
identified key areas for support or concern in the analysis below, noting section
numbers. Staff’s comfort with the proposed amendments varies but is reliant on the fact
that the scope of the changes should be somewhat limited as the ITOD zone is set to be
replaced with a new TOD zone this year.

Staff analysis is provided by reviewing the proposals against the criteria of Provo City
Code 14.02.020, below. The applicant has also prepared analysis and justification for
these proposed amendments and is attached to this report (“Amendment Justification” –
attachment #2).

Before recommending an amendment to this Title, the Planning Commission shall
determine whether such amendment is in the interest of the public and is
consistent with the goals and policies of the Provo City General Plan. The following
guidelines shall be used to determine consistency with the General Plan:
       (a) Public purpose for the amendment[s] in question.
       Staff response: The applicant has stated that the purpose of the proposed
       amendments is to provide greater flexibility in providing a dense, mixed-use
       environment with affordable, owner-occupied housing.
       (b) Confirmation that the public purpose is best served by the amendment[s]
       in question.
       Staff response: Staff believe that the above purpose could be met under the
       current ITOD zone regulations, as has been done before. However, while the
       scale and design of a mixed-use environment are important, these proposed
       changes would allow the developer to move forward with their plans and
       should have limited impact elsewhere as the city adopts a new TOD zone.
       There is some concern regarding the proposal to remove setback maximums
       (PCC 14.23.070) and to not define a required street frontage distance (PCC
       14.23.050). Also, jumping from a 2,500 sq. ft. scale to a 10,000 sq. ft. scale
       (PCC 14.23.020) may not provide for the human-scale, walkable environment
       that a transit-oriented development should provide.
       (c) Compatibility of the proposed amendment with General Plan policies,
       goals, and objectives.
       Staff response: The applicant has identified sections of the General
       Plan that support these changes, specifically related to providing
       more mixed-use development, alternative housing options, and
       increased density around transit. As noted above, these goals and
       objectives could be met without the proposed amendments, though
       they may help with this specific plan.

14.23.010
Purpose and Objectives.

The Interim Transit Oriented Development (ITOD) Zone is established to provide interim zoning
measures while more comprehensive transit oriented development plans and ordinances are
formulated for the planned intermodal hub area and other key transportation related locations
within the City. These interim measures are intended to prevent land uses and development
that would hinder long-range City goals while achieving quality housing, attracting long-term

residents, on properties surrounding the proposed Intermodal Hub site. This zone’s primary
use is for residential and residential/support mixed-use development which is intended to
provide housing and business opportunities adjacent to public transit and thereby facilitate
increases in the use of public transit and reduce City-wide traffic and congestion elsewhere. The
ITOD zone is characterized by clean, well-lighted streets, ample pedestrian ways, and attractive,
inviting, and residential uses, well-maintained shops, stores, offices, with a mixed use design.
This zone will tend to encourage an architectural theme which will strengthen the continuity of
the ITOD area and give it an identity with which the citizens of Provo can identify.

    ATTACHMENT 2 – APPLICANT “AMENDMENT JUSTIFICATION”

Provo City Code 14.23 Text Amendment Justification

14.23.020: Adjusting the maximum square footage to 10,000 for certain permitted uses
(conditional use approval required above 10,000 square feet) is more reasonable for
several of the uses listed. For example, many food, furniture (which already specifies a
10,000 square foot maximum size), sporting goods, fitness-related, and religious uses
would not be feasible at 2,000-2,500 square feet. This change will allow for greater
flexibility for retail uses in the ITOD zone while still preserving the higher-density residential
focus of the zone. According to the General Plan, Provo residents want to see more mixed-
use development. Placing unnecessary restrictions on this type of development makes it
more difficult to execute. A broader range of allowable uses with a reasonable maximum
square footage will help achieve Provo’s goal of creating places for businesses to thrive.

14.23.030 and 14.23.040: As currently written, these sections would not allow for the
development of owner-occupied housing within the ITOD zone. The proposed Provo Towne
Centre redevelopment includes townhome-style for-sale housing, but lot size and
dimension requirements must be reduced to accommodate this type of housing. The
provision of a mix of attainable housing options, particularly “missing middle” style owner-
occupied townhomes, is one of the major objectives of the General Plan. It is also one of
the stated purposes of the ITOD zone itself.

14.23.050: As written, this section also creates challenges for townhome-style units, which
are often approached from a private drive or alleyway and will not meet the minimum
frontage length requirement. Additionally, multifamily properties are often accessed by
driveways leading to parking structures which may not be deeded to the public. This could
present a particular issue for multifamily buildings that are interior to large parking lots
such as the proposed mall redevelopment. The City will still have the ability to ensure
adequate access and circulation as part of its project plan approval process.

14.23.070: As written, this section would make it difficult to develop large amenity areas
such as the plaza proposed at the interface between the retail and residential portions of
the Provo Towne Centre. It also creates potential interpretation challenges when applied to
owner-occupied housing units that are part of a larger community-owned common area.
The stated purpose of the zone, and one of the repeated themes of the General Plan, is to
increase density in locations with good access to public transportation. Limiting setback
requirements to those necessary for safety while providing flexibility for unique sites such
as the Towne Centre Mall should help accomplish this objective.

The addition of 14.23.070 matches the transitional setback requirements for uses abutting
residential zones that are already codified in 14.34.300.

OVERVIEW
As the redevelopment team for the mall talked over all the processes that need to be
completed, this General Plan Map amendment from Commercial to TOD seemed the
most simple but it has some significant details tied to it with the accompanying zone
map amendment and concept plan applications, so it has had to wait until city staff were
comfortable moving the entire proposal forward together.

The introduction of the TOD land use designation on the General Plan Map was with the
2009 General Plan comprehensive update. The description for this designation
indicates that these areas should include “commercial, office and residential uses” with
“pedestrian-scale design” that encourages “alternative transit”; while previous iterations
included language to “reduce parking and auto ownership”, focusing on “enhancing
transit ridership through carefully sited retail locations, civic and open spaces, and
density.”

STAFF ANALYSIS
The shift to a TOD designation in this area makes sense considering the Utah Valley
Express bus-rapid transit route comes through the eastern portion of the project area,
and portions of the property are also within half a mile of the Frontrunner commuter rail
station. If this area is redeveloped to be a harmonious pedestrian-scale environment,
the TOD is the right land use designation. The first phase of the mall redevelopment will
be important to tie the commercial and residential uses together and make sure if flows
well into future phases and meets the intent of the TOD designation. The rezone and
concept plan application staff reports will get more into those details, but there may be
some crossover of analysis throughout these proposals.

OVERVIEW
As part of a large redevelopment proposal for the north twenty-three acres of the mall
property and Shady Acres mobile home park, owned by Brixton Capital, a request to
rezone the area from the RM zone and SC3 zone to the ITOD zone has been submitted
and reviewed by staff.

As staff have reviewed this request along with the associated ITOD zone text
amendments, General Plan map amendment, and concept plans; staff have provided
multiple rounds of comments and corrections to improve functionality. There are a
variety of concerns from staff about these proposals, which have been noted to the
applicant, and of which many have been proposed to be addressed in a draft
development agreement that is currently being reviewed by the developers. The primary
concerns are related to available sewer capacity, impacts to the road network, and
phasing; but also include comments on the requested HTRZ (Housing and Transit
Reinvestment Zone) and PID (Public Infrastructure District) funding requests that the
City Council will decide on.

It should also be noted that staff have received more public feedback on this proposal
than is typical, which is not surprising considering the prominent location, the project
size and years of underutilization of the asset. Therefore, the final attachments of this
report include all written public comments received prior to the writing of this report. Any
received after will be read into the record at the hearing.

in that staff report, but it is worth noting in this report that the current iteration of the plan
is not meeting the full potential for a mixed-use, walkable development. The distance
between uses, the proposed street network, and parking in the current plan indicates a
car-dependent or auto-centric design currently. However, modifications can still be
made between now and when the developer comes back with full project plans to
address some of those issues, and the ITOD zone will enable that type of development.

OVERVIEW
PEG Development and Brixton Capital have worked together to develop a concept plan
to redevelop the north area of the mall properties. Along with the proposed text
amendments, a General Plan Map amendment, and the Zone map amendment, this
concept plan should be evaluated carefully to ensure that best practices are used and
that the plans could comply with city codes and standards. While a concept plan is
typically meant to paint a picture of what could come with a proposed zone change, this
concept plan will be an exhibit in a development agreement, so the Planning
Commission should verify its ability to meet code requirements as well as the purpose
and objectives of the ITOD zone.

A general overview of the concept will be provided here, with specific numbers and
details provided in the “Findings of Fact” section and attachments. The plan is to
complete a partial demolition of the northern section of the mall structure to make way
for new parking, commercial uses, open spaces, and a new east-west roadway. North
of that roadway it is proposed to build a phased series of apartments and townhomes,
totaling 1,383 new residential units at buildout.

The first phase would include the remodel of the mall, installation of new commercial
pads and open spaces, installation of the new east-west roadway and roundabout, 72
townhomes on the Shady Acres parcel, 11 additional townhomes directly north of the
mall adjacent to the north property line, and 209 apartments in the phase one apartment
building. The townhome products are proposed to be for sale, income restricted units,
while the apartments would all be for rent.

Phases two through five would add another 1,091 apartments across four additional
buildings, built in phases (shown in attachments). Each building has associated
amenities conceived in the concept plans to satisfy the amenity requirements of the
ITOD zone.

STAFF ANALYSIS
Staff have noted some important factors to consider regarding the overall
redevelopment proposal from this group in the earlier staff reports, so I will not repeat
those here. However, one of the main concerns related to this concept plan from staff
and from the Planning Commission study sessions has been the lack of connectivity
and accessibility without a vehicle. While the developer has provided a “travel paths”
exhibit (attached below), staff are underwhelmed with the current site layout and
pedestrian/bicycle connectivity, noting that the current version doesn’t do enough to
provide the “ample pedestrian ways” that the zone intends.

Another concern that may not be immediately evident when looking at the amount of
parking spaces around the mall is that the concept plans have not identified the proper
amount of parking stalls required for the new residential units. The calculations that
were used by the applicant show a city code requirement of 833 spaces at buildout, but


---

Planning Commission Staff Report                                                            Item 6
March 11, 2026                                                                             Page 3

the correct calculations show a requirement of 1,859 spaces (1,026 spaces short).
While the applicant could come back with parking reduction requests on future project
plan applications, this parking shortfall in the concept plans is worth noting.

A final concept plan concern is the availability of amenities. The total number and
acreage of amenities provided at buildout is more than sufficient to meet code
requirements. However, staff have concern that the townhome units don’t have
amenities of their own and could end up not feeling comfortable or not being provided
access to the amenities around and within the apartment buildings. Additionally, there
should be guarantees that the townhomes will not be charged in the future for use of
amenities that are off-site to the townhomes. Staff simply want to identify concerns now
that will need to be addressed with the preliminary project plan.

Outside of the concerns above and the concerns with future sewer and roadway
improvements noted in earlier staff reports, staff are encouraged by the idea of
redeveloping the mall property to bring more residents, businesses, and energy to this
area of the city. The overall idea is supported, but staff hope that working through the
details of the development agreement will provide comfort to move this proposal
forward, and that the future project plan applications for the development phases will
more carefully consider the goals of the ITOD zone to provide “mixed-use development
which is intended to provide housing and business opportunities adjacent to public
transit and thereby facilitate increases in the use of public transit and reduce City-wide
traffic and congestion elsewhere.”

14.23.010
Purpose and Objectives.

The Interim Transit Oriented Development (ITOD) Zone is established to provide interim zoning measures
while more comprehensive transit oriented development plans and ordinances are formulated for the
planned intermodal hub area and other key transportation related locations within the City. These interim
measures are intended to prevent land uses and development that would hinder long-range City goals while
achieving quality housing, attracting long-term residents, on properties surrounding the proposed Intermodal
Hub site. This zone’s primary use is for residential and residential/support mixed-use development which is
intended to provide housing and business opportunities adjacent to public transit and thereby facilitate
increases in the use of public transit and reduce City-wide traffic and congestion elsewhere. The ITOD zone is
characterized by clean, well-lighted streets, ample pedestrian ways, and attractive, inviting, and residential
uses, well-maintained shops, stores, offices, with a mixed use design. This zone will tend to encourage an
architectural theme which will strengthen the continuity of the ITOD area and give it an identity with which the
citizens of Provo can identify.

 Commission. Key issues raised in written comments received subsequent to the Staff Report or public comment during
 the public hearing included the following:
     • Planning Commission and staff received multiple emails with a variety of concerns on the mall redevelopment
         proposal, those names were read into the record.
     • Daniel Friend (District Chair) gave a report from the neighborhood meeting and shared concerns about the
         roundabout, more indoor parking in the mall, displacement of mobile home park, lack of for-sale products, and
         need for better walkability.
     • Jake Jensen shared concerns about the tax breaks for the proposal and affordability of the project.
     • Madison Nelson shared concerns about the loss of mall space/uses and the mobile home park.
     • Melissa shared concerns about loss of indoor mall community space and the current users, increased traffic, and
         other impacts.
     • Glenn Knapp shared concerns about crime, affordability, and when his mall business needs to move. He thinks
         the plan is good but does wonder about infrastructure costs.
     • Two other comments shared concern about the loss of the indoor walk space, lack of safety around the roundabout,
         and loss of culture in the mall.
     • There were additional comments sharing concern about the proposed road network, loss of specific uses in the
         existing mall, and the roundabout/safety.
     • Gary Killpack noted that this proposal could be a good thing for the city and believes that the questions and
         concerns have time to be addressed.

APPLICANT RESPONSE
 Key points addressed in the applicant's presentation to the Planning Commission included the following:
    • Robert Smith (PEG Development) gave an overview of their company, their participation in the proposal, and an
        overview of the concept. He noted that there are many questions and concerns, but those will be answered as the
        proposal moves forward.
    • Justin Long (Brixton Capital) gave additional detail on the concept plans, limits to what can be done, and the
        overall goals of the plan.

PLANNING COMMISSION DISCUSSION
 Key points discussed by the Planning Commission included the following:
    • Matt Wheelwright noted that this doesn’t feel like a TOD plan and was concerned that the zone was out of place.
    • Jon Lyons noted that the RM zone area seems out of place to be included in these requests, other Councilors noted
        that the trailer park could be zoned separately from the apartment and mall redevelopment. Staff noted that it
        would have impact on the parking and amenity spaces needed for the redevelopment.
    • Lisa Jensen verified with Engineering staff that the city does not currently have plans or funding to improve the
        road network around the mall that would be needed due to the development, and that those costs would be the
        responsibility of the developers.
    • Commissioners asked the applicant why the Shady Acres mobile home park needs to be part of the plan, to which
        they indicated that is where they plan to provide the affordable, for-sale units for their development.
    • Jon Lyons asked what the developers will be doing to help the displaced occupants of Shady Acres, to which they
        stated they are giving those residents a longer lead time to find other housing.
    • Jon Lyons asked the applicants why they need the roundabout and whether its location can be altered. The
        applicants responded that they need to bring easy access to the entrance of the mall, not meant to quickly move
        traffic through the area.
    • Jon Lyons also noted concerns about the outdoor spaces in our region not being used due to weather conditions.
        The applicants gave examples of similar developments in areas with adverse weather conditions.
    • There was discussion regarding existing users of the mall and the spaces that would be demolished.
    • The Commission clarified with the applicants what changes are being made in the mall, what uses are being
        brought in, and if existing uses could stay. The applicants stated that they are looking at a lot of different
        businesses, and that they don’t have final details about that.
    • Lisa Jensen asked specific questions on parking numbers and location to the applicants, they indicated that their
        goal is to have quick and easy parking for mall users.
    • Lisa Jensen also asked about the proposed amenity spaces, asking specifically about amenity space for the
        townhomes. The applicants indicated that being close to the mall is a good amenity, but they would have to lose
        some units to provide amenity within the townhome portion of the project.

                                                      Page 2 of 6


---

    •   Lisa Jensen opened the idea of using a different zone for the mobile home park, the applicant said they may be
        open to different zoning for that area.
    •   Joel Temple asked why so much parking is being provided when there is a lot on the site already, the applicant
        stated that most of the existing parking being provided is protected by contracts with mall anchors.
    •   Barbara DeSoto asked the applicant on lease rates now versus what they will be, the applicant stated that they
        don’t have those numbers but that they are working on short-term leases with many users.
    •   Commissioners asked about price points, the applicant responded that they would align with AMI rates and
        manage the tenants as the phases progress.
    •   Matt Wheelwright inquired about possible building heights for residential structures shown in the concept and
        how they plan to handle needed infrastructure upgrades.
    •   Barbara DeSoto shared her thoughts that the proposal isn’t a true mixed-use district and that there isn’t enough
        pedestrian space, that there needs to be more direct interaction between the uses, and the proposal needs more
        shaded/protected areas. She also noted that the proposal doesn’t match transit development, that the road network
        is a concern, and tax breaks are a concern. The project needs to mix in more for-sale/condo units.
    •   Matt Wheelwright noted that there is a lot more to be done but believes that the developers will create what the
        market demands; but doesn’t believe that concept plan can be approved with so much up in the air with the zone
        standards and Council decisions.
    •   Joel Temple spoke about his desire to provide some more for-sale units in the plan and going beyond the
        minimums, sharing his ideas about incentive zoning.
    •   Several commissioners noted that the concepts seemed very disconnected between the townhomes, the
        apartments, and the commercial space.
    •   Lisa Jensen inquired about moving the roundabout to provide more safety and pedestrian connectivity. The
        applicant was resistant to making that change, explaining their choice for the location, and also responded to
        earlier concerns by the public and commissioners.
    •   Commissioners gave overall impressions from the proposals for the mall redevelopment, echoing a lot from the
        staff report and public comments. Though there are concerns that need to be addressed, the idea to enliven the
        mall is supported.
    •   Lisa Jensen stated her position that the mobile home park should not be part of the General Plan or Zone map
        amendments. There are other zones that could allow for better redevelopment of that space.
    •   Commissioners were concerned that there are too many issues that need to be addressed before approvals are
        given, but that the developers have a feel for what can work and should keep working to adjust the plan to address
        concerns.

FINDINGS / BASIS OF PLANNING COMMISSION DETERMINATION
 The Planning Commission identified the following findings as the basis of this decision or recommendation:
 Planning Commission motion included a recommendation to exclude the mobile home park from the ITOD zone, and use
 a different residential zone due to needed amendments to the ITOD zone and possibilities to adjust plan.
