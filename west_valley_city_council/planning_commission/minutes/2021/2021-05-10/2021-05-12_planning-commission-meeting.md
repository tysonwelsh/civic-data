The applicant, ARCO/Murray representing Tovala, is requesting a conditional use amendment
for a Commissary at 5041 West 24500 South in Building 1 of the Lake Park Commerce Center.

include an Amazon delivery van parking lot to the west, Lake Park Commerce Center to the east,

This request is for approval of a Commissary for Tovala. They are a meal service prep and
delivery company. The use will be located in the south end of building 1. The overall square

standard warehouse delivery type trucks and not mobile food trucks or trailers like you would
typically see in a Commissary. The existing landscaping and masonry screen wall on site will
remain.

C-9-2021
Salt Lake County - Pioneer Crossing Park
1280 W. Cultural Center Dr.
C-2 zone, 24 acres

The applicant, Salt Lake County Parks and Recreation, is requesting conditional use approval for
the master plan of Pioneer Crossing Park at 1280 W. Cultural Center Drive. The zoning for this
property is C2, General Commercial and the General Plan designation is Mixed Use. This
property is also located within the Jordan River Overlay Zone which requires that any new
development be reviewed as a conditional use. The surrounding zones are C2, General
Commercial to the west and south and B/RP, Business Research Park, to the north. The
surrounding uses include vacant land to the west, the Cultural Celebration Center to the north
and west, and a Granger Hunter Improvement District facility to the north. There are commercial
uses to the east across the Jordan River in South Salt Lake which are zoned a mix of Business
Park, Flex and Open Space.

This request is for approval of a 24 acre park master plan. The first phase of the park will be
constructed this summer. This area will be approximately 10 acres along the south end of the
site. This will include parking, asphalt trails, some streambank restoration, a fishing pier and play
features. There will be an 80’ x 24’ block building constructed for park maintenance storage, an
office and public restrooms. The West Valley City Police Department will also have space on the
west end of the building for ATV storage to assist in patrols of the area.

The sidewalk will be expanded along the more improved areas of the site and connect the two
proposed parking areas. Then the internal trail system will provide the access to the south. There
is a large grade difference between the site and Cultural Center Dr and sidewalk is existing on
the west side of the road as well. The streetscape standards for 3500 South will be completed per
7-10 along the south boundary of the site.

The north end of the site will be constructed starting sometime next summer and will provide
connections to the existing trail system, additional parking areas as well as a disc golf course.

Staff Alternatives

Approval subject to any issues raised at the public hearing as well as the following conditions:

   1. The site shall be constructed per the approved plans and must meet the requirements of

ZT-2-2021
West Valley City
A zone text change to add regulations for internal accessory dwelling units


This application was continued by the Planning Commission on April 14, 2021.

City staff is proposing an ordinance amendment to add regulations for internal accessory
dwelling units. HB 82 was passed by the Utah Legislature in the 2021 General Session of the
Utah Legislature and later signed by the governor. A copy of this bill is included with this report.
Among other changes, the bill “requires municipalities and counties to classify certain accessory
dwelling units as a permitted land use” and “prohibits municipalities and counties from
establishing restrictions or requirements for certain accessory dwelling units with limited
exceptions”. In summary, cities must now allow internal accessory dwelling units within single
family dwellings and the State now limits the extent of local regulation of internal accessory
dwelling units.

Below is the list of items cities can regulate under HB 82:

   a) prohibit the installation of a separate utility meter for an internal accessory dwelling unit;
   b) require that an internal accessory dwelling unit be designed in a manner that does not
      change the appearance of the primary dwelling as a single-family dwelling;
   c) require a primary dwelling:
                   i. to include one additional on-site parking space for an internal accessory
                      dwelling unit, regardless of whether the primary dwelling is existing or
                      new construction; and
                  ii. to replace any parking spaces contained within a garage or carport if an
                      internal accessory dwelling unit is created within the garage or carport;
   d) prohibit the creation of an internal accessory dwelling unit within a mobile home as
      defined in Section 57-16-3;
   e) require the owner of a primary dwelling to obtain a permit or license for renting an
      internal accessory dwelling unit;
   f) prohibit the creation of an internal accessory dwelling unit within a zoning district
      covering an area that is equivalent to:
                   i. 25% or less of the total area in the municipality that is zoned primarily for
                       residential use; or
                  ii. 67% or less of the total area in the municipality that is zoned primarily for
                       residential use, if the main campus of a state or private university with a
                       student population of 10,000 or more is located within the municipality;
   g) prohibit the creation of an internal accessory dwelling unit if the primary dwelling is
      served by a failing septic tank;
   h) prohibit the creation of an internal accessory dwelling unit if the lot containing the
      primary dwelling is 6,000 square feet or less in size;
   i) prohibit the rental or offering the rental of an internal accessory dwelling unit for a period
      of less than 30 consecutive days;
   j) prohibit the rental of an internal accessory dwelling unit if the internal accessory dwelling
      unit is located in a dwelling that is not occupied as the owner's primary residence;
   k) hold a lien against a property that contains an internal accessory dwelling unit in
      accordance with Subsection (5); and
   l) record a notice for an internal accessory dwelling unit in accordance with Subsection (6).

Items a-f and h-j are all addressed in the proposed ordinance. Item g refers to septic tanks and
does not apply to West Valley. Items k and l can be done without an ordinance amendment.

Item f was addressed by prohibiting internal accessory dwelling units east of 4000 West. The
stated goal of HB 82 from the bill’s sponsor, Ray Ward, was to increase the availability of
affordable housing. The east side of the City has more affordable housing than the west side and
single family homes on the east side of the City are generally older and smaller than those on the
west side. For these reasons, staff is suggesting that internal accessory dwelling units not be
allowed east of 4000 West.

As of February of this year, the total area in the City that was zoned primarily for residential use
was just under 10,000 acres. The area of single family home parcels east of 4000 West that are
larger than 6,000 square feet (since lots 6,000 square feet or less in area are already excluded) is
nearly 1,900 acres. Hence, the proposed prohibition of internal accessory dwelling units east of
4000 West is below the 25% maximum of the area zoned primarily for residential use.

During the study meeting the City’s Chief Building Official, Jerry Thompson, provided
information on how building code requirements for internal accessory dwelling units were
amended as part of HB 82. His presentation is included with this report.

Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval. The ordinance should be approved as proposed by staff.
   2. Continuance.

               enough time to review this proposal and feels the regulations for internal
               accessory dwelling units proposed by staff are reasonable.

               Commissioner Fuller seconded the motion.

Z-3-2021
Russell Platt
3995 South Redwood Road
Zone change from C-2 to RM
1.12 acres

Russell Platt has submitted a zone change application for two parcels totaling 1.12 acres located
at 3995 S Redwood Rd. The property is currently zoned C-2 (General Commercial) with a
General Plan designation of Mixed Use (General Commercial and High Density Residential
along this section of Redwood Rd.). The proposed zone is RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling
Residential).

Surrounding zones include C-2 to the north and south and RM to the east and west. Surrounding
land uses include commercial to the north and south and apartments to the east and west.
According to Google Earth imagery, the property included three buildings that were demolished
sometime between 2007 and 2009. The property is currently vacant.

The applicant is proposing a 15-unit, 3-story, townhome development. The proposed density of
the project is 13.4 units/acre. Each unit would have a two-car garage and a driveway large
enough to park two vehicles in. The proposed unit size is 1,444 sq. ft. and all units would include
rooftop decks. The attached concept plan and application materials from the applicant provide
details on and support for the proposed project.
In reviewing this application, staff suggests that there are two primary questions to answer:
   1. Is this the right location for a townhome development?

   2. Assuming the location is appropriate, does the quality of the project further City goals?

These two questions are explored below.

Is this the right location for a townhome development?

According to the City’s zoning ordinance, zone changes to the RM zone can only be considered
by the City if the following four conditions are met:

   1. For properties without existing Structures which are proposed to be developed, the
      property must be a minimum of one acre and the Density must not exceed 12 units per
      acre. For properties with existing Structures proposed to be redeveloped, the property
      must be a minimum of 20,000 square feet and the Density must not exceed 20 units per
      acre. For properties that are west of Redwood Road and within one-half mile of the West
      Valley Central light rail station, the Decker Lake light rail station or the Redwood
      Junction light rail station, the Density may exceed 20 units per acre;

   2. The property shall meet any one of the following four requirements: (i) have access and
      Frontage on a Street with a planned Right-of-way width of at least 80 feet (for properties
      under two acres) or 100 feet (for properties two acres or larger) as indicated on the Major
      Street Plan; (ii) be adjacent to existing multifamily residential development on two sides;
      or (iii) be located west of Redwood Road and within one-half mile of the West Valley
      Central light rail station, the Decker Lake light rail station, or the Redwood Junction light
      rail station; or (iv) if the property is vacant, was vacant as of January 19, 2021, will be an
      exempt housing development under 24 CFR 100.304 or successor regulation with a
      density no greater than seven units per acre, and have access and Frontage on a Street
      with a planned Right-of-way width of at least 80 feet;

   3. A Development Agreement must be proposed with the zone change application that
      addresses Dwelling Unit sizes, exterior materials, architecture, Landscaping, and project
      Amenities; and

   4. A traffic impact study prepared by a registered traffic engineer is required for projects
      with 100 or more Dwelling Units.

On the first condition, the applicant contends that there are still foundations in the ground from
the buildings that were demolished that should be considered as structures since structures are
defined in City Code as “anything constructed or erected on or below the ground”. Assuming the
Planning Commission agrees with this assessment, the application meets the first condition in
that the property is over 20,000 square feet and the density is below 20 units/acre. If the Planning
Commission does not agree with this assessment, the property would still meet the first
condition; however, the number of units would need to be reduced to 13 so that the density did
not exceed to 12 units/acre.
For the second condition, the subject property has access and frontage on Redwood Road, which
has a planned right-of-way width of 115’. The property is also adjacent to existing multi-family
residential development on two sides. On the third condition, the applicant’s development
agreement standards are addressed later in this report. The fourth condition does not apply
because the project is under 100 units.

While the property is eligible for RM zoning, the City may still deny requests for RM zoning.

The points below may help the Commission answer the first question.

      The property has a relatively narrow frontage (91’) for a commercial property and is 556’
       deep at its deepest point.
      Redwood Road is currently a 7-lane arterial street that is serviced by UTA bus route 217.
      Average annual daily traffic (AADT) on this segment of Redwood Road has held steady
       at 35,000 from 2017 to 2019. A 7-lane arterial can handle a traffic volume of 40,000 at a
       level of service (LOS) C and 46,000 at a LOS D. According to the Institute of
       Transportation Engineers, townhomes generate 5.81 trips per day.
      There are commercial services like a Smith’s, CVS, and restaurants within walking
       distance of the site.

Does the quality of the project further City goals?

City Code requires requests for the RM zone to be accompanied with a development agreement
proposal that addresses dwelling unit sizes, exterior materials, architecture, landscaping and
project amenities. Each of these items is addressed below.

Dwelling Unit Sizes

The proposed units include 3 bedrooms with a minimum unit size is 1,444 sq. ft. For dwelling
units that are attached to the side of each other, City Code requires 1,400 sq. ft. for a 3-bedroom
unit. Other recently approved townhome projects have included units that are significantly larger.
Introducing a 2nd and 3rd floor cantilever over the 1st floor on the north elevation would increase
the unit area.

Exterior Materials

For materials, the City’s multi-unit residential design standards state: “All multi-unit residential
Building exteriors shall be brick, stucco, stone, tile, metal panels or fiber cement siding. With the
exception of brick or stone which may be used on the entire Building exterior, no single material
shall comprise more than 60 percent of a Building’s exterior. Where stucco, tile, metal panels or
fiber cement siding are used, at least 20 percent of the Building’s exterior shall be brick or stone.
Wood and vinyl may be used as trim, soffits and/or accent materials only.”

The proposed materials are stucco, fiber cement siding and tile. Without a development
agreement exception, a minimum of 20% brick or stone will be needed.
Architecture

Based on a preliminary review of the proposed building architecture by staff and the Planning
Commission, the following revisions are recommended:

      To comply with Subsection 4 of Section 7-11-404, introduce some design variety
       between buildings.
      To comply with Section 7-11-407 on the north elevation of the buildings, offset the
       middle unit by 2’.
      Cantilever the 2nd and 3rd floors over the 1st floor on the north elevation of the buildings
       by 1’ or more.
      On the north elevation, either change the color by unit or add relief to the where color
       changes occur.
      Increase the contrast in color changes.
      Make the roof of the stairway leading to the roof deck flat instead of angled or sloped.

Landscaping/Recreation Space

The recently adopted ordinance addressing landscaping and recreation space requires a total of
50% landscaping and recreation space for a planned unit development (PUD). The concept
submitted by the applicant shows 50.7% of the site as landscaping and recreation space.

Project Amenities

For a project of this size, the ordinance requires one amenity. The proposed project includes one
amenity from the ordinance, an outdoor fire pit, and one amenity not listed in the ordinance, a
dog run.

Other Considerations

Along the east side of the property and roughly the east half of the north side there is an existing
chain link fence with slats. Staff recommends a vinyl fence to replace or be placed next to the
existing chain link with slats and a masonry wall along the north side adjacent to the neighboring
parking lot to the north.

Staff Alternatives:

   3. Approval. This zone change application should be approved subject to a development
      agreement that incorporates the standards proposed by the applicant with the following
      additions recommended by staff:
          a. The architectural revisions addressed in this staff report shall be incorporated into
             the project.
          b. At least 20% of the building exteriors shall be tile.
          c. Fencing shall include a 6’ tall, vinyl fence along the east property line and along
             the north property line where a chain link fence exists. A 6’ tall, masonry wall
             shall be installed along the north property line adjacent to the parking lot on the
             adjoining lot to the north.
          d. Upon completion of all of the on-site improvements, the developer shall provide a
             letter to the City from an independent engineering firm which certifies that all of
             the on-site improvements are completed as shown on the plans approved by the
             City. For the purposes of this provision, on-site improvements shall include
             landscaping, irrigation, private streets, sidewalks, parking, lighting, entrance
             features, fencing, and any other outdoor recreational amenity.

   4. Approval. This zone change application should be approved subject to a development
      agreement that incorporates the standards in staff alternative #1 above with the following
      additions/revisions:
          a. The number of units shall not exceed 13 (12 units/acre).
          b. At least 20% of the exterior shall be brick or stone.

   5. Continuance. This application should be continued for reasons determined during the
      public hearing.

   6. Denial. This application should be denied. C-2 zoning is more appropriate at this
      location.


Discussion:   Steve Pastorik presented the application. Russell Platt explained there is a
              foundation and slab that needs to be removed on the subject location. He believes

              Commissioner Fuller agree with Commissioner Woodruff and feels a commercial

              Commissioner McEwen said as a Planning Commission, we need to balance the

              Chair Meaders re-opened public comment.
              Mr. Platt explained that this site has been vacant for a long time. He has reached

              commercial developers. He feels residents will enjoy the convenience of a

Motion:       Commissioner Woodruff motioned to deny Z-3-2021, the C-2 zoning is more
              appropriate at this location.
              Commissioner Fuller seconded the motion.


   Vote:      Commissioner Fuller           Yes

City Code requires requests for the RM zone to be accompanied by a development agreement

Dwelling Unit Sizes

Exterior Materials

For materials, the City’s multi-unit residential design standards state: “All multi-unit residential
Building exteriors shall be brick, stucco, stone, tile, metal panels or fiber cement siding. With the
exception of brick or stone which may be used on the entire Building exterior, no single material
shall comprise more than 60 percent of a Building’s exterior. Where stucco, tile, metal panels or
fiber cement siding are used, at least 20 percent of the Building’s exterior shall be brick or stone.
Wood and vinyl may be used as trim, soffits and/or accent materials only.”
The applicant is proposing a combination of fiber cement siding and brick or stone where the
brick or stone would be at least 20% of the exterior. This means that fiber cement siding would
comprise more than 60% of the exterior. The applicant is asking for an exception here as the
fiber cement siding will have a mix of horizontal, vertical, and shingle patterns in lieu of a third
material.

Architecture

Based on a preliminary review of the proposed building architecture by staff, the proposed units
appear to meet the architectural requirements in the City’s multi-unit residential design
standards. The percentage of windows on the front may be under the required amount. If this
application is approved, a more thorough review will be conducted by staff on the elevations
submitted for a building permit.

Other projects recently reviewed by the Planning Commission included an offset between
garages to add more building relief. Staff recommends that offsets between the garages be
included.

Landscaping

The recently adopted ordinance addressing landscaping and recreation space requires a total of
50% landscaping and recreation space for a planned unit development (PUD). While the latest
concept submitted by the applicant does not include a landscaping and recreation space area
calculation, a previous concept plan showed over 50%. No exception is being requested.

Project Amenities

For a project of this size, the ordinance requires one amenity. The proposed project includes two
amenities from the ordinance: a sport court and pavilion. Interior finishes and amenities include
two-tone paint and solid surface countertops in kitchens and bathrooms.

In the proposed development agreement standards, the developer is also proposing a concrete
address plate for each unit, streetlights, a combination of vinyl and concrete fencing installed on
the perimeter, and 11 visitor parking spaces.

GPZ-9-2021
Russell Platt
General Plan change from General Commercial to High Density Residential and a zone
change from C-2 to RM
1.81 acres

Russell Platt has submitted a General Plan/zone change application for two parcels totaling 1.81
acres located at 2351 South Redwood Road. The property is currently zoned C-2 (General
Commercial) with a General Plan designation of General Commercial. The proposed zone is RM
(Multiple Unit Dwelling Residential) and the proposed General Plan designation is High Density
Residential (12 to 20 units/acre).

Surrounding zones include RM to the east and south, A (Agriculture, minimum lot size ½ acre)
to the north, and M (Manufacturing) to the west. Surrounding land uses include townhomes to
the east and south, a single family home to the north, and commercial uses to the west.
According to Google Earth imagery, there were homes on the properties that were demolished
sometime between 2015 and 2016. The property is currently vacant.

The applicant is proposing a 24-unit, 2 to 3-story townhome development. The proposed density
of the project is 13.3 units/acre. Each unit would have a two-car garage and 18 of the units would
have a driveway large enough to park two vehicles in. The proposed unit sizes range from 1,763
to 2,117 sq. ft. and all units would include rooftop decks. The attached concept plan and
application materials from the applicant provide details on and support for the proposed project.

In reviewing this application, staff suggests that there are two primary questions to answer:

   5. Is this the right location for a townhome development?

   6. Assuming the location is appropriate, does the quality of the project further City goals?

These two questions are explored below.

Is this the right location for a townhome development?

According to the City’s zoning ordinance, zone changes to the RM zone can only be considered
by the City if the following four conditions are met:
    9. For properties without existing Structures which are proposed to be developed, the
       property must be a minimum of one acre and the Density must not exceed 12 units per
       acre. For properties with existing Structures proposed to be redeveloped, the property
        must be a minimum of 20,000 square feet and the Density must not exceed 20 units per
        acre. For properties that are west of Redwood Road and within one-half mile of the West
        Valley Central light rail station, the Decker Lake light rail station or the Redwood
        Junction light rail station, the Density may exceed 20 units per acre;
    10. The property shall meet any one of the following four requirements: (i) have access and
        Frontage on a Street with a planned Right-of-way width of at least 80 feet (for properties
        under two acres) or 100 feet (for properties two acres or larger) as indicated on the Major
        Street Plan; (ii) be adjacent to existing multifamily residential development on two sides;
        or (iii) be located west of Redwood Road and within one-half mile of the West Valley
        Central light rail station, the Decker Lake light rail station, or the Redwood Junction light
        rail station; or (iv) if the property is vacant, was vacant as of January 19, 2021, will be an
        exempt housing development under 24 CFR 100.304 or successor regulation with a
        density no greater than seven units per acre, and have access and Frontage on a Street
        with a planned Right-of-way width of at least 80 feet;
    11. A Development Agreement must be proposed with the zone change application that
        addresses Dwelling Unit sizes, exterior materials, architecture, Landscaping, and project
        Amenities; and
    12. A traffic impact study prepared by a registered traffic engineer is required for projects
        with 100 or more Dwelling Units.
On the first condition, the applicant contends that there are still foundations in the ground from
the buildings that were demolished that should be considered as structures since structures are
defined in City Code as “anything constructed or erected on or below the ground”. Assuming the
Planning Commission agrees with this assessment, the application meets the first condition in
that the property is over 20,000 square feet and the density is below 20 units/acre. If the Planning
Commission does not agree with this assessment, the property would still meet the first
condition; however, the number of units would need to be reduced to 21 so that the density did
not exceed to 12 units/acre.
For the second condition, the subject property has access and frontage on Redwood Road, which
has a planned right-of-way width of 115’. The property is also adjacent to existing multi-family
residential development on two sides. On the third condition, the applicant’s development
agreement standards are addressed later in this report. The fourth condition does not apply
because the project is under 100 units.

The points below may help the Commission answer the first question.

      The subject property was rezoned from A to C-2 in 2010 in anticipation of a commercial
       development that never materialized.
      Redwood Road is currently a 7-lane arterial street that is serviced by UTA bus route 217.
      Average annual daily traffic (AADT) on this segment of Redwood Road was 26,000 in
       2019. A 7-lane arterial can handle a traffic volume of 40,000 at a level of service (LOS)
       C and 46,000 at a LOS D. According to the Institute of Transportation Engineers,
       townhomes generate 5.81 trips per day.
      There are commercial services like groceries, a convenience store, and restaurants within
       walking distance of the site.

Does the quality of the project further City goals?

Dwelling Unit Sizes
The proposed unit sizes for the three unit types are 1,763, 1,841, and 2,117 square feet. Each unit
will have three bedrooms plus an additional room that could function as an office, bedroom, or
other living space. The ordinance requires a minimum area of 1,400 square feet for a three-
bedroom townhome and 1,700 square feet for a four-bedroom townhome.

Exterior Materials
The ordinance states the following concerning exterior materials: “All multi-unit residential
Building exteriors shall be brick, stucco, stone, tile, metal panels or fiber cement siding. With the
exception of brick or stone which may be used on the entire Building exterior, no single material
shall comprise more than 60 percent of a Building’s exterior. Where stucco, tile, metal panels or
fiber cement siding are used, at least 20 percent of the Building’s exterior shall be brick or stone.
Wood and vinyl may be used as trim, soffits and/or accent materials only.”

The proposed materials are stucco, fiber cement siding and stone. The two buildings adjacent to
Redwood Rd. include stone; however, it does not appear to be 20% of the exterior. The buildings
to the east do not include stone. Without a development agreement exception, a minimum of
20% brick or stone will be needed.

Architecture
Based on a preliminary review of the proposed building architecture by staff, the following
revisions are recommended:

       doors.
      For the buildings that include 2 and 3 stories, changes of material should be accompanied
       by a relief change.

Landscaping/Recreation Space
Townhome developments require at least 50% of the site as landscaping and recreation space.
According to the applicant, 50% of the site would be landscaped and the rooftop decks increase
the landscaping and recreation space to 66%.

Project Amenities
For a project of this size, the ordinance requires one amenity. The proposed project includes one
amenity from the ordinance, a playground, and two amenities not listed in the ordinance - a dog
run and picnic areas.

buildings with two stories or less, the maximum building height at the minimum side and rear
yard setback shall be 25 feet. For each foot of height over 25 feet, buildings shall be set back an
additional foot from the minimum setback, with a maximum total building height of 75 feet.”
Since the property to the south is landscaped for the first 140’ or so and since the home to the
north will very likely be redeveloped, staff recommends the following minimum setbacks:

     South Buildings
            o For the building facing Redwood Road, the minimum side setback shall be 10’.
            o For all other buildings along the north side of the property, the minimum rear yard
