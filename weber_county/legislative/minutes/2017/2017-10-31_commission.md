    1. DISCUSSION AND/OR ACTION ON A LIBRARY IN WESTERN WEBER COUNTY.
         Farr West City Mayor Lee Dickamore said that there is a need for a library in the northwest area of the
         county, that it is an issue of fairness and that in 1999 and in 2014 the commissioners had an opportunity to
         put a library in the area but it was taken out of the budget with the funds going for other needed
         improvements. He had met individually with the commissioners recently with a plan on how to fund a
         library—the City is willing to give the county 3½ acres worth $1+ million. The commissioners had pointed
         out the many capital project improvements that need funding. He subsequently met with the Library Board,
         various area school principals and the PTSA presidents. He said that the various groups are unanimous in
         their desire for a library. He has not heard back from the commissioners on a plan for a library.
         The commissioners expressed their strong support for libraries. They also have to be fiscally responsible
         with taxpayer funds. Scott Parke, County Comptroller, stated that there are a lot of county projects that
         need funding. The county went through a tax increase last year because it had not been funding its capital
         projects as needed, and there was a large amount (over $40 million, including library projects) of unfunded
         capital projects. There still is not enough money to fund all the project needs. Mr. Parke cautioned that the
         county is running out of sales tax capacity to issue new debt, and if the county were to issue debt for a
         library now it would hinder its capacity to issue debt for any infrastructure needs that may come up. He
         referred to the serious flooding issue that brought to light the under maintained storm water system, which
         ended up being a $25 million project, and if a similar problem arose and the county had used its sales tax
         capacity for issuing debt, it would be in a difficult position. He warned against maxing out our bonding
         capacity. John Bond, County Treasurer, presented two options for the library issue: 1) it could go to the
         public for a vote as a bond, which would result in a lower interest rate and reassurance that this is what the
         community wants. It was the method used in 2013 for the libraries, or 2) use the county’s sales tax revenue
         as collateral. He said that first and foremost a lot of information needs to be gathered, analyzed and
         discussed. He referred to the discussion he had with the county’s financial advisor. Last October the county
         went out for a $10 million sales tax bond.
   Mr. Bond stated that if the county took another $10-15 million sales tax bond right now, it would reduce
   the county’s coverage and the AAA bond rating would likely drop, which came at a high cost, and the
   county’s interest rate would also increase. His advice to the commissioners recently on large capital
   projects was to hold off a bit and gain a little more fund balance and proceed a little slower. There have
   been several large projects in the past five years. This is his advice to the commissioners now. Mr. Bond
   cautioned that it is not a good idea to go from a AAA to a AA bond rating; these are risks the county would
   encounter if it goes out for another $10-15 million bond using sales tax. He needed more information; an
   analysis will take 3-4 months. He explained that to go for a public vote sends a stronger signal to the
   bonding lending community. The Weber Center building bond will be paid off in three years.
   Commissioner Harvey pointed out other associated costs that have to be planned for besides the donated
   land—the structure would be another $10-12 million, another half a million dollars for books and
   infrastructure, and ongoing costs of about another half million to $600,000 dollars annually for operating
   costs. He stated that from today’s discussion it would be fiscally irresponsible to act too quickly on this
   and that data is also needed. Commissioner Gibson feels that the library system would be incomplete
   without a library in that area and it should be made as high a priority as possible. Chair Ebert reiterated
   that this Commission supports libraries, that the county has constraints but also understands fairness and
   equity and will prioritize a library.

5. RESOLUTION AUTHORIZING PARTICIPATION IN SMALL BUSINESS LOAN PROGRAM ADMINISTERED BY
   BUSINESS LOANS OF UTAH – RESOLUTION 44-2017.
   Douglas Larsen, Weber County Economic Development Partnership Director, stated that the county is
   appropriating $100,000 in 1/2018, when this program will start, to invest in the growth of small businesses
   by participating in this loan fund administered by the Business Loans of Utah. The funds provide access
   to critical investment capital for underserved county businesses. For every $1 the county appropriates it
   leverages $7. The loans have a 5-year term. He introduced Mike Plaizier and Paul Johnson, of Utah Center
   for Neighborhood Stabilization, as the brains behind the program, which Mr. Plaizier highlighted. Chair
   Ebert outlined the process including that the county will review the applications, Mr. Larsen will make
   recommendations and the credit committee will make the final determination. Mr. Plaizier stated that they
   have received approval on the fund from the lender, Zions Bank, for $7 million.

   8. APPROVAL OF THE WEBER COUNTY 2018 TENTATIVE BUDGET.
       Scott Parke, County Comptroller, outlined the budget process, which includes a 1% employee COLA
       adjustment and a .25 cent/hour increase to offset increased health insurance costs. The budget includes the
       capital projects plan, which last year had unfunded needs of about $19 million over the next five years in
       excess of what the county had available for those projects. There are still about $10 million of unfunded
       capital project needs over the next five years. This year about $10 million will be spent to finish the
       libraries, $600,000 for deferred maintenance, repairs and equipment, and about $500,000 to help improve
       the Weber Center, including the parking structure repairs to try stretching out its use a little longer. The
       budget includes the county-wide capital projects plan. There is also about $35.5 million in requests by
       department heads/elected officials with a huge shortfall. Additionally, there is a list of potential capital
       projects, not included on the list, because they have not yet been evaluated/prioritized, for about another
       $20 million.
       Commissioner Harvey moved to approve the 2018 tentative budget; Commissioner Gibson seconded.
