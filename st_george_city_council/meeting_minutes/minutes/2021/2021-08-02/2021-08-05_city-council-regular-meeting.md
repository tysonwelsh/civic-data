AMEND TITLE 7, CHAPTER 5 OF CITY CODE/ORDINANCE:
    Consider approval of an ordinance amending Title 7, Chapter 5 of City code
    regarding cemetery policies and abandonment of cemetery burial lots due to
    passage of time.

      Leisure Services Director Shane Moore highlighted the proposed amendments to Title
      7, Chapter 5 of City code pertaining to cemetery policies. If a person who owns a
      burial right would like someone other than themselves to be buried in the lot, the
      owner would sell it back to the City and have that other person purchase the lot. If
      the person who owns a burial passes away, their lot(s) can be transferred to a
      surviving spouse or child.

      City Attorney Shawn Guzman noted it has been difficult to determine who has the
      right to lots that were purchase many years ago. These changes will simply the
      process.

      Mr. Moore noted the ordinance will keep the abandonment of burial lots after sixty
      years. There would be no reimbursement of these lots as they would be deemed
      abandoned.

ZONE CHANGE/ORDINANCE:
     Consider approval of an ordinance changing the zone from R-2 (Residential
     Multi-Family) to RE-20 (Residential Estate, 20,000 sq ft minimum lot size)
     on approximately 17.51 acres and OS (Open Space) on approximately 15.38
     acres located generally at the southeast intersection of Riverside Drive and
     Foremaster Drive to be known as Knetta's Knoll. Case No. 2021-ZC-042

      Planner Dan Boles presented the request to change the zone from R-2 to RE-20 on
      approximately 17.51 acres and OS on approximately 15.38 acres located generally at
      the southeast intersection of Riverside Drive and Foremaster Drive to be known as
      Knetta's Knoll. He provided a PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics:
      Aerial map; Existing zoning map; and Existing general plan map. The Planning
      Commission recommended approval.

      After being asked by Councilmembers McArthur and Larkin, applicant Adam Allen
      explained they will be terracing. The preliminary plat and hillside development
      permit will be brought before the Council at the next meeting. They have done
      extensive soils research and testing regarding the stability of the slopes.

      Councilmember Larkin mentioned a letter received from Todd Prince, an
      archaeologist regarding petroglyphs.

      Mr. Allen noted he met with Greg Woodall who is an archeologist as well. They have
      hired Dale Gorley who did an archaeological survey who has identified the
      petroglyphs on site. Some of them will be moved to the open space while others will
      be left in place. There is an area at the north end of the property that has not been
      disturbed. The archeologist recommended thus area not be disturbed and advised to
      test the area for burials before grading.

      MOTION:
          A motion was made by Councilmember Larkin to approve the
                    ordinance changing the zone from R-2 to RE-20 on approximately
                    17.51 acres and OS on approximately 15.38 acres located generally at
                    the southeast intersection of Riverside Drive and Foremaster Drive.

CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT:
     Consider approval of a conditional use permit for the short-term rental of a
     local landmark to be known as the Thomas Cottam Home on approximately
     .20 acres located at 464 East 500 South Street. Case No. 2021-CUP-010

      Planner Carol Davidson presented the request for a conditional use permit for the
      short-term rental of a local landmark to be known as the Thomas Cottam Home on
      approximately .20 acres located at 464 East 500 South Street. She provided a
      PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics: Zoning map; Site plans; 10-
      13E-4B – conditions: 1) Low impact, 2) Substantial investment, 3) Renovations
      maintain historic features, 4) Minimal traffic or parking demand, 5) Additional
      condition; and Possible motion. She mentioned letters received from neighbors who
      had concerns.

      Councilmember Larkin comment that she has not seen an accessory dwelling unit
      (ADU) with short-term rental. She asked if this request brings up any issues with
      City code.

      Ms. Davidson explained staff discussed this item, concluding that having the owner
      living on the property is an excellent way to make sure the renters are not creating
      issues. When the ADU is finished, the deed will need to be changed stating that the
      owner will live on the property at all times.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard noted enforcement would be problematic.

      Councilmember McArthur suggested looking at the City’s landmark preservation
      code. When this came before the Council a while back, he appreciated the research
      that had gone into declaring the home a landmark; however, he was not aware there
      would be an accessory dwelling bigger than home itself. This no longer feels like a
      local landmark, rather making it a great rental property. He noted that this request
      has met all of the City codes but he doesn’t know if this were something he would
      like to continue moving forward.

      Councilmember Larkin agreed and commented that the Council should discuss the
      code with the Historic Preservation Commission. She is not opposed to a home
      owner building an ADU; however, she asked if the architecture should match the
      existing historic home.

      Ms. Davidson noted the City’s regulations require the ADU to match the main unit.
      This particular unit does match the existing home.

      Councilmember Hughes added the point of easing up on the ADU regulation is to
      provide additional housing; nightly rentals do not help the situation.

      Councilmember Larkin mentioned the City does not allow short term rentals in the
      downtown area other than in the historic homes to be able to make enough revenue
      to keep up the historic home or they tear it down and build a new home.

      Councilmember McArthur commented that he does not think this condition works for
      the area. He would like to visit the local landmark code in the future.

      Councilmember Larkin stated as far as short-term rentals go, it does help to know
      that the owner will be required to live in the home and that the ADU has to match
      architecturally.

      MOTION:
          A motion was made by Councilmember Larkin to approve the

                    be known as the Thomas Cottam Home on approximately .20 acres
                    located at 464 East 500 South Street.

PRELIMINARY PLAT:
     Consider approval of the preliminary plat for Willow Bend Townhomes, an
     81-lot residential subdivision located on the northeast corner of the
     intersection of Judy Lane and Riverside Drive.

      Public Works Director Cameron Cutler presented the preliminary plat for Willow Bend
      Townhomes, an 81-lot residential subdivision located on the northeast corner of the
      intersection of Judy Lane and Riverside Drive. He showed and outlined the
      preliminary plat. After being asked by Councilmember Larkin, he explained public
      vs. private road. Fire code has to be bet with private roads as well.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard advised staff would oppose these being public streets.
      He noted this property has not been developed as soils are not good. Typically, staff
      would prefer roads to be public; however, in the case of this item as well as the
      previous one, staff would prefer the roads remain private with recorded notices on
      each lot that advise the buyers that the roads are and will always remain private, the
      City has no intention of taking them over or maintaining them. He noted he would
      prefer it would be all of the streets.

      MOTION:
          A motion was made by Councilmember McArthur to approve the
                    preliminary plat for Willow Bend Townhomes with the stipulation that
                    all roads within development be private roads.
      SECOND:
          The motion was seconded by Councilmember Larkin.

      Applicant Dave Nasal stated that the plat was initially proposed as private; however,
      staff insisted that they be public so they rearranged things to accommodate that.
