12            Discussion of Agreement with Waste Management, Blake Leonelli, Manager, and
13            Public Section Solutions and Frank Santiago - David Brickey, City Manager
14
15   City Manager David Brickey explained that Waste Management would be presenting their slides
16   and there was not a need for comments and asked the Council should consult with Ryan Carter,
17   City Attorney if they had any questions.
18
19   Blake Leonelli thanked the Council for being willing to listen to the presentation and expressed
20   his desire for an open dialogue. He explained that Waste Management has seen that recycling
21   has a diversion rate of 11% the previous year and it is roughly 8% this year. Mr. Leonelli said
22   that recycling efforts had made a difference in the community. He explained that the diversion
23   rate could be a result of the pandemic because people were staying home more and creating
24   more trash.
25
26   Mayor Staggs asked Mr. Leonelli to clarify the diversion rate from the first slide he presented.
27
28   Mr. Leonelli clarified that the diversion rate was 10.8% and is now down to 8.3%, diversion,
29   meaning the amount of trash that is being diverted from the landfill because people are
30   recycling. Mr. Leonelli talked about the increased costs they have had due to COVID-19. He
31   explained that Waste Management puts safety first and has provided additional PPE to their
32   drivers and established other programs for their employees to provide wage guarantees and
33   assistance with child and elderly care.
34
35   Mr. Leonelli explained that they were interested in having the difficult conversation with Riverton
36   City regarding the contract that was entered and the future of that contract. He explained that
37   Waste Management has had a substantial increase in actual costs, which is related to the local
38   hauling site and the growing wages. He asked that the City consider two different options. The
39   first option being that the City work with Waste Management to adjust pricing and pass this on
40   to the citizens. The second option would be for Riverton City to do an RFP and look for other
41   options outside of Waste Management.
42
43   Council Member Wells asked when the contract with Waste Management began.
44
45   Mr. Leonelli explained that the initial contract was five years with an additional five year option to
46   renew and that the City is seven and one-half years into the contract.

 1   Councilmember McDougal asked why the Southwest Vision is considering 13400 South over
 2   12600 South and whether they have traffic studies to support their plans.
 3
 4   Trace Robinson explained that 13400 South option originally came from an engineer about six
 5   years ago and at that time we expressed that this would not be a good option because of
 6   Mountain View Village. Riverton City has been telling them for four years that 13400 South is
 7   not a good option but it continues to show up on their plans. Herriman City has provided support
 8   to Riverton for 12600 South to be the connection road because that is the road coming straight
 9   out of Olympia Hills.

11   Councilmember McDougal asked Mr. Robinson to clarify that Herriman wanted it on 12600
12   South or 13400 South. Mr. Robinson clarified that Herriman City supports the freeway to
13   freeway connection being built on 12600 South.
14
15   In response to Councilmember McDougal regarding traffic studies, Mr. Robinson said that there
16   are traffic studies that have been done but they vary. He said that there have been two studies
17   done. One of the studies done by Ryan Hales shows that with the Olympia Hills traffic, we
18   could have 80,000 vehicles a day on 12600 South where 13400 South was in the 70,000
19   vehicles per day range. Mr. Robinson stated that 12600 South actually showed a higher vehicle
20   count than 13400 South in the future.

22   Councilmember Wells said that he wanted to address the 12600 South detour for the next 6-7

27   but that the preference would be to tie it into 12600 South because 13400 South is not a road
28   that goes all the way to the I-15 freeway.

16       Riverton Police Department Report - Chief Hutson
17
18   Chief Hutson said that they have continued to battle COVID in their ranks through the month of
19   December, which has challenged manpower because they have been required to quarantine
20   some members to prevent mass spread.
21
22   He explained that they are continuing to see higher than normal numbers of vehicle burglaries in
23   the city and an associated dramatic increase in fraud as perpetrators are stealing credit cards
24   from vehicles and immediately utilizing the cards to commit fraud before the victim even realizes
25   the card is missing. It seems the suspects have realized victims are immediately canceling
26   cards and they are making a concerted effort to quickly “cash in” before they are rendered
27   useless. This new strategy accounts for a majority of the fraud cases reported during the
28   month.
29
30   He said that Riverton has had several citizens fall victim to a variety of phone scams which
31   encourage them to purchase gift cards and provide the number and pin of the card over the
32   phone. He said that one victim sold a trailer and the buyer sent her a check for much more than
33   the asking price, then asked the victim to refund the excess amount. Of course, the original
34   check ended up having no money associated with the account.
35
36   Chief expressed that he was extremely thankful that Officer Snell quickly returned to work with
37   no significant injuries after his patrol vehicle was struck by an impaired driver on New Year’s
38   Eve.
39
40   Councilmember Buroker asked Chief Hutson to provide more detail on the COVID vaccination
41   process. Chief Hutson said that the process was great. The police department went to the Salt
42   Lake County buildings and they were asked for a copy of their insurance card and then received
43   either the Moderna or Pfizer vaccine. He said that five officers did not receive the vaccine
44   because they already had COVID-19 and that of the remaining officers, 85% received the
45   vaccine.
