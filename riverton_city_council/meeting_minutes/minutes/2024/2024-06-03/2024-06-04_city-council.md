Discussion Items

       Budget Discussion

Councilmember Pierucci raised a question regarding the budget for the Justice Court,
specifically noting an increase in the cost for bailiffs, which doubled from $17,000 to $34,000.
He sought clarification on the need for two bailiffs and whether the additional bailiff is a part-time
position, given that it is not reflected in the total number of City employees. In response, Ryan
Carter, City Attorney, said the second bailiff was required by the Administrative Office of the
Courts (AOC). The AOC rules require a bailiff to operate within the courtroom, adhering to strict
protocols, and also to manage the magnetometer outside the courtroom entrance. Since these
responsibilities cannot be managed by one person simultaneously, it necessitates having two
security personnel: one inside the courtroom as a bailiff and another outside operating the
magnetometer and managing entrance security.

Councilmember Pierucci asked if the Riverton Police Department could fulfill this requirement.
Mr. Hicks said that the police department could indeed fulfill this role with additional training and
Page | 2 Minutes – City Council Meeting

support. However, he noted that this would likely need to be managed as an overtime
opportunity for existing officers, unless the police department were expanded or other services
were cut. Financially, it makes more sense to continue the contract with the constable due to the
additional costs associated with state retirement system benefits and other related expenses for
police officers.

Councilmember Buroker highlighted that a significant majority of residents in her area (70%)
expressed a preference to keep recycling, with 30% preferring not to continue it. She stressed
the importance of respecting this feedback from the residents. However, the goal remains to
ensure that the sanitation fund operates independently as an enterprise fund without subsidies
from the general fund.

For clarification, Mr. Hicks noted that making the sanitation fund fully self-sustaining while
maintaining recycling services would require an increase in household fees. Staff estimates
indicated that this would involve a $3 per household increase for the first garbage and recycling
totes, and an additional $3 for those with extra recycling totes. This pricing structure was
projected to cover costs for one year, with a potential 50-cent increase in subsequent years
under the current contract with Waste Management.

Mr. Hicks advised against separating the fees for garbage and recycling, as it could lead to
confusion and resistance from residents who might then opt out of recycling, which the current
contract does not support. For now, the focus should be on ensuring the combined fee is clear
and transparent to residents without causing contractual issues or operational complications.

Councilmember Pierucci asked how many Planned Unit Developments (PUDs) do not pay the
City for sanitation services. Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, said there are
approximately 3,400 units or households in Riverton that fall into this category.

Councilmember Pierucci discussed the need for the sanitation enterprise fund to be self-
sustaining. He mentioned his initial support for eliminating recycling to avoid fee increases but
changed his stance after engaging with residents. A survey showed that 70% of residents in his
district wanted to keep recycling despite potential fee increases. He emphasized the importance
of resident feedback and the value of engaging the public in discussions that impact their daily
lives. Based on this feedback, he supported keeping recycling and adjusting fees to ensure the
sanitation fund is self-sustaining.

Councilmember McDougal reiterated the importance of the enterprise fund being independent
and highlighted the misconception among residents about landfills. He explained that landfills
like Trans-Jordan convert waste into methane, which is then used to generate power, thereby
contributing to recycling efforts. He emphasized that this process is environmentally beneficial
and different from the notion that landfilling is purely detrimental.

Based on the data and feedback from residents, Councilmember McDougal expressed support
for eliminating recycling to maintain a self-sustaining enterprise fund, arguing that the landfill's
recycling process is sufficient.

Councilmember Haymond expressed strong support for maintaining curbside recycling,
reflecting the preferences of his constituents. He also advocated for the sanitation fund to be
self-sustaining.

Mayor Pro Tempore McCay clarified her understanding of the proposed fee increase, initially
thinking only the first recycling and trash cans would increase by $3, while additional cans would
remain at their current rate. Upon realizing that the proposal included a $3 increase for all cans,
she acknowledged the potential significant impact on residents with multiple cans, which could
result in over $100 per year in additional costs for some families. She reiterated her support for
curbside recycling, noting that 70% of her residents also favored maintaining this service.

Councilmember McDougal centered on the necessity of increasing fees to ensure that the
sanitation enterprise fund remains self-sustaining. He clarified that the proposed $3 increase for
all trash and recycling cans was essential to cover the costs associated with both the Waste
Management contract and the anticipated increase from Trans-Jordan Landfill. The analysis did
not yet account for the forthcoming cost increase from Trans-Jordan, which is projected to add
approximately $80,000 to the budget. As such, even with the proposed fee increase, there is a
risk of needing a mid-year budget adjustment if these additional costs are not fully covered.

Councilmember Pierucci expressed concern about the proposed fee structure for trash and
recycling services. He highlighted the issue of increasing the base price for all households to
cover the costs incurred by households with multiple trash and recycling cans. He argued that if
the Council is against subsidizing the enterprise fund through general funds, they should also
avoid subsidizing additional cans for some households at the expense of others.

Councilmember Pierucci proposed that households with multiple trash and recycling cans
should pay for the actual cost of those additional services. He supported the idea of a $3
increase across the board for the base service but emphasized that additional cans should have
a higher fee to reflect their true cost. He acknowledged that this might result in a significant fee
increase for households with multiple cans but maintained that it would ensure that residents
pay for what they use.

Councilmember Pierucci also expressed support for an educational campaign about recycling
and contamination rates. He stressed the importance of educating the public about the true
costs associated with Waste Management and the upcoming changes related to Trans-Jordan
Landfill.

Mr. Hicks recalled that a few years ago, a rate study was conducted for garbage collection and it
was recommended that the City adopt a tiered or gradual fee increase. He suggested this as a
potential solution for the current issue, proposing a smaller increase this year with incremental
increases in subsequent years.

Councilmember McDougal expressed concerns about the timing and potential inaccuracies of a
rate study, given the impending cost adjustments from Trans Jordan. He pointed out that
despite discussions about gradual fee increases for the past five years, the City has not
effectively addressed the issue, leading to continued financial challenges. He continued to
advocate for eliminating recycling to cover some costs and move away from subsidies. He
argued that now is the appropriate time to implement necessary changes, as residents have
been adequately informed about the potential fee increase through recent media campaigns.

Councilmembers agreed to continue this discussion later during the City Council meeting.

         Resolution No. 24-53 – Adopting the 2024-2025 Fiscal Year Municipal Fee
         Schedule

Mr. Hicks informed the Council that the proposed fee schedule does not currently include any
increases, despite the discussions earlier in the evening suggesting potential adjustments. The
Council has the option to either approve the fee schedule as it stands or to propose and
approve specific increases immediately. Alternatively, the Council could request an additional
public hearing at the next meeting to discuss and recommend any potential increases.

Councilmember Pierucci expressed a preference to table the discussion of the fee schedule
until a future meeting. He emphasized the importance of adopting the fee schedule
comprehensively rather than making piecemeal adjustments. Additionally, he proposed having a
conversation about implementing a different fee structure for renting City spaces to nonprofit
entities versus for-profit entities.

Councilmember McCay raised questions about the impact fees listed in item 144, specifically
pointing out the fee for daycare centers, which is $31,981 per 1,000 square feet. She noted that
this fee is significantly higher compared to other facilities such as elementary schools ($3,000
per 1,000 square feet), hospitals ($2,700 per 1,000 square feet), and even animal hospitals
($11,000 per 1,000 square feet). She expressed concern that the high fee for daycare centers
might contribute to the rising costs of childcare, making it difficult for families, particularly
working mothers, to afford it.

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that all impact fees are established
through a study, often conducted with the assistance of an outside consultant. These fees are
based on data regarding how different types of facilities impact roadways, including trip
generation numbers, which indicate the volume of traffic generated by a particular use. While it
was acknowledged that the specific details of the study were not immediately available, it was
noted that the high fee for daycare centers likely reflects their higher trip generation numbers
compared to other uses. He assured the Council that the impact fees would be reviewed and
updated soon, and the issue of daycare fees would be carefully examined during that process.

Councilmember McCay raised another question about the boarding fees for animal control,
specifically noting the $10 per day charge for dogs and cats. Additionally, the adoption fee is
listed as $150, which corresponds to only 15 days of boarding costs. She suggested that there
should be a note indicating that the adoption fee could be reduced, particularly during times
when the facility is reaching capacity or during special promotions aimed at encouraging
adoptions. She highlighted the importance of promoting adoptions and mentioned the idea of
organizing adoption events during Town Days, where adoptable pets could be showcased to
find homes.

Councilmember McCay asked about the fee schedule for City-owned properties and suggested
a review of the fees, especially for nonprofits. She referred to a previous resolution that
emphasized increasing opportunities for participation in arts programs and supporting a variety
of public arts initiatives, including theater productions. She advocated for a more detailed
discussion at the next council meeting to explore ways to offer reduced rental fees for
nonprofits, possibly suggesting a discount of half the regular rental fee.

Mr. Hicks estimated the total expenditures, transfers out, and additions to the fund balance

approximately $5.5 million with the additional transfer. This would slightly lower the percentage
of the fund balance relative to the projected revenue.

         Utah, Department of Transportation

Mr. Carter discussed the ongoing renovation of the Butterfield Creek North pond system,

explaining that the current agreement allows the City to commit to the collection of stormwater
from the detention pond immediately to the west. The agreement authorizes signing once the
City receives a $40,000 payment from a regional developer. This agreement addresses
UDOT's requirements and ensures proper stormwater management for the area.

Councilmember Buroker MOVED that the City Council approve Resolution No. 24-49 –
Approving a Cooperative Agreement with the State of Utah, Department of
Transportation. Councilmember McDougal SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for
discussion on the motion. Mayor Staggs called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows:
Buroker-yes, Haymond-yes, McCay-yes, McDougal-yes, and Pierucci-yes. The motion
passed unanimously.
