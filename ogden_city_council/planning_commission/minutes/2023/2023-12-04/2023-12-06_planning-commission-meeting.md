At the applicant's request, Mr. Simpson described the project as the final group dwelling review in order to
determine whether the conditions of preliminary approval are satisfied and whether all ordinance regulations
can be met. He reminded the Commission the property had been rezoned earlier in the year with the
commitment units would be able to be sold as individual units. The request includes some existing single-
family homes along North Street as well as new duplex lots on existing vacant property to the south. As the
group dwelling code limits projects to being under five acres, the project is divided into two group dwelling
projects able to stand on its own, but will interconnect, with a total of 75 dwelling units. Shared access is
provided from North Street, and two-car parking per unit is provided with attached garages for each unit. He
reviewed the location of visitor parking and indicated either roll-out garbage cans or a dumpster can be
provided but either need to be screened from the public view. Amenities are provided as required by the code,
and vehicle charging stations are provided for the surface lot and each unit is to be provided electrical
connections within each garage to allow the owners to install charging if desired in the future. He further
reviewed the requirements for trees within the site as well as perimeter fencing. Some details are not shown to
meet the ordinance requirement, including bike racks and secured storage, entry features/monument sign,
gathering areas, details of the proposed tot lot and landscaping details with an irrigation plan. He also
indicated a 6’ sidewalk is required with identified connecting crosswalks and access to amenity areas. Each
project is required to stand on its own merit, and many of these details can be worked out with Staff. He
identified access into the project as well as emergency access to the UDOT property, stating the developer is
also required to upgrade parking for the three existing homes along North Street with paved access and hard-
surface parking to meet the current regulations. He then reviewed the proposed building materials and design,
stating a stone wainscot is provided on all four sides, with earth-tone colors proposed. He stated dormers and
porches have been added and glazing meets the requirements of the code. Individual hook-ups for washers
and dryers are shown on the floor plans. Staff recommendation is for approval, subject to both projects being
platted so that all of the individual units in the projects can be independently owned, and satisfying all
department staff review comments which include:
1. Installing an entrance monument sign at the main entrance on North Street that meets group dwelling code
     requirements.
2. Planter areas having regionally listed water-wise shrubs that will cover at least 50% of each individual
     planter area at mature growth.
3. Turf areas be limited to areas of high foot traffic, and recreational areas and not exceed 20% of the total
     landscaped area for each group dwelling project.

7. The 6’ tall perimeter fence be a vinyl fence of white or earth-toned colors.

10. All open space amenities and bicycle rack designs be approved by planning staff.
11. The roll-out garbage can storage pads be sized to accommodate two cans and be completely located on

     view from North Street and the private drives.
13. Storm water pollution prevention plan (SWPPP) requirements be met.
14. Individual unit addressing requirements be met.
15. All sewer, water, grading, and storm water requirements be met.
16. All requirements established by the irrigation company for the existing ditch that runs through the property
     be met.
17. A financial guarantee contract be established with Ogden City for landscaping and engineering
     improvements.

4. Public Hearing, Petition to Amend Zoning Map, to rezone property at 218 Chester Street from R-1-6 to R-2S.
   Commissioner Southwick declared a conflict of interest and was recused from the Commission to act as the
   applicant.
   At the applicant's request, Mr. Simpson described the petition as a request to rezone property to allow small-lot
   development, with the applicant desiring to construct individually owned townhomes. The Commission is to
   review rezoning requests to determine their compliance with the General Plan and community plan of the area
   and make a recommendation to the City Council. Once the property is rezoned, the applicant would then be
   required to make an application for a group dwelling/conditional use permit to identify the design of the project.
   The requested zone would allow two-family small lot residential development and would encompass 2.3 acres
   of property containing an existing single-family home. He stated Chester is currently a private right-of-way and
   not dedicated as a public street. He stated the rezone petition includes a concept plan for individually owned
   townhomes. He reviewed the purpose of the R-2S zone, stating it is to allow moderate density development of
   single-family and two-family and single-family rowhouses, emphasizing home ownership. He then reviewed
   the policies of the Lynn Community Plan, stating the R-2S zone allows for residential infill and can create a
   good transition between the single-family neighborhood and the multi-family area to the west. The project can
   be designed so there is good buffering between the area and the existing single-family area. The proposed
   zone would not divide properties but would follow existing parcel boundaries. While the Lynn Community Plan
   is old, it identifies this area as R-2, and acknowledges the opportunity for development of areas which are in
   transition. The subject property is isolated and its redevelopment would allow both pedestrian and vehicle
   connections which currently do not exist. He stated the development as a group dwelling adds standards
   relating to building design, setbacks and screening which further protect the surrounding community. Staff
   feels the R-2S zone creates a transition between the single-family area to the east and the multi-family,
   industrial and commercial areas to the west and south. The proposed development would create better
   interactions between the surrounding community as there is a lack of frontage for much of the property. Staff
   recommends approval of the rezoning petition as requested.

5. Public Building Site Plan Review to determine consistency with the General Plan, for Weber Housing Authority
   32-unit permanent supportive housing and administrative offices at 2325 Madison Avenue.
  At the applicant's request, Ms. Girardo described the proposal as a public building which requires the
  Commission to determine whether it is consistent with the General Plan. The proposal is for County supported
  housing containing 32 units as well as administrative offices in the East Central Community. The building had
  previously been occupied as assisted living in the existing R-2EC zone, and is surrounded by a variety of uses
  including single-family, apartments and a church. The units would be offered for rent and offices would be
  added to provide offices for the Weber Housing Authority. No exterior renovations are proposed, with only
  interior remodeling. The application is as a residential facility for persons with a disability, which by the City
  definition, requires existing licensure with the Department of Human Services. Weber County is not currently
  licensed as such, and while they indicate they can become licensed, the certification currently does not exist
  and the use proposed does not qualify under the City’s standards. In addition, the density of 32 apartments
  exceeds the density allowable in the R-3EC zone, which limits the number of units to 20 on this size of a lot
  which also would require a conditional use permit.
  Ms. Girardo reviewed the existing General Plan, stating it does not support the increased density of the project
  proposed and the surrounding area is zoned for low density housing, and the future land use map shows the
  area as residential. The East Central Community Plan identifies a concern with a concentration of group
  homes and recommends separation be required as well as removal of existing uses which are concentrated in
  the community. There are more than 20 group homes in the East Central neighborhood, many of which do not
  meet current separation requirements. While this location does meet the separation requirement, there are six
  such existing facilities in the nearby neighborhood. Staff feels adding a new facility would be inconsistent with
  the goals and policies of the General Plan. In addition, the use as a public office use is not permitted in the
  residential zones, with the Plan giving direction that existing offices should be removed from neighborhoods.
  The East Central Community had been the location of many buildings being converted to office use until the
  early 1990’s when it was determined these create problems in the residential area, and direction was given at
  that time to eliminate offices from the area. The future land use map does not support either commercial or
  office uses and creation of a new office would conflict with the General Plan. Staff recommendation is that the
  Commission find the proposed public building is not consistent with the General Plan, citing it does not qualify
  as a residential facility for persons with a disability, the density exceeds what is allowable in the zone and is not
  consistent with the General Plan, additional group homes are discouraged in the language of the East Central
  Plan, and the proposed office use is not compliant with the East Central Plan.
  Ms. Andi Beadles, 237 26th Street, expressed Weber Housing Authority feels the use proposed is consistent
  with the General Plan, indicating State licensing is not required of local agencies, but is willing to obtain such
  license prior to occupancy of the building. She stated while the underlying zone limits residency to 20 units,
  Weber Housing intends to submit for reasonable accommodation due to the primary use being to house those
  with disabilities. She stated the General Plan identifies a need to accommodate residential uses with their
  associated necessary public services and activities as well as encouraging government subsidized housing at
  higher densities and provide an orderly transition between less intense density to higher density. The use
  proposed is government subsidized housing and provides services and activities for its residents. She felt the
  direction of the East Central Plan was to amortize facilities which do not meet the separation requirement but
  does not preclude new facilities which meet the separation requirement. Addressing the use as proposed
  office space, she felt the use proposed would not exceed the office space of the previous use. She stated the
  previous use has been operating in this area as an assisted living facility and accommodated 32 clients. She
  stated the intensity of the use is not being increased. She felt the use would improve housing conditions for its
  residents and would be more efficiently managed than typical group homes due to its being managed by WHA.
  She felt both standards of the Zoning Ordinance and General Plan can be satisfied.

7. Public Hearing, Petition to Amend Zoning Ordinance to create new zone classification as Flex Industrial Zone



                                                         9
  Mr. Rypien stated this is initiated by City staff in response to a petition to amend types of uses allowed in the
  Airport-Commercial zone, requesting additional options for warehousing and commercial uses. In working with
  them, Staff is suggesting a Flex Industrial Zone be created rather than allowing these uses throughout the AC
  zone. The proposed Flex zone would allow buildings with private leasable space to have a wider variety of
  uses with limited intensity. The applicant will then petition to rezone the property to be leased to the Flex zone.
  Areas to be considered for this zone should have access to major streets and pose no conflict with other
  industrial uses in the area. He reviewed uses which are proposed to be permitted in the new zone and those
  that would be restricted, as well as other proposed standards for the new zone. Restricted use would be those
  that create dust, noise, odors, traffic hazards or other nuisances. Buildings would likely have loading areas
  where products can be either delivered or picked up. The benefit of flex zoning is to allow space to be adapted
  for a particular use, as well as allowing it to evolve over time. He also reviewed additional standards which
  might need to be applicable for particular uses and denoted as a footnote in the use table. He also reviewed
  maximum building height, setbacks, parking and loading requirements and other site development standards
  being proposed. After reviewing uses which would be permitted under the new zone, he recommended the
  proposed language be forwarded to the City Council with a recommendation for approval.
  He stated the property proposed for the zoning has been zoned as A-C for some time and has remained
  vacant, and the new zone would allow a wider variety of uses, while still being compatible with the airport and
  the residential area to the west in Roy City.
  Commissioner Blaisdell asked if the use would affect the Airport Master Plan which was adopted a few years
  ago. Mr. Rypien indicated the uses proposed are intended to support the services of the airport area, providing
  entertainment or other uses such as personal services for patrons waiting for air service. Commissioner
  Southwick commented the language discussing objectionable noise and odors might be ambiguous, stating an
  odor may be objectionable to some people and enjoyed by others. Commissioner Safsten asked about the
  quality of building material for these types of uses, acknowledging while interior walls may be relocated to
  accommodate particular uses over time, the exterior should be sturdy.
  Mr. Jake Jackson the intent is for concrete tilt buildings with small tenants. Some areas would have drywall
  finish and might be fire-rated, while others may be concrete or CMU block. The space is mostly intended for
  small tenants to be used for assembly and warehousing with limited offices or retail space and would fit with the
  surrounding business park uses around the airport.
  Mr. Mike Nichols, representing Kemp Development, felt the flex zoning would fit well with other uses at the
  airport. While it still supports aeronautic uses and other uses now allowed in the Airport Commercial zone, it
  also allows a variety of services which support customers. He reviewed the history of Kemp and their existing
  project on the west side of the airport, stating they were guaranteed access to the airport for 20 years which is
  nearing its expiration. He felt the flex zone would provide opportunities for the future development of their
  portion of the airport as well.
