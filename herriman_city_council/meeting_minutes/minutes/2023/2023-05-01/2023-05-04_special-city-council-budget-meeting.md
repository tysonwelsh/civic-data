                            SPECIAL CITY COUNCIL
                                  MINUTES

                                  Thursday, May 04, 2023
                                  Approved August 9, 2023

The following are the minutes of the City Council meeting of the Herriman City Council. The
meeting was held on Thursday, May 4, 2023, at 3:30 p.m. in the Herriman City Council Chambers,
5355 West Herriman Main Street, Herriman, Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting, as required
by law, was posted in the City Hall, on the City’s website, and delivered to members of the Council,
media, and interested citizens.

Presiding: Mayor Lorin Palmer

Councilmembers Present: Jared Henderson, Teddy Hodges, Sherrie Ohrn, Steven Shields
(participated electronically)

Staff Present: City Manager Nathan Cherpeski, Assistant City Manager Wendy Thomas, City
Recorder Jackie Nostrom, Finance Director Kyle Maurer, Director of Operations Monte Johnson,
Public Works Director Justun Edwards, Communications Specialist Destiny Skinner, City Planner
Michael Maloy, Police Chief Troy Carr, Deputy Chief of Police Cody Stromberg, Unified Fire
Authority Assistant Chief Anthony Widdison, and Assistant City Attorney Matt Brooks.

3:30 PM – SPECIAL WORK MEETING: (Fort Herriman Conference Room)
   1. Call to Order
   Mayor Palmer called the meeting to order at 3:32 PM.

   2. Administrative Reports
       2.1. Fiscal Year 2023-2024 Budget Discussion – Kyle Maurer, Finance Director
   City Manager Nathan Cherpeski announced Herriman City had done a biennial budget and
   clarified the budget process would not include a full rebuild. He recognized Finance Director
   Kyle Maurer for having done a lot of work to put it together. He explained staff had modernized
May 4, 2023 – City Council Minutes                                               Page 2 of 9


the process this year as well. City Manager Cherpeski indicated the Council received the
presentation he would be sharing with them, and confirmed staff was not looking for a decision
tonight. The purpose of this session was just for the Council to hear information and could ask
questions.

Finance Director Maurer started the discussion with an overview of the developments since
the budget adoption. He observed the high inflation rate, and the assumption of the growth
rate would be different for property tax. He acknowledged water and storm water had faced
inflationary pressures, which could not initially be contemplated. He said there would be a
number of capital projects that impact fees would not have been able to fund. Finance Director
Maurer also explained after looking at all the projects for 2024, he projected deficits in the
General Fund and Police Services fund, which were the ones funded by the HCSEA property
tax.

Finance Director Maurer stated the largest component of the budget was personnel. He
explained employee insurance was utilized heavily, and costs would need to be increased for
the next fiscal year. City Manager Cherpeski added things grew at different rates, so some line
items were being split. Finance Director Maurer stated the initial cost had been reduced from
anticipated projections. In addition, he showed there had been a number of market adjustments.
He said they were saving money overall, although some categories showed a deficit. Finance
Director Maurer said there were a number of departments which had been budgeted
incorrectly, and highlighted the Police budget was unique in that the personnel side was fine,
but the benefits number was inaccurate. He emphasized he did not have data which showed
how the numbers had been derived. He also stated any market adjustments had primarily been
in Public Works, and shared that last year was a 4-percent increase, which was lower than the
7-percent rate of inflation. Finance Director Maurer discussed there had been aggressive
adjustments made by cities throughout the valley. He explained in the past, they generally
offered raises that were a couple points above inflation, although that had not been the case
for the previous year. He said their recommendation was to match CPI at 6-percent, which
matched what other cities were doing. He commented Public Works was a very tight market.

City Manager Cherpeski commented over time, everything should average out, and said they
had seen a lot of mid-cycle increase in cities. Councilmember Ohrn asked who conducted their
market analysis research, to which it was discussed that Human Resources Manager Travis
Dunn was responsible for the market analysis research. Councilmember Ohrn then inquired
as to what the target was, and City Manager Cherpeski discussed they generally wanted to be
in the top third, between the 50th and 80th percentile. He pointed out they did not want to
pay the highest, since that was usually indicative of other issues within the City. He further
discussed they were working on some of financial policies and were thinking about where they
wanted to be in the market regarding employees.

Finance Director Maurer moved into an overview of the General Fund. He stated development
related revenues and expenditures had been moved to a new fund, and there had been $3.7
May 4, 2023 – City Council Minutes                                                Page 3 of 9


Million in revenue and $3.6 Million in expenditures moved from the General Funds. He
reported the budget for the fiscal year 2024 had approved revenues at $6.6 Million, and he also
projected a deficit of $1.7 Million in the General Fund. Finance Director Maurer presented a
table which detailed the General Fund summary and highlighted some key points. He oriented
the Council to the main points, and specifically indicated the fifth column was the proposed
budget. He explained he had evaluated every position in the City to determine what every role
did regarding development. He stated 7-percent of the budget was in deficit currently, and he
emphasized while it was common to raise property tax to solve a deficit, it was not a viable
option to implement a property tax increase in the City.

Finance Director Maurer also discussed the majority of tax growth in the City was sales tax,
especially as the population of the City had gone up and online shopping had become more
prevalent. He reported the last five years had seen an average of a 20-percent increase per
year, and specifically, the franchise tax revenue increased about 7 percent per year.
Councilmember Henderson asked about sales tax revenue comparison of point of sale versus
the Statewide pool, so Finance Director Maurer showed a chart which depicted the revenues
received for each type of tax and went through tax revenue basic dissemination. He showed
sales tax drove the increase, even as some other sources of revenue dropped. He noted
population growth had slowed, which decreased the revenue made from taxes on new business
licensing. He then gave an overview as to how tax was calculated, and said 40-percent went
to the City, 20-percent to the County, and the remaining 40-percent went to UTA. He also
clarified the City did not get 100-percent of internet-based sales, and said sales tax generated
from online shopping went through the same breakdown. Finance Director Maurer also noted
population distribution was factored based on census data. Councilmember Ohrn commented
that was part of the reason it was so important everyone filled out their census.

Finance Director Maurer shared the breakdown of property tax distribution. He said about
half went to the school district, and the Salt Lake County Library and the County also received
a substantial percentage of property tax. He listed some other entities which received property
tax revenue, including Mosquito Abatement, South Valley Sewer, and Central Utah Water. He
compared the property tax rate to other cities in Salt Lake Valley. He said the reason some
other cities had a higher or lower rate was dependent on factors such as higher or lower
property values, as well as how many services the City offered.

Finance Director Maurer reported General Fund expenditures included personnel, operating
expenses, administrative fees, transfers, Public Safety including Police, as well as grants and
the debt service. He shared a chart which indicated which departments used the most
resources. He said streets and the Parks department took a large percentage of their revenue,
about 30-percent, but explained it included things beyond just their employees and operating
expenses.

City Manager Cherpeski spoke about the 10-year forecast for projected ending balances. He
explained it was previously projected a flat growth rate for revenue, but that it was not quite
May 4, 2023 – City Council Minutes                                                Page 4 of 9


accurate because rates would start to come down as the base grew. He shared some slides on
the presentation, and outlined what the General Fund would look like as they moved some
funds around. He said staff had been overly aggressive as to what could be put towards capital
projects, and discussed it was not a true structural imbalance; but rather there was an
imbalance because they had never had a plan in place. He said the Council could talk about
what that would mean moving forward, and urged the Councilmembers to come back in a week
or so with questions once they had time to digest all the information.

Finance Director Maurer continued with an overview of the Police Department and the HCSEA
Budget. He explained the Public Safety impact fee revenue had been budgeted as operating
revenue, which meant at the end of last year money out of the General Fund had needed to be
used to cover the deficit as the impact fee revenue were restricted funds. Finance Director
Maurer offered an explanation for restricted and unrestricted funds. He explained that while
the budget had seemed balanced, the fund balance had relied on impact fee revenue, which sat
in a restricted fund balance and the full amount was not allowed to be used. Councilmember
Ohrn commented it was important to use best practices when budgeting.

City Manager Cherpeski spoke about some challenges they faced regarding the software they
utilized, and said it was difficult to look at previous years and see accurate statistics on
employee retention. He said staff had under-budgeted some personnel in years past. He
observed he had spent a lot of time going through spreadsheets about personnel and explained
he had found that since they had been tracking by name and not allocation, they had not been
keeping accurate track of positions which ultimately caused people being dropped from the
budget. Moving forward, staff would track by position allocation.

Councilmember Ohrn shared she had spoken with someone from another City who had told
her they looked at their vacancy rate was over time, and then they budgeted at a certain
percentage of that figure. She said Herriman needed to have a similar policy in place, and City
Manager Cherpeski said they planned to budget at 100-percent. He elaborated they did not
want to budget at a lower percentage, because that assumed some employees would leave.
Councilmember Ohrn agreed it was better to budget at 100-percent, so they would not have to
come up with more money.

City Manager Cherpeski spoke about the Salt Lake Valley-wide fight for police officers, and
said it was an issue in many cities to find and retain good police officers. He said the cost of
police officers was outpacing property tax growth. Finance Director Maurer added since they
had not needed to purchase vehicles during the pandemic, they had had some extra funds
available which covered those costs, but now they needed vehicles again they were once more
at a deficit and needed a way to come up with revenue to pay for police personnel.

Finance Director Maurer shared a chart which indicated new growth in the City, and projected
how much revenue was lost to inflation every year. He clarified inflation was not built into
how property tax was calculated in Utah. He stated since the inception of the Safety Area,
May 4, 2023 – City Council Minutes                                               Page 5 of 9


there had been about $1.6 Million lost in revenue due to inflationary pressures. He said their
personnel costs outpaced their revenue lost. Finance Director Maurer said the average
property tax increase would need to be increased to 10.5-percent. City Manager Cherpeski
reiterated the point the district had saved money in the last two years by not needing to
purchase vehicles. City Manager Cherpeski added as they got back into a more normal
rotation, they would have to reconsider this.

Finance Director Maurer announced he had done a ten-year forecast of the Police Department,
and reported he had met with the Chief and Deputy Chief in order to get a sense of what their
suggested staffing plan was, and indicated the report was in the packet presented to the
Council. Finance Director Maurer reported by 2033, there would be almost a $12 Million deficit
between expenses and revenue for the HCSEA, given their current staffing plan and vehicle
rotation plan. City Manager Cherpeski pointed out they needed to weigh priorities, and
possibly consider pushing out vehicles to last longer and reevaluating fleet rotation.

City Manager Cherpeski discussed many of their calls to the police department were domestic
violence calls, which he noted were hard to decrease through programs run by the Public Safety
department. He commended the Police Chief for his ability to come up with staffing projections
based off hard data, and for forecasting coming needs as the City grew larger. Finance Director
Maurer reiterated he had taken the numbers directly from Chief, and assured the Council he
had not gone back and massaged the numbers at all. City Manager Cherpeski said they had a
large town and needed to plan on having a large police force.
Finance Director Maurer reported staff had created a new fund for Development Services,
which covered revenue coming from things such as building permits and planning fees, as well
as any direct expenses. He noted staff allocated some expenses. He said they were working
on a fee study that would begin in a couple weeks and said staff could build some fees into the
study so they would avoid the issue of possibly having to add a surcharge back in a couple
years down the line. He reported this would save them a lot of money regarding credit card
surcharges. Finance Director Maurer also reiterated he had spoken with all of the departments
in order to get a sense of how much work being conducted impacted development. He had used
the data collected to come up with percentages of how much those departments could use the
Development Services Fund. He lastly reported development related revenues and
expenditures showed a current balance of $89,718.

Finance Director Maurer next presented an overview of the Fire Safety Service Area. He
announced he had estimated $8.4 Million in property tax revenue, as well as about $60,000
from UFA as part of their settlement for the fund balance transfer. He also acknowledged there
had been some discussion about whether the service district should participate in the CDRA.
Councilmember Shields clarified the CDRA had a maximum of $11.5 Million, which Finance
Director Maurer said he would have to confirm later. City Manager Cherpeski added they
would have to schedule an HCFSA meeting to get more clarity on that as well as some other
issues.

Finance Director Maurer continued to say he had done a ten-year forecast for the HCFSA area.
He indicated the Station 103 rebuild was slated for fiscal year 2025, and the future station build
was planned for fiscal year 2028. He stated they could use the impact fee balance at the time
of construction to offset construction costs. He spoke about the yearly operating increase,
and discussed as the City grew, operation expenses would rise concurrently. He reported the
service area had a sufficient amount of money in the fund to be comfortable until about 2030,
at which point the operating expenses from the new station would begin to make the fund
balance start to drop. He added this assumed the stations were purchased with cash, although
he noted they also had the option to consider bonds or other funding sources. He detailed
some key features of a chart in the presentation which further broke down their revenues and
operating costs.

Finance Director Maurer also discussed the challenges of anticipating needs of the City so far
in the future, and noted many of the Fire Station’s calls were actually medical in nature, which
changed the way the stations should be spaced out throughout the City. He then shared
another chart which showed their recommended fund balance, based on their current
operating expenses. He pointed out there was a sufficient fund balance projected for the next
ten years and reiterated it would begin to fall in 2031.

Finance Director Maurer next discussed the water rate study. He said the study has yet to be
fully completed, as they were still finishing up the Master Plan. He said they would bring some
rate structure options in June and were looking to consolidate the rates. He said they had pages
to go through, which could be a challenge.

Finance Director Maurer presented some slides which illustrated the fund balance forecasted
for several different scenarios. He noted the green band through the middle signified the
recommended target reserve. He indicated the black line signified no changes. He said they
had assessed three rates which other cities used, but still needed more feedback.

City Manager Cherpeski pointed out these charts only showed CPI growths in rates and did
not see all the capital projects accounted for. He said they had recently received a grant which
would impact the rates. He noted inflation had drastically impacted development costs.

Finance Director Maurer stated the charts were provided by the consultants and included some
options, the first showing one large rate increase of 37.5-percent as well as smaller increases
of three-percent. Councilmember Ohrn asked what this would mean for the average citizen.
Mayor Palmer demonstrated with his own water bill what the mathematical difference would
be.

Finance Director Maurer said the second chart looked at 15-percent increases for the first few
years, followed by 10-percent increases and later three-percent increases. He said the final
chart was the one favored by staff, and that option issued two bonds with an initial 10-percent
increase the following year. He said they were not ready to present fully detailed findings just
May 4, 2023 – City Council Minutes                                                    Page 7 of 9


yet. Finance Director Maurer added the rate studies did not have many Capital projects and
said he was not sure what the issue was. He discussed the inflation rate was also much lower
than actual rates and said he could not tell if personnel costs were accounted for.

Councilmember Shields asked if this accounted for non-impact fee eligible projects, and
Finance Director Maurer said these charts also did not fully reflect the reality of inflation rates.
He added that the consultants were seeing these increases everywhere. He cautioned the
biggest downfall would be to ignore the study outright. City Manager Cherpeski noted they
would be regularly assessing models to see how well they worked. Finance Director Maurer
added they could always have the consultants do a “touch up” study. City Manager Cherpeski
also clarified they were in the middle of this study and were updating rates accordingly.

Finance Director Maurer shifted to discussing storm water funds. He said this fund was not
as complex as the water fund, but the expenditures were grossly underestimated and they are
due for another study. He said he did the study in-house which works assuming they are not
changing rate structures.

City Manager Cherpeski expressed they had a few issues which included a mandated storm
drain retrofit to increase clean water available, which had a $1.8 Million dollar cost associated
with it. He said there were also SCADA projects totaling $3.8 Million dollars, a lack of system
deficiency projects, and several personal requests including a new storm water tech and the
conversion of foreman to Manager. Councilmember Ohrn asked if they should be concerned
about the $1.8 Million cost. Councilmember Shields said they had a plan which identified costs
and improvements and said it would be beneficial. City Manager Cherpeski explained those
data projections were for operational functionality, although they would need to assess things
deeper from a staffing perspective.

City Manager Cherpeski said they had received systems which did not work as they were
supposed to. He gave an example of a pond drainage system at the local community college
which was not properly accounted for. He said they had yet to properly reimburse the
developers for their labor. He added there are always more unfunded mandates.

Councilmember Ohrn asked if these issues were incredibly pressing. City Manager Cherpeski
replied they would inevitably have to address these issues, although these funds were never
appropriately designed to deal with capital improvements. Councilmember Shields said they
had a reserve fund, but never a big fund for Capital Projects.

Finance Director Maurer moved to the next chart, which showed current rates assuming no
interest changes. He said they had likely been over-allocating costs to the Storm Water fund.
He said the next chart had the same assumptions but looked at standard minimum
recommendations. He personally recommended a 10-percent rate increases for four years and
then 3-percent increases afterwards. He said they were still looking for system deficiencies
from Engineering, which could always change. Finance Director Maurer added they may be
May 4, 2023 – City Council Minutes                                                   Page 8 of 9


able to find other methods of financing after those systems were vetted. He then presented a
few charts which showed a 10-percent increase on the Storm Water fund.

City Manager Cherpeski went through staff recommendations. He reported staff felt it was
good practice to have a fund stand on its own, but some of those funds only tracked special
revenues. He said they should avoid having funds subsidize one another, and suggested they
re-examine their Capital Improvement Plan to get a more comprehensive approach. He said
rebuilding their models led to the need to spread out projects to have their revenue catch up.
He continued to say they needed to focus on what is most prescient. City Manager Cherpeski
indicated staff would make a list of services and then make decisions based on their priorities,
and said they could better implement their strategy into their budget this way. City Manager
Cherpeski also recommended not hiring in the immediate future and seeking operating
reductions where it makes sense. He said State law put five-percent at a minimum rate and
35-percent at the maximum, and stated 25-percent felt like a reasonable amount for their
needs. Finance Director Maurer said these rates are determined by revenues, and added they
needed to set targets for fund balances.

City Manager Cherpeski then went through approved positions for fiscal year 2024. He said
the prudent thing was to hold off on positions that were not absolutely necessary, though
acknowledged each department had valid arguments. He gave the list as follows: three street
techs, one IT assistant, one arborist assistant, and one staff engineer. City Manager Cherpeski
clarified they may not fill all these positions. He then presented the vacant positions as follows:
one accountant, an engineering intern, a planning intern, a GIS intern, a Police mental health
specialist, and a building inspector. He said they would be looking for a more senior accountant
or budget analyst. He hoped this position would free up Finance Director Maurer’s time.
Councilmember Ohrn asked if they were paying interns, to which City Manager Cherpeski
replied they would pay $15 dollars an hour.

City Manager Cherpeski indicated some of these issues were structural and they needed to
assess changing their service levels, even when it was painful. He said Herriman typically tried
to spend what it needs and then invest the rest in capital projects. He then went through types
of revenue and expenditures. He said grants could be good for one time uses but were difficult
to rely on more than a few years. He then presented different tiers of budget reductions, and
recommended they look at those tiers more in depth. He said they needed to figure out how
they would do bigger projects which required more detailed budgeting. He said the challenge
with Capital Projects was that many of the payments were contracted and entirely impact fees.

City Manager Cherpeski said the budget is not a blank slate, and Councilmember Henderson
cautioned they needed to be realistic. City Manager Cherpeski said they could drop the budget
by about 1.2-percent by holding out on some positions. He said they also needed to assess their
ongoing expenses. He said they also needed to ask themselves if they were delivering on their
projects in a way that Herriman citizens found acceptable.
May 4, 2023 – City Council Minutes                                                Page 9 of 9


Councilmember Ohrn opined they owed it to people to better communicate how their projects
would impact people and City Manager Cherpeski echoed this thought. Councilmember Ohrn
voiced she was open to holding some town halls so people could better understand these
processes. City Manager Cherpeski said he had done several of these types of projects and
was not opposed to this.

Councilmember Ohrn expressed frustration over miscommunication over the capital plan. City
Manager Cherpeski said they had worked to make ten-year projections as accurate as possible
but needed to be direct about the changes. Councilmember Henderson wanted them to
prioritize having a realistic plan above all. He said he was not expecting perfection right away
but wanted to make progress. City Manager Cherpeski said whoever was doing Capital projects
needed to be especially on top of these changes.

Councilmember Shields thought there was a value in going through the process and asking how
they had got here. He said they needed to learn from their mistakes and apply better decisions
going forward. He hoped they would no longer manage without sound communication leading
to excess spending. He said he was tired of going off incorrect information, and acknowledged
making these changes may be painful given increased costs and procedural shifts. He said they
did not need to protect past decisions from past Councils and urged them to move forward
with positive changes.

City Manager Cherpeski asked everyone to come to the next meeting with questions and ideas.

3. Adjournment
Councilmember Henderson motioned to adjourn the Council work meeting. Councilmember
Hodges seconded the motion and all voted aye.
