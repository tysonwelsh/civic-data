Mountain America Credit Union
2531 S Redwood Road
General plan change from Residential Office to General Commercial and
zone change from R-1-6 to C-2
0.85 acres

Kristine Johanos with Mountain America Credit Union has submitted a General Plan/zone
change application for a 0.85 acre parcel located at 2531 S Redwood Road. The property is
currently zoned R-1-6 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 6,000 square feet)
with a General Plan designation of Residential Office. The proposed zone is C-2 (General
Commercial) and the proposed General Plan designation is General Commercial.

Surrounding zones include C-2 to the north, R-1-6 to the south and east, and RM (Multiple Unit
Dwelling Residential) to the west. The property is surrounded by an accounting office to the
north, a home and a church to the east, a duplex to the south, and fourplexes to the west on the
west side of Redwood Road. The property includes a home on the south end of the property that
was built in 1942.

If this application is approved, the applicant plans to build a Mountain America Credit Union
branch on the subject property. The existing home on the property would be demolished.
Included with this report is a concept plan for the property along with a letter from the applicant
that supports this application.

As part of the review of General Plan/zone change applications, staff sends the concept plan to
Public Works for any initial feedback. The following are comments received by Public Works:

      The curb returns installed by UDOT at the southeast corner of Warnock Avenue and
       Redwood Road at the northeast corner Stratford Avenue and Redwood Road are in the
       wrong locations and will need to be reconstructed.
      The Redwood Road Overlay Zone streetscape will be required and a Public Sidewalk,
       Lighting and Utility Easement will be required.
      Improvements will be required along the frontages of Stratford and Warnock.
      We do have a storm drain on the east side of the lot that they can drain to. However, our
       map shows it as a corrugated metal pipe and it will likely be in poor condition. We'd like
       them to replace that pipe with reinforced concrete pipe per current city standards. If they
       already have to relocate the pipe for the trees and other landscaping requirements it
       would be at their cost. We'd also need to look at any required easements if relocated to
       the west. If relocation isn't required for the trees and other landscaping requirements, we
       can reimburse them for the storm drain pipe replacement in it's current location.

Colin Subdivision
2781 West 4100 South
R-1-8
2 Lots
.63 Acres

BACKGROUND

Mr. Evy Colin is requesting preliminary plat approval for the Colin Subdivision. The
subdivision is bordered on the east, south and west by existing single-family development being
known as the Shorland Phase 5, 4 and 1 Subdivisions respectively. The north side of this
property is adjacent to 4100 South.


STAFF/AGENCY COMMENTS:
Granger Hunter Improvement District:

Project will need to run availability for water, sewer, and fire protection.
Subject to design and review inspections.

Utility Agencies:

Subject to all standard easement locations.

Public Works:

Revisions to plat are required.

Recommend circular drive or other method to prevent backing onto 4100 South.

Soils report will be required.

Public Improvements will be required to connect with new improvements to the east and
transition to older improvements at the western boundary of the subdivision.

Building Inspections:

Follow recommendations outlined in the soils report.

ISSUES:

Mr. Colin is proposing a new subdivision consisting of 2 lots on .63 acres. This property was not
included in the adjacent subdivisions to the east, south or west but is zoned R-1-8 and eligible to
be subdivided.

The proposed subdivision is adjacent to 4100 South which will be providing access to both lots.
This right-of-way is classified as a major arterial street. As such, the following language in City
Code requires the following:

On Lots with available access only onto an Arterial, a circular drive or some other type of
vehicular maneuvering area shall be provided to enable vehicles to enter traffic moving forward
rather than backing. The minimum depth of such Lots shall be 120 feet and the minimum width
shall be 85 feet.

These lots are of sufficient width (95 feet) and depth (143 feet) to accommodate this
requirement. A note will be placed on the plat to notify potential builders that the condition
above will be required for each lot that they provide a mechanism for a vehicle to front 4100
South as opposed to backing onto said street. This will be addressed at such time a building
permit is requested.
Both lots meet and exceed frontage and area requirements of the R-1-8 zone. Each lot is 13,585
square feet in size. The property does sit down in elevation from the existing top back of curb at
4100 South. Topography for lot 1 is approximately 6-7 feet lower than the curb elevation and
flattens out on the east end to 1-2 feet lower in elevation. Builders of new homes will need to
coordinate water and sewer connections with Granger Hunter and site plans for future homes will
need to show driveway accommodations with the existing slope. It is likely that a walkout
basement on the west lot could be accommodated with the slope of the property.

As with all new subdivision development, there is a concern with the potential of ground
water impacts. The adjacent subdivisions are too old to provide any useable information on
groundwater. And since this property sits below the existing right-of-way along 4100 South,
staff will require a soils report to be submitted for review. The report will simply need to
indicate the presence of any ground water and recommendations for footing depth if ground
water is encountered.

     STAFF ALTERNATIVES:

        1.      That compliance be made with Granger Hunter Improvement District regarding

        2.      That the subdivision name be approved by Salt Lake County.
        3.      That the developer provide a soils report. The report simply needs to identify any
                existence of ground water. If ground water is encountered, the lowest floor slab
                shall be 3-feet above the water elevation.
        4.      That the developer resolve all staff and agency concerns addressed in this report as

        5.      That the developer install public improvements along 4100 South including curb,

        6.      That a circular driveway or other alternative shall be provided at the building permit
                process that demonstrates vehicles will front 4100 South as opposed to backing out
                on to 4100 South.

        7.      That future site plans consider the existing slope when laying out home placement.
                This matter will be addressed with Engineering and Planning at the building permit
                process.

B.      Continuance to allow the developer an opportunity to address issues raised during the
        public hearing.

BACKGROUND:

Korey Kinder with DR Horton, representing Magna Township, is requesting consideration to
dedicate a potion of 4100 South west of U-111. The proposed dedication is necessary to provide
access to a future project within SL County to be known as Little Valley.

ISSUES:

Property west of U-111 at this location, the majority of which is located in Salt Lake County,
Magna Township, is undergoing a major transformation with a variety of land uses on the west
bench of the Salt Lake Valley. A critical component to the success of this development is
gaining access from 4100 South west of U-111.

Discussions about this access have been ongoing between the City and developer for quite some
time. The initial proposed alignment did not meet specifications of West Valley City. The
intersection at 4100 South and U-111 was off-set at an angle heading southwest due to the
location of a private property owner to the north.
It is staffs understanding that the portion of property that prevented the correct alignment has
been negotiated and resolved with that property owner, thus allowing a correct intersection
alignment at this location. There are going to be two subdivision plats: One for that portion
lying within the boundaries of West Valley City and the other plat for that portion lying within
the boundaries of Magna Township.

The portion within West Valley City occupies an area of approximately 1.7 acres. There are a
few engineering concerns with regards to section lines and proposed half widths, but those will
be resolved as future reviews take place.

In summary, this application dedicates right-of-way located in the City’s boundary west of U-
111 for a future project in Magna Township. Construction of said right-of-way will need to
comply with City standards and reviews of the City’s Engineering Division.

M-3-2022
Arby’s Landscaping Exception
3535 S 5600 W
C-2 Zone (1.07 Acres)

The property owner is requesting modifications to the requirements of Section 7-10-103 of Title
7, Landscaping Standards Applicable to All High-Image Arterials. This property is one of many
that was impacted by UDOT’s widening of the intersection at 3500 South and 5600 West, in
connection with the Mountain View Corridor project. 5600 West is subject to the regulations
outlined in section 7-10 of the West Valley City Municipal Code, which defines landscaping
standards for properties that have frontage on high-image arterial streets. The right-of-way
improvements shifted everything to the east on this site, so some of the landscaping adjacent to
the sidewalk was eliminated. The code requires a minimum of a 20’ landscape buffer adjacent to
the sidewalk, but there is currently only about a 12’ to 13’ buffer remaining. The Planning
Commission can grant a modification to the 20’ landscaped buffer requirement as outlined in 7-
10-104.

The building on the property is over 65’ from the new property line. Currently there is a drive
aisle that is next to the diminished landscape buffer, and then there is an additional 30’ of
landscaping in front of the building. While the drive aisle could potentially be shifted to the east
to accommodate for the required 20’ of landscaping next to the sidewalk, it would just be
relocating landscaping from one area to another. Staff believes that the layout of the site should
remain as is, but that the landscaping should be modified to meet all other requirements of 7-10-
103. These requirements include installing one tree for every 30’ of frontage, 50% lawn or
artificial turf and 50% live plant material in the remaining areas. If lawn is used, the code
requires any areas containing lawn be at least 8’ wide. The existing sidewalk that leads to the
building should also be connected to the public sidewalk. A landscape plan needs to be submitted
to staff for review before any work is completed.

Staff Alternatives


Approval. Twenty feet of landscaping is not required directly adjacent to the sidewalk, but the
following modifications shall be made to the site as described below.

     1. 1 tree shall be added for every 30’ of frontage and selected from the table in 7-10-
        103(3).
     2. At least 50% of the landscaped area shall be grass or artificial turf that meets the
        standards in 7- 2-127.
     3. All remaining areas between the sidewalk and the building shall be landscaped with at
        least 50% live plant material.
     4. The existing sidewalk that goes to the building shall be extended to connect to the public
        sidewalk.
     5. A landscape plan shall be submitted to staff for review and approval before any
        landscaping installation is done.
Denial. All 7-10-103 requirements shall be met, including the required 20’ of landscaping next to
the sidewalk.

M-4-2022
Kraig McGee Landscaping Exception
3535 S 5600 W
C-2 Zone (0.49 Acres)

The applicant is requesting modifications to the requirements of Section 7-10-103 of Title 7,
Landscaping Standards Applicable to All High-Image Arterials. The code requires a minimum
of a 20’-wide landscape buffer adjacent to the sidewalk with specific tree and plant material
requirements. The Planning Commission can grant a modification to the 20’ landscaped buffer
requirement if the property owner can demonstrate that compliance would necessitate
“elimination of improvements required by other ordinances which could not be relocated
elsewhere on the site” (7-10-104(1)(b)).

3500 South was widened several years ago, which made the landscaping along the frontage of
this property non-conforming to the code regulations. At the time, however, there was no
requirement in the code for replacing landscape buffers when they were reduced in size. The
landscaping requirement is being triggered now by a tenant improvement permit that was
submitted to the City, which has a valuation of over $50,000. The applicant is requesting an
exception to the 20’ requirement based on the location of the building and the parking.

The applicant provided a site plan and parking analysis as part of their application. The
building is only 8’ from the edge of the sidewalk and the edge of the parking lot is 9’ from the
sidewalk. The applicant has indicated that there will be 5,250 sf of retail floor area and 1,162
sf or warehouse area. Based on the parking requirements of 7-9-104 the site needs 21 parking
stalls. There are currently 22 parking stalls provided.

Staff is supportive of the landscaping exception in front of the building but recommends that
the Planning Commission still require the full 20’ of landscaping next to the parking lot. First,
the site could technically lose one stall and still comply with the code. However, staff believes
it wouldn’t even be necessary to remove a stall. The striping for the ADA stall could simply be
relocated to the south side of the stall and there would be no loss of parking spaces. Second,
having the full 20’ of landscaping would provide a better buffer for cars entering and exiting
the site. Also, once the site to the east is required to put in the full 20’ buffer it would provide
consistent landscaping along the frontages of both sites.

The code also requires 50% grass or artificial turf within the landscape buffer, 1 tree for every
30’ of frontage, and 50% live plant material in the remaining areas. Lawn is not allowed in areas
less than 8’ wide, so the area in front of the building needs to be landscaped with at least 50%
live plant material. At least 50% of the landscaped area in front of the parking shall be lawn or
artificial turf. The remaining area needs at least 50% live plant material. Three trees are required
in the buffer. A landscape plans needs to be submitted for staff review to verify code
compliance.

There are other landscaping issues on the site that will be addressed through building
permit and business license reviews.

Staff Alternatives


Option 1

Approval. The size of the landscaped area in front of the building may remain as-is, however,
the full 20’ of landscaping shall be installed next to the parking lot. The landscaping shall also
comply with the following requirements of 7-10-103:

     1. At least 50% of the landscaped area adjacent to the parking shall be grass or artificial
        turf that meets the standards in 7-2-127. Grass is not required in front of the building.
     2. 1 tree is required for every 30’ of frontage. Any existing trees may count
        towards this requirement and any new trees shall be selected from the table in
        7-10-103(3).
     3. All other areas shall be landscaped with at least 50% live plant material.
     4. A landscape plan shall be submitted to staff for review and approval before any
        landscaping installation is done.


Option 2

Approval. The size of the landscaped areas along the frontage may remain as-is,
however, the landscaping shall also comply with the following requirements of 7-10-
103:
    1. The landscaped area adjacent to the parking shall either be entirely grass or shall be
       artificial turf that meets the standards in 7-2-127 and covers 50% of the buffer area.
       Grass is not required in front of the building.
    2. 1 tree is required for every 30’ of frontage. Any existing trees may count
       towards this requirement and any new trees shall be selected from the table in
       7-10-103(3).
    3. All other areas shall be landscaped with at least 50% live plant material.
    4. A landscape plan shall be submitted to staff for review and approval before any
       landscaping installation is done.


Option 3

Denial. The site shall meet all requirements of 7-10-103.
