5.4) Consideration of Ordinance #67-2025, approving the Downtown Revitalization Plan and
       State Street Station Area Plan including parts of Main Street and State Street.
    The City’s consultant from Landmark Design presented the plan noting the time, effort and
    public outreach that went into the project. The Downtown Revitalization Plan and State Street
    Station Area Plan is a long‑range, visionary plan (20+ years) for Lehi’s downtown core, the
    Mills District near the Roller Mills, and the future UTA station area north of State Street. It
    is not a regulatory document by itself, but a guide for future ordinances, capital projects, and
    staff work. Key aims are to make Main Street more walkable and family‑friendly, add public
    gathering spaces and programming, support historic character, and encourage small‑scale
    retail, dining, and mixed‑use housing so more people can live within walking distance of
    downtown. For the station area, it anticipates compact, transit‑supportive development once

Lehi City Council Meeting                        5                               December 16, 2025
    UTA/UDOT finalizes the station and major infrastructure (street connections, utilities,
    moving the rail turnaround) is addressed.

    Transportation and parking are a central focus. The plan visualizes a future where more
    regional traffic uses Pioneer Crossing and 2100 North, so Main Street can function less as a
    pass‑through arterial and more as a pedestrian‑oriented main street. It shows conceptual
    cross‑sections that remove the continuous center turn lane mid‑block to widen sidewalks and
    improve the walking environment, while still allowing left turns at intersections. It also
    recommends active‑transportation improvements across downtown and refined design
    standards for new buildings. The number of units in the plan are framed as “up to” analytical
    scenarios that still must respect actual utility and roadway capacity, and the plan suggests
    reallocating existing citywide residential caps, not adding entirely new ones.

    Mayor Johnson asked for public comments.

    Brett Rollins opposed adding downtown housing, saying it would bring more car traffic, not
    just walkability, and that Lehi is a driving city where people need parking to access errands
    and city facilities. He also questioned relying on a survey, suggesting most might oppose
    downtown housing if broadly asked.

    Kaylin Osborne was concerned about traffic, added housing units, and parking, and felt those
    impacts need more consideration. She asked the council to table the plan and let the new
    mayor and council review it next year.

    James Harrison doubted the current downtown can support the envisioned live‑work‑shop
    pattern and objected to reduced parking, saying Lehi will remain car‑oriented. He supported
    improving State Street but wants it to stay a reliable arterial, and he urged delaying approval
    so the future council.

    Keith Campbell agreed with the prior comments, and said he wanted more time to examine
    traffic and parking issues, and asked that the plan be tabled and revisited by the new council
    rather than pushed through now.

    Kim Struthers stressed that any big street changes (like on Main) would only happen later as
    part of a major capital project with full design, engineering, public process, budgeting, and
    future council decisions—what’s shown now are concepts, not final designs. On the station
    area, he said the city needs to be ready for transit even though UTA’s timing is uncertain, and
    modest mixed-use improvements now can still make the area less car-centric. He also noted
    that in the future staff could bring back amendments (including reallocation of density caps)
    and that nothing in the plan is locked in permanently.

    Councilmembers discussed the substantial time, review, and public outreach that went into
    preparing the Plan and emphasized that the plan is amendable and visionary, not binding.
    They noted that any future Council can amend the plan. Councilmember Michelle Stallings
    had concerns with some concepts like removing the continuous left‑turn lane on Main and
    “bike boulevard”‑type treatments could worsen traffic and parking, similar to 700 South. She

    was concerned that the plan would drive staff work, and preferred tabling the plan for the new
    Council to review because they may have a different vision.

Motion:               Michelle Stallings moved to table this item understanding that we have a new
                      council coming on board, and though considerable time was spent on this, it
                      may not be the vision or the direction of the new council, so they should
                      provide input. Motion failed for lack of a second.

Chris Condie argued against tabling or denying the Downtown Revitalization Plan. He stressed it
is only a visionary document, not something that locks in funding or entitlements, and that a new
council can change or scrap it quickly if they want. He emphasized that 18+ months of work,
public input, and money have already gone into the plan, and waiting for a “solid majority” of
residents to agree on something this complex is unrealistic.

Councilor Newall emphasized the substantial effort from staff, the advisory committees, and
residents. She noted that councils will always change, and they may choose to amend or redo this
plan, but the current council—whose names are on the work—should adopt a starting vision now,
and future councils can amend or rebuild it if they choose.

Motion:               Chris Condie moved to approve Ordinance #67-2025, approving the
                      Downtown Revitalization Plan and State Street Station Area Plan including
                      parts of Main Street and State Street. Paul Hancock seconded the motion.

Roll Call Vote:       YES: Paul Hancock, Chris Condie, Heather Newall; NO: Michelle Stallings.
                      The motion carried: 3 - 1

      Agreement adding 30 residential units to the West Project Development Agreement,
      affecting 2146 West and 2198 West Stockton Lane.

John Hadfield was the applicant. Marlin Eldred and Lorin Powell explained the long history
behind the area and previous development agreements. It was clarified that this is an amendment
to an existing agreement. Lorin ensured the Council that the infrastructure can support it.
Councilor Stallings inquired about submitting a zone change instead. Marlin replied that it’s
because all other elements of the current development agreement need to remain in place. Kim
Struthers said that after the project is constructed staff prefers rezoning it to something that better
matches the use to avoid confusion. He also wants to ensure that the trail connection for the
current agreement is also included in this amendment.

density with nothing in return to benefit the city. Marlin stated that the east and west agreements
were predicated upon each other and circumstances have changed to the point that in order to
move the project forward and begin infrastructure improvements staff and the applicant agreed to
this amendment. Marlin said that the original intent of the developer was to come back to the
Council and ask for more units after the infrastructure is improved with the justification that the
developer has already given parcels or traded parcels to benefit the city.
