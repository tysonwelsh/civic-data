         The purpose of the work session was to review the agenda for the City Council meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.;
receive the GOAL Foundation Annual Report; discuss the proposed Synchrony Bank Line of Credit reapproval; discuss proposed
amendments to the Short-Term Rental Ordinance; discuss the proposed creation of a Flex Industrial Zone; discuss Council
business; and hear Council comments.

Agenda Review
        Chair Richey and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council meeting
scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

GOAL Foundation Annual Report
          The City Council has requested to receive information from local foundations and nonprofit organizations that have a
contractual agreement with Ogden City. These presentations help the Council to become more familiar with the objectives and
efforts of the organizations. Troy Callantine, Executive Director/CEO of the GOAL Foundation, used the aid of a PowerPoint
presentation to describe the Foundation’s organizational mission and goals, successes and challenges over the past year, and any
future plans for the organization. For a copy of the PowerPoint presentation in its entirety, see the information packet.

Proposed Synchrony Bank Line of Credit Reapproval
         Community Development Manager Smith explained that as a part of the Annual Action Plan (AAP) and the City’s
administration of Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) funding, program guidelines are developed for specific
programs targeting specific needs. The program guidelines serve as general parameters for how the City will use funding for that
particular program. These program guidelines are referred to in the AAP but are entirely developed and adopted by the City.
Amending the program guidelines is done through a resolution adopted by the City Council. The current proposal is not an
amendment to any program guideline; rather, it is the reapproval of a line of credit the City uses to supplement the funding of
Community Development programs. The terms for the Synchrony Bank line of credit of $1.4 million remain the same as
previously adopted. The Council approved the first line of credit with Synchrony Bank in 2013 and has approved the renewal
every two years since that time.
         Brief discussion among the Council and Mr. Smith centered on the past uses of the line or credit, housing projects that
have been completed with the proceeds of the line of credit, and the fluctuation of the interest rates associated with the line of
credit. There was also a brief discussion regarding the potential future availability of properties for purchase by the City to
facilitate additional housing projects.

Proposed Short Term Rental Ordinance Amendments
          Planning Manager Brierley explained the Planning Division staff has proposed a zoning ordinance text amendment
regarding residential vacation rentals. The proposed text amendment is meant to address complaints about residential vacation
rentals, specifically concerns of parking, noise, and ensuring owner occupancy for units meant to be owner-occupied. The
Planning Commission has recommended that the Council adopt the proposed ordinance.
          Mr. Brierley used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the proposed ordinance amendments relating to
owner occupancy, parking, hosting parties at a vacation rental, management of a vacation rental property, and to discuss State
legislation related to short term rentals. He emphasized that the major catalyst for the proposed changes is that it is difficult for the
City to enforce the current version of the ordinance, specifically the owner occupancy requirement. For a copy of the PowerPoint
presentation in its entirety, see the information packet.
          The Council received input from Building Services Manager Tremea regarding the manner in which the City’s Code
Enforcement Division will enforce the proposed ordinance, if it is adopted, and the response other property owners can expect if
they file a complaint regarding a violation occurring at a short-term rental property. The Council decided to continue the
discussion at a future work session.

Proposed Creation of Flex Industrial Zone
          Senior Planner Rypien explained Asilia Investments initially submitted a petition to amend the A-C (Airport
Commercial) zone to modify and clarify uses in the A-C zone and to allow a wider range of non-aeronautical commercial uses.
The A-C zone along with the A-I (Airport Industrial) zone make up the zoning for operations on and adjacent to the airport. While
the changes were given a recommendation of approval by the Planning Commission, concerns raised by the Council led the
petitioners and the City to amend the proposal from changes to the A-C zone to a proposal of a new zone altogether. The amended
proposal is to create a new Flex Industrial zone that would allow flexible commercial and industrial/manufacturing space. This
would be a new zone within the manufacturing classification and would have some similarities to the M-1 while allowing some
additional flexibility in the zone’s allowable uses and interior warehousing and manufacturing standards. The focus of the initial
petition was to develop property on the south end of the airport adjacent to the Kemp buildings. The zoning on that property is A-
C. While the updated proposal is to create a new zone, the petitioner’s goal is still to develop the property but under the new zone
rather than a modified A-C zone. If the Flex Industrial zone gets approval from the Council, it is anticipated that a petition would
be filed to rezone the subject property.
          Discussion among the Council and Mr. Rypien centered on the potential future uses of the subject property and
guidelines that will be listed in the zoning ordinance for the proposed zone. Mr. Rypien used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation
to expound on the permitted, conditional, and prohibited uses for the proposed zone. For a PowerPoint presentation in its entirety
see the information packet.
          Chair Richey invited input from the applicant. Jack Jackson, representing Asilia Investments, stated the purpose of this
petition is to clarify the types of flex developments that should be allowed in and around the Ogden Airport. For the Kemp
property specifically, Asilia Investments wants to be able to provide for incubation spaces that include aeronautical uses,
distribution, and light manufacturing for smaller and ‘up and coming’ companies.
          Council Deputy Director Symes stated that this item can be scheduled for action during the March 19 meeting, or the
Council can request additional discussion in a work session meeting before proceeding to final action. The Council requested
additional discussion of the matter during the March 12 work session before considering action on March 19.
