Unified Fire Authority Report

               Captain Ken Aldridge, Unified Fire Authority, updated the Council on the wildfires
and encourages homeowners to be proactive and take preventative measures to protect its home.
There will be a groundbreaking for a new fire station on July 13.

               Deputy Chief McConaghie, Sandy City Fire, reviewed calls for service from April
to the beginning of July. There was an average of 22 calls per month. The crews have been
driving by Big Bear Park more frequently to monitor it for fireworks.

              Council Member Perry, seconded by Council Member Price, moved to accept the
UFA report. The motion passed unanimously.

Unified Police Department Report

               Officer Jeff Fenton stated thefts from unlocked cars and yards are still high. There
are a lot of abandoned vehicles on the roads and there has been a transient living in a bush at
Big Bear Park. He would like the Council to give him suggestions on where it wants the speed
trailer.

Proposed 2022 Budget

                Dave Sanderson, CPA, stated he amended the budget sent by the MSD to use
as a talking point, the Council will ultimately approve the amounts. The legal, wages, and special
events were increased and are the major changes. The stipend reflects the amount the other
townships are paying its Council Members.

              Paul Ashton stated the budget being submitted will go the MSD to approve and
approved in November.

                Council Member Little reviewed various budget line items that he was concerned
with. In addition, in 2017 an ordinance was passed that requires the community council to provide
an accounting of funds expended in order to receive future funding. He would like to see a detailed
use of funds and follow the process outlined in the ordinance.

              Mayor Flint stated the White City Community Council presented a detailed report
of expenditures and provided a printed copy to the Council Members during the May 6, 2021,
Council meeting.

              Council Member Little, seconded by Council Member Price, moved to adopt the
preliminary budget. The motion passed unanimously.

White City Metro Township Council Member Resignation

               Mayor Flint stated Council Member Dickerson sent a letter officially resigning from
the White City Metro Township Council and read the following letter:

       White City Metro Township & Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling Board

       Board:

       It is with regret that I tender my resignation from the White City Metro Township &
       Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling Board, effective immediately.

       I am grateful for having had the opportunity to serve on the board of this fine
       organization for the past several years, and I offer my best wishes for its continued
       success.

       Sincerely,

       Kay Dickerson
       Mayor Pro Tem

               Council Member Perry, seconded by Council Member Price, moved to accept the
letter. The motion passed unanimously.

               Paul Ashton stated there is a statute that spells out the process the Council will
need follow to replace a council member that is no longer serving. The person who replaces
Council Member Dickerson will serve until December 31, 2021. A notice will be published
advertising the open position. When interviewing candidates, it must be done in the public meeting
and then the Council will vote on the appointment. If the Council does not appoint someone within
a certain amount of days then the County will appoint someone. There is a 30 day deadline.

               Mayor Flint instructed staff to move forward with publishing the notice of vacancy.

Resolution 2021-07-02

               Rori Andreason reviewed the following Resolution authorizing payment to the
Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District for COVID-related expenses:

RESOLUTION NO. 2021-07-02                                                      Date: July 8, 2021

       A RESOLUTION OF WHITE CITY METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL APPROVING
       AND AUTHORIZING A PAYMENT TO THE GREATER SALT LAKE MUNICIPAL
       SERVICES DISTRICT IN THE AMOUNT OF $5,527.26 FOR COVID-RELATED
       EXPENSES

        WHEREAS, the White City Metro Township (“White City” or the “Municipality”) recognizes
its need for municipal-types services; and

      WHEREAS, the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (“MSD”) is an Interlocal
Cooperation Agency that provides fire protection services to White City; and

        WHEREAS, Salt Lake County is a County pursuant to Utah Code Title 17: COUNTIES
and is the County where White City is located; and

        WHEREAS, due to the impacts to state and local governments nationwide, the United
States Government passed the CARES Act which, through Salt Lake County, provided White City
with certain funding that could be used to cover unbudgeted COVID-related costs for the delivery
of municipal-type services; and

                                                4
DATE           THURSDAY                               J U LY                                8, 2021


       WHEREAS, as White City’s municipal services are provided by local districts and interlocal
agencies, it is prudent for White City to assist them with their unbudgeted COVID-related costs;
and

        WHEREAS, the MSD has presented White City with an invoice and purchase information
to request $5,527.26 from White City’s CARES Act funds to cover unbudgeted COVID-related
costs, hereinafter known as Attachment A; and

      WHEREAS, providing this financial assistance benefits White City and softens the impacts
caused to a service provider due to COVID-19; therefore, be it

       RESOLVED, the White City Metro Township Council approves, and authorizes the Mayor
to approve the attached invoice for the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District
(ATTACHEMENT A) and authorize the payment of $5,527.26 to the MSD to assist them in
covering their unbudgeted COVID-related costs.

       APPROVED AND ADOPTED this 8th day of July 2021 by the White City Metro Township
in White City Metro Township, Salt Lake County, Utah.

Resolution 2021-07-04

               The Council reviewed the following Resolution requesting amendments to the
Master Interlocal Cooperation Agreement with the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District,

RESOLUTION NO. 2021-07-04                                                     Date: July 8, 2021

       A RESOLUTION REQUESTING AMENDMENTS TO THE MASTER
       INTERLOCAL COOPERATION AGREEMENT WITH THE GREATER SALT LAKE
       MUNICIPAL SERVICES DISTRICT AND SALT LAKE COUNTY AND OTHER
       AGREEMENTS AS NEEDED

                                          RECITALS

   A. The White City Metro Township (“White City”) is a Municipality pursuant to Utah Code §§
      10-2a-401 et seq., and

   B. The White City Metro Township Council (the “Council”) is the municipal legislative body of
      White City pursuant to § 10-3b-501, and

   C. The Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (the “MSD”) is a Local District pursuant
      to UCA §§ 17B-2a-1101 et al., and

   D. Salt Lake County (the “County”) is a county pursuant to Utah Code Ann. Title 17 Counties
      and is the contracted service provider of certain municipal-type service via the MSD; and

   E. White City receives service from the MSD and from Salt Lake County (through the MSD),
      pursuant to a Master Interlocal Agreement (the “Master Agreement”), by and between the
      MSD, County and Metro Township members of the MSD (the “Parties”), which Master
      Agreement is subject to amendments to ensure the services being provided are of
      satisfactory quality and consistent with the needs of the Council and White City. See, eg,.
      Article II, section 2.4; and

   F. White City desires certain amendments to the Master Agreement, together with other
      interlocal agreements by and between the Parties to improve effectiveness, efficiency,
      overall quality, and better control costs of services received by White City.

Council Member Reports

Unified Fire Authority (UFA) & Unified Fire Service Area (UFSA)

               Council Member Perry stated the 2021-2022 budget was approved. The fraud
risk audit was completed, and it came back low. Continuing discussions on overtime expenses,
mandatory staffing, and trying to find a balance between the two. The Board is looking to increase
wages so staff can be retained. The groundbreaking for the new Midvale Fire Station is on July
13.

                                 −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
Unified Police Department (UPD) & Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA)

                Mayor Flint stated a resolution was adopted modifying the pay scale for sworn
and civilian employees. The Utah Peace Officer Association K9 competition and awards were
given to K9 Jinx and Nitro. A budget amendment was approved for $200,000 to use for early
retirement incentives. Salt Lake County contributes 20 percent of the budget for special services
that include; administrative services, training, shooting range, Property and Evidence, Information
Systems, Dispatch, Special Victims Unit (SVU), Violent Crimes Unit (VCU), Forensics, Special

Weapons and Tactics (SWAT), K9 unit and Motors. UPD budget is on a fiscal year ending June
30 and SLVLESA is on a calendar year, funds do not come in until after the fiscal year. Every
year it has to do a bond anticipation note. A Financial Advisory Board was created to review and
interview all the bids. A budget resolution was adopted for property tax revenues.

                                    −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
South Salt Lake Valley Mosquito Abatement District

                Council Member Price stated testing for West Nile Virus started on June 15 and
there have not been any positive tests. With it being a drier year, there should be less sources of
irrigation that create mosquito problems. The District was audited and it was clean with no
deficiencies. The manager would like to know what is being done with the gully by Big Bear Park
that has not been completed into a trail. There have been 51 acres treated and 875 traps placed.
The tax rate was finalized and there will be no increase. Everyone has been hired and trained
and there have been no accidents.

                                    −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD)

                Mayor Flint stated a group presented information on broad band to the Board,
they want a piece of the CARES Act infrastructure money; however, the funds will not cover the
entire County. There will be a decrease in sales tax class b and c roads. A letter to annex Olympia
Hills into Herriman was accepted. Insignia sent out a postcard to residents directing them to fill
out a survey about the direction of the MSD. White City did well on the survey.
