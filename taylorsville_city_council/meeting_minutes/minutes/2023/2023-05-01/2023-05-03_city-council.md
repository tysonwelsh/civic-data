        1.1  Opening Ceremonies – Pledge/Reverence – Councilmember Harker (Opening

        1.2      Mayor’s Report

      Mayor Kristie Overson reported that she and Councilmembers Barbieri and Burgess had
attended a ribbon cutting at La Oxaquena. There was a great deal of music, dancing, and good
food.

The mayor was also invited to attend a mini ribbon cutting for three new professionals renting
space at Salon Suites at the Crossroads of Taylorsville. Salon Suites was doing well, and she
appreciated the invitation.

Taylorsville Girls Fast-pitch softball had been delayed several times, but the mayor and Chief
Cottam jointly threw simultaneous first pitches. She was pleased to report that their efforts did not
embarrass the city.

Mayor Overson attended ChamberWest’s annual Women In Business event where the key-note
speaker was Abby Cox, Utah’s First Lady and the wrap-up speaker was two-time Olympian
Shannon Happe. She took several city employees with her to the event.

ChamberWest also had a Business Leadership team that met each year in Taylorsville. She and
Councilmember Cochran met with them to discuss the city’s form of government and government
in general. She greatly appreciated the city’s ongoing partnership with ChamberWest.

Along with a majority of the council and John Hiskey, Mayor Overson attended the Utah League
of Cities and Town’s mid-year conference. With that many attendees, she believed they had
managed to have representation at nearly each class/presentation.

She had recently been invited to read to students at the John C. Freemont School. As always, she
enjoyed talking to the kids about civic engagement.

The city had recently hosted Taylorsville High students involved in the “Gear Up” program. Again,
she had the opportunity to speak with the students about city government in general as well as
some specific jobs serving the citizens of Taylorsville.

Taylorsville had been fortunate enough to receive funding from Salt Lake County’s Tourism,
Recreation, Culture and Convention (TRCC) program in the past and it was once again time to
apply for this annual funding opportunity. Applications were in the works.

Mayor Overson and Councilmember Harker had recently attended the Unified Fire Awards
Banquet and she was grateful to be able to see first responders honored.

Utah had recently installed 224 solar panels at the state office building in Taylorsville. These
panels were expected to power the 420,000 square foot building along with 16 electric vehicle
recharging stations. She applauded the state for their efforts to conserve energy.

The mayor explained that the city had been active in preparing for potential flooding. A large pile
of sand and sandbags were available at 4551 South Atherton Drive. She was astounded at how
many sandbags were already filled and ready for use should the need arise.

The mayor then reported briefly on various construction projects throughout the city.

Mayor Overson mentioned that later in the meeting the council would be asked to adopt a tentative,
fiscally responsible, conservative, and balanced budget. She expressed her appreciation to the
council who had expressed their budget priorities in a previous meeting. She and CFO Scott
Harrington were available to meet at any time to go over any specific questions that
councilmembers had.

The mayor encouraged everyone to check their calendars and plan to attend the Starry Nights
events which would take place every Friday night this summer beginning May 5th.

        1.3      Citizen Comments

      Chair Knudsen invited any members of the audience who wished to address the City Council
to step forward and do so.

Nicole McDermott expressed appreciation for the strong partnership between the Jordan School
District and the City. She was also grateful that she had been able to reach out to Councilmember

3.         REPORTS

           3.1   Salt Lake County Public Works – Crystal Hulbert

              Director of Salt Lake County Public Works’ Operations Department Crystal Hulbert
explained that it had been an exceptional winter which resulted in 50% more calls pertaining to
snow removal. In the fall she would return to the council and ask for their help in a public outreach
project to help citizens understand some of the realities of snow removal that her staff dealt with.
She was proud of what her staff had been able to accomplish during a difficult winter.

Ms. Hulbert went on to explain that water was the enemy of asphalt, and her staff was now working
to address potholes. She appreciated everyone’s patience with the large amount of repair work that
needed to be done. They had begun their annual overlay work in Taylorsville but much of her staff
would soon be diverted to assist with flood control/preparations.

Councilmember Harker inquired about the best way to report potholes. Ms. Hulbert responded that
the best way was on the Salt Lake County public works operations website. They could also call
the 24 public works emergency hot-line or send an email to the address provided on their website.

Councilmember Burgess and Chair Knudsen both expressed appreciation for the hard work of the
public works crews.

     3.2         Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling – Pam Roberts

           General Manager and CEO Pam Roberts explained that the SCRP program (Seasonal
Container Reservation Program) was about to wrap up in Taylorsville and it was her understanding
that there were some questions about the scheduling. They provided specific date ranges on the
postcards and provided notification of calendars that were opening up.

WFWRD had invested in truck wraps to promote recycling. Recycling cost more during the first
quarter but WFWRD was managing those costs as must as possible.

Ms. Roberts provided district-wide statistics regarding collection of various types of waste before
addressing stats specific to Taylorsville. There were approximately 200 subscribers to the curbside
glass pick up program and 1400 subscribers for green waste pick up.

The diversion rate for Taylorsville was 18% which referred to waste that was diverted from the
landfill. Some other notable 1st Quarter Taylorsville statistics were:

     •   3,657 tons of garbage delivered to the landfill
     •   746 tons of recycling diverted from the landfill
     •   26 tons of glass diverted
     •   21 tons of curbside green waste diverted

Ms. Roberts explained that this was the 4th year of the updated SCRP program. She reminded the
councilmembers that an ongoing nationwide shortage of CDL drivers was the reason that the
containers would continue to be placed in driveways as opposed to being placed on public streets.
Sixty reserved containers per day were delivered to Taylorsville homes between April 10 and May
4th.

Ms. Roberts concluded her comments by reminding everyone not to place their recyclables in
plastic bags and to follow the city ordinance requiring trees overhanging streets to be trimmed to
a minimum height of 13.5 feet. Doing so would avoid damaging resident’s trees and WFWRD’s
trucks.

         3.3     West Valley Animal Services – Melanie Bennett

               Animal Services Director Melanie Bennett presented information pertaining to the
first quarter of 2023 (January - March.)
      483 animal licenses were issued
      84 licenses for Millrace Park were issued
      Each clerk assisted an average of 616 people per month (walk-ins, phone calls, web
        inquiries, etc.)
      Fewer animals impounded (161 compared to 190 during the same time in 2022)
      Response times had improved significantly on higher priority calls
      Carl Fauver spotlighted Animal Services in a recent edition of the Valley Journal
      All Fur Dogs mobile pet grooming had donated their services to help a heavily matted dog,
        removing 13 pounds of hair from him
      One animal control officer attended professional training
      Ten cats and 23 dogs were adopted out during off-site adoption events

Councilmembers Harker and Burgess both commented how gratifying it was to see how animal
services had evolved and improved in recent years.
