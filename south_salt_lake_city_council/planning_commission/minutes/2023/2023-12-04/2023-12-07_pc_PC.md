2.      PUBLIC HEARING – An Application for Preliminary Plat Approval to Consolidate
        Two Existing Parcels into One Lot, for a Parking Lot Expansion, Located at 245 West
        Plymouth Avenue.

Action Item

Applicant:     Brett Bryson
Address:       245 West Plymouth Avenue and 3791 South 300 West, Sevillo Fine Foods

City Planner, Jed Shum, presented the Staff Report and explained that the application was for a
Preliminary Subdivision Plat, which would consolidate two existing unsubdivided parcels. The
properties were 3791 South 300 West and 245 West Plymouth Avenue. The current zone for both
parcels was the Transit Oriented Development (“TOD”) Zone. The current use on the south parcel
was food processing. Food Processing (Large-Scale) was a non-conforming use in the zone. That
property could only be accessed through the property to the west and had a non-conforming
amount of parking. The new site would provide sufficient parking and public road access to the
structure. Mr. Shum shared a Vicinity Map and explained that the property abutted a TRAX line
on the east side of the property is in close proximity to the Meadow Brook Station. Currently, the
workers at Sevillo parked on the road or at the station and then walked over. Providing the
additional parking would benefit the surrounding neighborhood by shifting the parking to the site.

Images of the current site were shared. Mr. Shum reviewed the Proposed Preliminary Plat and
pointed out Plymouth Avenue to the north. There had been work done with the City Engineers
and the applicant would dedicate the land needed for the road to meet the current standards. The
public utility easement along the north edge of the 3791 South 300 West parcel, abutting the
structure on the north, was only 5 feet instead of 10 feet as that was the existing distance between
the property line and the building. The Proposed Site Plan was shared. He explained that parking
alone could not be the sole use of the parcel, so the two lots needed to be combined and platted in
order to pave the area and provide the necessary parking for the existing structure. Mr. Shum
reviewed some of the relevant General Plan considerations, which included the following:

   •   Community Values Goal 1: Ensure that existing and new developments have equitable
       access to all services and amenities in South Salt Lake:
          o The proposed new plat will provide direct road access to Plymouth Avenue for the
               business located at 3791 South 300 West, which currently does not have public
               street access and must access the site through another property.
   •   Transportation Goal 1: Improve the active transportation system to enhance South Salt
       Lake as a safe and enjoyable place to walk and bicycle:
          o The workers at Sevillo currently tend to park on the roadside or at Meadow Brook
               Station and then walk over to the site. The approval of this plan will indirectly
               improve the active transportation system within the City by reducing traffic flow
               and parking cars in the active transportation system.

Based on the information submitted in the application, supporting documentation, and an analysis
of the relevant Code sections and General Plan goals, Staff recommended that the Planning
Commission move to approve the Preliminary Subdivision Plat amendment to consolidate the two
unsubdivided parcels, located at 3791 South 300 West and 245 West Plymouth Ave, based on the
Findings of Fact, Conclusions of Law, and Conditions of Approval listed in the Staff Report.

Chair Pechmann opened the public hearing.

David Nielsen reported that he is one of the owners of the property that was used as access for
Sevillo. The company has approximately five semi-trucks per day that need to back across 300
West and down the entire driveway. He asked if it would be possible for the semi-trucks to pull
through the proposed parking lot, into the right-of-way, and back into their dock, rather than
backing all across 300 West. In the summer months, there are usually 10 semi-trucks per day. He
felt that the access was a safety issue that needs to be addressed. Mr. Nielsen clarified that his
property is located to the west of the subject property. He identified where the semi-trucks are
backing into the site using a map of the area. There are safety concerns about where the trucks
backed up and he hoped that the Planning Commission would address that existing issue.

Brett Bryson identified himself as the applicant and was handling the layout of the parking lot. He
acknowledged that the proposal does not address all of the needs but it would increase safety for
the workers accessing the building and remove parking from areas in the neighborhood. What was
proposed would not solve the semi-truck access issues. He was not sure there was a different way
for the semi-trucks to enter the site but there was an easement to use the street. The parking lot
was intended to provide parking to employees and make it safer to enter and leave.

There were no further comments. The public hearing was closed.

The Commission discussed the application. Chair Pechmann noted that the traffic pattern issue
was concerning but does not fall within the purview of the Planning Commission to address.
