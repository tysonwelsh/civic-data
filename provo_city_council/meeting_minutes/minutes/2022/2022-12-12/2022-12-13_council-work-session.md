7.      A discussion regarding Provo-Orem Dispatch Services Agreement (22-047) (1:02:51)

Wayne Parker, CAO, presented, noting that Orem City was having this same discussion that
afternoon. He appreciated having Fire Chief Jim Miguel present to answer any questions. Mr.
Parker indicated that this services agreement was drafted by Brian Jones, City Attorney, and was
currently being exchanged and circulated between the two cities. Mr. Parker gave an update on
the dispatch operations. Orem dispatchers have been co-located with Provo since October. Calls
from both cities are being routed to one place, which has avoided issues that happened before
due to the proximity of the two cities. Orem employees will officially become Provo employees

on December 25 (to ensure there is not a lapse in their benefits and new benefits through Provo
can start in January). Mr. Parker shared some details of the agreement, including how leave
balances and other employee benefits will be handled, the cost for Orem to buy-in, and defined
cost-sharing. They are currently working on the budget for the first six months (through the end
of the fiscal year 2023). He indicated that there would be two items coming to the Council in
January—an amendment to the General Fund budget regarding dispatch expenses and revenues,
and a resolution approving the final services agreement and authorizing the Mayor’s signature.
Mr. Parker said this consolidation effort has been characterized by a high level of trust between
the two cities and has gone very smoothly.

Councilor David Shipley asked about the possibility and additional capacity for other cities to
potentially join. Mr. Parker explained that Provo and Orem are well-matched in population and
level of service. Orem City provides fire services to Vineyard and Lindon; combined, this
represents a larger population than Provo. He indicated that they wanted to be extremely cautious
not to dilute the decision-making ability of Provo and Orem in this process—if they allow the
possibility of other entities joining, they would implement a very high standard and allow either
city (Provo or Orem) to refuse. This would likely be handled by interlocal agreement to outline
the process and standards if it were to become an option in the future.

Councilor Bill Fillmore commented that one of the best reasons for this is the economy of scale
and asked for details on the efficiencies introduced by the consolidation. Mr. Parker explained
that the budget assumes staffing levels at a given spot based on call volumes, shift staffing, etc.
They may be able to eliminate several positions at the management level since there is just one
entity being managed rather than two separate ones as before. This consolidation also saves
significant square footage for Orem’s new city hall project. There are also some savings in
having both cities’ fire departments using the same communications channel. Mr. Parker
indicated they would know more about budget savings soon. Chief Miguel indicated that
combined, both cities have 51 FTEs; now they have 42 FTEs. He also thought this would aid
recruitment and retention. He also indicated it would improve the quality of life for dispatchers
by making it easier for them to take time off (due to a larger pool of employees to cover shifts).
They have already observed some cost savings, in employees and equipment. Presentation only.

8.      An ordinance amending Provo City Code regarding towing operations to add a
        grace period for patrol tows. (22-076) (1:18:01)

Brian Jones, City Attorney, presented. This has been a discussion topic for the Council for the
last few work meetings. Mr. Jones was reporting back and presenting draft language to add a
grace period for patrol towing. To conduct patrol tows, the towing operator must be certified
with Provo City and they are required to enter into a contract with property owners. Before a tow
truck can connect to a vehicle, the operator must document the violation is. This new language
would add a 10-minute grace period after they have documented the violation before they can
attach to begin towing a vehicle, as long as it was parked in a marked parking space that is open
to anyone in the lot. If a vehicle is in a numbered spot that is reserved for a specific user, an
emergency spot, or a maintenance vehicle spot, then the grace period does not apply. Councilor
David Shipley commented that he thought this matched the Council’s intent. He asked if there
was an unintended consequence of this new language changing how people design or mark

from Mr. Handley for a tool or framework that the Council could use to evaluate zone changes
for housing developments. This language is outlined below.

“Evaluating Proposed Rezone Applications for Housing Developments
“The following set of questions is listed to assist the city’s legislative body as they evaluate
rezone applications related to proposed housing developments:
     Would the rezone promote one of the top 3 housing strategies?
            Promote a mix of home types, sizes, and price points
            Support zoning to promote ADUs and infill development
            Recognize the value of single-family neighborhoods
     Are utilities and streets currently within 300 feet of the property proposed for rezone?
     Would the rezone exclude land that is currently being used for agricultural use?
     Does the rezone facilitate housing that has reasonable proximity (½ mile) to public transit
       stops or stations?
     Would the rezone encourage development of environmentally or geologically sensitive,
       or fire or flood prone, lands? If so, has the applicant demonstrated these issues can
       reasonably be mitigated?
     Would the proposed rezone facilitate the increase of on-street parking within 500 feet of
       the subject property? If so, is the applicant willing to guarantee use of a TDM in relation
       to the property to reduce the need for on-street parking?
     Would the rezone facilitate a housing development where a majority of the housing units
       are owner-occupied? Is the applicant willing to guarantee such?
     Would the proposed rezone facilitate a housing development where at least 10% of the
       housing units are attainable to those making between 50-79% AMI? Is the applicant
       willing to guarantee such?

“The above questions are not a set of requirements, but they will help in evaluating rezone
applications related to housing development. Answering affirmatively to a majority of the
questions above would make for a stronger rezone application, whereas an application that
answers affirmatively to only a few of the questions would be a weaker application. These
questions are not meant to limit the legislative body in their decision-making power.”
