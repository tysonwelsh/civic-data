3.4 Legislative Update 21-0111
Attachments: Staff Report
COW leg update 2_2_21
2021 Tracker - 020221 COW
Presenter: Kara Trevino, Legislative Director, Council (Approx. 1 hr). 
Discussion/Direction
Ms. Kara Trevino , Intergovernmental Relations Manager, Council Office, 
reviewed the following legislative bills:
‒         SB 102 Peace Officer Training Qualification Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this bill permits lawful permanent residents who have been in the U .S. 
for five years and have legal authorization to work in the U .S. to apply to become peace 
officers.  The Sheriff’s Office supports this bill.
Sheriff Rosie Rivera  stated this bill will help the Sheriff's Office move forward in a 
positive way for its community.
A motion was made by Council Member Bradshaw, seconded by Council Member Bradley, 
that the Council support SB 102.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
- HB 242 Ticket Reseller Amendments
Page 3 of 12
Committee of the Whole Meeting Minutes February 2, 2021
Ms. Trevino stated this bill helps the County's cultural venues with fraudulent ticket sales .  
When fraudsters print tickets at home, it creates multiple tickets for each seat.
Ms. Holly Yocom , Director, Community Services Department, stated this does not affect 
people reselling legitimate tickets.  
A motion was made by Council Member Winder Newton, seconded by Council Member 
Bradshaw, to support HB 242.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
- HB 243 Privacy Protection Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this bill creates a state committee to oversee privacy and technology 
throughout the state, thereby impacting local governments.  The Utah Association of 
Counties (UAC) and several County agencies have reviewed this.  There is concern about 
the bill ’s broad reach.  She recommended working with the sponsor to get some 
representation on the board.
Council Member Bradshaw stated the Councils takes privacy very seriously.  A 
committee that recommends standards is a good idea, but empowering the state to take 
punitive action is not the best route. 
A motion was made by Council Member Bradshaw, seconded by Council Member Winder 
Newton, to oppose HB 243.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
- HB 244 First Class County Highway Road Funds Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated the First Class County Highway Road Fund accumulates some 
money from revenue generated in Salt Lake County.  This bill distributes those funds 
equitably to various jurisdictions.
Council Member DeBry suggested the Council monitor this bill.
Council Member Newton  stated this was not a good process to determine road funds .  
However, the County desperately needs money for roads.  
Mayor Jennifer Wilson  stated she did not want to get into an area where all are 
contributing tax dollars and only some are receiving the road funds.  She reviewed the 
pros and cons of this legislation.
The Council agreed to monitor this bill.

- HB 256 County Land Use and Development Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this bill requires a super majority vote when an adjacent city protests 
a development.  
Council Member Bradshaw suggested the Council monitor this bill.
Mayor Jennifer Wilson  stated the County would want to engage with the cities around 
any undeveloped land.   She suggested meeting with the sponsor to let him know the 
County is open to engagement.
The Council agreed to monitor the bill.
- EBus Air Quality Monitor Appropriation
Ms. Trevino stated in the December budget, there was a $ 20,000 appropriation to go 
towards this pilot project.  Other partners have also committed. 
Mr. Michael Shea , Environmental Program Manager, Transportation, Housing & 
Economic Development Department, stated the idea is to create a more detailed solution 
mappings than is currently available.  This project will place air quality monitors on 
electric buses and then collect daily data from the bus route.  This will enable the mapping 
of pollution hot spots throughout the valley.  The data collected will be accurate and 
useable.
A motion was made by Council Member Winder Newton, seconded by Council Member 
Bradshaw, to support the appropriation.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
- SB 52 Property Tax Deferral Modifications
Ms. Trevino stated last week the Council took a position to see how discussions went 
with the sponsor.
Mr. Wayne Cushing , County Treasurer, stated this bill is a property tax deferral bill that 
allows those 66 years old and over and living in a house worth less than $ 500,000 to defer 
their property tax.  There are around 60,000 households that would qualify for this and the 
total taxes from that group would be $ 150,000,000.  He is opposed to this because it is 
too overreaching.  He also described other deferral and abatement programs offered by 
his office.

A motion was made by Council Member Bradshaw, seconded by Council Member 
Snelgrove, to oppose SB 52 as written.   The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
- SB 88 Local Option Sales Tax Distribution Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this is the ZAP bill.  Last week the Council opposed it with legislative 
intent.  She is trying to work with the sponsor with changes to Tier II funding.  
Council Member Newton recommended sending a letter to the sponsor.
Mr. Mitchell Park , Legal Counsel, Council Office, stated each individual Council 
Member can decide if they want to sign onto the letter.
Mayor Wilson  stated the more specific the Council is with the legislator, the more it is 
tied in with little latitude.
Council Member DeBry  stated Ms. Trevino, Council Member Alvord, Council Member 
Bradshaw, and Holly Yocom, Director, Community Services Department, would work 
together to draft language for this letter.  The letter can then be disseminated to all Council 
Members who wish to sign it.
- HB 82 Single-Family Housing Modifications
Ms. Trevino stated this bill is being held in committee and work is being done to find 
middle ground.
- HB 98 Local Government Building Regulation Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated last week the Council took a position to oppose this bill.
The Utah League of Cities and Towns and Utah Association of Counties (UAC) are 
working ferociously against this.  However, this is a priority for House leadership .  
Negotiators are to work on remedies with jurisdictions that are very bad about delays in 
permits and inspections.  She recommended keeping the same position.
- SB 18 Property Tax Exemption Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated last week the Council took a position to oppose this bill.  It was also 
opposed by the League of Cities and Towns, UAC, and school districts.  The sponsor 
met with UAC and adjusted the threshold to a level the  assessors are willing to go up to .

- HB 81 Mental Health Days for Students
Council Member Snelgrove  stated youth suicides are a tragedy and anything the 
Council can do to save a life is important.  This will would allow students to be excused 
from school for mental health reasons.  He would like the Council to support this bill.
After some discussion it was decided the Council will take another week to review the bill 
before voting on it.
- Mountainous Planning District
A motion was made by Council Member Theadore, seconded by Council Member 
Snelgrove, to have Ms. Trevino work with County lobbyists at the legislature to take the 
following position:
Carve the Town of Brighton out of the Mountainous Planning District  and it to have its 
own land use ability.  Allow the County to continue to use General Fund money for public 
safety of municipalities in a recreation area in accordance with Utah law.  Remove the 
sunset provision on the Mountainous Planning District and allow the County Council to 
determine the membership criteria for the Mountainous Planning Commission.
Council Member DeBry  asked if this issue could be discussed because it was not on 
the agenda.
Mr. Mitchell Park , Legal Counsel, Council Office, stated this could be discussed.  The 
agenda refers to legislative updates, not individual bills.  The Chair can also pull any item 
unless there is a motion and second on the table.
A substitute motion was made by Council Member Newton, seconded by Council Member 
Bradshaw, that the proposed language be sent to all Council Members so it can be reviewed 
by the legislative team and the Mayor ’s Office to get the details worked out. The motion 
carried by a unanimous vote.

SLCo Rental Assistance Efforts During COVID 2-2-21
County Council Briefing -- 02022021
Presenters: Mayor Jenny Wilson; David Schuld, Intelligence Section Chief, Salt 
Lake County COVID-19 Response; Gary Edwards, Director, Health 
Department; Jill Miller, Associate Deputy Mayor of Finance; and Dr. Audrey 
Stevenson, Health Department (Approx. 30 mins).
Discussion/Direction
Mr. Gary Edwards , Director, Salt Lake County Health Department, stated 
Covid cases in Salt Lake County have been going down.  Testing numbers, 
the positivity rate, and hospitalizations are all in decline.  This is reason for 
hope, but the country is still dealing with a pandemic that has a long way to 
go.
Mr. David Schuld , Salt Lake County Covid -19 Response & Recovery 
Coordinating Officer, delivered a PowerPoint presentation.  He reviewed key 
numbers, the active case curve, an infection snapshot, a zip code 
surveillance, current cases by age distribution, the rolling 14-day comparison, 
testing analysis, known versus community cases, potential exposure source, 
and the County transmission index.
Mr. Edwards delivered a PowerPoint presentation regarding the County ’s 
vaccine response.  He reviewed the vaccine priority chart, an administration 
report, and preparedness activities.
Ms. Catherine Kanter , Deputy Mayor of Regional Operations, and Ms. 
Erin Litvack,  Deputy Mayor & Chief Administrative Officer, delivered a 
PowerPoint presentation regarding County rental assistance during the Covid 
pandemic.  They reviewed housing stability, number of County renters, risks 
for housing stability and the cost of eviction.  They also covered steps being 
taken to improve stability and a look forward to a second Federal stimulus 
package.

Sheriff Rosie Rivera  stated this presentation highlights a part of the Unified 
Police Department.  She has committed to highlight the units paid for by 
countywide tax dollars.

there is currently a bill before the legislature to expand the Mobile Crisis 
Outreach Teams (MCOT).  Senator Kathleen Riebe is sponsoring the bill and 
there is a lot of appropriation attached to it.  The need for these mental health 
interventions is large and is statewide.
