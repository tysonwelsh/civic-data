Joint Proclamation declaring March 1, 2021 as "COVID Memorial Day" in
Ogden City
           A joint proclamation declaring March 1, 2021 as "COVID Memorial Day" in Ogden City came before the Council for
consideration. Council member Lopez read the joint proclamation, after which COUNCIL MEMBER LOPEZ MOVED TO
APPROVE THE JOINT PROCLAMATION. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER STEPHENS, ALL
VOTING AYE.
           Chair Blair then presented the proclamation to representatives of Intermountain Healthcare and Ogden Clinic.
           Council member Choberka thanked Donna Chapman from McKay Dee Hospital, and Kim Nichols from Ogden Clinic,
for their attendance this evening. Ms. Chapman stated she appreciates the opportunity to be involved in this meeting; she noted
McKay Dee Hospital has treated just over 1,100 COVID patients and has lost just under 100 patients to the illness. The impact on
families and caregivers has been significant. She is proud of the hospital's staff and their willingness to serve their community
through this difficult time. Her team is now offering COVID-19 vaccinations and she hopes that these efforts will further slow the
spread of the illness. She stated she appreciates the City's recognition that COVID-19 has had a huge impact on the community,
including those who have contracted the illness and survivors of those lost to the illness. Mr. Nichols echoed Ms. Chapman's
comments; Ogden Clinic has tested 60,000 people since July and he appreciates the great work performed by his staff as they have
strived to keep themselves, their co-workers, and other patients safe. This has been a 'heavy lift' for health care workers
throughout the world and he is thankful that Ogden City has recognized them and the service they have provided.
           Council member Hyer asked if Ms. Chapman or Mr. Nichols are aware of any data regarding the indirect effects COVID-
19 has had on the country in terms of mental health issues or suicide. Ms. Chapman stated that she does not have specific data, but
it is clear COVID has had far reaching impacts; behavioral units at medical facilities are also overwhelmed because the isolation
caused by the disease has led to mental health issues. Mr. Nichols agreed; not only is the mental health of patients impacted, but
the mental health of health care workers has been dramatically impacted. Stress levels are very high and people are working a
great deal of overtime, only to see young and otherwise healthy patients pass away. These conditions can be very discouraging,
and he is worried about the long-term impacts of health care worker burnout associated with the pandemic. Council member
Choberka added that rates of mental and behavior health needs have risen dramatically; the suicide rate in Utah has not increased,

         Joint Proclamation declaring March 2021 as "Women's History Month" in
         Ogden City
                    A joint proclamation declaring March 2021 as "Women's History Month" in Ogden City came before the Council for
         consideration. Council member Choberka read the joint proclamation, after which COUNCIL MEMBER CHOBERKA
         MOVED TO APPROVE THE JOINT PROCLAMATION. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER
         LOPEZ, ALL VOTING AYE.
                    Chair Blair then presented the proclamation to Katie Nelson, Executive Director of the Weber County Heritage
         Foundation, and Jessica McCowan, Junior League of Ogden President Elect. Ms. McCowan stated the Junior League of Ogden is
         so honored and grateful to be part of this recognition effort; the mission of the League has been focused on the needs, interests,
         and development of women in Ogden for over 100 years and the League is thrilled that Ogden has a City Council and Mayor who
         would take the time to acknowledge and actively celebrate Women's History Month. Moving into March, the League is excited to
         not only encourage the research of women's history, but also take the time and opportunity to engage and educate the coming
         generations, utilize voices, and be heard to hold up the legacy that has been laid by women leaders in this community. She thanked
         the City for its celebration and focus on a strong, healthy, and equitable future for the women of Ogden.
                    Ms. Nelson applauded Ms. McCowan's comments; she stated it is wonderful to live in a City that acknowledges
         Women's History Month. The City has a rich history to draw from and the Heritage Foundation has been working to unearth
         women's stories that often fall through the cracks. The Foundation is pleased to include three women on the National Votes for
         Women Trail; there are only five per state and Ogden got three of those individuals. The Foundation looks forward to celebrating
         this accomplishment later this spring with a ceremony to install the historical markers at three different sites in the City; one of the
         sites is at The Junction where Sarah Anderson lived. She left her home to cast her vote even though it was illegal to do so; she
         walked two blocks down the street to the place that is now home to the Salvation Anny and attempted to register to vote. She was
         turned away because she was a woman, so she sued and took her case to court where the judge ruled that Sarah Anderson and all
         women do indeed have the right to vote. The case was appealed to a court in Salt Lake and that judge overturned the previous
         ruling. The other two markers will be installed on private properties at the site of the former homes of the two women being
         honored. She thanked the Mayor and Council for their suppmt and indicated she hopes to build on the momentum of celebrating
         the diverse and incredibly fascinating history of the whole of Weber County.
                    Council member Choberka stated she appreciates the great work performed by both the Junior League and the Weber
         County Heritage Foundation.
                    Mayor Caldwell added that he also appreciates the great work done by both groups; he is the father of daughters and
         celebrating strong women is very meaningful to his family.

            David Timmerman stated he would like to know the process to request the creation of a new committee that would work
on justice and police reform in the City. He is interested in participating on or at least establishing such a committee; he feels the
time is right to address these important issues. It is important to evaluate how police are deployed and what responsibilities they
have to the community. He noted that the Redevelopment Agency (RDA) convenes in a closed session later tonight and there are a
few items that can be discussed in a closed meeting that are important to him: deploying security personnel and using updated
crime detention techniques. He noted Ogden has a real time crime center, which can be a boon to the community, but he is
concerned there are no 'guard rails' limiting the activity of that group. Any time there is a lack of supervision or specific
guidelines, the results can be a serious violation of constitutional rights by the City's men and women in blue. He noted he
supports the Police Department strongly but given the current climate it is important to monitor and record activities to avoid
violations. Sometimes it is necessary to evaluate the point of certain programs or processes, determine if dollars are being spent
wisely, and if there is enough supervision to ensure public safety officials are doing the right thing. He then noted that South
Ogden City has an officer who was involved in a fatal shooting; that man was not disciplined, took his fifth amendment rights, and
is back in service working for that entity. However, he probably should have been terminated due to his lack of cooperation in the
investigation ofthe shooting.
            Kevin Lundell stated he listened to the podcast oflast week's work session meeting and he thanked the City employees
who make the podcast happen. He has a few questions about the City's utility study and plans to increase certain utility fees; he
polled four of his friends to determine if they have ever paid a late fee on their water bill and all of them told him yes because of
the difficulty in signing up for the City's auto pay service. He stated he hopes the new utility billing system will simplify this
process and help residents avoid paying late fees. He understands the arguments to change the City's billing system in a manner
that will bring it in line with industry standards, but in terms of late fees, if those fees are not expected to result in a noticeable
revenue increase, there is no reason to implement them. Those who are most penalized by late fees are those that are on a fixed
income; these accelerated late fees could be very detrimental to people in that situation, and he asked that the Council consider all
of the unintended consequences of the proposal. He also asked them to consider if the current system is 'broken' and in need of
fixing.
            Angel Castillo stated she will talk to the Council tonight about an issue she has been raising for the past several years:
housing affordability. In October of2020, the Council had a discussion about the issue and acknowledged there was a problem;
Council member White indicated she would like to look into housing affordability issues that are specific to Ogden rather than the
metropolitan area around Ogden. She noted Council member White has spoken with experts in this field and was told that Ogden
has a shortage of2,205 affordable rental units, and 3,160 affordable and available units. She asked what the Council is going to do
about the issue. During the October 2020 meeting, Council member Nadolski was adamant that he wanted to begin working on the
issue. The City has access to experts in this area and she advised them to utilize those experts; they have current information and
can help to implement tangible goals. She noted that this morning Mayor Caldwell participated in a press conference during which
Governor Cox indicated the entire State of Utah has a housing affordability problem. She noted Mayor Caldwell has gone on
record to indicate that Ogden has a surplus of affordable housing, but that is based on Ogden metropolitan data, which includes
cities surrounding Ogden. She asked what the City Council is going to do about the issue; they have talked about it a great deal,
but it is time to begin working on a plan that will address opportunities for increasing availability of different types ofhousing in
the City. There are some great programs that the City is already employing, butthey will not solve all of the issues in the City, and
it is time to take the next step in working to get things done.
