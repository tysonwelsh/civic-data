                     NEPHI CITY WORK SESSION MINUTES
                            Tuesday, July 18, 2023
                                  7:00 p.m.
                                 21 E. 100 N.
                               Nephi, UT 84648

1. Mr. James White addressed the council about a claim that he made with the city’s
   insurance company about the asphalt in front of his home being damaged by the
   heavy equipment that worked on the culinary water project. He made a claim with
   Johansen Construction’s insurance and the city’s insurance and the claim was
   denied by both companies. Mr. White had been told that there are pictures from
   Google Earth showing that the low spot had been there before the water project. He
   said he would like to see the pictures. Mayor Seely said that the City would provide
   the pictures to him.

2.    Mr. Joel Cowan addressed the Council and said that he had attended a council
     meeting a few weeks ago where some residents spoke about the road sinking in
     their subdivision. He said that he had spoken to Councilor Parady about the one-
     year warranty period for the infrastructure in subdivisions and suggested that the
     warranty period be extended beyond one year. City Attorney Kasey Wright said that
     the warranty period of one year is mandated by state statute.

3. Mr. Jeff Parkinson, developer of Reed Ridge Subdivision, addressed the council
   about the Reimbursement Agreement his lawyer had drawn up. Mr. Parkinson
   stated that he was appalled at the personal shots directed towards him in the staff
   report. He said the staff report grossly misrepresented the agreement. He gave the
   council a history of his development and the demands the city had required of him.
   He disagreed with the City’s estimated 2600 kw of power capacity that he was
   required to put in his development. He said his development does not need that
   much power. He said he was willing to install 2200 kw. He said that he and the City
   have been arguing about the issue since November. City Attorney Kasey Wright
   said that what Mr. Parkinson was explaining about the issue of requiring too much
   power capacity had nothing to do with the agenda item of the reimbursement
   agreement. Councilor Memmott asked Power Superintendent Rust Finlinson if the
   same method of calculating power capacity requirements that had been used for Mr.
   Parkinson’s development had been used on other developments. Mr. Finlinson
   answered, “yes.” Mayor Seely stated that he trusted the experts in the City and
   supported their work. Councilor Worwood stated that the City is willing to use the
   reimbursement agreements template that had been used for other developments for
   Reed Ridge, but the agreement Mr. Parkinson had presented demanded too much
   liability on the City’s part.

4.   The Council discussed some options for fixing the road damage caused by the
     extreme winter, collapsible soils, and large trucks. One of the options for curb,
   gutter, and sidewalk is to use a foaming technique that would raise the infrastructure
   back to the original elevations. It is more cost effective than replacing the sidewalk,
   curb, and gutters. Mr. Atkinson provided the Council cost estimates and a map of
   the problem areas. Councilor Memmott said that the water needs to be able to run
   freely to the drains. Councilor Parady said we are seeing road damage all over the
   city and not just in the new subdivisions and it may just be looking at extra street
   maintenance in a an extremely wet year. City Planner Shauna Talbot said that the
   Building Inspector Layne Allred said that we may need to require specific
   geotechnical reports for specific sites where we know that there are collapsible soils.
     Mr. Atkinson said that he would notify the concerned residents in Chapel View
   Subdivision about how the City plans to proceed with repairing the damage to the
   roads and that the general recommendation is to get the water moving so that it
   doesn’t pool and cause damage.

5. The Council discussed a rezone request for 429 N Main from CU-1 To CU-2.
   Planning Commission member Heather Robertson explained that the Planning
   Commission had tabled the request for two months over concern changing zones
   with every developer that comes in. Planning Commission member Cory Thomson
   said that he was absent from the meeting, but he would have been in favor of
   changing the zone. The commission tabled the rezone to hear feedback from the
   council. The council voiced their approval of changing the zone.

6. The Council discussed the a issue of having a Hive tournament director to manage
   the Hive during the summer months. The City received one proposal from Travis
   Story. The council directed staff to moved forward in drawing up an agreement with
   Mr. Story to help run tournaments at the Hive during the summer months.

7. Mr. Atkinson reported that he had met with the engineers for the Crimson Flat
   development, and they may have some contacts for different funding opportunities.

8. Finance Director Lisa Brough asked the council if they would like to use Larsen and
   Company for the 2023 audit. The Council directed her to bring back and
   engagement letter for the audit to the next regular council meeting.

9. Councilor Callaway thanked the other councilors for their help with the Mammoth
   Parade. He said that the Mammoth Parade Committee Members consisted of the
   following: Brett Wilkey, Dave and Jessica Fletcher, Tanielle Callaway, Katie
   Callaway and McKade Poulsen.

10. Mr. Atkinson reported that we had a close call of losing power throughout the city but
    because of the efforts of our Power Superintendent requesting the high-power users
    to cut back some, the emergency was avoided. Rocky Mountain Power installed
    some relays so that we can now provide 29 megawatts instead of the 25 megawatts
    that it was incorrectly set at.
       11. Councilor Ostler shared some concern about the condition of the bathroom at the
           Vine Bluff Cemetery. Mr. Atkinson said that they are making plans to replace the
           restroom.,

       12. Councilor Memmott moved to convene into regular meeting. Councilor Worwood
           seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.

       13. Councilor Parady moved to convene into executive session to discuss the character
           and competency of an individual. Councilor Worwood seconded the motion The
           motion passed unanimously.

       14. The meeting adjourned at 9:26 p.m.
