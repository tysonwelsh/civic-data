      1. RATIFICATION OF THE BIDS FROM THE WEBER COUNTY TAX SALE HELD ON MAY 23, 2019.
            Roger Brunker, of the Clerk/Auditor’s Office, explained the tax sale statute and the intensive process staff uses
            to help property owners keep their properties. This year the list started with 181 properties and the final
            contained 8, three were not bid on and were struck off to the county. After the remaining five are ratified, the
            county will prepare and record a tax sale deed transferring the county’s interest in the properties to the successful
            bidders. The commissioners stated that they had not received any written dispute during the 10 days following
            the tax sale. Any bids above the tax amounts, go to the State. Chair Jenkins stated that the county owns 1,800
            little slivers of property and Commissioner Froerer said that there had been discussions on the best way to off-
            load these slivers from the county. He would like Mr. Brunker’s input as well. He noted the need to address
            this issue as soon as possible.
            Commissioner Harvey moved to ratify the bids from the Weber County tax sale held on May 23, 2019;
            Commissioner Froerer seconded.
            Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Froerer – aye; Chair Jenkins – aye

    5. AGREEMENT WITH UNION PACIFIC RAILROAD COMPANY FOR A PERMIT TO BORE UNDER THE RAILROAD
       TRACKS AT 7500 WEST.
         Gary Myers, of County Engineering, stated that the county is getting ready to construct the final improvements
         for this section of 12th Street and needs to increase the ability to move stormwater. The area is very flat and in
         some cases ditches push water in both directions. This is to bore an additional storm drain line under Union
         Pacific’s railroad tracks on 7500 W., which will allow moving more stormwater run-off, snow melt, etc., south
         and out of the area. This project is beneficial to the county as it currently does not have a way to move
         stormwater south down 7500 W., it will allow utilizing portions of existing channels, and it will also help
         farmers to use their fields more effectively. The county worked with area land owners on the design.
         Commissioner Harvey moved to approve the agreement with Union Pacific Railroad Company for a permit to
         bore under the railroad tracks at 7500 West; Commissioner Froerer seconded.
         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Froerer – aye; Chair Jenkins – aye

    3. PUBLIC HEARING TO CONSIDER GPA 2019-01, A REQUEST TO AMEND THE GENERAL PLAN FUTURE LAND
       USE MAP FROM INDUSTRIAL PARK TO RESIDENTIAL/AGRICULTURAL USE AT THE INTERSECTION OF 900
       SOUTH AND 7900 WEST.
         Steve Burton, of the County Planning Division, noted that on 10/2/2018 the Commission approved a rezone in
         this area which shifted A-2 property and M-1 property. One applicant did not realize that this was going to
         occur and had some other property rezoned to manufacturing, which was not what he had wanted. This is a
         county-driven request to amend the Future Land Use Map of the West Central Weber County General Plan in
         order for the rezone to meet the General Plan, and it changes the land use designation in this area from Industrial
         Park to Residential/Agricultural. Noticing on this item was followed according to State law. On 5/14/2019 the
         Planning Commission recommended approval and the item has been non-controversial. He addressed
         Commissioner Froerer’s question stating that the idea is to give a few property owners, who have their current
         property zoned M-1 and desire that zoning, the ability to keep it M-1. They cannot expand this zoning unless
         the General Plan is amended. The idea is to keep manufacturing more towards 8300 West. The rezone in the
         following item is for the few property owners who want their property to be agricultural. Chair Jenkins
         explained that these property owners were included in a previous rezone by accident and are being taken back
         out.
