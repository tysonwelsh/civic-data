PRESENTATIONS:

Application #: Z-1-2025
Applicant: Eric Nguyen
Address: 3001 W 4100 S
Request: Zone change from A to RM, 1.53 acres

This application was continued by the Planning Commission during the meeting held on April 23,
2025 due to concerns about the size and design of the proposed townhome units relative to the
surrounding homes. The applicant has since submitted revised plans that are described in this
report.

Eric Nguyen has submitted a zone change application on a 1.53-acre parcel located at 3001 W
4100 S. The current zoning is A (Agricultural, minimum lot size ½ acre) and the requested zoning
is RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling Residential). The General Plan designation on the property is
Medium Density Residential (7 to 12 units/acre).

Surrounding zones include R-1-8 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 8,000
square feet) to the north and south, A and R-1-8 to the east, and R-2-8 (Two Unit Dwelling
Residential, minimum lot size 8,000 square feet) and A to the west. Neighboring uses include
single family homes to the north, east, and south and the Sunnybrook Park Condos and Valley Jr.
High School to the west. The property includes a home that, according to Salt Lake County, was
built in 1940, a detached garage, and three other outbuildings.

The applicant is proposing to retain the existing home, demolish the existing detached buildings,
build a new garage for the existing home, and construct a 14-unit, two-story, for-rent townhome
development. The proposed density of the project including the existing home is 9.8 units/acre.
All townhome units would face the perimeter of the property and each unit would include a 2-car
garage and 3 bedrooms. All garages have driveways that are deep enough to park in. The attached
concept plan and application materials from the applicant provide details on and support for the
proposed project.

The original proposal included 3 buildings with 4 units each and 1 building with 2 units. The
latest proposal includes 7 buildings with 2 units each. In addition, the latest elevations include a
covered balcony and front porch along with a gable over the entry.

In reviewing this application, staff suggests that there are two primary questions to answer:

   1. Is this the right location for a townhome development?

   2. Assuming the location is appropriate, does the quality of the project further City goals?

These two questions are explored below.

Is this the right location for a townhome development?

According to the City’s zoning ordinance, zone changes to the RM zone can only be considered
by the City if the following four conditions are met:

   1. For properties without existing Structures which are proposed to be developed, the
      property must be a minimum of one acre and the Density must not exceed 12 units per
      acre. For properties with existing Structures proposed to be redeveloped, the property
      must be a minimum of 20,000 square feet and the Density must not exceed 20 units per
      acre. For properties that are west of Redwood Road and within one-half mile of the West
      Valley Central light rail station, the Decker Lake light rail station or the Redwood
      Junction light rail station, the Density may exceed 20 units per acre;

   2. The property shall meet any one of the following four requirements: (i) have access and
      Frontage on a Street with a planned Right-of-way width of at least 80 feet (for properties
      under two acres) or 100 feet (for properties two acres or larger) as indicated on the Major
      Street Plan; (ii) be adjacent to existing multifamily residential development on two sides;
      or (iii) be located west of Redwood Road and within one-half mile of the West Valley
      Central light rail station, the Decker Lake light rail station, or the Redwood Junction light
      rail station; or (iv) if the property is vacant, was vacant as of January 19, 2021, will be an
      exempt housing development under 24 CFR 100.304 or successor regulation with a
      density no greater than seven units per acre, and have access and Frontage on a Street with
      a planned Right-of-way width of at least 80 feet;

   3. A Development Agreement must be proposed with the zone change application that
      addresses Dwelling Unit sizes, exterior materials, architecture, Landscaping, and project
      Amenities; and

   4. A traffic impact study prepared by a registered traffic engineer is required for projects
      with 100 or more Dwelling Units.

This application meets the first condition in that the property has existing structures, is over
20,000 square feet in area, and has a proposed density below 20 units/acre. For the second
condition, the subject property has access and frontage on 4100 S, which has a planned right-of-
way width over 100’ at this location. On the third condition, the applicant has proposed
development agreement standards and those standards are addressed later in this report. The
fourth condition does not apply because the project is under 100 units.

While the property is eligible for RM zoning, the City may still deny requests for RM zoning.

The points below may help the Commission answer the first question.

   •   There are five existing multi-family developments (apartments, townhomes, and condos)
       that front or have access on 4100 S within the City – Boulder Pines Apartments at 1535 W
       4100 S, Sage Valley Apartments and Townhomes at 1812 W 4100 S, Sunnybrook Park
       Condos at 3121 W 4100 S, Village Green Apartments and Townhomes at 4150 S 4000 W,
       and Aspenwood Apartments at 4139 S 4080 W.
   •   This section of 4100 S is currently a 5-lane arterial street that is serviced by UTA bus
       routes 47 and F232.
   •   Average annual daily traffic (AADT) on this segment of 4100 S was 29,000 in 2023. A 5-
       lane arterial can handle a traffic volume of 26,500 at a level of service (LOS) C and
       30,500 at a LOS D.

Does the quality of the project further City goals?

City Code requires requests for the RM zone to be accompanied by a development agreement
proposal that addresses dwelling unit sizes, exterior materials, architecture, landscaping, and
project amenities. These items, along with other considerations, are addressed below.

Dwelling Unit Sizes
In the zoning ordinance, the minimum unit size for 3-bedroom townhomes is 1,400 square feet.
The proposed townhomes are 1,700 square feet.

Exterior Materials
For materials, the City’s multi-unit residential design standards state: “All multi-unit residential
Building exteriors shall be brick, stucco, stone, tile, metal panels or fiber cement siding. With the
exception of brick or stone which may be used on the entire Building exterior, no single material
shall comprise more than 60 percent of a Building’s exterior. Where stucco, tile, metal panels or
fiber cement siding are used, at least 20 percent of the Building’s exterior shall be brick or stone.
Wood and vinyl may be used as trim, soffits and/or accent materials only.”

The applicant is proposing a combination of stone and fiber cement siding. According to the
applicant, the percentage of brick meets the 20% requirement. While the fiber cement siding
exceeds the 60% rule, there could be a variety in color and a mixture of horizontal and vertical

Architecture
Based on a preliminary review of the proposed building architecture by staff, the proposed units
fall short of the City’s multi-unit residential design standards in window treatments. The
ordinance requires three window treatments with at least two window treatments per window on
the front of buildings. Based on staff review, two treatments – varying the size and/or style of
windows and window grids – were identified.

Landscaping
The ordinance requires a total of 50% landscaping and recreation space. The applicant’s concept
shows 50.3%.

Project Amenities
For a project of this size, the ordinance requires one amenity. The applicant is proposing to
include either a sports court or covered pavilion. Given the proximity of neighboring homes, the
Commission expressed their preference for a covered pavilion given concerns about noise from a
pickleball court.

Garages/Parking
The ordinance requires all townhome units to include a 2-car garage. The overall parking
requirement for townhomes is 4.75 with 2 in a garage, 2 in a driveway, and an additional 0.75
within the project. All of the units meet the 4.75 stall requirement.

Other Considerations
The City’s Public Works Department is recommending that the developer incorporate a parkstrip
along 4100 S similar to the property to the west. The applicant has agreed to this so, staff is
recommending that this provision be addressed in the development agreement.

During the Commission’s study meeting on May 21st, the Commission discussed adding
management standards to the development agreement. Staff has included with this report an
example of management standards used in other development agreements for rental housing
developments.

Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval. This zone change application should be approved subject to a development
      agreement that incorporates the standards proposed by the applicant along with the
      following additions recommended by staff:
          a. The developer shall install a 5’ parkstrip along 4100 South and replace the existing
             sidewalk.
          b. The project shall include an outdoor covered pavilion with a picnic table.
          c. All of the Multi-Unit Residential Design Standard in the City’s Zoning Ordinance

Discussion: Steve Pastorik presented. He noted an email from Wayne McKay, the son of the prior
owner, regarding irrigation water rights affecting the property and it would be addressed in a
future site plan review if the zone change is approved.

Commissioner Matagi asked Attorney Brandon Hill about the Fair Housing Act- if it defined
"family" in housing contexts. Brandon Hill explained that while there's no federal definition of
“family”, there are protections for certain groups, such as disabled individuals. He emphasized
that construction type, rather than “family” definition, is the best approach to addressing housing
concerns. The discussion also touched on the distinction between residential apartment
developments and boarding houses.

The applicant’s architect, Fred Cox, added that he contacted both adjacent canal companies trying
to get additional information. He spoke with Wayne, who had contacted the City, to find out
where the water comes from. Fred went out to the site and looked at the culvert that is supplying
the water to that open ditch, which is a 15 inch diameter pipe. The developer can easily match
that, or possibly larger, depending on the slope.

Fred Cox clarified that the units would not be rented out by the room, as previously assumed. The
Commission expressed concerns about rental properties in the area, but noted the proposed
development might offer better maintenance than individual rental homes. They agreed that the
homes themselves met the necessary standards, though questions remained about the applicant's
ability to maintain the rental agreements.

Mike Markam commented that he still feels that rented townhomes are not a good fit for the
neighborhood. He has concerns about the noise of garbage trucks and dumpsters, parties in the
south common area, and the lack of extra parking.

Motion: Commissioner Matagi motioned to approve Z-1-2025 subject to the four conditions
listed in the staff report. Commissioner Durfee seconded. A roll call vote was taken, and four
Commissioners were in favor of the motion.
