          Three memos from City Administration regarding the proposed purchase of the Forest Service Building (FSB) came
before the Board for consideration. The first memo addressed the offer to purchase real property and the acceptance contract and
stated Administration is seeking Board approval of a resolution authorizing the Executive Director to execute the Offer to
Purchase Real Property and Acceptance Contract provided by the General Services Administration (GSA) for purchase of the U.S.
FSB located at 507 25th Street. The purchase price for the FSB is $3.6 Million. The deadline to accept GSA’s Offer to Purchase is
December 16, 2024 (extended from November 25, 2024). If the Offer is accepted, a non-refundable 10 percent earnest money
payment will be due at the time of acceptance and the Agency must be ready to close within thirty days.
          The second memo addressed the intent to reimburse the Agency using bond proceeds and stated that if the Board
approves the Offer to Purchase Real Estate and Acceptance Contract with the GSA, an earnest money payment in the amount of
$360,000 must accompany the executed Contract. The timing of the earnest money payment will require the Agency to advance
funds for the purchase of the building. The Resolution of Intent to reimburse the Agency with bond proceeds is required under the
IRS rules governing the issuance of tax-exempt bonds. Adoption of the Resolution of Intent does not commit the Agency to issue
the bonds.
          The third and final memo addressed the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 budget amendment of $600,000, which
recognizes funds from the Adams Community Reinvestment Area (CRA) fund balance and loan proceeds and appropriates those
funds for the 10 percent earnest money payment ($360,000) and estimated maintenance costs for two years ($240,000). An
additional budget amendment will be required prior to closing to fund the remaining purchase price. It is anticipated that proceeds
from a private placement bond issue will reimburse the Agency for the earnest money payment and purchase of the building.
          Economic Development Director Meess summarized the memos and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss
the City’s goals for the purchase and reuse of the FSB, including:
               • Rehabilitation of a historically significant building;
               • Ensuring that reuse contributes to continued success of Nine Rails Creative District and Adams CRA;
               • Commercial or mixed-use programs, including:
                        o Supporting artists, entrepreneurs, and small-medium businesses; and
                        o Potential for residential use on upper floors.
               • Creative parking solutions
               • Publicly visible art; and
               • Avoiding detrimental uses and vacancy
          The City advertised a request for proposals (RFP) for redevelopment concepts for the building and received two
proposals with different reuse scenarios. Administration met with the developers who submitted the proposals to discuss their
ideas and outline a deal structure. The recommended proposal is to create a live/work environment that preserves the historic
building and contributes to the success of Nine Rails:
               • Mixed-use building that provides affordable housing and workspace targeted to creative and entrepreneurial
                   residents.
                        o Attracts artists and other creatives to Nine Rails.
               • Collaborative studio space on main level and additional artist and/or performance space in basement level.
                        o Generates additional activity and vibrancy through arts.
               • 40-50 residential units available to households at average 65 percent of area median income (AMI).
                        o Ensures Nine Rails remains attainable for working artists and creative entrepreneurs.
                        o Wealth building for residents through Perpetual Housing Fund.
               • Art pieces at building entrances on 25th Street and Adams Ave.
               • Parking at nearby property.
               • Short-term creative commercial use to keep building occupied and active.
          Ms. Meess concluded by discussing the use of Tax Increment Financing (TIF) for the project; the property acquisition
cost of $3.6 million with $360,000 in carrying costs for 24 months. Sources of fund include $1 million from property sale
proceeds, $2.4 million in TIF as a development incentive and $658,000 in TIF for the housing allocation. The total RDA funding
is just over $4 million.
          Board discussion centered on all options available to the City for future use of the building and the price range for
housing units to be included in the building if the redevelopment proposal is approved.
          Bond Counsel Laura Lewis discussed the proposed resolution dealing with the issuance of bonds to reimburse the RDA
for money allocated for the earnest money payment for the FSB; she discussed the steps the Board must take to proceed with the
bond action and noted the estimated bond amount will be $4.5 million to provide the City with some flexibility in terms of
acquisition and maintenance of the building.
          Council member Choberka then stated she would like to discuss the development proposal with Mr. Parker of Giv Group.
She asked him how his proposal will change if he is not able to secure Low-Income Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC) funding for the
project. Mr. Parker stated that the success of the project is not dependent upon use of LIHTC funding, and he would be happy to
pursue alternative uses if necessary. He is not tied to the development proposal that he submitted and is more interested in the
future success of the building and its surroundings. He will brainstorm other funding sources if LIHTC is not available. Council
member Choberka asked Mr. Parker to discuss the perpetual housing fund for the benefit of the Board and the public. Mr. Parker
stated that the perpetual housing fund exists to remove the red tape, down payment, and income barriers that keep a rapidly
expanding portion of the state from building equity where they live. This is done by building high-quality multifamily properties
and allocating the majority of all proceeds and equity in the building to resident-shareholders. He then discussed the success of
projects that have been associated with the perpetual housing fund to date.
          Chair Richey asked Mr. Parker how he plans to address the parking needs for the building. Mr. Parker stated that he has
been in discussions with the owner of the neighboring property regarding shared parking opportunities and he has included
information regarding those options in his proposal and pro-forma document.

          Heath Satow stated that he intended to use his time to suggest that the City avoid a contract scenario similar to that it
entered into regarding the Brown Ice Cream building, but after learning that the Giv Group is the developer selected by the City,
he has no worries about their ability to bring the project to fruition. He supports the perpetual housing fund model the project as a
whole.
          Pieder Beeli stated he wished to voice his deep concern that Ogden has been in competition with private enterprise and
stifling wise expenditures that the public and taxpayers would otherwise make. He would prefer that Ogden let property assets go
and allow the free market to engage in these types of projects. The taxpayer is not well represented at the meetings regarding these
types of issues, and he would love to see other changes made in terms of education costs, woke doctrines, and general taxation by
the federal government. He believes the market will find its natural equilibrium and determine appropriate housing projects for the
community.
          Laura Lewis asked if the budget amendment includes the allocation of funding for an earnest money payment; she asked
how that allocation will be accounted for in the budget and if additional taxes will be needed to cover those costs. She then asked
how the perpetual housing fund operates and how a renter will eventually receive money. She asked how the apartments will be
maintained to ensure they have a positive impact on the value of surrounding properties.
          There being no further persons appearing to be heard, BOARD MEMBER MYERS MOVED THE PUBLIC
HEARING BE CLOSED. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY BOARD MEMBER GRAF, ALL VOTING AYE.
          Chair Richey addressed Ms. Lewis’s question regarding the budget amendment; he indicated that the $600,000 will cover
the earnest money payment and some maintenance costs for the building and is being transferred from the General Fund and will
not have a direct impact on taxpayers. Comptroller Sorensen clarified that $240,000 is actually coming from the Adams
Community Reinvestment Area (CRA) project area fund, not the General Fund. Bond proceeds will be used to reimburse the
Agency for the $600,000, meaning that taxpayers will not be impacted.
          Chair Richey asked Bond Council Lewis to address debt service plans for the bond. Ms. Lewis indicated the general
concept is to pursue a direct purchase transaction with a bank to ensure some flexibility to repay the bond debt. The plan is to use
sales proceeds for the building to repay the bond.
          Chair Richey asked Mr. Parker to address Ms. Lewis’s question about hose the perpetual housing fund operates. Mr.
Parker stated that the goal of the perpetual housing fund is to ensure appropriate funding to cover maintenance costs for the
building, as well as to give renters access to the profits generated at the property. He stated there is no downside for the tenants of
the building and renters are actually incentivized to participate in the maintenance and upkeep of the building to ensure the
preservation of higher profits that they will receive a share of.
          Board member Hyer referenced the age of the building and asked Mr. Parker if he is aware of any need to pursue a
seismic retrofit of the building. Mr. Parker stated there are various levels of retrofits that could be needed and his budget for the
project does include a significant amount for that element of the project. The building has been through several minor earthquakes,
but some retrofitting is needed to ensure tenants of the building would survive a level seven earthquake. He is familiar with this
type of work due to efforts to retrofit other buildings in the area.
          Board member Myers asked Mr. Parker his feelings about leaving the building to the free market to purchase and
redevelop. Mr. Parker stated that Mr. Beeli’s question was a good one; the benefit of the City being involved in the project
includes transparency of the future use of the building. If the City does not purchase the building, the federal government will
auction it off and its future will be unsure. He feels the proposal he has made for reuse of the building is very positive and
interesting. However, he is neutral on the matter and will support a decision not to purchase the building as well. He will remain a
partner to the City as he has already invested heavily in downtown Ogden.
          Board member Hyer then addressed Mr. Beeli’s comments and noted that all members of the RDA Board are taxpayers,
meaning there is taxpayer representation in all discussion and actions taken regarding this matter

          Board member Blair stated he understands the desire to steer the future of the FSB, but this project feels more like a want
than a need for the community; the financing for the project is ‘tight’ and he does not want the City to be in a difficult position as
a result. That is the basis of his opposing vote. Board member Hyer agreed. He noted the City’s initial appraisal of the property
was almost $1 million less than the appraisal secured by the GSA; he would have been more supportive of the acquisition based
upon the City’s appraisal. Additionally, he believes the City’s current ordinances would have governed the reuse and
redevelopment of the property and it is not necessary for the City to purchase the property to ensure those directives come to
fruition.

          Executive Director Nadolski thanked the Board for their support of the actions needed to proceed with the acquisition of
the FSB. He thanked the Board and the public for their input, but noted the most important reason, in his opinion, for the City to
be involved in the acquisition of the property was the historic preservation element. The RDA has tools at its disposal to be
involved in these types of projects; he loves to rely upon the free market, when possible, but there are times when it is appropriate
to exercise the tools of the RDA, and this is one of those times. He thanked Ms. Meess for her professionalism and passion for this
project.

government aspirations. He believes the vote to purchase the FSB is being subsidized by the taxpayer and he feels that is immoral.

actions that are being taken by elected officials that will ultimately impact their wallets. He concluded that a good government is a
small government. It is immoral for any government to push away the free market when there is a solution in the free market.
