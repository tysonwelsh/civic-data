       Proposed transfers from the City's Utility Enterprise Fund to the General
       Fund
                  A memo from Council staff caine before the Council to consider a resolution approving enterprise fund transfers. The
       memo stated as required by Utah State Code §10-6-13 5.5, a city that is proposing to transfer funds from an enterprise fund to
       another fund for a pm-pose not directly related to the goods or services provided by the enterprise fund must provide notice of the
       transfers to the users of the enterprise fund. The city must also hold a public hearing on the transfers prior to adoption or the
       amendment of the City's budget. In Ogden, a budgetary transfer from the City's Water, Sanitary Sewer, Storm Sewer, and Refuse
       Utility enterprise funds to the General Fund has been done for many years; however, the requirement to provide notice of the
       transfers and to hold a separate public hearing is a recent change to state law. These utility funds are accounted for as enterprise
       funds in Ogden and are operated with user fees charged for goods and services. In the proposed fiscal year 2021 budget, the City
       is proposing a transfer from these utility funds into the City's General Fund to act as the collection of franchise and property tax.
       In order to comply with state law requirements, a public hearing on the proposed transfers is scheduled for June 23, 2020.
                  Council Deputy Director Symes summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
       proposed utility fund transfers; he reviewed the notification that has been provided to Ogden City utility customers regarding the
       proposed transfers, after which he noted the proposed transfer for the FY2021 budget is $5 .4 million. If the transfer is not made,
       utilities rates may be lowered; however, property taxes would have to be raised to remain fiscally solvent. Anticipated property tax
       revenue in FY21 is approximately $15.7 million. If these utilities were independent, the City would receive revenue from the
       collection of franchise and property taxes. In previous years - from 1996 to 2016 - the City consider trai1sfers from utility funds
       for public art; this method was a collection of an additional one percent of capital project costs, but that method was changed to a
       transfer based upon historical averages. Public arts are no longer funded from utility funds. The City Council is required to hold a
 \,1   public hearing to receive input regarding the proposed transfers; there is no action to be taken at the public hearing and, rather, the
)      transfers are incorporated into the proposed budget. The approval/denial of the transfers happens with the adoption of the FY21
       budget and the City will send a follow up notice to utility customers once the final budget is adopted.

Proposed Ordinance 2020-20 adopting the salary schedules for all
employees of the city
          A memo from Council staff regarding Fiscal Year (FY) 2020-2021 salary schedules came before the Council for
consideration. The memo stated each year the Council approves a salary schedule for all officers and employees of the City. The
salary schedule implements the proposed employee wage and benefit decisions.
          Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss
the FY 2020-2021 salary schedule proposal. Four salary schedules are proposed as follows: Non-Merit-Special Employees, Merit
Employees, Classified Employees within the Police Department, and Classified Employees within the Fire Department. The
schedules include reclassifications for positions in the City Council office and Management Services Department. Additionally,
six new positions are included in the schedules, all of which are grant funded. No cost of living adjustments (COLA) or merit
increases are included in the budget, but health insurance will increase eight percent and the City will continue to pay 100 percent
of the cost of the high deductible health savings plans. Increases to the traditional employee health plans will be passed on to
employees. Finally, Tier II retirement contributions are being increased and the City will absorb those increases rather than pass
them on to employees.

FY2020-2021 Budget, including changes outlined in Schedules A and A-1.
          Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide
an overview of the proposed final budget. Top priorities for the City Council include financial sustainability and maintenance of
essential services. She briefly highlighted the Council's budget goals, which are guided by strategic initiatives included in the
Five-Year Strategic Plan for economic development, community safety, recreation, City image and reputation, and fiscal
sustainability and transparency. She provided a comparison of the FY20 adopted budget with the FY21 proposed budget; the
FY21 proposed budget was be $4.4 million less than the FY20 budget. City Administration has suggested changes to the proposed

development, not inflation. She concluded the Council's current practice is to perfonn a quarterly budget review to compare actual

projections against actual receipts to track impacts of COVID-19 and to consider additional appropriations is revenues allow.

Proposed Ordinance 2020-24 amending the definition of taverns,
private clubs and drinking establishments
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed zone text amendments
pertaining to drinking establishment definitions came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated a petition has been
filed by Martin McFadden with Blue Ox Development to amend the definitions related to drinking establishments as well as the
regulations for drinking establishments in the Business Exchange Historical (BER) zone. While these requests are closely
connected, the two requests will be separated into different items for Council consideration. The proposed amendments with this
item would eliminate the definitions for tavern and private club and replace them with a new defmition for a drinking
establishment. The proposed definition for drinking establishment in the City's code would cover the different types ofalcohol-
serving establishments defmed in state code and regulated in the City's code. The proposed defmition for drinking establishments
would also clarify thatforthepurpose ofthe City's zoning and number ofdrinking establishments per linear block, alcohol sales must
exceed 50% oftotal annual receipts. A similar proposal to update the coderelatedtothedefmition of taverns and private clubs came
before the Council in 2016; however, no fmal action was taken on that proposal. The 2016 amendments came in conjunction
with a petition to amend the defmition of a linear block.
          Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and used the aid of aPowerPointpresentation to expound on the
petition and the Planning Commission's recommendation. The proposed amendments are indirectly tied to petitions 2019-24 and
2015-9 as well as State law changes; current City ordinances need to be updated to reflect State liquor laws; additionally, the
proposed ordinance amendment would accommodate proposed use for Ogden's own liquor only bar with no beer sales. Utah State
laws provide defmitions for restaurants and bars; restaurants must have receipts for 70 percent food sales and three types of
restaurants can serve alcohol: limited service, beer only, and full-service businesses. Bars and taverns are different; taverns are
beer-only bars. Bars must have food available, but patrons are not required to order food in order to purchase alcohol. Mr.
Montgomery then noted the current City Ordinance classified businesses with more than 5 0 percent beer only sales as taverns and
private clubs, while restaurants have more than 50 percent food sales. The petitioner is proposing to operate a liquor tasting
room/bar only with no beer sales that that type ofuse does not fit within either of the City's ordinance defmitions. The proposed
wording changes in the ordinance include:
               •     Replace tavern and private club with Drinking Establishment;
               •     Revise defmitions;
               •     City's defmition follows present city standards of 50 percent rather than the State's of 70 percent; and
               •     Revise wording in land use sections.
          Additionally, the defmition ofrestaurant will be changed to clarify Ogden's distinction from the State's. He summarized the
effects of the ordinance amendment proposal; the City ordinance would create a niche by allowing 'adult only' restaurants.
Restaurants that serve between 5 0 percent and 70 percent food sales are classified by State law as bars, not restaurants, and do not
allow minors. The regulation limiting the use to two per linear block applies only to 'drinking establishments' which sell more
than 50 percent alcoholic beverages per City ordinances. The ordinance proposal does not change development standards that the
downtown has developed under for the past 20 years. He concluded the Planning Commission voted unanimously to recommend
approval ofupdating City ordinances to adopt' drinking establishments' as a replacement for 'taverns' and 'private clubs'.
          Chair Choberka asked if it may be easier to simply adopt State regulations for drinking establishments. Mr.
Montgomery stated the City has additional restrictions regarding the number of drinking establishment uses allowed per linear
block.
          Council member Stephens asked if the State's provisions regarding the ration ofbeer to food sales will trump the City's
provisions. He also asked if the ordinance amendments would apply to the entire City or just to a specific zone. Mr. Montgomery
stated that the ordinance, if adopted, will apply to all areas of the City where drinking establishments are allowed. He noted that
the State's regulations are applied to licensure applications, while the City's regulations apply to zoning of properties where
drinking establishment uses are allowed. Council member Stephens asked if adoption of the ordinance could result in an increase
in the number of bars or taverns in the community, to which Mr. Montgomery stated it will provide an opportunity for drinking
establishments to locate in the City without the requirement to ensure a certain percentage of their sales are from food. Council
member Stephens asked if the requirements for drinking establishments to be located a certain distance from community
locations, such as churches and schools, will remain intact. Mr. Montgomery answered yes.

Proposed Ordinance 2020-28 designating the Stone Farmhouse, located at
301 West 2nd Street, as an historic resource
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a historical designation action came
before the Council for consideration. The memo stated petitioners Anna and Michael Keogh are proposing the home and property
located at 301 West 2nd Street, known as the Stone Farm, be listed as an historic resource on the Ogden City Register ofHistoric
Places. The designation would require any significant changes proposed for the building, including demolition, to be approved by
the Landmarks Commission through the approval of a Certificate of Historic Appropriateness. The designation would help ensure
the preservation of the property and would help to ensure that any changes proposed would maintain the character and historicity
of the home and property. The City's Landmarks Commission has reviewed the proposed designation and has forwarded a
recommendation of approval finding that the building meets both the historical and architectural criteria listed in the City's code
for local designation.
          Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
nomination to include the subject property on the register of historic places. He used the aid of an aerial photograph to identify the
location of the property, which is part of the historic Stone Farm. The home on the property was constructed in 1925 on a unique
fort remains foundation. There are four accessory structure, including a garage, chicken coop, three-sided tractor shed, and
workshop. The homeowner has approved the nomination. When considering the nomination, there is a focus on historic
significance of the property and below are the findings of historic significance:
              •    The five Stone Farm buildings were built in the 20's and 30's and tell a story of the property being used as a
                   diversified farm.
              •    The property was a first-rate dairy farm, transitioning to diversified farming of sugar beets, hay, and wheat.
               • Further transitions included from horses to tractors.
               •   The Stone farmhouse was a modem, new bungalow.
          The architectural significance of the primary dwelling unit was also considered; the Landmarks Commission found it is a
one-story bungalow constructed with a bungalow roof; it has two gables on the north fa9ade and gabled ends include arts and
crafts elements with half-timbered design of wood and plaster. There are original doors and windows on the main level and an
original enclosed frame porch altered in the 80s with new windows and siding. The Landmarks Commission voted unanimously,
five to zero, to recommend approval of the property at 301 West 2 nd Street to be in the local historic register based on the
property's historic and architectural significance.
          Council member Stephens asked if the historic designation would protect the accessory structures and if the petitioner
will be required to maintain those. Mr. Montgomery stated the designation will apply to the prope1ty and the prope1ty owner will
be required to maintain those structures on their property.

          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department came before the Council to consider a resolution
approving a Coronavirus Relief Fund (CRF) Business Grant Program. The memo stated the City has received an initial

and secondary--incurred due to the public health emergency related to COVID-19. The Department is proposing to use a portion
of Ogden City's allotted CRF funds to establish a Coronavirus Relief Fund Business Grant Program to support businesses
impacted by COVID-19. This grant program will augment existing business loan programs and fill a gap in the needs of various
sectors of the cmmnunity.
          Department Director Christopulos summarized the memo and stated the proposed Grant Program will be available to
businesses licensed Ogden City that fall into one of four general categories. Each category has a maximum grant amount:
              •     Locally Owned Companies. Local companies or companies that have distinct profit-and loss accountability at

              •     Ogden Residents as Employees. Any company, regardless of ownership, with employees located in Ogden with
                    non-localized profit-and-loss accountability, with parent corporations. Maximum $100,000;
              •     Sole Proprietorships. Maximum $50,000;
              •     CBD Retail Needing Physical Modifications. Retail Businesses (shops, restaurants, bars, etc.) located in the
                    Central Business District that must make physical modifications to accmmnodate physical distancing
                    (Maximum $50,000).
