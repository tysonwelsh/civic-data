 G. PUBLIC COMMENTS: David Rawson, of Ogden Valley, Recycled Earth owner, said that people care about recycling
    and the county should not stand in the way of individual options, that haulers could have bid the price for recycling
    and want the job, but that the county stood in the way of that option. Currently, in the Valley they spend $17 for the
    recycling can and it will be $17.46 for the new program, that most county residents have the recycling option, that
    compared to other unincorporated areas the cost is $12.50/can, that they should be spending $6.98 for a recycling
    can rather than $17, and that program may not be working well because it was done in a different time. He requested
    holding off on awarding the contract until haulers have opportunity to provide an option for that second can, which
    on average is 40% of the other one and would reduce waste going to the Transfer Station. Commissioner Jenkins
    said that they looked into this but the market for recycling is weak and he referred to tariffs on China, etc. He
    suspects it will be added in the future. The commissioners are committed that recycling needs to occur but it needs
    to be feasible, and currently there are also unknowns due to proposed incorporations in the western Weber County.
            Commissioner Jenkins moved that item E.6 be moved under the Action Items; Chair Froerer seconded.
            Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Froerer – aye

      1. WEBER COUNTY RAMP 2020 FUNDING RECOMMENDATIONS.
            Shelly Halacy, of the County Commission Office, presented this to WACOG last night and said that they seemed
            pleased with the recommendations. She spoke of the great enhancements to communities because of RAMP, a
            sales tax initiative approved in 2004, and appreciates the hard work by county staff as well as the tremendous
            amount of work and dedication by the volunteer board members. Maresha Bosgieter, Advisory Board Chair,
            submitted the list of funding for RAMP and recognized Ms. Halacy and the board members for their hard work.
            She gave an overview of RAMP since its approval, which included that one penny/every ten dollars spent in the
            county goes into RAMP, that since then it has given $42,811,210 back to the community to fund projects, that
            this year there was an increase in funding totaling about $4.2 million from about $3 million/year previously, and
            she outlined the distribution calculation. The commissioners thanked the board members and Commission staff
            for their long hours spent on RAMP and spoke of the good for our community and the ensuing positive economic
            impact resulting from out of State visitors to our recreation, arts, museums and parks facilities.
            Commissioner Harvey moved to approve the Weber County RAMP 2020 funding recommendations;
            Commissioner Jenkins seconded.
            Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Froerer – aye

    3. MUNICIPAL    MATERIALS MANAGEMENT AGREEMENT WITH REPUBLIC SERVICES                                      FOR    THE
         UNINCORPORATED AREAS OF WESTERN WEBER COUNTY AND UINTAH HIGHLANDS.
         Reece DeMille, from Republic Services, was present. Sean Wilkinson, County Community Development
         Director, gave contract highlights, including that Republic is the sole and exclusive provider of municipal solid
         waste collection for residential units utilizing 90-gallon containers in these unincorporated areas. It excludes
         commercial waste and recycling materials. Collection begins on/or after April 1, 2020, Republic will provide
         all necessary equipment and labor, service-related complaints will be made to Republic and will be given prompt
         and courteous attention. If Republic is at fault and misses containers, they will be picked up within one business
         day after receiving the complaints. All municipal solid waste collected by Republic in these areas will be taken
         to the county’s Transfer Station. Republic will perform the billing services on a quarterly basis. The initial
         contract term is for 5-years with automatic 6-year successive periods, unless canceled by either party. Quarterly
         costs are $43.17 for Uintah Highlands and $45 for western Weber County. Average cost savings to residents
         who will be using this service is about 50%. The service providers will be sending information to these residents,
         which will include the customer service information for complaints, billing, etc., thus residents are encouraged
         to first contact the providers and then the county if they do not receive resolution. Commissioner Harvey
         expressed appreciation to Mr. Wilkinson and Commissioner Jenkins for their work on this item stating that the
         county got involved to help residents save money. From what he has seen, this will save an average of
         $200/annually for the same service they are currently receiving in the south end of unincorporated Weber
         County. Commissioner Jenkins recused himself due to a conflict of interest.
         Commissioner Harvey moved to approve the Municipal Materials Management Agreement with Republic
         Services for the unincorporated areas of western Weber County and Uintah Highlands; Chair Froerer seconded.
         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Jenkins – abstained; Chair Froerer – aye
