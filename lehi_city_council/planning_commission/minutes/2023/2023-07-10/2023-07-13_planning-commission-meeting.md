3.6) Public hearing and recommendation of Bronson Tatton’s request for review of the Dry
Creek Village Zone Change on 16 acres of property located at approximately 1450 South
1100 West, changing the zoning from TH-5 (transitional holding) to Mixed Use.
Mr. Curtis did a presentation on this item.

Mr. Curtis made clarification about the number of units. In the staff report, there were references
to different numbers of units for a development project. Initially, it was mentioned as 142 units,
but later, it was revised to 150 units. The issue arose because eight units were not included in the
initial application, and they belong to the Alpine school district. However, the current review is
for phase one of the project, which comprises 150 units, and there is no request for a density
increase.

The overall land use plan allows for up to 500 units due to an overlay, but this is subject to
approval based on the applicant's proposal and compliance with requirements. The city council
has the discretion to approve or reject the project based on its layout and other considerations.

There were also comments and questions from the Commissioners regarding the layout and zone
change for the project submitted by the public through Engage Lehi online platform. The zone
change is aimed at transitioning the area from a holding zone to the next zone, allowing for water
dedication to facilitate development.
