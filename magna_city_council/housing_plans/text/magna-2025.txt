Park commercial center. The planned route will also intersect with an improved railroad crossing
on 8400 West, further enhancing pedestrian safety and mobility, especially for students and
residents navigating the corridor on foot or by bicycle.In coordination with Salt Lake County
Parks and Recreation, Magna staff have also helped to identify a preferred alignment for the
Utah & Salt Lake Canal Trail. As part of this effort, the trail route will include relocating an

G
Magna selected a strategy to, “amend land use regulations to allow for higher density or new
moderate income residential development in commercial or mixed-use zones near major transit
corridors” on pg. 60 of their MIH plan. Magna has limited access to public transportation within
the city, having only one public transportation option, the UTA route 35 bus line. The effective
terminus of this line is Magna’s Historic Commercial District. To facilitate the development of
additional affordable housing options within this transportation-served commercial district, on
March 28, 2023, the Magna Metro Township Council adopted Ordinance 2023-O-05, amending
Title 19 of the Municipal Code to reclassify several properties from C-3 Commercial to the
Downtown Historic District Mixed-Use (DH) zone. This zoning change was intended to
encourage reinvestment and promote a pedestrian-oriented, mixed-use development pattern in
Magna’s historic core.Since the adoption of the DH zone, Magna City staff have actively worked
to support and engage the development community in leveraging this new zoning opportunity.
Despite outreach and ongoing coordination, no new development has occurred within the
rezoned area to date. Through conversations with property owners and potential developers, it
became clear that certain limitations in the original ordinance were acting as a barrier to
investment, particularly the exclusion of drive-through commercial uses.In response, the city
undertook a zoning amendment to better align the DH zone with market interest while
preserving its foundational design intent. A key component of this amendment was the addition
of “drive-through” as a permitted use within the DH zone. Previously, the code restricted such
auto-centric features to maintain a walkable, historic character. The update allows for drive-
through retail or service windows, such as those for coffee shops, pharmacies, or financial
institutions, provided they are integrated into site designs that preserve pedestrian connectivity
and scale.This adjustment demonstrates Magna’s flexible, proactive approach to zoning, one
that responds to community and developer feedback while remaining committed to the
revitalization of the downtown area. By expanding the range of permitted uses, the city aims to
unlock new opportunities for investment, increase the viability of underutilized parcels, and
ultimately encourage development that complements both the economic and cultural fabric of
the community.

The third strategy selected by the city calls for zoning or rezoning for higher-density or moderate
income residential development. This strategy, identified on pg. 63 of the MIH plan, emphasizes
the importance of zoning and rezoning for higher density housing options with areas of the city
that are best equipped to absorb growth, especially those areas that are currently, or will be
serviced in the future by public transportation opportunities. During the reporting period, Magna
City and municipal staff worked in partnership with Habitat for Humanity to facilitate the rezoning
of a 0.5-acre parcel from A-1 (Agricultural) to R-1-8 (Single-Family Residential) zoning. This

to be adjacent to the future UTA route 31 bus line. The homes will be offered through Habitat for
Humanity’s affordable homeownership program, which provides 0% interest mortgages and
requires significant “sweat equity” contributions from future homeowners. These homes are
intended to serve households earning 30% to 60% of the area’s median income (AMI), helping
to expand affordable housing opportunities within the community.This project reflects Magna
City’s ongoing efforts to support attainable homeownership while balancing neighborhood
character and housing needs.

The final strategy selected by Magna City, as identified on page 69 of the Moderate Income
Housing (MIH) Report, focuses on fostering a regulatory environment that encourages the
development of “missing middle” housing types. In Magna, the most effective means of
increasing housing opportunities within established neighborhoods is through the promotion of
both internal and external accessory dwelling units (ADUs).To ensure the City’s regulatory
framework provides clear, accessible guidance on where and how ADUs may be constructed,
the City Council directed staff in the fourth quarter of 2024 to begin implementing targeted
strategies aimed at facilitating greater ADU construction. These efforts included:•A reduction in
annual business licensing fees associated with ADUs.•The elimination of addressing fees for
ADUs, reducing the financial barriers to their establishment.Additionally, Magna City is in the
process of developing an ADU Amenity Program designed to further support homeowners by
providing:•Design guidance to ensure ADUs are well-integrated within existing
neighborhoods.•Resources and potential incentives to encourage the creation of legal, safe, and
thoughtfully designed ADUs.These initiatives reflect Magna City’s broader commitment to
expanding attainable housing options through thoughtful, incremental infill development that
aligns with the community’s existing character.

access and safety.In addition to regulatory reclassification, Magna has proposed specific design

the curb design on both the east and west sides of the intersection to support a continuous
multi-use path, an 8-foot park strip, and traffic-calming curb extensions. These improvements

The decision to amend the Historic District Mixed-Use (DH) zone to allow drive-through
windows was not without debate. Some members of the City Council and planning staff initially
expressed concern, emphasizing that the original intent of the DH zone was to promote a
pedestrian-first development pattern within Magna’s historic core. They feared that introducing
auto-oriented uses could compromise the walkability and character that the zone was designed
to protect.However, following thoughtful discussion and strategic modifications to the ordinance
language, a consensus was reached. It was acknowledged that allowing drive-throughs, when
carefully designed and located, could expand the range of commercial opportunities without
undermining the zone’s pedestrian focus. The revised code was seen as a pragmatic response
to market interest, reflecting a willingness to adapt to attract new investment. By demonstrating
flexibility and responsiveness to the development community, the City hopes to stimulate new
projects on currently vacant or underutilized parcels, while still upholding the design principles
that make Historic Main Street a unique and welcoming destination.

W
Magna has experienced a noticeable increase in ADU building applications; however, applicants
rarely pursue the corresponding business license required for ADU rentals. In many cases,
applicants state that the unit is being rented to a family member, which exempts them from
licensing requirements under current regulations. While this practice is not inherently
problematic, it likely contributes to an underrepresentation of active ADU rentals within the
community.Additionally, both municipal staff and leadership believe there may be a significant
number of unreported or unpermitted ADUs operating in Magna. These assumptions are often
based on observations such as an unusually high number of parked vehicles at single-family
homes or perceptions regarding the number of occupants at a residence.In response, the City is
exploring the development of an ADU Amnesty Program. The goal of this program is to
encourage owners of unreported ADUs to voluntarily come into compliance with the City's
zoning and licensing requirements. By creating a supportive and non-punitive pathway to
legalization, the City hopes to improve the accuracy of ADU data, ensure safer housing
conditions, and reduce enforcement challenges related to occupancy and parking concerns.

The impetus for improvements to city and state infrastructure at the Cordero Drive intersection
and the surrounding corridors stems from two significant housing developments in the area:
Gateway to Little Valley and Mahogany Ridge. The Gateway to Little Valley development, led by
D.R. Horton and nearing completion, is a master-planned community featuring a mix of single-

trails, parks, pickleball and basketball courts, a playground, and a community pool with a

community, developed by Ivory Homes as part of a larger 800-unit master plan, has begun

affordable townhomes with two- and three-bedroom floor plans, alongside multiple

Magna City is currently processing its first development proposal within the newly established
Historic District Mixed-Use (DH) Zone, marking a significant milestone in the City’s efforts to
revitalize its historic core. The proposed project includes a coffee shop at the ground level, with
a drive-through window located at the rear of the building, accessed via a side street rather than

historic character and pedestrian-focused design.This project not only demonstrates the
success of recent zoning amendments, such as the addition of drive-through uses, but also
signals growing interest and investment in Magna’s historic district. It represents a meaningful
step toward the City’s broader goals of economic revitalization, housing diversity, and
placemaking in the downtown core.
