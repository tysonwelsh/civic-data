43
44   Chris Harding, Salt Lake County Auditor, explained an upcoming change to the County’s
45   property tax Notice of Valuation process. He shared that each property owner will receive a
46   notice in July, which will now feature a QR code linking to an enhanced electronic version of the
47   notice. This digital version will allow municipalities to add direct links explaining proposed tax
48   increases, giving residents clearer information about why increases are being proposed and
49   what they will fund. Additional improvements include an add-to-calendar feature for Truth in
50   Taxation hearings and a “pizza tracker”-style system for those appealing property valuations,
51   allowing them to receive real-time updates on the status of their appeal.

32            Ordinance No. 25-18 – “Priority Builders Rezone,” General Plan Amendment and
33            Rezone of approximately 4.28-acres located near the northwest corner of 11800
34            South and Redwood Road. Changing the General Plan Designation to High
35            Density Residential and the zoning to RM-8, a multi-family residential zone up to
36            8 dwelling units per acre
37
38   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that the applicant is requesting a
39   rezone and general plan amendment for approximately 4.25 acres located at the northwest
40   corner of 11800 South and Redwood Road. The site currently includes a mix of zoning: a
41   commercially zoned parcel that has not been developed, a half-acre residential (RR-22) zone,
42   and a recently rezoned R-4 parcel. Surrounding areas include RM-8 multifamily to the north and
43   west, and various residential zones to the south and east. The proposal is to rezone the entire
44   area to RM-8, with an SD (Special District) overlay to limit the development to townhomes only.
45   This meeting addressed zoning only—specific details like access, fencing, and layout would be
46   considered during a future site plan process. The Planning Commission recommended approval
47   with the townhome limitation, although the City code does not define the difference between
48   condos and townhomes. Staff supports the recommendation, but suggested additional
49   clarification from the applicant on how the townhome-only requirement would be interpreted.
50
51   Scott Yermish, Priority Builders, explained that the proposed project would be similar in style
     Page | 4 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1   and quality to Riverton Peaks, with slightly larger and upgraded units in three- and four-plex
 2   configurations. Regarding the townhome-only condition, he stated he was comfortable with it if it
 3   aligns with the legal designation used in Riverton Peaks, and clarified he is not proposing multi-
 4   story condo buildings. He also acknowledged prior concerns about flooding related to Midas
 5   Creek and committed to working with city engineers to address drainage issues.

15   Shelly Sieverts urged the Council to consider limiting the density of the project, noting that traffic

17   recent construction. She shared concerns about increased access through her side of the

28   the Council to carefully weigh how such changes would affect neighboring property owners and

31   Carol Sieverts said her primary concern was that the new project could restrict or block access
32   to her street, making it difficult for residents and emergency services like fire trucks or
33   ambulances to enter. She urged the Council to ensure that any new exits or road changes do
34   not interfere with access to her neighborhood.
35
36   Mayor Staggs called for additional public comments; there being none, he closed the Public
37   Hearing.

39   Mr. Yermish explained that, as part of the development process, an independent traffic impact
40   study would be conducted to assess how the proposed project would affect local traffic. This
41   study would include estimates of additional vehicles and recommend improvements, with results
42   reviewed by entities such as UDOT if necessary. He emphasized that his team is aware of the
43   community’s concerns and aims to be collaborative and said he is committed to addressing
44   traffic and other impacts responsibly and will take all feedback into account.
45
46   Councilmember Haymond expressed appreciation for the community’s understanding that
47   change is inevitable, even when it’s difficult. The main concern raised was the impact of any
48   development near Midas Creek on FEMA flood maps. He stressed the importance of
49   coordinating closely with the appropriate agencies to ensure that changes to the area do not
50   negatively affect downstream property owners—specifically, that no development should result
51   in others being required to purchase flood insurance due to altered floodplain boundaries.

 2   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, clarified that Salt Lake County—not FEMA—is responsible for
 3   regulating discharge rates into Midas Creek and Rose Creek. He explained that, due to the
 4   creeks being near capacity during a 100-year storm event, the County reduced the allowable
 5   discharge rate from 0.2 to 0.02 cubic feet per second within the last five or six years. This
 6   change means modern developments, including the proposed project, are now required to
 7   retain stormwater on-site rather than simply detain it, significantly reducing the potential impact
 8   on downstream properties.
 9
10   Councilmember Buroker expressed appreciation for the Seivert family's long history in Riverton
11   and acknowledged past efforts to address Midas Creek flood concerns. She stated her support
12   for the rezone, citing traffic safety on 11800 South, where multiple driveways already contribute
13   to congestion, and noted that consolidating access points could improve safety.
14
15   Councilmember Pierucci emphasized the importance of understanding stormwater impacts. Mr.
16   Lethbridge explained that discharge rates into the creek have been significantly reduced, and
17   stormwater management will be a major component of the site plan review, ensuring any runoff
18   is appropriately routed and does not adversely affect downstream properties.
19
20   Councilmember McDougal emphasized that the vote was solely on zoning and that much work
21   remains before development begins. While initially concerned about density, he appreciated the
22   developer’s willingness to reduce it and work with the City and Planning Commission. He
23   supported the development because it would create a more cohesive, managed area instead of
24   a patchwork of inconsistent growth. He concluded that the proposal will enhance Riverton’s
25   entrance, address current issues like trespassing, and ultimately create a well-integrated, safe,
26   and attractive community.
27
28   Councilmember McCay asked the developer to work with the adjacent HOA to gain access from
29   the west. She complimented the appearance of the existing townhomes in the area and voiced
30   support for the proposed development.
