       Assistance Compact ("EMAC") Fire Deployments, Staff Presenters, Fire Chief

Fire Chief, Warren James, presented the staff report and reviewed the prior request that Bluffdale
City provide support to Emergency Management Assistant Compact ("EMAC") fire deployments
to other states. A map from the National Mutual Aid Assistance System website listing the fires
currently being fought in the United States was displayed and reviewed. The Federal Government
has a step system in place to detem1ine how bad the fire need is around the nation. PL-5 is the
most severe, which means that the Federal Government is out of resources and money. This
usually occurs in October or November of each year, however, the government went to PL-5 the
previous week.

In Apri l, teams from the Fire Department were created to explore the potential of the above item.
One team analyzed the Type 6 engine to determine if it could be sent to an Incident. The second
team analyzed the abil ity to pay people. It was noted that all Bluffdale City Fire Fighters are part-
time with the exception of Chief James. The Affordable Care Act ("ACA") requirement limits
firefighters to an average of 30 hours per week. Engine types were identified.

Captains Jared Roberts and Bart Barton served as the team over the apparatus and indicated that
the vehicle is now prepared to leave. Captain Matt Evans, Alex Moyer, Carlos Lopez, and Nick
Percopis determined a way for the firefighters to deploy to California and still meet the ACA
requirement. Captain Evans owns the business, Wasatch Smokies, and it has been determined that
when ·the City's personnel are deployed to EMAC appointments, they will become employees of
Wasatch Smokies. Due to the firefighters being seasonal employees, they can work full -ti me for
up to six months, the City does not need to pay their benefits, and their hours will not be impacted.
The City's vehicle, as well as Wasatch Smokies' Type 6 Engine, will both be available for
deployment.

Deployment is typi cally 16 days and it is difficult to determine how much the City will earn due
to individually negotiated EMJ\C deployments. Chief James reviewed the negoti ation process and
indicated that any request can be rejected. During deployment, the Type 6 Engine is reimbursed
at $0.55 per mile to the Incident location. Once the vehicl e arrives, the minimum reimbursement
is $120 an hour. The firefighters wil l be paid $25 to $35 per hour depending on their experience
and qualifications.
