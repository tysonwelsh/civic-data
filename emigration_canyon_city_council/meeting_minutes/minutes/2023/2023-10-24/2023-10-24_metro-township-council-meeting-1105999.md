WORKSHOP MEETING
Unified Police Department Reorganization
Chief Jason Mazuran, Unified Police Department (UPD), stated H.B. 374 had a
significant impact on UPD and the Sheriff’s Office. The bill removed the Sheriff as UPD’s CEO,
necessitating the reevaluation of statutory functions. The reorganization relies on solid proven
principles and shared services for efficiency. The Metro Township had the opportunity to decide
what it wanted to see in its law enforcement. Entities could contract with another municipality or
self-provide. The UPD model is effective and allows some self-governance and a seat at the table.
With a contract model, the entity often does not have a voice.
Council Member Brems stated if the Council choose to stay with UPD, it would
be a member as it currently is. If it chooses to retain services from the Sheriff’s Office, Emigration
Canyon would not have a seat at the table because the Sheriff answers to the Salt Lake County
Council.
Council Member Harris asked what the reorganization is for the canyons and if
the different equipment is factored into the split.
Chief Mazuran stated canyon patrol will be under the Sheriff’s Office. UPD and
the Sheriff’s Office will continue to work together.
Lieutenant April Morse stated there would be different chiefs with the
reorganization. She has compiled a list of equipment that will need to be installed in canyon patrol
vehicles.

(UFSA), stated UFSA is a special district that serves as the taxing entity that collects taxes that
are used to pay member fees to Unified Fire Authority (UFA). She delivered a PowerPoint

Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area
Rachel Anderson, Legal Counsel, Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service
(SLVLESA), stated she is seeking approval of a resolution approving a 2024 tax rate in excess of
the certified tax rate. With the passing of H.B. 374, requiring the separation of the Sheriff’s Office
from Unified Police Department (UPD), has created a lot of uncertainty. The separation itself will
affect SLVLESA because there is a loss of economy of scale. SLVLESA is getting close to the
tax cap. For the legislature to increase the tax cap or get funding elsewhere, the current tax cap
needs to be reached.
Richard Moon, Treasurer, Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area
(SLVLESA), delivered a PowerPoint presentation regarding the tax increase, purpose of the tax
increase, 2021-2024 SLVLESA history, and FY 2022 UPD budget for SLVLESA.

WHEREAS, the Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (“SLVLESA”) was
created by Resolution of the Salt Lake County Council dated August 18, 2009; and

WHEREAS, the area of the Emigration Canyon Metro Township is included in the
boundaries of SLVLESA for the funding and provision of law enforcement services to the areas
within the boundaries of SLVLESA; and
WHEREAS, SLVLESA has proposed to budget a 7% increase in the tax rate in excess of
the certified rate as defined in Section 59-2-024 of the Utah Code in 2024 to fund the increased
costs of providing local law enforcement services and has proposed to increase property taxes
solely for the purposes of maintaining its fund balance, paying increases in the costs assessed to
SLVLESA by the Unified Police Department and, as needed, to other providers such as a newly
reinstituted Salt Lake County Sherriff’s Bureau, and allowing new growth in its tax base to be used
for the provision of required new services; and
WHEREAS, pursuant to Section 17B-1-1003 of the Utah Code, SLVLESA submitted a
report on the proposed tax increase to the Emigration Canyon Metro Township Council (“Council”)
at a duly noticed meeting on October 24, 2023, and
WHEREAS, the Council allowed time during the duly noticed meeting on October 24,
2023, for comment on the proposed tax increase from members of the Council and the public;
and
WHEREAS, Section 17B-2a-903 of the Utah Code requires the prior approval of an
increase in the certified rate by SLVLESA from the legislative bodies of any municipality and
county whose territory is located within SLVLESA, or approval of a majority of the municipalities
and two-thirds (2/3) of the County legislative body; and
WHEREAS, before the SLVLESA Board of Trustees considers to levy a tax rate that
exceeds the certified tax rate, the SLVLESA Board of Trustees shall hold a public hearing to
provide to all interested parties the opportunity to be heard regarding the proposed tax rate
increase; and
WHEREAS, SLVLESA shall provide notices of the public hearing as required by Section
59-2-919 of the Utah Code; and
WHEREAS, after considering all public input from the public hearing and all other
information available to them, the SLVLESA Board of Trustees will vote on whether to levy a tax
rate that exceeds the certified tax rate.
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE EMIGRATION CANYON METRO
TOWNSHIP COUNCIL as follows:
1.

The Emigration Canyon Metro Township Council hereby approves for 2024 the
budgeting of a 7% increase in property tax revenues in excess of revenues
budgeted in the prior year and the imposition of property taxes in excess of the
certified tax rate to generate the increase in budgeted property tax revenues as
defined in Section 59-2-924 of the Utah Code.

APPROVED BY THE COUNCIL in Emigration Canyon, Salt Lake County, Utah this 24th
day of October 2023.

Emigration Canyon Improvement District Request for an Exclusion or Modification of the Current
Road Moratorium Ordinance
Jeremy Cook, Legal Counsel, Emigration Improvement District (EID), stated the
EID operates in the canyon and has the same tax base as the Emigration Canyon Metro
Township. After the Metro Township imposed a moratorium on the roads, it has increased costs
that the EID incurs anytime it needs to make improvements and cut the road. Pre-moratorium,
the cost to finish a project was $2,500 every time a patch was needed. Those costs are now
$10,000 - $60,000 per patch. Depending on the number of patches that are needed in a year, it
uses up a significant portion of the EID’s budget. He is asking the Council to consider an exception
and allow the EID not to comply with the moratorium.
Mayor Smolka, seconded by Council Member Brems, moved to direct Jeremy
Cook and Cameron Platt to work on drafting an agreement that would allow the EID to be exempt
from the moratorium and go back to previous standards of two feet around the opening. The
motion passed unanimously.
