2. PUBLIC COMMENT
Mr. Floyd Mori  spoke in support of the revitalization of Japantown, which has been a 
very important element of his life.  Growning up, his family used to visit Japantown 
frequently.  During World War II, there were many Japantowns in the United States, but 
there are not many left.  Many Japanese Americans came to Utah after being incarcerated 
and having nowhere else to go.  After the war, Salt Lake City was a haven for many 
Japanese Americans.  
Ms. Metra Barton  spoke in support of the revitalization of Japantown.  Her mother lived 
there growing up, and her grandfather, after serving in World War II, had the address of 
Japantown listed on his discharge papers.  She would like to be able to introduce the 
community to her daughter.    
Mr. William Pruitt  stated he was the former arborist for the County, and he has 
complained to the Council numerous times about how the County cares for its trees.  He 
stated these costly assets were being seriously mismanaged and trees were dying at a 
significant rate as a result.  Mr. Pruitt implored the Council to do three things:  initiate an 
unbiased tree preservation board; instruct the board that the primary objective should be 
to create an ordinance pertaining to the care of County -owned trees; and hire a forester to 
oversee the care of the trees.

wanted people to know that all the ethnic groups who helped to build Utah ’s 
infrastructure should be remembered.  In this way, mistakes from the past 
would not be repeated.

RESOLUTION NO. 6240
A RESOLUTION OF THE SALT LAKE COUNTY COUNCIL 
SUPPORTING PRESENTLY EXISTING AGREEMENTS TO 
PRESERVE HISTORIC JAPANTOWN IN SALT LAKE CITY
WHEREAS, First South between 200 West and 300 West in Salt Lake City, 
renamed Japantown Street, is the location of The Japanese Church of Christ 
and The Salt Lake Buddhist Temple; and
 
WHEREAS, this area represents the last two vestiges of what was once a 
vibrant and thriving Japanese and Japanese -American business and cultural 
community; and
WHEREAS, in 1967 over 90 structures and businesses owned and /or 
operated by Americans of Japanese descent were mostly taken by eminent 
domain as part of the Urban Renewal project to build the Salt Palace, the 
Utah Museum of Contemporary Art; and
WHEREAS a series of expansion projects have further negatively impacted 
the area occupied by Japantown; and
WHEREAS, the Japanese Community Preservation Committee was created 
in 2000 to protect, and preserve, restore and promote the cultural community 
and legacy of this area; and
WHEREAS, a garden was created as a memorial to the history and culture of 
the area; and
WHEREAS, a Memorandum of Understanding exists between Salt Lake City 
and Salt Lake County for support of and access to cultural events in the area; 
and
WHEREAS, Senate Bill 272 passed in the General Session of the Utah State 
Legislature, and agreements being drafted between Salt Lake City and Smith 
Entertainment Group and other stakeholders will lead to major public and 
Page 8 of 33
County Council Meeting Minutes September 10, 2024
private changes and investment in the area;
NOW THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the Salt Lake County Council: 
1) That the Council supports past agreements regarding the use and support 
of the area of Japantown. 
2) That the Council supports the preservation and maintenance agreements 
regarding the care and upkeep of the garden. 
3) That the Council supports upholding the Memorandum of Understanding 
for shared parking and support of cultural and religious festivals held at 
the respective churches and communities in Japantown. 
APPROVED and ADOPTED this 10th day of September, 2024.
