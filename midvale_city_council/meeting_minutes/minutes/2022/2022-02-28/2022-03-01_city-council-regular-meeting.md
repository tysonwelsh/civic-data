City Code
2.12.020 Composition.
The city council shall be composed of six members, which shall be the mayor and five
city council members
2.12.030 Council members to be elected from districts.
A.    Council members shall be elected from districts substantially, as possible, equal in
population. To be elected from a district, the council member must be a qualified elector
residing in the dlstrict. To vote for the district candidate, the voter must be a resident of
the district in which the candidate resides.
D.    The legislative districts of the city are as provided on a map duly approved by the
council. The districts shall be enlarged by subsequent contiguous city annexations and
shall be reapportioned following each federal decennial census to maintain substantially
equal populations. A current copy of all such maps shall be on file with the city recorder
and available for public inspection.

    A. DISCUSS INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT WITH SANDY CITY FOR
             PAVEMENT WORK ON UNION PARK AVENUE
Keith Ludwig said Sandy City is planning on a mill and overlay project along Union Park
Avenue/1300 East for this summer. The project will start at South Union Ave/Creek Road
and run south. Several hundred feet of the west half of Union Park Avenue to the south
of South Union Avenue is within the boundaries of Midvale City.

Sandy City approached us and asked if we would like to participate with them in this
project and include our portion of Union Park Avenue. After reviewing the information that
I received, including the unit prices and cost estimate, and receiving input from senior
staff members, I accepted Sandy's offer. This project will help us as that section of Union
Park Avenue is in need of some work, and this way we can use the efforts of Sandy City
to get it done. lt also allows us to take advantage of lower unit prices associated with a
bigger project.

This project will be advertised for bid as soon as possible. We will be involved in the
bidding process and will be providing input.

Sandy City has prepared and interlocal agreement for us to participate with them in this
project. This agreement has been reviewed and approved by our legal department.

      B. DISCUSS MIDVALE HARVEST DAYS 2022
Laura Magness said events during the week of Harvest Days include block parties,
Midvale Arts Council Hall of Honors, Bingo Night, Friday Night Concert, Harvest Days

      C. DISCUSS BIKE LANE PARKING ISSUES
Matt Dahl said City Council members have expressed an interest in discussing city
policies regarding motor vehicles parking in bike lanes. Residents have observed cars
and trailers parked in bike lanes and have expressed concerns about the safety and
efficacy of this practice. Staff has conducted research on this issue in order to facilitate
the discussion by the City Council.

Bike lanes exist in many forms. The type and design of a bike lane is determined by a
number of factors including traffic patterns, road cross sections, available right-of-way,
safety concerns, and available funding. The safest and most costly type of bike lanes
include protected bike lanes and separated bike paths. ln these cases, the bike path is
physically separated from automobile traffic and parking. Many communities have opted
to support the development of an active transportation network through less costly
methods, such as using signage and striping to designate areas of a roadway where
bicycles should have priority but aren't separated physically from automobile traffic. ln
between these two approaches are a variety of hybrid options, including situations where
one bike lane changes from one form to another throughout its length. Policies dictating
how bike lanes are used, including how bikes and automobiles interact, should take into
consideration the type, design, and location of current and planned bike infrastructure, as
well as the overall transportation network of the city. Midvale City is in the process of
preparing different plans for the implementation of an active transportation network.

These planning efforts will provide the City Council with the ability to determine how
Midvale's active transportation network will be designed and operated. New policies will
be needed to support these decisions.

ln preparation for the policy discussion regarding parking in bike lanes, City staff reviewed
the bike lane policies of the State of Utah, Midvale, West Jordan, Murray, Cottonwood
Heights, Sandy, Salt Lake City, and Provo. The Utah Drivers Handbook indicates that a
driver should not stop or park in a bike lane, however there is no specific prohibition of
these actions in State Code. Midvale does not have an ordinance that specifically
prohibits stopping or parking in bike lanes. However, Midvale does have the ability to
prohibit parking along its roads as necessary. Of the Cities that we consulted; four cities
have enacted ordinances that prohibit parking in bike lanes. As an example, Salt Lake
City Code 12.52.140

BICYCLE LANES: VEHICLE RESTRICTIONS, says, "No motor vehicle shall at any time
be driven within or through, or parked or stopped within a marked bicycle lane, except
briefly when turning into an intersection, street, alley, driveway, or other parking area. A
motorbus or school bus may briefly drive within or through or stop within a marked bicycle
lane while in the process of taking on or discharging passengers but, when doing so, said
vehicles shall stop as near as practicable to the right-hand edge of the roadway. Any
vehicle entering or crossing a marked bicycle lane shall yield the right of way to all bicycles
within the lane that are close enough to constitute an immediate hazard. (Ord. 14-09 g 1,

Midvale's bike lane infrastructure is primarily comprised of striped bike lanes that are
located on or near the shoulder of the street. The exceptions to this are the separated
bike paths, such as the Jordan River Trail. lf Midvale were to consider a policy that
prohibits parking in bike lanes several issues must be considered:

. Would a ban on parking in bike lanes increase overall safety on Midvale streets?
. Would a ban on parking in bike lanes lead to more residents riding bikes in Midvale?
.Would it contribute to more people using an active transportation network in the future?
. How would a ban on parking in bike lanes impact the neighborhoods that current and
future bike lanes pass through? Would the reduction in parking be problematic for these
neighborhoods?
. Should there be different policies for different types of bike lanes?
.ls any additional investment required to support a policy banning parking in bike lanes?
.Will a communication campaign, signage, new striping, or other infrastructure be
needed?
. ls now the time to implement the policy? Would it be appropriate to wait for additional
infrastructure to be built?
. Are Midvale's bike lanes designed in a manner that makes sense to be paired with a
parking prohibition.
