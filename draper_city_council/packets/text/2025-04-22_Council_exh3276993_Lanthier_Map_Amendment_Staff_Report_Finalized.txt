BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY

This application is a request for approval of a Land Use Map and Zoning Map Amendment
for approximately 1.46 acres located at 13637 S 300 E and 13651 S 300 E (Exhibits C & D).
The property is currently designated as Residential Low – Medium Density in the General
Plan and is zoned RA1 and RA2. The applicant is requesting that the Land Use Map and
Zoning Map Amendments be approved to allow for future subdivision of the property.

The properties have historically been used for residential, with homes on both parcels.
According to County records, the home on the southern parcel was built in 1947 and the
home on the northern parcel was built in 1978.

Zoning Map Amendment.

The applicant is requesting to rezone the property from RA1 and RA2 to RM1 (Exhibit H).
According to DCMC Section 9-8-020, the purpose of the RM1 zone is to “permit well designed
apartments, townhomes, twin homes, and condominiums at relatively high densities that are
appropriately buffered from and compatible with surrounding land uses.”

Some key differences in the uses allowed in the RM1 zone as opposed to the current zoning
designations are:

    1. The RM1 zone does not allow uses such as agricultural businesses or animal
       specialties which are allowed in the RA1 zone;
    2. The RM1 zone also does not allow animal breeding or the keeping of animals and fowl
       for recreation and family food production which are allowed uses in the current RA1
       and RA2 zones;
    3. The RM1 zone does not allow for apiaries which are allowed in the current RA1 and
       RA2 zones; and
    4. The RM1 zone would permit two-family and multiple-family dwellings which are not
       permitted under the current RA1 and RA2 zones.

Allowed uses in the current RA1 and RA2 zoning that would remain allowed under the
proposed RM1 zoning include but are not limited to:

    1.   Agricultural use;
    2.   Single-family homes;
    3.   The keeping of chickens for familial gain;
    4.   Limited Daycare and Preschool operations; and
    5.   All currently allowed public, civic and municipal uses.

A copy of the complete land use table for residential zones is included as Exhibit K.

The applicant’s responses to the Zoning Map Amendment application questions are
provided in Exhibit J. In response to the question as to how the proposed amendment is
consistent with the goals, objective and policies of the City General Plan the response was
that the application was going to provide more housing for the community. The closest goal,
policy or objective in the General Plan that staff could identify related to the increased
quantity of housing was Goal HN-1 which encourages housing diversity and intensity along
major transit investment corridors. This property is not located on a major transit investment
corridor.

If approved, the Zoning Map Amendment would allow the property owner to submit an
application to subdivide the property into smaller lots. At this time, the applicant has
presented a concept plan to staff proposing the two (2) parcels be subdivided into seven (7)

Zoning Map Amendment
A Map Amendment is a matter committed to the legislative discretion of the City Council and
is not controlled by any one standard. However, in making a recommendation to the City
Council, the Planning Commission should consider the following factors in Section 9-5-060(E)
of the DCMC:

        1. Map Amendments:
            a. Whether the proposed amendment is consistent with goals, objectives and policies
               of the city's general plan;
            b. Whether the proposed amendment is harmonious with the overall character of
               existing development in the vicinity of the subject property;
            c. Whether the proposed amendment is consistent with the standards of any
               applicable overlay zone;
            d. The extent to which the proposed amendment may adversely affect adjacent
               property; and
            e. The adequacy of facilities and services intended to serve the subject property,
               including, but not limited to, roadways, parks and recreation facilities, police and
               fire protection, schools, stormwater drainage systems, water supplies, and
               wastewater and refuse collection.
