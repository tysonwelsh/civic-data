CONSIDERATION OF CANVASS OF JUNE 28, 2022 PRIMARY ELECTION RECOUNT

Clerk/Auditor Susan Lewis explained that because she lives in and voted in District 72 she recused
herself from being involved in the recount. Washington County has never had a recount requested
in a primary or general election. Because the ballots were not sorted by district prior to knowing a
recount would be requested, every ballot received had to be recounted.

In order to maintain accuracy and transparency, members of the public were invited to view the
audit process after the completion of the recount. The Audit process was overseen by the
Lieutenant Governor’s office, wherein, 1000 random ballots were selected to be ran through the
machine to have the vote verified.

There were four (4) ballots that were included in the recount final total due to adjudication made
with signature verification, post marks, and voter intent.

For House District 72, Joseph Elison received 4,134 votes and Willie Billings received 4,124 votes.
Joseph Elison will move on to the general election.

The Commissioners thanked the Clerk/Auditor’s office for their diligent work during the primary
election.

Elections Supervisor Melanie Abplanalp stated that there would be processes implemented moving
forward to ensure continued accuracy with elections.

Candidate Willie Billings expressed concern with the count of the audit ballots mentioning that he
had significantly more votes than Mr. Elison within the random 1000 that were audited and that it
did not make sense statistically.

Ms. Lewis clarified that a full recount of all 34,734 ballots was performed and those that were
audited were only to ensure the accuracy of the machine counting each vote and did not reflect the

final result total.

Deputy County Attorney Devin Snow clarified that under State Code, the results must be
canvassed as scheduled unless there is evidence of a clear mistake determined by the Election

Officer.

CONSIDERATION OF ORDINANCE NO. 2022-1213-O, AN ORDINANCE ADDING THE
WASHINGTON COUNTY CRIMINAL JUSTICE COORDINATING COUNCIL TO THE

COUNTY CODE

Deputy Attorney Snow explained that the Utah Legislature mandated that all counties participate
in criminal justice coordinating councils. The Washington County Attorney has coordinated a
Washington County Criminal Justice Coordinating Council as a group made up of various criminal
justice stakeholders that meets and discusses reforms and ways to improve the criminal justice
system in Washington County. This ordinance would formalize that council in the county code.

MOTION: Motion by Commissioner Almquist to Adopt Ordinance No. 2022-1213-O, An
Ordinance Adding the Washington County Criminal Justice Coordinating
Council to the County Code. Motion seconded by Commissioner Snow and
carried by unanimous vote, with Commissioners Iverson, Snow and Almquist

voting aye.

CONSIDERATION OF RESOLUTION NO. R-2022-2977, A RESOLUTION APPROVING
A COMMISSION AGREEMENT WITH CUSHMAN AND WAKEFIELD

Deputy Attorney Snow explained that the General Services Administration (GSA) issued a
Request for Lease Proposal (RLP) to lease space that would be used by the United State District
