Justice Court of the Year Award 2023 – Mayor Daines

Mayor Daines announced that Judge Lee Edwards and the Logan Justice Court were
recently presented with the Justice Court of the Year Award for 2023. Mayor Daines read
the following citation:

“The Clerks at the Logan Justice Court, under the supervision of Heather Steinfeld, have
successfully completed all the necessary training for Clerk Certification for 2022. The
court operated for a portion of 2022 with only 3 clerks, and throughout all the changes
Logan has maintained high percentages on their time to disposition: In speaking with
Judge Edwards, he believes highly trained clerks provide better customer service and
promote confidence in the Logan Justice Court. Their attention to detail, commitment to
timeliness, hard work, and outstanding service of all the Logan Justice Court clerks can
provide is worthy of recognition as the Justice Court of the Year for the State of Utah.”

Judge Lee Edwards expressed his gratitude on behalf of the staff. He said these days
people seem to be angry at the government, the police, family members, and everything.
When things are not going well in people's lives, that is when they end up with charges
and going to Court. He and the court clerks are the representatives of the Judicial System
that most members of the community will meet. That is why it is so important they are
professional, fair, and impartial. He believes that everyone in this room understands that
this award is because of the hard work of the clerks of the court.

Judge Edwards continued and said the Logan Justice Court has five clerks. The job is
much easier when you understand the process and have correct answers to provide to
citizens when they are handling cases. And more importantly, recognize the importance
of police security.

The justice clerks receive all sorts of calls from how to pay for a ticket, to where the court
is located, or to small claims court over a dog. Overall Judge Lee Edwards expressed his
appreciation for all that the justice clerks do.

City Floodwatch Preparations – Paul Lindhardt, Public Works Director

Paul Lindhardt, Public Works Director reported that previously Tony Grove had received
135% of seasonal snow. At present as of a few hours ago, Tony Grove is at 159%. The
Bear River is at 172% capacity. (Ironically the lowest in the State). The Southwest region
of the State is at 331%

As of two weeks ago, snowplows have been dispatched five times (to deal with snow).
Numbers are not yet in for today and last night, but with the most recent storm, we will
have received 100 inches of snowfall.

Sandbags are still available at the Public Works Service Center. Each household may
have up to 10 (self-filled) sandbags at the Public Works Service Center (only for
residents of Logan) at no extra cost. The plan is to also go to certain neighborhoods and
set up stations while the public provides the labor to fill the sandbags.

The traditional spring cleanup provided to residents of Logan will not be provided this
year. Due to the unprecedented winter season and the concern for potential flooding, the
public works crews are spending all of their time moving snow and preparing waterways
and streets for the runoff that is sure to come. In place of the curbside spring cleanup,
there will be green waste designated locations on specific dates which include the
following city parking lots: Lundstrom Park, Hyrum Gibbons Mt Logan Park, Merlin
Olsen Park (east), and Bridger Park.

The community can help by clearing out the storm drain or curb. If pumps are used,
please do not connect them to storm or sanitary water. It can quickly overwhelm the
system.

Councilmember Jensen inquired if the ponds are still available for use (belonging to the
sanitary system).

Mr. Hamud confirmed the ponds can still be used for stormwater management.

Chairman López asked what areas of concern are there in regard to flooding.

Mr. Lindhardt answered the area of concentration is on the Logan River corridor.

Councilmember Simmonds inquired if there is a set point at which a canal diversion will
open to offset the current.

Mr. Lindhart responded there is not a set point. The canal may offset some, but the point
is not to overwhelm their systems either. It will have to be a coordinated effort.
