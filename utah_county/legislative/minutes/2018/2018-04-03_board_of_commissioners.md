2.     APPROVE PROPERTY TAX ACTIONS CONTAINED IN RECOMMENDATION LETTER NO.
       2018-5
(Track A, 21:00)
Commissioner Lee mentioned he met with George King, who had submitted an application for the settlement or
deferral of delinquent property tax. Commissioner Lee explained the wrong address was recorded on the warranty
deeds for Mr. King, and while there is no county error Commissioner Lee does not want to punish the citizen for a
simple mistake. Utah County Deputy Attorney Paul Jones noted the Board has discretion in approving or denying
these applications but the address is completely within the taxpayer’s control. Mr. Jones further noted the application
is for five different rental properties which had been deeded from the applicant to the applicant (from himself to
himself) as the owner of a trust. The Attorney’s Office has been consistent in this issue by determining mailing
addresses are the responsibility of the taxpayer. When Mr. Jones brought up an ethical question regarding the applicant
being a county delegate, Commissioner Lee took offense and asked Mr. Jones whether he is assuming Mr. King is
being malicious. Utah County Clerk/Auditor Bryan E. Thompson mentioned he forwarded an email to the
Commission Office from Mr. King regarding his application, but it was irrelevant to his status as county delegate.
Commissioner Lee stated Mr. Jones is making implications and Mr. Jones replied he is only bringing it to the attention
of the Board. Commissioner Lee clarified he will potentially be re-elected to public office this year.

(Track A, 31:25)
Utah County Recorder Jeff Smith concurred with Paul Jones regarding addresses being the responsibility of the
taxpayer. Mr. Smith explained the county relies heavily on that information and changing the process for this applicant
will set a precedent for future applications. He stated the commissioners should not harshly judge county employees;
they go above and beyond in assisting citizens. Mr. Jones mentioned there are two other applicants within this same
letter which have received the same recommendation from staff as George King: deny the request as the mailing
address is the responsibility of the taxpayer. Mr. Jones volunteered to further review those two applications should it
be the Board’s direction.
