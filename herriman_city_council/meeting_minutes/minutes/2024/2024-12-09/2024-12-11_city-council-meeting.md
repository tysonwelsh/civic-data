    9.1. Discussion and Consideration of a resolution approving a petition to create one
         or more Public Infrastructure Districts and authorizing the execution of the Public
         Infrastructure District governing document for the Panorama Development –
         Blake Thomas, Community Development Director
Community Development Director Thomas presented item 9.1 and 9.2 simultaneously. He
oriented the Council to the location of the project and noted they were discussed during the
previous City Council meeting. Director Thomas noted that initially, a letter of intent was
submitted by the developer, which was evaluated for compliance with the PID policy. Upon
confirming compliance, the Community Development Committee reviewed the letter of
intent and assessed whether it met the requisite standards to proceed further. The
Committee endorsed moving forward, with a petition subsequently submitted to the Council,
accompanied by the governing document. During the previous work session, the governing
document was assessed against the PID policy to ensure compliance and identify any
deviations.

Director Thomas looked to address fundamental inquiries to clarify what a PID entailed: it
served as a versatile tool employed by developers to secure funding. This mechanism would
facilitate the prompt construction of infrastructure, as opposed to the delayed build-out often
observed. Public Infrastructure Districts are sanctioned by state legislation, with all
improvements financed by the PID required to be public. Ultimately, the decision to approve
rests with the Council, which holds sole discretion within the parameters defined by state
code. The regions in question encompass two districts, visually represented with the
Panorama and the Soleil Hills. According to the governing documents for both districts, a
maximum mill levy of 0.5 mills was established, coinciding with the intended construction of
$55 million worth of infrastructure improvements.

    11.3. Consideration of a Sales Tax Incentive Agreement with Elevated Acquisitions,
          LLC to facilitate infrastructure and site improvements associated with The
          Commons at Herriman Towne Center Mixed-Use Development Project – Blake
          Thomas, Community Development Director
Councilmember Henderson started the discussion on sales tax incentives, which was 50% for
20 years or until the total amount is reached. He expressed his concern about an unlimited
time frame. The Council thoroughly deliberated the sales tax incentive agreement's
provisions, primarily concentrating on the duration and the specific percentage split of
revenues between the city and the developer. After extensive negotiations and
considerations, they collectively arrived at a structured agreement reflective of both parties'
interests. The resolution entailed a mutual decision to implement an equitable 50/50 split of
any sales tax revenues generated by the project for the initial 20-year term of the agreement.
This agreement ensured that both the City and the developer would share equally in the
future financial successes stemming from the project's potential retail operations.

As the discussions progressed, the Council emphasized the importance of sustaining a
balanced approach beyond the initial two-decade framework to encourage ongoing project
development. As a result, the Council decided on a revised allocation for the subsequent five-
year period (years 21 through 25). During this phase, a 65/35 division was established,
favoring the City, thereby enhancing its revenue share while still providing the developer a
significant portion. This adjustment sought to motivate the early realization of project
potentials while aligning the developer's incentives with the city's long-term infrastructure
and financial planning objectives.
December 11, 2024 City Council Meeting Minutes                                Page 15 of 18


The Council further addressed the scenario extending beyond the 25th year, wherein the
incentive structure would necessitate a gradual reduction in the developer's share.
Therefore, for any additional years required to reach the maximum agreed-upon incentive
amount, the revenue split was to shift to a 90/10 allocation model. The City share would
increase, ensuring an expanded economic benefit for the community at large as the project
matured over the extended duration.

    11.5. Consideration for adoption of a Resolution authorizing the issuance and sale of
          not more than $20,000,000 aggregate principal amount of Sales & Franchise Tax
          Revenue Bonds; and related matters – Kyle Maurer, Director of Finance and
          Administrative Services
Finance Director Maurer presented information on the proposed bond issuance. He
explained that the bonds would fund various infrastructure projects arising from master
development agreements and reimbursement agreements that the City was contractually
obligated to complete. Director Maurer noted they propose to split the projects into two
bonds, with the first bond expected to be around $17 million. During the Council discussion,
the parameters of the bond issuance were addressed and modifications were made regarding
the designated officers who can execute certain functions related to selling the bonds.
Council consensus determined to restrict this authority to the Mayor, City Manager and
Finance Director.

Councilmember Shields moved to approve Resolution No R47-2024 authorizing the issuance
and sale of not more than $20,000,000 aggregate principal amount of sales and franchise
tax revenue bonds, series 2025 (the “series 2025 bonds”); fixing the maximum aggregate
principal amount of the series 2025 bonds, the maximum number of years over which the
series 2025 bonds may mature, the maximum interest rate which the series 2025 bonds may
bear, and the maximum discount from par at which the series 2025 bonds may be sold;
delegating to certain officers of the city the authority to approve the final terms and
provisions of the series 2025 bonds within the parameters set forth herein; authorizing the
