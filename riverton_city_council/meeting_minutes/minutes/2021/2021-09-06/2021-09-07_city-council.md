         Cemetery Rules and Regulations – Ryan Carter, City Attorney

Ryan Carter, City Attorney, explained that there have been some unevenly applied rules in the
cemetery. He said that there has been concern about how many headstones can be placed on
one burial plot. He said that in the new section the burial plots can be double stacked and the
names of both deceased persons buried there would be on one headstone. He said that the
rules and regulations state that an infant can be placed in a burial plot and an additional flat
headstone can be installed for the infant. He said there are times when there is no infant
involved and the family may want to do one upright headstone for person A and one flat
headstone for person B. He said that the Attorney’s Office and City Recorder’s Office has
determined that families should be able to have one upright headstone and one flat headstone
regardless of whether it is an infant or another person buried in the same burial plot. He said he
wanted to know if the Council had any problem with this decision conceptually.

Councilmember Stewart asked if the family could choose to do two flat headstones instead of
one upright and one flat headstone. Mr. Carter said that would be fine.

Councilmembers did not express concern over this new policy and Mr. Carter said he would
make the appropriate changes to the Cemetery Rules and Regulations.

         Ordinance No. 21-23 – Text Amendment, Proposing Changes to Section 18.25.110,
         Farm Animals, Regarding Residential Chickens in the RR-22 Zone, Amendments
         Proposed by Riverton City

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that this a public hearing for a
Zoning Text Change proposed by Riverton City. He said this amendment clarifies allowance for
residential chickens in the RR-22 zone. He said the RR-22 zone carries farm animal rights,
including chickens. He said that in zones without farm animal rights, a limited number of
chickens are allowed, but no roosters. He said in the RR-22 zone, a minimum of .5 acres is
required to qualify for farm animal rights so the purpose of this amendment is to clarify for lots
below that threshold in the RR-22 zone, farm animal rights are not allowed but may have
residential chickens and are allowed to have a rooster. He said the typical impacts of a rooster
in a residential zone will be mitigated by the fact that roosters are allowed elsewhere in the RR-
22 zone.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments there being none, he closed the Public Hearing.
