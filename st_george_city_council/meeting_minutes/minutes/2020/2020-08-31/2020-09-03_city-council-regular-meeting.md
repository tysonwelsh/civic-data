OPENING:
     Mayor Pike called the meeting to order and welcomed all in attendance. The
     invocation was offered by Reverend Alex Wilkie and the Pledge of Allegiance to the
     Flag was led by Mayor Pike.

      A resident by the name of Austin stated for the past week or so, he has been on the
      side of the road sharing love and happiness. He is trying to be the change he would
      like to see. Residents by the name of Katie and Kim joined him. Together, they
      presented a poster they have been holding, sharing words of hope, to the City.
      Citizens can find humanity within ourselves, be kinder than we were yesterday, love
      a little more than we have before, and forgive those who have wronged us is the
      message that he would like to share.

      Mayor Pike read a proclamation proclaiming the week of September 17 – September
      as Constitution Week.

      Karen Shuman spoke about the Constitution, Constitution Week, and the Daughters
      of the American Resolution.

      Valerie King presented a plaque to the Mayor and Council thanking them for
      supporting the Daughters of the American Revolution.

CHANGE RESTRICTIONS/ORDINANCE:
     Consider an ordinance to allow a change to restrictions placed on the South
     Bridge (fka Boulder Creek Commons) Planned Development as it relates to
     drive-thru facilities on property located east of the intersection of River
     Road and 1450 South.

      Planner Dan Boles presented the request to allow a change to restrictions placed on
      the South Bridge (fka Boulder Creek Commons) Planned Development as it relates to
      drive-thru facilities on property located east of the intersection of River Road and
      1450 South. He provided a PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics:
      vicinity map; zoning map; approved conceptual site plan; drive-thru standards; and
      photos of other restaurants with a drive-thru in the area.
St. George City Council Minutes
September 3, 2020

      Councilmember McArthur commented that it is hard to design for more cars than the
      required stacking.

      After being asked by Councilmember McArthur, Mr. Boles explained the limit on drive
      thrus was specific to restaurants only and any changes approved tonight would apply
      only to this development.

      Mayor Pike commented that this project cannot have issues the other such as Fizz
      have had with regards to stacking.

      Councilmember Larkin noted it would be better to regulate the stacking at the
      Planned Development level than to have a specific number allowed.

      Councilmember McArthur agreed.

      Councilmember Hughes spoke about Dinosaur Crossing and the number of drive
      thrus they have. All of them do well with regards to stacking.

      Spencer Hymus, representative for the applicant, pointed out this project has an
      internal road system. None of the drive thrus will have a direct access from any of
      the roads. They too are concerned with cars backing onto the street. He feels they
      can work within the proposed standards; five is what they can design around with
      the size of the current pads. He asked for an option in the event of a need during
      the PD-C process to have some leeway that if they cannot meet the standards.

      MOTION:
          A motion was made by Councilmember McArthur to allow a change to
                    restrictions placed on the South Bridge (fka Boulder Creek Commons)
                    Planned Development as it relates to drive-thru facilities on property
                    located east of the intersection of River Road and 1450 South with
                    adjustment of a minimum of five (5) stacking.
      SECOND:
          The motion was seconded by Councilmember Hughes.
      VOTE:
          Mayor Pike called for a roll call vote, as follows:

                    Councilmember Hughes – aye
                    Councilmember Randall – aye
                    Councilmember Smethurst – aye
                    Councilmember McArthur – aye
                    Councilmember Larkin – aye

                    The vote was unanimous and the motion carried.

CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT:
     Consider approval of a Conditional Use Permit (CUP) to operate a bar
     establishment in the arts district overlay zone located at 61 West St. George
     Boulevard.

      Planner Genna Goodwin presented the request for a conditional use permit to
      operate a bar establishment in the arts district overlay zone at 61 West St. George
      Boulevard. She provided a PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics:
      arts district overlay zone; bar establishments in arts district; site plan; exterior
      façade; and floor plan.

      Applicants Cameron and Kelli Payne outlined their plan. They will serve local beers
      and wine, making this a local event, with a fun and upscale clientele. The communal
      seating is to for everyone to sit and chat together and make new friends. After
      being asked by Councilmember Randall Mr. Payne explained upscale bar food
      includes frechettas, upscale craft burgers, tacos, etc, similar to what they serve at
      Twisted Noodle. There will not be any patio seating.

      Councilmember McArthur inquired about the food also. He asked if it would be
      similar to Red Rock Brewery where he visits for the food and drinks root beer.
St. George City Council Minutes

      Mr. Payne replied yes, it will be a similar atmosphere. He commented they will serve
      Dave Moody’s root beer. They want this to be community friendly and fun to visit.

      Councilmember Randall asked about parking and mentioned the concerns from
      neighbors who are concerned with having several bars in the same area. The
      Council does want another bar such as the one down the street.

      Mr. Payne stated he does not want to be like that bar either. He explained that he
      and his wife travel a lot and go out and have a fun time in different areas; they want
      to bring a synergy downtown. As for the parking, there are parking garages in the
      downtown area. They want the bar to be an adult place; there are a number of
      family-oriented business in the area, but there is not a lot of places for adults to
      socialize.

      Councilmember Randall mentioned the Council approved the updated ordinance
      recently; however, she would like to revisit that as she believes the arts district is
      too small for six bars.

      Mayor Pike commented the Council could have that discussion/

      Mr. Payne noted operating hours would be noon – 10:00 p.m. on Sunday through
      Thursday and from noon – 12:00 p.m. on Friday and Saturday.

      Deputy City Attorney Paula Houston commented this is a CUP, this is already allowed
      in an ordinance. With a CUP, what needs to be looked at are the standards in City
      Code, such as parking and if it would create a negative impact on the neighborhood
      and is there any way to mitigate the negative impacts. Staff feels like it meets all
      requirements and that no special conditions that need to be imposed for this item.
      This request is no different than that of the similar business in the Advenire. would
      have a similar impact.

      Councilmember Smethurst stated he is concerned with the parking in the garage
      area. A number of tenants are having trouble finding a parking spot.

      Community Development Director John Willis explained all of the floors have an
      occupancy permit. The first two or three levels are for the businesses while the
      others are for tenants of the building.

      Councilmember McArthur commented that he heard from a number of residents.
      They are concerned with the amount of these types of establishments in the area.
      He would like to revisit the ordinance.

      Councilmember Hughes stated it is okay to take a step back and look at the overall
      community values. This isn’t a Utah thing, there are dry counties where you cannot
      purchase alcohol in other states. He commented that he does not think the Council
      has ever denied a local consent. The question is what is the Council striving to do
      with the downtown area. He asked if families are going to avoid the area because of
      a bar establishment and noted there are 700 deaths a year in Utah due to alcohol.
