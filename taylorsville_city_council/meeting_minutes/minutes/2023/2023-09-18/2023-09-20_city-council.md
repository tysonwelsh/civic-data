1.      WELCOME, INTRODUCTION AND PRELIMINARY MATTERS

Chair Knudsen called the meeting to order at 6:30 p.m. and welcomed those in attendance. A roll
call was conducted wherein all council members were present.

              1.1     Opening Ceremonies – Pledge/Reverence – Councilmember Cochran
              (Opening Ceremonies for October 4, 2023 to be arranged by the Youth Council

Councilmember Cochran directed the Pledge of Allegiance and commented on the recent
commemoration of the September 11th 2001 tragedy. He expressed his appreciation for the first
responders and others involved in both the rescue and the recovery. He also took a moment to
thank Taylorsville’s police officers and Unified Fire personnel for the work they did every day in
the community.

        1.2      Mayor’s Report

Mayor Kristie Overson explained that former Councilmember Bruce Wasden had recently passed
away. He had been instrumental in Taylorsville’s 1996 incorporation and later served the
community in a variety of capacities over the years.

The mayor talked about the recent ULCT conference. She said the workshops, classes and speakers
were all very worthwhile and relevant for today’s many challenges. She thanked the
councilmembers who attended with her along with John Hiskey.

     The City was involved in many volunteer projects to commemorate September 11th . It was a
very productive day, and she was impressed with the number of people who participated. She also
mentioned the residents who worked at the food pantry and the wonderful work they do for
Taylorsville’s citizens.

TVPD and UFA had recently joined the mayor in representing Taylorsville at the Senior Expo for
mobile home communities. She had enjoyed visiting with the residents of Monte Vista Mobile
Home Community to hear about their needs.

Mayor Overson mentioned her cabinet meeting which was held on a quarterly basis. She stated
the personnel in attendance were an incredible group of people who helped Taylorsville serve its
citizens. These people included representatives from Unified Fire, Salt Lake County Public Works,
Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District, West Valley Animal Services as well as department
heads from within the city.

There was a hearing before the administrative law judge held the previous week. The mayor was
struck by the amazing rapport that sometimes existed between code enforcement personnel and
residents as they worked on different issues. She also mentioned attending a 75th anniversary for
the Salt Lake Community College celebration. She was given a book documenting their 75 years
as a college.

     The last “Starry Nights” event for the summer was held the previous week. Mayor Overson
commented that it was a lot of hard work from all involved, but it had been a very successful series
of events.

The mayor showed Unified Fire’s budget book and explained that she sat on their governing board.
At a recent meeting, she and the other board members had the opportunity to visit UFA’s state-of-
the-art fire safety trailer which helped to educate the public at various events.

Mayor Overson brought up the recruiting process for this year’s youth council. Interviews were
held earlier in the day and final decisions would be made soon.

3.      REPORTS

        3.1      Unified Fire Authority – Captain Rich

             UFA Captain Richard Rich presented his report which included statistics pertaining
to call volume, dispatched calls, response times, etc. He stated the response times continued to
improve. Councilmember Cochran asked about the bay doors on a new station which open more
quickly than the traditional roll-up doors. Captain Rich responded that the new doors opened
approximately 30 seconds faster than the standard ones, although stations with roll-up doors
typically had them open in advance to avoid the delay.

Captain Rich then discussed fire prevention activities during the 2nd and 3rd quarters of 2023. These
activities included inspections, plan reviews, and the issuance of hazardous materials permits
among other things. In the future he hoped to be able to report on the number of hours spent on
each of these activities.

UFA had recently finished a SWOC analysis (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities and
Challenges) to help them build their strategic plan. The captain mentioned there would be another
strategic plan meeting coming up in October if the council members wished to participate. He
explained that the SWOC process informed their budget preparation process.

Captain Rich updated the council members on fire station construction in the valley. He also
provided information regarding ongoing recruitment efforts, and out-of-state deployments of the
Utah Task Force. He talked about movement throughout the UFA and mentioned there were quite
a few retirements and promotions and noted that the rank of paramedic was the position that
struggled the most with recruitment, hiring and retention.

Captain Rich mentioned there would be a presentation at the October 4th City Council meeting to
present information regarding a proposed tax increase by the Unified Fire Service Area.
Councilmember Burgess asked about the increase and Captain Rich stated he did not have the
specifics yet, but it appeared to be a fairly significant increase.

       3.2       Taylorsville-Bennion Improvement District – Mark Chalk

       General Manager Mark Chalk provided a brief overview of the infrastructure behind water
meters and how they worked to measure water usage. He explained that over time, meters broke
down, became less efficient and needed to be replaced. An AMI/Meter replacement project was
underway which involved updating to a new type of water meter with no moving parts. This would
lead to a longer life of the meter with a higher level of accuracy.

     The new AMI meters would relay information to the District every 15 minutes. They would
be able to identify stopped meters, those that had been tampered with, and those with errors. It
would provide better communication with customers, resulting in savings of both money and
water. Residents would receive an announcement hung on their front door when their meter had
been replaced.

Councilmember Cochran thanked Mr. Chalk for his excellent presentation and asked if he had any
predictions for the upcoming winter. Mr. Chalk stated forecasts showed it could be a similar winter
to last years, but it was too early to tell.

6.      FINANCIAL MATTERS

        6.1      Resolution No. 23-25 A Resolution of the City of Taylorsville Approving a Non-
                 Exclusive License Agreement Between the City of Taylorsville and Utopia for
                 the Installation of Internet Network Facilities within the City Public Right-of-
                 Way – Tracy Cowdell

        City Attorney Tracy Cowdell explained Utopia’s desire to install infrastructure for
commercial internet in Taylorsville. Utopia would post a $50,000 master bond with the City before
starting their project. Mr. Cowdell stated normally there was no charge for running fiber and
federal law did not have a franchise fee. Utopia would pay a 2% fee paid to the city on a quarterly
basis. The service would be strictly for schools and state offices—not private residences.

MOTION:          Councilmember Barbieri moved to approve Resolution No. 23-25, approving
