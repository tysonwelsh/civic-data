       3.1 Request: Recommendation of approval to amend Title 10 of the City's Land Use Code
              regarding digital signs. (Public Hearing)
           Applicant: Herriman City/Sheldon Howa, Planner II
           File: Z2020-059
Chair Berbert reviewed the public hearing guidelines.

Planner Howa presented the proposed amendments to revise requirements for electronic signs. The
current ordinance restricted them within five hundred feet of residential use. Proposed changes would
remove the five-hundred-foot separation requirement to zones that were perpendicular to the digital
sign display. Electronic signs parallel to residential zones would require one-hundred-fifty-foot
setbacks from the corresponding property line. Proposed dwell times would be a minimum of eight
seconds, with a transition time of not less than three seconds. Staff further recommended brightness
reduction from 0.5 to 0.3 candle-foot at the property line.

Staff proposed the following changes to Title 10 of the City ordinance:
 1. 10-27-11(B) Location:
    a. Electronic signs which are not part of a monument sign or an installation where any
portion of the electronic sign is six (6’) feet above finished grade, shall not be
permitted within in five hundred feet (500’) of a residential use zone.
    b. Electronic signs which are part of a monument sign or an installation less than six
March 3, 2022 Planning Commission Meeting Minutes                              Page 3 of 8
(6’) feet above finished grade, and the display is parallel to a residentially zoned
property, are not permitted within one-hundred-fifty (150’) of the corresponding
property line.
 2. 10-27-11(D) Malfunctions: Any electronic sign with electrical or lighting components
operating in an erratic, broken, or damaged fashion shall have a default mechanism to
turn off the sign within twenty-four (24) hours of a reported malfunction. be turned off or
removed, and shall be programmed to go dark in the event of a malfunction.
 3. 10-27-11(E) Brightness: All electronic signs shall not exceed 0.5 footcandle, as measured
in a straight line from the sign to the nearest lot line.
    a. Sign illumination levels for digital signs shall never, at maximum display
intensity, exceed 0.3 foot-candle over ambient lighting conditions when measured
at the distance based on the formula of the square root of the product of the area of the display
multiplied by one hundred (100) [ �𝐴𝐴𝐴𝐴𝐴𝐴𝐴𝐴 𝑜𝑜𝑜𝑜 𝑑𝑑𝑑𝑑𝑑𝑑𝑑𝑑𝑑𝑑𝑑𝑑𝑑𝑑 × 100].
    b. All permitted electronic signs shall be equipped with a sensor, or other device
that automatically determines the ambient illumination and is programmed to
automatically dim according to ambient light conditions or that can be adjusted to
comply with the 0.3 foot-candle measurements. The applicant shall submit a photometric matrix
(showing the dispersal in foot-candles) showing that the sign
meets all the requirements of this code.
 4. 10-27-11(F) Dwell Time and Transitions: The sign copy displayed on electronic signs
shall be static. The transition from one static display to another shall be instantaneous and
without any special effects.
    a. The inclusion of text or video that flash, scroll, appears to flash, or strobe is
prohibited.
    b. An image shall not change more frequently than once every eight (8) seconds.
        i. Transitions between images shall be no less than three (3) seconds. In
order to better regulate changing images a fade in and fade out transition
must be used. The fade in and fade out prevents a dark image from
transitioning abruptly to a light image and vice versa, thus creating a flash
or strobe-like effect.
 5. 10-27-11(G) Frequency Of Changes: An electronic sign on which sign copy changes
more than four (4) times per minute (once every 15 seconds) shall be deemed an
animated sign and are prohibited.

Chair Berbert questioned if 10-27-11B clearly stated the distance required from residential property

       3.4 Consideration of Approval to Amend Title 10 Pertaining to Home Occupations (Public
               Hearing)
        Applicant: Herriman City
        File Number: Z2021-121
Planner Talia Wright provided an overview and history of the amendments to title 10 pertaining to
Home Occupations. In recent months, concerns have been expressed regarding certain limitations on
home occupations. The Planning Commission asked staff to review those standards and present
potential changes to the ordinance. One of the specific concerns involved limitations on daycares and
preschools and the maximum number of children and employees allowed. Care providers and
instructors wanted additional employees to increase safety for both the children and adults involved.
Other concerns have been about daycare hours, as they start earlier than technically permitted by City
Code. Staff also recommended clarifying group instruction due to recent applications for activities that
do not follow typical drop-off and pick-up schedules.

After the staff report was published for the March 3, 2022, Planning Commission meeting, the City
Council requested staff research allowing home occupations to use accessory structures for storage.
Proposed changes regarding this aspect were added to the Planning Commission presentation and
were included in the draft ordinance.

   Staff proposed the following amendments to Title 10:
   1. Allow three (3) additional employees – must be parked on premise
   2. Restrict where additional parking can be placed
   3. Clarify that only two (2) customers are allowed at one time
   4. Update employees allowed in Special Home Occupations
   5. Increase the number of children/students to 12 at a time/per session
   6. Define Group Instruction as drop off and pick up activities
   7. Allow storage of in accessory structures.

Commissioners confirmed accessory structure were for storage only. Commissioners debated the
definition of operating out of an accessory unit and allowing inventory storage. Commissioners
recommended additional language prohibiting the storage of equipment in yards. Planner Howa
reviewed the differences for accessory dwelling structure use for special home occupations in
agricultural zones. Commissioners discussed possible changes to 10-22-6 and recommended further
discussion at a future work meeting.

       3.5 Consideration of Approval to Amend Title 10 Pertaining to Conditional Use Standards
              (Public Hearing)
        Applicant: Herriman City
        File Number: Z2022-020
Planning Manager Spencer informed the Commission the Utah State Legislature recently modified the
Land Use Development and Management Act (LUDMA) to require municipalities use objective
standards when reviewing and deciding conditional use permit applications. Planning Manager Spencer
reviewed the current provisions which were largely subjective, regulated esthetics and required
excessive mitigation measures for enforcement. The City Code amendment containing specific
detrimental effects would be more objectively measured.

Chair Berbert opened the public hearing.

No comments were offered.

Chair Berbert closed the public hearing.

The Commissioners agreed this was a favorable approach for conditional use permits.

Commissioner Garcia MOVED to recommend approval to City Council of item 3.5 file number Z2022-
020 Consideration of Approval to Amend Title 10 Pertaining to Conditional Use Standards.
