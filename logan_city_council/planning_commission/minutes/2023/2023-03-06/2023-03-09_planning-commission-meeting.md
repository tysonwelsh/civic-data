PC 22-063 Old Ephraim Apartments 2 -Continued from the February 23 meeting. [Design
Review Permit] Nathan Whittaker/GBN Enterprises LLC, authorized agent/owner is requesting a
Design Review Permit to construct a new 10-unit student housing structure on a combined 1.29-acre
site located at approximately 655 East 400 North in the Campus Residential (CR) zone; TIN 06-058-
0007; -0006; -0005 (Adams Neighborhood).

STAFF: Russ Holley, Senior Planner reviewed the proposed project that was continued from the
February 23% meeting to address discrepancies with the parking, open space, and setbacks. An
updated site plan has been submitted since the last meeting proposing a total of 93 parking stalls on
site. The applicant is asking for a 15 percent reduction from the required 109 parking stalls. The
applicant is proposing 13 compact parking stalls with a 21.6-foot drive aisle. The Land Development
Code allows compact stalls at a 10 percent max, 93 stalls onsite would allow for 9 compact stalls.
The Land Development Code requires a standard drive aisle to be 24 feet wide and does not allow
for a reduction in compact parking areas. The front setback is required to be 25 feet to match the
adjacent Neighborhood Residential zone across 400 North. The applicant is asking for a 2 percent
reduction for the proposed 23-foot front setback. The applicant is asking for a 3 percent reduction
in open space from the required 30 percent to the proposed 27 percent.

The applicant has submitted an alternate offsite parking plan that shows 10 additional parking stalls
located at 651 East 600 North, 0.70 miles (1,318 feet) north of the project site. The Land
Development Code allows for offsite parking if it is located within a 600-foot radius of the use served
and there is a written agreement that runs with the land.

Commissioner Guth asked about the 10 percent parking reduction. R. Holley said that parking
reduction is tied to a viable alternate parking plan.

PROPONENT: Nathan Whittaker said if the commission would grant a 15 percent parking reduction,

he would not need an alternate parking plan. He spoke about the challenges with the site and the
many options that have been looked into in trying to get parking to work. He spoke about the


proximity to campus and said that when the Campus Academy building is full, they will only need 17
parking stalls.

PUBLIC: LeAnn Wilkins asked where they will be piling the snow when we have another winter
similar to the current one.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Heare said that the alternate parking plan is so far from the site it is
not even an option and he is not inclined to approve a 15 percent parking reduction. Commissioner
Heare said he is fine with allowing the 27 percent open space and the 23-foot front setback.
Commissioner Guth, Commissioner Croshaw, and Commissioner Lewis agreed.

PC 23-015 Willets Rezone [Zone Change & Design Review Permit] Jed Willets, authorized
agent/owner is requesting a rezone of a vacant .24-acre parcel from Mixed Residential Low (MR-12)
to Mixed Residential Medium (MR-20), to build a new 3,000 square foot duplex located at 1354 N
220 W in the MR-12 zone; TIN 05-041-0056 (Bridger Neighborhood).

STAFF: Russ Holley, Senior Planner reviewed the request for a zone change of Lot 6 of the Royal
Pointe Subdivision located at the corner of 200 West and 1400 North. There are seven 4-plexes
currently located on Lot 6, and in 1995, County tax identification numbers were created for each of
the buildings that were not approved by the city. The result was an illegal subdivision that left the
original parent Tax identification number as a vacant .24-acre in the southwest corner. When the
zoning changed to Multi-Family High in 2000 the property became legally existing nonconforming.
In 2016 when the same applicant applied for a rezone to MR-20 to build a duplex it was approved
but after several extensions with the building permit process, the building permit was never obtained
and ended up expiring in 2020.

The re-zone of Lot #6 from Mixed Residential Low (MR-12) to Mixed Residential Medium (MR-20)
would achieve a higher density allowing for a proper subdivision and the ability to build a multi-
family structure (up to 4 units for .24 acres) in the vacant area in the southwest corner. The MR-12
zone allows 12 units per acre. The MR-20 zone allows 20 units per acre. Currently, Lot #6 is
developed at 15.5 units per acre.

PROPONENT: Jed Willets spoke about his building permit being approved at the end of 2019 right
before the covid pandemic hit and his work hours increased to 12-14 hours a day and not having
enough time to start the development.

PUBLIC: LeAnn Wilkins who owns the neighboring home to the south spoke about her property
being flooded with the runoff from this lot and asked where the snow will be piled if a duplex gets
built and where they will store the dumpsters.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Guth asked why the staff is recommending denial of the rezone.
Commissioner Heare stated that rezoning this would go against the city’s stance against spot zoning.
Commissioner Guth said that most of the surrounding properties are medium density and nothing
can be done with the property without a rezone. Commissioner Croshaw asked Mr. Willets what his
time frame would be this time around if the rezone is approved. Mr. Willets said he would get it done
this year. Commissioner Peterson stated that he is fine moving forward with a recommendation of
approval of a rezone. Commissioner Guth and Commissioner Croshaw agreed with Commissioner
Peterson.

PC 23-006 Land Development Code Amendment 17.07, 17.08, 17.10 (Residential &
Commercial Standards) -Continued from the February 23" meeting. [Code Amendment] Mike
DeSimone/Logan City are requesting a Code Amendment to the Land Development Code, Chapter
17.07-Specific Development Standards; Residential Zones to include transparency, useable outdoor
space, open space & landscaping requirements, and minimum lot size in MR-12; amend Chapter
17.08-Neighborhood Residential Zone Uses to clarify permitted residential structure types in NC
Zone; and amend Chapter 17.10 Specific Development Standards; District and Corridor Zones to
updated useable outdoor space, open space, and landscaping requirements, and include min
building heights, min lot coverage, eliminate drive-through’s and modify commercial parking
requirements in TC zone.

STAFF/PROPONENT: Mike DeSimone, Community Development Director reviewed the following
updates made to the proposed code amendments since the last meeting.


Chapter 17.07 Residential Development Standards. Changed the maximum height of accessory
buildings to 25 feet.

Chapter 17.10.060 Town Center 1 Development Standards. Removed the minimum lot coverage of
50 percent, and added a 50 percent commercial parking reduction for lots fronting Main Street and
400 North with a sales and service use and an alternative parking plan. Added a 100 percent
commercial parking reduction for lots fronting Main Street and 400 North with a sales and service
use, alternative parking plans, at least 50 percent lot coverage, and at least 25 percent of useable
outdoor space. Added language that a change in use triggers parking compliance.

PUBLIC: Michael Timmons said the 15-foot height limit for accessory buildings has served the city
well for several years. 50 percent of the homes in Logan have an accessory structure of some sort
that all go unnoticed due to their small scale and height. A larger structure would undermine the
spirit of the community and would be an invasion of personal space that will change the ambiance
of the neighborhood and the views from a property.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Guth said that someone should be able to build a detached garage
that matches the height of their home and asked if there is a demand for larger accessory buildings.
M. DeSimone said that this is making it simpler for people to do what they like with their property. A
15-foot height limit is pretty limiting for a garage in a backyard. Commissioner Heare said he is fine
with all of the proposed updates to the code amendment. Commission Lewis said that 25 feet is still
a significant structure. The commission discussed lowering the accessory building height limit to 20
feet
