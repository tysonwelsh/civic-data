         3.1. Discussion of the South Hills Master Development Agreement to add
              approximately 74 acres of property to the project area (City File No. M2023-129)
              – Michael Maloy, City Planner
      City Planner Michael Maloy offered an overview of amendments to the development
      agreement and looked to align changes with the east area General Plan. He highlighted the
      current land use plan being amended with property adjustments and textual changes being
      proposed. He noted some parcel were owned by Staker, which raised concern about the
      ownership requirements outlined in the master development agreement. City Planner Maloy
      turned the time over to Wasatch Commercial Developers Director Leif Smith to present the
      proposal.

      Director Smith presented details about the proposed amendments, emphasizing additions of
      commercial areas and open spaces while maintaining lower density than what the developer
      had initially planned. He highlighted the history of approvals and corresponding zoning
      changes to align the development with the east area Master Plan. Director Smith observed
      the proposed land swap with Panorama development and infrastructure plans for the
      development.

      Councilmember Shields questioned the fairness in land swaps and property exchanges being
      tied to housing density. Director Smith relayed his position with the road layout, there were
      better areas to place commercial which would necessitate the need to move the residential
      to a different area. WesTech Engineering Vice President Craig Martin confirmed the land
      swap would take place and observed the contiguous nature of the properties and Mountain
      View Corridor, there would be a desire for commercial in the area.

      Councilmember Ohrn recalled the discussion about the complexity of the documents and
      expressed frustration with continual amendments existing instead of creating and restating
      an entirely new agreement. The Council suggested starting fresh with a new master
      development agreement for each new piece of land. Director Smith supported their idea and
      proposed the current amendment proposed should create a baseline to avoid revisiting past
      agreement. Councilmember Ohrn felt it was still taking a step back because the group was
      working from a plan dated back to 2009 rather than aligning the plan with the current goals
      of the City. Vice President Martin opined the amendments were to improve existing
      agreements and were not tied to specific historical periods. Councilmember Ohrn
      acknowledged challenges faced by both sides and appreciated efforts to improve the
      situation.

      In response to a question posed by Councilmember Hodges, Community Development
      Director referenced the design standards for driveway setbacks and suggested the Council
      adjust based on popular vehicle sizes. Director Smith observed the topography factor as an
      influencer in the design standards, especially for medium-density small lots.

          10.6. Notice of Pending Ordinance regarding Preliminary Subdivision Plats and Street
                Connectivity – Todd Sheeran, City Attorney
      City Attorney Todd Sheeran discussed the pending ordinance for preliminary subdivision
      plats and street connectivity. He explained that a developer had encountered issues with the
      existing code, which required any proposed street on a plat to connect to an existing public
      street. This requirement created a timing problem for the developer at the preliminary plat
      phase. To address the issue, the City proposed an amendment to the code.

      The amendments included provisions such as requiring the applicant to provide the
      approximate location of connection points between the preliminary plat and existing public
      streets. The information would help staff assess potential concerns such as elevation and
      road widths. Written authorizing from adjoining landowner would be mandated if the plat did
      not directly connect to a public street, ensuring that landowners were aware and consenting
      to future road construction.

      Another amendment clarified that all streets must connect to an existing public road before
      final plat approval, which would maintain the City’s current standards. The City also
      addressed the timing of subdivision plats, adjusting the vesting period from two years to a
      more common one-year period, with the possibility of extensions upon showing good cause.

      Councilmember Ohrn moved to approve Resolution No R18-2024 giving notice of a pending
      ordinance to make changes to City Code §10-5-15 (Preliminary Plats) and §10-28-13
      (arrangement of streets). Councilmember Hodges seconded the motion.
