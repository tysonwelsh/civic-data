2. Gold Coast Plaza Sign Relocation; 1411 West 9000 South; P-C Zone; Approval of sign
materials and design; Neal Knight (applicant) [#29120; parcel 27-03-426-095]
Mark Forsythe gave an overview of the request to relocate a pole sign due to widening of 9000 South.
The existing sign was approved by the Planning Commission and constructed in 1987 at a time when
pole signs were allowed in the P-C Zone. However, the current code no longer allows pole signs in the
P-C Zone. Since the sign is legal, non-conforming, the code provisions allow for the sign to be moved
as long as the size and height do not increase. It is also a requirement of the code that the sign be
enhanced in some way. The proposal is to add stone to the base of the sign. The Design Review
Committee reviewed the enhancements and gave a positive recommendation with the addition of a
finished concrete cap on top of the stone. He explained the clear vision zone as it relates to the stone
placement.
Staff recommended that the Planning Commission approve the sign materials and design for the Gold
Coast Plaza sign relocation located at 1411 West 9000 South in a P-C Zone, with the Requirements of
Approval listed in the report.
Requirements of Approval:
A. The overall height, sign area, and width of the new sign shall not exceed that of the existing
sign. Permit plans shall provide these dimensions for the existing sign.
B. New materials applied to an existing sign shall not increase the size of the existing sign beyond
the thickness of the new materials.
Cc. New architectural features shall be added to enhance the appearance of the existing sign and
shall not extend more than one foot (1') beyond any surface of the sign.

There was a discussion on sign design that has so many different tenants, which makes it look cluttered.

The Commission questioned how the city could prevent this type of sign or at least require a uniform

font or graphic. Speech cannot be regulated as a first amendment right. With the number of businesses

on the property and some located behind other buildings, a monument sign would not be practical.

Often the purpose of a pole sign is to consolidate signage for all businesses into one instead of having

excessive monument signs.

Neal Knight, applicant attending remotely, said that the landlord prefers this sign. The tenant signage

will be upgraded to today’s standards and the current businesses and installed on the structure, which

will be painted to reflect the building color.

The monument sign for the credit union located east of this site complies with the code and can be

moved without additional approvals. It was noted that this is a business item with no public hearing

required.

listed in the report. The motion was seconded by Matt Quinney and passed 6-0 in
favor. McKenna Marchant was absent.

3. West Jordan Comprehensive General Plan and Future Land Use Map; Continued from 1-

17-23; Recommendation to the City Council for the adoption of the updated West Jordan
Comprehensive General Plan and Future Land Use Map; City-wide applicability; City of
West Jordan (applicant)

Ray McCandless said the process to update the General Plan began in 2020. Much time and effort from

the General Plan Committee and staff was expended to bring it to this point. Each of the chapters have

been discussed in previous planning commission meetings and comments were incorporated. During
those meetings they talked about the State requirement for having a General Plan, about the General

Plan Committee, and the public involvement process to receive comments that were integrated into the

plan. This is now the formal public hearing stage. Since October when the Planning Commission last

heard the item, staff has been formatting the document into the draft plan before them tonight. They
have not received any additional comments from the public.

Mark Forsythe said there were some small changes in the land use map since the Planning Commission

last met. The community commercial designation on the entire Highlands South subdivision did not

take into consideration the residential piece that Peterson Development is planning to develop as
planned in the Highlands Master Plan. That is why the southern portion will remain as it exists today on

business research park. Some ideas have been shared that would change it to the mixed-use designation

Ammon Allen asked if staff had a preferred use for that area.

Ray McCandless said there are mixed feelings. It depends on what the vision for the entire area is.
Topography is within the foothills, and it can be expensive to develop large buildings that are typically
found within a research park on such topography.

George Winn referred to the Balanced Housing ordinance and commented that it could make sense to
dedicate part of the property to single family housing to get the numbers more in line with the
ordinance.

Scott Langford said business research park does not allow for any residential whereas the Southwest
Quadrant allows for single family and multifamily.

Matt Quinney said he would be hesitant to support any single family in that area due to the surrounding
industrial uses. If it is a large commercial center, then it should be designed to bring in people from
South Jordan and Herriman. He was not opposed to multifamily in that location.

Larry Gardner clarified that the piece near Home Depot that is being left as it is today because a change
to commercial would be inconsistent with the Highlands Master Plan that shows residential in that
location.

Trish Hatch asked if there is any hope for transit in the southwest area.

Scott Langford said it would be decades if anything is provided. The closest station is the Welby
station. Employers in the area were included in discussions about a circulator route for the short term.
Probably the closest BRT in the future would be on 5600 West.

Kent Shelton said he associates a research park as being near a university campus and he asked if it
would work without one.

Ray McCandless said it is important to have a transition between heavier industrial uses and residential
uses to the north, which is why this was in part designated as research park and to improve the overall
appearance with a more upscale look.

Scott Langford said in about 2017 the City Council talked about getting a university extension into this
area, but things have changed since then. A research park does not necessarily have to be tied to a
university or higher learning facility. There are more big industrial users in this area than we expected
when the last land use map was prepared. Staff felt there are merits to both options, but the physical
geography in that area is challenging and expensive for large pad sites.

Larry Gardner said if the use table for Research Park is very limited. The Southwest Quadrant allows
for all of those uses, but it also allows commercial, industrial, and housing. Because a master plan for
