1.      Public Meeting – A Petition for the Issuance of a Building Permit for a Building
        Located in the Historic and Landmark District.

Action Item

Applicant:     South Salt Lake City
Address:       3278 South 540 East

Ms. Ungricht reported that the Planning Commission Members are also members of the Historic
and Landmark Commission. There is one Historic and Landmark District within the City, which
is where the Historic Scott School is located. It is on the corner of 3300 South and 500 East, just
north of the new Granite Library. As the Historic and Landmark Commission, certain items need
to be reviewed. This included additions, renovations, alterations, exterior remodeling, and major
changes in color, material, texture, and roofing materials. The Building Permit application is
related to four new doors and three new windows at the Redwood Building on the Historic Scott
South Salt Lake City Planning Commission Meeting                                                4

School campus. The building was outlined in yellow on the Staff Report. It was noted that the
proposed improvements had already received funding. Additional images were shared.

Ms. Ungricht overviewed Figure 2: Door and Window Locations. Box A was where three new
windows would be installed. Boxes 1, 2, and 3 were existing doors that would be replaced. Box
4 was a new door that would be installed, as per the Fire Code, another exit out of that room was
needed. Figure 3: Existing Building Elevations was shared. It showed the existing doors on the
front façade. Those doors faced west. Figure 4: Proposed Building Elevations showed the
proposed additions. Ms. Ungricht reported that a Door Master Plan was approved in 2013. All of
the proposed doors conformed to the existing Master Plan. Renderings of the doors were shown.

will be used for exiting or if employees need to load items. The three proposed windows were
reviewed. Ms. Ungricht reported that the Reading Nook currently has no windows. The intention
was to add natural light there. Staff recommended that the Planning Commission approve the
Historic Landmark Design Review application with conditions.

Commissioner Southey was in favor of the application. Commissioner Carter had a question about
the elevation view. It showed gray trim around the door but the other images in the packet showed
white trim around the door. He wondered what the colors would actually be. Neighborhoods
Department Director, Sharen Hauri believed the existing trim was white. A similar treatment
would be done on the Redwood Building. Whatever trim is there will remain and the door itself
would be replaced. Commissioner Southey wanted to know if there would be more renovations
done on the site. Ms. Hauri reported that there had not been a lot done to the building in the last
10 years. However, there would likely be more improvements to come. This was considered
Phase 1 of the Redwood Building improvements, which were being funded by the Community
Development Block Grant (“CDBG”) Program. A CDBG application was submitted for Phase 2,
which would be for site improvements, such as sidewalks and walkways on the east side as well
as improvements to the parking lot and pathways. The intention was for two projects to be done
this year, so there would likely be another project presented to the Planning Commission within
the next several months.

         from Residential Multiple (“RM”) to Commercial Corridor (“CC”).

Action Item

Applicant:     Katmark Partners, LLC
Address:       130, 140, and 148 East Winslow Avenue and 3549-3555 South 200 East

Ms. Ungricht reported that the applicant has four separate applications. The first was a rezone for
the following four properties:

     •   130 East Winslow Avenue;
     •   140 East Winslow Avenue;
     •   148 East Winslow Avenue; and
     •   3549-3555 South 200 East.

There are three existing homes on Winslow Avenue that the applicant proposed to rezone from
Residential Multiple (“RM”) to Commercial Corridor (“CC”). One of them was the marketing
office for Mark Miller Subaru and the other two were vacant homes. In addition to the properties
on Winslow Avenue, the proposal was to rezone 3549-3555 South 200 East. That was previously
a fourplex, but that had been removed, and the land had been vacant since late 2019. Ms. Ungricht
shared an image of the existing conditions. The proposal was to raze the first two homes at 130
East Winslow Avenue and 140 East Winslow Avenue to expand the display lot. 148 East Winslow
Avenue would be converted into an office space. The vacant area would become part of the
parking lot. One of the follow-up applications would be a subdivision to consolidate all of the
parcels into one. There needed to be consistent zoning throughout for that to happen. Ms. Ungricht
explained that the zone change was the first step in the process. There would also need to be a
vacation of the public right-of-way, which was the next item on the Planning Commission Meeting
agenda.

4.      Public Hearing – Z-22-004 – A Petition to Amend the Zoning Map for Five Parcels
        from the Commercial Corridor (“CC”) Land Use District to the Flex Land Use
        District.

Action Item

Applicant:     Jason Head
Address:       800 West Central Valley Road

Ms. Ungricht reported that the above item was a rezone application for the property where Golf
the Round is currently located. The property is owned by Central Valley Water who decided to
end their lease with Golf the Round and enter into a new lease with Dakota Pacific for
redevelopment of the property. In order for this to occur, there were two steps. The first was the
rezone and the second was a subdivision to consolidate the parcels. The rezone was needed
because it was best to have one zone for the property. Additionally, an industrial park, storage, or
warehouse use was not permitted in the Commercial Corridor (“CC”). She explained that those
uses are permitted in the Flex Land Use District. The proposal was to have an industrial park in
the area. Ms. Ungricht shared the existing conditions of Golf the Round as well as the proposed
site plan. She clarified that the site plan is currently in development and that a more finalized
version would come back in the future.

The proposal met many of the General Plan goals and strategies. For instance, it identified areas
of the City for higher-density employment-based uses and would take advantage of existing
planned infrastructure. The location next to 3300 South, I-15-, I-80, and Roper Yard was a good
location for an industrial park due to its proximity to the various highways. Ms. Ungricht also
noted that the proposal also related to Land Use and Neighborhoods Strategy 6: “Implement best
practices in the regulation of development and land uses.” An industrial park was the best use of
the property since there were limitations due to the prior business there, the Vitro Uranium Mill.
The proposal would also conserve natural resources, as the water that was used to water the golf
course would no longer be needed. A lot of water would be saved as a result. Ms. Ungricht pointed
out that the proposal would increase the business hub and the General Plan called out 3300
Corridor as a business hub.

Commissioner Dahlberg understood that the site plan was preliminary but wanted to confirm that
if the project were approved and something was built, there would be a requirement for a sidewalk
and landscaping. Ms. Ungricht confirmed that there would be a requirement for a sidewalk. The

Chair Pechmann opened the public hearing at 8:27 p.m.

Suzanne Slifka gave her address as 3849 South Salt River Way. She noted that the area currently
has three lanes of traffic. On 900 West, there is a tight turning radius and not a lot of good traffic
flow. She understood that this was a preliminary design but wondered whether the details would
be discussed in the future to ensure that the traffic flow is appropriate. Chair Pechmann was not
certain when the Traffic Study would be completed but he knew one would be done. Ms. Ungricht
reported that traffic studies are done as part of the subdivision application. Some preliminary
studies had been done already. A route within the subdivision had been created to show how trucks
would move. 3300 is a Utah Department of Transportation (“UDOT”) road, so there would need
to be some collaboration with UDOT to receive the necessary approvals related to the route.

Sophie Bellina gave her address as 3702 South Luchars Lane. She state that the road is owned by
UDOT but suggested that there be a requirement that a 10-to-12-foot sidewalk be maintained.
There could be a condition that the sidewalk needed to be a certain width with landscaping.
Ms. Ungricht explained that the Commission could make a recommendation to the City Council.

Michael Reynolds stated that he lives at 164 West Lady Bank Way. The proposal seemed to cover
approximately half the site of the Golf the Round. He wondered what would happen to the northern
half of the existing residential amenity. Mr. Reynolds asked if something was contemplated for
the remaining Golf the Round site. Ms. Ungricht explained that Central Valley Road divides Golf
the Round and the sewer treatment plant. Everything south of Central Valley Road was what was
in the Lease Agreement with Dakota Pacific for the redevelopment. Everything below the dashed
line on the map shown would be part of the redevelopment proposal.

There were no further comments. The public hearing was closed at 8:33 p.m.
