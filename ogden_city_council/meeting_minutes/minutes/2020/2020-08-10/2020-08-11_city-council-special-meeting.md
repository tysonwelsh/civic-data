Proposed Ordinance 2020-30, amending the Ogden City General Plan by
approving and adopting the Southeast Ogden Community Plan
         A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the Southeast Ogden Community Plan
came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated City Administration has proposed an update to the Southeast Ogden
Community Plan. The community plan covers the area generally south of 36th Street and east of Gramercy A venue. The
community plan sets forth vision statements for land use, housing, parks and open space, and transportation. The community plan
prescribes vision strategies for topics and issues within each of the vision statement areas. The Southeast Ogden Community Plan
update will be the first major rewrite of the community plan for the area since 1987. The Council reviewed the proposed plan at
the February 11, 2020 work session. At that meeting, questions arose about accessory dwelling units, code enforcement and
student housing. The Council requested that additional work session time be allocated for discussion and that members of the
Planning Commission be invited to share their perspective about the plan. The Council held a work session on March 10, 2020 to
discuss the plan further. Members of the Planning Commission were invited to attend and representatives from the City's Code
Enforcement office were present to discuss code enforcement issues. The plan was presented to the Council at the July 14, 2020
meeting. The Council accepted public input on the proposed plan and voted to table the consideration of the plan to August 11,
2020. The Council expressed interest in alternative language regarding the ADU restrictions. Any alternative language will be
presented and discussed during the meeting.

               Deputy Planning Manager Spencer summarized the memo and noted that during that meeting, there was a great deal of
    public comments regarding the housing component of the Community Plan and how to address the Accessory Dwelling Unit
    (ADU) land use in the Community Plan area. Staff has carefully considered the feedback provided and has offered alternate
    language for the Council to consider relative to the next steps following the adoption of the Cmmnunity Plan. He then used the aid
    of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the Plan document. He stated that the general objectives of community plans are to
    balance interests and develop partnerships. There was a perceived breach of trust from the Southeast Ogden community when the
    City approved the ADU land use City-wide. The community proposed an amendment to their Community Plan when learning of
    the 2018 ADU ordinance. The Council determined to wait to learn the outcome of the Southeast Community Plan process before
    taking action on allowing ADUs in the Plan area. The Council felt that the Cmmnunity Plan update process would be beneficial
    relative to providing sufficient information to make sound decisions. The Council also indicated they would support a narrower
    focus on the College Heights and Forest Green neighborhoods. Goal two, vision strategy one, in the Community Plan deals with
    ADUs in the Plan area; the Plan specifies the community desired limits on ADUs and calls for a zoning ordinance amendment
    process to commence after the Plan is adopted. Language in this section of the Plan has been updated responsive to the July 14
    discussion to include the following factors for consideration:
                   • Additional housing pressures a neighborhood experiences given its adjacency to WSU campus;
                   • Previous decisions for areas that have stemmed from petitions to have, or not have, ADUs;
                   •    Types of housing needs, including consideration of how student housing, multiple family housing and low-
                        density housing options are planned to occur according to this plan;
                   •    The city's overall approach to affordable housing; and
                   •    Getting equal representation of residents within each area of the community.
               There are pressures on the cmmnunity relative to its proximity to Weber State University (WSU); there are heavier traffic
    and parking demands on sh·eets from 36 th Street to Country Hills Drive. In addition to ADUs in the area, there are other owner-
    absent rental properties. There are 11 in the Forest Green neighborhood, 20 in the College Heights neighborhood, and 27 in the
    Skyline area. Residents of the Community Plan area have provided additional information in support of limiting ADUs in their
    neighborhood. There are 13 illegal AD Us in the Forest Green neighborhood and the residents would prefer limiting the number of
    ADUs to two per block, or 20 percent. They would also like the City to improve ordinance enforcement efforts; if enforcement
    measures improve, the residents would be open to allowing more ADUs in their neighborhood.
               Other language amendments that have been made include removal of the word 'intrusion' when referring to multi-family
    and student housing encroaching on single family neighborhoods. Section six, goal three relative to a trail entrance has also been
    amended to read as follows: "The trail entrance does not have a designated trailhead, as the university staff explained the
    challenges of making this an official trailhead. The land is not used for development or infrastructure, but could allow for City
    access, and maintain and restrict vehicle access. The key issues with making trailhead improvements include management,
    maintenance and funding for said improvements."

recycling program. Last week City Administration made a commitment to fix the City's website to provide updated infonnation
about the status of the recycling program, but that did not happen. It should not be so hard to fix the website and she asked City
