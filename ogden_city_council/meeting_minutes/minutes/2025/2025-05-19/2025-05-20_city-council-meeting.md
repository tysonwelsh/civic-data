Proposed Ordinance 2025–12 amending the budget for the Fiscal Year July
1, 2024 to June 30, 2025 by increasing the anticipated revenues and
appropriations for a gross increase of $118,395,800
          A memo from the Finance Director regarding a proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 budget amendment came before
the Council for consideration. The memo stated the proposed amendment recognizes revenues and appropriating funds totaling
$118,395,800. A significant portion of the revenues come from excess fund balance ($4,000,000), interest income ($3,000,000),
state and federal grants ($21,317,075), and bond proceeds ($77,000,000). The remaining revenues come from donations, RAMP
grants, loan proceeds, equipment replacement funds, and balances from various funds or retained earnings ($13,078,725). Funding
is proposed for Airport projects ($11,415,000), Union Station ($3,517,300), the canyon waterline project ($87,549,375), Golf
capital improvements ($750,000), and refuse trucks and other equipment ($4,934,175). The remaining appropriations are for other
projects and regular operational costs ($10,229,950).
          Finance Director Sorensen summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the
proposed budget amendments; he provided a spreadsheet illustrating the source and use of funds in the General Fund, Capital
Improvement Project (CIP) Plan Fund, Enterprise Funds, Grant Funds, and Strike Force Fund. For a copy of the presentation in its
entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Chair White referenced budget adjustments pertaining to wages in the Police Department; the Council made a significant
adjustment last year for the Police Department, and she understood that additional adjustments would be needed. However, she
asked if the Administration anticipates additional significant increases in future budget years. Mr. Sorensen stated that the FY
2025-2026 budget includes proposed increases to operating expenses for the Police Department; the Department’s budget is $30
million and 95 percent of that is payroll, so most adjustments will be related to wage adjustments.
          Council member Graf referenced comments made during tonight’s Redevelopment Agency meeting about a proposed
budget amendment and whether such actions have an impact on residents’ taxes; he noted that a past budget amendment was to
allocate $1 million to the Airport Fund, which yielded a return on investment of approximately $10.5 million, and these types of
activities create a positive outcome for residents.
           Chair White introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2025-12, entitled:
                    “An ordinance of Ogden City amending the budget for the Fiscal Year July 1, 2024 to June 30, 2025 by
                    increasing the anticipated revenues for a gross increase of $118,395,800 from sources as detailed in the body of
                    this ordinance; and increasing the appropriations for a gross increase of $118,395,800 as detailed in the body of
                    this ordinance and by providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final
                    passage.”
          Chair White then opened the public hearing.
          Linda Sanchez stated that she agreed with Council member Graf’s comments about the Ogden Airport and noted it is an
advantage for Ogden residents to have access to an airport close by to save them a trip to Salt Lake City. However, Roy City is
building a 10-story high-density housing project to the south of the Airport, and she asked if that would create problems for the
Airport. She referenced budget adjustments for the Police Department and stated she ‘would like a refund’; the City gave the
Department additional money last year because officers were underpaid. She came before the Council last year and asked that
officers receive de-escalation training and she was told that was going to happen and that a training simulator would be located in
the Police Station for that purpose. She does not believe that has actually happened based upon the statistics about Police
shootings in the community. The other day she noticed a situation with a homeless man on 25th Street and there were eight police
cars at the scene; she feels this is excessive for one person and she encouraged the Council get a handle on the situation. She no
longer believes that the Police Department is focused on protecting and serving; they do not even understand the rights that the
residents of Ogden have, and they are using force in response to situations that do not warrant it. She concluded she would
probably only call the Police if she wanted someone dead.
          There being no further persons appearing to be heard, COUNCIL MEMBER MYERS MOVED THE PUBLIC
HEARING BE CLOSED. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER CHOBERKA, ALL VOTING AYE.
Council member Richey was not present when this vote was taken.
          ON A MOTION BY COUNCIL MEMBER BLAIR AND SECONDED BY VICE CHAIR HYER, ORDINANCE
2025-12 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – COUNCIL MEMBERS

Proposed Ordinance 2025–13 amending the zoning map of Ogden City, as
adopted by section 15–3–3 of the Ogden Municipal Code, to reclassify as
Multiple Family Residential Zone / Conditional Overlay Zone (R–3/CO)
property heretofore classified as Regional Commercial Zone (CP–3),
generally located at 2850 Washington Boulevard
         A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed rezone of 2850
Washington Boulevard came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated that Heritage Land Development has
submitted a petition to rezone 0.61 acres from CP-3 (Regional Commercial) to R-3 (Multiple-family Residential). The petitioner
requests to establish zoning on the property to allow for condominiums or townhomes that will be subdivided for individual sale.
On February 19, 2025, the Planning Commission considered the proposal and recommended approval of the rezone to R-3, with a
Conditional Overlay (CO) and development agreement to require the residential units to be platted to allow individual ownership
(5 – 2 vote). The R-3 zoning designation would allow for approximately 11 dwelling units on the subject property.
          Planning Director Brierley summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide the Council
with information about the subject property and surrounding properties. On February 19, 2025, the Planning Commission
considered the proposal and recommended approval of the rezone to R-3, with a Conditional Overlay and development agreement
to require the residential units to be platted to allow individual ownership. The recommendation was approved on a five to two
vote. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Council member Choberka inquired as to how the subject property would be accessed. Mr. Brierley stated the property
was subdivided in the past to create two lots, a front and rear. The accessway is a joint common area for both lots. Chair White
asked if the common area is owned by the applicant. Mr. Brierley stated the owner of the rear lot has right to the accessway based
upon condominiumization of the two lots. The front lot has been sold to another owner. Council member Choberka asked if the
driveway is the only access to the rear lot, to which Mr. Brierley answered yes. Council member Choberka then referenced the
property between the subject property and the Maverik store could be developed with commercial use, to which Mr. Brierley
answered yes. Council member Choberka inquired as to setbacks for commercial use adjacent to the proposed use of the subject
property. Mr. Brierley stated the commercial use could be built right up to the property line, there would be no setback
requirement.
          Council member Graf asked if the two homes behind the commercial building that are facing Washington Boulevard are
non-conforming, to which Mr. Brierley answered yes.
          Council member Blair stated the packet materials indicated the applicant would need to obtain a variance for the width of
the driveway if the zone change is approved. Mr. Brierley stated that is correct; the variance would also be needed if the zone
change is not approved.
          The Council and Mr. Brierley then engaged in a discussion regarding ownership of the subject property and adjacent
parcels, two of which are owned by Ogden City as a result of a transaction to address an encroachment issue. The City only owns
the back 15-feet of properties in the area.
          Council member Choberka stated she is conflicted about this application; she is generally supportive of infill housing
projects but is concerned about the limited access to the subject property. It is also concerning to her that a future commercial
building could be built right up to the property line of the subject property. Vice Chair Hyer agreed and discussed some of the
challenges with the property; if more property were acquired to address the development and access challenges, he would be more
supportive of the zone change in the future.
          Chair White invited input from the applicant.
          Selvoy Fillerup, Heritage Land Development, stated Mr. Brierley has done a good job of addressing the issues he planned
to discuss tonight, but he addressed the comments made by Council member Choberka and Vice Chair Hyer about their desire for
additional property to be included in the zone change application in order to address concerns about configuration and access. He
stated he has made several attempts to contact the owner of the adjacent parcel with offers to purchase the property, but he has
been unsuccessful. There is a realtor sign on the property, and he has called the phone number at least five times, but he has never
heard back from anyone. He agreed it would be more favorable to develop both lots together, but he has been trying for over a
year to acquire it and has been unsuccessful. He is not sure that it is a feasible option at this point. He acknowledged the
challenges associated with the subject property, but he still thinks his proposal is the highest and best use; owner occupied
condominiums are a good fit for the area.
           Chair White introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2025-13, entitled:
                      “An ordinance of Ogden City Utah, amending the zoning map of Ogden City, as adopted by section 15-3-3 of
                      the Ogden Municipal Code, to reclassify as Multiple Family Residential Zone/Conditional Overlay Zone (R-
                      3/CO) property heretofore classified as Regional Commercial Zone (CP-3), generally located at 2850
                      Washington Boulevard, rear; and by providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon
                      posting after final passage.”
          Chair White then called for public input regarding the proposal.
          Linda Sanchez stated she likes the proposed use of this property; she has met people who live in the high-rise apartments
on Washington Boulevard. They have been concerned by the lack of a setback from the sidewalk and insufficient parking at their
property. One resident parked in a stall that did not technically belong to her, and her vehicle was towed, and she had to pay $300
to retrieve it. If there were other housing options available to residents, they would choose it over the high-density projects in the
area, especially if there were a place for them to park their vehicle.
          Teresa Bramwell stated she is familiar with the subject property; there were previously two homes on the property behind
the commercial building, and she would like to know the history of the property that has contributed to its current state. She drove
to the rear lot many different times and there was plenty of space to park at either of the two homes. She is not sure about the
intended number of units on the property, but as long as there is sufficient space to access the rear area and park a vehicle, it could
be useful. The property was awkward 30 years ago and it is still awkward today and she is not sure that can be solved without the
entire block being redeveloped. She feels the applicant has a right to use his property and the proposed use is as good as any
possible commercial use that could be built there.
          Ty Chaston stated he owns Brinker Apartments and has a situation similar to Mr. Fillerup’s; a few matters that should be
addressed are to make sure the utility access is located in the single access to the property and ensuring a fire truck can fit down
the access to the rear property. Additionally, if the units are built tall enough, it is necessary to ensure a ladder truck can fit down
the access. His property abuts a commercial property, and the commercial use was creating light pollution for residents of his
project; he suggested that the applicant and the City work with nearby commercial property owners to ensure their lights are
appropriately shielded. Finally, a snow removal plan should be considered, and he wondered if the snow would be pushed out to
Washington Boulevard. Perhaps the unit count should be reduced by one to provide sufficient space for snow storage or water
retention on the site.
          No additional persons came forward to be heard.
          Chair White addressed Mr. Brierley and inquired as to the process the applicant would follow to secure a variance for the
access width. Mr. Brierley stated that the applicant would apply to the Board of Zoning Adjustments (BZA) for the variance.
Chair White asked if the applicant has presented a concept plan that includes a unit count. Mr. Brierley stated that approximately
three years ago an applicant provided a concept plan that included one building, but there was no unit count. Chair White asked
where the patrons of the business located on the front lot park their cars, to which Mr. Brierley answered there is a designated
parking area and street parking area and patrons have the right to use the shared access to get to the parking area.
          Council member Choberka asked if it is correct that there are a couple of apartment units in the upper level of the
commercial building on the front lot, to which Mr. Brierley answered yes. Council member Choberka stated the residents of those
apartments park behind the building. She added she was also curious about whether a fire truck and garbage truck can fit down the
access to service the rear lot. Mr. Brierley stated there would need to be a dumpster on the property and a garbage truck would
drive down the access to collect the trash.
          Council member Myers agreed with Mr. Fillerup that his proposal is the highest and best use for the subject property; he
does not believe there is any real commercial value for the property.
          COUNCIL MEMBER MYERS MOVED TO ADOPT ORDINANCE 2025-13. THE MOTION DIED FOR LACK
OF A SECOND.
          Council member Graf stated that the assumption has been made that there is no commercial viability for the subject
property, but he believes there are some different uses that would fit on the property, and he referenced a few similar properties in
Salt Lake City that have been used for attractive commercial or office space uses. He has concerns about access to the property
and believes the viability of a residential zone change and development would be improved at some point in the future.
          ON A MOTION BY VICE CHAIR HYER AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER GRAF, ORDINANCE
2025-13 WAS REJECTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – COUNCIL MEMBERS
BLAIR, CHOBERKA, GRAF, VICE CHAIR HYER, AND CHAIR WHITE. VOTING NO – COUNCIL MEMBER
MYERS. Council member Richey was not present when this vote was taken.
          Council member Myers maintained his belief that the best use of the property is residential, and he would prefer that the
Council be more friendly to developers in this situation, especially when there is no clear opposition to the proposal. Council
member Choberka stated she appreciates Council member Myers’ position, but there are many potential changes to other
properties in the area, and she remains concerned about limited access to the property.
          Vice Chair Hyer reiterated his main concerns about the application relating to the access.

Proposed Ordinance 2025–14 adopting the proposed Tentative Budget of
Ogden City for Fiscal Year 2025–2026
         A memo from Council staff regarding the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2025-2026 budget came before the Council for
consideration. The memo stated on May 6, 2025, Mayor Benjamin K. Nadolski presented his proposed FY 2025-2026 Budget.
The current proposal is to set a public hearing for June 3, 2025 regarding the budget.
         Council Assistant Executive Director Symes used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide information regarding
the City’s annual budget process; public resources regarding the tentative budget document include the Mayor’s proposed budget
book and his presentation from the May 6 meeting; line-item budgets including revenues and expenditures; agenda packet
materials; department specific budget information; staff presentations; and video/audio recordings of work session and Council
meetings.
Mr. Symes expounded on Council staff’s analysis of the Mayor’s proposed budget. He also provided links to different pages of the
City’s website containing budget information. The Council has engaged in review of various sections of the budget and
Department-specific budgets over the course of several work sessions and will continue their review process up to the June 17
Council meeting. Additionally, an open house dedicated to the annual budget is scheduled for May 27, 225 at 7:00 p.m. in the
Municipal Building. He briefly offered a year-over-year comparison of various funds in the budget, concluding the total FY 2025-
2026 Ogden City budget represents a 7.5 percent - $20 million – increase over the FY 2024-2025 adopted budget. Some
significant budget matters for the public to consider include:
              • No Property Tax Increase
                      o Proposed to adopt county-provided certified tax rate
              • Unused salary and benefits carryforward
                      o $2M of unused wage and benefits proposed to be used for wage and benefits increases in FY2026
              • No significant changes to Financial Principles or Budget Goals and Guidelines
         Proposed personnel changes in the budget include:
              • 3 positions eliminated
              • 4 positions added
              • Net 1 new position for a total of 698 total FTEs
              • 12 reclassifications
              • 8 title changes
             • 2 transfers between departments
             • 2.97% cost of living (COLA) increase for all employees
             • 1.5% merit increase for non-sworn employees
             • 4% step increase for sworn fire and police
        The budget also includes funding for 42 Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) projects totaling $29.7 million; this includes 18
one-time projects, 21 perpetual projects, and three Recreation Arts Museum Parks (RAMP) projects.
        For a copy of Mr. Symes’ presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
        Council members communicated their interest in hearing public input and feedback regarding the proposed FY 2025-
2026 budget.
        Chair White then introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2025-14, entitled:
                  “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, adopting the proposed tentative budget of Ogden City for Fiscal Year
                  2025-2026.”
        VICE CHAIR HYER MOVED A PUBLIC HEARING ON THE PROPOSAL BE HELD IN THE CITY
