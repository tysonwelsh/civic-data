                                  PLANNING COMMISSION MINUTES
                                          Meeting of February 11, 2021
                             Logan City Council Chambers (290 N 100 W Logan, UT 84321) www.loganutah.org

 Minutes of the meeting for the Logan City Planning Commission convened via electronic session
  on Thursday, February 11, 2021. Vice-Chair Newman called the meeting to order at 5:30 p.m.

Commissioners Present: Roylan Croshaw, Jessica Lucero, Tony Nielson, Dave Newman, Eduardo
Ortiz

Commissioners Absent: Regina Dickinson, Sandi Goodlander

Staff Present: Mike DeSimone, Russ Holley, Tanya Rice, Debbie Zilles, Bill Young, Paul Taylor,
Jeannie Simmonds (City Council Liaison), Amy Anderson (City Council)
Commissioner Croshaw moved to approve the minutes from the January 28, 2021 meeting as
submitted. Motion seconded by Commissioner Lucero. The motion was approved unanimously.

PUBLIC HEARING

 PC 21-005 Deer Pen Subdivision [Subdivision Permit] Logan City, authorized agent/owner,
 request to subdivide the 33 acres of City-owned Deer Pen property into 22 residential building
 lots on 8.63 acres and 2 remainder lots on 23.42 acres to be designated as parks/open space
 located at approximately 1500 North Aspen Drive in the Neighborhood Residential (NR-4) zone;
 TIN 05-109-0096.

STAFF: Mr. Holley reviewed the request for the Deer Pen Subdivision located adjacent to Aspen
Drive and 1500 North. The proposal is to create a total of 22 residential lots, eight (8) of which will
be accessed off of Aspen Drive, seven (7) of which will be accessed off of 1500 North with the
remaining seven (7) lots accessed off of a proposed new cul-de-sac (Sharptail Circle). The
proposal includes two remaining parcels totaling 23.11 acres that are planned to become a future
City Park. The building lots range from 12,097 SF to 26,692 SF in size and are compatible with
surrounding neighborhoods. This bench area of the Hillcrest Neighborhood slopes upward towards
the mountain range to the east. Some of the 22 lots have been identified with greater than 30%
slopes on the preliminary plat. Existing drainage easement re-alignments are shown along lots 12-
22 to accommodate the proposed layout. Considering these slope issues, a new sewer easement
is proposed outside of Aspen Drive and along the west side of lots 15-22. An above-ground, high-
voltage power line running east/west is located between proposed lots 15 and 16. This 22-lot
subdivision was approved by the Planning Commission on July 14, 2016, but the final plat was not
recorded and has since expired.
PROPONENT: Mike DeSimone, the Community Development Director representing Mayor
Daines, explained that the property is owned by the cemetery fund. He reminded the Commission
that tonight’s discussion is not about park planning or aesthetic issues, only consideration for the
proposed subdivision. The Park & Rec Department will have a future public forum when the park
development begins. He explained that this was originally approved in 2016. The only change
from the original plan is that Sharptail Circle changed slightly. The current proposal is more
defined and outlines the sewer easement. This area will be put up for sale (a public hearing will be
held in March) and the City’s General Fund will be used as a bond. The money will go toward the
irrigation improvements and the existing cemetery.

PUBLIC: Emails from Gary Etherington, Christine Wingar, Jane Erickson, Kathryn Graham,
Debbie Brough, Michael Timmons, and Maria Rodriguez were received and distributed to the
Commission before the meeting and have been added to the project file.

Michael Timmons is concerned with what is being indicated as a 1,600’ long 10’ wide gravel drive
terminating in a hammerhead and another 400’ long path coming in from 1700 North. Mr. Young
explained that these are maintenance roads which allow for access to the sewer line manholes for
upkeep and repair. There is a detention pond in the southwest corner (just east of 1300 North on
the east side of the canal) that also requires access. Mr. Timmons said these will encroach on the
park landscape and will create a physical imprint on, and compromise, the design of the park.
There is an intended trail along the approximate alignment and asked if the construction road could
serve as a basis for the trail. The realignment of the drainage ditches seems severe and harsh;
there is no reason why the grades could not accommodate a more natural, pleasing flow. These
things are key to the design of the park so it would be critical to work together. Mr. DeSimone
agreed and would prefer a native type of drainage design. Mr. Young said if the maintenance
roads provide access to the manholes and support maintenance/equipment vehicles, there is no
reason the road must be the same alignment as the sewer line. Development of the drainage
ditches will be before the development of the park, however, as the recreation area develops, the
drainage channel could be modified to fit with the design of the park. Mr. Timmons asked about
the timeline for grading. Mr. Young said that is unknown as the intent is to sell this to a developer.

Greg Podgorski asked if the alignment of the walking path is intended to go to Sharptail Circle. Mr.
Holley explained that this the maintenance roads that were just discussed. The trail may have to
be realigned due to the drainage ditches, but it is intended to continue as a canal trail through that
area. Mr. Podgorski said his other concern is that crossing 1500 North is quite dangerous and he
is worried about safety, especially with additional traffic.

Alex Bell would like to the grading plans. He is currently designing his home, setting elevations,
and figuring out the view and is disturbed with some of the proposed changes and possible homes
being situated higher and blocking his views, especially lots on 1500 North because they will be in
his view to the Wellsville mountains. Chairman Newman pointed out that the homes will have to
follow the height requirements. Mr. Bell said the slope off 1500 North drops precipitously and he is
worried about building up those lots. Mr. Holley said each house will be lower as they come down
the road. The midpoint of the lot will be the starting point, which can vary based on home design.

Maria Rodriguez said drainage is a concern. The proposal is misleading in the maps and the
current plat has a different shape of the zone for lots 12, 13, and 14. There is drainage that comes
from the mountain and crosses Aspen Drive and the intention is to make the direction of the
drainage ditch go directly into the land where the power lines are located. Her question is whether
all the costs of landscaping and realignment of drainage will be covered by the cost of selling the
land and whether the money will be used for the cemetery. Mr. Holley said, as previously
discussed, there will need to be some coordination, and a more natural alignment would be better
suited in the area she is concerned about. The drainage will terminate into a culvert and will not go
where the power lines are located. He mentioned that the substation, that had been previously
proposed in this area, is under tentative contract to be located on USU property. When the
subdivision is recorded, the zoning will follow the property line. Mr. DeSimone said revenue
generated from the sale of this property will go to the cemetery fund and will not be used to fix
drainage issues. Mr. Young said when the property is sold, those issues will be the responsibility of
the developer, not the City. Ms. Rodriguez said the lots are all above 4,850’ in a very complicated
type of topography and she still has concerns about the direction of the drainage. She hopes there
will be some assurance in place that all the new drainage will be conducted to have proper no
flooding in the area.


Brittney Ogden is concerned about the height of the proposed homes and whether there is a way
to change the Code concerning height. She lives in Hillcrest and said it will take away many of the
views along the canal trail. Mr. DeSimone said the Code was modified in 2016 and it will not be
changed again. They will be able to build to 35’, just like other homes in the area. Clay Ogden
said it should be established where the elevation is measured from. Mr. Holley explained that it
depends on the home plan, once the mass, the roofline, and orientation are set, height can be
measured exactly. It is measured from either the existing or the finished grade, whichever is lower.
Once a home is sited it becomes an exact measurement. Currently, it is unknown where, on each
lot, homes will be located. He reviewed LDC 17.62 “Height, Building” means the maximum
vertical height of a building or structure at all points measured from natural or finished grade,
whichever is lower, to a point ½ of the distance between the roof ridge and the roof eave for a
sloped roof, or the top of the parapet for a flat roof. Architectural elements that do not add floor
area to a building, such as chimneys, vents, antennae, towers, or other similar elements, are not
considered part of the height of a building. To measure the maximum allowed height of the
structure, project that distance from natural or finished grade, whichever is lower, as shown. Figure
17.62.A shows how the maximum building height limitation applies in the single- family residential
zones. The same concept applies to all zones, but with different height limits.”
                            Figure 17.62.A: Determining Building Height


Mr. Timmons said all the lots are above 4,850 and must conform to the required conditions
Buildings should be low in height, conform with hillside topography by stepping or staggering the
mass of the proposed building up or downslope, avoid flat pad construction and vertical massing,
and utilize structure elements, building materials, and color tones that blend artificial surfaces with
surrounding native elements. Which means no blight and no blank 35’ wall on the west-facing side
of the homes. They must be stepped and staggered back as they move up the hillside. This
needs to be enforced and underscored in the transfer of property. Mr. Holley said these items will
be addressed during the building permit process. Mr. Timmons asked if CCRs could be imposed by
the City before the sale of the land, that fences adjoining the park utilize a natural wood color, that
would harmonize with the park edge and blend in with the landscape. Even more sensible would
be to require the developer to build the fencing along the property line before the sale and
development of individual homes. This is not an uncommon practice in many developments and
would ensure a pleasing uniformity along the park’s edge.

Anita Watts said her biggest concern is with traffic coming down 1500 North. The crossing for the
trail at the bottom of the curve is a safety issue, she would like to see traffic slowed down.


Jackson Graham is concerned that the property zoned as Recreation remains as a park, there is
nothing to secure that.

COMMISSION: Mr. Holley confirmed for Commissioner Croshaw that there is quite a bit of space
between the proposed back of the east lots and the deer fence. He also clarified that due to the
steep topography to the northeast, any more potential home development would be difficult and
unlikely. Because that area is not zoned for homes, it would require a future zone change. Mr.
Young, the City Engineer, also noted that in addition to the steep slopes, there are water pressure
issues with anything much higher. There are current restrictions on the construction of homes in
this area due to water these issues.

Mr. Holley answered for Commissioner Ortiz that this subdivision expired due to the change in
administration, the new administration did not prioritize this project until now.

Commissioner Lucero asked about the differences between the 2016 plan and the current
proposal. Mr. Holley said the original concept plan in 2016 had Sharptail Circle coming in further
east. Two established curb cuts will connect Sharptail in a more level area. The sewer has always
been a concern, a gravity line cannot be put in. The sewer easement should not be an issue with
the development of the park. The sewer line will be underground and landscaping and/or a trail
can be put on top of the easement. Commissioner Lucero asked about the concern regarding
drainage issues around the power lines. Mr. Holley said the ditch will be realigned and does not
interface with the power lines. The drainage will be 90’+ away from where the power lines are
located.

Commissioner Nielson asked how many lots are above 4,850’ elevation. Mr. Holley said there
would be quite a few. He also noted that the height requirement for homes is 35’ maximum, which
is measured to the midpoint of the gable from the existing or finished grade, whichever is higher.
35’ could allow for 2-3 stories depending on the home design. Commissioner Nielson asked if there
could be a deed restriction put in for the remaining area to ensure it remains park. Mr. Holley said
a restriction cannot be put in, but to change the use of that area, a zone change would have to be
submitted, which is a legislative action.

Commissioner Nielson agrees with many of the comments that have been presented. He would
like some advice on how to protect the park areas and wondered if a deed restriction could be
considered. He is also concerned about traffic and would like to see that area become safer. He
agrees with Mr. Timmons’ suggestion about fencing along the park.

Mr. DeSimone pointed out that the Commission is only considering the technical requirements of
the subdivision code and fencing is not within that purview. The concerns about fences can be
passed along to the Mayor to possibly be considered as a condition in the sale of the property but
cannot be added as a condition of approval for a subdivision. He pointed out (in reference to the
park property) that any type of zone change is a legislative process that is decided by the City
Council, who are elected officials. There is a commitment from the current Mayor to begin
construction of the park. He also noted that speed bumps do not work, there are similar issues on
many other streets that have traffic. The City tries to deal with these issues as they can.

Mr. Holley questioned what the desire is for fencing, whether it be one uniform color or material
because there can be many “natural” designs/colors. This can be very subjective, especially with
different property owners. Mr. DeSimone noted that in LDC 17.24 Critical Lands Overlay Zone lists
“Utilize construction materials, glass, roofing, fencing and other surfaces that are non-reflective”.


MOTION: Commissioner Nielson moved to conditionally approve a Subdivision Permit for
PC 21-005 with the conditions of approval as listed below. Commissioner Croshaw seconded the
motion.
The Commission asked that the Mayor be notified and encouraged to consider the following
concerns:
1. Fencing- (concerns raised about color, materials and uniformity)
Possible Action: Property Purchase Agreement requiring certain color/type of fencing or default to
Logan City LDC compliance
2. Home Design - (concerns raised about height, massing, grading, color and materials)
Possible Action: Adoption of new CC&R’s/Property Purchase Agreement for the 22-lot subdivision
or default to Logan City LDC compliance
3. Trail Alignment - (concerns raised about straight roads adjacent to naturalistic trails)
Possible Action: Coordinate Public Works with park designers to revise sewer access roads with
park trail alignment
4. 1500 N. Traffic Crosswalk - (concerns raised about traffic speeds &amp; safe trail crosswalk)
Possible Action: Coordinate Public Works/Police/Parks & Recreation Departments on street design
and enforcement.

Ms. Simmonds emailed the Police Chief and asked for a traffic study to be done on 1500 North.

CONDITIONS OF APPROVAL
1. All standard conditions of approval are recorded and available in the Community Development
   Department.
2. 22 residential building lots are approved with this subdivision permit with two remainder lots of
   10.44 acres and 12.67 acres.
3. A note shall be added to the final plat informing future buyers of the additional regulations
   concerning lands above 4850’ elevation and steep slope development in the Land
   Development Code (LDC) Critical Lands section.
4. The final plat shall be recorded within one (1) year of this action or comply with LDC 17.58
   Expirations and Extensions of Time.
5. Before the recording of a final plat or issuance of a building permit, the Director of Community
   Development shall receive a written memorandum from the following departments indicating
   that their requirement has been satisfied:
     a. Engineering
         i. Coordinate with Deer Crest Subdivision owner(s) for connection of sewer-to-sewer main in
            1700 North. This was considered a private line during the development of the Deer Crest
            subdivision due to not being in a City Road
        ii. Storm Water detention/retention to be modified to accommodate the new MS4 requirements
            of the 90th percentile storm
       iii. The development shall incorporate LID post-construction best management practices per
            the new MS4 stormwater permit
       iv. The plat shall show all steep hazard areas as defined by the Land Development Code.
            Special reviews and approvals shall be required by Public Works and Building Department
            before any building permits being issued to ensure a safe building site and access to parcels
            affected by this hazard
        v. Provide 50’drainage easement through north drainage channel (this is located between the
            cul-de-sac lots and the existing Phase II Deer Crest Subdivision)


       vi. Design of City infrastructure (roads, curb, gutter, sidewalks, water, sewer, and stormwater
            shall be submitted and approved before final recordation of the plat
     b. Water/Cross Connection
         i. Any landscape irrigation systems fed from Logan City water must have an air gap or high-
            hazard rated backflow assembly and be tested for city’s protection.
        ii. Any building/house that has Logan City water serving it that is three stories above grade
            must have a minimum Double Check Backflow Assembly (ASSE1015) installed and tested
            on the water main before any possible branch offs or connections.
     c. Environmental (Waste Management)
         i. Residential carts will be provided.
FINDINGS FOR APPROVAL
1. The subdivision is compatible with surrounding land uses and will not interfere with the use and
   enjoyment of adjacent properties as the subdivision meets the minimum requirements of the
   Land Development Code (LDC)
2. Each lot conforms to the requirements of Land Development Code Title 17 in terms of lot size
   and development requirements.
3. Each lot is suitable for development within the Neighborhood Residential (NR-4) zone.
4. The project conforms to the requirements of Title 17.47 concerning hearings, procedures,
   application requirements, and plat preparations.
5. The project meets the goals and objectives of the Logan General Plan and the Hillcrest
   Neighborhood Plan.
6. The project met the minimum public noticing requirements of the Land Development Code and
   the Municipal Code.

Moved: T. Nielson Seconded: R. Croshaw       Approved: 5-0
