3.             REPORTS


               3. 1 Police Department Report— Chief Brady Cottam


Chief Cottam presented the quarterly crime and activity report, noting no dramatic increases in
any crime category. He highlighted a continued downward trend in DUI incidents, attributing this
in part to the widespread availability of rideshare services. Vehicle theft was noted as a continued
area of focus. The quarterly report showed no homicides in the period, a positive outcome for a
city of Taylorsville' s size. Chief Cottam also reviewed traffic accident data by district and provided
maps showing the geographic distribution of incidents, concentrated largely along Redwood Road,
4700 South, 5400 South, and 6200 South.


Chief Cottam presented a detailed breakdown of citations issued, evidence management statistics,
GRAMA request volumes, victim advocate activity, and priority call response times, which
improved compared to the prior quarter.

Chief Cottam then reported on the Shield 616 initiative, a nonprofit organization that raised funds
to provide law enforcement officers with enhanced rifle-resistant body armor plates. He explained
that the standard soft Kevlar vest worn by officers provided protection against handgun rounds but
not high-powered rifles. Through Shield 616' s fundraising efforts and support from the city, the
department received nine rifle- rated armor plates, with five more anticipated. Each plate carrier
cost approximately $ 2, 700. Chief Cottam passed the equipment around for council members to

examine. Council Member Barbieri shared her impressions from attending the Shield 616 event,
expressing admiration for the founder's story and the weight and effectiveness of the armor. The
department's goal was to equip all 35 patrol officers with enhanced plates.

                   3. 1. 1     Swearing in of Police Officers

5.             PLANNING MATTERS


               5. 1      Ordinance No. 26- 06 Consideration of an Ordinance of the City of Taylorsville
                         Adopting a Zoning Text Amendment to Section 13.44.040, Related to the
                         Manner of Height Measurement for Single- Family Attached Buildings withing
                         the   SSD- R Taylor Villas Zoning District.— Terryne Bergeson


Planner Terryne Bergeson presented Ordinance No. 26- 06, a proposed zoning text amendment
affecting height measurement standards for townhouse buildings within the Taylor Villas
subdivision. She provided background explaining that the Taylor Villas SSD- R zoning district was
established in 2024, with construction proceeding on both single- family detached homes and
townhouse    units.
                    During permit review, staff identified that the townhouse building plans
measured height from the top of the foundation rather than from average finished grade to the peak
of the roof as required, resulting in heights ranging from slightly above 35 feet to approximately
37 feet, exceeding             the 35- foot   ordinance    standard.

Ms. Bergeson explained that staff proposed amending the height measurement standard for
townhouses only within this specific district to measure from average finished grade to the
midpoint of the highest roof surface, consistent with language in the 2021 International Residential
Code. This change would not affect the single-family detached homes within the district, which
were verified to comply with existing standards. The alternative, if the ordinance were denied,
would require the removal and replacement of roofs already under construction.

The Planning Commission voted 6- 0 to forward a favorable recommendation following a properly
noticed public hearing. No comments in opposition were received. Council Member Harker
confirmed that approving the amendment would not change the physical appearance or
construction of the buildings. Council Member Burgess noted the importance of ensuring the
corrected measurement standard would prevent similar discrepancies on future projects.

          7. 1 Resolution No. 26- 12 A Resolution of the City of Taylorsville Designating
               Certain Areas Where Orders to Disperse Are Authorized and Gang
                Loitering is Prohibited— Chief Cottom &          Lt. Nate Hutchinson


Chief Cottam introduced Lt. Nate Hutchinson of the Utah Metro Gang Unit to present on
Resolution No. 26- 12, which renewed the gang loitering prohibition ordinance specific to the
Taylorsville Dayzz event at Valley Regional Park. Lt. Hutchinson explained that gang- related
incidents at city celebrations across the county had been a recurring problem, citing a quadruple
homicide at West Fest the prior year as an example of the severity of the risk. The ordinance
allowed officers to ask documented gang members to leave the designated area and, ifthey refused,
to proceed with an arrest. Lt. Hutchinson noted that the criteria for documenting gang membership
are   defined   by state statute, providing   a   built- in check against arbitrary enforcement.
