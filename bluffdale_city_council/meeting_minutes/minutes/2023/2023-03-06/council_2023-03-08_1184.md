       2.2     Healthy Biuffdale Coalition Organization and Grant Discussion. (Julia Glade
               and Caroline Marino from the Salt Lake County Health Department),

Julia Glade and Caroline Marino from the Salt Lake County Health Department were present to
discuss a grant that was awarded to Biuffdale City. Ms. Glade shared information about the
Coalitions Lite grant that Biuffdale received. She explained that Commxmity Coalition
frameworks help community members engage in a structured process to identify what is important
to their community and how to work together to efficiently and effectively make an impact.
Community coalitions are strategic planning processes where there is an assessment and priorities
and strategies identified. There are several evidence-based frameworks and structured processes
that guide the development and activities of a Community Coalition. Those frameworks can be
fairly complex, expensive, and time-consuming. As a result. Coalitions Lite was established. It
provides a research-based framework that is more efficient due to the shortened timeframe. This
is a pilot program that is being tried out in the community.

For Coalitions Lite, there is a grant and application process. For those who apply, there are
eligibility requirements. Ms. Glade reviewed the following requirements:

   • There needs to be a Community Coalition;
   • The Coalition must be formed or ready to be formed;
   • Stakeholders, partners, and key leaders must be at least aware and supportive of the
     Coalition;
   • The Coalition must focus on an aspect ofa healthy community;
   • The Coalition must have a minimum of five sectors represented and committed (i.e.
     residents, parents, schools, local government,relevant non-profit organizations); and
   • All funding must be spent to carry out the Coalitions Lite strategic planning process.

Ms. Marino reported that $35,000 was awarded and would be paid out in two installments. One
would be paid out upon execution of the contract and the second in mid-June. There were also
reporting requirements, which were outlined in the contract. Since the money was coming through
the Salt Lake County Health Department from the State, there was a requirement to report through
the State's database. There would also be post-meeting surveys. Everyone who participates in
Coalition Meetings will fill out a briefsurvey afterward. Since it is a pilot program, it is important
to understand what is working and is not working. Biuffdale would be asked for notes and plans
as well as one hour per month to discuss the process.

Ms. Marino presented an overview of the process and explained that there are six steps and
meetings associated with each step. The first meeting would focus on the timeline at which time
the assessment process would begin. There would be community discussions emd data collection
to better understand the priorities. From there, strategies would be determined to address the
selected priorities. There would need to be communication about the plan so residents and other
stakeholders would be informed. It is a fairly straightforward strategic planning process that will
take four to eight months, which is less time than required by other frameworks. She noted that it
would be possible to walk away with clear goals and strategies.

Mayor Hall reported that the Healthy Bluffdale Coalition applied for a grant to write a Strategic
Plan. Based on the presentation, she assumed that Staffsupport would be needed since data would
need to be collected and there would be an education component. She asked how many meetings
would be needed to create a Strategic Plan. Ms. Marino responded that the framework outlines six
separate meetings. The Community Coalition would do a lot of the work. The Salt Lake City
Health Department would also be able to assist in obtaining the data and presenting it to the
Coalition. She stated that it would be beneficial to have a member of Staff plan and facilitate the
meetings. This would ensure that all the material is worked through.

Council Member Crockett reported that Healthy Bluffdale involves members of the community.
The initial brainstorming had been done. The grant was applied for so that a Strategic Plan could
be created and there would be a framework in place. Once that is established, it would be possible
for anyone to run the program and it could be passed on for years to come. Her concern was that
some of those who are currently involved may not want to take on the responsibilities associated
with the grant.

Mayor Hall wondered ifit was possible to take some ofthe grant money and use it for Staffsupport.
City Attorney, James Dunkelberger believed there needed to be some determination about how the
City can spend the money. He believed a separate body set up within the City should facilitate
Healthy Bluffdale and spend the money. To determine how the money is spent, it would be best
to hold a meeting and implement a more formal process.

Mr. Reid asked for examples ofthe types ofstrategies that could come out ofthis type of process.
Ms. Marino clarified that the Coalition will determine what the focus will be. It is broad to start
and then there would be more specific decisions made going forward. For example, if the broad
area ofinterest is walkability, over time it would be possible to determine certain neighborhoods
that need attention and create strategies to address those needs. The Coalition will make the
determination and look at a wide range of strategies. The $35,000 would allow the Coalition to
move through the strategic planning process.

Mr. Reid wondered what types of projects other coalitions have done in relation to mental health.
Ms. Glade shared information about different coalitions she has worked with. She explained that
if the broad issue is mental health, it is necessary to determine what the focus should be such as
suicide prevention, opioid use, or mental health issues in older populations. The group would

Salt Lake County Council Member, Sheldon Stewart, introduced himself and reported that he
represents Salt Lake County in District 5, which includes Bluffdale. The bill from the Legislative
Session that would have the most impact on Bluffdale is Senate Bill ("S.B.") 260. There was an
attachment to that bill related to mental health. A lot of work had been done to remove that and
clarify language related to funding for the Utah Transit Authority("UTA"). He referenced the 5^^
of a 5'^ that the County would have to enact. When an attempt was made to pass that through a
ballot initiative, it failed in Salt Lake and Utah counties. His concern with UTA was that nothing
happened in the southwest. Additionally, nothing in the southwest was added to the 10-Year Plan.
Riverton Mayor, Trent Staggs, had been very vocal about that.

Mr. Stewart shared information about House Bill("H.B.")374, which relates to the Unified Police
Department("UPD"). The reason that bill was something he supported was because the County
currently utilizes shared services, however, the delivery of services does not include Bluffdale,
Riverton, Herriman, or South Jordan. It all goes into UPD cities. As an example, in the Gang

serious gang issue, he believed the issues were likely comparable to other cities. He reiterated that
there is a disparity in the service and numbers.

Council Member Kallas discussed the 5*^ of a 5^^ and noted that the bulk went toward UTA and
there is no UTA service in Bluffdale. There have been requests to be included in the plan but that
had not happened. Bluffdale residents pay the full tax and receive a smaller portion of their
contribution.

         8.1    Possible Discussion Regarding a Special Service District for Police and/or Fire.
                (StaffPresenters* Bruce Kartchner, and James Dunkelbereer\

City Attorney, James Dunkelberger reported that Staff prepared information for the Council
outlining altemate Public Safety funding options. Rather than passing ordinances with increases
or having an increased budget for Public Safety from the General Fund, it was necessary to
consider other options. He reviewed the following two options:

     • Utah Code Title 10 - Special Fund or Special Levy:
           o Utah Code Title 10 regulated municipalities. Within Title 10, there was a section
              that allowed the governing body of a city to make a special levy where those levy
              taxes would be placed in a special fund. The special fund would be a separate line
              item on the budget, but would otherwise function largely within the existing
              structure of the city. It would be created by Resolution, drafted by the City
              Attorney, and brought before the City Council for consideration.
     • Special Service Area or Special Service District:
           o Utah Code Title 17B. A Special Service Area and Special Service District would
              work effectively the same way within the City from a pragmatic standpoint. Other
              cities, such as Riverton and Herriman, had Special Service Areas in place.

Mr. Reid clarified that a Special Service Area creates an area that is the City boundary. He noted
that a Special Service District typically has multiple boundaries. The functions are similar,
however, in this case, a Special Service District does not need to be discussed because a Special
Service Area would take care of everything that is needed for Bluffdale. Mr. Dunkelberger
reported that there had been previous discussions with the Council about Special Service Areas
and Special Service Districts. The Special Fund option was introduced at the last meeting but was

not discussed in detail. Essentially, it was another option to earmark funds for public safety and
remove them from the General Fund. Mr. Reid noted that there had been discussions about
separate funds within the General Fund such as the Arts Council and Miss Bluffdale, which are
General Fund sub-funds. The Special Levy would be a separate fund that only addresses Public
Safety needs. Additional information would be shared later in the meeting.

Mayor Hall asked which cities have done Special Service Areas. Mr. Dunkelberger reported that
Riverton has both a Law Enforcement and Fire Protection Area. There was one for each. Herriman
did so as well. South Salt Lake does not have an area but uses the Special Levy in a separate fund.
He pointed out that the South Salt Lake City Attorney had been very helpful and shared information
about their process.

Mr. Dunkelberger reported that both funding options would start with a resolution. Ifthe Council
decides to move forward with a Special Service Area, it was recommended that one more attomey
be hired to serve as a Consultant for that process. There are only a few attorneys in the area who
have experience with the Special Service Areas. He noted that there had been correspondence with
one such attomey by the name of Rachel Anderson. Ifthe Council was interested in pursuing that
option, Mr. Dunkelberger would do the drafting and Ms. Anderson could provide additional
expertise.

Mr. Dunkelberger described what would happen if a resolution was passed that the residents do
not support. Under Title 17, there is a specific procedure for "adequate protest." If there is a
certain threshold of landowners in the district who are opposed to the resolution, the creation of
the district could be halted. With respect to a Title 10 Special Fund or Special Levy,there was no
particular statutory protest procedure but it was subject to a referendum.

In terms of organization, a Special Service Area is a separate entity. The Special Fund, however,
is simply a separate fund. Mr. Kartchner noted that for a Special Service Area or Special Service
District, there would be a separate Board and potentially separate administration. There are
additional costs associated with the Special Service Area that differ from the Special Fund. The
Special Fund essentially falls under the existing organization. Mr. Dunkelberger reported that the
Special Service Area Board is referred to as the Board ofTrustees and there are different mles for
appointments. He explained that Council Members can serve as trustees but there are mles that go
along with those appointments.

Mr. Dunkelberger presented information regarding accounting for both options. For a Special
Service Area, there would be a separate budget and audit process. The Special Fund would use
existing City infrastmcture. Meetings would likely be similar to what is done with the LBA and
RDA. The statute specifies that the Special Service Area can use City facilities but there is a
requirement to pay for the services and facilities. That was an area where there would be potential
complications to be considered. There was discussion regarding the administrative process for the
