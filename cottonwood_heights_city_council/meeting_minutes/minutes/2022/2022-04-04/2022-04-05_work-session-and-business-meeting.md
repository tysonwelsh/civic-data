2.      LEGISLATIVE REPORT – City Lobbyists Greg Curtis, Brian Allen, and Chantel Nate.

Mayor Weichers reported that this would be the final report related to the Legislative Session. City
Lobbyists, Greg Curtis and Brian Allen were present via Zoom and Chantel Nate was present in
the City Council Work Room. Ms. Nate reported that the Legislative Session had been busy and
quite a few bills dealt with local authority and local land use authority. House Bill (“H.B.”) 36 –
Commission on Housing Affordability changed the structure of the Commission on Housing
Affordability and added a State Homeless Coordinator to the Commission. H.B. 146 – Local
Licensing Amendments modified licensing and inspection requirements for food trucks. Food
trucks would not require background checks unless it was an ice cream truck. The bill also requires
local governments to accept licensing from other entities. She explained that this means that food
trucks do not need to be licensed in every municipality to operate.

H.B. 151 – Retail Facility Incentive Payment Amendments prohibited incentives unless the project
is located in an area where 51% or more residents are at or below 70% of the county median
income. There could be a mixed-used development with one housing unit for every 1,250 square
feet of retail space and at least 10% of the units needed to qualify as moderate-income housing.
Any retail facility must be less than 20,000 square feet. Council Member Birrell wondered if there
were areas like that in Cottonwood Heights. It was noted that the language applies to development.
If a developer wanted that retail incentive, certain criteria needed to be met.

Ms. Nate explained that there are two competing bills related to water-wise landscaping. H.B. 282
– Water Wise Landscaping Amendments ultimately passed. She reported that local governments
cannot pass an ordinance prohibiting water-wise landscaping. This was due to drought and water
issues in the State. Mayor Weichers noted that the City was fairly happy with the bill that passed.
In the beginning, there were strict requirements, but ultimately, the bill stated that the City could
not prohibit someone from xeriscaping. It included more flexibility than the originally proposed
bill. Ms. Nate noted that one issue connected to the other water-wise landscaping bill was that a

large community development would not have been allowed to have much turf. There needed to
be enough flexibility to allow outdoor recreation spaces in large developments.

H.B. 303 – Local Land Use Amendments was discussed. Ms. Nate reported that the bill changed
the noticing requirements for annexations and zoning changes. For zoning changes, information
about the change needed to be posted on the Utah Public Notice website. Mr. Allen explained that
the bill started badly but the amendments had made it far better.

H.B. 357 – Eminent Domain Appraisal Amendments required plaintiffs to obtain an additional
appraisal if more than 90 days had passed since an offer was made. The amendment allowed the
defendant to waive the additional appraisal if desired. Ms. Nate noted that the bill could have been
problematic but they were able to successfully include amendments that would add more flexibility
to the process. City Manager, Tim Tingey noted that it could be difficult to schedule an appraisal
during those processes. Additional appraisal requirements could extend the process further. The
bill would provide additional protections to both the municipality and the homeowner. Council
Member Birrell asked why a homeowner would have attorney fees if a house was being taken by
eminent domain. Mr. Tingey explained that an attorney could better navigate the process.

Ms. Nate reported that H.B. 440 – Homeless Services Amendments would greatly affect local
governments. That bill created provisions for Councils of Governments to develop a plan for
overflow facilities and allow capacity changes to existing facilities under certain circumstances.
Mr. Tingey noted that a lot of information had been communicated about the bill. Recently, the
Mayor was in a meeting with the Conference of Mayors and some decisions would be made about
the requirements. Mayor Weichers explained that the Conference of Mayors had until September
1, 2022, to come up with a plan and would meet every month until that time. Council Member
Birrell asked if there would be any direct consequences for Cottonwood Heights. Mayor Weichers
did not believe there would be as the City did not have the necessary facilities. The bill stated that
an overflow option needs to be created and some cities had already come forward.

H.B. 462 – Utah Housing Affordability Amendments was a comprehensive bill that required
municipalities to adopt moderate-income housing provisions into the General Plan. Ms. Nate
explained that the bill started in a very different place than it ended up. She felt that it ended up
in a more positive place and the bill would be beneficial for both cities and citizens. Mr. Allen
reported that the Utah League of Cities and Towns (“ULCT”) pushed hard on the bill to remove a
lot of the mandates and simplify the language. It was not a perfect bill, but much less severe than
it was originally and would allow for some flexibility.

Senate Bill (“S.B.”) 110 – Water as Part of a General Plan required water use and preservation to
be part of the General Plan for a city. S.B. 140 – Housing and Transit Reinvestment Zone
Amendments allowed housing and transit reinvestment zones around light rail and bus rapid transit
facilities. Ms. Nate felt the bill ended up in a better place than it started, as there was a bit more
flexibility. Each transit hub was very different and the final version of the bill gave the cities added
flexibility to determine how development around each of those transit hubs would look. Council
Member Birrell wondered if TRAX was considered rapid transit. This was confirmed. It was also
noted that FrontRunner would also fit into the definition.

3.      REVIEW OF BUSINESS MEETING AGENDA – Mayor Mike Weichers.

Mayor Weichers reviewed the Business Meeting Agenda. There were two items on the agenda
that would have public comment periods. Item 5.1 was related to the Fort Union rezone and
Community and Economic Development Director, Michael Johnson would present.

Item 5.2 pertained to the On-Street Permit Parking Code. Mr. Johnson explained that there had
been discussions at the last City Council Meeting about the topic. Based on Council feedback,
there were some proposed changes to the Permit Parking Ordinance. Rather than being called the
Permit Parking Petition Ordinance, it would be retitled to Permit Parking Administration. The
proposal was to remove the authority for the Council and residents to create a permit parking area,
which was an area where some could park but others could not. A lot of the language in the
previous ordinance related to the petition process had been removed. Existing areas could be
administered and modified but new areas could not be created.

Mayor Weichers reported that the Business Meeting Agenda would also include two Staff Reports.
One would be a Police Report from Lieutenant Dan Bartlett and the other would be a Public Works
Report from Public Works Director, Matt Shipp. There was one action item on the agenda, which
was the consideration of Ordinance 378 – Amending the City’s Code of Ordinances regarding
water-wise landscaping standards. Mr. Johnson explained that the ordinance had been reviewed
by the Planning Commission. In January 2022, the Commission unanimously recommended
approval. It would allow City residents to remain eligible for landscape conversion subsidy monies
that came from State-level water districts. For instance, the Central Utah Water Conservancy
District. To remain eligible for funding, all participating cities need to adopt their own ordinances.

Mr. Johnson reported that the ordinance applies to new construction and does not affect existing
residences. It also limits the amount of wasteful lawn that can be installed in a new single-family
residential property. The focus was to limit lawn to activity areas. Mr. Johnson shared the lawn
requirements, which include limiting lawns on single-family properties to 35% of the landscaped
area. The same type of restriction would apply to multi-family and commercial developments but
there was an exception. If a multi-family development has an activity area, such as a park or
playfield, it would be exempt from the requirement.

        a.       Arts Council Proposed Sculpture – Culture Manager, Ann Eatchel.

Culture Manager, Ann Eatchel presented the Staff Report related to the Arts Council sculpture.
She shared photographs with the Council as well as background information. Ms. Eatchel reported
that the Arts Council determined that it was important to have a piece of art outside of City Hall.
In November 2020, the Arts Council issued a Request for Proposals (“RFP”) to find an artist for
the sculpture. The artist was commissioned to do a sculpture of a tree. However, the concept and
the piece did not match and the artist was asked to create something different. The Arts Council
came up with a different idea for the sculpture, which was a Cottonwood leaf.

Ms. Eatchel reported that the artist was still in the process of building the sculpture. The leaf
sculpture would be 6’ x 5’ and the stem would be approximately three feet. She noted that the
sculpture is made of recycled metal and will be mounted on a large boulder. The boulder would
likely be 5’ x 6’. The boulder needed to be the same coloring as the City Hall Building so that it
would blend in well. The leaf would be drilled into the boulder. Mr. Eatchel noted that Public
Works would prep the area where the statue will be placed. The boulder will be placed and the
artist would install the cottonwood leaf. It would be up to the Arts Council to determine whether
a plaque or lighting should also be added.

There was discussion regarding the placement of the boulder. Ms. Eatchel explained that it was
important to consider views as well as the placement of the sprinkler system. Council Member
Petersen was excited about the sculpture design. Ms. Eatchel reported that the sculpture would
cost $14,000. In fiscal year 2021, the Arts Council funded half of the cost and the other half would
come out of the Arts Council budget for fiscal year 2022. She noted that the sculpture was titled,
“Awareness of Art and Nature.” It would likely be installed in May 2022.

Mr. Shipp reported that there had been previous discussions related to the Public Works Building.

with the building design. However, there was discussion at the time about two different options -

back and discuss those options with the Council. Mr. Shipp shared an artist’s rendering of what
the front of the Public Works Building would look like. An image of the existing Public Works

as the Public Works trailers. New additions would include office space, areas for future garages,
wash bays, and storage areas. It would house all of the snowplows and other equipment.

Option #2 would start over on the yard design to maximize space. Mr. Shipp noted that some of
the suggestions pertained to improving the storage shed for the salt and adding additional bays so
that everything could be parked inside of a garage. Council Member Birrell asked about the size
of the fleet. Mr. Shipp reported that there are approximately 22 pieces of equipment. The intention

regulation to the zoning ordinance pertaining to future residential and commercial development.
It pertained to regulating irritation, landscaped beds, lawn, and lawn areas to create more
efficiency. Mr. Johnson reported that ordinances like this one are required for cities to remain
eligible for landscape conversion subsidy programs. The water district that oversees Cottonwood
Heights is the Central Utah Water Conservancy District which offers reimbursements for residents
who convert their lawns to more water-wise landscaping.

Numerous other cities adopted similar ordinances, including Riverton, Herriman, and West Jordan.
The ordinance limits the amount of lawn that is permitted. The intention was that the grass would
be purposefully designed to be used for gathering areas. Mr. Johnson shared a sample image and
explained that in the future, new development would have a better balance of lawn and planting
beds that do not use as much culinary water. There are already park strip landscaping standards
in place. The amendments would modify those slightly to remain eligible for the program.
