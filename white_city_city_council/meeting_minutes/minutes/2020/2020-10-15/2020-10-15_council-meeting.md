Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) Ordinance

                Jim McNulty, Planning Manager, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District
(GSLMSD), presented a PowerPoint presentation regarding accessory dwelling units (ADU). He
reviewed the differences between ADU’s and duplexes, what type of updates a home would need
to allow for an ADU, How ADU’s connect to utilities, and what the average home and lot sizes are
in White City. He also reviewed the standards that are used for ADU’s and why ADU’s are
recommended to be a permitted use, owner occupancy, parking, square footage requirements,
and if the ADU’s should be limited in White City.

Transportation Chapter of the General Plan

               Mikala Jordan, Planner, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (GSLMSD),
stated that the long-range planning website is up and running. It has a summary of the general
plan process, planning milestones so far, vision statement, explanation of the different parts of
the process, priorities that were voted on by White City residents, technical assessment, moderate
income housing plan, and the transportation plan will be added when it is finished. There will also
be information on how the public can get involved and a spot for public feedback. She will keep
the website updated as the process goes along. She reviewed Utah State Code section 10-9a-
403.

               The Council reviewed the transportation plan and made various recommendations.
Overall the plan is good with some technical changes that are needed.

              Paul Ashton recommended emailing Ms. Jordan with any changes that needed
to be completed.

               Ms. Jordan stated she will take the Council’s feedback to the Steering Committee.

CARES Act Funds

                Council Member Little stated White City was given just over $320,000 from the
CARES Act funding. The biggest impact in White City is education. In the spring schools switched
to distance learning because of COVID-19. This was hard on families that did not have access to
computers or tablets especially families with multiple children and parents working from home.
The schools have limited supplies of Chromebooks that were being shared between classes, now
children are not allowed to share items in class. He would like to see White City CARES Act
funding go to the purchase of Chromebooks for children that reside in White City Metro Township.
Chromebooks are approximately $225.00. Another option is to work with the Canyons Foundation
that provides scholarships and they are willing to be involved in the process and would let us
potentially give them funding.

               The Council believes it would be a good idea to look into options.
