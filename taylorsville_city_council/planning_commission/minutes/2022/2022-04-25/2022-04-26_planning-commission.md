Conditional Use

     3.         8C22 – Dream Venue (Miriam Grando) - Consideration of a Final Non-Administrative Conditional Use
                Permit to Operate a “Reception Center” on the Property Located at 3193 West 4700 South, as well as
                consideration of Extended Hours of Operation. (Karyn Kerdolff/GIS Planner)

3.1                   Ms. Karyn Kerdolff presented this item. She stated this was previously presented and tabled due
                to applicant not being present to answer questions. Ms. Kerdolff advised a reception center in a
                Professional office Zone requires a non-administrative conditional use permit hearing before the
                Planning Commission. There are no particular land use concerns regarding this proposal.

3.2 FINDINGS: (Ms. Kerdolff review the following Findings with the Commissioners)
        3.2.1  This application was initiated by Miriam Grando.
        3.2.2  Per 13.08.020, a Reception Center in the PO Zone requires a Non-administrative Conditional Use
               Permit granted by the Planning Commission.
        3.2.2  The applicant is requesting to stay open until 1:00 A.M. on Thursday, Friday, and Saturday nights.
        3.2.3  The property is located within 250’ of a residential zone which requires an hours of operation
               extension granted by the Planning Commission.
        3.2.4  The applicant will not be serving alcohol and is not requesting an alcohol permit at this time.
        3.2.5  Conditional Use Permits are governed by Utah State Code and must meet the provisions outlined
               in 10-9a-507.
        3.2.6  A similar request for a Reception Center was approved at this location by the Planning
               Commission on November 13, 2018.

3.3 STAFF RECOMMENDATION: Staff recommends approval of the Reception Center and hours of operation
    extension request based on the information presented at the time of the Staff Report.

3.4 CONDITIONS OF APPROVAL:
       3.4.1  The applicant complies with all requirements of all applicable reviewing agencies.
       3.4.2  The Conditional Use Permit is subject to review and revocation upon substantiated complaint or
              citations.
       3.4.3  Any changes to the structure will require a building permit.
       3.4.4  The applicant shall remain compliant with all business licensing requirements.
       3.4.5  The business may only extend hours of operation to 1:00 A.M. on Thursday, Friday, and Saturday
              nights. Otherwise, the business hours are limited to 10:00 P.M.
       3.4.6  [Changed during meeting] Alcohol is not allowed on the premise without an alcohol license
              issued by the City of Taylorsville and the DABC. If alcohol is found on the premise, the CUP will
              be revoked. No use of alcohol is allowed on this site due to it being within 250’ of a school.
       3.4.7  All parking must be on site unless a shared parking agreement is documented with the City.
       3.4.8  The business must conform to Salt Lake County’s Noise Ordinance.

3.5      DISCUSSION: Commissioner Quigley             said the applicant has said there will be no alcohol, but he
         wanted to know if once approved, they be able to come back later with a request to serve alcohol? Ms.
         Kerdolff said that would not be an issue because the use of alcohol is not allowed by Code within 250’ of
         a school. Commissioner Quigley expressed concern because the Commission has learned in working with
         7-11’s that that distance requirement can be circumvented. He just wanted to make that point.

3.6      APPLICANT ADDRESS:            The applicant, Miriam Grando advised that they will work on making cosmetic
         changes to the parking and landscaping on this site. That they will comply with all required conditions.
         That the reason for the request to extend the hours of operation was to allow for time to do proper clean-
         up after an event Commissioner Willardson expressed concern for the neighbors and did not see why a
         party or wedding should go until 1:00 a.m. Ms. Grando said they would mostly go until midnight, with the
         extra hour needed for cleaning up. Commissioner Quigley asked if she had talked to the physician with
         the office next door about possibly sharing his parking during the evening hours when he and his staff are
         not there, and she said she had not. Commissioner Quigley felt there should be some type of agreement
         in place to assure his property is protected and security maintained. Commissioner Wendel want to make
         sure the applicant knows the fire codes which limit the number of people to occupy a space. Ms. Grando
         advised there are 26 parking spaces, and the occupancy limit is 112 people. Commissioner McElreath
         wanted to know if there would be someone from her staff on site during the events or would they just
         open it up to the people renting it. Ms. Grando said no that they would give the keys to the ones renting
         it. Commissioner Russell commented that the neighbors are used to this building be in use a lot and

3.7      Commissioner Wright opened the public hearing and asked if there was anyone wishing to speak either

         Colleen Norris advised the biggest challenge is that the parking lot is not large enough to host such large

3.10     DISCUSSION:

         3.10.1    Commissioner Quigley said he only counted 20 parking stalls and not 26. That there had been a
                   similar business operation in Carriage Square a few years ago which opened with good intentions
                   but ended up losing their conditional use based on numerous police response and neighborhood
                   complaints. He could see potentially what occurred at the previous site after hours subsequently
                   happening at this one. His main concern was the lateness of the hour and potentially having the
                   activities spilling over into the adjoining neighborhood.

         3.10.2    Commissioner Wilkey was also concerned that there was not enough parking for this type of use.

         3.10.3    Commissioner Willardson agreed that there was not enough parking as proposed and he was not
                   in favor of extending the hours to 1:00 A.M. as being too late. He felt the only way to control the
                   spread of parking is for the doctor next door to put some type of chain across his driveway.

         3.10.4    Commissioner Wendel said that historically this location has been approved as a reception
                   center. She felt that if that previous use had created problems for the neighbors more of them
                   would have turned out tonight to comment.             The Planning Commission has been trying not
                   to overpark in the City and felt it was the obligation of the owner to only book events they could
                   accommodate. She felt there was a need to be more trusting of business owners. She was in
                   favor of honoring the applicant’s request and was hopeful that they make a great success of their
                   business. Worst case scenario is if someone doesn’t do what they are supposed to then the
                   owner must take ways to mitigate the situation in order to prevent their conditional use permit
                   from being revoked. She suggested possibly negotiating not having such late hours on Thursdays
                   and possibly reevaluate the hours of operation again in six months.

         3.10.5    Commissioner McElreath said he agreed with Commissioner Wendel’s comments. That this was
                   previously approved for this same type of use, and it is the responsibility of the present business
                   owner to deal with any subsequent issues. He was in favor of approving this application, adding
                   that he was not in favor of allowing alcohol use.

         3.10.6    Commissioner Russell was apprehensive of this use but did support approval.

         3.10.7    Commissioner Quigley added that Commissioner Wendel made an excellent point in trusting
                   business owners more. He thought her suggestion to lessen the hours on Thursdays was a good
                   one. He also supported letting people do what they want with their property as long as it meets
                   Code.

Subdivision

  4.          9S21 – Taylor Smith/Brandon Snarr - Consideration of an Amendment to a Planning Commission
              Condition of Approval Related to Requested Fencing Adjacent to a Canal for a preliminary
              Subdivision Plat at Approximately 4924 South 3200 West. (Jim Spung/AICP/Senior Planner)

4.1      This item was presented by Jim Spung. Brandon Snarr, property owner, is requesting the Planning
         Commission reconsider a condition of approval related to required fencing adjacent to the South Jordan
         Canal for a preliminary subdivision plat at approximately 4924 South 3200 West, Taylorsville, Utah. The
         preliminary subdivision for File 9S21 was reviewed and approved (subject to conditions) by the Planning
         Commission on February 22, 2022. The motion for that item follows:

         4.1.1      “I move that we approve File #9S21 a Preliminary Subdivision Plat “GT Estates” for approximately
                    1,972 acres of real property, as identified in Exhibit E, to include 20 percent for flag lots subject
                    to the Findings and Conditions outlined in the Staff Report and to include that the plat indicate
                    that the lots next to the private drive are responsible for maintenance of that private drive and
                    what is on current Lot #9, the storm drain is the responsibility of the owner of Lot #9.” The
                    motion passed 5-2. Following is Condition of Approval #1: “A 6-foot-tall wrought iron fence or a
                    masonry wall satisfying the standards in Section 13.23.070, shall be constructed along the
                    northern edge of the subdivision adjacent to the irrigation canal.”

         4.1.2      The applicant is requesting the Planning Commission remove/modify Condition #1 requiring the
                    developer to construct a 6’foot tall wrought iron fence or masonry wall along the north property
                    line, adjacent to the South Jordan Canal.

         4.1.3      The reason for the request is that the applicant has received a significant financial loss with having
                    to remove one lot due to the flag lot percentage being too high on the original plan per Code and
                    the significant difference in the price of the wrought iron/masonry wall fencing versus vinyl.

         4.1.4      The Land Development Code designates the “Director” as the approval authority for fencing
                    adjacent to canals; however, the Planning Commission is the decision-making body for
                    preliminary plats Since this request is associated with a Planning Commission condition of
                    approval, the Planning Commission is the authority to modify or amend a condition of approval
                    associated with a prior action.

4.2      FINDINGS:
         4.2.1  On February 22, 2022, the Planning Commission approved a preliminary subdivision plat for File
                #9S21, as outlined in the meeting minutes.
         4.2.2  Condition of Approval #1 for File #9S21 requires that a 6-foot-tall wrought iron fence or a masonry
                wall satisfying the standards in Section 1323.070 of the Land Development Code be constructed
                along the northern edge of the subdivision adjacent to the irrigation canal.
         4.2.3  The applicant/developer is requesting the Planning Commission modify Condition of Approval #1
                to allow a 6-foot-tall non-climbable vinyl fence in lieu of the 6-foot-tall wrought iron fence or
                masonry wall.
         4.2.4  Exhibit C in the Staff Report includes statements from the applicant/developer and the South
                Jordan Canal Company supporting the request to modify Condition #1
         4.2.5  Based on the evidence and information presented by the applicant, city staff supports the
                applicant’s request to modify Condition #1.
         4.2.6  The Planning Commission is the decision-making authority for preliminary subdivision review.

4.3      CONDITIONS OF APPROVAL: Staff does not recommend any conditions of approval be associated with
         this request.

4.4      STAFF RECOMMENDATION: Based on the information provided to City Staff and the Planning
         Commission, Staff recommends the Planning Commission approve the applicant’s request to modify
         Condition of Approval #1 for File #9S21, as stated in the February 22, 2022, Planning Commission meeting
         minutes, to allow a 6-foot-tall non-climbable vinyl fence in lieu of a 6-foot-tall wrought iron fence or a
         masonry wall.

4.5      APPLICANT ADDRESS: Brandon Snarr said they are asking to be able to have a different fence type than
         was approved originally by the Planning Commission. The reason for this request is during the approval
         process they were forced to lose one lot due to the requirement that flag lots can only comprise 20 percent
         of the subdivision and they would have had 22 percent. That was a significant financial loss and having to
         install wrought iron or masonry would again increase the costs as the difference between those and vinyl
         is considerable. That inclusion of the vinyl fence meets all requirements levied by the Canal Company.


4.6      Commissioner Wright opened the public hearing and asked if there was anyone wishing to speak in
         favor or opposition to this proposal to please come forward to speak.

4.7      SPEAKING: One neighbor spoke (not identified), saying she was fine with the vinyl fence but would like
         something done with the Skunk problem. Commissioner Wendel suggested that she contact Salt Lake
         County Animal Services about the Skunk problem but thanked her for coming to the meeting and
         expressing her concerns tonight.

4.8      Commissioner Wright closed the public hearing and opened the meeting for discussion or a motion by the
         Planning Commission.

4.9      DISCUSSION: Commissioner Quigley advised he had no problem with the vinyl fence and that it made
         more sense for the developer financially. He thanked them for their efforts to make this development
         work. Commissioner Willardson expressed his concern about the vinyl being more of an attraction for
         graffiti and was informed that probably be easier to clean should there be graffiti and would be the
         responsibility of the homeowners like it is everywhere else.
