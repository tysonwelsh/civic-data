Proposed Ordinance 2023-47 amending the Ogden Municipal Code to
adopt fee for Commemorative Street Names, amending provisions related
to and establishing procedures for Commemorative Street Naming

A memo from Council staff regarding a proposed commemorative street name ordinance came before the Council for
consideration. The memo stated the Council will consider the proposed ordinance providing review criteria and procedures for
proposed commemorative street naming’s. The proposal, previously referred to as honorary street naming, was initiated by
Council member Choberka in January 2021. A commemorative street name is a name that is in addition to the standard street
name that may be assigned to commemorate a person, place, cultural group, or event that has had a significant impact on the
Ogden community.

Council Deputy Director Symes summarized the memo and stated In January 2021, Council member Choberka submitted
a Council Initiative form for the development and consideration of an honorary street name ordinance. The initiative came in
response to the discussions from a petition to assign an honorary street name to a portion of 2nd Street west of Wall Avenue. The
initiative was submitted with the direction that an ordinance be drafted that would look at how and who is able to make requests
for honorary street names and to work with the Diversity Commission to review and revise the ordinance to create equity and
inclusion in the core of the process. The Council’s initiative form was developed in 2018 as a way Council members could
formally submit ideas and proposals. Once submitted, the proposal is to be reviewed by Council staff and discussed at a Council
work session. Once reviewed by the Council, Council leadership is to provide direction to staff to proceed with further
development of the policy or request a recommendation from the Planning Commission or other entity as desired by the Council.
Council staff have been working with Council member Choberka and members of the Diversity Commission, in conjunction with
legal staff, to develop a proposal aimed at meeting the direction given from the Council initiative. Council staff have met with
leadership from the Diversity Commission to receive input on the proposal several times. In addition, Council staff have led
discussions with the Council on the proposal at several work sessions over the last two years. The name of the ordinance itself
has been changed from honorary to commemorative. This change was made based on a suggestion from a community member
and was suggested as a way to allow the story and the history of Ogden to be told more fully. A commemoration can encompass
both the honoring and the remembrance of a person, place, community, or event. Commemoration allows for recognition of
significance without the high bar of bestowing an honor. Mr. Symes reviewed the timeline of the initiative since it was submitted
in January of 2021 and discussed the steps Council staff has followed and the groups they have worked with to pursue an
ordinance review and proposed amendments. He then noted the proposed ordinance builds on the existing street naming ordinance
and adds language specific to commemorative street names. He provided a summary of the significant policy points incorporated
into the proposed ordinance.

Proposed Ordinance 2023-45 amending the Ogden Municipal Code to
revise design standards for multiple-family dwellings, modifying site
development standards on commercial buildings, and amending provisions
for multiple-family dwellings and single-family rowhouses

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed zone text amendments
pertaining to multi-family residential design standards in residential zones came before the Council for consideration. The memo
stated the Ogden City Planning Division is proposing design standard changes for new multi-family residential development in
residential zones. In 2020, the Planning Division conducted an extensive study researching design standards for multi-family
residential development in commercial zones and significant changes were made as a result. The 2020 changes were specific to

multi-family design standards in commercial zones and did not apply to residential zones. As a follow-up to those changes, the
current proposal is to update the design standards for multi-family residential development in residential zones. An initial work
session was held on October 11, 2022, to review the proposed changes. At that meeting, Council members requested additional
information on options for electric vehicle parking standards. At the November 29, 2022, work session, Planning staff presented
options for EV parking for the Council’s consideration. Council members also expressed interest in input from the City’s
Sustainability Committee. At the June 20, 2023, work session, Planning staff discussed the input from the Sustainability
Committee and options for moving forward. Council direction was to move ahead with the proposed design standards:
amendments and revisit the electric vehicle standards in the future.

Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
proposed ordinance amendments, the goals of which are:

e Creating neighborhoods in which residents are involved in creating a safe, livable and attractive environment. .

e Implement architectural controls for new apartments and multi-family developments to provide for qualityand | |
attractive housing.

e Creating site and building design guidelines or alternatives for rental projects that encourage the interaction of
residents, create common open space and encourage pedestrian orientation.

Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
proposed ordinance amendments and the Planning Commission’s recommendation regarding the petition; the current Ogden City
residential vacation rental ordinance, according to stated spacing requirements, allows one vacation rental per block face. On May
2, 2023, council considered a proposal which would amend the residential vacation rental text to the benefit of the newly restored
Bristol Manor at 2480 25" Street. The petitioner, Jason Boal, sought for all eight of the Bristol Manor’s condominiums to be
eligible as residential vacation rentals, and proposed necessary criteria for the mansion (and future prospective properties) to be
able to do so:

¢ The building is on the local or national historic registry.
e The building is located along the BRT route.

e The building contains eight dwelling units of fewer.

e The dwelling units are condominiums.

The Planning Commission reviewed the proposal, as summarized above, and forwarded a unanimous recommendation of
denial to the City Council, stating that the zoning ordinance amendment was not consistent with the General Plan policies.
Further, the Commission claimed that the amendment would remove housing options for the area, contrary to one of the
development goals in the East Central Community Plan. The Council again reviewed the item on June 6 and further expressed a
desire to assist the petitioner in their goals. At the request of Council, Administration created the proposed zoning ordinance
amendment, which would allow up to two condominium units in a single building to be treated as a single residential vacation
rental, assuming that the building:

e Has fewer than nine residential units,

e Was condominiumized prior to January 1, 2023,

e Is located along the Ogden Express (OGX) Route, and

e Is an historic building on the Ogden City Register of Historic Resources.

Mr. Symes concluded the current proposal is to amend Ogden Municipal Code to permit a locally registered historic
building that is located along the Ogden Express (OGX) route, was converted to condominiums prior to January 1, 2023, and
contains fewer than nine units, to have two non-owner-occupied residential vacation rental units in the same building. Relative to
the petition, the Planning Commission unanimously recommended denial to the City Council. The recommendation was based
largely on the zoning ordinance amendment not being consistent with the General Plan policies. The East Central Community
Plan states that transit-oriented development includes higher density residential development, commercial and business services
within walking distance of the stops. According to the Commission, there is a need for housing along the BRT route, and
amending the ordinance would remove housing options in the area.

Council member White asked Mr. Brierley to address the recommendation to change the parking requirements for this
type of use. Mr. Brierley stated the Planning Commission has not recommended that the parking requirements be changed;
however, the petitioner has asked for an adjustment because their property has eight units, which would require 16 parking spaces,
but there is not sufficient space on the property for that many parking spaces. He added the Planning Commission is working on
an ordinance that would amend the vacation rental regulations to require on-site parking because parking issues ate the subject of
most of the complaints the City gets regarding vacation rentals. Chair Choberka asked how that would be enforced. Mr. Brierley
stated that enforcement would be complaint driven and the property owner would receive a citation if their tenants were parking
on the street or in areas not permitted.

Council member Blair asked if there will be a definition of the term ‘along the BRT route’ in order to determine which
properties would be subject to the proposed ordinance amendment. Mr. Brierley stated that if the Council wishes to proceed with
an ordinance that allows the requested use along the BRT route, staff will develop a map and formal definition to clearly identify
the properties that would be eligible for the requested use.
