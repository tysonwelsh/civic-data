Proposed Warehouse and Storage Unit Amendments
          Ogden City Planning is proposing several amendments meant to clarify and defme cmmnercial warehousing and personal
storage. In addition, the proposed amendments would remove the uses from the commercial zones limiting warehousing and
personal storage uses to the M-1 and M-2 zones. Ogden City posted a notice of pending ordinance on June 26, 2020. A notice of
pending ordinance limits new land use applications related to the proposed change for a period of 180 days.
          Planning Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the proposed ordinance
amendments; he first clarified the differences between warehousing and personal storage uses; warehousing is typically industrial
or commercial in nature and it has a related use for a business' storage of product intended for sale. The personal storage/self-
storage use is space for storage of personal belongings. Given recent development trends, staff is concerned that personal storage
can be lumped into the warehousing defmition and there may be pressure to convert commercial buildings to personal storage or
warehousing uses when that building becomes vacant. The tax production (sales and property) would be reduced by a conversion
of a commercial building to storage units and the use could potentially create 'dead zones' in important community building areas.
He provided a chart communicating the zones in the City where storage and warehousing uses are presently pennitted and noted
there are currently seven storage unit businesses in the C-2 zone, 12 in the M-1/M-2 zone, and three nonconforming uses in other
zones. Staff has recommended creating a defmition for personal storage units and clarify the warehousing defmition by excluding
it as an accessory to the main use. Relative to the pressure to convert vacant buildings to storage units, it would be necessary to
evaluate whether the use would still meet the defmition of commercial, which is to provide for the sale and supply of a complete
range of retail and wholesale goods, entertainment, and personal services for the area and also a center for recreational,
entertainment, and cultural activities. Businesses need interaction and activity between one another and allowing a storage unit use
in the middle of a retail or commercial center would create a 'dead zone'. The General Plan discusses rehabilitation of empty
commercial block buildings, indicating that main floors of that building should be devoted to active retail uses with upper floors
used for a range of services or residential uses. The Planning Cmrunission considered staff's recommended amendments and voted
six to two to reco1mnend approval of the following:
               • Revisions to defmition section to create defmition of personal storage units and clarification of warehousing
               • Remove personal storage and warehousing as standalone uses in C-3 zone                                        •
               •   Continue to allow warehousing and personal storage in M-1 and M-2 and warehousing in other manufacturing
                   zones presently allowed.

Fiscal Year 2021 Budget Amendment- Grant for Homeless Service Advocate
          The City Council will discuss a proposed budget amendment to recognize and appropriate $114,250 in grant funding
received by United Way of Northern Utah on behalf of the Alliance for the Detenninants of Health for a homeless service
advocate position.
          Chair Choberka disclosed that she is employed by Inte1mountain Healthcare (IHC) and the Alliance for the Detenninants
of Health, but she had no decision making authority relative to this grant and she does not feel there is a conflict of interest
associated with her participation in discussion and action on this matter.
          Comptroller Stout reported the Police Department has applied for and received a grant from United Way of Northern
Utah to cover the wages and benefits for a Homeless Service Advocate who will address the needs of the homeless population in
the City. With the award of this grant, the Department was able to create a full-time temporary position who will serve as a
coordinator between healthcare, behavioral health, and social services organizations. The individual will also work with fire and
police personnel to visit locations where high-risk individuals are located and to educate and assist them in connecting to support
care. The total grant award was $114,250, which will cover the costs of the position for 18 months. Once funding is expired, the
Police Department will need to pursue other funding programs unless the Council chooses to allocate funding for the position.
          Council member Stephens asked if the grant covers equipment, supplies, and other suppmt for the position. Chief Watt
stated that the employee has access to the same resources that the rest of the Department has access to. He reported the individual
has been hired to fill the position and last month alone, she made 482 contacts and referred individuals to support services. This is
an incredible, invaluable effort.
          Council Policy Analyst Mabey reported that action on the budget amendment can be scheduled for September 15, 2020.
          Chair Choberka thanked the Police Department for being innovative in pursuing funding options for this type of service;
there is definitely a need for support in the community.
