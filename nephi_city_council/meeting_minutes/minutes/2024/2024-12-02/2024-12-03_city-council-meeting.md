SCHOOL RESOURCE OFFICER AGREEMENTS APPROVED:

    Mr. Atkinson reviewed with the council that the city currently has two active school resource officer positions.  One of the positions is funded through a three-way agreement between Juab County, Nephi City, and the Juab School District.  The second position is partially funded through a federal grant, Nephi City, and the Juab School District.  Once the federal grant expires, this second position will be funded 50/50 between Nephi City and the Juab School District.  
     Due to some changes in the state law regarding school resource officers, it has become necessary to adopt new agreements.  These agreements have been drafted by the City Attorney.  Most of the new agreement language mirrors the duties and requirements found in state code.  The agreements also allow for additional input from the school district on selecting the school resource officer.  In areas of disagreement or discipline, the ultimate decision rests with the Chief of Police.  The term of the agreement is for three years.  Feedback from the School Board is that they would like the length of the initial contract to be long enough to finish up the current school year and then to examine a longer term going forward.  Any party may terminate the agreement with ninety (90) days written notice. 
    Mr. Atkinson also stated that the county has indicated that they would like some language added to put guard rails on wage increases for the school resource officers.  The county has not given any direction or language to our attorney to put into the new agreement.  
     Councilor Skip Worwood moved to authorize the mayor to sign both agreements as presented.  Councilor JD Parady seconded the motion.  The motion passed unanimously. 

FEDERAL GRID RESILIENCY GRANT AGREEMENT APPROVED:

     Mr. Atkinson updated the council that Nephi Power had applied for, and received, a grant from the federal government for electric grid resiliency as part of the 138 kV transmission line project.  This grant is for over $1.6 million dollars and will greatly assist the city in the costs of expanding the city electrical grid.  He presented an agreement that had been reviewed by the city staff 
      The agreement sets forth the terms of employment for the project, records practices, standard legal practices, insurance coverage, and termination provisions.  The agreement allows the state to terminate the agreement within five (5) days written notice.  It is unlikely for this termination process to be carried out by the state entity.  This grant is specifically for the construction of the 138 kV transmission line and a ring bus at the Nortonville interconnect point.  The city received a separate grant from the State of Utah for the Nortonville metering yard and improvements to Substation #3.  The city will be required to pay Davis-Bacon wages as part of the project.  
     Councilor Callaway asked about the Davis-Bacon wages because some of the work that the grant would pay for had already been completed and wages have been paid.  Mr. Atkinson answered that most of the expenditures have been for materials.  Mr. Atkinson said he hoped that the city could get the grant in one pay request.  
     Councilor Shari Cowan moved to authorize the mayor to sign the Federal Grid Resiliency Grant Agreement.  Councilor Jeramie Callaway seconded the motion.  The motion passed unanimously. 

GSBS ARCHITECT AGREEMENTS APPROVED FOR PUBLIC SAFETY COMPLEX PROGRAMMING SERVICES:

      Mr. Atkinson updated the council that the staff had been meeting with the architect from GSBS on the design for the police station.  Prior to beginning design in earnest, it is necessary to complete a programming exercise to determine what kinds of things the police would like to see in the new station.  Some site visits to other entities have been conducted to determine what elements could be incorporated into the design of the police station.  A programming document will help determine what kinds of elements will need to be included in the design.  The programming document is very specific and will investigate the needs and requirements of each room and office in the police station.  The cost of the programming services is $65,000 and the funds will come out of the capital projects budget. 
   The two contracts presented would allow GSBS to continue to work on this process and begin billing the city.  The architect from GSBS has been very helpful in putting the process together and having the staff consider options that haven’t been thought about before.  Representatives from the UHP and the state were also present at these programming meetings.  
     Attorney Kasey Wright suggested that a true estimate be added.  Councilor Skip Worwood asked about a timeline on the project.  Mr. Atkinson said that when the programming document is completed and a go ahead for the design is received it could be approximately 4 to 6 months from now. 
   Councilor Callaway moved to authorize the mayor to sign the agreement with GSBS Architect.  Councilor Skip Worwood seconded the motion.  The motion passed unanimously.

WORK SESSION 8:04 p.m.

	•	 Recreation Director Craig Oswald introduced Jeff Peay from the firm Civil Science.  Civil Science is the company that is designing the Red Cliffs Park and the Crimson Flats development.  Mr. Peay expressed his appreciation for the opportunity to work with Nephi City.  He stated that his background is in architectural landscaping with expertise in parks and recreation.  He walked the council through the concept plan for Red Cliffs Park.  He said his firm had surveyed the site and can now prepare a masterplan and generate some estimates.  He said the park will cover 5.6 acres and would be considered a neighborhood park.  The concept plan showed a baseball diamond, but he assured the council that the open space would be a multi-use area.  
Mayor Seely said that he had received responses from residents that felt that there are too many baseball fields in the city.   Mayor Seely said he is leaning towards not having another baseball field.  Mr. Oswald said that there is some concern that the city may lose access to the Nebo View fields in the future because the school district owns the land and someday, they might want to expand their facilities and will have to use the fields for the expansion.  
Mayor Seely asked about adding a skate park element to the park.  Councilor Cowan said that many people have asked her about having a soccer field and an older children’s playground and a younger children’s playground.  
Mr. Peay said that there will be 30 parking spaces and a walking path loop.  He also reviewed the Crimson Flats ball field complex concept sketch.
  
	•	 The council reviewed a draft of the Red Cliffs Storage Annexation agreement.  Mr. Atkinson said the agreement specifies that the city will have ultimate control over what goes into the triangle parcel included in the annexation.  Mr. Atkinson said the agreement also has language to control road width in any development that is built in the annexation area.  

	•	The council reviewed a revised Crimson Flats Development agreement.  The revised agreement is based on a mutual understanding between the city and Wright Direction.  The new cost estimates of the development came back way more than the original estimates.  The council directed staff to bring the agreement back at the next council meeting so that the public is aware of the arrangements the city is making with a private entity.    

	•	 Councilor Skip Worwood moved to adjourn the meeting.  Councilor JD Parady seconded the motion.  The motion passed unanimously.
