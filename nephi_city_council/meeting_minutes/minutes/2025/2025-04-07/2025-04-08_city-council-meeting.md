1. Jacque Thorn discussed the desire to have food trucks at the Farmer’s Market this
   year. The market will be 9 am to 1 pm each Saturday from June 7 to September 27.
   The food trucks would be located on Main Street. She hoped that would draw more
   people to the market as they saw the trucks. Mayor Seely expressed support for the
   food trucks but noted that food trucks do not typically do well at the early morning
   markets. The timing of the food trucks was discussed and the need to block off
   sections so there is no other parking on Main Street. The mayor also mentioned that
   the park does not have power. Mrs. Thorn was aware of that and had that
   information on the application. Seth Atkinson mentioned the need for safety
   measures for pedestrians around the food trucks. The council discussed wanting to
   allow local vendors the first opportunity to apply. Individuals for Cody’s Gastro
   Garage and Eldorado Tacos were there in support of food trucks. Jacque also
   discussed incentives for other local businesses like a coupon for their business if a
   person visits so many vendors. Lisa Brough brought up the need for a special event
   permit and associated fees that may need to be associated with that because the
   Farmer’s Market is for profit. She mentioned that to rent a bowery is $25 for 3 hours,
   but they were utilizing the entire park. The council will discuss possible fees and let
   Mrs. Thorn know when they decide.


2. Newly elected president Lucinda Kay and secretary Noreen Harper, representing
   Daughters of the Utah Pioneers, updated the council on some projects they are
   working on right now needs they have.
      a. They discussed their efforts to preserve the old jail. It was built in the 1800s
         and is on the National Historical registry as well as having a marker from the
         International DUP. Because of those things they are required to maintain that
         building. An engineer looked at the building and stated the foundation
         needed to be repaired. Craig Childs from Child’s Historical Masonry wants to
         come down and get the foundation back to original form as well as repair
         some bricks. Because the DUP is non-profit they suggested finding
         individuals also willing to volunteer to help with repairs. She said they
         currently have sterling scholars that help with museum. She also mentioned
          that The National Historical Society offers grants to certified governments,
          but neither Nephi city nor Juab county are qualified. Mrs. Kay stated they
          needed a Historical Commission and are putting together a board and
          suggested the mayor could be a part of that.
       b. The DUP also plans to create a display of past sheriffs inside the jail and a
          bench outside to recognize them. Another building they want to work on is
          the Brough building. They want to talk with the Ute stampede committee and
          get a display in there. They would also move some saddles and leather
          goods from the DUP museum to that building and then make that building
          available for tours as well.
       c. Mayor Seely complimented Mrs. Kay and Mrs. Harper for their efforts in
          preserving our pioneer heritage and wondered how to involve more people.
          Mrs. Kay said they now have a Facebook page and wondered if they could put
          a link to it on Nephi City’s page and then have the council’s help in other
          community outreach. She also requested because they a non-profit
          organization if they could have the power bill for the Brough Building ($25)
          waived. Lisa Brough stated the city currently pays the power bill for the
          museum. The council will discuss both bills with the county since the
          museum is run by the special district of Juab County, but stated the bill could
          be covered.


3. City Administrator Seth Atkinson informed the council that the airport has received
   an increase in noise complaints. The complaints have primarily been generated
   from an aerobatic (stunt) plane that uses the Nephi Municipal Airport as a practice
   area for aerobatic activities. Due to the nature of the plane used for aerobatic
   activities, engine noise is typically louder than other planes.
   The City Council has inquired about the possibility of noise restrictions at the
   airport. The Nephi Municipal Airport is used mainly for general aviation and is
   considered a public airport. The airport receives grants from the Federal Aviation
   Administration (FAA) to maintain and improve the airport facilities. Along with these
   federal grants come requirements called grant assurances. The grant assurances
   require the airport to be open and operational for all hours of the day (subject to
   reasonable restrictions for safety, weather, or conditions of the airfield such as
   runway reconstruction). Grant Assurance #19 states:
           The airport and all facilities which are necessary to serve the aeronautical
           users of the airport, other than facilities owned or controlled by the United
           States, shall be operated at all times in a safe and serviceable condition and
           in accordance with the minimum standards as may be required or
           prescribed by applicable Federal, state, and local agencies for maintenance
           and operation. It will not cause or permit any activity or action thereon which
           would interfere with its use for airport purposes. It will suitably operate and
           maintain the airport and all facilities thereon or connected therewith, with
           due regard to climatic and flood conditions. Any proposal to temporarily
           close the airport for non-aeronautical purposes must first be approved by the
           Secretary.
   This also means that the airport cannot enact mandatory noise restrictions on
   aircraft using a general aviation airport. After discussing the concerns with the
   airport’s consultants, Woolpert, voluntary noise abatement procedures can be
   created. Woolpert will send a template copy of voluntary noise abatement
   procedures and a draft will be modified for the Nephi Municipal Airport. This draft
   will then be presented to the Airport Advisory Committee, and then subsequently to
   the City Council for consideration. Staff will also contact the aerobatic pilots and
   try to work with them to comply with the voluntary noise abatement procedures.

4. Mr. Atkinson reported that the airport has historically issued Notices to Airmen
   (NOTAMs) through an online system created by the FAA. A new system to issue
   NOTAMs has been developed along with an accompanying Memorandum of
   Understanding (MOU). The MOU has been reviewed by staff and had some initial
   concerns about the original NOTAM system being removed but this system will
   eventually be phased out. He requested feedback from the council for the staff
   concerning the NOTAM Systems Manager Program MOU.

5. Administrative and Council Updates:
         a. Mr. Atkinson praised the cemetery services and local mortuary for their
             handling of a recent event.
         b. Mrs. Brough stated that she found the agreement between the city and
             Juab County stating that if the fire station ceases to be used as a public
             fire station facility or similar use approved by the city, the ownership of
             parcel one, where the fire station is , shall revert to the city. The same
             applies to the ambulance.
         c. Mr. Atkinson advised the council to consider alternative uses for the
             property purchased for the fire station, given the rejection of the current
             agreement.
         d. The council discussed the bylaws of the Fire Board. Two County
             commissioners are assigned to the board and then there are two
             additional board members assigned by the county commission—one
             must have experience on an elected board and one of them must have
              experience with business. Nephi only has one vote on the board even
              though the city is the biggest contributor.

6. Miscellaneous items from recreation department
          a. Craig Oswald proposed getting a pool salt chlorine regeneration system
             and discussed the cost savings associated with that. He wondered
             whether getting it now or waiting for pool masterplan to be updated in a
             few years. Mr. Atkinson suggested moving forward with the system and
             incorporating it into future pool updates if necessary.
          b. Mr. Oswald discussed the progress of various grant applications after
             hiring a grant writer, including the Utah Outdoor Recreation Grant which
             has made it to interview stage. Mr. Oswald, Mr. Atkinson, and the mayor
             are going to that interview on May 1, and will find out in June if the grant is
             awarded to them. This grant would be used to help fund the Jamestown
             Park.
          c. Mr. Oswald mentioned the possibility of developing mountain biking trails
             in the canyon area near Reese’s flat. NICA (National Interscholastic
             Cycling Association) develops interscholastic mountain biking programs
             for student-athletes across the United States and is interested in
             developing the trails. The trail system could be completed within a
             couple weeks, but road improvements would be necessary to access the
             trails. The trails would be for public access but would also be used a
             couple times a year for events.
          d. Mr. Oswald reported that Junior Jazz nominated Nephi City Recreation as
             Department of the year. He will be attending a banquet April 9 with his
             family.
