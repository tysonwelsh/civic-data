2.1 Discussion and Consideration of Ordinance 19-36, Rezoning Property at
Approximately 4236/4248 South 900 East from R-1-8 to R-1-5

Robert May said the Council first heard the application on June 24, 2019. The applicant,
Brandon Pehrson, was requesting the rezone to allow for an 8-unit PUD. He said the staff
sharpened the requirements in the development agreement and provided copies of the
updated agreement to the Council. The difference in the agreement was removing the

agreement. Council Member Marchant seconded. Mayor Silvestrini said he appreciated the

3. Business Matters
3.1 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution 19-24, Approving an Interlocal
Cooperative Agreement with Cottonwood Heights and City of Holladay for
Sustainability Related Services
Mayor Silvestrini said Millcreek has been discussing the need to promote sustainability
and he had been having conversations with Cottonwood Heights and Holladay about
sharing an employee to work on sustainable issues in the three cities. Millcreek is
responsible for $7,000 of the person’s salary. He said the employee could educate the
public about recycling and could ensure businesses have anti-idling signs in the parking
lot. Council Member Catten asked where the employee would primarily work. John
Geilmann said they would be employed by Cottonwood Heights and will use their
resources. Holladay will also contribute $7,000 to the salary. Geilmann said this
employee will work with Kurt Hansen and he will track their time with Millcreek. John
Brems recommended the Council approve the agreement with the $7,000 amount in it.

Council Member Jackson moved to adopt Resolution 19-24, Approving an Interlocal
Cooperative Agreement with Cottonwood Heights and City of Holladay for Sustainability
Related Services to the amount of $7,000 inserted into the blank in Section 3
Compensation. Council Member Catten seconded. The City Recorder called for the vote.
The motion passed unanimously.

3.2 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution 19-25, Approving an Interlocal
Cooperative Agreement to Provide Funding for the Central Wasatch Commission
Mayor Silvestrini said the Council budgeted for the item previously. The stakeholders
committee has been meeting and they have been planning a pilot transportation plan for
Millcreek Canyon. The interlocal agreement will cover the contribution from each entity,
and it is based on population. Council Member Jackson asked about the amount. Mayor
Silvestrini said $75,000 was the budgeted amount for Millcreek.

Council Member Jackson moved to adopt Resolution 19-25, approving an Interlocal
Cooperative Agreement to provide funding in the amount of $75,000 for the Central
Wasatch Commission. Council Member Marchant seconded. The City Recorder called for
the vote. The motion passed unanimously.

3.3 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution 19-26, Approving an Interlocal
Cooperative Agreement with Salt Lake County to Conduct a Cultural Facilities
Master Plan Study for Millcreek

Rita Lund said the resolution was for the Millcreek Arts Discovery Program. There will
be a feasibility study which will help determine what Millcreek has by way of arts and
culture. She said Millcreek is cooperating with Salt Lake County on it because they are
doing a similar plan. The selection committee chose Create Today as the consultant. The
consultant will interview stakeholders next month. Council Member Marchant asked
what the consultant would be doing. Lund said they will do an inventory of the arts and
cultural programs and facilities in Millcreek and provide direction for future funding. The
amount for the project is $50,000; $25,000 came from a received grant and a $25,000 will
be the match by Millcreek. Mayor Silvestrini said before the Council decides what to
build in Millcreek, they need to know what currently exists. The study will also help
determine what projects should be a priority.

Council Member Catten moved to approve Resolution 19-26, Approving an Interlocal
Cooperative Agreement with Salt Lake County to Conduct a Cultural Facilities Master
Plan Study for Millcreek. Council Member Jackson seconded. Council Member Uipi arrived
(7:25 p.m.) in time to participate in the vote. The City Recorder called for the vote. The
motion passed unanimously.

3.4 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution 19-27, Authorizing the Use of Bond
Funds to Acquire Real Property Acquisitions, and Authorizing the Mayor to Issue
Letters that Property was Acquired Under Threat of Condemnation

Mayor Silvestrini said pursuant to the approved small area plan for the city center and to
acquire park space on behalf of the residents, the Council authorized the issuance of
bonds. Millcreek received a AA+ bond rating and KeyBank was retained as the
underwriter for the bond issue. The bond sale took place last week and there was a
fantastic response. The underwriter lowered the interest rate as a result of the
oversubscription by two basis points net. KeyBank has guaranteed that Millcreek will
receive the bond funding for Mill Park in the City Center. He said Mike Winder has been
in negotiations with property owners where Mill Park will be.

Mike Winder highlighted the city center map for the Council and said half of the
properties would be purchased in phase 1 of the park from Woodland Avenue to 3300

Millcreek City Council Meeting Minutes 22 July 2019 Page 4 of 6

South. He said he was working with Plowgian Auto and other businesses on relocation.
He then went over the properties that he is currently in discussion with. He said property
owners have requested the City provide a letter of threat of condemnation because they
can get a 1033 exchange, which gives the owners three years to redeploy the capital gains
before getting taxed. Winder said the park is in the Millcreek Center Community
Reinvestment Area and the intent of the bond payments is to pay them back with the tax
increment financing, but the bond is backed by sales tax. Council Member Marchant
thanked Winder for his work and said the interest rate payment will be $551,000 a year.
Winder said the Council’s financial competency is going to save the City money because
Millcreek was able to get a low interest rate. Mayor Silvestrini said Millcreek is
negotiating for the purchase of the properties based on appraised values and is assisting in
relocation. He said the Planning Commission approved the Plowgian Auto relocation
project. The City has not commenced any condemnation proceedings. This Resolution
allows the Mayor to issue a threat letter because the property owners have requested it.
Council Member Marchant asked if the condemnation could be done property by
property. Mayor Silvestrini said that is how it would be done, a separate action for each
unique property. Winder said it is a tax benefit for those looking to relocate or sell their
property. Council Member Catten asked about the name, “Mill Park.” Winder said Mill
Park has been the working title and there will be public engagement to determine the
final name. Mayor Silvestrini said Millcreek will have the funding by the end of July. He
said the residents will help determine what goes in the park. Council Member Uipi
reiterated that issuance of the letter of condemnation would be at the request of the
property owner. She thanked the City Manager, Mike Winder, and the Mayor for their
work on the bond.

Mayor Silvestrini asked for public comment. There were no comments.

Council Member Catten moved to approve Resolution 19-27, Authorizing the Use of Bond
Funds to Acquire Real Property Acquisitions, and Authorizing the Mayor to Issue Letters
that Property was Acquired Under Threat of Condemnation. Council Member Marchant
seconded. The City Recorder called for the vote. The motion passed unanimously.
