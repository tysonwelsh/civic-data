At the November 5, 2024 Council meeting, Fire Chief Nate Thompson addressed the
Council regarding the proposed code amendment. He explained that the State legislature
has granted general welfare power to the City Council, independent, apart from, and in
addition to, its specific grants of legislative authority, which enables the City of Logan to
pass ordinances as are necessary and proper to provide for the safety, promote the
prosperity, improve the peace and good order, comfort, and convenience of the City and
its inhabitants, and for the protection of property in the City. Take home vehicles utilized
by law enforcement officers and fire department administration are equipped with lights
and sirens that enable them to respond to an emergency from their home in a shorter time
than vehicles that are not equipped with lights and sirens. Not all line firefighters have
take-home vehicles. Recruitment and retention of law enforcement officers and fire
department members has become increasingly challenging due to other agencies’ higher
compensation funding, lesser or unrestricted residency restrictions, and increased lack of
affordable housing within existing residency restrictions.

He proposed that Section 2.19.010 of the Logan Municipal Code be amended as follows:

(Continued from November 19, 2024) - REZONE – Consideration of a proposed
rezone (Jed Willets) of approximately 1.80 acres of property located at 1360 North
200 West from Mixed Residential Low (MR-12) to Mixed Residential Medium
(MR-20) – Ordinance 24-23 – Russ Holley, Planner (1:14:10)

At the November 19, 2024 Council meeting, Planner Russ Holley addressed the Council
regarding the proposed rezone.

RECOMMENDATION
Staff recommended that the Planning Commission recommend denial to the Municipal
Council for a rezone of approximately 1.80 acres of property located at approximately
1360 North 200 West (TIN# 05-041-0056) from Mixed Residential Low (MR-12) to
Mixed Residential Medium (MR-20).

Land use adjoining the subject property

North: CS: Commercial Uses East: COM: Commercial Uses
South: NR-6: Residential Uses West: MR-12: Residential Uses

Background
In 1992, the Logan City Planning Commission approved the six (6) lot Royal Point
Subdivision located south of 1400 North between 200 and 400 West. Each lot was
approximately 1.30 to 1.80 acres in size. Lot #6 is 1.80 acres and the location of this
request. In 1992, the zoning was R4, which allowed multi-family at a rate of one unit per
every 6,000 square feet (SF) and an additional 1,000 SF for each subsequent unit (4-plexs
would require 9,000 SF). Over the course of two (2) years, seven 4-plexs were built on
Lot #6. In 1995, after the seventh fourplex was built, the owners illegally subdivided lot
#6 into eight tax identification numbers (TIN) at the county and never received city
permits or approvals for this action. The remainder eighth area (0.24-ac.) was
subsequently sold off and is now the subject property for this request. In 2000 zoning
changed to MFH-14 and the project became "legally existing nonconforming" because
lot#6 (the city recognized 1.80-acre building lot) sits at 15.5 units-per-acre with the
existing seven permitted four-plex's. In 2013 zoning changed to MR-12, and the project
remains nonconforming. Since 2000, zoning has been based on a units-per-acre density
rather than the 6,000 SF plus 1,000 SF formula.
                                                                                 11 | Page
            Logan Municipal Council Minutes ~ Logan, Utah ~ December 3, 2024
The current applicant (Jed Willets) purchased this remainder property (illegal building
lot) thinking he could build a duplex because it was zoned MR-12, and it was a 0.24-acre
property size. Logan City decided to rezone the 1.80 acre building lot to MR-20 in 2016
to allow for a duplex to be built. This same applicant received a Design Review Permit
for a duplex but never pulled a building permit and allowed this entitlement to expire.
Again in 2023, the City upzoned the 1.80 acres to MR-20 and issued a new Design
Review Permit for the duplex. Again, this same applicant let this entitlement expire in
March of 2024 without obtaining a building permit.

This request is from the same applicant; however, this request is an upzone to build a
four-plex rather than the duplex. If up-zoned to MR-20, the 1.80 acre building lot could
support 36 dwelling units max. With seven existing four-plex's (28 units existing), that
could allow for a new 12-plex.

GENERAL PLAN
The Future Land Use Plan (FLUP) identifies this area as Mixed Residential. It does not
distinguish between medium and high. As part of the revised Land Development Code
{LDC) and Zoning Map update in February of 2011, the area was zoned Mixed
Residential Medium (MRM) rather than Mixed Residential High (MRH) because this
area was over-saturated with multi-family structures exceeding the balance of housing
options outlined in the General Plan.

Numerous Bridger Neighborhood meetings and public comments from the Logan City
School District has indicated a desire to down-zone developable areas to get more low-
density detached single-family homes for better neighborhood balance and longer-term
occupancy in a neighborhood that struggles with transiency. There have been several
down zones in the area and new housing developments in the neighborhood over the last
several years have been predominately single-family homes (Quayle Meadows, Lloyd
Estates, Cottonwood Estates).

LAND DEVELOPMENT CODE
The Land Development Code (LDC) allows for up to 20 residential units per acre of land
in the MR-20 zone. The MR-20 zoning district permits a range of multi-family residential
structures. The current zoning designation of MR-12 allows for similar structures but at a
lower density.

SUMMARY
Jed Willets originally purchased the property thinking he could build a duplex. After
discovering the illegal subdivision, the City of Logan rezoned the property twice. Once in
2016 and again in 2023, for the applicant to build a duplex. Both times, the applicant
failed to build a duplex. The most recent entitlement was issued in March of 2023. The
applicant submitted a duplex building permit application just days prior to the one-year
(March 2024) deadline of that entitlement.

Over the last six months, since March of 2024, the applicant has not completed the
building permit plans as instructed and paid the fee for final building permit issuance.
The Logan City Building Department cancelled the building permit application after six
                                                                                 12 | Page
            Logan Municipal Council Minutes ~ Logan, Utah ~ December 3, 2024
months and the failure of the applicant's part to finalize and pay the fee, in September of
2024. A typical duplex building permit application is finalized and issued with fees paid
in 2-4 weeks.

This application is requesting an upzone for a different reason now, the applicant is
seeking to build a four-plex, not a duplex. A four-plex was never discussed or
contemplated on this property and in this situation. Staff is recommending denial of this
rezone because the Bridger Neighborhood has deliberately made numerous and consistent
efforts to downzone remaining developable areas into single-family zones, to better
balance out the housing stock that currently has a much higher concentration of multi-
family as compared to other Logan City neighborhoods. Going from a duplex to a
fourplex is contrary to this neighborhood goal.

RECOMMENDED FINDINGS FOR REZONE DENIAL
The Planning Commission bases its decision on the following findings supported in the
administrative record for this project:
1. The Logan City FLUP identifies the area as MR. The surrounding areas are zoned MR-
12 and this property should also be zoned MR-12 for consistency.
2. The Bridger Neighborhood was specifically downzoned to reduce the number of new

Planned Development Code Amendment
Chapter 17.27 is the PDO Land Development Code (LDC) chapter, and if approved, new
language specific to the proposed project will be adopted and codified into the LDC. The
specific language adopted as part of the PDO process would address proposed uses,
residential densities, building placement and design, setbacks, heights, amenities, etc.,
and tie a project to a higher level of specificity than just a general zoning district. PDO’s
are intended for unique and specific projects that would not otherwise fit into regular
                                                                                    28 | Page
             Logan Municipal Council Minutes ~ Logan, Utah ~ December 3, 2024
zoning districts and is designed to provide flexibility and discretion to all parties involved
in the normal rezone and Design Review processes. With that, the City’s expectation is
that a project coming through the PDO process should be fully vetted with the market so
that future changes are minimal. If any future changes to the project are proposed, an
applicant would have to go back through the PDO process seeking an amendment to the
approved PDO language.

Density
Although the proposed phase 5 site sits seamlessly into the overall Willow Lake
community, the PDO is exclusive to the 12-acre phase 5 remainder parcels. Unless the
applicant wants to subject phase 1-4 to future changes, that area of Willow Lakes has
already been approved and platted. Phase 1-4 are not included in the PDO application and
thus should not be considered in the approval process because the City has no authority to
go back and change the approvals for phases 1-4 unless included in the PDO proposal.
The PDO process is not bound to a zoning district density and the Planning Commission
and City Council can assign any density they feel is appropriate for this area. At 12 acres,
and with 165 dwelling units, the proposed density is approximately 13.5 units per acre.
The assisted living center is not calculated in the unit per acre density count, but rather is
viewed as a structure and/or use similar to group treatment homes or hotels.

Building Heights
The proposal shows building heights as tall as 49 feet. The fitness and assisted living
building are shown at 43 feet tall, and the luxury condominiums are shown at 49 feet at
the top of the roof peak and 44.5 feet at mid-gable. Typical height restrictions in the LDC
are based on the mid-gable points in the roofline. The current height limitation in the NR-
6 zone is 35’.

Parking Requirements
Based on the different land use categories and their associated parking requirements, the
proposed project meets or exceeds all parking stall requirements either on-site or within
the off-site 600-foot proximity requirements. They show, collectively, 32 parking stalls
above base LDC requirements with a total of 563 stalls in this project. The Condo’s
include 192 stalls of structured parking underneath the residential uses.

Pedestrian Circulation & Open Space
The proposed site layout shows standard sidewalks along public streets within phase 5
areas. There are no crosswalk enhancements or safe delineated sidewalks through the
parking lot areas. The proposal does not show any trails or pathways that would allow
people to use alternative transportation.

The proposed project narrative explains the overall Willow Lakes open space and
conservation easement areas totaling 75 acres of easement area and 36 acres of open
space in phase 1-4 (primarily the lake surfaces). The beachfront areas near the fitness
center have been detailed in this proposal, but the remaining areas of phase 5 show little
open space detail or connectivity to the bigger overall Willow Lakes open space areas.

Process
The concept review gives citizens, elected officials, and appointed officials an
opportunity to review a project and work with an applicant in providing design and
element feedback for further project refinement. City officials may suggest that the
project, as presented, is acceptable and no changes are requested, or they may provide
feedback that suggests the concept is nowhere near what would be acceptable and that
significant changes are warranted. This is a workshop for a concept plan that will inform
the formal legislative and design processes. This workshop review does not provide any
zoning entitlements or vested property rights beyond the current rights allowed under the
current zoning designation of NR-6 and any other applicable City land use regulation.

Staff recommended sufficient neighborhood and public outreach buy-in on this project
because it is contrary to the long-range planning efforts of the General Plan and the
Specific Neighborhood Plan. Both of those plans call for more single-family homes. If
determined that multi-family is better for this area, then staff recommends more trails and
open space specific to the 12-acre phase 5 be implemented to justify the uptick in density
and land use intensity. Staff also recommends enhanced setbacks, i.e., a 25-foot
minimum front yard setback, for the condominium buildings as the area to the north will
likely be single family homes. A requirement to include building setback and height
transitions is consistent with the City’s multi-family to single-family building transition
requirements. The larger front yards would give more area to plant large specimen trees
to help buffer the taller building heights. The Concept Plan review process also requires
that the applicant engage with the neighborhood for input and feedback into the proposal.
Staff encourages engagement from nearby residents to gauge their opinions on how this
proposal would impact their property and neighborhood, and what mitigation strategies,
if any, could be employed to off-set any potential impact. If the review bodies feel that
this project is viable, then the next steps would be for the applicant to incorporate any
changes, recommendations, etc., into the project layout and design and submit formal
applications for Design Review, Rezone, and Code Amendments.
