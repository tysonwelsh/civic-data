3.4) Public hearing and consideration of Mint Architecture’s request for approval of a Plat
Amendment for Gardner Point Phase 1, creating two commercial parcels at approximately
1500 North 3600 West.

Jacob Curtis presented the item. The Plat Amendment creates a new lot within the current lot and
facilitates the first stage of construction for development.

Brooke Moore, the applicant, was present. She thanked city staff for their help with the project.

Commissioner Jackson opened public comment.

Scott Larsen lives nearby the property. The development will be happening in an alleyway
adjacent to his property. He has concerns over two private roads being used for access. He
doesn’t want commercial and construction traffic driving through his neighborhood. He would
prefer the bulk of traffic to come through 1500 North and suggested that the Commission add an
easement to allow that.

Commissioner Jackson closed public comment.

Gary Ellis thought that the project has the right to bring traffic in through private roads. The
property is too close to the intersection to have an access point added for this particular section
of the property. Craig Chambers said that the construction company would need to work with the
private owners of the road, and that the city has no role in determining whether they can be used
or not.

Commissioner Lockhart asked to see the previously decided upon access points. Kim Struthers
pulled up the site plan. Moore showed her phased plan. She clarified that only the plat
amendment was up for approval at the moment, and not construction. The plat amendment
images show only the property around the building and cut off other access points that are shown
in the full plan.

Commissioner Jackson asked how much traffic there would be in the interim between
construction beginning and the other access points being installed. Moore did not have estimated
numbers. She is planning on continuing to the next building as soon as she has tenants in the
first. Commissioner Jackson clarified that Moore is still in the paperwork phase.

Commissioner Everett clarified that there would only be ingress from the private neighborhood.
He asked when the utilities for Phase 2 would be put in. Moore explained that there is not a set
date yet. Commissioner Everett asked if a crash gate could be a requirement.

Commissioner Jackson asked if Moore has a right to use the roads if they are private. Kim
Struthers informed the Commission that there will eventually be an ingress off 1500.

Commissioner Kunze asked about the property to the South. Struthers and Moore said that it is
residential. Moore explained that they are trying to make the neighborhood commercial buildings
accessible and walkable for adjacent neighborhoods.

Commissioner Lockhart said she doesn’t think development can happen without public access.
Moore explained that construction will have access from the other plats in the development plan,
those points are just cut off from the plat map being discussed. She said that she can use the
private access, but that the main access will be from 1500. Ellis clarified that the access was too
close to the intersection, but there may have been an exception made. Struthers explained that the
site plan had already been approved. Commissioner Eyre asked why the site plan had access
points that were against code. Struthers believed that Luke Seegmiller granted an exception to
the project.

Commissioner Kunze asked if construction would be coming through the private roads. Moore
said it would be accessed from the South until access from 1500 was finished. Commissioner
Kunze asked if there would be permanent access from the South. Moore confirmed that there
would be.


Struthers found that an exception was granted for 1500 North access. Commissioner Jackson
clarified that as a property owner in the exchange she has a right to use them during construction.

                Motion: Commissioner Eyre moved to approve the request including all DRC
                comments and including that the imagery in the application which appears to
                show a stub road is strictly there because of scale and scope and is not to
                supersede or override the site plan which shows a connection to 1500 North
                which will still be put into place. He also mentioned the findings that the
                proposed subdivision does meet the requirements of the development code.
                Commissioner Everett seconded.

                Vote: Commissioner Everett, yes. Commissioner Lockhart, yes. Commissioner
                Eyre, yes. Commissioner Jackson, yes. Commissioner Kunze, yes.

                Motion passed unanimously.

3.6) Public hearing and recommendation of DR Horton’s requests for preliminary
subdivision review of Inverness Plat D, including 278 single family homes and 126
townhomes located at approximately Horton Way and Edinburgh Avenue.

Jacob Curtis presented the item. This is the northernmost area of the Skye Area Plan and has the
steepest slopes. The development doesn’t currently meet city requirements, but the steep slopes
justify an exception, in his opinion as a planner.

Commissioner Everett asked about the connectivity of the suburb in regard to emergency vehicle
access. Curtis explained that the city has to balance the needs of the community with what it is
possible to create in a development. Gary Ellis explained that the development meets the code
requirements as far as access points go.

Scott Bishop was present representing DR Horton. The company is taking the advice of DRC
comments and are planning on installing pedestrian pads on longer block lengths. They are also
adding trails and pathways in every place that makes sense, which will increase walkability and
shorten block lengths for pedestrians.

Commissioner Eyre asked if staircases were being avoided as they aren’t accessible to those with
mobility aids like wheelchairs. Bishop explained that there will be staircases. He also clarified
that some of the trails would be asphalt and some would be dirt trails.

Commissioner Jackson opened and closed public comment.

Commissioner Eyre asked city staff if the two access points would become a problem once the
neighborhood is built out. Ellis and Bishop explained that there are three access points to the
neighborhood. Ellis also stated that while it is a valid concern, it hasn’t come up until this point
in planning. He asked for the opinion of the Planning Department. Kim Struthers explained that
the development is not abnormal or unusual. Ellis explained that the wide roads will help with
traffic flow. Curtis also mentioned that the fire department already suggested having turnarounds
on dead end streets.

                Motion: Commissioner Kunze moved to give a positive recommendation to the
                Lehi City Council with the findings that the proposed subdivision has significant
                grade that justifies an exception to the connectivity standards, the proposed
                subdivision meets the requirements of the Skye area plan, and that the proposed
                subdivision meets the requirements of the Lehi City Development Code in regard
                to subdivision approvals. She included all DRC comments. Commissioner
                Lockhart seconded.

                Vote: Commissioner Everett, yes. Commissioner Lockhart, yes. Commissioner
                Eyre, yes. Commissioner Jackson, yes. Commissioner Kunze, yes.

                Motion passed unanimously.

3.7) Public hearing and recommendation of Lehi City’s request for review of a
Development Code Amendment to Table 05.040-B, removing the side and rear yard
setbacks from the Neighborhood Commercial zone.

Jacob Curtis presented the item. The planning division is trying to integrate the neighborhood
commercial zone better with adjacent zones. The setbacks are being made to match other
nonresidential zones. Kim Struthers explained that buffering will still need to be met even if
setbacks are removed. The power department would like a larger front setback for ease of access
for utilities installation and maintenance.

Commissioner Everett asked if setbacks were ever a part of commercial zones. Struthers
informed him that there has only ever been front setbacks for commercial zones.

Commissioner Jackson opened public comment.

Ken Roberts thanked the commission for their work. He likes the idea of getting rid of setbacks.

Commissioner Jackson closed public comment.

                Motion: Commissioner Lockhart moved to give a positive recommendation to the
                Lehi City Council with the findings that it will bring consistency across
                commercial zones, and that it matches International Building Code. She included
                all DRC comments. Commissioner Kunze seconded.

                Vote: Commissioner Everett, yes. Commissioner Lockhart, yes. Commissioner
                Eyre, yes. Commissioner Jackson, yes. Commissioner Kunze, yes.

                Motion passed unanimously.
