G. ACTION ITEMS:
   1. APPROVE THE RECOMMENDATIONS OF THE RECREATION, ARTS, MUSEUMS AND PARK
      (RAMP) BOARD FOR THE 2026 MAJOR, RECREATION & PARKS AND ARTS & MUSEUMS
      GRANTS.
         Kevin Burns (RAMP Committee Chair): Our RAMP Board is made up of all volunteers. They're a diverse
         group from around the county overseeing these funds and reviewing these applications. We received 90ish
         applications altogether that all have to be reviewed. That is done after folks are working their own their own
         jobs and doing their own things and then review all of those applications meeting with each applicant. We
         assign each grant applicant a liaison from the board. We meet with them, review their applications, and do
         site surveys where they're going to complete their projects. Over the last 20 years, $78,991,111 in RAMP
         funds have been distributed throughout the County for a variety of projects. We have some pretty large parks
         that have been funded through RAMP grants. RAMP was a part of the skateboard park on 40th Street there
         by the South Ogden City office and Smith Family Park in Farr West. Washington Terrace has been good
         with phasing in and finishing some things at Rohmer Park. And then there's some smaller parks such as the
         West Ogden Park on West 24th Street and Beus Pond Park. Everybody gets to benefit from this a little bit.

         Commissioner Harvey: Every city and unincorporated Weber County has received RAMP funds out of the
         $78M?

         Mr. Burns: Yes, it's spread around. It's really accomplished some pretty cool projects. We had 189 total
         requests this year that includes our EZ Grants. This year we collected $6.6M in RAMP tax. That money is
         divided into thirds with one third going to major grants. The other two thirds are put into a category and then
         we subtract from that. We subtract municipal grants which is the $1 per city per resident that goes to each
         city. And then we take out EZ Grants, which we have coming up. This year we boosted our easy grants
         from $1,500 to $3,500 because $1,500 just doesn't go very far. And then our free summer Saturdays, we
         back out $80,000 for that. That includes a number of facilities around Weber County that offer free
         admission on Saturdays such as the Dinosaur Park, Treehouse, Nature Center, the aquatic centers, and
         Peery's Egyptian Theater. We take the remainder and that's where we pull our money from for our grants.
         This year in our major grants, we had $3M more in requests than we had funding. We had $2M more in
         request for Arts and Museums and we had in excess of $220k in Parks and Recreation. When we finish the
         application process, we move the money that we have remaining in Parks and Recreation and move that to
         Arts and Museums or to Majors, whichever we can best fund something with that money. At the end of the
         day, we want zero dollars in our RAMP account and start fresh every year. This year was a hard year
         particularly in Arts and Museums and tears were shed in the meeting because our folks really take it serious
         and they have a lot of passion for all those projects. That's just the way they are. Before we give you our
         recommendation, we want to say RAMP would not survive without you and your support and your support
         of Shelly and all of the staff in your office that manage RAMP. Shelly has so many applications to go
         through, she puts all our boxes together for us so we have all of our paperwork, and she answers all of our
         questions. RAMP season is 24/7. Some people can wake up at 2 o'clock in the morning with a RAMP
         question, shoot Shelly a text, and she'll get back to us when she wakes up. We know we're getting an
         answer. Thank you for your support and the support of our volunteer board that puts in so much time.

    3. APPROVAL OF A RESOLUTION 15-2026 APPROVING AN INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT WITH
       CACHE COUNTY FOR LIMITED LAND USE, SUBDIVISION AND BUILDING PERMIT
       AUTHORITY AT POWDER MOUNTAIN.
         Sean Wilkinson (Community Development Director): Powder Mountain is asking permission to build a fire
         station and improve the Timberline parking lot at the ski resort which lies mostly within Cache County. This
         agreement would make Weber County the land use authority over these projects, including subdivisions, site
         plans, building permits and inspections, and any other land use permitting required for these developments.
         Weber County would be allowed to collect associated fees and termination of the agreement would occur if
         the projects are abandoned or 10 years from the agreement approval date. Cache County has not yet approved
         it but has had it for several weeks.
         Commissioner Bolos moved to approve Resolution 15-2026 approving an Interlocal Agreement with Cache
         County for limited land use, subdivision and building permit authority at Powder Mountain; Commissioner
         Harvey seconded.
