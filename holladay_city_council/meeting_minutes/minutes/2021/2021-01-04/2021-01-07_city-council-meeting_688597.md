public comments have been received.

He referenced a hotel and an office building on 5900 South and Van Winkle Parkway. Council

V.      UPD Mental Health Update – Lieutenant Ackerman.
Mayor Dahle reported that the Unified Police Department (“UPD”) Chief Justin Hoyal and Lieutenant
Tyler Ackerman were present. Chief Hoyal noted that Mike Archibeque was also present and is part
of the Mental Health Unit at UPD. One of the major issues in law enforcement relates to mental
health. The UPD Mental Health Unit is a regional service that includes all of the different precincts.
Chief Hoyal reported that the unit maintains a queue of people who struggle with mental health issues
and determine how to assist them most appropriately.

Officer Archibeque explained that the Mental Health Unit falls under the Investigation Division and
is available to all communities served by the UPD. The unit includes:
    • Unit Leader, Sergeant Sampson;
    • Full-Time Detective, Officer Archibeque; and
    • Social Worker, Ken Martinez.

Mr. Martinez was previously an employee at the University of Utah under the Mobile Crisis Outreach
Team (“MCOT”) through a grant from Salt Lake County. Officer Archibeque and Mr. Martinez were
part of a co-responding model and available to provide services for those in crisis. That includes
people in momentary states of crisis as well as those dealing with chronic mental illness.

Officer Archibeque noted that the resources are limited and the Mental Health Unit cannot be
everywhere at once. However, he reported that there are secondary assignments in each of the
precincts. In the City of Holladay, Detective Arlan Bennett was assigned to the Mental Health Unit.
Once a month, Detective Bennett takes part in the co-responding model with Mr. Martinez to check
in with individuals in need throughout the City of Holladay.

It was reported that in the last seven days, there have been over 30 crisis calls UPD wide. Officer
Archibeque commented that the Mental Health Unit is trying to expand. There is a monthly
roundtable that includes State agencies, Adult Protective Services, the Division of Child and Family
Services (“DCFS”), hospitals, and mental health facilities. He stated that the Mental Health Unit
works with as many resources as possible and noted that the work cannot be done alone. Council
Member Petersen commented that the Mental Health Unit is a great service.

VII. City Manager Report – Manager Chamness.
Ms. Chamness reported on changes in the Planning Department. Ms. Davenport began work as the
new Community and Economic Development Director. Jonathan Teerlink assumed responsibilities
as the Planning Manager and Paul Allred transitioned to a Senior Planning Advisor role. Staff
internally hired a Building Official who began work as a City employee on December 28, 2020. Ms.
Chamness also reported that the Business License Official resigned. This allowed staff to evaluate
workflow and processes in the department.

Ms. Chamness discussed the Holladay@20 work and stated that the website now includes videos with
explanations about how property taxes are calculated. Ms. Chamness reported that a more
comprehensive conversation would take place regarding Holladay@20 at the next meeting.

Council Members Quinn and Gibbons previously identified sidewalk fill-in work that needed to be
done around Cottonwood Elementary School. Staff applied for a grant related to that work and was
hopeful that the work would be completed before school starts in the fall. The grant requires a local
match and the cost could be partially covered with the sidewalk funds for the current year and next
year.

Legislative updates were shared with the City Council. Ms. Chamness reported that S.B. 61 –
Outdoor Advertising Amendments, would allow existing billboards to be converted into electronic
billboards. In the past, the City of Holladay took a strong position against billboards. Ms. Chamness
noted that there is an ordinance that prohibits new billboards and electronic signs. She asked for
direction from the City Council moving forward. The Council members expressed support for the
existing ordinances. Council Member Quinn liked the fact that the City is not littered with billboards.
Ms. Chamness reported that she would convey this message to the lobbyist team.

       a.     Open Meetings Training – Todd Godfrey, City Attorney.
              • Ethics.
Mr. Godfrey shared a video produced by the State Auditor’s Office related to the Open and Public
Meetings Act. The video highlighted the following:

   •   Types of open and public meetings;
   •   Reasons for closed meetings;
   •   Restrictions related to closed meetings;
   •   Reasons for emergency meetings;
   •   Notice requirements;
   •   Public meeting agenda requirements; and
   •   Meeting minutes and recordings.

Mr. Godfrey also updated the Council on the Ethics Act. He reported that there had not been any
significant changes to the Ethics Act since the last training was conducted. Mr. Godfrey overviewed
some of the standards and requirements. He noted that there were prohibitions in the Ethics Act
related to the use of an office for any kind of personal benefit. The Council members were also
informed that an offense of the Ethics Act is a criminal act that requires removal from office and
could also involve prosecution.

Most situations that would be considered a conflict of interest could be handled by disclosing the
conflict. Mr. Godfrey recommended that any Council Members with involvement in a matter that
comes before the City Council recuse themselves from the consideration of that matter. Mr. Godfrey
noted that it could be a violation of the Ethics Act to accept a gift with a value higher than $50. It
was best to avoid those situations. Mr. Godfrey discussed the seriousness of ethics violations. He
reported that not only would a violation result in immediate disqualification from office but the review
of this type of situation is often conducted outside of the City.

        b.      Review of Council Policies – Manager Chamness.
Ms. Chamness discussed Section 3, which outlines limitations on Council travel. Ms. Chamness
noted that it has been an area of concern for some Council Members in other jurisdictions and asked
the City Council to review that particular section. Ms. Chamness also addressed Section 12, which
relates to Council expenditures, and Section 14, which specifies that the annual financial audit was
done on behalf of the City Council. The Council Policies also outlined the agenda setting process
and conflicts of interest.

Mayor Dahle wondered if $1,000, as mentioned in Sections 12.2 and 12.3 was a reasonable amount.
Ms. Chamness reported that in previous years, the amount had been $500 but was increased to $1,000
based on Council direction. Mayor Dahle brought up the language in Section 23.3 and if that language
was appropriate. He noted that there was a required vote of four to pass any ordinance or resolution.
Ms. Chamness believed the language was consistent but suggested that it be reworded to state:

   •   In a regular Council Meeting, a majority vote of all Council members shall be required for the
       passage and adoption of any amendment, revision, or addition to these internal policies.

Mayor Dahle wondered if the phrase, “all Council Members” would assume that all six Council
Members need to be present for such a vote. There was discussion regarding the most appropriate
language for the Council Policies document. Mr. Godfrey suggested removing the phrase, “in a
regular Council Meeting” from the sentence.

        c.      Fraud Assessment Checklist Review – Manager Chamness.
Ms. Chamness thanked the Council for completing the Fraud Assessment Training on the State
Auditor’s website. The State Auditor recently released a checklist that was required for all
municipalities as part of an effort to reduce fraudulent use of taxpayer dollars. Ms. Chamness reported
that the City of Holladay received a score of 330/395. As a result, the City was in a low fraud risk
category.

Ms. Chamness reported that Mayor Dahle and Council Member Fotheringham have acted as an
informal Audit Committee in the past. She proposed that a more formal audit structure be added as
part of the Council Policies.

Mayor Dahle mentioned the transition to credit card use. Ms. Chamness discussed the cash receipting
and deposits portion of the fraud risk assessment checklist. She reported that there were written
policies for that process but the governing body had not adopted those policies. However, the amount
of cash received was much lower than it used to be. Ms. Chamness stated that the City had moved
almost exclusively to the use of credit cards for most permits. Business licenses would likely be the
final area where cash would continue to be received.

        f.      Update on Holladay Backs the Blue – Mayor Dahle.
Mayor Dahle reported on the Holladay Backs the Blue event. The event is scheduled to take place
on January 17, 2021, and will honor the fifth anniversary of the shooting death of UPD Officer, Doug
Barney. Mayor Dahle noted that the COVID-19 pandemic created challenges but it was important to
host the event in some capacity.

Chief Hoyal discussed some of the plans for the Holladay Backs the Blue event. He informed the
City Council that a tribute video for Officer Barney will premiere on Facebook Live on January 17,
2021. There will be a virtual ride-along via Zoom. It will be interactive and children can participate
and ask questions. Chief Hoyal reported that there will also be a coloring contest at the schools. He
believed the event would allow the UPD to pay tribute and further engage with the community.

Council Member Fotheringham felt it was important to be careful about the event messaging. He did
not want Backs the Blue to be misinterpreted as supporting one group over another. Mayor Dahle
hoped the event would not come across that way. He reported that the City of Holladay and Chief
Hoyal engaged in conversations with Black Lives Matter organizations and other organizations within
the UPD to ensure that procedures and policies do not promote profiling or discrimination.

Council Member Gibbons agreed with Council Member Fotheringham. It was important that the
event not be perceived as a political statement. Council Member Durham agreed and felt that the
messaging needs to be clear. Mayor Dahle noted that Backs the Blue is a City of Holladay event.
