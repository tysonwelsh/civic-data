and liked the idea of the committee having a say in appointees as some members are particularly good at

committee would play a bigger support role rather than making all of the decisions. There was then discussion
regarding volunteers fulfilling the duties of a committee that does not exist. There were recommended changes
regarding trail construction. Interim City Manager Thomas pointed out they could go through existing templates
and drafted an Off-Highway Vehicle (OHV) committee as per Council direction.

noted was originally brought up a year ago. He explained setting up a non-profit organization should be simple
as it will not be directly connected to the City; rather, it would be its own entity. That being said, City Attorney
Andrizzi noted Council Members could have a larger role in selecting the nonprofit board members and
functions. He also brought up funding as a concern for the nonprofit.

have excess grants. Attorney Andrizzi reiterated the Council could easily set up the nonprofit as its own entity,

     2.2. Park Ordinance Update: Dog Park Rules and Regulations - Anthony Teuscher, Deputy
          Director of Parks, Recreation and Events
Deputy Director of Parks, Recreation, and Events Anthony Teuscher began by indicating this would be for the
first dog park ever started in the City and ran through drafted rules and regulations. Section A was definitions.
Section B was a legal disclaimer. The rules were as follows:

   1.  Each Park User may bring no more than three dogs into the park. (at a time)
   2.  Dogs must enter and exit the Park on a leash.
   3.  Dogs must wear collars at all times.
   4.  Park Users are prohibited from using the agility components.
   5.  Park Users are required to quiet their barking dogs or remove their barking dogs from the Dog
       Park. A violation of this subsection may be charged as a nuisance under Herriman City Code
       § 5-1F-2(E).
   6. Aggressive dogs, including dogs who jump on other Park Users, are prohibited.
   7. Dog defecation must be immediately cleaned up by the person or Park User who is handling,
       harboring, or responsible for each dog within the Park.
   8. At all times while in the Dog Park, Children under the age of 12 must be supervised by
       someone over the age of 18.
   9. All dogs must be at least 4 months old and have valid proof of up-to-date rabies vaccination.
   10. All dogs must be spayed or neutered.
   11. Bicycles, skateboards, rollerblades, scooters, and motorized vehicles are prohibited in the Dog
       Park.
   12. Only Dogs and their handlers are permitted in the Dog Park. All other animals are prohibited.
   13. All dogs must be licensed with Herriman City or another city or county agency.
   14. Hours of use for the Dog Park are from dawn 7:30 AM to dusk.
   15. Dogs must be in sight and under voice control of their owners at all times.
   16. No food of any sort, including for dogs, is allowed in the Dog Park.
   17. Smoking is prohibited in the Dog Park.
   18. Park Users bringing dogs into the Dog Park must be at least 18 years old.
   19. Glass bottles and containers are prohibited.

There was concern expressed regarding park hours and daylight. For instance, if the park were to close before
sunset, park users could potentially complain. Mayor Watts asked if dusk would be an appropriate closing time
given changes in daylight from season to season. There was further review of the above-listed regulations, as
well as a discussion regarding enforcement. The body also read a stipulation authorizing law enforcement the
right to enforce these rules and regulation and noted offenses would be subject to a 50-dollar penalty for first
offense and 100-dollar penalty for each subsequent offense. Trespassing offenses would be reviewed
independently. Councilmember Henderson asked about prohibiting the sale of puppies or dogs in the park.
Attorney Andrizzi stated staff would implement revisions based on discussion and come back later for final
approval.

    2.3. Fire Station #103 follow-up discussion - Monte Johnson, Operations Director
Operations Director Monte Johnson began by noting the Fire Station was affected by a voting decision and
tonight they would narrow down the implications of said decision. In meeting with the Unified Fire Authority
(UFA) Facilities Manager and Chief Burchett, they narrowed down areas needing repair in the Fire Station.
These repairs included the kitchen cabinets, window replacement, plumbing repairs ($4800 for minimal repairs
to shower and urinals), tile repairs, and kitchen appliances (though this would be a UFA responsibility). The total
cost of repairs was estimated around $19,939.

When asked if the window replacement just included awnings, Director Johnson noted it would largely involve
the silicon window insulation. He offered design options for the Council to consider including either the UFA
standard small prototype or the new, small station design. Director Johnson then went through the proposed
repair schedule. He explained that the schematic design would be eight weeks, design and development would
be six weeks, and construction documents would also be six weeks.

Councilmember Ohrn asked for a breakdown of these costs. Director Johnson noted this was all just an
estimate, and with inflation and rising costs of building materials, this was all up to change. He also noted this
estimate came from the engineer and costs would become clearer as they went through the construction phase.

The question was raised as to whether there was an offer to participate in covering any of these costs, because
these repairs can’t just happen overnight. Despite construction timelines, the body was optimistic about the
schedule offered. The Council discussed how much of the price tag was set in stone, and some Members felt
there were certain repairs that might be able to be pushed off. However, the majority of the repairs—
particularly plumbing and windows—needed to be completed as soon as possible. There was subsequent
discussion on the matter. Mayor Watts asked if they could look into the fiscal impact of these costs on the
City. The Council agreed to continue looking at the process for covering this project.

    2.4. Flag lot follow-up discussion - Michael Maloy, City Planner
City Planner Michael Maloy began the discussion by noting this item is also on next week’s docket and the next
step after would be to schedule a Public Hearing with the Planning Commission. He reiterated this lot was
approved by the county in 1997 and was called the Old Wilder Subdivision. The original flag lot was on the
north side of this property. He noted they currently have an Ordinance stating there will be no flag lot created
as part of a subdivision where there are more than two lots in the subdivision. The applicant was noted of this
Ordinance, so the applicant revised the proposal and sent it to the Planning Commission for approval. 16 lots
were approved by the Commission though this property has ten acres, and the density was eight point six.

City Planner Maloy noted they considered a rezone request last year to split the area up though Council said no
meaning the A-1 zone is still in place while they consider how to split this area up into four lots. One suggestion
May 26, 2021 – City Council Minutes                                                     Page 5 of 13
was two split it into two separate subdivisions which would bring about four access points on 13900 South.
Though he noted if standards were changed in regard to flag lots sharing access points, he noted all four potential
lots could share an access point. He noted consolidating points of access is better for traffic flow. Another
issue he pointed out is that flag lots are not allowed on collector streets and said collectors are classified with
major and minor streets. Another issue indicated was a lack of discretion and ability for the Planning
Commission to modify these standards. This brings up conflicts; for example, when a flag lot comes in, the
requirement is a fence though there is also an ordinance prohibiting double fencing. He noted the flag lot
changes feelings of privacy, so any flag lot added needs to consider the inclusion of a fence but also note ways
to do that in accordance with previous Ordinances.

City Planner Maloy acknowledged there is not currently consensus on this issue but if they took the next step
the Public Hearing process could begin to help encourage a decision. The question was whether to modify
standards, make flag lots more accessible, or consider if these rules are intentional as a means of regulating
development standards as the City expands.

Councilmember Ohrn asked how the body could best ensure City expansion while honoring the original
neighborhood vision and keeping things in line with common sense. Councilmember Henderson asked if this
was the only way for this zoning to work and asked why they could not just make one main road and have
residencies addressed off of said road. City Planner Maloy responded noting that past incidents revolved around
trying to preserve the agriculture of the neighborhoods. It was also added that the body needed to consider if a
lot was a residential zone or an agricultural zone. As a Council, they needed to be looking at the specific needs
of a particular property versus addressing the larger issue. City Planner Maloy said it could be divided into two
lots easily to split into two subdivisions.

There was a discussion about changing the code, even if it seemed like a rule workaround. Councilmember
Ohrn asked for additional input before continuing the discussion as a means of better understanding the
regulatory intent. The Council discussed a part of Herriman that didn’t seem to meet this requirement and
questions were raised as to why the current zone problem had been such an issue. City Planner Maloy explained
the neighborhood he had cited partially inspired these changes as the sheer number of driveways present created
issues. Councilmember Henderson felt maybe it was the one-size-fits-all approach that has been creating this
issue.

    10.1. Updated Bonneville Study Presentation - Blake Thomas, Community Development Director
Community Development Director Blake Thomas presented the findings of Bonneville research which was
subcontracted with traffic experts. The study was original surveying Olympia Hills though it has recently been
re-approved for a lower density sample. The study looked at traffic density’s effect on quality of life. He turned
it over to Bonneville research to go over the findings of their analysis.

Mr. Bob Springmeyer began the findings announcement and went through the names of those who conducted
the research. He wanted to look at what had and hadn’t changed between now and 2018. The general plan was
supposed to fit until 2025 though Mr. Springmeyer felt Herriman had outgrown this plan a few years ago due to
fast growth. There were 3,342 new dwelling units that were brought to the area which naturally affected the
results. Other changes since the previous plan included new mayor, governor, president, and complete re-
organization of City administration. Salt Lake County also adopted the Herriman Hills Master Development
Agreement (MDA) which reduced residential units to six point eight. Hales Engineering working for Olympia
Hills which had 6.78 residents per acre. He felt this was a good but not great unit as it does not look at the
specifics of these populations or look into the zoning. Significant new planning documents were also developed
since 2018 along with impact fees. Despite this, Mr. Springmeyer noted annexing Olympia Hills would “throw
these plans out the window.”

Another Bonneville research member, Tom Burdett, spoke next on the previous Olympia Hill plan adopted
2020. He went through a color-coded map with the Council though noted it would change if the area were
annexed. He then went through the acreage under the current plan subject to change. He did note a new plan
would change the acreage lines for school districts. Design standards were also attached to the new plan; this
included zone standards and trail heads. Designations were made between office and retail space in the case of
mixed-use development scenarios.

Next, Joe Perron and Amy Coleman spoke on traffic and density issues. They found between what they’ve used
