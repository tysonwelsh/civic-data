 6. PUBLIC HEARINGS
        a. Ordinance No. 24-12 a petition from Ross Dinsdale/Ivory Development requesting an
           amendment to the Dry Creek Highlands Sub-Area 1 Development Plan encompassing
           89 acres of property located at approximately 6400 West 9000 South and amending
           a single provision in the Jones Ranch Master Development Agreement
           Ross Dinsdale with Ivory Homes said he worked for Lehi City prior to working for Ivory
           Homes and had a great appreciation for work done by the City Council. Mr. Dinsdale
           explained the request for amendment to the Dry Creek Highlands Sub-Area 1
           Development Plan to make most roads in the Sub-Area public roads, and amendment to
           the Jones Ranch Master Development Agreement to change triggers for construction of
           both the pool and clubhouse to 300 homes.

           Chair Jacob asked about current occupancy, and how long it was anticipated to take to
           reach the proposed 300 trigger. Mr. Dinsdale said 30 homes were currently occupied,
           with a goal to build 100 homes per year. With that information, Chair Jacob estimated the
           pool and clubhouse would probably be completed for the 2027 season.

           Assistant Planner Mark Forsythe explained the proposed change in amenity space for
           Sub-Area 1. Responding to a question from Council Member Green, Mr. Forsythe said
           Ivory was contractually obligated to provide passive open space. Mr. Dinsdale said the
           Sub-Area 2 plan would include Barneys Creek passive open space. Mr. Forsythe said the
           overall density in the development would not change despite reduction in Sub-Area 1
           from 408 to 404 units. Mr. Forsythe answered questions about planned roads and
           bridges.

           Council Member Bedore said the statement in the MDA that 10% of housing in the
           development would be “affordable housing”, implied that 90% would be unaffordable. He
           said he found such language frustrating, and suggested using different language. Council
           Member Whitelock shared the same frustration but pointed out that affordable housing
           in the agreement was based on definitions and requirements set by State Legislators.

           Chair Jacob opened a public hearing at 7:14 pm.
           Comments:
           Chris McConnehey, West Jordan resident, said he was a new resident of the subject
           subdivision. He had spoken with many of his neighbors and said most were okay with
           what was proposed. He said he thought building the pool and clubhouse together made
           sense. Mr. McConnehey said the document would still push Ivory to develop
           infrastructure. He had no objection to the trail being in the second phase, spoke of
           dependencies with the Army Corps of Engineers, and would like to see things done right.
           Mr. McConnehey appreciated Planning Commissioner John Roberts for asking about the
           impact to residents.
           Chair Jacob closed the public hearing at 7:18 pm.

           Council Member Green said he appreciated Ivory connecting the trail to the elementary
           school, and appreciated traffic calming measures included in the plan for the
           neighborhood.

           Responding to a question from Council Member Shelton, Mr. Forsythe said construction
           of the pool and clubhouse would need to be started by the 300-unit trigger. Chair Jacob
           said the previously adopted language specified construction would commence and be
           substantially completed by the unit trigger, but new proposed language would only
           require construction to be commenced, which was a substantial change. Mr. Dinsdale said
           the plan was feasible with proposed wording, and requested the proposed language
           remain unchanged. Vice Chair Lamb understood that to cover the cost of construction and
           maintenance, a certain number of homes needed to be occupied. He did not have a
           problem with needing 300 certificates of occupancy to obtain the necessary funds.

           Chair Jacob felt more comfortable keeping the requirement as substantially complete,
           considering the requested change in the trigger for the pool from 200 to 300. Council
           Member Whitelock believed Mr. McConnehey had been under the impression the
           requirement was for completion at 300 and was equally concerned. Council Member
           Shelton expressed concern for the impact on residents based on their current
           understanding.

           Mr. Dinsdale said it would be difficult to meet the “substantially complete” requirement
           for both the pool and clubhouse by 300 units. He said Ivory was working on designs for
           8600 South with a deadline to be completed by 2025. He also noted that construction of
           9000 South would take a full year, and 8600 South would take a majority of 2025 to build.
           He believed it would be a couple years before Ivory could even start construction of the
           clubhouse. Mr. Dinsdale believed the language in the original agreement was unclear
           regarding whether the trigger was for construction commencement or substantial
           completion.

        f. Ordinance No. 24-36 amending the 2009 West Jordan Municipal Code regarding
           Interchange Overlay Zone (IOZ) Development Standards Section 13-6K-5:
           Development and Design Requirements and the IOZ Design and Development
           Standards and Requirements: 5. Commercial Buildings and 8. Site Design
           City Planner/Zoning Administrator Larry Gardner explained the IOZ Development
           Standards currently allowed up to a three-story commercial building and a four-story
           office building. Mr. Gardner said a developer wanting to build a hotel at Copper Rim
           requested the Council amend the standards to allow up to 60 feet in height or up to five
           stories (or if adjacent to Mountain View Corridor 85 feet or seven stories) for commercial
           buildings. Mr. Gardner said the proposed amendments would allow parking on all sides of
           a building, and 15-foot setbacks for side and rear.

           Mr. Gardner said the 85-foot maximum was recommended based on existing building
           heights adjacent to Mountain View Corridor. Council Member Whitelock said she
           appreciated the step down with closer proximity to residential.

           Council Member Shelton asked why the City would change the entire Code rather than
           make adjustments for the one developer. Mr. Gardner said granting a variance for the one
           developer would skip the public process. He said a variance was not available for the
           requested amendments. Vice Chair Lamb said he agreed with Council Member Shelton
           that he would rather grant a variance for the one application than change the entire Code.

           Responding to a question from Chair Jacob, Mr. Gardner said parking on all sides of a
           suburban hotel was desirable. He said the subject area was a suburban environment, and
           the IOZ Design Standards were more urban in nature.

           Chair Jacob opened a public hearing at 8:46 pm.

           Comments:
           Chris McConnehey, West Jordan resident, referred to a hotel near Jordan Landing that only
           had parking in front, and said he thought there could be flexibility in which sides had
           parking. He said he appreciated the questions asked by the Council.

           Aaron appeared to be unable to unmute. Chair Jacob encouraged him to leave the Council
           a voicemail or email with comments.

           Chair Jacob closed the public hearing at 8:51 pm.
