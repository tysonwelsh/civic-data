RODERICK ENTERPRISES DEVELOPMENT AGREEMENT APPROVED:

     City Administrator Seth Atkinson reviewed with the council that Roderick Enterprises is developing the Light Industrial property on the parcel formerly known as the Worthington parcel.  Roderick has been installing the necessary improvements to their subdivision to eventually receive a building permit.  Mr. Atkinson explained that certain improvements can be bonded but some improvements need to be completed in order to receive a building permit. To date, all of the improvements to the Roderick Enterprises subdivision have been complete except installation of conduit along Highway 28 to install streetlights and the laying of road base.  The conduit for the streetlights is awaiting the approval of a UDOT encroachment permit and could be part of site improvements.  Road base is an item that is required prior to the issuance of a building permit.   Due to the type of construction that will be used on the Roderick subdivision, Mr. Ben Wheat representing Roderick Enterprises is concerned about the time available for construction and would like to receive a building permit as quickly as possible.  
      Due to the extraordinary weather pattern that has occurred this winter, Mr. Atkinson stated that staff is willing to recommend a development agreement that would essentially allow Mr. Wheat to proceed with his development, bond for road base and the conduit along Highway 28 and any other bondable items allowed by the city code and receive a building permit.  The agreement would require Mr. Wheat to provide a specific access to the site for construction and mitigate any mud/dirt/debris that could be carried out to Elevator Road.  The agreement would also require road base to be installed by a date later this year and that a certificate of occupancy would be withheld until the improvements have been completed.  The city already has one development agreement in place with Roderick Enterprises and this would be a second agreement.  
    Councilor Worwood moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the Roderick Enterprise Development agreement.  Councilor Callaway seconded the motion.  The motion passed on a unanimous vote. 

4-D PLUMBING FIREHOUSE WELL REPAIR AGREEMENT APPROVED:

     Mr. Atkinson informed the council that the Firehouse Well has had water coming from the sounding tube.  The water was infiltrating and electrical panel.  The water department employees were able to temporarily fix the problem but now it needs to have a permanent fix before summer arrives.  
   The city put out a bid for the repair project and 4-D Plumbing was the winning bidder.  Councilor Ostler moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the Firehouse Well repair agreement with 4-D Plumbing.  Councilor Callaway seconded the motion.  The motion carried on a unanimous vote. 

COMMUNITY PROJECT FUNDING DISCUSSED:

    Mr. Atkinson informed the council that a memo was recently released to the city from Representative Burgess Owens’ office.  The memo informs the city regarding some funding for communities for projects that are “a transparent, detailed, and specific community-based project” that “will have a high impact to the state and the 4th Congressional District.”  These projects would also need to have broad community support and show how the project will be fully funded. 
    Mr. Atkinson suggest two possible projects that would fit the criteria outlined.  One may make the case for the Crimson Flats development.  It would have an impact to the 4th Congressional District and the project is fairly fleshed out and can be shown to be fully funded.  The addition of the Police training facility could bolster the project.  This may also be able to garner some support from other law enforcement agencies. 
     A second project could be the Agricultural Park that is being studied by Juab County and the Six County Association of Governments.  This project would most likely fit the criteria of having a high impact on the state and the 4th Congressional District, but the project has not been studied completely.  Also, the budget for the project and how it would be fully funded has not been determined.  It would generate broad support and several letters from supporting counties, the AOG, and the city could be included. 
       Mr. Atkinson said that the applications for the funding are due to the Congressional Representative’s office by 10 pm on Thursday, March 16th.  The council directed staff to proceed with submitting an application for the Crimson Flats’ development project.
