                                 PLANNING COMMISSION MINUTES
                                            Meeting of January 9, 2020
                             Logan City Council Chambers (290 N 100 W Logan, UT 84321) www.loganutah.org

 Minutes of the meeting for the Logan City Planning Commission convened in regular session on
     Thursday, January 9, 2020. Chairman Nielson called the meeting to order at 5:30 p.m.

Commissioners Present: Roylan Croshaw, Regina Dickinson, Sandi Goodlander, Jess Lucero,
Dave Newman, Tony Nielson, Eduardo Ortiz

Staff Present: Mike DeSimone, Russ Holley, Aaron Smith, Debbie Zilles, Kymber Housley, Paul
Taylor, Bill Young, Jeannie Simmonds (Council liaison), Mayor Holly Daines

Minutes from the December 12, 2019 meeting were reviewed. Commissioner Goodlander moved
to approve the minutes as submitted. Motion seconded by Commissioner Lucero. Approved
unanimously.

PUBLIC HEARING

 PC 20-001 Quick Quack Carwash [Design Review & Subdivision Permit] Joseph
 Earnest/Todd Yates, authorized agent/owner, request a 3,820 SF automatic car wash facility
 with associated vacuum stations and a concrete parking lot located on a proposed 1.07-acre
 building lot at 1224 North Main Street in the Commercial (COM) zone; TIN 05-014-0071.

Commissioner Goodlander recused herself from the discussion and vote of this project.

STAFF: Mr. Holley reviewed the request for a 2-lot subdivision and construction of a new 3,820
SF automatic car wash building with associated outdoor vacuum stations. The proposed 1.07-acre
building lot would be positioned south of the current Arby’s restaurant and north of the 8th & Main
furniture store along the east side of Main Street. The proposed new stucco and concrete masonry
unit (CMU) carwash building is oriented east-west with traffic circulation entering the rear (east)
side of the building and exiting on the front (west) side of the building.

PROPONENT: Joseph Earnest said they agree with the conditions of approval, however, are
asking for leniency with alternatives regarding building frontage due to the nature of the business.
The vacuum stations will have a roof canopy and they will be putting in landscaping. They have all
the declarant approvals for a car wash to be a permitted use and are excited to be part of the
community.

Todd Yates, the owner of 8th & Main Furniture Store, has been looking for something for this open,
vacant area. He has been a business owner in Logan for over 28 years. He indicated that with the
existing parking and available parking at the Seagull Book site (which he also owns) parking
should be sufficient.

PUBLIC: One phone call was received prior to the meeting asking questions.


COMMISSION: Mr. Holley clarified for Commissioner Newman that the LDC requires a minimum
50% building frontage (building width to lot width ratio) to ensure attractive architectural mass
framing streetscapes. The proposed stucco and CMU car wash building has a building frontage of
approximately 24% and lacks transparency on the street-facing (west) facade. The south facade
shows a series of windows with the rear and north façades containing a limited amount. The
proposed building shows wall articulation every 40 linear feet on three of the four facades, with a
blank wall on the north side. The Planning Commission has the discretion to consider function
over form and allow for exceptions and/or alternatives.

Commissioner Ortiz asked about the parking availability at the mall. Mr. Holley does not have the
accurate numbers, however, the mall is generally only 30% full at any one time.

Commissioner Lucero asked about the implication for Arby’s drive-through. Mr. Holley explained
that it currently swings south, onto the applicant’s property, so it will impact three parking stalls.
The applicant is working with the Arby’s.

Chairman Nielson asked about the retention pond location. Mr. Holley said if they are located in
highly visible areas, dense landscaping is required.

Mr. Holley confirmed for Commissioner Croshaw that Arby’s has enough parking. Quick Quack
does not have a parking requirement because they are an automated car wash, however, they are
providing three parking stalls. The question is whether 8th & Main will have enough for use as a
furniture store. The applicant has indicated they can provide stalls within a 600’ radius of the site
with a shared parking agreement.

Mr. Holley clarified for Chairman Nielson that the vacuum house on the east side is considered an
accessory structure that will be 7’ tall and require a 5’ setback.

Commissioner Croshaw thinks parking can be easily worked out, however, he would like to see
enhanced landscaping and something to dress up the north side.

Commissioner Dickinson is fine with the 24% frontage if landscaping is added, however, she is
concerned about the type of landscaping and how it will be designed due to the amount of
concrete. Mr. DeSimone said a landscaping plan is required and will be reviewed by staff.

Commissioner Lucero would like to see more vertical landscaping versus all low-level, and would
like to see the north side have a bit more transparency. 56 stalls for a furniture store use seems to
make sense. She asked if a parking agreement needs to be required. Mr. Housley, the City
Attorney, advised that the CCR’s are recorded, he does not think parking will be an issue,
however, there will need to be a written agreement allowing a car wash use (it is currently a
prohibited use).

Chairman Nielson would feel more comfortable with a signed parking agreement with the mall.
The proposed 24% frontage is half of what is required and he is concerned with what precedent
this may set for future approvals. Commissioner Newman agreed and suggested possibly
requiring evergreens to help. Mr. Housley also agreed and said the Commission should be willing
to accept something comparable in a similar situation. If the Commission is not comfortable doing
that in the future than it should not be permitted now; the question to consider is why it should or
should not be allowed.

Commissioner Croshaw would like to have a workshop on parking, realizing that every business
will have different parking needs.


Commissioner Lucero said 35 parking stalls (warehousing requirement) seems adequate. Mr.
Housley pointed out that anything located off site would require a written agreement.

Commissioner Dickinson said if landscaping is required to bring the frontage up to 50%, then it
should be required to be tall enough.

MOTION: Commissioner Newman moved to conditionally approve a Design Review &
Subdivision Permit as outlined PC 20-001 with the amended conditions and findings for approval
as discussed and listed below. Commissioner Croshaw seconded the motion.

CONDITIONS OF APPROVAL
1. All standard conditions of approval are recorded and available in the Community Development
    Department.
2. Two (2) lots are approved with this permit.
3. The remainder lot (#2) with the existing building shall provide 35 parking stalls for a furniture
    store, as determined by the Planning Commission. If the 35 stalls are not located on-site, a
    signed, written and binding alternative cross-parking plan shall be submitted and approved by
    staff as per the Land Development Code.
4. The remainder lot (#2) with the existing building shall provide street trees along both street
    frontages at every 30’ on center and replace the five (5) on-site trees located on the proposed
    Lot #1. These trees shall be planted prior to final plat recordation or a surety bond may be
    submitted and approved delaying planting times for up to 6 months in case of weather or
    other unforeseeable issues.
5. The recorded CC&R’s contain restrictions on land use, new buildings in common areas, and
    the closure of common areas. Quick Quack Car Wash will need written authorization from
    the Cache Valley Mall (declarant) prior to issuing building permits.
6. The new building shall provide 30% transparency on the ground floor street-facing facade. A
    transparent roll-up door will count towards this requirement.
7. As per the Planning Commission, the new car wash building shall provide dense shrubbery
    on both sides of the building, and near the dumpster pad, to achieve 50% building/shrub
    frontage along Main Street.
8. The new building shall provide wall articulations on the south facade where blank spaces of
    40 linear feet or more exist with additional windows and features to accomplish four-sided
    architecture.
9. The new project shall provide a performance landscaping plan, prepared in accordance with
    LDC §17.32 for building permit approvals. The plan shall include the following:
      a. Street trees along Main Street every thirty (30) feet on center unless otherwise noted by
           the City Forrester. Place 30’ away from the edge of driveway approaches for site
           visibility purposes.
      b. Open and usable outdoor space areas shall total a minimum of 20% of the project site
           (9,321 SF).
      c. A total number 20 trees and 51 shrubs/perennials shall be provided. Existing trees and
           shrubs located on-site and planned to remain can count towards plant totals.
10. The dumpster enclosure shall have either a masonry wall or a combination of fencing and
    dense landscaping to screen the dumpster.
11. Considering the existing 6-foot-wide landscape planter, the Planning Commission approves
    the rear parking setback of 3 feet (east).
12. Storm water retention/detention surface ponds or basins shall be positioned outside of the
    front setback and screened with dense vegetation from adjacent public street views.
13. Rooftop mechanical and building wall mechanical equipment shall be placed out of view and
    screened from adjacent streets.


14. Exterior lighting shall be concealed source, down-cast and shall not illuminate or cast light
    onto adjacent properties.
15. No signs are approved with this permit. All signage shall be approved and permitted by staff in
    accordance with the Land Development Code.
16. No fences are approved with this permit. All fences shall be approved and permitted by staff in
    accordance with the Land Development Code.
17. Prior to issuance of a building permit, the Director of Community Development shall receive a
    written memorandum from each of the following departments or agencies indicating that their
    requirements have been satisfied:
      a. Fire
            i. Provide a fire hydrant within 400’ as measured by an approved route around the
               exterior of the building
      b. Water
            i. Any landscape irrigation system connected to Logan City Water must have high-hazard
               rated backflow assembly installed and tested. The current system along main will need
               B/F assembly updated and leaks fixed.
           ii. Any fire suppression systems connected to Logan City Water must have a minimum DC
               (ASSE1015) installed and tested.
          iii. The water main must have an RP (ASSE1013) installed on the water main as it enters the
               building before any branch offs or possible connections, this is for containment only (City
               water supply protection). Then split the water line and supply the culinary purposes, and
               then another RP (ASSE1013) on the line to feed the car wash (high hazard) non-potable
               system.
      c. Parks and Recreation
            i. Owner is responsible for irrigation and maintenance of landscape strip(s) on Main Street
               and ensure a water meter for irrigation of their landscape strip(s) is in their name.
      d. Engineering
            i. Provide water shares or an in-lieu of fee for increased demand placed on City water
               sources. The amount required shall be per Utah Administrative Code R309-510-7.
           ii. Provide on-site retention of the 90% storm event utilizing Low Impact Design methods.
               The original site was designed to retain the 10-year 1-hour storm event. Provide on-site
               storm water detention/retention for the delta between current storm water design
               standards and the current storm water design.
          iii. Ensure that all existing drainage systems currently in use are maintained to existing
               storage and drainage capacities and connections
          iv. Any existing water or sewer services associated with this parcel that are not going to be
               reused with the new development shall be capped at the City utility main line.

FINDINGS FOR APPROVAL
1. The project is compatible with surrounding land uses and will not interfere with the use and
   enjoyment of adjacent properties because of the conditioned building design, site layout,
   materials, landscaping, building orientation, heights and setbacks.
2. The project conforms to the requirements of Logan Municipal Code Title 17.
3. The project provides open space and useable outdoor space in conformance with Title 17.
4. The project provides off-street parking in compliance with the LDC.
5. The project complies with maximum height, density and building design standards and is in
   conformance with Title 17.
6. The project met the minimum public noticing requirements of the Land Development Code and
   the Municipal Code.
7. The surrounding streets provide access, utilities and are adequate in size and design to
   sufficiently handle all traffic modes and infrastructure related to the land use.


Moved: D. Newman Seconded: R. Croshaw Approved: 5-1
Yea: Croshaw, Dickinson, Lucero, Newman, Ortiz Nay: Nielson          Abstain:

 PC 20-002 Dr. Harris Urgent Care Center [Design Review] Matthew F. Harris/Stronghold
 Opportunity Fund LLC, authorized agent/owner, request to demolish four (4) existing storage
 buildings and construct a new building for a medical clinic and salon. The existing perimeter
 storage building will remain and be remodeled. The project is located on 1.5 acres at 939
 South Main in the Commercial (COM) zone; TIN 02-085-0007. (Woodruff Neighborhood)

Commissioner Goodlander rejoined the meeting.

STAFF: Mr. Smith reviewed the request to construct one (1) new 13,500 SF commercial building
on an approximately 1.56-acre (68,000 SF) parcel. The parcel currently has 5 storage unit
buildings. The proposed project would demolish 4 of the buildings. Excluding the remaining
building and drive aisle, the project area includes approximately .89 acres (38,500 SF) of the site.
The development includes the two-story building and associated surface parking and landscaping.
The development connects to Hwy 89/91 via a shared access to the north of the property. The
proposed use for the development includes a medical clinic, medical offices, and a salon.

PROPONENT: Steve Mansfield, the project architect, advised that they are in negotiation with the
property to the south to add a second entrance and parking. If for some reason they do not get
cooperation from the property owner, another option could be removing the north unit to
accommodate the need.

PUBLIC: Jeannie Simmonds asked for clarification on the retention pond. Mr. Smith advised it will
be located on the northeast corner of the property (against Main Street). Ms. Simmonds asked if a
property has legally-existing nonconforming structures, and part of them are removed, why the
others are not required to be brought up to Code. Mr. DeSimone said it is because it is not
increasing the degree of nonconformity.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Lucero asked about the apartment. Mr. Smith said there was an
apartment originally listed on the application, however, that use has been withdrawn.

Commissioner Croshaw asked if the existing north and west buildings are on the property line. Mr.
Smith said they are and are considered legally-existing nonconforming structures. The storage
units will remain, but will be reclad.

Mr. Smith confirmed for Chairman Nielson that access will be reviewed by CMPO and approved by
UDOT, however, there is no anticipation of it adjusting the current site plan.

Chairman Nielson said the drawing shows a shared access through Spindlers. Mr. Smith said that
is correct. He outlined parking for the other buildings in the area, all of which are adequate. Any
change in use will require compliance with parking through the business licensing process. Written
parking agreements are recorded with the County.

Matthew Harris, the property owner, has talked with the property owner to the south and will be
drafting a written, perpetual agreement for parking, which will be recorded.

Mr. DeSimone said the Code allows nonconforming structures and/or uses to continue in
perpetuity as long as the use is not abandoned for more than 12 months and that the use cannot
be increased.


Commissioner Goodlander said she is uncomfortable approving so many things that are not
currently in writing.

MOTION: Commissioner Lucero moved to conditionally approve a Design Review Permit as
outlined PC 20-001 with the amended conditions and findings for approval as listed below.
Commissioner Croshaw seconded the motion.

CONDITIONS OF APPROVAL
1. All standard conditions of approval are recorded and available in the Community Development
    Department.
2. A minimum of 55 parking stalls is required, or, if off-site parking is proposed to meet the
    minimum parking standard, an Alternative Parking Plan shall be provided and approved by the
    Community Development Director per the requirements of LDC 17.31.050.
3. A bike rack that accommodates a minimum of 5 bikes shall be installed.
4. A performance landscaping plan, prepared in accordance with LDC §17.32, shall be
    submitted for approval to the Community Development Department prior to the issuance of
    the building permit. The plan shall include the following:
      a. A minimum of 3,500 SF of both open space and usable outdoor space, for a total of
          7,700 SF, shall be provided
      b. A total of 18 trees and 45 shrubs, flowers and ornamental plants shall be provided.
      c. Minimum of 2 tree species is required.
      d. A minimum of 25% of plant materials shall be evergreen trees and shrubs.
      e. Street trees shall be provided every thirty (30) feet on center and outside of site-
          distance triangles near intersections or driveway access points or otherwise determined
          by the City Forrester.
      f. Interior parking lot landscaping areas are required to have a minimum of 2 trees and a
          50% plant material coverage at plant maturity.
      g. Surface storm water retention and detention facilities located in areas that are visible
          from the public street shall be landscaped to provide an amenity to the development
          and utilize landscaping materials that reflect the natural traditions of Logan.
5. Exterior lighting shall be concealed source, down-cast and reviewed and approved prior to the
    issuance of a building permit and shall comply with current LDC regulations.
6. Rooftop mechanical and/or building wall mechanical equipment shall be placed out of view
    from the street or screen from view from the street.
7. The dumpster is required to be enclosed with a 6’ high masonry wall with a screed gate or a
    chain-link fence with landscaping.
8. No signs are approved with this permit. All signage shall be approved and permitted by staff in
    accordance with the LDC.
9. No fences are approved with this permit. All fences shall be approved and permitted by staff in
    accordance with the LDC.
10. Remaining storage units shall be re-clad with materials that are complementary to the
    approved building.
11. Prior to issuance of a building permit, the Director of Community Development shall receive a
    written memorandum from each of the following departments or agencies indicating that their
    requirements have been satisfied:
      a. Fire
           i. Provide a fire hydrant within 400’ as measured by an approved route around the
               exterior of the building.
      b. Engineering
           i. Since there appears to be a significant change of use and the amount of traffic being
               generated, with only one access into and out of development on a State Route.


             Provide documentation from UDOT approving this increased flow and any
             requirements assigned by UDOT.
         ii. Provide water shares or an in-lieu fee for increased demand to existing City water
             sources. The amount of water required per Utah Admirative Code R309-510-7.
        iii. Provide onsite retention of the 90% storm event utilizing Low Impact Design Methods.
             Provide onsite retention/detention for the flood control storm event per City Standards.
        iv. Provide City with private water line agreement for any fire lines which are required
             support new development.
         v. Provide City with a Storm Maintenance Agreement.
        vi. Nearest sewer main is on east side of US 89/91. Coordinate with City and UDOT on
             making this connection.
     c. Water/Cross Connection
          i. All landscape irrigation systems feed from Logan City water must have a high hazard
             rated backflow assembly installed and tested. Backflow assemblies must be tested
             within 10 days of turning in water to them, and reports turned into Logan City water.
         ii. Fire suppression systems feed from Logan City water must have a minimum DC
             (ASSE1015).
        iii. Each unit / business must have their own water main with their own RP (ASSE1013)
             backflow assembly installed and tested, to prevent cross contaminating each other
             and the city water source and water service interruptions to the different businesses.

FINDINGS FOR APPROVAL
1. The project is compatible with surrounding land uses and will not interfere with the use and
   enjoyment of adjacent properties because of building design, materials, landscaping, and
   setbacks.
2. As conditioned, the project conforms to the requirements of Logan Municipal Code Title 17.
   The project provides adequate open and useable outdoor space in conformance with Title 17.
3. As conditioned, the project provides adequate off-street parking.
4. Highway 89/91 provides access and is adequate in size and design to sufficiently handle
   automobile traffic related to the land use.
5. The project meets the goals and objectives of the Commercial (COM) designation within the
   Logan General Plan by providing services near high-capacity roadways and is designed for
   easy circulation of both pedestrian and vehicles.
6. The project met the minimum public noticing requirements of the LDC and the Municipal Code.

Moved: J. Lucero Seconded: R. Croshaw Approved: 7-0
