        DISCUSSION – Watt Smart Communities Program

 Mr. Prescott the Watt Smart Communities program is a partnership between the city and Rocky
Mountain Power. They are building on past projects and working together to create an energy
action plan for the future. This is a free and voluntary program for businesses and residents to
help then with their energy efficient needs. The plan included 4 areas of focus which include:
    1) Decide to Thrive
           a. Community leadership discussion and buy-in
    2) Where are we now?
           a. Community Energy profile
           b. Framing and Anchoring
    3) Where do we want to go?
           a. Vision
           b. Focus areas
           c. Goals
    4) Course of Action
           a. Strategies
           b. Community energy plan
           c. Implementation approach
Mr. Prescott continued to share names of other communities in the county that are working with
this program and feels they are being successful. He continued to view specific outcomes they
would like to have for the Orem Action Plan which are:
     • Tailored to Orem’s unique needs & goals
     • Anchored community energy data
     • Short-term actionable strategies leveraging existing resources to show immediate impact.
Mr. Prescott reviewed the targeted stakeholder team members.

General discussion ensued regarding involving the community and residents by social media and
city newsletter.

     DISCUSSION – Water Infrastructure Update

Mr. Tschirki stated that over the past few weeks they have been meeting and discussing this issue
with several individuals and wanted to update the Council as to the list of events that has occurred
regarding this issue. He continued to present some background including the culinary water master
plan, prior participation from Vineyard in the water system, the need for future water tanks in the
City of Orem and bonding for those improvements. He continued to present possible locations for
the area of the needed water tank, costs for construction and ongoing maintenance. They will be
meeting with two different neighborhoods in the next week regarding the current options.

Melissa Kingsolver
I live in a cul-de-sac just off of 400 West at 1750 North. Following are my concerns about the
proposed high-density housing unit at the corner of 400 West and 1600 North in Orem. Parking:
All developers of high-density housing units should be REQUIRED to provide a minimum of 2
parking spots for each unit in their development. This has not occurred in the HDHUs that are
now scattered throughout Orem. I have observed the trouble this causes for residential
neighborhoods firsthand, as my married children have lived in these communities. They had to
wait a full year before even 1 spot was available for one of their two vehicles. Until then, their
only option was to park wherever they could find a spot within 1-2 miles of their housing
complex. This will happen here as well. In fact, there are already MANY personal vehicles
parked along 400 West that belong to residents of the homes there. It is not safe, and it is not fair
that our neighborhoods become inundated with the vehicles of people who will live in this
proposed housing complex. I do not want our streets to become permit parking either. Where
will our family guests park when they come to visit our homes if every space along the roads
within 1-2 miles is already taken? We did not choose to live in this beautiful part of Orem to deal
with this encroachment. I respectfully request that the developer be required to provide 2 parking
spaces for each housing unit WITHIN THE HOUSING COMPLEX & ON THE HDHU
PROPERTY. If there isn’t enough space to accommodate that, then the number of units must be
reduced until it can be done.
Safety: The above situation will negatively impact the safety of the children in our
neighborhood. We all chose to live in these cul-de-sacs so our children could be safer as they
play. Having significant vehicles coming and going throughout the day and night will pose a
danger to the kids by having more cars on the streets. Also, one way to maintain a safer
neighborhood is to know the people who frequent the area. We want the status quo to continue,
that our children can play outside without worrying about many “strangers” in the area. Also,
many children walk to school or ride their bikes along these routes, and children and adults alike
walk or bike these roads. That will become significantly more dangerous with parked cars lining
the road and pulling into/out of traffic. 400 West is already narrow and very busy; please do not
make it worse.
Traffic Impact along 1600 North: 1600 North is already known as ‘I-16”. It is a major route to
the freeway and becomes very backed up at high-traffic times throughout the day. It is made
particularly challenging with the short distance between 400 West and State Street. Having
significant additional traffic pouring onto 1600 North from the proposed HDHU is only going to
make it worse. If 1600 North had additional lanes in each direction or there was a greater space
between 400 West and State Street, it would be an easier transition. But as it is, it will be very
challenging. People leaving the HDHU onto 1600 North and wanting to turn south onto State
Street will have a hard time getting across the traffic already waiting at the light. If they leave the
HDHU by exiting onto State Street, they will have to turn North only. I am sure many will then
get into the center lane and do U-turns so they can drive southbound if that is the direction they
wish to go. This will create significant traffic problems on State Street, which is already a very
high-flow traffic area all day, every day. I urge you to relocate or significantly modify this
proposed development to avoid the above problems. It is a small parcel of land in a tight,
cramped space adjacent to a long-established neighborhood. Please respect those who have lived
here for 20+ years. Help us preserve this part of Orem “as-is”.

Jeff Wilks
My wife and I have owned our home on 322 W 1750 North for the past 20 years. We are deeply
opposed to any project on the State Street Corridor at 1600 N. that would affect our traffic (on
400 W or the intersections at 1600 N), parking on 400 W, or the crime rates in this area. We do
not think sufficient attention is being given to these factors with the currently proposed
apartments at the 1600 N 400 W location. When the State Street Corridor was approved, the
incredibly tiny space at that corner was thought small enough that only small businesses or
housing could be put in there. We assumed no housing along 400 W could be re-zoned, and
certainly no access from 400 W would be granted to any properties there. Now we see that these
very things are being considered and we are very upset. There is simply no space for any such
building on the corner of 1600 N 400 W if the last house on 400 W remains as currently zoned.
We are also deeply concerned about the likelihood of the proposed housing being turned into
Section 8 housing before or soon after completion. That seems to be something
developers/owners have done in the past in order to get additional credits and other payouts. All
in all, this side of the corridor being so close to our homes should not be allowed to have such
high-density housing. I recommend that the city council not approve any variances from current
zoning at all, and that the city council examine traffic and parking in more detail around such a
project. Jason Bench mentioned in a recent meeting that a gas station was once considered for
this same space, and the traffic for this new housing project wouldn't be any worse than the gas
station. I disagree with that thinking. Gas stations do not attract traffic. They are a stop of
convenience for people already leaving or coming through that area. The fact that a gas station
was once considered should not be used to justify the traffic and parking problems the proposed
building and commercial use would create. In summary, my wife and I are opposed to this
project because of the likely traffic, parking, crime impact, and potential for Section 8 approval
that would follow. We call on all city council members to change the Corridor Zoning in
whatever way necessary to prevent such out-of-place proposals from ever being built so close to
single-family residential areas.

We, the residents of the neighborhood along 400 West adjoining the State Street, North Village
District, wish to convey our concerns about the proposed North Village District zone changes.
We understand that the fifty-foot setback limiting the height of new developments along 400
West is a positive change to help limit the impact and flow from a commercial area to the
residential area. However, we are concerned that not enough is being done to help this problem.
The North Orem neighborhoods are much quieter and less commercial than other areas of Orem
City, such as the City Center District, the Hub District, The Canyon Crossing District, and even
the Arts District Zones. We fear that, as developments begin to build in this area, the quiet, safe
neighborhood we love is in jeopardy. Many families have resided in these neighborhoods for
decades and include multi-generations from aged parents to small children, for which we have
special concerns. Therefore, we are asking that our concerns be considered before any decisions
are made. The houses that are currently located in the North Village District that are zoned as
NVD-Res(B) and the other homes located along 400 West should be more protected from
encroaching developments. We feel the fifty-foot setback that is proposed is insufficient and

that we have an adequate buffer in the heights allowed in the edge zone immediately next to our
neighborhood. In addition, we feel that these homes located in this North Village District should
have the setback for height restrictions measured from their backs and sides of the properties to
