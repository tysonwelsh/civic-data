1.     Public Hearing: City-Initiated Water-Wise Landscaping and Site Amendments Text
       Amendment (Legislative Item).
       On the Request of Draper City for a Text Amendment to Titles 9 and 17 of Draper City
       Municipal Code in Order to Amend Regulations Regarding Definitions, Site Plan Review,
       Site Design, Landscaping including Water-Wise Landscaping, Screening, Parking Area
       Design, Outdoor Storage and Display, and Related Subdivision Requirements. This
       Application is known as the City Initiated Water-Wise Landscaping and Site Amendments
       Text Amendment. Application: TEXT-187-2022. Staff contact: Todd Draper, 801-576-
       6335, todd.draper@draperutah.gov.

City Planner III, Todd Draper, presented the proposed changes made since the document was
provided to the Commission for review. A modified motion was provided as well as comments
received from Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District (“JVWCD”). Section 9-3-040 was
reviewed and the modified definitions were identified. The definitions were removed for
ornamental trees and shade trees, which were replaced with deciduous or evergreen trees. The
intent was to maintain the terminology used in the landscaping profession. Since the most recent
iteration, updates to the definition for open space were incorporated. The term is used in the code
to mean more naturalized, undeveloped areas, however, it was noted that in some instances it is
intended to refer to an open area on a lot.

Requirements were added to Section 9-5-090 with respect to the submittal of a landscape plan
package with the site plan application. Mr. Draper explained that they have always required a
landscape plan to be submitted. The proposed change would tighten up those requirements and
make it a bit stronger. Staff also noted the amendments to require floor plan submittals since there
have been occasions where having the floor plan would be beneficial for calculating parking. That
addition was made to ensure that they have the correct numbers when bringing matters to the
Planning Commission.

Mr. Draper reported that in Section 9-22-050 pertaining to site design, changes were proposed to
ensure that the main entrance is on the main façade. They also eliminated the current requirement
for a prominent architectural feature on the corner of a building that is on a corner lot. Where both
facades are already required to have a main façade and treatment, the requirement sometimes
detracts from trying to make the building work with the lot. Site design elements from the
landscape chapter were included in this site design section and were removed from the landscape
chapter. There were also miscellaneous considerations that were reformatted and reconfigured into
the specific design standards proposed for service areas and trash collection. Quantitative
standards were added for providing the required amenities. Landscape standards were removed
from this chapter and added to Chapter 23.

Mr. Draper explained that a portion of the proposed text was reworded to specify that the
constraints and requirements of service providers could also alter a building not being located on
the front property line. Commissioner Fowler asked if this applied to residential, Mr. Draper noted
that this applies primarily to commercial. The intent was for them to be as close to the front
property line as possible but consider the specific characteristics of the property. Planning
Manager, Jennifer Jastremsky noted that the actual building setback is determined by the zoning
designation. A separate section in the Code will list the setbacks for each zoning designation.
There are specific standards in the landscaping code as well. What is proposed gives staff more
leeway in the event there are conflicts.

Commissioner Fowler asked about a building located on 900 East. Ms. Jastremsky clarified that
the building did meet setbacks.

Mr. Draper next addressed changes pertaining to shopping cart corals. He stated that it was not
clear whether they are required. The proposed change was to clarify that they are required if the
establishment provides shopping carts. They must be anchored to the ground and cannot be in the
landscaping.

With regard to fencing of utility boxes, generators and mechanical equipment, it was clarified that
it must be opaque. Mr. Draper explained that it was reference to an existing ordinance that was
taken from another source and added to the section. Grammatical and minor verbiage
modifications to subsection 9-22-050(C) were presented.

The next amendments presented were to the landscaping chapter. In the first section, the purpose
statement was edited for clarity and eliminated ambiguous statements and requirements. With
regard to exemptions, that section was updated to specify that agricultural use is exempt.

Agricultural-related businesses, however, are not necessarily exempt. A specific exemption was
added to the section for single and two-family dwellings. The ordinance should read that
everything in the landscape chapter is required for single-family dwellings except for certain
sections. The intention was to keep the standards fairly uniform between commercial and
residential.

In section 9-23-030 the ability to ask for deviations was removed in its entirety. It was clarified in
section 9-23-040 that a landscape designer can also stamp plans in certain instances. Mr. Draper
stated that the State allows for engineers, land surveyors, and architects to also stamp landscape
plans. One of the three is required to sign off on all commercial plans. Other landscape designers
would also be allowed to create non-commercial or residential plans. The City does not require a
landscape plan to be submitted for a residence but there are standards that a residential property
needs to adhere to. The intent was to clarify who can stamp landscape plans.

Section 9-23-050 includes the design criteria set forth in the landscape ordinance. Most of the
current criteria were moved to the site design section of the ordinance. The section is proposed to
be retitled as landscape design criteria. Most are new design standards based on the JVWCD’s
standards document, the model ordinance they have put out, or other sections from the Draper City
Municipal Code (“DCMC”) that were added. The proposed standards are a mix of all three.
Maximum coverage rates for turf were added. Commercial uses would be limited to 20% of the
landscape area in turf, and residential front and side yards to 35%. A different requirement was
added for residential rear yards allowing them to go up to 65% coverage with turf with a maximum
of 10,000 square feet while guaranteeing at least 3,000 square feet of turf in a rear yard. Ms.
Jastremsky clarified that for multiple-family residential the requirement would be not more than
20% turf.

Commissioner Nixon asked if the landscaping must be installed in a front yard before issuing a
Certificate of Occupancy (“C of O”). Mr. Draper responded that it is not a requirement. There is
no timeframe imposed although some Homeowners Associations (“HOA”) may impose time
limits. Time limits could be added but the concern was that they might be burdensome in some
cases. There are also state laws that prohibit certain things with respect to residential landscaping.
The City can impose regulations for rear yards but only if they are water-wise.

Commissioner Hawker asked about other cities that require landscaping before occupancy.
Planning Manager, Jennifer Jastremsky, mentioned that many cities require front landscaping to
be installed within one year of occupancy. She mentioned that the State Legislature has considered
not letting cities require the installation. Mr. Draper stated that because it is required, developers
often put it into a contractor standard and complete the bare minimum on every lot. That leaves
the homeowner to redo it. Chairperson Adams noted that if there is a downturn in the real estate
market and someone cannot afford to do it, there could be issues. It was recommended that
verbiage to that effect be added. Staff agreed to look into it.

Commissioner Nixon commented that the front yard landscaping should be required at a minimum.
There could be instances where the homeowner simply does not want to do it and may remain
undone years later, which is an eyesore for the neighbors. In the absence of an HOA to ensure that
the front yard is put in, it may be advisable for the City to make sure it is done. Chairperson Adams

clarified that under the proposal the City does not require any plans to be submitted on residential
but there is a limit of 35% coverage of turf product in front yards and up to 65% in rear yards with
a maximum of 10,000 square feet. If someone were to exceed that, the City would provide
enforcement upon complaint. The reason behind the ordinance was to show JVWCD that the City
is serious about conserving water. Specifics about how landscaping area would be measured were
discussed.

Commissioner Hawker raised a question regarding where the water will go in the event an entire
neighborhood decides to install water-wise landscaping and rock. He stated that he felt that grass
absorbs much of the runoff water. There was some question as to whether there could potentially
be issues or unintended consequences as a result of an overabundance of turf products. Mr. Draper
stated that the ordinance includes a couple of provisions. The irrigation section, for example,
addresses the fact that all plants need to be on drip irrigation or bubblers for trees. He explained
that artificial turf is intended to serve as a replacement for turf. Mr. Draper explained that artificial
turf is still supposed to be installed over a permeable surface. Water, however, would still
percolate through it but likely at a slower rate.

Mr. Draper reported that provisions were added for minimum landscaped areas that were removed
from the site design chapter. The regulations for plant coverage were set at 75% coverage at a
maturity or at 10 years. Most landscapes are considered mature at about 10 years. It was clarified
that trees do not count as coverage. A species diversity rule was proposed to place limits on
allowing for the same planting materials to be planted over and over in a design or having trees of
only one species that could be susceptible to disease. The current ordinance requires soil testing,
which was clarified and expanded to specify that it is only required for commercial landscapes.

Mr. Draper next addressed the irrigation design standards and stated that very technical standards
were added. When reviewing the plans the intent was to ensure that they have the basic
components. It will require a watering schedule to be provided to ensure the establishment of plants
and long-term. The idea was to have the landscape architect or designer tie what they are designing
to the actual irrigation system so that the correct amount of water is provided.

Commissioner Hawker raised a question about wind shut-off sensors. Mr. Draper explained that
for lawn sprinklers if it is windy, a sensor is included to shut off the water in those cases and
prevent evaporation. Section 9-23-070 pertains to tree and vegetation protection and contains
regulations that were put in one place from other areas of the ordinance. Conditions were provided
under which tree and vegetation protection would not be required. The proposal includes the ability
of the Zoning Administrator to determine if those conditions are met. Mr. Draper explained that
the intent is to modify landscape requirements where a building is allowed to be located closer to
a property line, where the requirements might conflict with buffer or perimeter landscaping
requirements or where a site has lot of existing trees that need to be removed. The intent was to
provide preservation for those within the perimeter and land use buffers on the property. If it is
central to the property it would not make sense to try to protect it since that is where the building
and parking will be located. Additional, clarifications were added that open space not be irrigated.
Commissioner Fowler indicated that she felt that the description of watering by hand was vague.

Miscellaneous text changes were identified. Commissioner Nixon raised a question regarding
enforcement for those who overwater during a drought. Mr. Draper stated that those types of
enforcement measures would involve public utilities and the actual water provider. The proposed
ordinance does not include that type of restriction. It was noted that the City has complaint-based
enforcement. They have received complaints regarding sprinklers at which time the code
enforcement officer visits the site. If the water provider gives a watering schedule, there could be
enforcement of that. Community Development Director, Fred Aegeter noted that the City has only
one code enforcement officer.

Mr. Draper next addressed park strip landscaping and stated that the requirements were moved
from the subdivision chapter to the landscape ordinance and set quantitative standards for street
trees and added provisions requiring additional plants to be put in. Park strips that are eight feet
wide or less would be prohibited from having turf. Property owners should understand their
maintenance responsibilities, which were added to Section 9-23-140. The section pertaining to
perimeter landscaping left the minimum 10-foot wide perimeter in play and reformatted some of
the existing regulations to make them clearer. Exceptions were also listed as well as the conditions
under which those exceptions could be utilized. The Zoning Administrator would determine if the
conditions had been met. Clarification was added to specify that street trees need to be provided
from the Draper City Street Tree Guide. Mr. Draper explained that if the streetscape already
includes street trees, they do not need to be provided. If the streetscape does not have them, they
need to be provided and come from the Draper City Street Tree Guide. Commissioner Fowler
made the comment that she felt that the wording regarding the use of the word “provided” is vague
and more clarity such as use of the word “selected” should be added. It was noted that the Street
Tree Guide is a separate document. It has not been codified but has been adopted and is not part
of the Code. It is, however, available on the City’s website. The Guide was prepared by the Tree
Commission.

In response to a question raised regarding tree planting standards, Mr. Draper stated that an
American Public Works Association (“APWA”) standard would apply since Engineering has
adopted many of those standards as Draper City standards. It was suggested that engineering
standards be included for the cross-section.

Mr. Draper next addressed parking lot landscaping. Significant changes were made with the
majority of what was in Chapter 9-25-080(H) being moved here. There were various components
regarding how to design interior parking lot landscaping. It was noted that the City will no longer
allow tree diamonds or turf within parking lot landscaping. With regard to land use buffers, they
removed the ability to get a deviation. Applicants could get it reduced down to 10 feet from the
Zoning Administrator. The Code currently allows a deviation to five feet, from the Planning
Commission which seemed incongruent with the purpose of the buffers. As a result, staff is
recommending that it be removed. Additional clarification was added for the tree and plant
retention requirements in the land use buffer. Generators and trash receptacle enclosures were also
added to the list of structures and items not permitted in a land use buffer.

To the site design section, they addressed what needs to be done physically with walls and fencing.
It also outlines what kinds of shrubs and trees need to be included to help screen those features.
Regulations were added to help accommodate utility company clearances. Roof-mounted

mechanical regulations are part of the landscape ordinance but already exist in other chapters, and
the proposal removes these. The Material Standards section was cleaned up and the allowance for
artificial turf was added. It was noted that the size standards only apply to commercial sites and
that for single-family and two-family residential landscapes in the Code the only size standard
would be for street trees within the park strip.

Mr. Draper stated that the last requirement added to the section was to add landscape edging
between different sections of landscaping, between mulch and grass, or planting areas and rock
areas. They expanded and clarified some of the regulations regarding when ground treatments
with rock and no plants are acceptable and set a 10% limit when it is around a building. To that
section installation of irrigation maintenance replacement, they removed irrigation because it was
made into its own section. A landscape architect or designer would be required to first inspect and
provide a Certificate of Completion before the City performs its final inspection of the landscaping.
That should reduce the number of times staff gets called out to do an inspection and they are still
trying to complete the install. Mr. Draper reported that there was a subsection on the maintenance
replacement was created that included provisions that require plants that die to be replaced. That
would likely apply to many of the complaints staff receives with residential. Ms. Jastremsky
clarified that this often pertain to dead trees, trees crossing property lines, or trees impacting
walking pathways for sidewalks.

Mr. Draper reported that property owners are required to take action to prevent the establishment
of undesirable and harmful plant and tree species. It was noted that the species are not defined in
the Code. There was a question raised regarding how the City can provide regulation without clear
definitions. It was noted that Utah State University has a list of things that are considered to be
noxious weeds or invasive species in the State of Utah that can be referenced. It was suggested
that noxious weeds and invasive species be clearly defined.

Reference was made to xeriscaping and zeroscaping. It was noted that shrubs, perennials, and
annuals were being required in the ordinance. In a commercial environment, the Code would only
require the finished product to have a minimum amount of live plants as specified in the Code.
They need to be water-conserving or drought-tolerant plant species. The comment was made that
commercial xeriscape or waterwise landscaping is always better maintained, more attractive, and
installed better because they paid an architect to do the work. It was noted that russian olives are
on the list of noxious weeds put out by the State Department of Agriculture and Food.

Section 9-23-160 addresses the temporary suspension of landscape installation. It is already in the
ordinance in a few places. Clarifications were made to make it more succinct. Landscaping
installation should be suspended during the winter months when the conditions for plant
establishment are not optimal. During that time, an applicant would need to post a guarantee of
completion assurance or bond to obtain a Temporary Certificate of Occupancy (TCO). The
landscaping could then be completed within eight months or by the following September 30. This
period runs from October 1 to March 31. It would not prohibit someone from installing plant
materials during that time but the City will not check it off as having survived and installed
properly. That would be done the following spring.

Mr. Draper next referenced the last section of the landscape chapter, section 9-23-170, which
included tables. Modifications were made to the perimeter plantings. Ornamental trees were
removed as well as shade trees and replaced with deciduous and evergreen. They also added
additional requirements for shrubs. Table 1 included additional details regarding the height of the
landscape screening. In parking areas, it would be two to three feet tall. There were conditions
under which exceptions or alternatives could be given. He explained that as a building gets closer
to a property line the standards change, the trees need to be columnar or eliminated and replaced
with shrubs.

All of subsection 9-25-080(H) was moved to the landscape chapter and removed from the parking
chapter. Chairperson Adams noted that the only thing that will prohibit tree diamonds is the
absence of being mentioned in the language. The Code requires landscape islands between parking
rows, which is the requirement. It was noted that landscaping between head-to-head rows was still
required in the ordinance. Staff felt it should remain since that is how the minimum percentages
of parking lot landscaping are reached, without them, there would not be enough landscaping. In
a smaller parking lots, the islands would likely not be missed. The larger the parking lot, the more
it becomes a sea of asphalt without the landscape islands. A comment was made that the landscape
diamonds might also be for safety to prevent people from driving in the spaces. Staff commented
that while there is a level of safety, the intent is the drainage for the parking lot and getting trees
into the middle of the parking lot to prevent a heat island. One concern was that often the trees
die and the diamond become a hazard for vehicles. Mr. Draper stated that the diamonds were only
allowed as a deviation for those trying to cram in parking and not put in any landscaping. This
reinforces that the intent is to put the landscaping in. The islands are required to have a tree that
helps shade the parking lot and should be spread throughout the parking.

Mr. Draper next referred to the outdoor storage and display section in 9-27-030. He clarified that
the masonry or solid metal fencing around it needs to match the adjacent building if there is one.
Additional clarification was added that if the fences or walls exceed six feet in height up to 10 feet,
a Conditional Use Permit will be required. Since outdoor storage already requires a Conditional
Use Permit, it would likely be rolled into that at the same time it is brought forward. The intention
was that the developer would need to designate that on the plans. Additions included giving the
Planning Commission the ability to add mitigating conditions and require a specific material, color,
or design if it would better mitigate negative impacts over what an applicant is proposing.

Mr. Draper presented minor modifications to clarify the regulations pertaining to the outdoor
display areas. In section 17-1-040 the definitions section of the subdivision chapter, staff is
proposing that they reference the definitions that have been proposed in the zoning ordinance for
section 9-3-040. For subdivisions in 17-5-060, Landscaping and Trees, it was clarified that
applicants need to submit landscaping and irrigation plans for park strips. Street trees would be
required for all subdivisions and are not permitted to be a single species. They must follow the
requirements and not exceed certain thresholds set forth in the zoning ordinance. It was noted that
completion assurance must be posted before the plat is recorded. Mr. Draper noted that the
landscape improvements could be installed before recording the plat but most developers will post
the bond and record the plat.

A question was raised regarding how the new ordinances will affect any homeowner or
Development Agreements and potential legal implications. It was stated that going forward new
development will be impacted. Development Agreements that are already in place will not be
impacted.

Mr. Draper reported that the last portion of the proposed text was intended to clean up something
that happened in error to ensure that the bonding is for the landscaping within public areas of the
subdivision. Graphical drawings were then reviewed to help describe the various landscaping
elements and how they might look in certain instances. The first drawing showed an urban area
with a building built to the property line on two sides that fills most of the lot. There would be a
perimeter landscaping requirement of at least 10 feet on one side that is adjacent to a commercial
use. If it were adjacent to residential, it would require a 30-foot landscape buffer. Examples of
how the parking lot landscaping might look were presented. Mr. Draper explained that the City
only counts along the outside edge of the parking and does not go out and include landscaping
some distance from the actual parking. When looking at the percentage of landscaping, they are
looking only at the interior or center of the parking lot. Another example was shown of a transition
between an urban and suburban area. The building would be pulled to the front perhaps with a
loading area and screening of the loading area and perimeter and land use buffer landscaping
requirements would remain the same. An example of a suburban example was also presented.

Illustrations were shown of potential residential designs, however, they did not completely follow
JVWCD’s suggested standards. They prefer that the grass be the central open shape but it doesn’t
have to be. A rendering was shown of what front and back yards of varying sizes would look like
with 65% and 35% grass. It was noted that one of the reasons for having landscaped areas is that
they help define circulation in terms of vehicles on the site. Timing issues were discussed.
Because of the amount of information to digest, the Planning Commission felt they may need more
time to identify and address issues.

With regard to perimeter landscaping, the Code currently specifies that there must be 10 feet along
all sides. The building can be built up to the property line but everything else must still have a 10-
foot setback. Language was included in the draft regarding possible exceptions where the 10-foot
requirement could be reduced or eliminated. There was discussion about the appropriateness of
five feet between commercial properties. Mr. Draper explained that in commercial zones there is
no setback from the property line on the sides or front. He referenced ordinance sections which
allow them to bring the building to the property. One of the proposed sections allows the Zoning
Administrator to adjust the landscaping to what property is left. The comment was made that some
may place the building closer to avoid having to put the landscaping in. As a resident, however,
the desire is often to pull the buildings back.

The comment was made that Draper City is nearly built out and all they have are unique smaller
parcels. If unusual landscape barriers are allowed, it will be very difficult to construct a
commercial building on many of them. It was noted that there is a philosophical discussion to be
had as whether the intent is to create an urban or suburban space. The concept of bringing the
building to the street is tied to the concept of being more walkable. This is a transition zone since
the building will be close to the street perhaps with a minimal setback. Ms. Jastremsky stated that
there has been zero setbacks in the commercial zone since she has been employed by the City and

Perimeter landscape requirements were discussed. Ms. Jastremsky commented that if landscaping

 Chairperson Adams commented that because there is not much land left in the City, by not
allowing for deviations they could stymie the rest of the growth in the City. He did not want to
see the government get in the way of growth. Ms. Jastremsky commented that in the landscape
code, they are proposing deviations for the perimeter buffer. The current draft provides for this to
occur in three instances. In addition, the table that lists the trees and shrubs that are required within
the different landscape areas is set up so that the closer the building is to the property line, there
are different planting options. It was suggested that over the next two weeks, staff identify

Mr. Draper commented that anything new that has been added to the regulations has not increased
the amount of required landscaping. It has, however, set standards for the coverage and the
numbers and types of trees, plants, and bushes but none of it was intended to increase the burden
on a developer over what exists currently.
