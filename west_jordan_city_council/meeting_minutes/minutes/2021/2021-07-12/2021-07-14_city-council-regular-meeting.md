A.     Internet Fiber Optic Discussion
Council Office Director Alan R. Anderson introduced three possible pathways for providing internet
fiber optic as an option in West Jordan.

       •   Community-Based
               o Partnership with other public entities
               o Backbone infrastructure
               o Residents choose participating internet service provider (ISP)
               o Example: UTOPIA
               o Infrastructure funded through third-party revenue bonds
               o City is guarantor on revenue bonds
               o City covers gap on any debt service
       •   Private Investment
               o Franchise agreement
               o Private investment
               o Investor sets timeline
               o Provider=ISP
               o Examples: CenturyLink, Comcast, Google Fiber
               o Financial risk and benefit accrue to the private entity
               o City bears no risk, also no control on buildout obligations
               o City receives franchise tax or licensing agreement revenues for use of rights-of-
                  way
       •   City Owned and Operated
               o City-funded/Utility
               o City owned and operated
               o Residents choose participating ISP
               o Examples: Ammon and Idaho Falls, Idaho
               o Infrastructure funded by revenue bonds issued directly by city
               o City is guarantor on bonds and covers any gap in revenue

                o City directly controls deployment of buildout, distribution of the service
                o City could levy a utility transfer of funds in lieu of a franchise tax

Mr. Anderson asked if the Council was interested in providing internet fiber optic to the community,
and asked which financing pathway the Council would be interested in pursuing. Council Member
Whitelock said she brought the topic forward because a resident had brought the subject to her
attention, and because she was aware many cities in the area were getting some type of fiber option.
She stated she did not want another City-operated utility, and did not want all citizens subsidizing
a small percentage of the community using the service. She said the only acceptable option she could
see was a private investment franchise agreement, and listed several cities in the area choosing to
work with Google Fiber.

Council Member McConnehey said he believed private investment would be the best option, and
said he received requests from citizens every couple of months for the City to provide a fiber option.
He spoke of difficulties experienced by UTOPIA since 2014, and said he would not want to compete
against current businesses offering the service in West Jordan. He also mentioned the level of
service that may be available with 5G. Council Member McConnehey said he would want citizens to
understand the financial commitment involved.

Vice Chair Green referred to speeds available with different providers. He said he liked the idea of
private investment because the City would not take on the risk. Economic Development Director
Chris Pengra said availability of fiber was a factor for small businesses and for individuals working
from home. Mr. Anderson stated upload and download speeds definitely impacted individuals who
worked from home.

Responding to a question from Council Chair Jacob, all Council Members indicated they were not
interested in a city-owned utility and were not interested in a UTOPIA-style option. Council
Members Pack, McConnehey, Whitelock, and Green indicated interest in the private investment
option. Council Member McConnehey said he would be willing to learn more about the UTOPIA-
style option but would not want to pursue that as the first option. Council Member Pack said he
agreed. Mayor Burton said he was glad the Council was not interested in a city-owned internet fiber
option.

Council Member Lamb said he had not heard great things about Google Fiber. The Council discussed
fiber to the node versus fiber to the home. Council Member McConnehey suggested reviewing
development standards with the goal of encouraging or simplifying fiber options for new
development.

Mr. Anderson summarized that the Council wanted a report regarding private investment fiber and
secondary community-based options at a future work session.

B.      Southwest Quadrant Discussion
Economic Development Director Chris Pengra showed a Future Land Use Map of the Southwest
Quadrant of the City, and a map that indicated entities with ownership or interest in development
of Southwest Quadrant properties. He provided the following list of what was wanted from future
development in the Southwest Quadrant, and explained that a well-defined vision combined with a
land-use plan and partnerships could equal success.

        What do we want from the Southwest Quadrant?

        •   Sustainable jobs
        •   Above-average wages
        •   High tech/innovative industry
        •   Above-average tax generation
        •   Recreation and entertainment amenities for residents
        •   Retail opportunities for west side residents
        •   Exceptional quality of life (live/work/play)

Ben Seastran with the Gardner Company, a local commercial real estate development company with
property in the Southwest Quadrant, showed photos of recent Gardner Company projects in the
area, and spoke of macro-planned development goals for property in the Southwest Quadrant with
mixed-use, office, retail, and industrial uses. He emphasized a focus on commercial development.

Brad Wilson with Destination Homes said he was honored to be working with the Gardner
Company. He showed photos of former projects, and said the mission of Destination Homes was to
execute meaningful communities. Mr. Wilson emphasized that Destination Homes would do what
they commit to do.

Mr. Pengra described proposed steps to move forward with the Southwest Quadrant. Council
Member Lamb asked what was different from the plan currently in place for the Southwest
Quadrant, and commented he liked what Midvale City had done. Responding to a question from
Council Member Whitelock, Mr. Pengra said he had interfaced with the General Plan Committee and
talked to them about the Southwest Quadrant.

Vice Chair Green said he liked the idea of pulling Ivory into the discussion because it would be like
planting a flag saying the Southwest Quadrant was a key part of the City with a good commercial
base, and that the City was a key economic player in the State.

Council Member McConnehey said he believed the proposed resolution was very generic and said
he did not see how it would push any of the Council’s vision forward. Mr. Pengra said Staff would be
recommending the Council table the proposed resolution later in the evening. He repeated that the
proposed updated land uses for the Southwest Quadrant were put together with input from the
General Plan Committee.

Council Member Worthen said she would prefer the City to work together with the landowners
regarding development of the Southwest Quadrant. She said she did not think it would work as well
to sit back and let individual developers ask for amendments.

Mr. Pengra spoke of the inland port and the asset it could be with a focus on manufacturing in the
Southwest Quadrant. Mr. Wilson expressed the opinion that the inland port would fail without the
type of development proposed for the Southwest Quadrant. Council Member McConnehey spoke of
the need to work with other entities regarding transportation needs in general. He mentioned the
potential for a connection between the Salt Lake Valley and Tooele Valley as a valuable example.

Robert Lively, Chair of the West Jordan General Plan Committee, said he considered it a pleasure

 4.    EXECUTIVE REPORTS TO COUNCIL
       a. Mayor’s Report
Mayor Dirk Burton spoke of the success of the Western Stampede and 4th of July fireworks. He
reported on a recent groundbreaking, and expressed appreciation for painting done on the curb at
City Hall by a group of 12-year-old volunteers. The Mayor reported that City Administrator
Korban Lee was out of the office and would return the next week.

Chief Wallentine reported that the 4th of July was fairly quiet with respect to fireworks, with a total
of 48 calls related to illegal fireworks. He added that a majority of the calls did not end up involving
illegal fireworks when checked. Chief Wallentine said he was not aware how many of the calls were
located within the area the Council had prohibited fireworks.

Chief Maxfield said he agreed it had been a fairly quiet 4th of July. He showed on a map the location
of ten firework-related fires that occurred, with only four of the fires requiring the Department to
spray water (no fires west of 5600 West). Chief Maxfield reported ten firework-related fires
occurred in 2020 requiring the Department to spray water. Council Chair Jacob said he felt the
message had gotten out. Council Member Worthen said she was glad people had listened.

Mayor Burton reported COVID-19 cases in West Jordan were higher and vaccination numbers were
lower than other cities in the County.

 5.    REPORTS
       a. Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District Report
Jordan Valley Water Conservancy Board Member Lyle C. Summers congratulated the City for
recently adopting updated water efficiency standards. He stated the JVWCD had a growing service
area and increasing water demand. He outlined JVWCD revenue and explained a proposed property
tax increase to help finance and support growing demands over the next five years. Mr. Summers
commented that many West Jordan residents had participated in water conservation programs.

Council Vice Chair Green asked why the JVWCD would implement a property tax increase rather
than a water rate increase. Mr. Summers responded that property tax was a stable source of income,
which was a key factor in bonding.

Council Member Whitelock asked if the JVWCD had considered recycling water and asked what was
being done to reach out to golf courses to encourage reduced water use. Bart Forsythe, General
Manager of Jordan Valley Water, stated that water recycling would be seen in the valley in the future
but was not yet cost effective. He spoke of concerns related to reduced water flow to the Great Salt
Lake. Mr. Forsythe said area golf courses had been encouraged to be selective about areas needing
water. Responding to a question from Council Member Lamb, Mr. Forsythe stated JVWCD property
tax revenues paid for debt service. He expressed the opinion that the proposed increase was a
modest increase, staying close to the debt service.

        b. Economic Development Update
Economic Development Director Chris Pengra provided a quarterly economic development update.
The 24 strategic priorities for 2021 were broken down into four categories: organization, business
development and business care, RDA and revitalization, and promotion of the City. Mr. Pengra
reported that 13 priorities were completed, with some requiring ongoing sustaining effort, ten
priorities were in various stages of progress, and one (create RDA strategic plan) had not yet started.

Mr. Pengra highlighted four priorities: implement customer relationship management (CRM)
software; develop Southwest Quadrant EDA vision; develop business retention and expansion
(BRE) program; and retail recruitment. Paul Coates provided an update regarding the airport
master planning process.

appreciation for Paul Coates. Council Chair Jacob thanked Staff for the update.

year the event broke even financially, thanks in part to sponsorships. Ms. Barker made suggestions
for improving the event in the future.

Council Vice Chair Green spoke of the great success, and commended members of the Western
Stampede Committee. Council Member Whitelock thanked Ms. Barker and her Staff and suggested
locating the beer garden inside the arena area rather than the carnival food court. Ms. Barker
explained plans to change the food and drink arrangements for future events. Council Member
Worthen thanked Ms. Barker and her Staff for their many hours of hard work. Ms. Barker said she
agreed West Jordan had the best Staff.
