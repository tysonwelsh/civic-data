               PLANNING COMMISSION MINUTES
                               Wednesday, April 17, 2024
                                  Approved June 6, 2024


The following are the minutes of the Herriman Planning Commission meeting held on
Wednesday, April 17, 2024, at 6:00 p.m. in the Herriman City Council Chambers, 5355
West Herriman Main Street, Herriman, Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting, as required
by law, was posted in the City Hall, on the City’s website, and delivered to members of the
Commission, media, and interested citizens.


Presiding: Chair Andy Powell
Commissioners Present at Work Meeting: Brody Rypien, Heather Garcia, Darryl Fenn,
Adam Jacobson, Alternate Terrah Anderson, and Alternate Preston Oberg

Excused: Forest Sickles, Jackson Ferguson, Andrea Bradford

Staff Present: Planning Manager Clint Spencer, Planner II Sheldon Howa, Deputy Recorder
Angela Hansen, Planner I Laurin Hoadley, Assistant City Attorney Matt Brooks, Trails &
Open Spaces Coordinator Joe Zimmerman, City Engineer Bryce Terry, Deputy Director of
Parks, Recreation, & Events Anthony Teuscher
6:00 PM WORK MEETING (Fort Herriman Conference Room)
Chair Andy Powell called the meeting to order at 6:07 p.m.
1. Commission Business

   1.1. Review of City Council Decisions – Clint Spencer, Planning Manager
Planning Manager Spencer conveyed the consideration for the rezone for Alan Prince was
approved by the City Council. There was a lot of conversation regarding compatibility with
the surrounding area. A comment was made during the meeting that the developer took

offense to. He reminded the Planning Commission to be respectful of their comments.
Commissioner Garcia pointed out the Council made an adjustment to Planning
Commission’s recommendation for approval. Planner Howa explained the council didn’t
approve the requested rezone changes on the two properties that were not involved in the
actual subdivision plans. Those property owners may ask for a rezone if they decide to
subdivide.
Commissioner Jacobson inquired about the status of the trailer ordinance? Planning
Manager Spencer informed it went back for re-discussion again. The Council wanted to
increase the number of RVs allowed on a property. Also, staff will be proposing at a certain
threshold the property owner will need a home occupation business license for RV storage.
The license would require a provision that the city can come onto the property to verify the
trailers are registered and in good repair. This would require a new ordinance for home
occupation RV storage license to be approved. Commissioner Jacobson mentioned he has
noticed a lot more backyard trailer yards. He would like to see it started sooner than later.
Commissioner Anderson quired if the council is hearing more complaints or are they
hearing the community likes the ability to have more on their property. Planning Manager
Spencer remarked the people wanting to have more have been more vocal. Staff have also
proposed requiring storage on a hard surface, but some council members wanted it
removed.


   1.2. Presentation and consideration of a recommendation to the City Council regarding
        the design and construction of the following public parks and trails projects on
        parcels owned or controlled by Herriman City, which are permitted land uses in the
        MU-2 Mixed Use Zone and all Agricultural, Residential, and Recreational Zones in
        the City of Herriman: the Bonneville Shoreline Trail, the Blackridge Hiking Trail, the
        Stampede Multi-Use Trail, and the Stampede Hiking Trail located approximately
        west and south of the Blackridge Reservoir at 14942 S Ashland Ridge Drive; and the
        Juniper Canyon Trail located approximately at 4094 W Autumn Spring Drive; and
        the Midas Creek Trail Extension located approximately at 12111 S Mustang Trail Way;
        and Hidden Oaks Open Space Phase 1 adjacent to Butterfield Creek and located
        approximately at 6758 W Pacific Maple Way – Joe Zimmerman, Trails and Open
        Space Coordinator

Planning Manager Spencer disclosed the new policy for public parks and trails establishes
the plans come to the Planning Commission just for review. It is an opportunity to ask
questions, get clarity, make comments, but the Commission can’t make any requirements.
Trails & Open Spaces Coordinator Zimmerman showed maps of the new trail routes. He
shared the Bonneville Shoreline Trail (BST) has been worked on for a couple years. This is
our second section of this trail with funding held with Trails Utah. This a contractor built,
April 17, 2024 Planning Commission Meeting Minutes                                 Page 3 of 5


multi-direction, and multi-use trail meaning anyone can use this. It’s generally a flatter and
easier trail. It creates an additional loop close to Blackridge Reservoir. The trail is just over
a mile, offering a nice view of the valley. The Blackridge Reservoir Hiking Trail will be for
hiker use only; no bikes or horses allowed. This is a volunteer-built trail. It’s approximately
two (2) miles and goes just below the ridge line. This trail will be steeper, narrower, and
have a more rustic native feel. He is hoping to start building it this summer, but the build
can be carried out till next year if necessary. They will use the Friends of Herriman Society
and the Herriman website to advertise for volunteers. He detailed the labor process of
creating the trail. The Stampede Area Trails are the farthest out bordering up against Camp
Williams. The blue dashed trail on the map will be a multiuse trail open to everyone and will
be about a half (½) mile long. Currently there are no funds for this trail, but Friends of
Herriman are looking to use it as a fund-raising trail. It is estimated to cost around $15,000
to $18,000. They are hoping to have this as a 2025 build. The brown dotted tail on the map
will be a hiker-only trail that will take longer to build, it’s approximately one (1) mile and will
eventually make a loop. This is the longest section of the loop. The best access to these
sections are Eric’s trail and Blackridge trail. Commissioner Jacobson inquired if hikers want
to use these trails, but the bikers are taking over the trails? Trails Coordinator Zimmerman
reported the established trails are seeing faster more experienced bikers making it difficult
for hikers to use. This new section will help provide a quieter route for the hikers.
Commissioner Anderson sought if the city gets a lot of complaints about the dirt bikers?
Trails Coordinator Zimmerman revealed they do get complaints and he encouraged them to
keep contacting the non-emergency police lines when you see the dirt bikers so they have
records and can patrol the areas. Commissioner Oberg voiced he has noticed more trail
damage from horses and bikes in muddy conditions. Has there been any closures or signage
for muddy conditions? Trails Coordinator Zimmerman noted the trails committee has some
ideas for new signs but it’s a hard thing to manage. There is a volunteer day coming soon
that will work on rehabbing the trail surface from the ruts and damages. Commissioner
Rypien inquired if there are any plans for a BMX bike jump area to be created? Trails
Coordinator Zimmerman recounted one of the new developments off Juniper Crest Road
has some features for bikes. Deputy Director of Parks Teuscher divulged there is also a
section in Juniper Canyon Recreation area that will have some mountain bike features.
Also, an outside group is pursuing some grant funding for a jump park.
Planning Manager Spencer asked for any suggestions from the Commission. Commissioner
Fenn suggested looking at the jump park across from Glenmore in South Jordan.
Commissioner Jacobson added there is also a new park in Bluffdale that will have a dog
park, jump park and a skate park in the same area.
Commissioner Jacobson questioned if the city knows what the trail usage is? Trails
Coordinator Zimmerman mentioned the city doesn’t currently track that information. City
Engineer Terry voiced that some data from the Strava app will be available for cities soon.
Commissioner Jacobson inquired how the city advertises the trails. Trails Coordinator
Zimmerman stated the Friends of Herriman website, the city website and social media sites.

City Engineer Terry detailed he will talk about the asphalt trails that are more inside the
city.
The Juniper Canyon Trail map was shown with existing trails explained. The trail will have
some bridges across the Mountain View Corridor (MVC), the trail will go under the existing
MVC bridges. The trail will connect close to the Providence Hall Schools with the
possibility to connect to The Panorama Point Development in the future. It is an eight (8)
foot asphalt trail with a lot more amenities planned for that area. The funding was available
for this section of the trail now. Commissioner Fenn pondered how the eventual addition of
MVC freeway would affect the trail. City Engineer Terry clarified they have been working
with the MVC team. MVC will also build a bridge for the area and the trail will go below the
freeway. The intent is to have a trail head with parking, pavilion, and restrooms on the
southside. Commissioner Rypien voiced that he is impressed with the trails the city creates.
City Engineer Terry admitted the city was young enough to become partners with the
developers to create these visions from the beginning.
The Butterfield Creek Trail was continued into the Hidden Oaks Open space by the
developer. Eventually the Olympia development will continue the trail all the way to
Butterfield Canyon. The city will be adding landscaping to the trail with native grasses,
meadow grass, rock, mulch, and trees. A parking lot will be added at the trailhead on Dansie
Blvd.
The Midas Creek Trail extension project will go from the Mustang Trail where it dead ends
now and go west to the future 6400 west. This is a dual use trail with an asphalt trail and a
recycled asphalt trail for equestrian use. The trail will be on both sides of the creek with a
pedestrian bridge to cross to the other side. Chair Powell mentioned Riverton is looking
into a Rose Creek Trail extension across the Mountain View Corridor.
