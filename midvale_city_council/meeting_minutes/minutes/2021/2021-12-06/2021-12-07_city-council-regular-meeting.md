    a    Active Transportation: Midvale City is focusing on creating an active
         transportation network within Midvale City. Midvale has adopted the Mid-Valley
         Active Transportation Plan, which identified bike routes that would serve and
         connect several different communities in Salt Lake County. Midvale has begun a
         process of developing more in-depth plans that will help implement the proposals
         included in the Mid-Valley Active Transportation Plan. The improvements needed
         to implement dedicated bike and pedestrian facilities can be costly, and Midvale
         will pursue all available funding opportunities. Midvale would encourage the
         expansion of resources provided by the State to support active transportation.

Law Enforcement and Justice Courts
    . Homeless Shelter and 7200 South Corridor - Community lmpacts and
         Funding: Midvale is home to the largest homeless shelter in the State. Midvale's
         law enforcement related expenses associated with the shelter and the adjacent
         7200 South corridor exceeds $2,000,000 annually. Midvale receives 20% of the
         funds generated by the State Homeless Shelter Cities Mitigation Fund annually,
         which will result in an estimated $1.'1 million in FY23. Midvale also receives an
         additional $210,000 in direct State funding for crime mitigation. These two sources
         leave Midvale to cover the remaining $700,000 in costs associated with the State
         mandated shelter. This has a huge impact on Midvale's budget. The State Shelter
         Mitigation Fund currently generates approximately $5.7 million annually, which is
         significantly less than the cost to mitigate crime in the five communities that host
         shelters. Legislation is being considered to expand the mitigation fund by $5.0
         million. This expansion will also be included in the Governor's budget proposal.
         Midvale supports efforts to expand the State Homeless Shelter Cities Mitigation
         Fund and focus its resources on the State's five shelter communities.

    a    Law Enforcement Costs: Law enforcement wages have risen significantly in
         2021. Midvale dedicates 47o/o of its general fund to law enforcement. Further
         wage increases and the potential for expensive changes to the retirement system
         for emergency service providers will impact Midvale's financial resources, diminish
         the City's ability to provide other services, and will require property tax increases
         or budget cuts. While some of these changes are warranted, Midvale is concerned

    a   Department of Corrections Mental Health Probation Facility: This summer
        Midvale was contacted by the Department of Corrections regarding their interest
        in using the old Work Force Services Building 7290 South State Street for a 100+
        bed Mental Health Parole Facility. Midvale expressed in no uncertain terms that
        the proposed location was inappropriate. lt is located near Midvale's highest
        concentration of crime, it is adjacent to a residential neighborhood, is within
        walking distance of the Midvale Family Shelter, and could stifle development
        opportunities on State Street and along the 7200 South Corridor. The Department
        of Corrections told Midvale City Staff in July that they will explore other options but
        have not explicitly said that the facility won't go in Midvale. We remain vigilant and
        concerned about the possibility of this facility being located in Midvale, and
        especially at this location. Midvale would appreciate support in discouraging the
        location of the proposed facility in Midvale.

    a   Justice Courts: The Midvale Justice Court has jurisdiction over Class B and
        Class C misdemeanors, infraction level citations, and small claims actions that
        occur within the boundaries of Midvale City. These include all traffic, parking, and
        criminal offenses. The Midvale Justice Court is commifted to providing an open,
        fair, efficient, and independent system for the advancement of justice under the
        law, as well as a convenient resource for Midvale residents to access the justice
        system. Midvale thinks it is important to provide these services, however the
        Justice Court does not pay for itself and is subsidized by the City's general fund.
        Midvale is concerned about recent legislative proposals that would affect Justice
        Court revenue. Midvale is also concerned about a report that was issued by the
        Justice Court Reform Task Force in August that provides recommendations for
        major changes to justice court system. While Midvale City is open to change, the
        report's recommendations could negatively impact the services provided by
        Midvale's Justice Court and increase the burden on the general fund. Midvale
        would encourage the legislature to work closely with municipalities prior to
        adopting justice court reform policies or new policies that would negatively impact
        justice court revenue. Midvale City staff is willing to provided support for any
        discussion related justice court reform.

    a    Resources to Support Quality Development: Midvale has been successful in
         working with the private sector to turn over 400 acres of contaminated brownfields
         into a mixed-use center that benefits the city, region, and state. Today the
         Bingham Junction and Jordan Bluffs Project Areas include the corporate
         headquarters for Savage, Overstock, FlSmidth, CHG Healthcare, and Zagg, as
         well as major facilities for lntermountain Healthcare and Zions Bank. ln addition to
         the ofiice uses, the project areas include 3,000 units of housing, with a mix of types
         and income levels. ln order to facilitate this transformation Midvale utilized state
         approved financial tools, including Community Reinvestment Areas, the State
         lnfrastructure Bank, and direct transportation funding allocations. ln addition to the
         continued development of the Bingham Junction and Jordan Bluffs Project Areas,
         other areas such as the 7200 South Corridor and State Street require public and
         private investment to transform into vibrant mixed-use corridors that support the
         needs of current and future residents, as well as addressing issues of crime and
         blight. ln order to achieve this vision, ongoing state participation will be required.
         Midvale would encourage the State's ongoing support for Community
         Reinvestment Areas (CRAs), the State lnfrastructure Bank (SlB), and Housing and
         Transit Reinvestment Zones (HTRZs). These tools support the development of
         parking structures, mixed-use development, and public infrastructure that create
         great neighborhoods, but that are not achievable by through the partnership of
         Cities and the private sector alone.

         Point of the Mountain Development: A partnership between Midvale City, local
         taxing entities, and the State of Utah have invested in converting over 400 acres
         of contaminated brownfields into a mixed-use center that benefits the city, region,
         and state. These investments were sufficient to overcome the baniers to
         development associated with the brownfields, but not enough to give developers
         a windfall. Midvale is concerned that State policies may give advantages to the
         development at the Point of Mountain that will hurt the ability of Midvale and other
         communities to compete in the market. Midvale would encourage the State to hold
         the development of the Point of the Mountain to the same standards, tools, and
         resources available to other communities and not provide subsidies that would
         unfairly affect the market.

lnfrastructure

          Water, Sewer, and Storm Water lmprovements: To meet the current and future needs of
          Midvale residents substantial improvements to the water, sewer, and storm water systems
          are required. Midvale has completed long range plans for the water system. The cost of
          the system improvements and storage is 525,800,000. The plan also recommended
          spending an additional 52,300,000 annualfor maintenance. Midvale is currently in the
          process of preparing long-term plans for the sewer and storm water systems, which will
          require millions of dollars in additional public investment. To fund the water and sewer
          improvements Midvale has increased user fees and committed the use of its allocation of
          ARPA funding. Midvale has applied for, and will continue to seek, State funding
          opportunities. Midvale submitted an application for State ARPA funding earlier this year,
          but the program was oversubscribed and our application was not selected. Midvale would
          encourage the State to make additional ARPA funds available for infrastructure, especially
          water system improvements.

          Midvale Recreation Center: Midvale City and its residents have expressed a strong desire
          to build a new recreation center and pool. Midvale does not currently have a competition
          sized public pool and the Copperview Recreation Center will need to be replaced or
          renovated in the next decade. Midvale commenced discussions with Salt Lake County in
          2017 in hopes of receiving an allocation of ZAP funds in 2026, however this is a competitive
          process. Mldvale has allocated a substantial amount of funding for project planninB and
          matching requirements. The Canyons School District has also committed over a million
          dollars to the project. Similar projects constructed throughout Salt Lake County have
          exceeded S40 milllon to construct. Midvale is exploring all options available to fund the
          construction and operation of the recreation center.

          Active Transportation: Midvale City is focusing on creating an active transportation
          network within Midvale City. Midvale has adopted the Mid-Valley Active Transportation
          Plan, which identified bike routes that would serve and connect several different
          communities in Salt Lake County. Midvale has begun a process of developing more in-
          depth plans that will help implement the proposals included in the Mid-Valley Active
          Transportation Plan. The improvements needed to implement dedicated bike and
          pedestrian facilities can be costly, and Midvale will pursue all available funding
          opportunities. Midvale would encourage the expansion of resources provided by the State
          to support active tra nsportation.
Law Enforcement and Justice Courts

       Homeless Shelter and 7200 South Corridor - Community lmpacts and Funding: Midvale is
       home to the largest homeless shelter in the State. Midvale's law enforcement related
       expenses associated with the shelter and the adjacent 7200 South corridor exceeds
       52,000,000 a nnually. Midvale receives20%of the funds generated bytheState Homeless
       Shelter Cities Mitigation Fund annually, which will result in an estimated Sl.l million in
       FY23. Midvale also receives an additional$210,000 in direct State funding for crime
       mitigation. These two sources leave Midvale to cover the remaining 5700,000 in costs
       associated with the State mandated shelter. This has a huge impact on Midvale's budget.
       The State Shelter Mitigation Fund currently generates approximately S5.7 million annually,
       which is significantly less than the cost to mitigate crime in the five communities that host
       shelters. Legislation is being considered to expand the mitigation fund by $5.0 million. This
       expansion will also be included in the Governor's budget proposal. Midvale supports
       efforts to expand the State Homeless Shelter Cities Mitigatlon Fund and focus its resources
       on the State's five shelter communities.


       Law Enforcement Costs: Law enforcement wages have risen significantly in 2021. Midvale
       dedicates 47% of its general fund to law enforcement. Further wage increases and the
       potential for expensive changes to the retirement system for emergency service providers
       will lmpact Midvale's financial resources, diminish the City's ability to provide other
       services, and will require property tax increases or budget cuts. While some of these
       changes are warranted, Midvale is concerned about the total increase in expenses, the rate
       at which the increases in expenses occur, and the method and timing for paying the costs.
       Midvale encourages the legislature to consider the impacts on effected cities. Midvale staff
       would be willing to provide any information required to support decisions related to this
       issue.


       Department of Corrections Mental Health Probation Facility: This summer Midvale was
       contacted by the Department of Corrections regarding their interest in using the old Work
       Force Services Building 7290 South State Street for a 100+ bed Mental Health Parole
       Facility. Midvale expressed in no uncertain terms that the proposed location was
       inappropriate. lt is located near Midvale's highest concentration of crime, it is adjacent to
       a residential neighborhood, is within walking distance of the Midvale Family Shelter, and
       could stifle development opportunities on State Street and along the 7200 South Corridor.
       The Department of Corrections told Midvale City staff in July that they will explore other
       options, but have not explicitly said that the facility won't go in Midvale. We remain
       vigilant and concerned about the possibility of this facility being located in Midvale, and
       especially at this location. Midvale would appreciate support in discouraging the location of
       the proposed facility in Midvale.

       Justice Courts: The Midvale lustice Court has jurisdiction over Class B and Class C
       misdemeanors, infraction level citations, and small claims actions that occur within the
       boundaries of Midvale City. These include all traffic, parking, and criminal offenses. The
       Midvale Justice Court is committed to providing an open, fair, efficient, and independent
       system for the advancement of iustice under the law, as well as a convenient resource for
       Midvale residents to access the justice system. Midvale thinks it is important to provide
       these services, however the Justice Court does not pay for itself and is subsidized by the
      City's general fund. Midvale is concerned about recent legislative proposals that would
      affect Justice Court revenue. Midvale is also concerned about a report that was issued by
      the Justice Court Reform Task Force in August that provides recommendations for major
      changes to justice court system. While Midvale City is open to change, the report's
      recommendations could negatively impact the services provided by Midvale's Justice Court
      and increase the burden on the general fund. Midvale would encourage the legislature to
      work closely with municipalities prior to adopting justice court reform policies or new
      policies that would negatively impact justice court revenue. Midvale City staff is willing to
      provided support for any discussion related justice court reform.



Economic Development and Land Use

      Sales Tax Distribution: Sales tax is the single largest source of revenue in the general fund.
      It accounts for 42% of revenue. ln addition to funding City activities, salestax isalso used
      as the backstop for some bonds issued by Midvale. For severalyears, proposals to modify
      the sales tax distribution formula have been discussed. There has been pressure to change
      the formula to reward population, rather than providing revenue in the City the sales tax
      was collected. As a small city with a significant sales tax base, it is likely that any change to
      the distribution formula that increases the amount distributed based on population will
      negatively impact Midvale. Midvale City would strongly oppose any formula that would
      modify the sales tax distribution formula.

      Resources to Support Quality Development: Midvale has been successful in working with
      the private sector to turn over 400 acres of contaminated brownfields into a mixed-use
      center that benefits the city, region, and state. Todaythe Bingham Junction andJordan
      Bluffs Project Areas include the corporate headquarters for Savage, Overstock, FLSmidth,
      CHG Hea lthca re, and zagg, as well as ma.ior facilities for lntermountain Healthcare and
      Zions Bank. ln addition to the office uses, the project areas include 3,000 units of housing,
      with a mix of types and income levels. ln order to facilitate this transformation Midvale
      utilized state approved financial tools, including Community Reinvestment Areas, the State
      lnfrastructure Bank, and direct transportation funding allocations. ln addition to the
      continued development of the Bingham Junction and Jordan Bluffs Project Areas, other
      areas such as the 7200 South Corridor and State Street require public and private
      investment to transform into vibrant mixed-use corridors that support the needs of current
      and future residents, as well as addressing issues of crime and blight. ln order to achieve
      this vision, ongoing state participation will be required. Midvale would encourage the
      State's ongoing support for Community Reinvestment Areas (CRAs), the State
      lnfrastructure Bank (SlB), and Housing and Transit Reinvestment Zones (HTRZs). These
      tools support the development of parklng structures, mixed-use development, and public
      infrastructure that create great neighborhoods, but that are not achievable by through the
      partnership of Cities and the private sector alone.


      Point of the Mountain Development: A partnership between Midvale City, local taxin8
      entities, and the State of Utah have invested in converting over 400 acres of contaminated
      brownfields into a mixed-use center that benefits the city, region, and state. These
      investments were sufficient to overcome the barriers to development associated with the
       brownfields, but not enouBh to give developers a windfall. Midvale is concerned that State
       policies may give advantages to the development at the Point of Mountain that will hurt
       the ability of Midvale and other communities to compete in the market. Midvale would
       encourage the State to hold the development of the Point of the Mountain to the same
       standards, tools, and resources available to other communities and not provide subsidies
       that would unfairly affect the market.

       Land Use Control: Mldvale has experienced significant growth over the past decade in both
           residential and commercial development. Recent changes to land use regulations by the
           legislature have been concerning because they are broad in nature and do not take into
       account the unique character of neighborhoods or communities. Midvale has supported
       residential development, including mixed-income communities and varying unit types. We
       recognize that not every community has planned for or acted on addressing Utah's housing
       crisis, however Midvale would encourage the legislature to consider incentivizing needed
       development rather than creating one size fits all legislation. Midvale feels strongly that
       land use regulation is one of municipal governments most important roles, because it is
       closest to the people.
