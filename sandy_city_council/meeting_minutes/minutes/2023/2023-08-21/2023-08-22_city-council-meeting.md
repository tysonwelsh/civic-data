                                  tax budgeted revenue by 14.87% above last year's property tax budgeted

                                  residence would increase from $317.77 to $365.09, which is $47.32 per 
                                  year. The Sandy City tax on a $628,000 business would increase from 
                                  $577.76 to $663.80, which is $86.04 per year.

                                  At 6:00 pm, The Council Chair convened the Truth in Taxation Public Hearing. 
                                  Council Chair D'Sousa expressed to all in attendance, the need for decorum and 
                                  professionalism during this hearing. Both Agenda Items 3 and 4 will be discussed and 
                                  included in this Public Hearing. Following the Public Hearing, the Council will move to 
                                  Item 1 on the Agenda and hear General Citizen Comment.

                                  Mayor Zoltanski introduced the item and provided background information regarding the 
                                  city's budget process. The City Budget is a collaborative process that began thirteen 
                                  months ago. Our main goal during this year's budget process was to maintain our city's 
                                  high standards of excellence in public safety and citizen services. Sandy is a well-run 
                                  city and our residents expect and receive a high level of service. Mayor Zoltanski spoke 
                                  about the financial impact a 15% tax rate increase would have on the average homeowner 
                                  in Sandy. She cited the example of a typical homeowner who currently pays $317 per 
                                  year in property taxes would see an increase to $365 per year if the tax rate proposed 
                                  this evening passes. As the Sandy City Mayor, she fully endorsed the tentative budget 
                                  and corresponding tax rate increase of approximately 15% so that our city can continue 
                                  to deliver and maintain the high standards relative to our services provided by the City. 
                                  Mayor Zoltanski introduced Brian Kelly, Finance Director to present to the Council.

                                  Brian Kelley, introduced the FY2024 Truth in Taxation Hearing presentation and provided 
                                  an overview of the budget process. The city gathers citizen feedback throughout the year 
                                  and this feedback is considered and taken into account when creating each fiscal year's 
                                  budget. The Finance Department staff develops short and long term forcasts, finalize 
                                  internal service and administrative cost studies, reviews department budget requests, fee 
                                  schedules and revenue forcasts, and finalizes the proposed compensation plan. Based 
                                  on this information, the budget is prepared and the tentative budget is presented to the 
                                  Council in May. Mr. Kelley reviewed the ten key challenges impacting this year's tentative 
                                  budget: meeting citizen service expectations, public safety service needs, historic 
                                  inflation, employee retention, fleet replacement, reinvesting for the future, community

                   Council Member Brooke D'Sousa expressed appreciation for all the diverse feedback. 
                   This has been a difficult process and it is always difficult to raise taxes. The city's 
                   budgets are reviewed every year and reflect the changing needs of the residents and the 
                   city. This year, the city was faced with $90 million in capital improvements. While this 
                   need cannot be addressed in one year, it does require a long-term strategy. While this 
                   year's budget is not perfect, it addressed the impact of inflation and a tight labor market 
                   and moves in the direction of covering core city services. She is aware this may place a 
                   strain on some residents, and so while this is a difficult decision, she expressed support 
                   for the proposed tax rate increase. She is supportive of the city's continued efforts to 
                   increase economic revenues through sales tax and redevelopment projects. While the 
                   city has always shied away from increasing property taxes, Sandy City is facing 
                   challenges and is on the precipice of needing to prioritize services. Taking this into 
                   account, Council Member D'Sousa is supportive of the proposed tax rate increase.
