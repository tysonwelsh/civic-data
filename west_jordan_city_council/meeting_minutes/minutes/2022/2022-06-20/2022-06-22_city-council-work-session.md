A.     Agency Meetings
The City Council held the Fairway Special Service Recreation District, Municipal Building Authority,
and Redevelopment Agency meetings, where they adopted and amended budgets for each.

The Council reconvened in Work Session at 5:46 pm

B.     Moderate Housing Discussion
City Planner/Zoning Administrator Larry Gardner explained changes to State requirements for
Moderate Housing Plans. The City was required to adopt four moderate income housing strategies
(with measures and benchmarks) by October 1, 2022 or adopt six strategies to qualify for priority
consideration for funding by the Transportation Commission. Mr. Gardner stated “affordable
housing” was considered housing attainable for anyone in the moderate-income range. Moderate
income was defined as 80% or less of the Salt Lake County median gross income, which was
reported to be $86,000 per year (80% of $86,000 = $68,800). He spoke of current residential prices,
and stated renting or purchasing a home had become very difficult. Mr. Gardner felt the State
seemed to think high density residential equaled moderate-income housing but he did not believe
that was necessarily the case.

Mr. Gardner noted that the Planning Commission reviewed and recommended six strategies, with
measures and benchmarks. Council Member Jacob shared calculations he made using the guideline
that housing would cost 30% of an annual income and expressed the opinion that housing would be
very difficult to find at the reported median gross income level.

Chair Whitelock suggested the Council consider adopting the following strategy: demonstrate
creation of, or participation in, a community land trust program for moderate income housing.
Council Member McConnehey felt the strategy regarding station area plans stood out to him. Chair
Whitelock was strongly opposed to the strategy regarding reduced parking requirements. Council

C.      Short Term Rentals / Boarding House Discussion
Chair Whitelock and Vice Chair Green added discussion of short-term housing rentals to the work
session agenda in response a constituent question and concern. Chair Whitelock believed guardrails
needed to be in place, including parking and noise restrictions, a local contact available 24/7 listed
on the permit in case corrective action was needed, a method for neighbors to report issues without
using law enforcement, and a limit placed on the number of short-term rentals allowed. She stated
“short-term rental” was defined as 30 days or less. She spoke of short-term rental situations used
for long, loud parties.

Council Member Pack hoped property owners renting a home short-term or long-term would want
to vet those staying in the home. He spoke of property rights, and emphasized the need to look at
both sides of the issue with any action taken by the Council. Chair Whitelock agreed with the need
to respect property rights, but believed the question was whether or not the City wanted single-
family homes or residential units that were more like hotels. To her, a short-term rental was a
business in single-family residential. Chair Whitelock commented that behavior in a hotel situation
was, for many people, different from behavior in their own home.

Council Member Jacob pointed out that big, loud parties were already regulated for everyone in City
Code. Chair Whitelock did not think existing restrictions worked. Council Member Bloom suggested
some sort of regulation should be in place for corporations purchasing houses and turning them
into rentals. Council Member Worthen asked if a distinction could be made between purchase by a
business versus purchase by a private individual. Assistant City Attorney David Quealy did not think
the City could legally restrict what could be done with a property based on ownership. Council
Member Bloom suggested the City should separately address the issue of corporations purchasing
large numbers of residential units and turning them into rentals. She felt the situation was causing
havoc in the local homebuying industry.

Council Member McConnehey cautioned against demonizing short-term rentals. He pointed out that
problems also occurred in owner-occupied residential units. He shared a positive personal
experience with a short-term rental. Council Member McConnehey suggested the City could
