Washington County Land Use Authority Meeting
October 12, 2021

The Public Meeting of the Planning Commission of Washington County was called to order by
Chairman Brandon Anderson at 1:30 p.m. on October 12, 2021, in the Washington County
Commission Chambers and via Zoom, at 197 East Tabernacle Street in St. George, Utah.
Comunissioners in attendance were Brandon Anderson, Kevin Jones, Slade Hughes, Frank Leuck,
Reed Scow, and Mark Owens. County Staff in attendance were Scott Messel, Community
Development Director; Victoria Hales, Deputy Attorney; Ron Whitehead, Public Works Director;
Todd Edwards, County Engineer; Laura Thurber, Planning and Permits Specialist; Kurt Gardner,
Building Official; Kyle Layton, County Building Inspector; Brent Cardall, Zoning and Code
Specialist.

Chairman Anderson welcomed everyone and let the Pledge of Allegiance. He then explained
meeting protocol and turned the time over to Mr. Messel for the first agenda item.

1. CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT / PUBLIC MEETING. Review a Conditional Use
Permit for Above Zion, a Rural Recreation Grounds and Facility Project located in Kolob
on Parcels 102-A-49-1-B-HV, 71-C-HV, 102-A-49-1-C-HV, 102-A-50-HV, 98-A-3-HV,
102-A-45-HV, 116-HV, 98-A-2-HV and 102-A-49-1-A-1-HV. The applicant is Zions Gate
Management Co. The acreage has zoning designations of Open Space Transition, Open
Space Conservation, and Seasonal Forest Residential. Applicant has requested conditional
use approval of a project that has 442 units under the formerly existing provisions for Rural
Recreation Grounds and Facilities. The proposed units include cabins, yurts, tree house
units, glamping units, improved campgrounds and related facilities. The Planning
Commission will determine whether the conditional uses requested by the applicant will
be approved, if approved the conditions for approval, or denied.

Mr. Messel stated that the agenda item is a Conditional Use Request for a proposed glamping
facility called Above Zion. At the time the application was submitted, the county code allowed
this type of development as a conditional use under the term Rural Recreational Grounds, and
although the code has changed, the application is being processed under the code in which it was
submitted.

He went on to explain that the project is approximately 1,200 acres in size on Kolob Mountain.
The acreage consists of Seasonal Forest Residential, Open Space Transition and Open Space
Conservation Zones. The project has been broken up into several phases with different names and
Mr. Messel went on to explain the project plans for each phase, including lighting, parking, unit
numbers, and unit types. He explained that the overall density is approximately one unit for every
two acres. Mr. Messel stated a conditional use is an administrative decision and that there are
conditions and requirements set out in both State Code and County Code as to what improvements
need to be made and what is required of a conditional use.

He communicated that the process for today’s meeting would be allowing the applicant to present,
then afterwards, county staff, water conservancy district and other separate entities that have

Mr. Baird, the council for the applicant, introduced himself and his team. He stated they had not
had time to review the proposed conditions in detail and respond to them. Instead, he stated he
hopes to hear from the applicable service providers and ask questions to fully understand the
proposed conditions of approval. He went on to give a few examples of questions concerning the
conditions of approval from various entities. He stated that his team is not in a position to come
back with a detailed response and didn’t know whether they would be ready in time for the
November meeting, or not. He explained that they are not in a hurry and want to do the process
right.

Chairman Anderson asked the Planning Commission if anyone had any comments to add or
whether they should allow the service providers to explain some of their conditions and then
reconvene and at that point.

Mr. Messel ran through the Community Development Department’s conditions for the project,
including general review standards such as site plans, traffic safety, parking areas, emergency
access and utility capacity. He stated there shall not be any increase of the maximum number of
units and the development of each phase shall be subject to the approval of the applicable land use
authority. At this time, the Planning Commission is the land use authority and it must meet all the
requirements of County Code Chapter 10. Ms. Hales agreed with Mr. Messel that all laws must be
followed and the County is able to ask for additional conditions, if they are necessary, to mitigate
the detrimental effects of the proposed use. She stated the County has listed 28 conditions it feels
are related to the detrimental effects of the proposed project. Mr. Messel then asked the County
Building Official to explain the conditions of the building department. Mr. Baird said that they
have no issues with the conditions that have been listed by the building official and would like to
move forward and state that his team has no issues with Conditions 14 through 17, does not have
any issues with Conditions 24 through 25, and no issues with Condition 27.

Ms. Hales then stated that the reasonable condition for the Clerk’s office is that a business license
would be required for any business that takes place in Washington County and proceeded to turn
the time over to the Public Works Department. Mr. Whitehead introduced himself and spoke
briefly on each condition presented by the department. Conditions included secondary access and
emergency access to be improved, the traffic and impact analysis study be implemented, a detailed
phasing plan submitted, directional signage, meet septic density requirements, and the project must
be subject to Washington County Hillside Development requirements. Further discussion was with
the applicant’s council about Public Works’ conditions.

Ms. Hales turned the time over to Hurricane Valley Fire District for their presentation of reasonable
conditions. Mr. Gildea introduced himself and explained that the department sent the proposed
project out for third party review to avoid a conflict of interest being that a board member owns a
piece of property on Kolob. Water supply for fire must be addressed and the plans submitted do
not contain enough information to determine exact water needs to meet the standards for Hurricane
Valley Fire District. He stated that currently there is some fire hydrant coverage on Kolob, but no
fire hydrants exist in the proposed areas for this project. No water supply details have been

Emergency response times were discussed. Mr. Gildea explained that there is currently no fire
station on Kolob and the closest station is in Virgin and is manned by volunteers who respond
from home. The response times from Virgin to calls on Kolob will be in excess of 30 minutes for
a fire unit. The closest manned station with an ambulance is the station in Hurricane and response
times will be in excess of 40 minutes. He stated that Hurricane Valley Fire is uncomfortable having
commercial development this far from fire and emergency resources. Further discussion ensued
about fire protection needs.

Ms. Esplin from the Washington County Water Conversancy District stated that the District has
not seen any application for the proposed conditional use, so when they fill out the application they
will be able to evaluate the project for water and wastewater. Commissioner Hughes asked Ms.
Esplin if the District will be ok if the applicants comply with all applicable State Codes? Ms. Esplin
agreed.

Mr. Messel turned the time over to Mr. Bradybaugh with Zion National Park. Mr. Bradybaugh
expressed concern over water rights and that the application does not state how the necessary water
rights would be obtained. Detailed discussion of water rights agreements between the Park,
Conservancy District and State Engineer was held. Mr. Bradybaugh pointed out that there is a
discrepancy in the required amounts for water capacity, and that the wastewater may also be
underestimated as well. The soils testing for the proposed septic system was not particular to the
site that is currently being discussed, and certainly needs to be corrected. He also mentioned the
importance of the wildlife habitat in the Kolob area and that the proposed project could have
impacts on the local species.

A five-minute break was agreed upon and subsequently, the podium was then turned over to
Avenue Consulting for a presentation on their submitted traffic report. Mr. Amundsen introduced
himself and proceeded to explain that the County engaged the firm to evaluate the proposed project
site’s roadways and determine what standards the County would apply to development such as
this. He went on to explain that the firm visited the site in August and has determined that the
development is anticipated to generate 1,500 vehicles per day on weekdays and 3,800 vehicles on
weekends at full build-out. At least two improved accesses to the development are required and a
design speed of 35 miles per hour is required. The installation of a southbound right turn
acceleration lane at the intersection of SR9 and Kolob Terrace is also warranted.

Ms. Hales pointed out that the County subdivision ordinance relates to the division or development
of land, and so we believe that the subdivision ordinance applies. The subdivision ordinance would
create an orderly development where easements are identified and the infrastructure lines would
be identified and dedicated. Ms. Hales stated that the County is requesting the applicant bring in
each project by phase and that they create a subdivision plat.

Discussion was had whether to table the item until the next month’s Planning Commission so the
applicant has time to address the conditions set forth by the County. Mr. Baird that the Planning
Commission table to a date certain, on November 9". Ms. Hales stated that she felt comfortable
with that. Chairman Anderson stated that no public comment will be taken at this time, and the
