 5. PUBLIC HEARINGS
       a. Ordinance No. 23-09 amending Title 13-13-10(G) “Landscaping Requirements” and
          13-18-10(E)(2) “Urban Forestry” changing the clearance of tree canopy height from
          15 feet to 14 feet and make other clarifying changes

           Council Member Worthen said a constituent reached out to her regarding the City tree
           height ordinance. Council Member Worthen explained her recommendation to reduce the
           tree canopy height requirement in West Jordan from 15 feet to 14 feet, and said she found


City Council Meeting Minutes               Wednesday, March 22, 2023                          Page 2
           through research that no other city had a 15-foot tree height requirement. Mr. Anderson
           said the Planning Commission reviewed the matter and forwarded a positive
           recommendation at 15 feet.

           Chair McConnehey opened a public hearing at 6:47 pm
           Comments:
           None
           Chair McConnehey closed the public hearing at 6:47 pm

           Council Member Green commented that the damage done to trucks was all at the 10-foot
           level. He spoke in favor of picking a consistent spot to measure from rather than “all
           perpendicular heights”, which he viewed as a moving target. Council Member Green
           suggested postponing the matter to work on the language.

           Council Member Worthen said 14 feet was determined based on research and
           conversations with staff making sure trucks would not damage trees or get damaged by
           trees. Vice Chair Bloom referred to December 2022 Council Meeting minutes indicating
           the intent of the Council had been 14 feet above any portion of the parking lane or travel
           lanes. Council Member Jacob said it did not seem difficult to him to carry a pole and trim
           a tree to 14 feet above any point of the street. He said he was comfortable with the
           language.

           Chair McConnehey referred to language recommended by the Planning Commission,
           including “15 feet” and “gravitationally vertical”. He pointed out that urban foresters also
           recommended staying with the 15 feet. Chair McConnehey said the legal height for
           commercial vehicles designated by UDOT was 14 feet and said he would not want to
           reduce the tree canopy height below 15 feet.

           Council Member Whitelock said she believed the Council’s job was to listen to the
           residents. She expressed the opinion that if UDOT said trucks could not exceed 14 feet in
           height, it would be okay if trees were cut at 14 feet and above.

           Brock Hudson said the officers usually measured from edge of asphalt. He said they had
           poles that extended up to 15 feet. He said sidewalks were measured from several different
           locations.

           Responding to a question from Chair McConnehey, Mr. Clegg said the tallest City vehicle
           was the Hydrovac at 12 feet 2 inches high. He commented that sweeper trucks and
           garbage trucks drove right next to the curb.

           Vice Chair Bloom moved to APPROVE Ordinance No. 23-09 amending Title 13-13-
           10(G) “Landscaping Requirements” and 13-18-10(E)(2) “Urban Forestry” changing
           the clearance of tree canopy height from 15 feet to 14 feet and make other clarifying
           language. Council Member Worthen seconded the motion.

           Council Member Green felt the motion should refer to Attachment 2.

 6. BUSINESS ITEMS
       a. Resolution No. 23-015 approving an interlocal agreement with the Salt Lake County
          Clerk to administer the 2023 municipal elections
          City Recorder Tangee Sloan presented an interlocal agreement with the Salt Lake County
          Clerk for administration of the 2023 municipal elections for $225,995. She explained that
          the initial agreement she received was for approximately $30,000 more but was reduced
          after she asked the County to reevaluate the amount. Ms. Sloan showed the planned
          location of a new ballot box at Ron Wood Park, and said she was working on finding a
          location for a third ballot box. Responding to a question from Council Member Pack, Ms.
          Sloan explained that ballot boxes would include a solar powered camera during election
          season for security.

           Council Member Jacob expressed concern with the planned ballot box location at Ron
           Wood Park considering the food truck events held at that location. Ms. Sloan said staff had
           a plan for moving the food trucks to a different spot during an election.

           Council Member Jacob asked how the potential decision to change to ranked choice voting
           would impact the proposed agreement. Council Office Director Alan Anderson said the
           deadline to make a formal decision to participate in ranked choice voting was April 28th.
           Ms. Sloan said the contract with Salt Lake County would not need to be modified, but she
           was not prepared that evening to discuss ranked choice voting. The ranked choice voting
           discussion would need to be brought back to the Council.

           Council Member Worthen suggested placing a third ballot box at Public Works.

                    approving an interlocal agreement with the Salt Lake County Clerk to

       b. Ordinance No. 23-12 an ordinance amending the 2009 West Jordan City
          Code (General Business Regulations and Licensing Requirements; amending city
          code sections 4-1A-1 through 4-1D-5 inclusive)
          Code Enforcement Manager Brock Hudson oriented the Council with proposed
          amendments to the Business License Regulation Code. Council Member Green said he
          thought the proposed amendments were a great start, but did not go far enough. He said
          he was concerned about language requiring business license applicants to be adults, and
          expressed the opinion that some of the language was overly restrictive. Council Member
          Green suggested delaying the amendments to give the City’s two new business license
          coordinators an opportunity to look at the language.

           Council Member Jacob suggested including a definition of the term “personal benefit” in
           the document. Council Member Whitelock said she would agree with having additional
           individuals look at the document, and said she thought the City could do better. Council
           Member Whitelock questioned why the City would care if a business remained closed for
           more than 10 days at a time.

           Responding to a question from Council Member Whitelock, Paul Dodd clarified that
           language referring to Class B misdemeanors only applied if the misdemeanor was related
           to the type of business. Council Member Whitelock said she understood the move to
           online applications but said she would still want paper applications to be an option.
           Mayor Burton said he believed a computer could be provided in the office where staff
           could help an applicant fill out an online application if needed. Mr. Hudson confirmed a
           computer kiosk would be available at City Hall for business license applications.

           Chair McConnehey said there were a number of changes he would like to see to the
           document, but he was willing to vote in favor of approval, with the understanding that
           additional changes would be brought back. Council Member Green said he would prefer
           to approve all changes at one time.

       c. Ordinance No. 23-11 amending 2009 West Jordan City Code (Provisions Regarding
          Conditional Use Permits and Appeals, to conform to recent revisions to Utah Code Ann.
          Section 10-9a-507; specifically amending City Code Sections 13-7e-1 through 13-7e-
          13 inclusive and Sections 15-5-1, 15-5-3, 15-5-6, and 15-6-1)
          Council Member Green asked why the matter was not scheduled for public hearing,
          considering the substantial changes regarding land use. Council Office Director Alan
          Anderson said a public hearing was held regarding conditional use permits on November
          16, 2022. Council Member Green said he believed substantial changes had been made and
          proposed postponing the matter to schedule a public hearing.

           Council Member Green moved to postpone the item to hold a public hearing to be
           scheduled at the discretion of Council Leadership. The motion died for lack of a
           second.

           City Planner/Zoning Administrator Larry Gardner presented proposed amendments to
           City Code regarding Conditional Use Permits to conform with recent revisions to Utah
           Code. Council Member Green said he submitted substitute language to clean up criteria
           language. Council Member Green said he knew the Property Ombudsman would prefer
           standards to be more specific and commented that the State put burden of proof on the
           City to make sure conditions were imposed correctly, based on substantial evidence.
           Council Member Green explained his substitute language and suggested further
           discussion as a Committee of the Whole.

           Chair McConnehey said minutes of a previous meeting showed the Council voted
           unanimously to bring the matter back to the Committee of the Whole. He suggested the
           Council could (a) bring the matter back to the Committee of the Whole, or (b) if a majority
           of the Council were comfortable with the alternate language provided by Council Member
           Green, the Council Office could be asked to coordinate with the Mayor’s Office to have legal
           counsel review the language and bring it back to the Council for a vote.

           Vice Chair Bloom and Council Member Whitelock spoke in favor of having legal counsel
           review the alternate language (option b).

           Council Member Green moved to have Council Office work with the Mayor’s office
           for option b and bring it back to the Council for a vote at a time determined by
           Council Leadership to hold a public hearing. Chair McConnehey seconded the
           motion.

           City Attorney Paul Dodd said the public had an opportunity to comment on the sections
           in question during a public hearing held on November 16, 2022.
