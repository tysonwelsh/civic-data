6:00 PM – WORKSHOP
   • Employee Policy: Telework on Red Air Quality Days

Rori Andreason discussed the handout regarding the Remote Work Policy Pilot
Program.

Matt Dahl discussed the pilot program and reiterated that if employees are working from
home one day a week, a bad air day does not permit them to take another day. He also
said those employees that want to come into work, can.

Council Member Bryant Brown said he likes the program but does not want it to be a
mandatory program.

Council Member Dustin Gettel recommended starting with the mandatory air action
days and making it voluntary, but highly encouraged, for employees to work from home.
He said this policy is very popular with the State; employees really like it. He said the
more cities that incorporate this type of policy will greatly reduce the bad air quality.

Mariah Hill suggested employees be encouraged to use alternative means of travel as
well.

VI.     ACTION ITEM
        A.    CONSIDER RESOLUTION NO. 2024-R-41 AUTHORIZING THE MAYOR
              TO SIGN THE COMMUNITY RENEWABLE ENERGY AGENCY
              INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT AND COMMUNITY RENEWABLE ENERGY
              PROGRAM AGREEMENT, APPROVING THE PLAN FOR LOW-INCOME
              ASSISTANCE FOR MIDVALE, AND APPOINTING MIDVALE CITY
              REPRESENTATIVES TO THE COMMUNITY RENEWABLE AGENCY
              BOARD.
Erin Summers said the Community Renewable Energy Agency (Agency), also known as
Utah Renewable Communities Program, was established under HB411, in 2019 (Utah
Code Ann. § 54-17-901). The Agency is an interlocal entity, made up of Utah communities
who have their electricity provided entirely, or in part, by Rocky Mountain Power (RMP).
The Agency collaborates directly with RMP to provide net-100% renewable electricity by
2030 to participating communities. The renewable energy assets needed for the program
must be acquired through a competitive solicitation process (such as a request for
proposals) and must provide RMP with the option to own or purchase the assets. The
Agency will not own any assets, including real property (as per section 12(e) of the
interlocal agreement). The cost for obtaining and maintaining the assets will be covered
through the participating customer rates and not by the Agency members. Each member
of the Agency is responsible for paying a portion of the organization’s operational costs
(legal and technical consultants, RMP program design and filing, etc.), which is calculated
by both population and electricity usage. Participating customers in member communities
will see anywhere from $2 to $7 extra in their monthly energy bills due to the higher price
of renewable energy production, unless they are a part of the Low-Income Lifeline
Program (LILP). LILP enrollees will receive a credit on the monthly bill to offset the
increase in cost from the program. RMP customers in member communities who are not
interested in participating in the program will have the opportunity to opt-out of the
program.

The interlocal agreement has a term of 50 years, and the utility agreement with RMP will
remain in effect as long as the program is in effect and the communities have residents
who are participating customers. Entities can withdraw from the Program with 30 days’
notice prior to the passage of an ordinance. In the event of a withdrawal from the program,
any payments already made will not be refunded. Should the Agency dissolve, any
unspent fund balances will be returned to the communities.

On June 18, Midvale City Council passed Resolution 2024-R-26 authorizing Mayor
Stevenson to submit an application on behalf of Midvale City. To move forward with
joining the Agency, Midvale needs to sign onto the Community Renewable Energy
Agency Interlocal Agreement and Community Renewable Energy Program Agreement,
approve the Plan for Low-Income Assistance for Midvale, pay a $11,000 fee, and appoint
a representative to the Agency board, by August 31, 2024. Midvale City will have to pay
an additional $11,000 fee by August 31, 2025, as well as pass an ordinance outlining
details of the final program, if the City Council determines that they want to implement the
program.

Christopher Thomas, Sr. Energy and Climate Program Manager with the Salt Lake City
Sustainability Department, said there is currently no way for a city to join after the
application is submitted to the Utah Public Service Commission.

Garrett Wilcox said he understands the program to be right sized for the communities that
initially join and cities that join later would have to do their own program based on their
city and any others that join with them.

Christopher Thomas agreed with what Garret Wilcox said. He then explained that the
program is in its infancy and the board will first seek to acquire energy from a resource.
For the power purchase agreement to go into effect, the board must raise a reserve
amount to cover the cost of the power purchase agreement. The reserve fund can be
used to cover the portion of the power purchase agreement left if customers have opted
out.

Garrett Wilcox said he believes that the cost would be between two to seven dollars per
customer. Once the program is adopted and in place the individual cities are not obligated
to each other.

Christopher Thomas said that he anticipates that Rocky Mountain Power will insist on a
termination clause so that if the program ceases to exist, the power purchase agreement
is legally void.

Matt Dahl reminded everyone that this is a due diligence phase so the City can see what
the final project will look like. The City can still say no.

Garrett Wilcox made it clear that the City would be joining an Interlocal Agreement that
night if the Resolution is passed.

Council Member Dustin Gettel said as concerns about other cities participation and if the
City is bound for 50 years or can a future Council terminate the program?

Garret Wilcox said based on other similar agreements, this agreement will be binding.

Council Member Dustin Gettel said he wants to know if the fee could be raised in the
future.

Erinn Summers said that there is a cap, and costs would raise for everyone if there were
a drop in participation.

Christopher Thomas said if a price adjustment were needed it would have to be approved
by everyone involved, but there is a 10% cap.
