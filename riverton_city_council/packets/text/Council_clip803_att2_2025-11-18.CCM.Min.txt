37          Group Homes
38
39   Kevin Hicks, City Manager, explained that the residential facilities section of the code applied to
40   all residential zones and was driven by federal requirements intended to ensure disabled
41   individuals had access to affordable housing. He reviewed how the code defined a dwelling and
42   a family, noting that more than four unrelated individuals living together required additional
43   approval. He outlined that the City used the business licensing process primarily as a tracking
44   tool for these facilities and that standard residential requirements, such as vehicle parking on
45   public streets, applied the same as they would for any family. He also noted that any operation
46   functioning as a day or outpatient treatment center would require a conditional use permit. He
47   described the reasonable accommodation process for facilities seeking to house more than four
48   unrelated persons, including an application reviewed by the Development Services Director and
49   a final decision made by a hearing officer with expertise in ADA and federal housing law.
50
51   Councilmember Pierucci asked how “dangerous” was defined and who determined it, noting that
     Page | 2 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     November 18, 2025



 1   the term carried significant weight. In response, Ryan Carter, City Attorney, said the definition
 2   was written in the City code, which prohibited facilities from housing anyone who posed a direct
 3   threat to others’ health or safety or who was likely to cause substantial property damage. He
 4   explained that determining danger could involve reviewing a person’s history, medical
 5   information, or behaviors such as severe outbursts or wandering.
 6
 7   Mr. Carter explained that, when determining what constituted a direct threat, a hearing officer
 8   could consider whether an applicant had a documented history of violence, especially in the
 9   context of group living where residents often shared rooms. He stated that the combination of a
10   shared, intimate living environment and a person’s violent background could reasonably be
11   viewed as a direct threat, allowing the City to prohibit that individual from residing in the facility
12   as a condition of approval. He also noted that individuals considered dangerous under federal
13   law were not treated the same as individuals with disabilities and did not receive the same
14   protections. A facility that repeatedly accepted or housed dangerous individuals could ultimately
15   lose its status.
16
17   Councilmember Pierucci asked whether the City could further define “direct threat” or
18   “dangerous” in the code to make the standard more explicit when approving these facilities. Mr.
19   Carter explained that narrowing the definition could actually be counterproductive, because a
20   rigid list might exclude concerning situations that didn’t fit the exact criteria. He said it was more
21   effective to keep the standard broad and rely on an experienced hearing officer to evaluate each
22   case.
23
24   Mr. Hicks explained that the City currently had 11 licensed residential facilities, with three more
25   in the approval process. He outlined two proposed changes in the fee schedule. First, because
26   these facilities require additional staff time compared to typical businesses, staff recommended
27   creating an annual licensing fee specific to this type of use. Second, staff proposed establishing
28   a fee structure for reasonable accommodation requests. Since hiring a hearing officer is costly,
29   the proposal included requiring an initial deposit that would be applied to the final invoice for the
30   hearing officer’s services. Any remaining balance would be either refunded or billed before the
31   license was issued.
32
33   Councilmember Pierucci expressed concern about the definitions in the code and indicated a
34   desire to discuss them further. He also raised a concern that the City had not historically
35   enforced the four-unrelated-person limit. He emphasized the need for public accountability so
36   residents could understand past missteps, while acknowledging the City’s need to maintain
37   enforcement authority. In response, Mr. Hicks explained that the code contains two definitions of
38   “family”: one limiting unrelated persons to four, and another defining a family as a group of
39   people who have consented to live together. He noted that prior interpretations had not been
40   consistently applied in all cases, such as with certain student housing, and recognized the need
41   to clarify enforcement going forward.
42
43   Mayor Staggs said that the issue was not a misinterpretation of the code, but rather that
44   enforcement had been lax. He explained that staff’s current efforts aim to formalize and clarify
45   the administrative process going forward. Historically, existing facilities only obtained a business
46   license without specifying the number of occupants. Moving forward, outpatient facilities will be
47   processed through a conditional use permit, while inpatient facilities will continue under federal
48   law. The standard four-unrelated-person limit will be enforced, with any requests for additional
49   occupants handled through a reasonable accommodation request reviewed by a qualified
50   hearing officer. The $4,000 deposit in the fee schedule is intended to cover administrative costs
51   without burdening Riverton taxpayers.
