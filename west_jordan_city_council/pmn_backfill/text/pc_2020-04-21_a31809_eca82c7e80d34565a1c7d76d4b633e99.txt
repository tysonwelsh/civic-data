3.    2020 Jordan Landing Sign Plan; 3774 West Center View Way; Amended Sign Program;
      SC-3 Zone; Plaza at Jordan Landing, LLC (applicant) [#20668; parcel 21-29-176-003]

Mark Forsythe said that in 2016 Jordan Landing refurbished their signs to a more contemporary look.
The current proposal includes a pylon sign to the east of the entertainment village that was excluded in
2016, and a large wall sign will be added on the southwest corner of the new Rush Funplex building
south of the theater. Pylon signs and wall signs are common in the SC-3 zone, so the changes are
appropriate for a commercial center. Minimum separation distances have been met. He showed the
existing and proposed renderings. Conditions of approval are the same as the 2016 plan adding a
requirement that there are no flashing or strobing lights on the wall sign. There may be a gradual
fading to different colors.

Ammon Allen said the lighting on that corner might be distracting to drivers. He asked if there any
similar situations in the city that have a gradual fading.

Mark Forsythe said the closest thing might be an LED sign, but Jordan Landing is not proposing an
electronic message center. It will just be backlighting.

Dan Milich, applicant, said they missed the pylon sign with the prior review. When Rush Funplex was
proposed to the Commission, this sign was shown as a place of reference and context. This is a large
building and the view of Cinemark will be blocked from Jordan Landing Boulevard. This sign will also
showcase the users on the redeveloped plaza. There won't be flashing lights, but they will mostly be
fixed.

located at 3774 West Center View Way in an SC-3 zone, with the Conditions of Approval listed

6.    Copper Rim Clubhouse; 5959 West Verdigris Drive; Final Site Plan; P-C Zone; CW
      Land Co./Greg Day (applicant) [#20625; parcel 20-26-326-014]

Greg Day, applicant, said their project is under construction with roads, and they want to bring in the
clubhouse ahead of schedule.

Ray McCandless gave an overview of the request for the clubhouse that is a required amenity for
Copper Rim. The applicant proposed 17 parking spaces, but staff is recommending 24 based on the
parking code of one space per 150 square feet of floor and pool area. Staff would also like a decorative
masonry fence between the clubhouse and the homes to the east. A slatted steel fence will be installed
around the pool area. He showed the building elevations.

Staff recommended that the Planning Commission approve the final site plan for the Copper Rim
Clubhouse located at 5959 West Verdigris Drive in a P-C zone subject to the approval conditions listed
below:

1.     The proposed development shall meet all applicable 2009 City Code requirements.
2.     Approval of a Final Site Plan shall become null and void if development does not commence
       within two (2) years of final site plan approval (13.7B.5).
3.     Planning Commission approvals do not include Public Safety, Fire, Building and Safety, or
       Engineering approval.
4.     Provide 24 parking spaces in the parking lot near the clubhouse.
5.     Provide a 6’ decorative masonry wall along the west sides of lots 101, 102 and 103.

Matt Quinney asked why the applicant was asking for 17 stalls if the code requires 24, and if there is a
way to allow them to have only 17.

Ray McCandless explained that there are no parking standards in the code for a clubhouse within a
planned community zone, so they had to find the closest match in the zoning code. Staff felt that one
per 150 square feet for pool and clubhouse area would be the best fit. The more stalls the better. The
clubhouse would take 136 occupants and 88 occupants in the pool area. The increase in stalls will help
improve the use and keep cars off of the collector road.

Kent Shelton asked where the additional stalls would be located.

Greg Day said the parking lot will extend to the south with the same basic geometric shape. The plans
are drawn up that show the change. Clubhouse is an in-between use in the code, not quite used like a
gym. They looked for other standards that could justify 17 stalls, but they weren’t successful so they
will proceed with 24 spaces.

Kent Shelton asked what on-street parking availability will be.

Trish Hatch agreed that they want to encourage walking in a planned community such as this. She

11.   2009 West Jordan City Code Recommendation to City Council for Re-approving the
      entirety of Titles 5 (Environment and Health), 8 (Public Works, Public Ways and
      Property), 9 (Utilities), 10 (Building Regulations) and 11 (Land Disturbance) as presently
      constituted; City-wide applicability; City of West Jordan (applicant)

Nathan Nelson said this item is in the same situation as the last item. It is not known if the adoption
went through the Planning Commission originally, so this is for ratification.

Trish Hatch asked what the process is and how the codes were approved without going to the Planning
Commission.

Duncan Murray said both the ordinances and engineering standards were approved as ordinances but
not as land use regulations. LUDMA had major overhauls in 2003 and 2015, each time increasing the
realm or scope of what a land use regulation is, or what had to be approved by the Planning
Commission in a public hearing. There are a lot of ordinances that have always gone through the
Planning Commission, but over the years others have been added that are viewed as land use
regulations. Many of the Titles pre-date changes in the law. The City doesn’t want someone to say that
the ordinances are not applicable to them because they weren't adopted properly under current law.

Matt Quinney opened the public hearing.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

MOTION:        Corbin England moved, based on the information and findings set forth in the staff
               report and upon the evidence and explanations received today, to forward a
               positive recommendation to the City Council for re-approval as land use
               regulations the entirety of Titles 5, 8, 9, 10, and 11 as explained. The motion was
               seconded by Jay Thomas and passed 7-0 in favor.
