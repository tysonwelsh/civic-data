1.0   Report: Annual Sales Tax – Jared Zacharias

1.1   Mr. Zacharias began by explaining why they conduct an annual review of Draper’s Sales
      Tax data. First, he noted that sales taxes were the largest tax revenue for the city in FY20
      (2019-2020) and accounted for 42% of the General Funds revenue. Sales taxes also serve
      as an indicator of how the local economy is doing. In response to COVID-19, the FY21
      budget reduced expected sales tax revenues by $2,500,000. Looking at the sales taxes
      midway through the year helped to evaluate how Draper City has done by the prediction
      and budget adjustment. Finally, the City had committed a lot of revenue streams to bonds
      that would be paid based on sales tax earnings.

      Mr. Zacharias displayed a detailed graph. He pointed towards the slight curve in the blue
      bars of the graph and explained that the trend represented the last experienced recession.
      He noted that since this dip, Draper has seen a strong growth trend that sometimes had an
      11-13% increase in revenues from year to year. He pointed to this period because he
      predicted a similar decrease in revenues as a result of the pandemic, a dip that he did not
      feel was too severe, and that was followed by continued growth.

      Mr. Zacharias displayed a second graph that showed when residents were leaving their
      communities and spending their dollars somewhere else. He noted that there were a lot of
      residents from neighboring communities who came to Draper to spend their money.

      Before moving on with his presentation, Mr. Zacharias said that there were some key points
      to consider in regards to the data in the report. These points included:

         (1) The state does not list payer type in the monthly sales tax report. Payer type is
             therefore a manual process by which City staff researches and assigns a type for
             reporting purposes only.
         (2) Amounts reported do not reflect the amounts Draper City received in distributions.
             The amounts shown reflect 1% of actual sales. The amount Draper receives varies
             but is usually around 80% of the total.
         (3) The amounts for each month have been consolidated for better reporting. This was
             done by eliminating small tax filings.
         (4) Re-running the same report at a future date will not yield the same data. This is
             because monthly reports include data from past periods filed late. Thus, as time
             goes on the amount in a given period usually increases.

      Analysis of the types of sales taxpayers (auto dealers, online sales, grocery, etc.) shows
      that certain types of retail, specifically online sales, have been booming over the last couple
      of years. There was almost a 200% increase in online sales in the past five years. Mr.
      Zacharias also noted that the pie chart depicting the percentage of types of payers showed
      that Draper’s sales tax revenues came from diversified retailers. That meant that if one

                                                2
      industry experienced a decline, the City was still in a good and strong position to recover
      and get the needed revenues.

      When looking at how the pandemic had affected sales tax revenues, there was a decline in
      sales tax revenues in March and April, but a significant increase in June and July compared
      to previous years. Despite the short period of decline, when Mr. Zacharias totals all of the
      sales tax revenues to date that he has data available for, there was a strong upward trend
      (or increase) in sales tax revenues for FY21.

      Mr. Zacharias said that overall sales tax revenues were looking good.

1.2   Councilmember F. Lowry asked if Mr. Zacharias could explain why he had projected that
      the FY21 numbers would go down. Mr. Zacharias explained that the budget reflected a
      projection that the sales tax revenues would go down based on the numbers that the State
      put out on how much the pandemic might impact sales tax revenues. The reports indicated
      anywhere from a 4-18% decrease in sales tax revenues. Because of this, when the City
      built its budget in March and had no idea what was going to happen, they took a
      conservative approach and budgeted projected revenues around the worst-case scenario, an
      18% reduction in sales tax revenues, a $2.5 million reduction in what would normally be
      earned in sales tax revenues. The actual data, however, has not seen a reduction in sales
      tax revenues but has seen an increase. By combining how the City underbudgeted for the
      worst-case scenario, a reduction of $800,000 to date, and the increase in sales tax revenues
      that has been seen of $700,000, the City is $1.5 million ahead in sales tax revenues at this
      point in the fiscal year than anticipated in the FY21 operating budget.

2.0   Proclamation: Extra Mile Day

      Mayor Walker explained that whereas the City of Draper was a community that
      acknowledged that a special vibrancy exists within the entire community when its citizens
      collectively go the extra mile in personal effort, volunteerism, and service. He said that
      they were recognizing those individuals that had gone the extra mile.

      He then named the individuals who had gone the extra mile. First, he recognized Fire
      Battalion Chief Kevin Holt who oversaw the training of medical services and treatment
      from the fire department. Mayor Walker explained that the response to 911 calls had to
      change during the COVID-19 pandemic to provide additional protection to both the
      personnel and patients and their families. Chief Holt had worked tirelessly to update the
      response protocol in coordination with our Offline-Medical Control Doctor and the
      dispatch center. Chief Holt also put department policies in place to safeguard personnel
      from potential exposure while at work through the daily health temperature screening and
      ensuring staff had appropriate PPE for day-to-day operations.

      Then, Mayor Walker named three residents–Shawna Borg, Natalie Watson, and Lisa
      Dixon–who founded The Local Bee. The organization was founded to share their love for
      service in the community and unite it with their love of supporting local charities, shops,
      restaurants, and businesses. Every month The Local Bee partners with a new local charity
      to fulfill a specific need, host a simple monthly service opportunity for local friends,
      neighbors, and the community to participate in while highlighting local restaurants, shops,
      businesses, and organizations. Last month they partnered with For The Kids. Draper
      residents collected and donated 5,860 meals, enough to provide 732 complete weekend
      meal kits to kids. This month they intended to partner with the Granite Foundation to
      collect beauty kits to be included in the Santa Sacks program for elementary girls.

      Mayor Walker thanked The Local Bee and Chief Holt for going the extra mile. He said
      that Draper City was proud of them and their hard work and accomplishments.

6.0   Action Item: Motion to Reconsider a Decision regarding Ordinance #1457, the Short
      Subdivision Zoning Map Amendment Rezone (Legislative Action)
      The applicant has requested that the City Council reconsider this matter in order to
      provide additional information that was not provided to the City Council prior to its
      decision on October 6. Draper City Council Meeting Policies and Procedures allow
      the City Council to reconsider a prior decision. If the request to reconsider the
      application is approved, all interested persons will be provided an opportunity to
      address the City Council at a future City Council meeting.

6.1   David Dobbins, City Manager, said that the City Council’s vote tonight was to schedule
      another public hearing that would happen in November. He said that he had been in contact
      with the neighbors to explain what the process would be. Because the applicant felt that
      there was information that should have been provided to the City Council that wasn’t and
      that the neighbors feel as though they should have an opportunity to respond, he
      recommended that the Council approve the recommendation to reconsider the item. Staff
      will schedule a public hearing. The Council will get all the information, which the
      applicant will have the opportunity to speak to before the Council will make a final vote.
      He stated that their vote to reconsider the item tonight was not an indication of support or
      lack of support for the application. It was simply an action to re-open the public hearing
      to make sure that all of the information was provided to the Council.

      regarding Ordinance #1457 to be heard on October 6, 2020. Councilmember Roberts
      seconded the motion.

7.0   Public Hearing: Ordinances #1462 and #1463, Four Bears Estates Land Use Map
      Amendment and Zoning Map Amendment Request (Legislative Action), on the
      request of Todd Bradford, for a Land Use Map Amendment from Residential
      Low/Medium Density to Residential Medium Density and a Zoning Map Amendment
      from RA1 (Residential Agricultural, 40,000 square foot lot minimum) to R3 (Single-
      family Residential, 13,000 square foot lot minimum) on 0.96 acres of property located
      at 12758 South Boulter Street.

7.1   Jennifer Jastremsky, Planner, explained that the property under consideration was one lot
      north of Cherry Canyon Way on Boulter Street. The current land use designation was
      residential low-medium density which allowed for one to two units per acre. The applicant
      proposed to change the land use to the medium density residential land use designation
      which allowed for three to four units per acre. The property was adjacent to some
      residential medium density areas directly to the south of the property. The zoning
      designation was RA-1, one unit per acre zoning with a minimum of a 40,000 square foot
      lot zone. The applicant requested the R3 zone, a third-acre zoning designation with a
      minimum lot size of 13,000 square feet. The property was adjacent to the R3 zone to the
      south. The property was .96 acres and did not have enough square footage to allow for two
      half-acre lots. Because of this, the applicant could not request the RA-2 zone and be able
      to subdivide the property when taking into account dedication on both properties and what
      would be required for a flag lot stem or a private lane. Also, with the R3 zone, with the
      acreage of 0.96, when the private lane or the flag lot stem is considered, the property is not
      large enough to get three third acre lots. As a result, with the R3 zone, the property was
      looking at two 18,000 square foot lots. The item was recommended for approval by the
      Planning Commission with a favorable vote of 5-0.

7.2   Garrett Daw, the applicant, said that they were requesting to put two large third, almost
      half-acre lots on the parcel. He said that they were not trying to do three third acres. He
      said that there was a lot of redevelopment going on in the area and that this redevelopment
      would fall in line with what was already going on, and beautify the area. He said that the
      lot in question was run down with a lot of barns and unsavory things happening near the
      trail. He said that the developers had at times been contacted by the police. They felt that
      it would enhance and beautify the area. He requested that the item be approved so that they
      could move forward with creating two new lots.

7.3   Councilmember F. Lowry clarified that the applicant planned to take down the existing
      structures.

7.4   Mr. Daw said that was correct. They intended to get rid of everything, such as the barns,
      off the back of the property. He said that they did currently have someone interested in
      buying the front lot and taking down 80% of the existing house and potentially using some
      of the structure. Other than trying to keep some of the trees, most of the structures would
      be bulldozed and removed to build two new houses.

8.0   Action Item: Resolution #20-50, Draper City Fraud Risk Assessment.

8.1   Bob Wylie, Finance Director, explained that this item was a new item that the state
      auditor’s office implemented for governments in the state this year. It was a self-
      assessment, fraud risk assessment. When staff conducted the self-assessment they scored
      355 points, falling in the high end of the low fraud risk. He said that they were not
      completely free of the risk of fraud, but were very low risk. Mr. Wylie then talked about
      some of the areas in which the City was evaluated. In terms of separation of duties and
      mitigating controls, the City received full points. There were nine written policies, all of
      which the City followed, on separating duties. Another item addressed certification and
      expert levels. Mr. Wylie noted that there were people on the finance staff who had the
      designations. Item five was an area that the City did not receive any scoring. It asked if
      all four auditors had completed the state online training. Mr. Wylie said that the
      information had been sent out to the Council members to take the training. He said that the
      training took about an hour with a few questions and answers at the end. Another area of
      the evaluation was if the City had a fraud hotline. Mr. Wylie said that there was a phone
      number to call on the City’s website. Point eight asks if the entity has a formal internal
      auditing function, Mr. Wylie said that only a few very large companies have an internal
      auditing function, so Draper does not currently have this. However, they were looking at
      other ways to mitigate this through contracting. Even with there being a couple of areas
      where the City didn’t receive any scores they were still in the very low-risk category.

8.2   Councilmember Green moved to approve Resolution #20-50. Councilmember T.
