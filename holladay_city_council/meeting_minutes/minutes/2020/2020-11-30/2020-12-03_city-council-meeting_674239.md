III.   Continued Public Hearing on Proposed Storm Water Utility Fee Ordinance.
Mayor Dahle reported that the item was a continued public hearing on a proposed Storm Water Utility
Fee ordinance that was addressed during previous City Council Meetings. The public hearing
remained open to ensure that citizens were able to share comments.

Mayor Dahle discussed a potential issue related to mixed-use properties and specifically stacked
condominiums over retail locations. He wondered how those properties would be assessed for the
Storm Water Utility Fee. Council Member Fotheringham asked if the fee would be the responsibility
of the property owner or if each individual condominium unit would be assessed. Mayor Dahle noted
that condominium owners own air space rather than land space. City Manager, Gina Chamness stated
that the issue would be fleshed out over the next four to five months. She believed that condominiums
would be assessed as a residential unit but noted that there may be a different recommendation in the
future.

Kris Longson - 4954 S Fairview Dr. He stated that his understanding was that condominiums and
commercial spaces will be assessed based on land area and use. Vertical mixed-use buildings could

VIII. Consideration of Resolution 2020-32 Amending Chapter 2 of the City’s General Plan.
Council Member Quinn commented that Mr. Allred explained how the Planning Commission arrived
at the amendments for Chapter 2 during the previous City Council Meeting. However, there was one
line she wanted to see altered. Under the section, Key Land Use and Urban Design Issues, Council
Member Quinn asked that the line, “Preserving what is best about Holladay and avoiding the negative
impacts of future growth are the key areas of focus as the City moves forward,” be changed to state,
“Preserving what is best about Holladay and avoiding the unplanned negative impacts of future
growth are the key areas of focus as the City moves forward.” She felt this would soften the sentence.

Council Member Fotheringham noted that the current version of the sentence was ambiguous.
However, he did not believe it indicated that all impacts related to future growth were negative. He
felt that it indicated that there were positive and negative impacts on growth and the City wanted to
avoid the negative ones. Several Council Members expressed support for the suggested change from
Council Member Quinn.

Mayor Dahle reported that a suggestion was made by Ron Hilton via email. The suggested placement
was under the section, Principle 1 – Supporting Stable Neighborhoods, and stated, “The suggested
zones are the maximum recommended density within a district. Lower density residential rezones
may be advisable to achieve a smooth transition in density between districts.” There was discussion
regarding where the suggested addition could be placed within the section. Mr. Allred believed the
suggested language stated that a lower density may want to be considered to avoid an abrupt density
change from one zone to the next. He made note of previous issues Mr. Hilton had with zone changes
and density. Mr. Allred commented that every rezone is handled on a case-by-case basis. Most

bridge between the maximum density and a potentially lower suggestion.

Council Member Gibbons was opposed to adding in language from Mr. Hilton. He referenced
controversies involving Mr. Hilton’s property and residents on Kentucky Avenue. He did not believe
the suggestion was negative but felt it was unwise to make last-minute changes to the Chapter 2

XI.   Consideration of Resolution 2020-36 Amending the Agreement to Develop Land (“ADL”)
      with Holladay Hills, LLC Relating to a Statutory Two-Year Extension of the Subsidy
      Period.
Mayor Dahle reported that the item would be discussed during the Redevelopment Agency (“RDA”)
Meeting. However, since the Agreement to Develop Land (“ADL”) contract was between City

Holladay City Council Meeting Minutes 12-03-20                                            6
Council, the developer, and the RDA, it needed to be approved by both the City Council and the RDA.
Developer representatives, Steve Peterson and Kris Longson were both present to answer questions.
Mayor Dahle shared an overview of the agreement with the Council. There was a 20-year increment
agreement through the ADL that began on January 1, 2017, and terminated at the end of December
2036. The State Legislature stepped in and introduced SB-6001 that allowed municipalities to
consider requests for extensions due to COVID-19.

Council Member Gibbons reflected further on the situation and read through all of the foundational
documents. He felt it was important that the developers not be prejudiced against competitors in the
marketplace. Council Member Gibbons pointed out that the decision could be held and looked at
again in 2021, but he believed delaying a decision may negatively impact the developers on a
competitive level. He expressed support for the full two-year extension.

Mr. Peterson reported that there had been a lot of potential leasing activity prior to COVID-19.
However, that stopped when COVID-19 hit. He informed the City Council that there had been
infrastructure delays as well. It had been difficult to hold meetings with some of the utility companies
and communication was somewhat limited. Mr. Peterson noted that nothing had been brought to the
Planning Commission or City Council since the original entitlements for Block D and preliminary
entitlements for Blocks B and C. He stated that tenants were important for the site and noted that the
development was competing with other cities and downtown areas. Sometimes deals needed to be
given to gain momentum and to be as competitive as possible. It was important to incentivize tenants
to choose the Holladay Hills development.

Mr. Peterson believed the extension would move the project forward sooner rather than later. He
noted that the money generated would help subsidize parking and off-site improvements around the
site that the developers had already committed to. Mr. Peterson added that certain costs had also
raised substantially since COVID-19. Pipes for the sewers doubled in price since the pandemic began
and there had been a notable increase in lumber prices. Mr. Peterson believed that Holladay Hills
would have a bright future but noted that the development needed to gain momentum. He asked for
City Council support on the two-year extension.

Mr. Longson reported that the retail world had been heavily impacted by COVID-19. While some
companies benefited from the pandemic, many were struggling to survive. The development was
competing for tenants that fell into both categories. Mr. Longson felt that the two-year extension
would allow the development to make up the budget needed to put the infrastructure in now. That
would create more confidence and allow the development to be more aggressive in attracting tenants.
The extension would make it possible to be competitive in the market and accelerate development.
Mr. Longson believed this would be a benefit to all taxing entities.

City Manager, Gina Chamness reached out to Salt Lake County and Granite School District. She
reported that Granite School District was supportive of the extension. A definitive answer had not
been received from Salt Lake County. However, Ms. Chamness believed that if Salt Lake County
had strong objections, those objections would have been communicated. Mayor Dahle noted that the
City Council did not need the blessing of Salt Lake County or the Granite School District. It had been
a courtesy to reach out to them and let them know about the extension.

was common for property owners to expect a personal visit when their property is involved in a broad
zone change. However, that was not necessarily feasible. Notices by mail were required by law.

Mr. Allred reported that some discussions had taken place with property owners. Those they had
spoken to were largely supportive of the Holladay Crossroads Zone. Mr. Allred explained that if the
zone were adopted, property owners would need to apply for the zone in order for it to be
implemented. It would be on a case-by-case basis. However, if a property owner wanted to expand
the footprint of the building or change the outside of the building by more than 50%, the new rules
and regulations would apply. Ms. Chamness added that there was no retroactive requirement for
property owners. Mr. Allred commented that the Holladay Crossroads Zone would allow property
owners to work together to create a more unified vision. He believed that some property owners were
uncertain about what the zone change would really mean. Further discussion and clarification would
be beneficial for all involved.

There was discussion regarding the zone change and density. Council Member Fotheringham did not
believe the zone change would add intensity or density. The objective was to reorganize the area and
make it more aesthetically pleasing. Mr. Allred noted that much of the Holladay Crossroads Zone
was patterned after the Holladay Village Zone. A lot of the same language and principles were
involved. The Holladay Crossroads Zone would create possibilities that did not currently exist in the
C-2 Zone due to the verticality and location of buildings.

Mr. Allred mentioned consultants, Bruce Parker and Dr. Joe Perrin, and work they had done related
to land use and transportation elements. He reported that they fed all existing inputs, such as
buildings, parking, and economic data into a software program. The same was done with the inputs
for the proposed zone. What resulted was not all that different economically. Mr. Allred stated that
the zone change was more about urban design and form than intensification.

Council Member Gibbons discussed some of the concerns he had heard, including the 65-foot
building heights with only a 10-foot setback and traffic-related issues. He noted that the Small Area
Master Plan for the Holladay Crossroads Zone mentioned a roundabout. Mr. Allred address the
concerns and stated that the Planning Commission debated at length about the building height. They
