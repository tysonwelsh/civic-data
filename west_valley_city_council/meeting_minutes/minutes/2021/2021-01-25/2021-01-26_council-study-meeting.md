RESOLUTION 21-15: MAKE A STATEMENT OF SUPPORT FOR NEEDED
TRANSPORTATION INFRASTRUCTURE FUNDING AND OTHER SOLUTIONS
THAT BRING TRANSPORTATION INVESTMENT TO WEST VALLEY CITY
AND THE WEST SIDE OF SALT LAKE COUNTY
Nicole Cottle, Assistant City Manager, presented proposed Resolution 21-15 that would
make a statement of support for needed transportation infrastructure funding and other
solutions that bring transportation investment to West Valley City and the west side of Salt
Lake County.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       Each year the City seeks additional transportation funding from the State in order
       to secure and improve the City’s specific transportation needs. The City also
       recognizes that transportation needs are often regional and that there are many
       organizations that advocate for regional dollars for improvements. This resolution
       simply supports the request for funding for City infrastructure as well as provides
       a general statement of support for those regional facilities that would improve the
       network.

       Transportation is essential. The City enjoys great access to many regional
       transportation facilities and recognizes those are important to the system. The City
       also has its own internal transportation needs that connect to these regional
       facilities. This resolution simply recognizes those need and supports state funding.

Councilmember Christensen stated that this proposal looks similar to what Western
Growth suggested. Mayor Bigelow suggested language that transportation effects residents
and businesses. He also stated West Valley City is a major area for northwest transportation
and the City works with many different communities to facilitate traffic flow. Mayor
Bigelow stated he would like to highlight these points in the proposed resolution.

The City Council will consider Resolution 21-15 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
February 2, 2021 at 6:30 P.M.

A.  BANGERTER HIGHWAY INTERCHANGE DISCUSSION
    Blake Unguren, Avenue Consultants, presented a PowerPoint presentation
    summarized as follows:
          - Selected Concepts
                 o Option 3b – 6-Lane Commuter Lanes + 4-Lane Frontage; 3500
                      S to SR-201
                 o Option 6 – 6-Lane Freeway + Frontage Roads; 3500 S to SR-
                      201
                 o Option 7 – 6-Lane Freeway + Center Running Frontage Roads
          - Traffic Volumes
                 o Option 3b – Freeway (Commuter Lanes) Volumes
                 o Option 3b – Frontage Road / Arterial Volumes
                 o Option 6 – Freeway Volumes
                 o Option 6 – Frontage Road Volumes
                 o Option 7 – Freeway Volumes
                 o Option 7 – Frontage Road Volumes
          - Freeway Volumes
          - Intersection Operations
                 o 2040 AM Peak Hour Approach & Intersection Level of Service
                 o 2040 PM Peak Hour Approach & Intersection Level of Service

      Councilmember Buhler verified that all 3 options are similar to one another in a
      traffic operation perspective. Mr. Unguren replied that is correct but noted that
      additional lanes had to be added to option 7 to make it function as well.
      Councilmember Buhler verified the connectivity options between all three options.
      Councilmember Buhler asked if Option 3.B. has a potentially smaller footprint. Mr.
      Unguren replied that is being evaluated. Councilmember Buhler stated that he
      would lean more toward option 3.B with the information provided. The Council

       discussed differences and similarities between option 3.B and 6. Councilmember
       Lang stated that she prefers option 6 because slower traffic would be closer to the
