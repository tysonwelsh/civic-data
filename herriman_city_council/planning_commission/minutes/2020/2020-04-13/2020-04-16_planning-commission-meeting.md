meeting was held on Thursday, April 16, 2020 at 6:00 p.m. in the Herriman City Hall Council Chambers, 5355 West
Herriman Main Street, Herriman, Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting, as required by law, was posted in the City
Hall, on the City’s website, and delivered to members of the Council, media, and interested citizens.

Item 3.2 - changes to cul-de-sac length, trail access and park location changes were discussed. Commissioner Lorin
Palmer stated he would leave the meeting, and not vote on item 3.2.

             1.3. Discussion of recessed windows in Parkside
City Planner Maloy mentioned the design guidelines required recessed windows. They were being discussed due to
leaking issues. Commissioners offered concern with changes to the look of the building and the flat appearance of
homes that did not have recessed windows. Locations for three inch recessed windows were proposed. Community
Development Director Blake Thomas informed the Commission that the builder committed to recessed windows
along the park side. Some other sides of buildings would add elements to add character such as shutters. City Planner
Maloy stated the Commission could amend the conditions to allow the builder to install windows that were not
recessed. Design elements such as window trim can create the look of recessed windows, and add depth.

Commissioners requested that the applicant submit renderings or photos of the original and proposed changes, for
comparison, and identify what they were proposing that would make the change equal or better than the original.

            1.4. Training
City Planner Maloy stated no training was prepared.

The Hidden Oaks pod developments were reviewed. Commissioners pointed out an error on page 94 of the packet,
with the incorrect pod displayed. Commissioners requested density transfer clarification and were informed that with
schools included the pods would be no more than 799 units. Density may be transferred between pods. Expansion
of 13400 South and water lines in the road were described by Community Development Director Thomas. The City
saw no issues with water lines or potential road widening. The contaminated soil removal plan involved for it to be
removed to a repository site on Kennecott property. The grading plan was defined and various pods were reviewed.
Assistant City Planner MacDonald reviewed the MDA requirement for lot size variation and identified the area that
would need to be changed. Specific driveway locations were identified for some lots and road access would be
restricted. Grading for Item 3.9 was for development only and the City may review every six months, but there was
no time limit, and it may not take place within 600 feet of a residence. The land could be reclaimed by the City with
bond money, if necessary.

The Planning Commission work meeting was adjourned by consensus at 6:59 p.m.

Requirements:
   1. The operation shall not be located within 600 feet of a residence.
   2. Hours of operation will be limited to 7AM to 7PM, unless an exception is granted by the City with an
       approved noise and lighting plan.
   3. The use will be temporary and shall be reviewed every 6 months.
   4. Submit a plan which shows the limits of disturbance for the entire property and the surface area for each
       phase that can be disturbed at one time.
   5. A development site plan to be implemented later must be submitted for review and approval by the
       Planning Commission prior to sand and/or rock removal and the operation shall be carried out in

       replaced within 60 calendar days.
   9. A reclamation bond shall be required sufficient to ensure restoration of the site to original or other

       premises. No outside material shall be brought on the site.
   11. Must have access onto an approved truck route and not gain access through any local streets.
   12. Obtain a state stormwater discharge permit.
