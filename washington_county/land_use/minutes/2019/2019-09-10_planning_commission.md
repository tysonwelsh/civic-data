Washington County Planning Commission Meeting
                              September 10, 2019
The Washington County Land Use Authority Meeting was held on Tuesday, September 10, 2019
in the Council Chambers of the Washington County Administration Building at 197 E. Tabernacle,
St. George, UT. Chairman Slade Hughes opened the meeting at 1:30 p.m. He led the Pledge of
Allegiance and then explained meeting protocol.

Commissioners present: Brandon Anderson, Dan Briggs (Alternate Acting as a regular voting
member), Shayne Durrant, Kevin Jones, Reed Scow (Alternate Acting as a regular voting
member).

Excused: Mark Owens, Doug Solstad, and Jon Walter.

Staff present: Scott Messel, Community Development Director; Natalie Nelson, Deputy County
Attorney; Doreen Bowers-Irons, Administrative Assistant; Kurt Gardner, Building Official; Todd
Edwards, County Engineer; Kim Hafen, County Clerk.


1.     REVIEW CONCEPTUAL MASTER PLAN OF THE JUNIPER INVESTORS
COMMERCIAL PROJECT. Review the conceptual master planned commercial development
of Parcels 2011-A-1-NS, 2011-A-2-NS, and 2011-A-4-NS. The combined parcels are
approximately 21.84+/- acres in size and located on Old Highway 91 and Kolob Canyon Road in
New Harmony. The applicants are Juniper Investors, LLC./Stephan Swindle.

Due to technical issues the power point presentation was unavailable.

Mr. Messel reported that during the February 12, 2019 planning meeting, the applicants requested
a zone change of Parcels 2011-A-1-NS, 2011-A-2-NS, and 2011-A-4-NS from the A-20
(Agriculture 20 acre minimum lot size) zone to the C-2 and C-3 (General Commercial) zone. The
parcels are located at Old Highway 91 and Kolob Canyon Road and is approximately 21.84 acres
in size. The zone change was requested to accommodate potential future commercial development.
The planning commission recommended the county commission approve the zone change. The
county commission reviewed the request but denied the zone change. The area is in a prominent
location and they requested a more concise detailed plan of the uses on the parcels. The applicants
have now submitted a more detailed concept plan of their vision for the properties. There are still
issues regarding culinary water and septic or a sewer system to accommodate the project which,
the applicants are working on. The applicants have been in contact with the State and County
entities and are working through the issues with the appropriate offices. The intent is to install a
commercial advanced system for the septic. They will also be reviewing lighting for the project to
minimize the dark sky impact. This item was put on the agenda for the planning commissioners,
staff, and the developer to discuss the proposal for feedback, suggestions, and recommendations.
The applicant would then take those comments back for further improvements to the plan. The
next step would be to resubmit the project plan and zone change request for approval.

Chairman Hughes asked if they were still looking at this as a commercial use which, the planning
commission had approved. Mr. Messel confirmed it was.

Chairman Hughes asked if the applicants were being required to go through a planned commercial

Mr. Messel explained the locations of the proposed phases and their type of uses. Those uses
include RV camping, hotels, retail stores, parking, service station, restaurants, etc. He explained
the conceptual building architecture.

Commissioner Durrant wanted to ensure that the buildings would fit in with the area and not
distract from the natural beauty. He suggested CC&R’s being put place. He asked if that was being
discussed. Mr. Messel answered at this time they are not at that phase. He pointed out what would
be allowed by the county’s standards.

Chairman Hughes asked if this body recommended that the county commission approve the C-2
and C-3 zone changes then if the applicant should sell the property and the new applicant comes
in and wants to build a specific style building, would they be able to do that. Mr. Messel answered
in a standard commercial zone, unless a development agreement was in place, they could. This
being a planned development they are held to the PD standards as approved by the county or they
would have to request an amendment to the PD.

The commission and staff discussed the aesthetics of the proposal. Chairman Hughes announced
his concern that aesthetics is beyond the scope of governmental duty, that taps right along with a
communistic duty and it bothered him.

A short “fly over” video of the proposed buildings and architecture of the project was shown.

Adam Allen of Bush and Gudgell, stated he was representing the developer and would answer the
commissioners questions.

Commissioner Jones asked if the RV area in Zone 7 was for temporary RV stays only. Mr. Allen
answered yes. He then explained the vision the developers had of the project and how they got
there.

Commissioner Jones inquired if there would be sufficient amounts water to serve the project. Mr.
Allen answered Mountain Springs Water Company will provide the water and there would be
enough. He elaborated on the water sources.

Mr. Allen noted the entire project is based on what the county commission wanted the development
to look like. Right now, it is a concept plan. There may be some minor changes but it should stay
close to what is being shown at this time.

2.      HILLSIDE DEVELOPMENT. Review of the Hillside Development for a proposed zip
line on Parcels 1157-D-HV and 1157-E-HV located at 3050 East SR-9 near Virgin. The parcels
are zoned C-3 (General Commercial). The applicant is Zion Canyon Zipline/Paul Budge.

Mr. Messel presented a diagram of the proposed road for access to a previously approved zip line.
He reported during the January 09, 2018 Planning Meeting, the applicant requested and received
approval to construct a zip line on their property. The applicant is moving forward with the project
and needs to create a roadway for the zip line access. The applicant will need to conform to the
Hillside Development Standards. Because this project is located along the scenic byway there were
concerns of scaring of the mountain and how it would be mitigated. The applicants were asked to
be sensitive to that and that any material removed should use native material to replace it. They
have been working with Staff to comply with those standards and aesthetics. The road will be a
lava rock base and will resemble an old pioneer wagon trail. Staff has been reviewing the cuts and

3.     PUBLIC HEARING REVIEW AND DISCUSSION OF COUNTY CODES. Update
and review of County Ordinances: Title 10, Chapter 4: Definitions; and Title 10, Chapter 9:
Planned Development.

Mr. Messel provided the commissioners with a large packet of the proposed code changes. He
stated the moratorium that was put in place for rural recreation grounds and facilities expires on
October 1, 2019. The county is required to have an ordinance in place if they are making changes
to the use. It takes 15 days from the date of approval from the county commission for the new
ordinance to go into effect. Staff and the county commissioners have been working on code
changes to adopt the vision of how to address the rural recreation grounds and facilities use in the
county. The planning commission will need to make a recommendation to the county commission
to approve the new code at their September 17, 2019 regular meeting.

There were several questions, answers, comments and requested changes to the document from
the planning commissioners.

Kurt Gardner, County Building official commented this started out as private recreation. He asked
if there was a provision for property owners of 20 acres or more to be able to build a pavilion,
bathroom, something in that nature for family gatherings. Mr. Messel answered whether it is
private or profit, there is no distinction so there was not a provision.

Commissioner Durrant stated because he received the document so late, he was unable to review
it and was not prepared to vote for approval. He would like more time to study the changes and
