3.  Public hearing and possible action regarding a 30-lot Master Planned 
Development (MPD) with a remainder parcel outside of the platted subdivision; 
located off of Cherry Canyon Road; Parcel NS-227-230 and Parcel NS-230-G; Brett 
Hollberg representing Trail Ridge Partners LLC and LRH INC., Applicant. – Amir Caus, 
AICP, County Planner 
 
Planner Caus shared the background for this application. This application was discussed 
at the February 4th Planning Commission meeting and the majority of the application met 
the MPD requirements. The Planning Commission did express some concern over several 
items. Since then, a plat note has been added to state that no development is allowed on 
slopes over thirty percent. There are also plat notes about any RVs on site must be in 
operable condition, no unscreened outdoor storage will be permitted. Well protection 
zones and water easements have been included. A trail access agreement will go out to 
every owner in the subdivision for the open space surrounding the area. Lot boundaries 
now go to the edge of the right-of-way instead of the center line of the road. The applicant 
has proposed to limit the height for buildings on lots 19, 20, and 21 to twenty-six feet (if the 
Planning Commission requires), and to require building materials for those lots to be earth 
tone colors to help mitigate hillside development. The applicant feels it is not feasible to 
have a public water company for the entire Cherry Canyon area. 
Commissioner Sargent thanked the applicant for addressing the concerns of the Planning 
Commission. The twenty-six-foot height limitation on ridgeline development may not be 
helpful. Earth tone colors and well-honed designs will help the most. 
Commissioner Clyde stated he likes the reduced heights on hillside development, but is 
unhappy with drilling more wells. Joining the water together helps the most. Chair 
Sonntag shared the same concern with the proliferation of water companies and lack of

Findings of Fact:  
1. Trail Ridge Partners LLC is the owner of record of Parcel NS-227-230 (337.16 acres), located 
in Cherry Canyon, Summit County, UT.  
2. LRH Inc. is the owner of record of Parcel NS-230-G (10 acres), located in Cherry Canyon, 
Summit County, UT.  
3. Parcel NS-227-230 and Parcel NS-230-G comprise one (1) Conforming Parcel.  
4. Parcel NS-227-230 is currently undeveloped while Parcel NS-230-G is developed with a 
single-family residence.  
5. The applicant is requesting to subdivide through the MPD process Parcel NS-227-230 and 
Parcel NS-230-G into thirty (30) lots with one (1) remainder parcel outside of the platted 
subdivision (31 units total).  
6. Parcel NS-227-230 would be used for the development of the 30 lots with the associated open 
space.  
7. Parcel NS-230-G would become the Remainder Parcel outside of the platted subdivision.  
8. Parcel NS-227-230 consists of 337.16 acres and breaks down into ~182 acres in the AG-10 
Zone and ~154 acres in the AG-20 Zone.  
9. An equivalent zone base density allows for 25 units at base density.

10. The applicant is seeking a Density Bonus and the bonus density calculation provides for the 
31 units requested by the applicant.  
11. The applicant proposes to set aside ~248 acres (~78%) as permanently deed restricted 
agricultural open space which based on the bonus density calculation provides the additional six 
(6) units requested by the applicant.  
12. No more than 50% of the required deed restricted open space consists of undevelopable lands 
as per MPD Appendix B.  
13. The applicant has stated that the open space within the subdivision will be managed as 
private open space.  
14. Per Section 11-4-12(B.2) of the Development Code, the Master Planned Development 
process is required for any application to subdivide at base density resulting in four (4) or more 
lots or parcels.  
15. On February 4, 2021, the Eastern Summit County Planning Commission held a public 
hearing regarding the proposal.  
16. During the February 4, 2021, the Eastern Summit County Planning Commission directed the 
applicant to address the outstanding items identified in the February 4, 2021 Staff Report and 
directed Staff to return with a set of Findings of Fact, Conclusions of Law, and Conditions of 
Approval to support a positive recommendation to the Summit County Council.  
17. The outstanding items discussed at the February 4, 2021 included;  
• No development on slopes over 30%.  
• Trailers and how they will be managed.  
• Adding of well protection zones and water access easements.  
• Open Space and trail access.  
• Lot boundaries ending at the road right-of-way.  
• Hillside development of Lots 19, 20, and 21.  
• Need for a public water company for the entire Cherry Canyon area.  
18. The applicant is not requesting to deviate from the development code setback requirements.  
19. The applicant is not seeking a reduction in lot size requirements.  
20. The applicant is proposing ~248 acres (~78%) as permanently deed restricted private 
agricultural open space.  
21. The property has historically been used for cattle grazing and private recreation purposes and 
the applicant has stated that they will maintain private ownership of the open space but will 
provide private trail access for the residents as outlined in the Exhibit D private Trail Access 
Agreement and Map.  
22. Slopes over 30% have been identified on the property.  
23. The Eastern Summit County Development Code prohibits development on slopes over 30%.  
24. Slope analysis information provided by the applicant identify developable building areas 
outside of slopes 30% or greater.  
25. During the February 4, 2021, the Planning Commission discussed the requirement of 
building pads and have decided not to require them.  
26. The applicant has added a plat note that states; No buildings permitted on slopes greater than 
30%.

2. Discussion on possible amendments to the Eastern Summit County Development 
Code to create a neighborhood commercial zone. – Ray Milliner, Principal Planner 
 
Planner Milliner shared the possibility of creating a new commercial zone. The Planning 
Commission has stated their dislike of higher impact uses in some areas, and it can get 
complicated with neighborhood commercial areas. The current language in the Code could 
be utilized with the uses changed for General Commercial with a rezone requirement 
included. The Code is currently written for Neighborhood Commercial, but some uses are 
not appropriate for the current Neighborhood Commercial Zones. For example, a storage 
facility would be more appropriate in a General Commercial Zone. General Commercial 
would also require an MPD process.

Chair Sonntag suggested that Neighborhood Commercial could have size limitations, but 
the changes should not take away any current commercial rights.  
Commissioner Sargent stated that municipalities depend on the commercial tax base, but 
Neighborhood Commercial makes sense outside of the municipalities. The higher-intensity 
commercial uses should be driven toward the municipalities that have the infrastructure to 
support them. There should be a focus on areas where water systems are available. The 
best Neighborhood Commercial will support the local neighborhood. 
Commissioner Clyde stated there should be two zones and it should be decided where 
they each would be applicable. Maybe there should be a cap on the number of units allowed 
in a Bed and Breakfast. There should be a separate multi-family zone. Home-based 
businesses should be an allowed use. 
Planner Milliner stated he will abandon the General Commercial idea and have more focus 
on Neighborhood Commercial in Eastern Summit County.
