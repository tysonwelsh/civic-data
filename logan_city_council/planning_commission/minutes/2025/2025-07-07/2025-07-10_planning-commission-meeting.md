PC 25-037 LDC Amendment — 17.24 Critical Lands Overlay Zone [Code Amendment] Mike
DeSimone/Logan City is proposing to amend Chapter 17.24 to modify the riparian and wetlands
setback standards and update the range of uses and activities permitted within setback areas.

Staff/Proponent: Mike DeSimone, Community Development Director reviewed the proposed code
amendment to the Land Development Code (LDC), Chapter 17.24 Critical Lands Overlay Zone, to
modify the Riparian and Wetland setback standards, and modify the range of uses and activities
permitted within setback areas.


M. DeSimone reviewed the last amendment to the Critical Lands Overlay Zone completed in 2023,
which also included an amendment to the Logan Municipal Code 15.27, Flood Damage Prevention,
regulating develapment in a floodplain. M. DeSimone stated that these changes were driven by
issues raised during the Willow Lakes Subdivision project as well as State and Federal requirements
to update the City’s Flood Damage Prevention regulations.

Since 2023, the City has had a number of different projects raise questions about the efficacy of our
current approach to regulating development within or adjacent to various Critical Lands. City staff
met to evaluate if the current regulations add any type of protection above and beyond current local,
State or Federal law, or if they just add a layer of complexity to an increasingly complex review system
with no, or minimal, realized benefit. The current regulations also fail to consider bridges, parks,
trails, roads, and utilities located within or adjacent to Critical Lands.

M. DeSimone reviewed the proposed changes to the Riparian Areas section, including replacing the
current three tiered approach with a standard setback of 25’, modifying the clearing limits, exempting
certain types of uses and activities allowable in a riparian area, clarifying the expansion of non-
conforming uses, and simplifying the Riparian Definition.

M. DeSimone explained the reasons for the proposed riparian area changes. Logan City is generally
built out and there are approximately 20 vacant lots adjacent to the Logan River that would not be
able to be built on under the existing setbacks. The proposed changes will simplify the regulatory
approach and reduce potential conflict when reviewing a project in relation to neighboring built out
properties. M. DeSimone stated that staff also realized that certain uses and activities such as parks,
trails, utilities, or bridges were left out of the earlier revision and by their very nature are located within
a riparian area.

M. DeSimone reviewed the three factors that are used to identify a wetland: hydrology, soils and
vegetation. M. DeSimone reviewed the proposed changes to the Wetlands section including clarifying
that these requirements only apply to jurisdictional wetlands regulated by the U.S. Army Corp of
Engineers, the modification to the wetland setback from 50' down to 25', when a wetland is required
with a new project, and when a Corp permit is required with a new project. M. DeSimone also
explained the requirements for off-site wetland identification and the limitations on requiring a
delineation on an off-site wetland. M. DeSimone discussed the proposed changes to existing
setbacks from 50’ to 25’, setbacks along a ditch or canal, wetland setbacks for new residential
property lines, and wetland setbacks for road construction. Maintenance and ownership of wetlands
by an HOA were also discussed.

M. DeSimone reviewed several maps of the Willow Lakes project area including maps of the potential
wetlands and floodplains before the project started, a map of the delineated wetlands and
endangered species, a map of the wetland impacts and mitigation areas, and a map of the
conservation easement areas in order to show the differences in potential wetland on NWI maps,
formal wetland delineations, and formal mitigation plans approved by the Corp and other regulatory
agencies.

M. Desimone also reviewed the revised Hoggan Estates Subdivision project to demonstrate how the
new rules would be applied in a new residential subdivision.

Commissioner Lucero asked about the proposed setback for agricultural land and if

would make more sense for the setback to stay at 50 feet. M. DeSimone stated that there is not a
lot of remaining agricultural uses currently in the City adjacent to the Logan River and it is the City’s
current policy to not annex productive or prime agricultural land unless it is ready to be converted to


new development. Staff agreed with Commissioner Lucero that the existing riparian and wetland
setback for new agricultural uses should remain at 50 feet.

Public: No public comments were made.

Commission: Commissioner Doutre asked if the Army Corps of Engineers requires a setback from
their delineation. M. DeSimone said the Army Corp approves the delineated boundary of a wetland
and do not have any setback requirements from that boundary.

Commissioner Lucero asked about the floodplain and future annexed properties being developed.
M. DeSimone said that there are floodplain regulations and delineation requirements in place for
annexation and development. There was also discussion about the extent of future areas of
annexation based on the City’s adopted Annexation Plan. Commissioner Peterson asked whether
these changes were more of a simplification of the rules. M. DeSimone responded that the Hoggan
project demonstrated to staff that our current rules and decision making procedures were not very
clear and needed to be reevaluated, and pointed out the fact that we just failed to address the
practical issues such as road improvements or road construction in a fixed right of way next to a
critical area.

There was additional discussion about the Corp’s regulatory processes relative to the City’s wetland
regulatory processes, their jurisdiction based on the Clean Water Act, and the timing of when the City
requires a formal wetland delineation and a copy of the Corp wetland permit. There was also
additional discussion about floodplains, floodplain requirements for future development, the City’s
liability if a flood happens, current requirements to build in a floodplain, and floodplain protections
relative to future annexation requests.

Commissioners Lucero and Doutre stated that the setback for agricultural lands and uses should
remain at a 50-foot setback in both the Riparian and Wetland areas (LDC Chapters 17.24.110.1 (3)
and 17.24.060.A (5)).
