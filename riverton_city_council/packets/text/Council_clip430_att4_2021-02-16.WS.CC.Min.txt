23            Farm Animals on Small Lots - Councilmember Stewart
24
25   Councilmember Stewart explained he had received a complaint about pygmy goats in his district
26   and wanted to review the ordinance that Riverton City currently has adopted. He asked Ryan
27   Carter to give an update.
28
29   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, said that officers were sent out to the property in question and found
30   too many chickens on the property. The property owners also had pygmy goats. He explained
31   that Riverton City’s ordinance requires at least two pygmy goats and the ordinance does not
32   give direction as to what lot sizes can have goats. He suggested that the council look at what
33   qualifications a property should have to allow goats on the property as well as how far from
34   neighboring property the goats could be kept.
35
36   Councilmember Stewart talked about setbacks on different size properties and how this related
37   to how far the goats would be from another resident’s house.
38
39   Councilmember McDougal asked the council if farm animals should be allowed on small lot
40   sizes.
41
42   Councilmember Stewart asked what the requirement is for chickens.
43
44   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, said that there is a 10,000 square foot
45   minimum for chickens.
46
47   Councilmember McDougal said that he would support an ordinance that required setback to
48   have goats and that he personally does not believe we should have pygmy goats on small lots.
49
50   Councilmember Stewart also supported a setback to allow distance from the neighboring home.

10            Discussion Regarding Riverton Personnel Policies and Procedures Manual - David
11            Brickey, City Manager and Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director
12
13   David Brickey, City Manager, explained that employees that retire are given 10% of their yearly
14   salary upon retirement. He asked if the council would want to change this policy.
15
16   Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, said that this policy was approved in October
17   2011. There are two parts of the policy, employees retiring receive 10% of their sick leave and
18   10% of their yearly salary. He analyzed the costs over the past 10 years and noticed that it is
19   not a significant amount of money. He asked if the council would like to keep the current
20   structure or add a years of service requirement in order to get the 10% salary payout.
21
22   Councilmember Stewart said there should be a 5 or 10 year requirement for the 10% of the
23   salary payout. He said he did not have an issue with the sick leave policy.
24
25   Councilmember Wells said that he would also like to have a years of service requirement.
26
27   Councilmember McDougal said that he would like a years of service requirement or to do away
28   with the 10% salary payout and increase the sick pay percentage.
29
30   Councilmember Buroker said she would like the 10% salary payout to be tied to years of
31   service.

 7         Ordinance No. 21-02 - Rezoning 8.32 Acres at 12761 S 3600 W from C-PO & M-1 to
 8         C-PO/HOV (Professional Office with Elderly Housing Overlay), Ben Oveson
 9         Representing DEJ Holdings, Applicant (Tabled from CCM 01-05-21)
10
11   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director said that Ordinance No. 21-02 was previously
12   presented to city council in January. The property is 8.32 acres and is north of Stampin’ Up. He
13   explained that the property is currently zoned professional office and the general plan is
14   business park and light manufacturing. He said the applicant has proposed zoning this property
15   professional office with elderly housing overlay. He said that most of the public comments were
16   concerned with the number of units per acre which is a maximum of 20. He explained that the
17   city council could lower that figure and that the applicant has proposed 10 units per acre. He
18   said that the Planning commission recommended denying this rezone. Mr. Lethbridge
19   explained some possible SD designations that the council could include in a motion to approve
20   this application.
21
22   Ben Oveson, Real Estate Director of Rocky Mountain Care and applicant for the rezone,
23   displayed a drawing that showed how the property could look if he was granted this rezone. He
24   explained that Riverton has a shortage of senior housing options especially compared to
25   surrounding cities.
26
27   Lengthy discussion was held among the Councilmembers, Mr. Lethbridge, Mr. Carter and Ben
28   Oveson, the applicant. Councilmembers expressed their concerns regarding access to and
29   traffic on Water Road, density and number of units allowed.
30
31   Mayor Pro Tempore Buroker opened a Public Hearing and called for public comment.
32
33   John Lindley provided public comment. He is opposed to the rezone because he believes the
34   density is too high for this area and feels like the council should not change the general plan
35   because they relied on this plan when they purchased their homes.
36
37   Stacy Woolley provided public comment. She has lived in Riverton for 25 years and feels
38   strongly that the density for this area is too high.
39
40   David Magelby provided public comment. His primary concern is traffic on Water Street and
41   does not want an access point to Water Street.
42
43   Preston Fueling provided public comment. He said that he is opposed to high density housing
44   and also traffic on Water Street.
45
46   Adam Allred provided public comment. He said that he was opposed to high density housing
47   and does not want any access to Water Street.
48
49   Mayor Pro Tempore Buroker closed the Public Hearing.
50

 1   Ben Oveson said that he had spent a lot of time talking to neighbors and tried to incorporate the
 2   feedback he received into his plans. He requested that the council rezone to include 10 units
 3   per acre.
 4
 5   Councilmember Buroker explained that these decisions are very difficult and encouraged
 6   residents to continue to come and share their comments.
 7
 8   Councilmember McDougal said that the process for rezoning does not include a site plan. He
 9   explained that the applicant cannot bring a final project for approval until the zoning is approved.
10   He believes the density is the biggest concern and that the site plan should be developed to
11   have the least impact on traffic in this area.
12
13   Councilmember Stewart explained that he wanted to mitigate the impact of this rezone and
14   thought that an elderly housing development would be a good option because it wouldn’t burden
15   schools and would bring great residents to the community.
16
17   Councilmember Wells believes that we should rely on the general plan because it provides an
18   assurance to residents when they buy land and believes there should be input when there is a
19   change proposed.
20
21   Councilmember McCay clarified to Councilmember Wells that the units per acre can be
22   determined by the council. The developer said originally that he was looking at 6 to 8 units per
23   acre. She believes that housing would be a better option than industrial in this area.
24
25   Councilmember McDougal said that it is the council’s job to give this applicant a zoning
26   designation. He thinks that a housing development would increase the value of the homes in
27   this area and believes that they could put a restriction on the project that would protect Water
28   Street.
29
30   Councilmember McDougal MOVED that the City Council approve Ordinance No. 21-02, an
31   ordinance rezoning 8.32 acres at 12761 South 3600 West from C-PO & M-1 to C-
32   PO/EHOV/SD (Professional Office with Elderly Housing Overlay with specific
33   development designations as follows:
34        1. Density shall be limited to six (6) units per acre.
35        2. No more than 4 attached units per building.
36        3. Units shall maintain residential character with attached garages and pitched
37           roofs, with maximum height of 35 feet.
38        4. The project shall be age restricted in compliance with all applicable laws to
39           minimum of 80% at 55 or older.
40        5. No vehicular access to 12600 South or to the adjacent office/care facility site
41           shall be allowed.
42        6. Units shall not front onto Water Street, and no individual driveway access onto
43           Water Street shall be allowed.
44   Councilmember Stewart SECONDED the motion. Mayor Pro Tempore Buroker called for
45   discussion on the motion; there being none, she called for a roll call vote and the vote was as
46   follows: Councilmembers Buroker-Yes, McCay-No, McDougal-Yes, Stewart-Yes, and Wells-
47   Yes. The motion passed 4 to 1.
