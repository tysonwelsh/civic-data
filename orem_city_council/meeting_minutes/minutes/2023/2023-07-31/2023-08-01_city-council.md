Proposed City Code Updates: Section 2-27-4 (Order of Succession for City Manager): Section
2-5-5 (Powers and Duties of City Manager): and Section 2-3-4 (Election Code) Presenter: Steve
Earl, City Attorney

Mr. Earl explained that there are areas of the City Code that need updating. Section 2-3-4
of the Orem City code deals with Election Code. Orem City’s Code is out of compliance with
the State Code. Mr. Earl is proposing that rather than continuing to update our code every time
the State Code changes, Orem City Code will just refer to the State Election Code. Ms. Millett
would like to consider keeping one item that is different from the state election code and that is
any campaign money received 30 days after the election would need to be reported to the City of
Orem. She even suggested the possible lobbying of the State Legislature to have that added to
State Code. Section 2-3-7 is another section that is in conflict with State Code. This section has
to do with vacancy of office for the Mayor or Council Members. Mr. Earl again suggested that
Orem Code refer to the State Election Code.

The second code change is Section 2-27-4, Order of Succession for City Managers. Mr.
Earl said that the City Code isn’t clear for City Manager succession if it is due to resignation or
dismissal. He is suggesting that the code be clarified for those types of possibilities.

The third code change is Section 2-5-5, Powers and Duties of the City Manager. The
final item in the list of the City Manager duties is that the City Manager has the power to
appoint with approval by the majority vote of the full membership of the council an acting
municipal manager.

Employee Engagement Survey Results: Presenter: Ron Gailey, CEO/Founder of OnPointe
Insights

https://www.voutube.com/watch?v=aA9xqHjPvVc&t=268s

Mr. Gailey is here to report on an employee engagement survey that was conducted at the
request of the City Council. The report started on June 27, 2023 and completed July 18, 2023.
The city had 95% of their full time staff participate.

Mr. Gailey’s summary is that Orem employees tend to agree that the city is heading in the
right direction (80%). Also, 45% of employees feel the city has improved (45%) in the past 1-2
years, which is an excellent sign of positive progress. Compared to other cities, 87% said
working for Orem was somewhat or much better, and no one said Orem was worse. Motivation
is high, with 46% being very motivated and 34% being somewhat motivated. Few were
unmotivated (8%). Also, few employees see themselves leaving in the next year (8%).

A deeper analysis reveals that Orem employees love working for the city. By addressing
a few frustrations, their feelings would improve. This survey will help city leaders prioritize
wisely.

* We asked employees to prioritize over 30 topics. They then rated their high priority

Public Hearing - Amending the development agreement for the property located at 1981
South Geneva Road in the C2 zone Presenter: Jason Bench, Assistant Development Services
Director

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aA9xqHjPvVc&t=4038s

Mr. Bench explained that the property, located at 2000 South Geneva Road, was rezoned
back in 2020 to a C2 zone for the purpose of adding a gas station. The City accepted a
development agreement at that time with specific details for the proposed gas station. Mr. Bench
presented the layout of the gas station for that development agreement. This included a gas
station with two fast food type businesses on each side of the convenience store.

Now Holiday Oil has purchased the property and their intent is to put their branding on
the gas station which would be a different floor plan from the original development agreement.
Originally they were proposing a car wash, but the car wash was removed from the plans and
they moved their trash enclosure closer to Geneva Road and further from the residential
neighborhoods after the planning commission did not recommend that the City Council approve
the amendments to the development agreement. They would like to add a drive thru to the
amended plans. The City Council members asked clarifying questions about the original
development agreement as compared to the proposed changes.

Mr. Peterson asked Mr.Earl to explain what options the City Council has for tonight's
vote. Mr. Earl explained there are 3 ways the City Council can vote. First they can accept the
proposed changes to the development agreement, second, they can reject the proposed changes
or ,third, they can accept the changes with some modifications. Mr. Peterson asked if the City
Council didn’t like the original development agreement, what would be their options. Mr. Earl
explained that the land could be rezoned, but reminded the council that development on C2
property is more valuable and expensive than residential development. This could mean a
serious loss for the developer.

A discussion was held about the possible need of gas stations in the City of Orem as per
the Sales Tax report from the State. Debby explained the use of the report as a tool to capitalize
on Sales Tax. Mr. Peterson showed concern about using that report in this decision.

Mr. Neal with Holiday Oil explained that they went through neighborhood meetings and
planning commission. They listened to the public and made modifications to accommodate
some of the concerns. They plan to dim lights in the evenings and not have the additional
tenants to the side, as well as no car wash. Mr. Neal confirmed that the land was purchased at the

Resolution - Place renewal of CARE Tax on Ballot Presenter: Steve Earl, City Attorney
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aA9xqHjPvVc&t=10300s

Mr. Earl explained that it is time to put the CARE Tax on the November 2023 Ballot.
This is a renewal of the current CARE tax. This tax is proposed to be 50% recreation and 50%
Cultural Arts. The tax is 1/10th of 1%. This means $1 for every $1000. Ms. Lauret spoke of the
benefit of this tax because neighboring cities who shop in Orem help pay for this tax. Mr.
Peterson is opposed to many of the taxes but he is in support of this tax. Mayor Young explained
this is a current tax that is very beneficial to the community and we get to see a benefit from the
taxes we pay. Other council members showed support for this tax.

Mr. Lambson moved to approve a resolution of the Orem City Council to place an
opinion question on the November 21, 2023, municipal general election ballot as to whether or
not the City of Orem should renew a citywide local sales and use tax of 1/10 or 1% to fund
city-owned recreational and cultural facilities, the ongoing operating expenses of city-owned
recreational facilities, and the ongoing operating expenses of private, non-profit cultural
organizations in Orem; and approve the ballot title and proposition. Mr. Spencer seconded the
motion. Those voting yes: David Young, David Spencer, Debby Lauret, Terry Peterson, Tom
Macdonald, LaNae Millett, and Jeff Lambson. The motion passed.
