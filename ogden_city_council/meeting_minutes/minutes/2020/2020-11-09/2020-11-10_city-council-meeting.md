Proposed Ordinance 2020-52 amending the Fiscal Year2021-2025 Capital
Improvement Plan by adding Project WU001 for replacement of aging water
meters and hardware systems
         A memo from the Community and Economic Development Deparhnent regarding a proposed amendment to the Fiscal
Year (FY) 2021-2025 Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the City is
need of replacing old water meters with new meters that can be read remotely. The intention is to increase meter reading
efficiency, accuracy, and access to water infonnation for residents. If approved, outdated water meters are anticipated to be
replaced in Fiscal Year 2021. The equipment costs are $1,000,000 and water bonds are anticipated to be used for project funding.
         Public Utilities Manager Herd summarized the memo and this proposed CIP project would provide for upgrades and
service improvements in the Public Utilities Division; it will make it possible to replace and upgrade water meters to allow for
remote reading. It will also improve data collections from new meters. He explained the Planning Commission voted m1animously
to recommend approval of the CIP amendment with a determination that this project is consistent with the General Plan by
creating a more reliable means to obtain water usage information and providing an ability to better detect system usage problems.
         Chair Choberka asked why this individual project is being considered independent of a larger package of water utility
CIP projects that will be coming to the Council soon in association with a budget amendment. Mr. Herd stated it is his
understanding that this project is different from typical CIP projects in that it is a purchase of supplies that could be assigned to the
'maintenance' category. Comptroller Stout added that the action before the Council is to include this project into the cruTent FY
CIP plan so that when funding is allocated to this project, it is deemed an approved CIP project. Chief Administrative Officer
Johnson added that the other water utility projects referenced by Chair Choberka are already included in the CIP.
         The Chair then called for a public hearing on proposed Ordinance 2020-52, entitled:
                   "An ordinance of Ogden City amending the FY 2021-2025 Capital Improvement Plan by adding project
                   WU00 1 for replacement of aging water meters and hardware systems; and providing that this ordinance shall
                   become effective upon posting after final passage."
         The Chief Deputy City Recorder presented written notice of said hearing and affidavit of the publication in the Standard-
Examiner on the 31st day of October 2020, specifying the time and place of this meeting as the time and place when and where
the proposed ordinance would be given a public hearing and be considered for final passage. The proof of publication was
accepted and filed.
         There being no persons appearing to be heard, COUNCIL MEMBER HYER MOVED THE PUBLIC HEARING BE
CLOSED. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER WHITE, ALL VOTING AYE.

Public Comments
          Heath Satow thanked Chair Choberka for her response to an email she sent to the Council and Mayor last week; he has
sent several emails to the Mayor over the past several weeks but has not received a response. He has yet to hear the Mayor's plans
for dealing with skyrocketing housing processes, or ifhe has any plans. He has only heard complaints from the Mayor about him
being called out for his lack of vision and action. He thanked the City Council for listening to his concerns about the COVID-19
pandemic months ago and having the courage to do as much as they could by formally encouraging all citizens of Ogden to wear
masks in public places. Sadly, government leadership - from the President to the Governor to the Mayor - have not felt a similar
urge to protect lives by doing whatever they could until the Governor finally ignored political pressure and did the right thing this
week. Infection rates are spiking as winter approaches and that is because so many acted like the virus would go away if almost
nothing was done about it. Even though the deaths in this community are tragic, there is a wider issue of lingering health issues
resulting from COVID. Every case that is prevented now will reduce the burden on the health care system down the road. He
referenced a peer review study in a medical journal indicating that 20 percent of people who contracted COVID-19 and survive
are experiencing mental illness issues; that is one in five people. With Utah's case numbers hovering near 3,000 new cases per
day, that is potentially 600 more people experiencing mental illness every day. Utah is a state that suffers from the fifth highest
suicide rate in the nation. Considering rising homelessness, skyrocketing housing prices, and potentially soaring mental illness, it
does not take much to imagine that the City may be entering a situation that will be much, much worse ifnothing is done. While
the Governor finally took action about the pandemic, he is not doing enough to address housing costs and he wondered ifit will be
necessary to wait for a true crisis before state leadership takes action. More could have done about the pandemic and citizens may
be paying the price for poor choices for a generation or more.
