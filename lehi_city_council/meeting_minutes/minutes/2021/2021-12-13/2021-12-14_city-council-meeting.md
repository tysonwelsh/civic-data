108   6. Consideration of Concept approval for the Thanksgiving Station concept, proposing new
109      land uses for a mixed-used development including approximately 5,300 new residential
110      units within the Thanksgiving Point Area Plan located at Executive Parkway and Ashton
111      Boulevard.
112      Representing the applicant was Nathan Ricks, Chris Parzio, John Bankhead and McKay
113      Christensen.
114
115       Mr. Ricks said that the State is facing growth issues that include doubling the population,
116       urban sprawl, air pollution and traffic. Growth is inevitable, so we need to improve and
117       create sustainable communities. Density needs to be in places of transit. He said that1,000
118       units in this area is not big enough to sustain a functional TOD. He explained that traffic can
119       improve in a well-planned TOD.
120
121       Mr. Parzio explained how they could create amenities and pointed out some of the design
122       elements that they plan to use to make it highly walkable.
123
124       Mr. Bankhead stated that the layout of the streets is the best way to deal with pedestrian and
125       bike traffic. They are encouraging people not to get in their cars. They also would like to
126       make room for a nearby school site.
127
128       Jordan Swain, with UTA, stated that they support projects like this that are built around
129       transit. A cohesive vision is important to mitigate concerns.
130
131       Josh Gibbins, with Hales Engineering, stated that they propose improvements to mitigate
132       existing congestion and to prepare for future growth. He also talked about road and transit
133       improvements to the area.
134
135       Nathan Ricks explained how the project could be funded using public private cooperation,
136       enact HTRZ and PID statues. He said that they will be motivated to work with the State and
137       others to plan for infrastructure and explained the phases for road triggers.
138
139       McKay Christensen, with Thanksgiving Point, stated that they value the partnership with
140       Lehi. They are a non- profit, so they look to all sources of funding to meet their budget needs
141       and try to find the best sustainable model. Constructing this would create an ongoing budget
142       source.

301        Motion: Councilor Koivisto moved to deny this proposal with the findings that it does not
302                meet the TOD zoning description of having the highest densities centered and
303                adjacent to transit stops with the sites being within one half mile walking or biking
304                distance of an existing or future transit station along a rail line. It also does not
305                fulfill the TOD requirement of open space in that a minimum of 10% of the total
306                gross average in a TOD shall be developed as open space, this site is approximately
307                146.5 acres and only 10.3 acres are open space in the project; it does not meet the
308                pedestrian circulation that provides a way to minimize pedestrian/auto conflict as
309                the project is presented and with the council of city staff, the projected deemed
310                improvements with the requested density would only compound the traffic issues
311                within the Thanksgiving Point Area, not alleviate them, and lastly, the projected
312                use of the project’s impact fees does not afford enough funds for the build out of
313                the proposed needed infrastructure and places a financial burden on the taxpayers
314                of Lehi for that buildout of power, water and sewer facilities.
315
316        The Motion failed due to lack of a second.
