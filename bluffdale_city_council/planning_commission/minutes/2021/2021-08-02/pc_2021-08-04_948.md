Mrs. Robison identified on the site plan where the parking will be located. She also identified

Mrs. Robison next reviewed the different elevations of the proposed building from a variety of
angles. In light of the design standards that have undergone substantial discussion as of late, the
applicants were requesting to use insulated metal panels for the siding. The center of the building
will have a blend of opaque glass and clear glass. There will be clear glass on some of the walls.
The site lighting around the building will meet the standards of the City Code. A truck lane will
also go around the perimeter of the building. It would be behind the fence line.

Mrs. Robison repo11ed that approximately 82% of the project will use the insulated metal panel.
The remaining 18% will use glazing or windows. The proposal will meet the required 40%
threshold for the first floor. The materials being requested were deemed to be best to address the
slope and seismic concerns of the subject prope11y. The proposed building design is contemporary
and will be compatible with the other buildings on the campus. The Architect said in a written
statement that the building fa9ade was designed primarily with consideration for the building use in
mind and how the exterior materials will benefit the project as a whole. They will also meld as part
of the campus aesthetic.

The building was designed to have the best-used space in Porter Rockwell. The use of the Insulated
Metal Panels (" IMP") was deemed the best option to meet all of the design challenges. The seismic
and topography concerns will best be addressed by a more lightweight building material rather than
concrete. The use of IMP will also allow for a weather-tight envelope that could be installed
quickly and efficiently because the panels interlock with each other and will meet the latest energy
conservation standards. Although IMP will be the primary building material, they will have two
different finishes to give the impression of being different building materials. The panels will be
designed to blend in well with the other buildings on the campus. The glazing and glass in the
center of the building will be where customers enter the building.

       a.     Discussion Regarding Planning Commissioners Training Sponsored by the Utah
              League of Cities and Towns and Other Training Opportunities.

Chair Cragun added the above item to the agenda after receiving an email about requirements for
Planning Commissioners to receive special training regarding their duties. Mr. Crowell reported
that the training requireme nt came from the Utah Legislature as HB-409 on May 5, 202 1, in an
effort to enhance the continuing education of Planning Commissioners.

HB-409 specifies that each Planning Commissioner must undergo four hours of land use training
each year. One hour will address General Powers and Duties under Title 10. The other three hours
would be training on land use, which could include such items as appeals and variances;
Conditional Use Permits; exactions; impact fees ; vested rights; subdivision regulations and
improvement guarantees; land use referenda; property rights; real estate procedures and financing;
zoning, including use-based and fo rm-based; and drafting ordinances, and Code that complies with
the statute.

Newly appointed Planning Commissioners would not be allowed to participate in public meetings
as appointed members until they have completed the training. Approved training would be
provided by municipal staff, the Utah League of Cities and Towns, or the Division of Real Estate.
The municipality would be responsible to maintain a record of the training completed by each
Planning Commissioner. Mr. Crowell anticipated that Associate City Planner, Ellen Oakman,
would be the City' s Training Officer and create a spreadsheet to track and ensure the City's
compliance with this piece of legislation. Chair Cragun learned a lot of what was included on the
list by her attendance at the Economic Development Work Meetings over the past two years. She
wanted to know if that training would count. Mr. Crowell would confer with the City Attorney to
asce11ain the acceptability of that information as training. He added that Pl anning Commi ssioners
would also need to keep a log of any training they receive.

Mr. Crowell explained that elected officials do not have the same training requirement as the
appointed officials. The onus is on the appointed officials to help the elected officials admini ster
the training being received.

Mr. Crowell presented a slide dealing with online classes in land use, provided by the Land Use
Academy of Utah ("LUAU"). A class called Land Use 101 : Powers and Duties, would cover the
first hour required. The City should keep a record of training completed by Planning
Commissioners. Di scussion ensued on how the training could be provided or incorporated into
Planning Commission meetings. Mr. Crowe ll stated that the Utah League of C ities and Towns
holds two annual conferences and may cover topics that would apply. A lso, the State Planning
Association holds a conference on September 9 and 10 that might be applicable.

Commissioner Loumis commented that short meetings would be an excellent time to complete
some training. Appeals and variances were identified as a good topic. For those who cannot attend
a meeting, Emergency Preparedness Manager, Natali e Hall, indicated that meetings are recorded, so
Planning Commi ssioners could watch or listen to the proceedings.
