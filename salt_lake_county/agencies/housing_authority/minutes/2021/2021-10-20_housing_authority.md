expense budget could be a potential i ssue, so th ey want to vet as much as possible  prior to closing .  He remarked that on the design

6. PRESENTATION 
 
Housing Connect CPO Zach Bale gave a HousingNow presentation to the Board.  He informed the Board that he co -chairs the Ho using 
Core Function Group (which is part of the Salt Lake Valley Coalition to End Homelessness - SLVCEH), initially with Mike Akerlow and 
presently with Becky Brown, and then gave a brief background on the SLVCEH. 
 
He showed a slide about strengthening the economic health of our community and improving the lives of those in the County through 
strategic investments in housing, supportive services, and financial stability. 
 
He pointed out that homelessness is a housing crisis and that a signi ficant investment in deeply affordable housing will result in stability 
for families, reduced reliance on Homeless Resource Centers, and a thriving community across Salt Lake County and the state of Utah. 
 
He explained that a lot of the discussion centers around federal funding that is coming (or has already come) through the American 
Rescue Plan.  He mentioned talk of $100 million to $200 million at the (state) legislature for housing.  He indicated that ro ughly 30% to 
40% of homeless individuals need long -term support, with a housing need of 2,950 units and an additional 1,400 units to meet projected 
estimates. 
 
He reviewed housing types (permanent supportive housing, rapid re -housing, etc.) and went over how much each type of housing 
resource is needed (and associated costs), along with projections into the future. 
 
He then showed the total number of units needed against all affordable units and the homeless set -aside pipeline, which demonstrated 
the gap that exists with the current resources being far les s than what is needed (current funding tools aren't sufficient to meet the 
need). 
 
For the financing plan, he pointed out that the numbers are quite daunting (with the estimate being just above $1 billion), n oting that a 
lot of it could be supported by market forces. 
 
 
 
 
 
He also remarked that we know these different types of housing work, but that more is needed. 
 
He showed how housing reduces other costs (in the medical system; in the judicial/criminal system; etc.) when there is suffic ient 
affordable housing and that as a community, money shifted from one activity to housing is the strongest solution. 
 
He also went over some of the recommendations that have been made. 
 
Commissioner White asked about how refugees fit into the plan.  CFO Bale responded that mos t of the Afghan refugees fall outside of 
government housing support due to their current status. 
 
Commissioner Nguyen noted that there are pockets of people, including those on Medicaid waivers, who live in restricted circumstances 
and who have been allowe d to stay in higher services due to COVID, but that will be ending soon, and they’ll need to move to their own 
independent situations, and there just isn’t housing out there, even if those individuals have vouchers to use. 
 
Commissioner Bernal recommended similar presentations to the Board every few months as a reminder of the entire picture of wh y the 
agency is doing what it is doing. 
 
7. CEO’s REPORT 
 
Chair Moffat asked Housing Connect CEO Janice Kimball about her report, and she touched on the following items: 
 
- The Hub of Opportunity:  Housing Connect closed financing on The Hub the day before (Tuesday, October 19).  She also thanked the 
Board for its support and guidance on the warranty issues.  Housing Connect and Columbus met with the owner's representative (Mike 
Ferro), who offered to try to resolve the issue directly with R&O Construction.  He has talked with R&O, and CEO Janice Kimball reported 
that there has been really good movement with R&O since then. 
 
- Sunset Gardens:  Sunset Gardens was recently approved for $2 million in funding to complete the work on the property.  The agency will 
begin tenant relocation on November 1.  Housing Connect will work with tenants to issue vouchers, and there are several vacancies in 
public housing which are being held, if tenants aren't able to use their vouchers.  Housing Connect expects construction to start in the 
second quarter of next year. 
 
- Parkhill:  There was a meeting on October 5, with 20 of the 25 owners in attendance.  The owners approved Housing Connect assuming 
the ownership of the property.  Housing Connect will close with Rocky Mountain Community Reinvestment Corporation (RMCRC) on 
December 1 and will assume management.  The agency will need to meet with each individual family/household to do an assessment and 
get a lease signed. 
 
- Holladay City:  CEO Janice Kimball had a conversation recently with the Holladay City Community Development Director. Holladay City is 
struggling with what to do regarding re-development funding, as they had an agreement with Salt Lake County to add 100 units of 
affordable housing (for households at 80% area median income - AMI - and below), which is tied to the Cottonwood Mall property.  CEO 
Kimball gave them some options to consider. 
 
- Moving To Work (MTW):  There will be an MTW advisory board committee meeting in the first part of November.  Housing Connect is 
working really closely with HUD and research consultants on the Rent Reform study.  The agency will want the Board to sign the Annual 
Contributions Contract (ACC) at the December meeting. 
 
- NAHRO (National Association of Housing and Re-development Officials) representation:  Housing Connect CEO Janice Kimball reported 
that the organization is well represented at several levels in NAHRO.  She is pleased with this because of the networking opportunities, 
the information the agency receives on policy and (legislative) bills and how to implement the work, and the great opportunities it gives 
staff in terms of leadership development. 
 
- Visit by students:  Housing Connect hosted students from Lowell Bennion this past week for their Fall service program.  They were 
interested in affordable housing and advocacy.  Housing Connect Housing and Supportive Services Director Russell Opatz and his team 
provided a "Housing 101" session, and the students helped the agency's Parents As Teachers (PAT) staff to move offices and supplies, and 
they assisted with organizing things. 
 
- Funding:  Housing Connect has applied for additional funding capacity for case management. 
 
- Website:  The Housing Connect website was down most of the previous week.  The agency is looking for additional security support.
