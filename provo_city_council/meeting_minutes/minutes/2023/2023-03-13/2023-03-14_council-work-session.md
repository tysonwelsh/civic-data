4.      A discussion of revised west side FEMA maps (23-028) (2:27:44)

Jamie Huff, Holly Strand, and Tracie Harrison of the Utah Division of Emergency Management
presented. Ms. Huff briefed the Council on the background of the study which was conducted.
Flood risk is not a static issue; it changes over time. It is important to review these studies
periodically in order to appropriately plan for risk. The flood risk information used since 1988
was in paper form until 2020 when it was digitized. Ms. Huff reviewed more of the history of
including the Provo River in a new study conducted by FEMA. There is a levee identified on the
maps and levees must be certified in order to be included on FEMA maps. The existing levees
were not able to be certified and the study scope was revised to perform the study without levee
analysis. The local levee partnership team is comprised of a number of stakeholders and was
organized to involve these different stakeholders in the study.

Three jurisdictions are included in the analysis, including Utah County, Provo City, and Orem
City. The Provo River, airport, and Lakewood subdivision levees are not certified. This study
effort is not to certify the levees—the intent is to show the current risk assessment of the river
today within FEMA standards. Uncertified levees cannot be shown on any FEMA maps as
providing protections. Even certified levees can have some level of risk of failure or compromise
during a flood event. The study was conducted from the mouth of Provo Canyon down to Utah
Lake. Ms. Huff reviewed the data sets they collected and that informed the risk assessment,
including new Lidar data (digital topography), Provo River field survey, hydrology information
on Provo River, and hydraulics of water entering the ground. For Utah Lake, they conducted
field studies including hydrology, wind, and wave analysis.
