1. Discussion of Zoning Ordinance amendment limiting multiple-family in C-2 and C-3 zones.

Mr. Brierley mentioned this item had been addressed at the last hearing, and there had been some people
since then who wanted to add comment, but the hearing had been closed. So, we're anticipating that you
would deliberate at your next meeting but not open it up.

Chair Sandau asked if they need more public?

Mr. Aaberg said they were good, depending on the changes they were presenting.

Ms. Shale commented there were big concerns from developers with the 10-acre thing because smaller local
developers wouldn't have the ability to build something like that.

Mr. Aaberg added that the Washington Blvd area was a concern as well as to whether it should be included.
Mr. Brierley reminded, why the city administration proposed this was just to get the right thing in the right
place and make sure we are putting apartments in the appropriate place for apartments. We talked about
quality of the neighborhoods, that you have parks, bike trails and places to walk and safety and all those
things that make a good neighborhood, and there's a concern about avoiding over saturation that deteriorate
and create problems over time. One of the things that there was a lot of discussion about was mixed use
development. So, we had proposed a carve out that says, if you had a mixed-use development of 10 acres or
larger, that you could have residential in there, but 75% of the ground floor as nonresidential use. What we
had in mind is a real mixed-use project where you have the ground floor retail, and you have a lot of amenities
for the residents inside the project. You might have a plaza, you might have a fitness center, you might have
a grocery store, you might have transit, all self-contained within that development. The idea is a big enough
project where you can add amenities and it not be just a single development. So, when we put the 10-acre
in, that was really the idea, is to get a big enough chunk that you can actually develop a full neighborhood,
rather than just do a one off. He suggested they make their best recommendation on what they think it needs,
whether it be change or tweaking and express their likes and dislikes. (slide shown). He informed they could
also do an amendment. He was suggesting with some of the fringe areas, maybe just changing the zoning
some, if it really makes sense to put housing there. If it's going to be a mixed use, make the whole thing look
like that. Also, we looked at the percent of affordable housing in the county. We have about 30% of the
population and almost 70% of the affordable housing. This is subsidized housing, and Ogden has like 82%
of the bad, and then we looked at areas of racial and concentrated poverty, which HUD guidelines say this is
