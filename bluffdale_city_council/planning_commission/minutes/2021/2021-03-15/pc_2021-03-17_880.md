Associate City Planner, Ellen Oakman, presented the staff report and identified the location of the
live/work units in Independence. She then reviewed the zoning of the surrounding areas. The
proposal under consideration was to add two pennitted uses specifically to the existing live/work
units. She next identified the location of the nine townhomes that were approved as live/work units
in the Marketplace Development. They were intended to serve as a buffer between the residential
units and the nearby commercial properties.

The two additional permitted uses being proposed are Professional Services and Personal Service
Establishment. The rationale for adding the permitted uses was the fact that they already exist in
the Definitions Chapter of Title 11.20. Some residents have requested these uses, but since they are
not on the existing list of pennitted uses in the live/work units, the City was seeking to add the two
permitted uses. Ms. Oakman reviewed the prohibited uses for the live/work units. She reiterated
that the maximum work area allowed is 500 square feet with only one employee in addition to the
residents of the dwelling. Parking requirements were also set forth in City Code.

In response to Commissioner Walston's question regarding the genesis for the change, Ms. Oakman
stated that the City received feedback from several people who want to have businesses, such as a
nail salon, that are not currently permitted in the live/work units. City Planner/Economic
Development Director, Grant Crowell explained that some people who have moved into the units
discovered they cannot have the business they were planning on. The City Council was apprised of
the situation and directed staff to make the permitted uses more permissive. The two uses are
consistent with what the City already has in its list of business definitions.

In response to Chair Cragun's question regarding whether the permitted uses in Bluffdale's
live/work units are consistent with what other cities allow, Mr. Crowell responded that he had not
reviewed what other cities do. Chair Cragun did not have a problem with the proposal and was just
curious as to how Bluffdale's permitted uses compare to those of other cities.

Commissioner Walston did not object to the proposed additions to the live/work units. Ms. Oakman
reminded the Planning Commission that the live/work units are near other commercial zones.
Mr. Crowell explained that the concept of live/work units is a new idea in the Mixed-Use Zone.
Chair Cragun emphasized that there are only nine such units, so the impact will be nominal.
Mr. Crowell added that they are small, and the homeowner must live on-site.

Chair Cragun opened the public hearing. There were no comments. The public hearing was closed.

Kory Luker moved to forward a positive recommendation to the City Council for the Text
Amendment Application 2021-08 based on the following:

Findings:

4.     PUBLIC HEARING, CONSIDERATION, AND RECOMMENDATION on a City-
       Initiated Text Amendment Application to Amend Title 11, Chapters 11.20, 11.90,
       11.120, 11.150, 11.160, 11.220 et seq. of the Bluffdale City Code Regarding Required
       City Commercial Site, Building, and Landscaping Standards, in the GC-1 Zone and
       City-Wide, along with other Related Administrative Provisions. (Application 2020-54).
       Staff Presenter, Grant Crowell.

Mr. Crowell presented the staff report and stated that there has been some concern over the past two
years that the type of development on 2700 West near Bangerter Highway was not what was
anticipated. That served as a catalyst for developing an ordinance to clarify and enhance the design
standards for the 2700 West corridor. The City does not have an Architect on staff, so it has taken a
lot of consultation and study to develop the proposed text amendments. The goal was to encourage
economic development, however, the market dictates what can and what cannot be done. Mr.
Crowell 's dual role as City Planner and Economic Development Director makes for a tricky
balancing act of developing ideas and standards that are compatible with the City's goals of being
economically robust and aesthetically appealing. The options are endless, so developing design and
architectural standards is daunting because it involves balancing regulation versus encouragement.
He stated that the proposed text amendments will include a lot of landscaping standards to enhance
water conservation.

Mr. Crowell next reviewed the document included in the meeting packet relating to the GC-1 Zone.
Some of the changes included additional definitions and updating of processes. In response to
Commissioner Walston's question regarding what 2700 West is supposed to look like, Mr. Crowell
said it was his understanding that the City Council wants the frontages on the 2700 West corridor to
NOT look like warehouses. That is the primary objective, along with looking more like retail. The
City Council wants the entryway into the City to reflect what the City wants people to see as they
enter Bluffdale. Mr. Crowell stated that one approach would be to eliminate uses that the City does
not want, however, some desirable businesses, such as carpet stores, would require high ceilings.

Chair Cragun stated that based on her participation in the Joint Economic Development Meetings,
this issue has proved to be challenging since everyone wants the gateway to look nice but struggle
to articulate what that means. That difficulty in articulation results in push-back from builders and
architects. Chair Cragun commended Mr. Crowell for the work he has done on the draft document
being reviewed. Mr. Crowell hoped to get meaningful feedback from architects to help him
understand what does and does not work and why.

Areas of focus included 2700 West and 14000 South. He enumerated the many issues that highlight
the complexity of coming up with design guidelines that meet everyone' s needs. Some situations
may work and others may not, especially with building materials and window types. Some builders
want materials that do not require much maintenance. The existing ordinance discourages metal
buildings except in areas such as gravel pits. There are, however, options for enhancing the
aesthetics of metal buildings. Color preferences are a moving target, so it is difficult to codify color
schemes. Lighting requirements are also an imp01tant consideration. He next addressed parking
standards. Landscape architecture requirements were also added.

Mr. Crowell stated that once the Legislative Session ends, there may be needed updates to design
requirements on townhomes. The designation of who the Land Use Authority might be could
change. Mr. Crowell again addressed landscaping that would maximize water conservation while
retaining aesthetic appeal. Mulch needs to be used without resorting to gravel and sod use needs to
be reduced when possible. He referenced the section on buffe1ing and perimeter planning to soften
the exterior of the site. Mr. Crowell addressed the use of walls to provide a buffer between
commercial and residential.

Commissioner Luker needed to leave the meeting, which would result in the loss of a quorum.
Commissioner Walston noted that the infonnation being provided is intense and abundant. He
asked if it could be summarized in bullet fonn to ease the processing of the material.

Chair Cragun suggested the matter be tabled as it would not be practical to take action tonight.
Mr. Crowell agreed to provide a more concise document at the next meeting.

Chair Cragun opened the public hearing.

Debbie Cragun moved to table the item until the next meeting with the public hearing to
remain open to get more feedback at planningmeetingcomments@bluffdale.com. Kory Luker
seconded the motion. Vote on motion: Stephen Walston-Aye, Kory Luker-Aye, Debbie
Cragun-Aye. The motion passed unanimously.
