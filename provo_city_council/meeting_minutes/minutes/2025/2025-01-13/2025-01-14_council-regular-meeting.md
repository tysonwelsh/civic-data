9.         An ordinance regarding a text amendment for the prohibition of sham property
           transactions. (PLOTA20240309) 0:50:20

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2025-3, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Jessica Dahneke, City Planner, presented. She said the text amendment aimed at closing a loophole in
the owner-occupancy requirement for accessory dwelling units. Currently, some property owners use
trusts or LLCs to bypass this requirement. The amendment clarifies that if ownership within an LLC or
trust changes within the first year of establishing a rental or obtaining a rental dwelling license, or if
there is a pattern of ownership changes over time, it will be considered a sham transaction. She added
that this clarification helps both applicants and city enforcement officials understand the expectations
and ensures compliance with the ordinance.

Chair Garrett opened the item for public comment. With no public comments, and no council discussion,
he called for a vote.

      Vote:        The motion passed 7:0 with Councilors Bogdin, Christensen, Garrett, Handley, Hoban,
                   MacKay, and Whipple in favor.

Industrial (AI) zone. The property in question has a history of different development plans, and about a
year ago, the general plan was updated to designate this area for airport-related future use instead of
residential. The proposed zoning change to AI aligns with that update. He said the concept plan for the

Councilor Christensen asked why these would not be three story buildings.

Mr. Yergensen explained that the proposed buildings would primarily function as live industrial
warehouses, typically combining storage and manufacturing space. He noted that increasing the height

Councilor Christensen asked if he would be able to consider 100% owner occupancy since the council
feels strongly about home ownership.

Mr. Bragonje clarified that the requirement for 51% owner occupancy is a minimum, and he believes all

large rental property due to parking limitations, noting that there would be a minimum of three parking
spaces per unit. Additionally, he pointed out that the owner-occupancy condition has not typically been
required for other recent projects in the area, emphasizing his intention to support owner occupancy
given the prevalence of rental units nearby, and reiterating that the proposal is for 16 units.

Councilor MacKay noted that the trail language from the previous project's development agreement
was still present and needed updating. She inquired if Mr. Bragonje would be open to ensuring that at

Councilor Whipple addressed a question about enforcing homeownership through deed restrictions. She
explained that recording a deed restriction would inform potential buyers of the requirement for owner
occupancy for at least a year before renting out the property, as this information would be public record
and part of the property's chain of title. This notice would ensure that anyone purchasing the property is
aware of the restriction upfront. Whipple suggested the city could also maintain a list of properties that
would not be eligible for rental dwelling licenses during the first year of ownership. She emphasized that
the deed restriction serves as a strong enforcement tool, independent of HOA rules, which can change
over time.

Councilor MacKay discussed the importance of owner occupancy in ensuring the success and
maintenance of developments. She noted that while market prices dictate housing costs, owner-
occupied properties typically receive better care than rentals, which often suffer from maintenance
issues. She cited a specific development in Buckley Draw that mandates owner occupancy and has a
strict HOA to oversee the properties. MacKay shared her experience as an HOA president, emphasizing
how a higher percentage of ownership leads to improved community upkeep and pride. She highlighted
the need to create more opportunities for homeownership in a city where 60% of residents are renters.
MacKay expressed optimism about the city’s efforts to incorporate these requirements into
development agreements to foster successful and desirable communities.
