BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY
This application is a request for approval of a Zoning Map Amendment for approximately 2.57
acres located on the west side of Bangerter Parkway, at approximately 14178 S. Bangerter
Parkway (Exhibit C & D). The property is currently zoned CG (General Commercial). The
applicant is requesting that a Zoning Map Amendment be approved to allow for the
development of the currently vacant portion of the site as multifamily residences. Only the
vacant portion of the lot is proposed to be rezoned.

The property currently is home to Ashford Assisted Living and Memory Care of Draper, an
assisted living facility owned and operated by Greg Nield and Rob Aird. They plan to develop
the southwest portion of the lot, which is currently vacant, into multifamily residences. The
current facility covers approximately 3.80 acres and was built in 2018.

According to Draper City Municipal Code (DCMC) Section 9-8-020 the purpose of the CG
(General Commercial) zone is to “provide areas to accommodate commercial uses which are not
within shopping centers of integrated design. This zone is intended to apply to existing "strip
commercial" developments. The zone should not be used for new commercial development
unless integrated shopping center development is not practical or desirable because of difficult
size, shape, topography, or similar problems related to land otherwise deemed to be
appropriate for commercial use. Typical uses in this zone include offices, retail stores, personal
services, heavy commercial, and institutional uses.”

Criteria for Approval

Zoning Text/Map Amendment 9-5-060(E)
A Map Amendment is a matter committed to the legislative discretion of the City Council and is
not controlled by any one standard. However, in making a recommendation to the City Council,
the Planning Commission should consider the following factors in Section 9-5-060(E)(1) of the
DCMC:

              a. Whether the proposed amendment is consistent with goals, objectives and
                 policies of the city's general plan;
              b. Whether the proposed amendment is harmonious with the overall character of
                 existing development in the vicinity of the subject property;
              c. Whether the proposed amendment is consistent with the standards of any
                 applicable overlay zone;
              d. The extent to which the proposed amendment may adversely affect adjacent
                 property; and
              e. The adequacy of facilities and services intended to serve the subject property,
                 including, but not limited to, roadways, parks and recreation facilities, police and
                 fire protection, schools, stormwater drainage systems, water supplies, and
                 wastewater and refuse collection.

STAFF RECOMMENDATION

Staff recommends that the Planning Commission review the request, receive public comment,
and make a decision based on the findings listed below and the criteria for approval, or denial,
as listed within the staff report.

The findings for approval as are follows:

    1. The proposed amendment is consistent with goals, objectives and policies of the city's
       general plan;
    2. The proposed amendment is harmonious with the overall character of existing
       development in the vicinity of the subject property;
    3. The proposed amendment is consistent with the standards of any applicable overlay
       zone;
    4. The proposed amendment should not adversely affect adjacent property; and
    5. The facilities and services intended to serve the subject property, including, but not
       limited to, roadways, parks and recreation facilities, police and fire protection, schools,
       stormwater drainage systems, water supplies, and wastewater and refuse collection, are
       adequate.

The findings for denial as are follows:

    1. The proposed amendment is not consistent with goals, objectives and policies of the
       city's general plan;
    2. The proposed amendment is not harmonious with the overall character of existing
       development in the vicinity of the subject property;
    3. The proposed amendment is not consistent with the standards of any applicable overlay
       zone;
    4. The proposed amendment may adversely affect adjacent property; and
    5. The facilities and services intended to serve the subject property, including, but not
       limited to, roadways, parks and recreation facilities, police and fire protection, schools,
       stormwater drainage systems, water supplies, and wastewater and refuse collection, are
       not adequate.
