    2. PUBLIC HEARING TO CONSIDER & TAKE ACTION ON A REQUEST TO VACATE A PUBLIC UTILITY EASEMENT
       LOCATED AT REAR OF LOT 35 (5131 S. 3550 W., ROY) OF COUNTRY MEADOWS SUBDIVISION.
         Steven Burton, of the County Planning Division, stated that the lot owner, Dallas Rowe, requested to vacate a
         7-foot public utility easement at the rear of his property to build an accessory structure about 3 feet to the rear
         property line. Planning staff recommends approval of this vacation based upon the findings in the staff report.
         According to State statue, the Commission may adopt an ordinance granting a petition to vacate some or all of
         a public easement if it finds that good cause exists for the vacation and neither the public interest nor any person
         will be materially injured by the vacation.
         Commissioner Ebert asked for the purpose of placing this original easement (in 1977) and Mr. Burton explained
         that when development occurs the county likes to see easements along property lines and sometimes developers
         just place them arbitrarily on all property lines. Today a development would generally place utility easements
         at the front property lines. Commissioner Ebert noted that the rear of the property is undeveloped and asked
         about future development. Mr. Burton does not believe it would have any effect on that property, which
         currently is owned by Weber Basin Water and is a pond and has a road stub. If it were developed, utilities
         would likely follow that stubbed road. State code requires all affected entities to be sent notice, which the
         county did. Public utility companies, including Questar Gas, Rocky Mountain Power, Weber Basin Water
         Conservancy District, North Davis Sewer District, and Hooper Water Improvement District, have consented to
         the easement’s vacation. Regarding drainage issues, Mr. Burton responded that County Engineering did a site
         inspection and determined that there is no need for a drainage easement there. If an easement is needed on
         future developable property, one could be placed as part of the development process. Mr. Rowe stated that he
         had done due diligence and contacted seven utility companies which gave permission to vacate the property.
         There are already structures closer than 3-ft. to that property line on the same side of the street.
     3. Public comments: Steve Parkinson, of Roy City, said that Roy has a letter where the applicant can get all the
        utility companies to sign off and then the applicant can encroach into the easement but the easement stays. The
        City would like either to vacate the entire thing or have it denied. Commissioner Ebert asked what negative
        impact to the City Mr. Parkinson envisioned if the easement was granted, and he responded that if a utility
        company would need to go back there it would be easier to give a variance or have the letter stating that it can
        encroach within that easement than to go back and create the easement. Mr. Burton said that offering a variance
        of easement is an option but generally one has to show site-specific hardship (i.e., due to a ditch, slope) that
        warrants a variance to allow building within that easement, and this may be difficult to demonstrate because it
        appears to just be an arbitrary easement at the back of the property.
