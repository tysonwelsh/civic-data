REVIEW AGENDA FOR REGULAR CITY COUNCIL MEETING OF MARCH 10,
2020
Steve Pastorik, CED, provided a clarifying summary on application ZT-17-2019/
Ordinance 20-13 (attached to minutes). Councilmember Huynh asked what the proposed
radius from TRAX stations is. Steve replied 0.5 acres. Councilmember Huynh verified that
if the ordinance were approved, these areas would be eligible for RM zoning. Steve replied
yes. Councilmember Huynh clarified that properties within a half mile radius of the
Chesterfield TRAX station, even those not adjacent to Redwood Road, would be eligible
for the RM zoning if this ordinance were approved. Steve replied yes. Councilmember
Buhler stated that someone could assemble property now and develop high density housing
as long as there was frontage on Redwood Road. Mayor Bigelow asked if the City has ever
used eminent domain to purchase a property for high density housing. Steve replied there
was one in Fairbourne Station where the property owner was deceased, and the process had
to occur for the City to move forward. Mayor Bigelow stated that this ordinance does not
physically change anything or negatively impact the neighborhood. He indicated this
concept simply allows more opportunity for growth but is not requiring any type of change.
Councilmember Huynh stated that he feels this will open the floodgates to significant
change in Chesterfield. Councilmember Fitisemanu asked if there has been interest in any
properties included in this ordinance from developers. Steve replied no. Councilmember
Lang stated that she is not comfortable with high density housing around other Trax
stations but is comfortable with it around the Fairbourne Station/City Center area. She
indicated that neighborhood roads cannot handle high capacity traffic. Steve stated that
traffic studies would be required as part of the zone change. Mayor Bigelow indicated that
the Moratorium will be expiring and when it does, developers and property owners can
request zone changes or other text changes to the RM ordinance. Councilmember Buhler
stated that this ordinance also requires increased building standards, amenities, and
parking. Councilmember Huynh distributed minutes from a high density housing proposal
near the Maverik Center that was denied by the Council in 2013 (attached). He stated there
are businesses in the area that are growing strong and healthy and he feels the Council
made a good decision. Councilmember Huynh stated that he is okay with the proposal but
doesn’t believe Trax stations in Chesterfield should be included. Councilmember Lang
agreed and noted that she would like to place a cap on density and suggested 20 units per
acre or increasing parking standards to 3 stalls per unit.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Bigelow, members of the Council had no further questions or
concerns regarding items listed on the Agenda for the Regular City Council Meeting
scheduled later this night.

 ACTION: ORDINANCE NO. 20-15, AMEND THE GENERAL PLAN TO
 SHOW A CHANGE OF LAND USE FROM MIXED USE TO LIGHT
 MANUFACTURING FOR PROPERTY LOCATED AT 4850 WEST 3500
 SOUTH ON 3.89 ACRES
 Steve Pastorik, CED, discussed proposed Ordinance 20-15 that would amend the
 General Plan to show a change of land use from Mixed Use to Light Manufacturing
 for property located at 4850 West 3500 South on 3.89 acres.

 Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
 information as follows:

        The applicant is proposing to utilize the existing building, which was
        previously used as a Shopko, as primarily a warehouse with the secondary
        use being assembly and disassembly of furniture. Since the C-2 zone only
        allows up to 50% of a building to be utilized as a warehouse, the M zone is
        needed to facilitate the applicant’s proposal. Included with this report is a
        letter from the applicant describing the proposed use as well as reasons to
        support this application.

        In the applicant’s letter he mentions that the current owner, Mike Kehoe,
        has tried unsuccessfully to secure retail tenants to fill the building. Included
        with this report are two emails from the owner that provide a list of tenants
        that were contacted.

        The applicant’s original proposal was to change the General Plan and
        zoning on the entire 7.73 acre parcel. However, given input from the
        Planning Commission, the applicant agreed to reduce the scope of the
        application to the north 3.89 acres. To further reduce the impact of the
        change, the Planning Commission recommended a development agreement
        that addresses use limitations, hours limitations and a few required
        improvements.

RESOLUTION 20-29: AUTHORIZE THE CITY TO ENTER INTO A
DEVELOPMENT AGREEMENT WITH MOUNTAIN AMERICA FEDERAL
CREDIT UNION FOR APPROXIMATELY 1.05 ACRES OF PROPERTY
LOCATED AT APPROXIMATELY 2850 WEST 3500 SOUTH
Steve Pastorik, CED, presented proposed Resolution 20-29 that would authorize the City
to enter into a Development Agreement with Mountain America Federal Credit Union for
approximately 1.05 acres of property located at approximately 2850 West 3500 South.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       MACU has acquired the property at 2850 West 3500 South to redevelop the site
       into a new credit union branch. The existing building on the site was a David Early
       Tire Center.

       The City Center (CC) Zone allows the requirements of the Zone to be superseded
       by a development agreement. MACU is seeking two exceptions to the CC Zone
       requirements. The first exception allows metal panels, fiber-cement and tile as
     MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – MARCH 10, 2020
                           -8-


       exterior materials. Metal panels were used on the Fairbourne Station Office Tower,
       Granger Medical and Embassy Suites. Fiber-cement was used on both apartment
       buildings within Fairbourne Station. In staff’s opinion, tile is a good complimentary
       material given the design of the building.

       The second exception allows the drive-through canopy to not meet the minimum
       building height requirements. Staff is supportive of this exception because the rest
       of the building meets the minimum height and the drive-through canopy is placed
       behind the rest of the building.

Mayor Bigelow asked if similar exceptions were granted for the America First or Zions
Bank in the area. Steve replied that both of these are located in the C-2 zone. Mayor
Bigelow asked if the proposed canopy is similar to those on other banks and credit unions.
Steve replied yes. Councilmember Lang suggested adding these materials into the City
Center ordinance.

The City Council will consider Resolution 20-29 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
March 17, 2020 at 6:30 P.M.

RESOLUTION 20-30: APPROVE A FIRST AMENDED AGREEMENT OF THE
UTAH MUNICIPAL RISK MANAGEMENT ASSOCIATION
Mayor Bigelow presented proposed Resolution 20-30 that would approve a First Amended
Agreement of the Utah Municipal Risk Management Association.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

   Since the creation of URMMA, the membership of the entity has changed and the
   circumstances confronting member entities have changed. The amended interlocal
   agreement updates the original agreement to take these circumstances into account.
   Significant changes include:

       1) The renaming of the entity to “Utah Risk Management Agency” to account for
          non-municipality members.
       2) Updated references to statutes amended since the adoption of the original
          agreement.
       3) Clarifications to language concerning governmental immunity and nonwaiver
          of claims or defenses by members and the organization.
       4) Formatting changes for clarity.

The City Council will consider Resolution 20-30 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
