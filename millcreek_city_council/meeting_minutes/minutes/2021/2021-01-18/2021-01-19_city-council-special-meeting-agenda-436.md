Amanda        Dillon,       MHTN,         showed       images        and explained           the benefits        of public-private
partnerships         from     real examples           with    Lenexa       City     Hall,     Long      Beach      Civic      Center,      Redmond
City Hall,      and Orange           County        Civic     Center.


Benj Becker,          Zions      Public    Finance,          said the goal of a public-private                    partnership          was to maximize
tax dollars.      This could         happen        in four different          scenarios.        The first was a sale/leaseback                     scenario
where       the city owns land and sells to a developer                           at discount          and the developer               leases office
space to the City           for below        market        rent. The second was a ground                       lease scenario           where     the City
owns land and creates a ground                      lease to a developer              and the developer              leases office            space to the
city for a duration           of the ground           lease. The improvements                       would     go back to the city at the end of
the lease. The third             was a ground          lease scenario         where         the city leases land, constructs                    and
occupies       a building,        and leases the remaining                 space to other entities.               Additional           revenue
generated       from     renting       corninercial         office     and retail     spaces would             go back to the city. The fourth
was a condominium                 scenario       where       the city owns land, agrees with                     a developer           to construct       a
building.      The developer            does not pay for the land, and the city is given                              the opportunity             to buy a
condominium             unit at a reduced           price     commensurate            with      the land value.           Additional          revenue
generated       from     renting/selling           corni'nercial        spaces could          go to the developer               and/or     the city.
Council      Member          Marchant        asked if the condominium                       option     could     avoid the city having                to bond
to pay for it. Becker             said it would        just be the cost for the condominium                            and not the construction
if the city owned           or leased the land to developers,                      so the city would             be less likely          to bond based
on the cost of the condominium.
