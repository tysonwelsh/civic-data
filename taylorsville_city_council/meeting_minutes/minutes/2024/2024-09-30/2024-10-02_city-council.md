Chair Cochran commented that Century Link fiber appeared to be expanding into more
Taylorsville neighborhoods, and he was getting many questions from constituents.

City Administrator John Taylor explained that they were expanding their service and installing
new boxes. If any of the boxes were the above- ground variety, he asked that the City be notified
immediately. Placement of underground boxes was not a problem.

Council Member Burgess commented that there were still several in his district which were above
ground. Mr. Taylor pointed out that the older above- ground vaults would likely be replaced with
other above- ground vaults.

Community/Economic Development Director Wayne Harper clarified that a permit had been
issued to Century Link just the day before which was conditioned upon them going back and
replacing everything that was above ground with those that were below ground. City attorney
Tracy Cowdell pointed out that there were many utilities with boxes in the area; they weren' t all
related to Century Link.

Council Member Burgess reiterated that he did not want those    living in District#   1 to be forgotten.


On another subject, Chair Cochran wondered if there were any regulations regarding the paint used
by utility companies to mark various lines, cables, etc. He explained that the paint Google had
used two years ago in his neighborhood was still evident on the sidewalk and streets, even after he
used a pressure washer. CFO/Assistant City Administrator Scott Harrington mentioned that he
thought the legislature had run a bill approximately three years ago which would have required
such paint to dissolve within 60- 90 days. No one seemed to know what had become of that
legislation.


Council Member Burgess returned to the topic of Century Link. Mr. Taylor indicated it was
important for people to understand that there were utility easements that had been in place for
many years. Utility companies had the right to access private property and to update their
infrastructure, no matter that it was disruptive to property owners.

                    1. 2. 1     Proposed Tax Increase for Unified        Fire Service Area—    Chief Dominic
                                Burchett, UFSA District Administrator Rachel Anderson and CFO Tony
                                Hill

IIEJ Rachel Anderson explained that UFSA was a taxing entity which was the funding mechanism
for Unified Fire. They collected revenue and then distributed it to UFA who provided the fire and
emergency medical services for twelve member jurisdictions, one of which was Taylorsville. She
and her colleagues were present to provide information about an upcoming proposed tax increase.
As part of the tax increase process, they were required to do so for each of the entities for which
UFA provided services. This was phase two of a tax increase that had begun the previous year.
Rather than approve a large increase last year, the Board voted to have several smaller increases
spread out over several years.

There were four reasons a tax increase was needed:
          To build and update fire stations ( Four new stations had been built in the last 18 months)
          Additional staffing needs due to increases in population
          Rising cost of services
          To maintain a board- approved minimum fund balance


Ms. Anderson explained that the average value of a home in their district was $ 525, 300. Such a
home would          see   a   monthly   increase of approximately $   7. 77. An additional increase was being
proposed to address some staffing needs that Chief Burchett would address later in the meeting.
That increase, if approved, would mean an additional $ 3. 45 per month for the aforementioned
 average" home.


Factors contributing to the need for a tax increase included general inflation, an increase in wages,
increased costs of replacing capital assets such as fire- fighting apparatuses and continually rising
construction costs. It was critical to remain competitive with other departments            so as to ensure the
UFA was staffed with the most experienced and effective employees. Ms. Anderson then turned
the time over to Chief Burchett.

UFA Chief Dominic Burchett explained that there had been an 86% increase in the cost of a turnout
set and each set needed to be replaced               after   5- 7 years.   Fuel had gone up by 32% and the medical
supply budget had increased by 21%.

He explained that while the consumer       price index ( CPI) had increased by 26. 5%, the wages for
an   entry level firefighter had only increased  by 22. 10%. Regardless of the CPI, their goal was to
attract the best employees they possibly could.

They had a robust program for replacing capital assets.                     A Type     1 fire engine   had cost $ 822, 216
the previous year which          was
                                         up 32% since 2018. An ambulance that cost $ 270, 709 in 2018 cost
 344, 209        in 2023 while   a     ladder truck had gone from $ 1. 005 million to $ 1. 7 million during the
same     time frame which represented           a   69% increase.


Regarding construction costs, the UFA had been in the process for almost ten years of updating
their fire stations to be seismically sound. Two separate bonds had been approved to replace those
stations that could not be retrofitted and instead, needed to be entirely replaced. A grant from the
Federal Emergency Management       Agency( FEMA) had covered the retrofitting— five stations with
structural retrofits, and twenty stations that needed non- structural retrofitting. Chief Burchett
pointed out that construction costs had increased by 91% since 2017.

Staff had been added to stations in both Kearns ( which served Taylorsville)                      and Eagle Mountain.

In the past five years, UFA' s district- wide population had increased from 270, 101 to 305, 126. In
those    same    five years, call volume had seen more than a 13% increase.


Now that all the stations were retrofitted and/ or newly built, the costs continued. The eighteen fire
stations were occupied 24/ 7 and needed to be well-maintained to ensure that they lasted. Chief
Burchett turned the time over to UFA' s Chief Financial Officer Tony Hill.

Mr. Hill explained        that   the    UFSA    Board would         consider    a
                                                                                    proposal for $ 11. 4 million in new
revenue. If approved, this would be the fourth increase since its inception in 2008. The Board had
committed to keeping a fund balance of no less than 15%                        of total revenue which was in line with
what was recommended by the Government Finance Officers Association. Mr. Hill concluded his
comments by pointing out that there would be a public hearing regarding the proposed tax increase
on December         10, 2024 at 6pm at 3380 South 900 West.

          Community/Economic Development Director Wayne Harper explained that this building

approximately 1, 800 square foot building with a rear garage, a paved and striped rear parking lot,
and approximately 10, 000 square feet of undeveloped land on the rear, most western portion. The

location, but       property was zoned Community Commercial which prohibits that use. The
                     the
