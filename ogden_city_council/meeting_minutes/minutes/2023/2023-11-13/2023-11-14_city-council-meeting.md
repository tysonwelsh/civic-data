Proposed Ordinance 2023-61 amending the Ogden Municipal Code with

regard to Flood Hazard Regulations

A memo from the Public Services Department regarding proposed amendments to the City’s Flood Hazard Regulations

- came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated City Administration is proposing a series of updates to Chapter 26,
! Floodplain Overlay Zone, in order to keep Ogden City compliant with the requirements of the National Flood Insurance Program

(NFIP) administered by the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA). FEMA will be updating the flood maps for Weber

County after a recent flood hazard determination. The new maps will be effective November 30, 2023, and the changes to the

City’s ordinance must be completed by then for the City to remain compliant. The changes are based on requirements as set forth

in Title 44 of the Federal Code. The changes generally include minor wording and grammatical corrections, adoption of updated

Flood Insurance Rate Maps (FIRM) by reference, several new and updated definitions, the designation of the City Engineer as the

Floodplain Administrator, and updated procedures for amendments to FIRM maps. The City made similar updates to this section

of code in 2015.

Assistant City Engineer Nielsen summarized the memo and presented several maps to orient the Council to the old
floodplain delineations and the new delineations that have already been approved by FEMA; Ogden has been proactive in |
updating its maps as changes have occurred in the community.

Council member Nadolski inquired as to the data source for the adjustments to the maps. Mr. Nielsen stated that when
LIDAR data is accurate enough, it is used to adjust flood maps; when the LIDAR data is not reliable, a physical survey is
performed by the State of Utah to develop flooding models. Council member Nadolski stated that last spring during times of
increased flooding risk, City staff relied upon the data included in these maps and worked with neighborhoods to strategically
sandbag areas where flooding risks were higher. He stated this information was used proactively to protect the community and he
acknowledged staff for their knowledge and efforts to be efficient in mitigating flooding risks.

Chair Choberka introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2023-61, entitled:

“An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Ogden Municipal Code by amending Chapter 26 of Title 15
to revise provisions of the Floodplain Overlay Zone; and providing that this ordinance shall become effective
immediately upon posting after final passage.”

Proposed Ordinance 2023-63 amending Ogden Municipal Code Title 12
Chapter 12 to establish procedures and costs for negligent, false, and
nuisance fire alarms

A memo from the Fire Department regarding proposed fire alarm regulation amendments came before the Council for
consideration. The memo stated the Fire Department is proposing a new ordinance authorizing the Fire Chief or their designee to
issue notices, warnings, and citations to buildings owners and tenants where a false or nuisance alarm has been triggered.
Warnings would be given for the first and second offenses. Fines would be assessed for repeat offences.

Fire Chief Mathieu summarized the memo and stated the Fire Department frequently responds to false fire emergencies
where the activation of an alarm system is the result of mechanical failure, lack of proper maintenance, malfunction, improper
installation or other unknown causes not fire related; on one unique situation, the Fire Department has responded to the same
building 50 times over the past year. Dispatching fire apparatus and personnel to non-emergency calls burdens limited resources
and may limit the ability of the Department to respond to actual emergencies. The cost of a response to a false or nuisance alarm
can cost thousands of dollars and is not an appropriate or efficient use of taxpayer funds. The Ogden City Fire Department
currently has no authority to encourage alarm system repairs or replacement or fine building owners for repeated false alarms.
Chief Mathieu summarized the proposed ordinance amendments intended to help the Department curb nuisance alarms in the
community.

Discussion among the Council and Chief Mathieu centered on the hierarchy of alarm notifications to other parties before
the Fire Department is notified and the most prevalent causes for the nuisance alarm.

Proposed Ordinance 2023-65 establishing the Elected Officials
Compensation Commission ;
A memo from the Council staff regarding the creation of an Elected Officials Compensation Commission came before
the Council for consideration. Appointed members of the Commission will make recommendations regarding the annual
compensation--both salaries and stipends--for the City’s elected officials. The proposed ordinance addresses the commission
member appointment process, identifies preferred expertise of the appointees, establishes the terms for filing vacancies,
establishes rules under which the Commission will operate, and sets forth the commission’s powers and duties.

the creation of the Elected Officials Compensation Commission. The purpose of the Commission is to advise the Council on
compensation for elected officials. Commissioners must be at least 21 years of age, a resident of Ogden or have strong ties to
Ogden, and have professional experience in finance, human resources, corporate management, local government management, or
. a closely related field. Terms of service will be three years with a three-term limit. The Council may provide a per-diem for

Development Department; the Museum Foundation was devalued, and the museums disbanded, resulting in a loss of years of
expertise. This is one of the biggest losses in the City of Ogden and decisions were made by people with no experience in
museums and artifact preservation.

Caren Warner publicly thanked the City Council and Ogden City staff for their support and facilitating the clean-up of
the hoard around the exterior of her neighbor’s home; it took 75 volunteers several hours to get the cleanup done. This shows the
power of the community when working toward a conumon goal. For several years, the home has been packed with debris, junk,
and nonpermitted structures. She has tried to solve the problem with little success until she reached out to Chair Choberka who
facilitated a meeting with Code Services and the Ogden Community Action Network (CAN). She is relieved by the change; the
clean up has lifted her spirits and given her hope that things can and will improve. She is grateful for the partnership that Ogden
has made with My Hometown, an organization that has worked tirelessly to improve the property; their efforts will continue to
make the community better.

Steve Jones also referenced the Union Station; he listened to the conversation about the Centennial Celebration during
the work session tonight and he thanked City Administration for their efforts to commemorate the 100" birthday of Union Station.

The following individuals addressed the Council, thanking them for their action to remain in the Community Renewable
Energy Act (CREA) Program: David Timmerman, Heath Satow, Angel Castillo, Linda Carroll. Tiana Tonga stated she disagrees ,
with the decision to remain in the program, based upon the fact that the survey that was conducted regarding participationinthe | !
program only considered the input of 669 residents in the community. Linda Sanchez stated she does not believe an opt-out
program is appropriate and, for that reason, she is concerned about the City’s participation in the CREA program. She suggested
the City perform an audit to determine abuse of public funds. Laura Lewis stated she is concerned about the City participating in
the CREA Program because it is poorly defined; she does not think an opt-out program is good for residents.

Teresa Bramwell discussed the history of her property on Monroe Boulevard; she purchased it in 1997 and got a letter
from the Mayor in 2001 with an offer to buy the property. She indicated the property was not for sale but wondered why he
wanted to purchase it. The Mayor told him that another investor owned the rest of the block and was going to develop it ina way
that would be detrimental to her property value. She offered to sell it for $250,000, but the City got an appraisal and found the
value to be $200,000. The City did not purchase her property but did pursue other projects in the area surrounding her property.
She felt good about keeping the property because she believed these other projects would ultimately increase the value of her
property. She later learned of the Ogden on Board plan and it showed a high-rise development on her property; however, she has
never had a seat at the table to provide input on what she feels is the best way for her neighborhood to develop or how her
property should be used in the future.
