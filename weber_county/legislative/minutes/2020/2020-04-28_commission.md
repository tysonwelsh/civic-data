    1. RESOLUTION DECLARING THE COUNTY’S INTENT TO PICKUP A CERTAIN PERCENTAGE OF CERTAIN PUBLIC
       SAFETY EMPLOYEES’ CONTRIBUTIONS TO UTAH STATE RETIREMENT SYSTEM – RESOLUTION 11-2020
       Sarah Swan, County Administrative Services Director, stated that the 2020 State Legislature passed a
       requirement that public safety employees in the Tier II Hybrid Retirement System contribute 2.27% to
       their own retirement. The legislation allows participating employers to elect to pay all or part of the
       required employee member contribution on behalf of the employee. It also requires employers who make
       this election to make additional non-elective contributions to the defined contribution plans at 2.27%. The
       county agrees to pay 100% of these contributions effective 7/1/2020 in order to maintain a competitive
       edge in the recruiting market until superseded or revoked by the Commission or the law changes.
       Commissioner Jenkins said that the Legislature passed Tier II relief a couple of years ago, which he had
       objected to, and now the county pays more because of it. The other two commissioners concurred,
       preferring to have had a revenue stream to accompany the unfunded mandate.
       Commissioner Harvey moved to adopt Resolution 11-2020 declaring the county’s intent to pick up a
       certain percentage of certain Public Safety employees’ contributions to the Utah State Retirement System;
       Commissioner Jenkins seconded.
       Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Froerer – aye

    3. CONTRACT WITH STANLEY CONSULTANTS, INC., FOR CONSTRUCTION MANAGEMENT FOR THE NORTH

   4. CONTACT WITH DOBE CONSTRUCTION FOR INSTALLATION OF A WATERLINE FOR NEW ARCHERY PARK
      Todd Ferrario, County Ice Sheet and Parks & Recreation Division Director, stated that this contract went
      through the RFP process and the low bid was $86,500 for work that includes an 8” water line with a
      connection at 26th Street and F Avenue, one 1-inch culinary connection, and one 6-inch fire system
      connection on the project. They were allowed to use all native soils for bedding. He said that this item is
      a good value for the county, and Commissioner Jenkins stated that this is a very fair price. Commissioner
      Harvey is continually impressed with the great improvements that the team is doing in that area and the
      other commissioners concurred. Mr. Ferrario added that with all the work that county staff will do
      internally, there will be a savings of about $150,000. Additionally, the funds are coming from State grants
      so no county monies are being used at this time.
      Commissioner Harvey moved to approve the contract with Dobe Construction for the installation of a
      waterline for the new Archery Park; Commissioner Jenkins seconded.

    2. COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT BLOCK GRANT FIRST PUBLIC HEARING FOR CARES ACT FUNDING
       Chair Froerer gave the number that anyone interested in participating in the public hearing could call.
       Christy Dahlberg, Wasatch Front Regional Council (WFRC) Community Development Administrator,
       stated that the purpose of this public hearing was to provide citizens with pertinent information about funds
       allocated to the Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) program authorized by the Coronavirus
       Aid Relief and Economic Security Act and to allow for discussion of possible applications for the 2020
       funding cycle. These special funds are to be used to prevent, prepare for, to respond to the COVID-19
       pandemic in the Wasatch Front’s AOG region, and to mitigate its impacts. Weber County is eligible to
       apply under the Utah Department of Workforce Services and the WFRC is eligible to apply through Weber
       County. Grant money must be spent on projects benefiting primarily low-moderate income persons. The
       Wasatch Front region is expecting to receive approximately $499,000, with additional funding possible.
       Eligible activities must prevent and respond to the spread of infectious diseases and examples include, but
       are not limited to, constructing a facility for testing, diagnosing or treating, rental assistance and small
       business grants. The 2019-2020 State Annual Action Plans are under revision to add this funding and
       activities. All comments will be considered by the WFRC. Ms. Dahlberg invited anyone wishing to speak
       to state their name and where they were from. Chair Froerer noted that this would be administered by the
       WFRC and Ms. Dahlberg concurred stating that there would be a contract with the county for the portions
       of money that would be allocated through WFRC and if the WFRC partners with other agencies, the county
       would have contracts with those entities as well. If the county applies for these funds, a second public
       hearing will be held.
