 A.     General Plan Overview
Chief Administrative Officer Korban Lee provided an overview of the General Plan in anticipation of
the Council beginning the process of updating the General Plan. Mr. Lee explained that the Future
Land Use Map, the Water Master Plan, the Sanitary Sewer Master Plan, the Stormwater Master Plan,
the Transportation Master Plan, and the Parks Master Plan all feed into the City’s General Plan. Many
other sub-plans feed into and are influenced by the General Plan. Mr. Lee emphasized that the General
Plan provided general direction, vision, goals, and policies for the City without getting into the details
addressed in the various sub-plans. He stressed that an update to the General Plan would need to
remain at a high level without getting into “the weeds”. Mr. Lee commented that the General Plan and
the various sub-plans represented direction from the Council to be following and carried out by
Administrative Staff.

Council Office Director Alan R. Anderson provided details regarding USC 10-9a-401 and West Jordan
City Code 13-1-6 General Plan requirements. The West Jordan General Plan was last updated and
adopted in 2012. Mr. Anderson shared results of a survey of Council and Staff regarding priorities for
the General Plan update. Economic Development was indicated as a high priority by both Staff and the
Council. Revisiting the Future Land Use Map was also designated a high priority. Mr. Anderson
reported that the Council Committee for the General Plan had met and reviewed applications received
from community members to participate on the General Plan Committee. Council Member Green
City Council Work Session APPROVED Minutes – September 9, 2020                                 Page 2

commented that the citizens on the General Plan Committee would participate in the same survey
completed by the Council and Staff.

Mr. Anderson emphasized that the General Plan was a document approved by the Council to provide
direction, and added that the direction should be flexible enough to handle changing conditions. He
commented that the market had changed a lot since 2012. He suggested the length of the General Plan
could be reduced by referencing various Master Plans and sub-plans. Mr. Lee suggested the Council
should keep in mind that a General Plan update would drive changes to the Future Land Use Map and
the many sub-plans. The purpose of an update would be to plan and set the expectations for one or two
generations ahead.

Council Member Pack, who participated in the previous General Plan update process, said the process
took almost three years. He said the General Plan Committee met monthly, with additional sub-
committee meetings. Council Member Pack commented that feedback from the community was
helpful during the previous update process and helped the Committee arrive at the best decisions
possible.

Mr. Anderson spoke of changes in the City since adoption of the 2012 General Plan, and spoke of
changes yet to come. He mentioned the need to consider historic preservation, land use, sanitary sewer
demands with each new development, and many other dynamic considerations.

Responding to a question from Council Member Jacob, Community Development Director Scott
Langford said the previous General Plan Committee used the 2003 General Plan as a starting point.
Council Member Pack added that they had also looked at what other cities had adopted. Mr. Lee
requested Staff representation from all City departments on the General Plan Committee because of the
interconnectedness of the General Plan with the many Master Plans. Council Member Pack agreed and
added that representation from citizens in all four districts would be important. Mr. Lee added that
input from large stakeholders such as the School District and UDOT would be critical as well. Chair
McConnehey explained how to view the General Plan on the City website.

       b. General Plan Moderate Income Housing Element (continued from August 26th)
City Planner Larry Gardner explained an amendment to the Moderate-Income Housing element of the
General Plan. He provided statistics regarding income and housing in West Jordan, and commented that
West Jordan was continuing to grow at a good pace. Mr. Gardner spoke of housing needs along the
Wasatch Front.

Mr. Gardner reviewed the goals and policies in the Moderate-Income Housing Plan with the Council.
Council Member Whitelock said she did not believe a majority of the Council supported Goal 5 regarding
reduced parking requirements for residential development in transit station overlay districts. Mr. Gardner
explained that Goal 5 was required by State statute, and pointed out a clarifying statement included in
the document prior to the two goals that Goals 5 and 6 were required by Utah Code. Council Member
Whitelock suggested noting the statement with an asterisk on both goals, with the clarifying statement
placed after the two goals. Council Member Lamb said he did not believe that reducing parking
requirements motivated citizens to use public transportation. Mr. Gardner confirmed that on-street
parking was required to be provided in transit station overlay districts by City Code. Council Member
Lamb stated he was against requiring on-street parking.

Council Member Green said he would be fine with Goal 6 and most of Goal 5 but would not be fine with
reducing the required number of parking spaces to one per multi-family unit. He suggested changing the
language to “examine the possibility of reducing parking requirements in transit overlay districts”.

Council Member Pack asked if data was available showing that affordable housing located near
workplaces with public transportation available would reduce the need for parking spaces. Mr. Gardner
responded there were volumes of study data available with that conclusion. Mayor Burton commented
that the on-street parking requirement was added to help cover the need for parking not met by parking
lots. Mr. Gardner agreed that the requirement was implemented around 20 years ago. Mr. Langford
commented that on-street parking tended to slow traffic, contributing to a more walkable environment.

Chair McConnehey opened the public hearing at 7:56 p.m.

Citizen Comments:
City Council APPROVED Minutes – September 9, 2020                                                 Page 4

None

Chair McConnehey closed the public hearing at 7:56 p.m.

Council Member Green said he agreed with the idea that on-street parking was supposed to create a more
pedestrian friendly environment. However, he pointed out that transit oriented development included a
commercial element, which did not contribute to a pedestrian friendly environment, and did not reduce
the need for residents to have vehicles and parking spaces. Council Member Jacob said he did not believe
West Jordan had sufficient public transit options available to eliminate the need for vehicles and places
to park them.

Chair McConnehey said he was not concerned about Goal 6 with the cap and grade requirements in
place, but expressed concern with Goal 5. Mr. Gardner said he could change the goal as directed, and
added that it would not need to be implemented any time soon. Chair McConnehey said he would be
uncomfortable with a goal that the City did not intend to implement, and said he would be interested in
a comparison with other cities.

Chair McConnehey suggested requesting that Staff change Goal 5 to retain current parking limits and
evaluate their effectiveness.
