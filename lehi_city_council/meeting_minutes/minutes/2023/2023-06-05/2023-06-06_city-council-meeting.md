2. Discussion Items
   2.a. Discussion of facilities at Dry Creek Reservoir
   Cameron Boyle, Assistant City Administrator, presented on the history of the Dry Creek
   Reservoir and proposed options for the Council to consider.

   There was discussion around different options for developing the recreational area around a Dry
   Creek Reservoir. Option one at full buildout would require the pipeline installation in order to
   store water, paved parking, restrooms, food services, rentals, beach, grass area and piers. The
   estimated cost for this option is $10.1 million, plus the pipeline construction at $10-12 million,
   plus ongoing expenses.

   Option two is similar to the first, but without a road going through. It would consist of a parking
   lot, foot access to the lake, and a trail around the reservoir. Residents can swim, paddleboard,
   and fish with their own equipment. The estimated cost for this option is around $2 million,
   including a restroom facility, plus ongoing expenses.

   lake, with no fishing and no restrooms. The estimated cost for this option is around $200,000,
   plus additional costs for pit restroom facilities.

   The discussion also touches on the need for a pipeline for water supply, with the understanding
   that it could be added later if necessary. The staffing and maintenance costs for each option
   were discussed, with the acknowledgment that additional employees may be needed to manage
   the reservoir along with other park responsibilities.

   During a discussion about different options for a project involving water access and parks
   planning, several considerations were brought up. One consideration was the need to ensure
   water and emergency access, including concerns about safety in the water and the possibility of
   a retaining wall to prevent sand from washing away. Another consideration was the study of
   water use and loss, including evaporation and seepage, as well as the need for clarity on
   irrigation company usage and potential agreements.

   The discussion also touched on the potential drawbacks of certain options, such as the lack of
   beachfront and the need for water circulation and oxygenation. The pipeline option was
   mentioned as a way to maintain water levels, but it was acknowledged that it wouldn't guarantee
   pristine recreation. Before decisions are made to proceed with any park amenities, concerns
   about balancing costs and water availability need to be addressed.

   2.b Discussion of funding for sidewalks
   Dean Lundell, Finance Director, presented on the issue of downtown sidewalks, acknowledging
   that it had been a long-standing problem. The challenge was the high cost involved in
   constructing sidewalks, especially in older parts of town where they were not initially required.
   Some downtown sidewalk projects have been completed using federal Community
   Development Block Grant (CDBG) funds, specifically allocated for certain types of projects in
   qualifying areas. However, the available funding is limited, with only two to three hundred
   thousand dollars granted annually.

   Mr. Lundell presented some cost estimates, stating that installing sidewalks, curbs, gutters, and
   asphalt for the entire downtown area would exceed 16 million dollars. The current funding of
   two to three hundred thousand dollars per year from CDBG would take an extensive amount of
   time to cover the expenses. He explained that the amount of CDBG funding received was
   determined by a formula based on the city's population and economic conditions, and it might
   increase slightly as the population grew.

   Mr. Lundell proposed the idea of using special assessment areas, also known as special
   improvement districts, where the city would make the necessary improvements benefiting
   individual property owners. The property owners would then repay the city over time through
   monthly payments. He mentioned the need for public hearings and a protest period, during
   which property owners could voice their objections to the proposed improvements.
