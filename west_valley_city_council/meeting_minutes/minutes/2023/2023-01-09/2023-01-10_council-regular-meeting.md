A.   ACCEPT PUBLIC INPUT REGARDING APPLICATION ZT-6-2022,
     FILED BY WEST VALLEY CITY, REQUESTING TO ADD
     RESTRICTIONS ON THE USE OF LAWN IN LANDSCAPING TO
     CHAPTER 7-16 OF THE ZONING ORDINANCE
     Mayor Lang informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular Council
     Meeting scheduled January 10, 2023, in order for the City Council to hear and
     consider public comments regarding application ZT-6-2022, Filed by West Valley
     CIty, Requesting to Add Restrictions on the Use of Lawn in Landscaping to Chapter
     7-16 of the Zoning Ordinance

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

              During the 2022 legislative session, the State legislature appropriated $5
              million for a statewide grass removal rebate program as part of HB 121.
              Applicants may receive up to $1 per square foot for replacing lawn with
              drought-resistant landscaping. This program will be launched in the spring
              of 2023 through the website UtahWaterSavers.com. According to the
MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING JANUARY 10, 2023

         Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District, this new program can be
         combined with existing lawn replacement programs so that applicants can
         receive up to $2 per square foot for replacing lawn.

         To be eligible for the program, a project must be located within a county or
         municipality with the following lawn limitations on new residential
         development:
              No lawn on parking strips or areas less than eight feet in width in
                 new residential development.
              No more than 35% of the front and side yard landscaped area in new
                 residential development is lawn.
         The City Council directed staff to prepare an ordinance revision to adopt
         these standards so that property owners within the City will be eligible for
         the new lawn replacement funds. While the City already has a regulation
         prohibiting lawn in parking strips and areas less than eight feet in width,
         this standard only applies to developer installed landscaping.

  Mayor Lang opened the Public Hearing. There being no one to speak in favor or
  opposition, Mayor Lang closed the Public Hearing.

B.     ACCEPT PUBLIC INPUT REGARDING APPLICATION ZT-7-2022,
       FILED BY CABCO 5600 LLC, REQUESTING AN AMENDMENT TO
       SECTION 7-14-404 OF THE ZONING ORDINANCE TO CHANGE THE
       LOCATION OF RESTRICTIONS ON AUTOMOTIVE SALES,
       AUTOMOBILE SERVICE, AND AUTOMOTIVE PARTS SALES FROM
       BETWEEN 2455 SOUTH AND 3100 SOUTH TO BETWEEN THE RITER
       CANAL AND 3100 SOUTH
       Mayor Lang informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular Council
       Meeting scheduled January 10, 2023, in order for the City Council to hear and
       consider public comments regarding application ZT-7-2022, Filed by Cabco 5600
       LLC, Requesting an Amendment to Section 7-14-404 of the Zoning Ordinance to
       Change the Location of Restrictions on Automotive Sales, Automobile Service, and
       Automotive Parts Sales from Between 2455 South and 3100 South to Between the Riter
       Canal and 3100 South.

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

              Mr. Bob Furstenau with Cabco 5600 LLC owns the property directly north
              of the Riter Canal on the west side of 5600 West. His property is currently
              impacted by the 5600 West Overlay Zone restriction on automotive uses
              and he would like the option of pursuing an automotive use.

              Mr. Furstenau provided the following justification for the proposed change:

                     “The Riter Canal is a natural boundary between the retail section
                     south of the canal versus the industrial/manufacturing uses north of
                     the     canal.     There    are     approximately     87      existing
                     retail/restaurant/service businesses south of the Riter Canal that
                     meet the requirements of the retail overlay zone. There are 5 existing
                     businesses north of the canal none of which are retail.”

              In support of his justification, Mr. Furstenau provided a list of businesses
              located north of the Riter Canal and businesses located south of the Riter
              Canal. He also provided a map of the area that shows the location of many
              of the businesses on the list. The list and map are included with this issue
              paper.

ORDINANCE NO. 23-03, ADOPT THE REVISED 2022 MODERATE INCOME
HOUSING PLAN AS PART OF THE WEST VALLEY CITY GENERAL PLAN
Nicole Cottle, Acting City Manager, discussed proposed Ordinance 23-03 that would adopt
the Revised 2022 Moderate Income Housing Plan as Part of the West Valley City General Plan.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       During the 2022 general session, the Utah legislature passed HB 462 that
       modified requirements for municipalities’ moderate income housing plans as well
       as reporting. In response, the City adopted an updated version of our moderate
       income housing plan on September 20, 2022 through Ordinance 22-36. However,
   MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING JANUARY 10, 2023

       after staff submitted the adopted plan to the Housing and Community
       Development Division of the Utah Department of Workforce Services, the City
       was informed, along with other cities, that the adopted plan did not fulfill State
       code requirements in that specific benchmarks, measures, and timelines were not
       described for the City’s chosen housing strategies.

       The City was given until February 16, 2023 to cure these deficiencies. The
       proposed revisions address these deficiencies and staff has reviewed the revisions
       with the State and confirmed that they will bring our plan into compliance.

RESOLUTION 23-01: AUTHORIZE THE CITY TO ENTER INTO AN
INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT WITH SALT LAKE CITY TO TRANSFER
OWNERSHIP OF A TACTICAL VEHICLE TO WEST VALLEY CITY
Nicole Cottle, Assistant City Manager, presented proposed Resolution 23-01 that would
authorize the City to Enter Into an Interlocal Agreement with Salt Lake City to Transfer
Ownership of a Tactical Vehicle to West Valley City.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:
   MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING JANUARY 10, 2023

       Both SLC and WVC were participants in the Urban Areas Security Initiative grant
       program (“UASI”) for the Wasatch Front urban area as established by the United
       States Department of Homeland Security. As part of the UASI, grant funds were
       allocated to Salt Lake City to construct and maintain several MITS Vehicle’s for
       use in emergencies and other operations throughout the Wasatch Front urban area
       and the State of Utah.

       The vehicles were titled and held in the name of SLC as it was the entity primarily
       responsible for their creation, staffing, and maintenance. However, the grant funds
       were allocated for the benefit of all participants in the UASI and WVC has
       participated in the MITS Vehicle program. The grant funding has since been
       terminated. As a result, individual members of the UASI will now become
       responsible for the maintenance of the assets acquired with grant funds.

Mayor Lang verified that the vehicle is not a trailer. Nicole replied that is correct.
