2.         25-320                 Public Works Department presenting a Solutions Development Study for
                                  SR 209
                   Attachments:   SR-209 SDS Final Report Presentation

                                  Britney Ward, Public Works, presented the Solutions Development Study (SDS) on
                                  SR-209 (9400 S) from 1300 E to Wasatch Blvd. She summarized the purpose and need
                                  for the study. The study identified corridor issues/needs, gaps in the Little Cottonwood
                                  Canyon (LCC) EIS and reviewed winter/summer travel operations, traffic flow and existing
                                  conditions. The study also included a review of the active transportation needs. 76% of
                                  the traffic accidents along 9400 S occur between 1300 E and 2300 E.

                                  Note: There was an issue with the Zoom webinar. Residents notified the Council staff that
                                  they were unable to access the zoom webinar. We sent out a post on social media to
                                  communicate to the residents of the technical difficulties and suggested accessing the
                                  meeting via the meeting's call in number.

                                  Council Member Marci Houseman joined the meeting at 6:07 pm.

                                  The Solutions Development Study is an innovative planning process developed by UDOT
                                  to capture an area's unique context and provide need driven solutions. The SDS included
                                  stakeholders, incorporated past studies and identified adopted land use. She reviewed
                                  the project timeline, the public engagement, public survey, and the suggestions and
                                  concerns generated at the public open house. Key takeaways included improved
                                  pedestrian and bicycle safety, enhanced public transit options, addressing traffic
                                  congestion, and improved road infrastructure. Council questions occurred throughout the
                                  presentation. Ms. Ward reviewed the study's recommended solutions along the 9400 S
                                  corridor to 1300 E, 1700E, Tramway Drive, Little Cottonwood Center, Highland Drive,
                                  Raintree Drive, Eastdell and continued intersections to Wasatch Blvd. Many of the
                                  recommendations included adding a pedestrian over/underpass (by 1300 E), adjustments
                                  to signal timing, adding advanced warning signals, raised medians, enhanced bike lanes
                                  and roundabouts. Ms. Ward reviewed several concept plans with the Council. She also
                                  reviewed future studies planned and interim projects. Council questions and comments
                                  continued. Following the presentation, Council moved to General Citizen Comment.

Council Member Business

                    Council Member Cyndi Sharkey attended the Wasatch Choice Vision meeting. It was a
                    great opportunity to talk to staff at UDOT and the WFRC and learn about what is planned
                    and coming. She attended the opening of the Shamrock Foods Warehouse opening.
                    Shamrock Foods has been a family business for 100 years. As part of their opening, they
                    donated $5,000 to the food bank. She provided an update from the Legislative Policy
                    Committee. Topics discussed include property taxes, motor fuel exemptions,
                    homelessness and winter alternatives, and possible impact on the mitigation funding.
                    There was also discussion on issues related to political signage, including the location
                    and time frame the signs would be permitted.

                    Council Member Marco Houseman thanked all staff involved with the coordination of the
                    housing workshops. She has received positive feedback from residents. She also thanked
                    Park's staff for addressing residents' concerns.

Mayor's Report

                    Mayor Monica Zoltanski: Everyone at the City was deeply troubled by the shooting that
                    occurred at Main Street Park. We all value Historic Sandy. We have made great strides
                    in the area and will continue our efforts in Historic Sandy to ensure the community feels
                    safe.

                    Chief Severson spoke. He was looking forward to working with the residents in the
                    Historic Sandy and surrounding areas. We have made great improvements and have
                    increased our patrols in the area. We are committed to ensuring the safety for residents
                    in Historic Sandy. Our next Coffee with a Cop is on November 15th from 9 am to 11 am at
                    Oh LaLa Coffee shop. We will hold another one in January. Our community and residents
                    are our eyes and ears and he stressed the importance of community involvement.

                    Mayor Zoltanski congratulated Margaret Turner on celebrating her 100th birthday. She
                    toured the MVP Shelter with state representatives and board members and spoke about
                    the importance of continued funding through the state's mitigation funds. She announced
                    that the City had received $3 million in funding from the Salt Lake County Tourism,
                    Recreation, Culture and Convention (TRCC) Board for the new Sandy Community &
                    Recreation Center. As part of the funding requirements, the city's community center
                    would participate in the County's program and extend a free membership to all residents
                    under 18 years of age. November 7th is the groundbreaking event for the new Sandy
                    Community & Recreation Center. The community is welcome to attend the
                    groundbreaking.

CAO Report

                    Martin Jensen, Deputy CAO had no report.
