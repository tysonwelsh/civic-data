A.   ACCEPT PUBLIC INPUT REGARDING APPLICATION ZT-9-2020,
     FILED BY RANDY MOORE, REQUESTING A ZONE TEXT CHANGE TO
     ALLOW PROPERTIES WITH ACCESS AND FRONTAGE ON A STREET
     WITH A PLANNED RIGHT-OF-WAY WIDTH OF AT LEAST 80 FEET
     WHERE AN EXEMPT HOUSING DEVELOPMENT IS PROPOSED TO
     APPLY FOR THE RM (MULTIPLE UNIT DWELLING RESIDENTIAL)
     ZONE
     Mayor Bigelow informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular
     Council Meeting scheduled January 12, 2021, in order for the City Council to hear
     and consider public comments regarding Application ZT-9-2020, filed by Randy
     Moore, requesting a Zone Text Change to allow properties with access and frontage on
     a street with a planned Right-of-Way width of at least 80 feet where an exempt housing
     development is proposed to apply for the RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling Residential)
     Zone.

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

               Randy Moore’s original application defined qualified 55 or older
               development and allowed properties with access and frontage on a street
               with a planned right-of-way width of at least 80 feet where a qualified 55 or
               older development in which the density must not exceed 8 units per acre is
               proposed to apply for the RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling Residential) zone.
MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING JANUARY 12, 2021
                       -3-

         The Planning Commission recommended approval subject to the density
         being capped at 7 units per acre.

         After reviewing Mr. Moore’s proposal, the City’s Legal Department
         modified the language to not include the federally defined term “qualified
         55 or older development” which could change. Despite the revision, the end
         result is the same because qualified 55 or older developments are the only
         form of exempt housing.

         Included with this issue paper is a map that shows remaining vacant land
         within the City as well as roads designated on the Major Street Plan as an
         80’ right-of-way and roads designated as 100’ right-of-way or more
         excluding nonaccess highways and freeways like I-215 and Bangerter
         Highway. The map also highlights the one-half mile areas within eligible
         light rail stations. The purpose of this map is to illustrate where rezone
         requests to the RM zone are currently allowed and where they would be
         allowed if this application is approved.

         Also included in this report is a graph showing the population of the City
         by age. As of 2018, 17% of the City’s population was aged 55 or older. Over
         the next 10 years, just over 15,000 more residents will fall into the 55 or
         older cohort.

  Mayor Bigelow opened the Public Hearing.

  Randy Moore thanked the Council for the opportunity to discuss this item. He stated
  that this process began over a year ago. He indicated that there were two parcels of
  land on an 80 foot right of way that he wanted to develop but was informed that he
  could not based on changes to the Ordinance. Mr. Moore stated that he doesn’t
  understand the nature of the ordinance or the reasoning behind it. He expressed
  concern that he can not even apply for an RM project for the Council to consider.
  Mr. Moore stated that townhomes and 55 and up developments are in demand. He
  stated that many people want to stay in West Valley City but don’t have a lot of
  options. Mr. Moore stated that most often younger families do not move into the
  55 and up communities. He explained the requirements for 55 and up communities.
  Mr. Moore explained the process he went through with the Planning Commission.
  He indicated that they were in favor of the change and he provided comments from
  those meetings. Mr. Moore shared the options he determined were best for the
  proposed ordinance change.

  Jim Vesock suggested that the Council study the item further and delay the vote
  until they can be fully educated.

  Mayor Bigelow informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular
  Council Meeting scheduled January 12, 2021, in order for the City Council to hear
  and consider public comments regarding Application Z-9-2020, Filed by Michelle
  Krogius and Brett Garrett, Requesting a Zone Change for Property Located at 2454
  South 5600 West from A (Agriculture, Minimum Lot Size 1/2 Acre) to M
  (Manufacturing)

  Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
  information as follows:

         The intended use is a distribution warehouse on the two subject parcels
         along with an 18.5-acre parcel to the east that is already zoned M. The
         proposed building would be similar to the buildings in the Mountain View
         Industrial Park and ARA Industrial Park to the west. The total building area
         is 575,000 square feet. Given the size of the proposal (over 10 acres), a
         conditional use permit would be required for the use approval assuming the
         zone change is approved.

         Next year, the City is planning to extend 2540 S to the east and provide a
         road connection between 2400 South and Parkway Blvd as shown in the
         attached exhibit. To facilitate this construction, staff recommends a
         development agreement requiring the dedication of right-of-way for the
         construction of these roads. The City’s Public Works Department is also
         requesting that the development agreement include a provision that requires
         the developer to reimburse the City for utilities installed in the roads that
         will service the proposed development.

  Mayor Bigelow opened the Public Hearing.

  Scott Godfrey indicated that he is favor of the application.

  Mayor Bigelow closed the Public Hearing.
