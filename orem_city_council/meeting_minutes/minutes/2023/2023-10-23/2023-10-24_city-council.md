### Proposed Ordinance 13-1-11 Amending Park Lighting and Closure:
*Presenter: D. Jacob Summers, Deputy City Attorney*

Mr. Summers explained that the new amendment would allow or extend park hours under certain circumstances. The reason for the amendment is to attract and secure regional, national and international recreation. The way this can be done is by a creation of a permit process that would be managed by the recreation department. The current language of 13-1-11 says "All City parks shall be closed between the hours of 11:00 pm and 5:00 am. It shall be unlawful for any person other than City employees performing their duties to be in a park when it is closed. Exception for Oremfest Celebration." The proposed amendment would include the following parks: City Center Park, Community Park, Hillcrest Park, Lakeside Park and Windsor Park. The proposed amendment and permit process will include first, submission of application and pay fees, second, ordinance provides for denial of permit for inference with city-approved events or if the undue disturbance to the neighborhood. Third, the ordinance provides conditions, fourth, it requires City staff to be on-site for beyond normal operating hours. And fifth, permits may be revoked if conditions are violated.

Mr. Peterson suggested keeping Hillcrest Park into the late night hours since there are no close neighbors. Mr. Macdonald is worried about the impact on neighborhoods, especially Windsor Park. Mr. Merritt explained that these late hours would be for limited weather exceptions and the goal would be to have rentals completed prior to 11:00 pm.

### Proposed City Boundary Adjustments:
*Presenter: Jason, Bench, Assistant Development Services Director and Gary McGinn, Assistant City Attorney*

Mr. Bench is discussing three boundary issues that need to be cleaned up. Mr. McGinn explained that state law allows two communities that have joining boundaries to adjust their boundaries so that the jurisdictional limits shrink or grow depending on the ultimate resolution. There is a potential for three boundary adjustments with Provo City. Two are initiated by staff and one is applicant initiated. In State Code it requires a council to pass a resolution indicating an attempt to adjust the municipal boundary. Once the resolution is passed and not less than 60 days, a public hearing will need to be held on whether boundaries should be adjusted. The passing of the resolution is not adjusting boundaries of the city, it is merely indicating an intent that a boundary might be changed. The public hearing will allow for discussion on the topic with the residents. A week ago, Provo City passed a resolution for the boundary change process. The goal would be to pass this resolution on November 14, 2023.

Mr. Bench continued to show the proposed boundary changes. The first is on 2000 South from Columbia Lane to approximately 400 East. This road is currently part of Provo City but Orem maintains and plows the road. Part of the area is owned by Orem but is in the Provo boundary. Provo has agreed to shift the boundary to the South curbline.

Mr. Bench showed the second proposed boundary change that is located at app 1030 East and 1700 South. The new boundary would follow the property lines of current properties. This will put one property in Provo city limit instead of having one property split between two cities. This will not benefit or hurt Orem; it would just clean up the boundary lines. These are the two staff initiated boundary changes.

Mr. Bench presented the third proposed boundary change that an applicant initiated. This boundary change is located near the mouth of the canyon, approximately 1550 East and 700 North. The applicant is wanting to change the boundary to enable them to build residential units in Provo. The property in Orem they intend to build out commercially. The Mayor and other Councilmembers are concerned about the commercial side of this project. They would like to see a timeline or commitment to finish the commercial side and not just the residential. Mr. McGinn discussed the possibility of a development agreement.

The applicant and developer, Mr. Jamie Chapman and Jim Ivory were present and spoke to the group. They explained that 90% of the residential area is Provo with only 10% in Orem. They believe that splitting this development is problematic for a few reasons like school districts, deliveries and address confusion. The main reason is the ease to the water main. Provo also has deeper sewer lines and that will allow them to gravity flow into Provo instead of pumping into Orem. There are 3 phases: phase 1 residential, phase 2 hospitality (boutique hotel or short-term rentals) and phase 3 Restaurant Village. The city council had questions about the timeline, the funding, and possible development agreements. Mr. McGinn explained the resolution approval is the next step. He also reviewed the concerns of the project.
