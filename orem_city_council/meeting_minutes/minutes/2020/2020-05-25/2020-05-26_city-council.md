43   accepted in curbside recycling bins. Other items were commonly confused for recyclable items
44   but were in fact waste, like pizza boxes and Styrofoam. Mr. Mertz said education would go a long
45   way to reducing or eliminating issues of contamination.

48   willing to pay for recycling services. Nearly 80% of respondents supported recycling outright, and

39          DISCUSSION – City Retention Schedule
40
41   Mrs. Bates said in the last several years the State Archives had been reviewing, changing, updating,
42   and grouping agency specific records into one schedule. This forced municipalities who had not
43   adopted their own retention schedule to use the State’s voluminous retention schedule, which had
44   combined records for entities like municipalities, special service districts, school districts,
45   counties, and more. Mrs. Bates said this was a good idea in theory, but the realities of using a “one
46   size fits all” retention schedule were constant changes and confusion. She shared examples from
47   the State’s retention schedule and the discrepancies in record title and length of retention.
48

11            RESOLUTION – Declaring the City of Orem’s intent to purchase the Hillcrest Elementary
12            School property from Alpine School District
13
14   Mr. Davidson presented the city’s recommendation to declare the intent to purchase the Hillcrest
15   school property. Utah Code Section 53G-4-902 gives cities the right to purchase surplus school
16   district real property within their jurisdictions. In a letter dated March 11, 2020, Alpine School
17   District declared the former Hillcrest Elementary School property to be surplus and asked the City
18   to express its intent regarding the potential purchase of the property. If the City desired to purchase
19   surplus school district property, Utah Code Section 53G-4-902 required the City adopt a resolution
20   declaring its intent to purchase the property. The proposed resolution declared that intent.
21
22   Alpine School District received an appraisal estimating the value of the Hillcrest Elementary
23   School property at $6 million as is, or $8.5 million if rezoned commercial. Utah Code Section
24   53G-4-901(2) provided that the purchase price for a city’s purchase of school district surplus
25   property shall be the average of the School District’s appraisal and the City’s appraisal, based on
26   the predominant zone in the surrounding area. If the City proceeded with the purchase of the
27   property, the next step would be to deliver an earnest money offer to the school district within
28   ninety (90) days of the date the school district received the resolution declaring the City's intent to
29   purchase the property. Once purchased, the City may only use the property for the following
30   purposes: (a) a county, city, or town hall; (b) a park or other open space; (c) a cultural center or
31   community center; (d) a facility for the promotion, creation, or retention of public or private jobs
32   within the state through planning, design, development, construction, rehabilitation, business
33   relocation, or any combination of these, within a county, city, or town; (e) office, industrial,
34   manufacturing, warehousing, distribution, parking, or other public or private facilities, or other
35   improvements that benefit the state or a county, city, or town; or (f) a facility for a charter school.
36
37   If the City decided to resell the property, it must first offer it to the school district at the price paid
38   by the City, plus the cost of existing improvements that the City made to the property after
39   purchase. If the school district did not repurchase the property, the City must equally share with
40   the school district any sale proceeds that exceeded the amount of the City's original purchase price
41   plus the cost of existing improvements that the City made to the property after purchase.
42

46   section of the property for a park and the City had operated the Hillcrest park. He said the City had

28
29   Mr. Davidson said last week the City transitioned to the yellow COVID-19 risk category, and was
30   in the process of reopening and adjusting the availability of city facilities. The SCERA pool was
31   opened on Memorial Day, the library was operating on modified hours, and city employees were
32   returning to work but still had the opportunity to work remotely. He said they anticipated this
33   yellow stage staying in place until further instruction came from the Governor’s office.
34
35   Mr. Davidson provided information regarding Utah County’s reimbursement approach to COVID-
36   19 funding from the federal government. He said money was given to the State and divided among
37   the counties. There was not clear criteria established for the reimbursement, which put the city in
38   a difficult position as the County “held all the cards”, or relief funding dollars. Thus far no money
39   had come from Utah County for small business relief, and all of the mayors in Utah County had
