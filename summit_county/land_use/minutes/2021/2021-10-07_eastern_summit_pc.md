1.  Discussion on The Corridor Master Plan along the Union Pacific Trail. – Madlyn 
McDonough, County Planner. 
 
Planner McDonough shared the background for this discussion. The Corridor is the 
working name for a thirty-mile arts, culture, recreation, and tourism corridor running 
along the Union Pacific Rail Trail. It has been a long-term goal of many (County Council, and 
the Community Development Department) to foster a stronger connection between 
Snyderville Basin and Eastern Summit County to create opportunities for local economic 
development and to highlight the unique history and culture of this area that extends far 
beyond mining. The inspiration for this project comes from the idea of restlessness that, 
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission 
October 7, 2021 
Page 2 of 13 
 
when channeled can lead to discovery and rebirth. The trail serves as an historic 
connection between our communities in our county. So why not use this existing 
connection to make progress toward our long-term goals? The project will be viewed 
through the lenses of art and culture, economic development, and recreation and tourism. 
There are specific challenges that are intended to be addressed through this project. An 
inventory of existing conditions has been collected. 
Smaller towns like Wanship and Hoytsville have been without economic development 
opportunities for decades. Revitalization of the Rail Trail corridor will provide 
opportunities for local businesses to flourish once again, as both locals and tourists are able 
to enjoy the amenities. Community involvement and advanced land use planning will 
ensure that environmentally sensitive areas can be protected, and areas of development 
can be centered around sufficient infrastructure. A current lack of amenities near the trail 
limits usability, while a lack of informative materials limits engagement in the trail’s 
historical and cultural value. 
The project is currently in the ideation and diagnosis stage. The Planning Department is 
currently working with a group of Park City High School students to build community 
engagement and plan to work with both North Summit and South Summit High School 
students on agricultural preservation and agri-tourism. 
The Rail Trail connects two State Parks and numerous trail heads. This project is an 
opportunity to proactively plan how there will be growth. The community will be highly 
involved in planning and there will be opportunities to collaborate. There are some existing

4.  Public hearing and possible recommendation to the County Council for an 
amendment to the Eastern Summit County Development Code to create a Recreation 
Commercial (RC) zoning district and regulations creating an agritourism process. – 
Ray Milliner, Principal Planner. 
 
Planner Milliner shared the background for this application. The RC Zoning district has 
been reviewed several times in the past. The RC zone would be used to foster development 
and enhance the recreational component of the area. The RC zone would require at least 
ten acres, and the applicant would have to prove the ability to provide water and 
wastewater. No specific density is included in the Code, and would be determined by the 
ability of the applicant to meet the standards. The RC Zone process would be part of the 
MPD process with a Development Agreement. There have been some uses added to the Use 
Chart. Commissioner Clyde suggested taking offices out of the Use Chart. 
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission 
October 7, 2021 
Page 11 of 13 
 
Planner Milliner stated that agri-tourism would give agricultural operations the ability to 
enhance their financial situation. The process would include a Conditional Use Permit with 
a specific use from the Use Chart listed in the application. Agri-tourism could be a good 
benefit to farmers and ranchers in Summit County and would promote the goals of 
preserving the rural character of Eastern Summit County. Agri-tourism adjacent to urban 
areas has been very successful. 
Commissioner Wheaton suggested that a ten-acre pumpkin patch or corn maze could 
justify having a restaurant for the convenience of the guests, but stated the language is not 
specific enough. Planner Milliner stated that an applicant can ask for more than one use, 
but the application must meet the standards. Any use must be specific and incidental to 
agricultural use. A gas station would not be allowed under agri-tourism. 
Commissioner Clyde stated that a twelve-foot side setback does not seem adequate, and a 
side setback should be at least fifty percent of the height of the building. It is unclear how to 
distinguish a special event from agri-tourism. Some uses need to be allowed under a special 
event permit. “Ice cream and cheese” should be changed to “dairy products.” There needs 
to be a better definition for hotel density.
