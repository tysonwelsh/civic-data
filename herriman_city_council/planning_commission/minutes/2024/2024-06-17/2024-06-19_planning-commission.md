1. Commission Business

   1.1. Review of City Council Decisions – Michael Maloy, Planning Director
Planning Director Maloy announced Council adopted the budget, detailing there are always
periodic amendments throughout the year. There was a presentation by the Olympia
development partners to explore the creation of a housing incentive zones. This is a new

tax incentive zone from the State called First Home Investment Zones (FHIZ). They are
looking into adding a FHIZ to the densest part of their development. It would align with the
Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) if it passes. It’s all still exploratory but the council wanted to hear
more about it. One question that wasn’t answered was if Olympia was asking for more
units or just reallocating units. That type of development would include attached housing,
condos, apartments, and townhomes. Commissioner Oberg probed does that type of
housing include low-income housing? Planning Director Maloy responded not necessarily,
there would be no vouchers but if they are willing to sell the property at a reduced price the
state would supplement them with a tax structure with a new tool adopted in the latest
legislative session. Commissioner Sickles pressed if this would open the Master
Development Agreement (MDA) to some changes. Planning Director Maloy believes it
might, currently staff are thinking this could be a zoning overlay. The city would need a
housing administrator, someone who monitors that the agreement restrictions and
expenditures are honored over time. Commissioner Anderson commented that all these
different programs have different requirements.
Planning Director Maloy reported the council approved three different zoning amendments.
The council appreciated the Commission’s and staff’s work on the C-2 commercial zone
material and site design guidelines. There was some discussion on the carwash and credit
union limits. Once they realized it did not apply to anything outside of an MDA, they
supported it more. It sends a signal to the marketplace to bring something besides those.
The city is trying to encourage a mix of services that we are currently lacking. We have
other land uses that are prohibited within the city. Commissioner Sickles probed if the city
can incentives what we do want instead of restricting what we don’t want. Planning
Director Maloy theorizes communication and personal connection with the developers is
the most useful. We have in the past been able to establish incentives, but the state has
restricted that. Commissioner Jacobson checked where staff is on the trailer storage.
Planning Director Maloy explained last he heard it will be on the July council agenda. Staff
have made some changes, but we might have some unintended consequences. There is also
talk about an RV storage home business license with some regulations involved with it.
The council had a long conversation about the amendment to the South Hills MDA that was
tabled. The Council gave directions to move away from the 2008 general plan for East
Herriman and change the agreement to be based on the new general plan and to identify the
zoning districts that the applicant is requesting. This team has been difficult to negotiate
with, they have a lot of newer employees and partners that don’t have a history of
development under the MDA. South Hills area doesn’t have water access. The city and
another property owner have worked on starting a water plan with additional tanks. This
will come back to the Council with the 5th amendment apply for a zoning map amendment
and possibly a general plan amendment. A possible land swap with another development
could bring a 6th amendment. Commissioner Anderson quired if it was unusual to update to
the new general plan. Planning Director Maloy expressed we have tried to use a similar
structure but the details are so unique. He voiced there are cities that don’t do
development agreements. If the city is doing a lot of infill, you won’t have the development
agreements.

   4.2. Review and consider a recommendation to amend Chapter 10-9 Agricultural
        Zones of Title 10 Land Development Code to remove density bonus options in
        the A-1-10 Agricultural Single-Family Zone. (Public Hearing)
        Applicant: Herriman City
        File No: Z2024-066
Planning Director Maloy presented the zoning text amendment. It was previously proposed
to have staff draft an amendment for agricultural zones for full, half and ¼ acre lots. The A-
1-10 zone in the past was used for larger developments, it included a bonus density
structure. As a development partnership added certain amenities they have received a
bonus density up to 2.5 units per acre maximum. The A-1-10 zone lot is a 10,000 square foot
lot. In an acre you would get close to 4 acres a lot. For larger developments this bonus made
sense. Recommendation is to remove the bonus density provision from the code and allow
development with minimum ¼ acre lots and agricultural standards. The objective for this
amendment is to simplify subdivision regulation, improve predictability, reduce subjectivity
and comply with the state code. The draft ordinance was shown.

No comments were offered.

   4.3. Review and consider a recommendation to amend sections of Title 10 Land
        Development Code to clarify street connectivity requirements for subdivision

       plat applications, reduce the expiration and extension of preliminary plat
       approval regulations, and update related subdivision standards and procedural
       requirements in Herriman City Code. (Public Hearing)
       Applicant: Herriman City
       File No: Z2024-069
Planning Director Maloy explained this item was initiated by the city council. Cities have
been recording plats for centuries. We are still adjusting and modernizing them every year.
The council was asked to adopt a moratorium on this process by legal. At the preliminary
subdivision plat application review we currently have an ordinance that every street must
connect to an existing public street. The request is to have the ordinance change for an
alignment to have a portion of commitment at preliminary stage and a full construction
commitment at the final review. At the preliminary stage an alignment and agreement with
any property owner the road would cross would be required. At the final stage it would be
required to include civil plans and the roadway dedication plat. This will allow the
developer to postpone the expense until it is partially approved. The amendment also
reduces the subdivision approval to a one-year initial term and reduces the extension to a
one-year addition for a total of two years. Currently we have a two-year initial approval
with an extension of twice the initial amount which is up to six years total before requiring
it to be plated. The draft amendment was shown. Staff recommends a positive
recommendation to the city council.

No comments were offered.
