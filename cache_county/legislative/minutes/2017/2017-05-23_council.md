OTHER ITEMS:

© FEMA has set up an office at the Emergency Operations Center working with Will Lusk

and the Development Services office. Executive Buttars asked Director Runhaar to
explain a problem with the Mendon Road damage. Runhaar said the Mendon Road is
under a UDOT classification where once you hit a road that is minor arterial or above
FEMA will no longer fund projects. It is turned over to Federal Highways Administration
which considers anything less than $700,000 to be maintenance. The Mendon Road
repair estimate is $200,000 to $250,000 and is considered maintenance. An additional
funding source was looked into, but the funding is only for temporary repairs and the
Mendon Road needs a permanent fix. Cache County will have to pay for it. Other than
this problem, FEMA has been good to work with.

Events Center Update — Executive Buttars has met with Dallin Craven, Design West,
and went over a good cost estimate. A more detailed list will be reviewed at the

June 13, 2017 Fairgrounds Advisory Board meeting. The price of steel has gone up
significantly, but the county was able to negotiate a better price with Nuccor Steel.
However, the current proposed cost is $6.4 million which is more than initially planned.
Buttars will give details at the June 27, 2017 budget opening for the Council's review.
Bids for the bonding were only for $3.2 million, not for the full $3.5 million so the bond
level could be raised another $300,000. There is a good working relationship with the
contractor, engineer and architects. Buttars would also like to have a discussion in the
budget opening about hiring an events planner.

GBS Benefits, Inc. - The Employee Compensation Committee has decided to go with
GBS Benefits, Inc. as Cache County’s broker for health insurance plans at a cost of
$24,000 a year. Chairman Merrill asked about UAC’s self-insuring program. Buttars
replied UAC is still working on a self-insuring program. UAC also goes through GBS
which will make for an easy transition if they develop a self-insuring program. Buttars
believes the $24,000 cost can possibly be offset with an in-house wellness program.

EDCUtah membership dues are $11,000 and if the Council wants to be involved, Buttars
recommends discontinuing funding for the Washington lobbyist (Bob Weiner) and use it
for EDCUtah. The county is also currently paying the Chamber of Commerce $35,000 a
year for economic development.
