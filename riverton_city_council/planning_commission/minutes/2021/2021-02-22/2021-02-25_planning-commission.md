2.     PUBLIC HEARING ITEMS

       a.     CONDITIONAL USE, BRANDON OQUENDO IS REQUESTING A
              CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT FOR AN EXCEPTION OF THE SOLID
              MASONRY WALL REQUIREMENT FOR ASPEN COURT SUBDIVISION AT
              1546 WEST 11745 SOUTH.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the request is for
fencing in the Aspen Court subdivision. It is a four-lot subdivision approved by the Planning
Commission in 2020 and is currently under construction. The property has since been sold
and a new applicant has purchased the property. He is requesting a conditional use to install
RhinoRoc along the east property line instead of the six-foot masonry that was approved
with the subdivision. A zoning rendering was displayed.

At the time of the original application, staff review included creating a site plan. The existing
fence will remain on Lot 1 with six-foot vinyl along the north and west side and six-foot pre-
case or masonry along the canal. An adjacent neighbor disputed the posted property line
stakes and requested fencing be six-foot masonry allowing them to continue farming.
Mr. Prestwich noted that the motion included site fencing being installed as shown on the
plans. He noted that it must be installed prior to issuance of building permits.

Mr. Prestwich reported that the applicant requested an exception from the Ordinance. The
fencing to the south of the project was approved with the subdivision in 2014. As the result
of a tragedy involving a child drowning in a canal in 2015, Riverton City undertook a

significant and exhaustive look at fencing focusing specifically on canals. The Ordinance
was changed in 2015 to require solid masonry fencing. Every City park that abutted a canal
was retrofitted with pre-cast masonry fencing along with all new City projects adjacent to a
canal. Mr. Prestwich conveyed Council Member Stewart’s expressed concern recalling the
difficulty of 2015 and emphasized that the pre-cast was what he understood would be
installed everywhere along the canals.

Mr. Prestwich reported that the RhinoRoc product is constructed of hollow foam with
concrete on both sides. The manufacturer produced evidence of its strength by including a
major league baseball pitcher throwing baseballs at their product. Meanwhile, the concrete
company showed the RhinoRoc product being hit with a screwdriver or small bar resulting
in a puncture. Incidentally, they had testimony from the Parks Department who installed
RhinoRoc along a non-waterway boundary of a park where Little League football players
punched holes in it with their helmets. Mr. Prestwich believed there was no conclusive
evidence presented to the City Council that RhinoRoc is not equally as safe as solid masonry
fencing. The City Council chose to require solid masonry.

Mr. Prestwich reported that special circumstances may be reviewed by the Board of
Adjustment in order to grant a variance where specific State laws guiding the variance must
be met. The applicant was not asking for a variance but for an exception to the Ordinance.
He referenced Section 18-155-150 of the Ordinance regarding fencing required in specific
areas. It specifies that fences along ditches, canals, or other irrigation lines shall consist of
minimum decorative solid core pre-cast concrete, or colored, textured block, brick, or other
masonry fencing. Hollow foam core fiberglass concrete mix or other alternative fence types
are not permitted. He further explained that alternative fencing types may be allowed
adjacent to properties with animal rights and irrigation canals or other waterways provided
that the Commission finds that the proposed fence type is not detrimental to the safety and
general welfare of the public.

Mr. Prestwich emphasized that approving the use of RhinoRoc will set a precedent for future
projects. Should the Commission determine that foam core fencing is acceptable, he
suggested that a better alternative would be an Ordinance change.

City Attorney, Craig Bott, stated the request has been reviewed and a quandary is created.
When there is ambiguity in the statute, the ambiguity is construed against the maker or the
City. The City Council found that the legislative intent with regard to canals was to only have
solid masonry fencing. He recommended tabling the matter and sending a request to the
Council to make a statutory language change to clarify the intent of the statute, removing all
ambiguity. When completed, the matter could be re-heard and the direction of the Council
applied as expressed in a clarified statute.

Commissioner Cluff clarified if the Planning Commission recommends approval, it will
essentially set a precedent that overrides the decision of the City Council regarding the type
of fencing that is deemed safe.

A comment was made that Council Member Stewart has indicated a preference for solid
masonry fencing. If there is an ambiguity, it needs to be corrected prior to moving forward.

A question was raised regarding the original approval as to if the property had not been sold
to a different developer if there would have been an avenue to bring it back with this request.
Mr. Prestwich explained that ultimately, the request is for a conditional use seeking a change
a previous approval. He noted that the previous owner could have done the same.

Commissioner Breinholt was leaning toward the original statute as written and suggested
the matter be sent back to the City Council requesting clarification.

The applicant, Brandon Oquendo, identified himself as the owner and developer of the
Aspen Court Subdivision. He was requesting a Conditional Use Permit to allow an
alternative fence to be installed along the property line he shares with the City that borders
the South Jordan Canal. His intent was to install an alternative metal material wall that has
a well-established reputation and design to replicate a solid masonry wall. The City has
approved this type of material in the past and it is nearly identical to the required product.
Images of the proposed fencing were displayed. Mr. Oquendo explained that an alternative
material wall was approved and installed along the perimeter of an adjacent property to the
south prior to the current requirements. There are no structural concerns and the fencing
material replicates the newly required walls almost identically. Images of the property were
described. Mr. Oquendo argued that his is not a request for a park or playground and it will
not benefit the residents to destroy their own fencing. It was his opinion that the required
solid masonry fencing could not be installed without compromising the bank of the canal due
to its large footings and weight. The fencing company that installs the alternative product
has offered an extended warranty and emphasized that the solid masonry wall cost is more
than double the cost of the alternative material, which is a burden to the developer.

Commissioner Russell asked if the applicant was aware of the masonry fencing
requirements when purchasing the property. Mr. Oquendo was aware of the pre-cast
requirement but was not informed that it was to be made up of solid masonry. He considered
pre-cast and concrete to be ambiguous in terms of the wall. The walls are manufactured
and pre-cast is intended to mimic the existing concrete wall. He was under the impression
that it has to replicate the existing masonry wall, not a solid core wall.

Commissioner Cluff believed the issue of safety and the difference between public and
private land was an argument better made to the City Council to change the Ordinance and
is not necessarily within the purview of the Planning Commission.

There were no further public comments. The public hearing was closed.

Commissioner Lefgren explained that as a developer, he questioned the positioning of the
fencing companies. He considered the RhinoRoc product to be sufficient and something
that should be considered in the back of residential by the City Council. He commented that
there is a distinct difference between the safety of having it behind a residential unit. His
opinion was that it may be less safe having pre-cast with their extensive footings and gaps.

Commissioner Russell moved to table the request for a Conditional Use Permit for an
exception to the solid masonry wall requirement for Aspen Court Subdivision located
at 1546 West 11745 South and forward it onto the City Council for input and
