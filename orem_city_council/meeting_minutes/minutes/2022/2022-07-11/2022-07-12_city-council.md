Cissy Rasmussen an Orem resident stated her kids have attended Orem schools for 17 years and

       PUBLIC HEARING / ORDINANCE – Amending Section 22-5-3(A) and the zoning map
       of the City of Orem by rezoning the property located at approximately 659 South 350
       West from the R8 zone to the R6 zone.



Mr. Bench stated Michael and Lisa Kelly own a vacant residential lot located at 659 South 350
West as well as the adjacent lot directly to the south. They would like to subdivide the northern
parcel into two lots so that two of their children with young families can build homes there.

The vacant parcel is currently zoned R8, but the lot is not quite large enough to be divided into
two lots under the R8 zone requirements. However, the lot could be divided into two lots under
the R6 zone with one of the lots being a deep lot. The R6 zone requires a minimum lot size of
6,000 square feet and flag lots need to be one-hundred twenty-five percent (125%) of that or
7,500 square feet. If the vacant lot were rezoned to R6 it could be subdivided into two lots with
one lot having 7,464 square feet and the second lot having 7,555 square feet. The proposed
subdivision would also meet the frontage requirements of the R6 zone as the standard lot would
have a width of 87.83 feet and a “net” width of 67.83 feet after subtracting the twenty foot wide
access easement to the deep lot. If they are able to subdivide the lot into two lots under the R6
zone, the Kelly’s feel they can help provide their neighborhood with some young families.



Mr. Macdonald inquired if there had been a neighborhood meeting held and asked what the
response was regarding this proposal. Mr. Kelly indicated they have support of the
neighborhoods.



Mr. Spencer inquired as to the length of the street for fire safety and if this met the requirements.
Mr. Bench indicated it does fit code and is not long enough to require a turnabout. He continued
to state that Orem City Code requires lot 2 to have a perpetual access. Lot one will own the
entire property but there will be a 20-foot easement across there that will be in perpetuity and
will be required to be paved.



Mayor Young opened the public hearing at 6:55 pm.

Hearing no comments Mayor Young closed the public hearing and brought the item back to the
City Council for further discussion.



Mr. Peterson moved to approve by Ordinance amending Section 22-5-3(A) and the zoning map
of the City of Orem by rezoning the property located at approximately 659 South 350 West from
the R8 zone to the R6 zone. Mr. Macdonald seconded the motion. Those voting aye: David A.
Young, David Spencer, Debby Lauret, Tom Macdonald, Jeff Lambson, Terry Peterson and LaNae
Millett. The motion passed.

PRESENTATION - School District Split Feasibility Study
Mayor Young indicated that each City Council member had the opportunity to meet with the
consultants and ask any questions they had before the study began. He welcomed Mr. McCarty
and Mr. Wankier of Discover Education Consultants (DEC). He introduced and reviewed some
of Mr. McCarty’s recognitions, achievements, history and qualifications.



Mayor Young continued to introduce Michael Wankier and gave some background as to his
qualifications. Mr. Wankier explained to the council his role as an auditor and explained the
means in which they requested information from Alpine School District (ASD) and how they
looked at other school districts, county, state and other records to get all the data necessary to
conclude the study.



Mr. McCarty began his review of the study by stating the table of contents which they felt was
the direction from the council to be not only a feasibility study to see of the creation of a new
school district would be feasible and sustainable but also what would be the impact on students
and teachers, so this is what they focused on which include the following chapters:

1)      Financial Evaluation

2)      Student Test Scores, Student Achievement, Title I

3)      2022 &2016 FEMA “Earthquake Safety Study

4)      ASD Teacher & Administrator Salaries

5)      Class Size and Student Teacher Ratios

6)      Student Accessibility to ASD Specialty Classes

7)      Research Review of School District Size & Intimal Enrollment

8)      Trends to Consolidate Two or More Schools into One Large School

The Study also includes the following:

·    2004 Pioneer School District Feasibility Study – Utah County Commissioners

·    History of the Canyon/Jordan Split & Resulting Tax Impact

·    Vineyard & Lindon – Prospects of Students Attending New District Schools

·    High Schools’ in New District and Continue Participation in UHSAA Activities
·   Proposed New District Organizational chart

·   New District Boundary Map

·   How Funding Works for Utah Public School Education



Mr. Wankier stated they believe it is feasible to have a new school district for the City of Orem
and be possible to maintain the status quo without significant changes to taxes. He continued to
review the taxable value comparison of various school districts that are primarily neighbors to
Alpine School District (ASD). In 2020 the taxable value for ASD as presently constituted was
about $34.2 billion based on a student population of 80,953, which is a value per student of
approximately $422,000 to $423,000.             He continued to state that if that was split into two
districts, out of that $34.2 billion, 6.7% of that value is related to the residents of Orem. He
continued to review the value information of the study for 2021 and various situations that would
affect the outcome.



Mrs. Lauret inquired if they factored in the value and growth or just value. Mr. Wankier
responded they built their model using both but separated them out into new growth and
historical value growth.



Mr. Wankier continued to state when they built their model, they went through 3 different
estimated household property tax scenarios. He continued to explain each scenario’s outcome
and how property taxes are affected. They developed a budget estimated with state and federal
fees and got the balance for property taxes for each scenario. They felt it was important to
calculate each scenario to show the possibilities. He continued to state they thought this was the
best way to approach the different scenarios because it gives them something to compare to
based on what they are experiencing today. The baseline is keeping everything together with no
change from how it is today and just breaking out the numbers and forecasting what the average
per year is going to look like for the next 10 years. Mr. Wankier continued to review and explain
the 4 baseline scenarios.



Baseline scenarios are as follows:
1)      No split and a new bond of $595 million issued; Orem shared in $116 million of the bond
debt with no guarantee of reinvestment in Orem schools and all taxpayers see a 12.8% increase
in taxes.

2)     Orem leaves, no new bonds: Tax increase of 4.2% in Orem; effectively no change in
remaining ASD taxes

3)     Orem leases, ASD bonds for $595 M: Tax increase of 4.2% in Orem; tax increase in
ASD of 15.9 Orem leaves & bonds for $125M (receives 100%), ASD bonds for $595M: Tax
increase in Orem of 17.9% (compared to baseline; tax increase in ASD of 15.9%



Mr. Macdonald asked for clarification regarding the information presented. It is his
understanding that taxes would go up in 3 of the 4 scenarios except the one where they stay with
the Alpine School District. Mr. Wankier indicated Mr. Macdonald was correct.



Mrs. Lauret inquired if they figured in the new bond if they were rolling off others or just on top
of the existing bonds. Mr. Wankier responded they based it on the amortization of the existing
bonds and just layered the new bonds on top.

Mrs. Millett asked if he could clarify the “net zero” statement. Mr. Wankier responded it comes
back to how the calculation works without any bond. The amount of debt service gradually
decreases each year and because of that your tax rate should go down each year. Your value
increases and the amount that has to be paid stays the same the rate is going to change but the
total dollars that have to be paid reduce a little bit each year as the bonds amortize. The goal in a
net zero situation would be as the property taxes increase you can issue a new bond and have the
same rate but the total dollars that you pay still has to increase because you have to cover the old
bonds plus the new bonds, he continued to stated it all depends on whether you look at the rate or
you look at the dollars. He feels the right way to look at it is the dollars because that's what
actually comes out of your wallet. Mrs. Millett asked if what he was talking about would be
considered as a tax burden. Mr. Wankier responded she was correct.



Mr. Peterson inquired if Orem has ever received their 19% share of past bonds. Mr. Wankier
indicated historically Orem has put in approximately 19% overall and only received
approximately 13% of all bond allocations from 2002 to 2021.
Mr. McCarty reviewed the historic bond overview that included the following information:

        · From 2002 to 2021, approximately $120 million of Orem taxes subsidized the
        building of schools outside of Orem ($6.3 million annually). This funding comports
        with the 2004 Pioneer school district study.

        · If the $595 million bond passes, Orem taxpayers would be responsible for the
        payment of approximately $116 million (19.44%) over 20 years, with no guarantee as to
        the amount of reinvestment that would be made into Orem schools.
