          The purpose of the work session was to review the agenda for the City Council meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.;
receive the Ogden Downtown Alliance Annual Report; receive the Fiscal Year 2022-2023 Grants Report; discuss proposed partial
street vacation – Cahoon Street between F Avenue and G Avenue; discuss proposed rezone – 218 Chester Street; receive a
legislative update from City Administration; discuss Council business; and hear Council comments.

Agenda Review
        Chair Choberka and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council meeting
scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Ogden Downtown Alliance Annual Report
          The City Council has requested to receive information from local foundations and nonprofit organizations that have a
contractual agreement with Ogden City. These presentations help the Council to become more familiar with the objectives and
efforts of the organizations.
          Thomas Kiernan, Ogden Downtown Alliance (ODA), used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to describe the
organizational mission and goals, successes, and challenges over the past year, and any future plans for the organization. For a
copy of the PowerPoint presentation in its entirety see the information packet.
          Discussion among the Council and Mr. Kiernan centered on the growth of the Farmers Market; partnerships between the
ODA and various businesses in the Ogden community; and opportunities for the ODA to assist the City in communicating
changes to ordinances that impact business owners. Chair Choberka thanked Mr. Kiernan for the information provided tonight.

Fiscal Year 2022-2023 Grants Report
         Deputy Comptroller Jensen used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide an overview of the Fiscal Year (FY)
2022-2023 Annual Grants Report. This report outlines the expenditures relating to local, state, and federal grant funds as of June
30, 2023. The City managed more than $58 Million in various grant funds during the fiscal year and expended more than $12
Million during Fiscal Year 2022-2023. For a copy of the PowerPoint presentation in its entirety see the information packet.

Proposed Partial Street Vacation – Cahoon Street between F Avenue and G Avenue
         Planning Manager Brierley explained petitioners, Tyler Gray, Teresa McCandless, and Rental Investors, LLC, are
requesting to vacate a portion of the Cahoon Street right-of-way between F Avenue and G Avenue. The portion of the public
right-of-way proposed to be vacated is 137.9 feet long, with a total area of 9,320 square feet. The Planning Commission made a
recommendation to deny the petition, finding that the potential for material injury existed relative to the City’s ability to plan for
utilities in the area. This request is on the Council work session agenda so that the Council can discuss possible next steps,
including directing Council staff to place the item on a future Council meeting agenda for a public hearing. Mr. Brierley also used
the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to orient the Council to the development of the area surrounding the portion of Cahoon Street
that is subject to the requested vacation; the petitioners’ conceptual site plan for the property they currently own and the portion of
the street to be vacated; plans for the future construction project to convert the 24th Street and Interstate-15 interchange from a
partial interchange to a full interchange; and the impact the street vacation could have on future infrastructure improvement
projects needed in the area. For a copy of the PowerPoint presentation in its entirety see the information packet.
           Discussion among the Council and Mr. Brierley centered on the timing and scope of the future infrastructure projects;
details surrounding the Planning Commission’s findings in support of denial of the petition; and the timing of the 24th Street
interchange project. There was also brief discussion with Council staff regarding the timing of scheduling a public hearing to
consider the petition.
           Council member Myers indicated he would like to hear from the petitioners regarding their motivation for the street
vacation petition. A representative of the McClary Company introduced Mr. Gray and Ms. McCandless, the owners of the
property, and also his clients. He then used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss historical vacation of other portions of
Cahoon Street, which included the adjoining property owners granting the City a municipal utility easement to provide for the
future infrastructure projects. His presentation also touched on the petitioners’ development concept plan; the types of businesses
that would locate in this type of development; and the projected property and sales tax revenue associated with the development.
           Council member Graf addressed Public Services Director Anderson; he noted that the City plans to install a 24-inch
water line in the area in the future, but the petitioners are proposing a 16-inch water line. Mr. Anderson stated that a 24-inch line is
needed on Exchange Road, and it will connect to the 24-inch water line on the west side of Interstate-15. The purpose of the line is
to connect to the 30th Street reservoir for water storage for the zone and to ensure proper water supply to the areas west of
Interstate-15 and the Ogden Airport. Council member Graf stated that he thinks the project is great in concept but acknowledged
the need to address and reconcile the concerns of staff and the Planning Commission.
           Council staff indicated they will schedule the public hearing for February 20, 2024. Council Deputy Director Symes
discussed the options that will be available to the Council at the conclusion of the public hearing; they can either uphold the
Planning Commission’s recommendation to deny, or direct Council staff to create an ordinance that would allow them to consider
approval of the street vacation.
           The Council also heard from a Planning Commissioner in attendance; Council member Hyer asked him if the Planning
Commission received the same presentation provided tonight by the petitioner and, if not, if the information provided would have
changed their recommendation. The Commissioner indicated the Commission was informed of some possible options for
rerouting the water line, but that Cahoon Street is the preferred route for the water line.

Proposed Rezone – 218 Chester Street
          Deputy Planning Manager Simpson explained Rick Southwick has submitted a petition to rezone two parcels, totaling
2.38 acres located at 218 Chester Street, from R-1-6 (single-family residential zone) to R-2S (two-family and small lot residential
zone). The developer’s intent is to establish zoning on the property, that allows for owner occupied townhomes. The Planning
Commission has made a recommendation to adopt the proposed rezoning ordinance based upon the finding that the proposal is
consistent with the General Plan and the Lynn Community Plan. Mr. Simpson also used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
identify the location of the subject property and its surroundings, as well as the petitioner’s concept plan for the property. For a
copy of the PowerPoint presentation in its entirety see the information packet.
          Discussion among the Council and Planning staff centered on the differences between the R-1-6 zone and the R-2S zone
and any potential conflicts associated with those differences; open space requirements in the R-2S zone; and whether the City has
the ability to impose an owner-occupancy requirement on a private development in the R-2S zone.
          Chair Choberka invited input from the petitioner.
          Gary Cannon stated he and Mr. Southwick are working on development of the subject property; he and Mr. Southwick
feel very strongly that it is best for the neighborhood to build a for-sale project and to encourage owner-occupancy. If there is a
way to avoid a situation where an investor may buy multiple units in the project and convert them to rentals, he will pursue that
option. The product will be affordable, but not low income and it will enhance the neighborhood. Mr. Southwick added he feels
that the R-2S zone is a great zoning option for this type of infill project; it will provide for affordable for-sale units at a target price
under $400,000. He is committed to home-ownership opportunities and is excited to proceed with this project. The neighborhood
is laid out in a way that provides for efficient circulation.
          Council Policy Analyst Burton summarized the timeline for considering the rezone application; action can be scheduled
for February 20, 2024, and the Council can approve, deny, or table the petition at that time.

Legislative Update – Administration
         Chief Administrative Officer Brown and Mayor Nadolski provided the Council with an update regarding the 2024
Legislative Session. Topics of interest included Property Rights Ombudsman amendments; laws regarding the types of fees, in
addition to utility fees and taxes, that can be charged to a household or business; single child changing areas/unisex spaces inside
public facilities; diversity, equity, inclusion legislation; and legislation aimed at allowing a local government entity to secure
sufficient water in drought years.

work to draft a Code of Conduct for the Council.
