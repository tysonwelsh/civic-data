Mayor Silvestrini called the meeting to order. Mike Winder introduced Josie Angerhofer as the

1. Discussion Items
1.1 City Center Update; Francis Lilly, Planning and Zoning Director and Mike Winder,

Economic Development Director
Mayor Silvestrini said Millcreek has been closing on properties in the City Center with
bond money. Mike Winder said properties will close by the end of February 2020 and
announced that a version of the City Center timeline would be printed in the newsletter.
Francis Lilly said there would be an open house the following evening on the proposed
City Center Overlay Zone. He said the intended purpose of the open house is to discuss
the overlay zone adoption and to begin a public conversation about the open space. The
Council should have the first reading of the proposed overlay zone in October. Lilly said
he sent 900 mailed notices to every property owner within the city center boundaries and
within 600 feet from the city center boundaries in addition to notice being posted on the
website and on Millcreek’s social media. Council Member Jackson asked to be included

in mailings sent to her district. Council Member Uipi asked that all council members be
included in city center mailings. Lilly said the Planning Commission will have a public
hearing on the proposed ordinance September 18".

Lilly said the city center will have three sub districts; Market Place, Mill Center, and
Neighborhood District. Lilly briefly explained the differences between the districts with
building heights and uses. The nature of the overlay zone is so new development or
redevelopment meets the standards of code. Lilly said the heights will be feathered in
with the slope of the land with the greatest height being in the center of the city center.
Winder said the proposed height transitions map came from feedback from the Planning
Commission. Lilly said the proposed overlay zone ordinance matches the existing
boundaries of the overlay zone. A characteristic of the proposed overlay zone is that it
goes into how a building addresses a street with required step backs. He said he received
a recommended tree list for the area from a resident. Mayor Silvestrini asked about
having a County Parks arborist being involved in planning. Rita Lund said the County
does not have an arborist on staff but contracts the service out. Lilly said the plan dictates
specific frontage and amenities on surrounding streets. Winder mentioned that permanent
awnings would also be required. Lilly said the goal of the ordinance is to add regulatory
tools to ensure the City gets a quality project. The buildings around the open space should
be a legacy for Millcreek. John Miller pointed out that the proposed street rendering for
the area was pedestrian friendly. Winder said there would be required setbacks from the
open space. Lilly said property lines in the open space have not yet been determined.

Mayor Silvestrini brought up looking at city center models in cities in Kansas, California,
Oregon, and Arizona as a source of guidance for the city center. Winder said there was
currently a request for proposals (RFP) out for design consultants for the open space and
the consultants may present model ideas from other cities. Mayor Silvestrini brought up
examples in Salt Lake City the Council should also look at. He suggested having an
envision committee for planning the open space. Lilly said the consultant should include
a branding and public engagement plan. The City will be paying an honorarium to the
short-listed consultants to be able to use their proposals on the project even if the firm is
not selected. He said every important step with the city center would have public
engagement. Mayor Silvestrini brought up an example with the Jordan River project
where creative proposals had been considered. Council Member Marchant asked about
the lilac strip on Highland Drive, known as the Mountair streetscape. Lilly said there was
an expensive proposal for the design of it and he was working on the terms. A small
conversation took place about the existing plants and maintenance of that streetscape.

The Council took a break from 6:15-6:19 p.m.

1.2 Projects Update; John Miller, Public Works Director

John Miller said for Millcreek to be more transparent, his team created a webpage for
capital improvement projects on the City’s website. The page shows the description and
status of projects since September 2018. There were nine projects on the page and Miller
highlighted two of the projects. Miller said he negotiated a contract with Lochner Inc. for
design and right-of-way acquisition for the 3900 South project. Mayor Silvestrini said
Millcreek and Holladay would mostly be operating in the existing right-of-way on the
project, though there would be a little property acquisition for rounding sidewalk corners.

1.3 Budget and Strategic Planning; Laurie Johnson, HR-Finance Director
Laurie Johnson handed out copies of the newly adopted Employee Handbook and
corresponding forms from it to the Council. Mayor Silvestrini said Utah has legalized
medical marijuana and it could affect job performance. He said there were issues and
protections with employees using it and with random drug tests. He said staff should
explore the issue. Johnson said there is a form in the handbook in which employees must
declare prescribed medication if it could affect job performance.

Johnson gave an update on the implementation of the financial management software.
She said Tyler Technologies is currently configuring it and it will go live in October. She
said at the beginning of July, the City Manager reorganized staff. She gave a summary of
the changes: “Building Services” has been added as a new department (includes building
inspection; building permits); “City Services” is now the “Legislative Policy
Department;” “Community Development” is now titled “Planning & Zoning;” IT has
been added to ““Communications;” the Information Center Manager is now also
responsible for IT functions; and the business license division is now part of the
“Economic Development Department.” Mayor Silvestrini said he has observed that John
Geilmann is constantly rethinking how Millcreek provides services by getting the right
people in the right seat and allowing for cross training to make the organization the best it
can be. Council Member Uipi and Council Member Marchant commended John
Geilmann. Johnson said she would reorganize the budget according to the department
changes in the first budget amendment. Geilmann said there are 38 fulltime employees
and the department directors work the same as the employees.

Johnson announced that Millcreek was initiating a new program, Millcreek Team
Appreciation and Recognition Program (TARP). The Utah Local Government Trust
(ULGT) has developed a Team Appreciation & Recognition Program whereas member
cities can earn a refund of 5% of their insurance premiums. The potential savings from
the last fiscal year budget could have been $5,500. The cost of the TARP program this
fiscal year would be $3,800. Johnson said city employees will be divided into three teams
based on their work schedule and teams that do not have any accidents (worker’s comp,
auto accidents, etc.) will receive a $25 Amazon gift card each quarter. The ultimate goal
is to have the safest environment possible for employees and incentives will help to
achieve that goal. She said the program would be introduced to staff at the next all hands
staff meeting. She reported that the ULGT did a safety inspection the prior week of City
Hall to follow ensure compliance with the Occupational Safety and Health
Administration requirements. They identified 10 items that needed to be fixed:

* monthly inspection documentation

* dark exit sign - storage room

* ladders and table not properly secured — storage room

* open ceiling panels - exercise room

1.4 Mayor and Council’s Discretion on Discussion Topic
Mayor Silvestrini said at the next Council work meeting, Travis Jones from Rocky
Mountain Power would come to discuss H.B. 411. The bill allows cities to opt into a
renewable energy program by 2030. The Council must adopt a resolution by December
31, 2019 that will commit the City to pay for a rate making study by Rocky Mountain
Power which would assess what it would cost Millcreek to get all energy from renewable
sources. The study would help the Council know what the rate would be for Millcreek
residents. If the Council decided to then move forward, they would adopt an ordinance
based on the information from the study saying that Rocky Mountain Power would give
notice to residents to opt into the renewable energy program. He said of the comments he
has received on the matter, about half were in favor and half were against Millcreek
opting in. He said other cities have not adopted a resolution yet that complies with the
bill. Council Member Marchant asked about getting input from a source who may be
opposed to the opt in.

Mayor Silvestrini said he met with Salt Lake City representatives last week about
boundary adjustments. Both cities will adopt a resolution on why considerations for
boundary adjustments should be made. The resolution also considers adoption of an
interlocal agreement that is subject to public engagement. The boundary adjustment
includes the property in the city center between Miller Avenue and Woodland Avenue
and 1300 East and Highland Drive and the property around the 2300 East roundabout. He

said the tax revenue currently generated by those properties (about $61,000) would be
given to Salt Lake City annually for 10 years and the agreement states that Millcreek will
not promote or pursue legislation regarding the Brickyard Plaza. The resolution would
initiate the process of the boundary adjustment and adoption of the interlocal agreement.
The boundary adjustment process is a statutory process and the adjustment will not take
effect until July 1, 2020. He said there would be a joint press conference on September
3"! The Salt Lake City Council will hear the resolution at their meeting on September 3
and then on September 17". The Millcreek Council will hear the resolution on September
9'" and September 23". The resolution says each city is adopting the interlocal agreement
and the interlocal agreement is subject to being voided if the public process does not go
according to plan. A public hearing will then be set for not less than 60 days from the
adoption of the resolution. After the public hearing, the public can then file a protest. If
there is no protest, then each Council will adopt an ordinance. The cities will file a local
entity plan by December 31‘. The County Surveyor will prepare the plats. Mayor
Silvestrini said both cities would work together on the public engagement.
