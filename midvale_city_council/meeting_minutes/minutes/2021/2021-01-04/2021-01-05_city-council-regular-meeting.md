(CAFR) for fiscal year 2020. The CAFR represents a “step above” the minimum
information legally required by the State of Utah and Generally Accepted Accounting
Principles. The CAFR supplements the basic financial statements in the following ways:
* Introductory information — Additional background on the City’s structure,
environment in which it operates, and the types of services it provides.
Management also has a forum to offer a more subjective analysis of the City’s
financial situation.
* Information on individual funds — A CAFR provides individual fund data for
nonmajor funds.
* Statistical data - The CAFR includes a statistical section that offers multi-year
trend information, along with relevant economic and demographic information.
He discussed the different sections of the CAFR, and the underlying meaning behind
each statement. He also discussed financial performance for each fund.

Jana Ward said LuAron Foster is proposing a text amendment to update the Regional
Commercial zone to modify the impervious surface requirement in the front yard. The
ordinance currently limits impervious material coverage to 15% of the front yard1. Ms.
Foster’s request proposes to limit the impervious requirement to the front setback instead.
As the RM-12, RM-25, 7200 S Overlay, and Cl zones contain similar language regarding
impervious surface coverage, Staff recommends extending this request to include those
zones as well. Additionally, while working with the applicant on this proposed amendment,
Staff identified two tables in the RM-12 and RM-25 zones where building height had not
been updated in an earlier text amendment and is proposing to correct that oversight in
this request.
The Regional Commercial (RC) and Clean Industrial (Cl) zones both include the following
provision:
Impervious Surface. Impervious surfaces shall cover no more than fifteen percent of the
front yard. (§§17-7-12.3(B) and 17-7-13.3(B))
Both zones are intended to allow for a wide variety of uses, utilizing landscape buffers
and other requirements to mitigate potential impacts on neighboring properties. Both
zones have also experienced variances requests and a loss of development opportunities
due to this requirement. The issues created by this requirement are seen most obviously
with parking lots and drive throughs. Past versions of the RC zone allowed parking lots
and drive-throughs in the front yard. Based on current code, however, parking lots and
drive throughs are not allowed in the front yard because they add too much impervious
surface and violate the 15% limit. This has resulted in numerous lots with access
management and dimensional issues that are challenging to develop because regional
commercial and clean industrial projects increasingly rely on drive throughs and visible
parking lots. Narrow lots have also suffered because the simple installation of a driveway
can result in exceeding the impervious surface requirement. The proposed text
amendment would change the language in each zone to limit impervious surfaces in the
front yard setback, instead of the front yard, thereby resolving these issues while
maintaining the overall objectives of the zones.
The Multifamily Residential - Medium Density Zone (RM-12), Multifamily Residential —
Medium to High Density Zone (RM-25) and 7200 S Overlay Zone (7200 S) contain similar
language regarding impervious surface coverage in the rear yard2:
RM-12: /mpervious Surfaces. For new multifamily development, up to forty percent of the
rear yard may be an impervious surface. (§17-7-3.4(A)(3)(c))
RM-25: Impervious Surfaces. For new multifamily development, a maximum of forty
percent of the rear yard may be an impervious surface. (§17-7-4.4(A)(3)(d))
7200 S: Impervious Surfaces. A maximum of thirty percent of the rear yard may be an
impervious surface. Mixed-use development may include parking within the rear yard
setback. (§17-7-6.4(B)(2)(c))

These existing requirements limit the parking and recreational amenity opportunities in
the rear yard by forcing parking to the side yard and constraining site layout. Other
requirements in the ordinance, such as the build-to line and landscaping requirements,
push parking and recreation amenities to the rear yard, creating conflicts with this
requirement. The proposed text amendment would resolve these conflicts by limiting the
impervious surface requirement to the rear yard setback instead of rear yard, maintaining
a landscape buffer between adjacent properties while still encouraging parking behind
the building. |
This text amendment also proposes a correction to the Lot and Development Standards
Tables in RM-12 (Table 17-7-3.4) and RM-25 (Table 17-7-4.4) discovered while preparing
the proposed impervious surface coverage amendments. The changes proposed to these
tables correct building height language, so they align with the changes adopted in
Ordinance No. 2019-O-05. This ordinance was approved by the City Council on May 7,
2019 and increased the maximum building es in the RM-12 and RM-25 zones. These
tables were not updated as part of that change and this proposal corrects that oversight.
Staff has prepared a text amendment for the Council to consider that addresses the
proposed changes. The Planning Commission held a public hearing on the proposed
changes on December 9, 2020 and forwarded a positive recommendation to the Council
regarding the proposed |
| language.

MIDVALE’S SEWER AND WATER UTILITIES
Glen Kennedy said the City has previously conducted annual in-house reviews of water
and wastewater utility rates. To ensure funding for the implementation of the water and
wastewater master plans, the City has sought proposals from professional firms to
provide an outside, comprehensive review of the following aspects of Midvale’s water and
wastewater utilities:
1. Utility rates;
2. Rate structures;
3. Capital replacement costs;
4. Operating costs;
5. Revenues;
6. Required system development to accommodate growth; and
7. Appropriate rate levels and funding options to maintain adequate service levels.
| The recommendations from this study should cover Midvale’s operational and capital
bes needs over the next five years and provide revenue projections for the next ten years.
