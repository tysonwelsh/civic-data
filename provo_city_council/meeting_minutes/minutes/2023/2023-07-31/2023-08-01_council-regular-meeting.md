3.         An Ordinance amending Provo City Code to allow meeting halls for rent as a conditional
           use in the Agricultural Zone and establishing standards for that use. Citywide Application
           PLOTA20230082 (0:23:50)

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2023-33, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Aaron Ardmore, Planning Supervisor, presented. He discussed the proposed text amendments which
included adding “meeting halls for rent” to the list of conditional uses in the A1 zone and adding
standard conditions to Section 14.34.250 of code.

Councilor Whipple asked what percentage of the land would be converted into the meeting hall.

Mr. Ardmore said the proposal states no more than 49 percent could be used for the structure, including
parking, and added that it could be adjusted if the council desired.

Chair MacKay invited Barry and Judy Finch, the applicants, to present.

Mr. and Ms. Finch shared the impact their farm has had in previous years due to the development of
Lakeview Parkway and the Regional Sports Park. They addressed the process that they have been
through including attending neighborhood meetings and meetings with Development Services staff and
the planning commission. They discussed the concerns of staff and said they feel like their project is in
alignment with the General Plan and the Southwest Neighborhood Plan.

Chair MacKay opened the item for public comment.

Scott Campbell, owner of Strider Security, shared his support for the project. He shared his opinion of
instances when it would be appropriate to have more than one security officer present.

Kathy Sorensen, of Provo, shared her belief in being able to protect one of the last pockets of agriculture
in the city, drawing from her upbringing on the Finch family farm spanning almost six decades. She
emphasized the farm's importance in offering unique experiences like pony rides and hands-on
interactions with animals. She said she views the farm as a long-standing community asset that could
fade if a barn was not built. She said her fear is the land transforming into a generic housing
development. She urged the council to help preserve the farm.

Adam Finch, of Provo, spoke about his deep connection to the farm, where he grew up and is set to
inherit alongside his siblings. He said the addition of this building to the property would simplify its
maintenance and make the decision to keep it much smoother. Instead of contemplating selling the
property, they would focus on constructing it together as a family, ensuring the preservation of
agriculture for future generations. He highlighted the enduring nature of the building, designed to last,
and symbolize family unity. He believes the construction would foster a stronger sense of community
among his family and beyond. He also noted that if similar initiatives arose, it would benefit agriculture
and the city.

Halley Finch, of Provo, shared her experience of getting married in her in-laws' backyard, a chance she
never anticipated having while growing up. The first time she visited the farm, her mother-in-law took
her on a horseback tour, which filled her with immense joy. Her desire was to extend this opportunity to
other families and girls like her who had dreamt of a farm wedding with horses in the backdrop. She
hoped to provide her daughter and potential future children with the chance to grow up on the farm,
engaging with animals and experiencing the same upbringing she treasured.

Aaron McCullough, of Provo, said he only sees this project as a positive for the neighborhood and an
asset for the city.

Kirsten Zimmerman, of Provo, said she lives in a cul-de-sac very close to the Finch property, and
cherished the presence of the farm. The sounds of animals interacting in the mornings brought her joy.
The fact that there was still farmland in West Provo was something she valued. She hoped that the Finch
family could maintain their farm in a manner that suited their needs. She believes this goal was in
harmony with the city's regulations and could enhance the neighborhood while adding to the farm's
beauty.

Kathryn Blomberge, of Provo, stated she views this as a chance to preserve a special place. She
mentioned her involvement in the community activities hosted by the Finch family, such as her son's
horse-riding experience. These opportunities would not have been possible without the farm. Her
daughter was also learning horse work with Judy, an experience she had observed that morning. She
appreciated the hands-on learning and the extra benefits like being invited to pick blackberries. She
emphasized the importance of maintaining such experiences for those without farms, who could truly
benefit from them.

With no other public comments, Chair MacKay brought the discussion back to council.

Councilor Fillmore asked Mr. and Mrs. Finch to address some concerns that were emailed to council,
specifically noise complaints and security.

Councilor Whipple asked how they plan to balance farm life with an event space. She also asked if they
plan to continue to board horses.

The Finch’s responded that the event space is something that will be operated by the family together.
They said the horses that they have on the property are taken care of by their owners. They explained
their plan to upgrade some of the existing buildings that are visible and discussed which buildings would
be removed.

Councilor Whipple expressed her desire to maintain enough of the property to not impact the ability to
farm and preserve the land for agricultural type uses.

Chair MacKay shared she believes it would be more appropriate in an overlay zone.

     Motion:      Councilor Handley made a substitute motion to instruct staff to come back with a
                  proposal exploring the option of an overlay zone. Chair MacKay seconded.

Mr. Finch expressed his frustration at the goal post being moved.

Brian Jones, City Attorney, explained the purpose of overlay zones and shared the pros and cons of an
overlay zone were discussed in the work sessions.

Councilor Ellsworth asked for clarification on the motion.

Mr. Jones shared his understanding that the motion made by Councilor Handley was a substitute
motion. He also explained that the substitute motion implies that no action should be taken until staff
comes back with the alternative proposals.

Councilor Shipley shared the potential for a farm owner to engage in agribusiness as beneficial for the
city. However, he also considered the concerns raised by the staff regarding property use and
conditional permits. He acknowledged that conditional use permits might restrict decisions to specific
criteria, disregarding external factors. While he understood this could be frustrating for the applicant, he
believed in the staff's intention to safeguard the city's interests. He said that the overlay zone could
provide a middle ground, allowing the protection of agribusiness and farmers while offering more
nuanced decision-making tools. He expressed his support for the substitute motion.

Councilor Whipple emphasized the importance of getting things right to ensure protection for the
property and other aspects. She believes that rushing could lead to failures and preferred a careful
approach. She suggested that alignment with city staff was crucial, considering the applicant's security
measures and operational costs. She expressed concern about imposing additional conditions that might
strain the business's viability. She said an overlay could be a better solution and she does not want to
penalize their enthusiasm by imposing unnecessary conditions.

Councilor Shipley added that timing should be considered for the applicant’s sake.

Chair MacKay called for a vote on the substitute motion.

4.         An ordinance amending the General Plan to adopt the Conservation and Resiliency Plan.
           Citywide Application. (PLGPA20210367) (1:31:55)

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2023-34, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Hannah Salzl, Planner and Sustainability Coordinator presented the plan.

Chair MacKay opened public comment.

Tim Jeffick, BYU Student, shared his enthusiasm and gratitude as a BYU student and future-oriented
individual. He admired the factual foundation of the plan, praising Hannah and her team for their
extensive efforts in ensuring its effectiveness. He believes that local action drives broader change,
making Provo a shining example. He anticipates contributing to the Citizens to Sustainability Council and
supporting the initiative. He encouraged the council to approve the plan, celebrating their role in its
development.

Don Jarvis, Sustainability Advisor, highlighted the significance of the changes he had witnessed for
Provo. He acknowledged that the sustainability committee had played a role in the plan but stressed
that it was not solely their work. Hannah, the driving force behind the plan, collaborated extensively
with every department and gathered input which enhanced existing efforts. The plan had evolved into a
consensus-based program, reflecting a collective desire to create positive change. He expressed
appreciation for the city council's review and thanked everyone who contributed to the plan's
development.

Isak Blomberge, said he lived in Sweden, where clean energy and sustainability are prioritized in the
energy sector. He acknowledged that his long-term residence in Provo was not certain, but if he did
decide to stay, it would be due to the city's commitment to sustainability. He expressed his hope that
sustainability would receive significant attention and become a central direction for the community.

Sandra Shumway, of Provo, shared her concern that many residents had not been made aware of this
plan.

Chair MacKay closed public comment. She explained that the plan did not enforce mandates on citizens.
She said instead, it aimed at education and aligning departmental goals. Any significant changes like
altering water pricing or implementing mandates would undergo an individual voting process with
public input. She highlighted the extensive citizen involvement in the plan's development, including
surveys and public input sessions facilitated by a citizen committee.

Councilor Fillmore thanked Mr. Jarvis and the sustainability committee for their hard work on this plan.
He also explained that this plan is aspirational.

Councilor Whipple asked Ms. Salzl when the final numbers and statistics would be added into the
document.
