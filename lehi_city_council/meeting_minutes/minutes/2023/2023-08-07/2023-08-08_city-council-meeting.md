119   8. Consideration of Ordinance #42-2023 the Fox General Plan Amendment from Medium
120      Density Residential to Light Industrial on 2.73 acres located at 501 & 535 North 500
121      West.
122      Brent Fox was the applicant.
123
124       Councilor Koivisto was hesitant to support the change, emphasizing the city’s investment in
125       the general plan and expressed concerns about potential impacts on home values.
126
127       There was discussion about the potential uses allowed in light industrial zoning and concerns
128       were raised about the impact on adjacent properties. Kim Struthers explained that the light
129       industrial zone permits more intense commercial uses, including storage units, outdoor
130       storage yards, printing and publishing facilities, and other heavy commercial activities.
131
132       Motion:     Councilor Koivisto moved to deny this Ordinance with the finding that the
133                   proposed amendment would have a negative affect on the character of the
134                   surrounding area and is not consistent with the goals and policies of the general
135                   plan. Councilor Albrecht seconded the motion.
136
137       Roll Call Vote: Councilor Albrecht, Yes; Councilor Condie, No; Councilor Hancock, No;
138       Councilor Koivisto, Yes; and Councilor Southwick, No. The motion failed with 3 opposed and
139       2 in favor.
140
141       Councilor Condie asked the applicant some clarifying questions about the business’s
142       intended use on this property. Mr. Fox stated that he would like to use the property for
143       equipment storage.

201   10. Consideration of Ordinance #43-2023 the Dry Creek Village Zone Change on 16 acres
202      of property located at approximately 1450 South 1100 West, changing the zoning from
203      TH-5 (transitional holding) to Mixed Use.
204       Tony Trane was representing the applicant. He presented their plan for a mixed-use
205      development on the property near the school, emphasizing a combination of single-family
206      homes, townhomes, and potentially some condos. He highlighted the desire to create a
207      walkable community with trails connecting various amenities like the school, park, and
208      commercial areas. He mentioned their efforts to address traffic concerns and suggested
209      potential solutions, such as a temporary construction connection over to 1700 West and
210      improved connectivity.
211
212       Council members raised questions about lot sizes, density, and the potential impact on nearby
213       residents. Concerns were expressed about the traffic situation and the overall impact on the
214       neighborhood. The developers acknowledged the traffic issues and mentioned ongoing
215       discussions with city staff to find solutions.
216
217       The Strains, neighboring property owners, wrote their concerns about property boundaries and
218       felt that their issues hadn't been adequately addressed. The developers explained that they had
219       discussed these matters with the Stains but emphasized that a resolution would depend on the
220       approval of the zoning change.
221
222       The council expressed varying opinions on density, zoning, and the overall plan. Some
223       members were cautious about the potential impact on traffic and property values, while others
224       saw the mixed-use development as an opportunity for a balanced and walkable community.
225       The discussion highlighted the complexity of balancing development goals with concerns from
226       neighboring residents.
