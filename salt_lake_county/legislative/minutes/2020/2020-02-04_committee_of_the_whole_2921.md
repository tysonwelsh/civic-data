3.6 Verification of 72,000 Salt Lake County Signatures 
Regarding Referendum on Taxes
20-0086
Attachments: Staff Report
Ms. Sherrie Swensen , County Clerk, stated her office received 2,032 
packets containing over 86,000 signatures regarding the referendum on taxes .  
It was the responsibility of the Clerk ’s Office to verify those signatures .  
Staff was called on to work extended hours from 7:00 a.m. to 7:00 p.m., and 
temporary staff was hired to help accomplish this task before the deadline .  
On January 28, 2020, when the count was called off, there was a total of 
46,347 verified signatures. Salt Lake County needed 43,211 signatures to get 
this tax increase placed on the ballot.   On January 28, 2020, Governor Gary 
Herbert repealed the law, and on January 29, 2020, it was decided to no 
longer verify signatures  because there was no need to continue in order to 
repeal a law that no longer existed.
Council Members Snelgrove, Ghorbani, and Newton stated this was put

3.7 Legislative Update 20-0099
Ms. Kara Trevino ,  Intergovernmental Relations Manager, Council Office, 
reviewed the following legislative bills and proposals.
-S.B. 83 Voter Registration Information Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this bill allows political parties or candidates to have access to voter 
information that is marked private.  The Clerk ’s Office and the Clerk ’s affiliates oppose 
this bill.
Ms. Sherrie Swensen , County Clerk, stated this bill would undo all the previous bills 
passed to protect personal information of a registered voter.  The bill would require the 
Lieutenant Governor to send out a letter to all of those who have classified their record as 
private giving them 30 days to sign an affidavit to keep their record private if they are a 
member of law enforcement, member of the armed forces, federal, state, or local elected 
official, are protected by a protective order, and /or reside with an individual who is a 
victim of domestic violence or is likely to be a victim.   
Council Member Snelgrove stated this bill would discourage people from participating 
in the electoral process. 
A motion was made by Council Member Snelgrove, seconded by Council Member 
Jensen, to oppose SB 83.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.  
-S.B. 35 Circuit Breaker Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this bill modifies the qualifications for circuit breaker tax relief by 
changing the consumer price index to adjust annual income qualifications.
A motion was made by Council Member DeBry, seconded by Council Member Jensen, to 
support S.B. 35.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.

-H.B. 32 Crisis Services Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this bill creates a behavioral health receiving center pilot program to 
provide mental health crisis services. It would also establish a statewide warm line. 
Mr. Tim Whalen , Director, Behavioral Health Services, stated the State is looking to 
implement a statewide warm line similar to what Salt Lake County has done.  If this bill 
passes, the State would pay for this statewide, resulting in a savings for the County. 
A motion was made by Council Member DeBry, seconded by Council Member Jensen, to 
support S.B. 32.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
-H.B. 35 Mental Health Treatment Access Amendments
Mr. Whalen stated this bill requires the Forensic Mental Health Coordinating Council to 
study the long -term need for adult patient beds at the state hospital.  It also allocates $ 4.8 
million in on-going funding to open a 50-bed forensic unit at the state hospital.
Council Member Jensen asked for a definition of forensic in this text.
Ms. Darcy Goddard , Deputy District Attorney, stated the term forensic patient is for an 
individual who needs restoration services before they can stand trial. In the past, if a 
forensic patient was found to be non -restorable, the state hospital allowed the District 
Attorney to keep the individual in a forensic bed even though the commitment was 
changed to a civil commitment. This is no longer allowed due to the Disability Law Center 
settlement.  These non -restorable individuals must be moved to a civil bed.  Once an 
individual is in a civil bed, then Medicare requires a discharge plan for them.   
A motion was made by Council Member Jensen, seconded by Council Member DeBry, to 
support H.B. 35.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
-H.B. 38 Substance Use and Healthcare Amendments
Ms. Goddard stated this bill directs the State Medicaid Office to apply for a waiver at the 
federal level to allow for the use of Medicaid funds to provide services for inmates in their 
last 30 days of the stay in jail.  This bill would also provide up to a $ 10,000 tax credit for 
people who were willing to work 30 hours or more a week in a correctional facility. It is

correctional facility. 
A motion was made by Council Member DeBry, seconded by Council Member Jensen, to 
support H.B. 38.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
-Behavioral Health Workforce Reinvestment Proposal (not numbered yet)
Mr. Whalen stated this bill provides a funding mechanism to address the critical shortage 
of mental health providers in Utah.  Among other incentives, it would provide $ 10,000 to 
graduate students (upfront) who commit to three years of service working in a private, 
non-profit, or a governmental sector job.
A motion was made by Council Member Ghorbani, seconded by Council Member 
Jensen, to support this bill.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
-H.B. 133 Trail Improvement Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this bill would reinstate the ability to use eminent domain for certain 
types of trails.  The trail must be part of a “regionally significant trail system” and included 
in the master plan.  In the past, the County Council has supported the use of eminent 
domain for trails.
Mr. Martin Jensen , Director, Parks & Recreation Division, stated one of his division ’s 
core services is to provide regional trails.  This would be another tool in the toolbox to 
use on regional trail development if needed.
Council Member DeBry  stated he thought the use of eminent domain to acquire trails 
was wrong.  Ownership of property is a fundamental right in the United States.
Council Member Jensen stated the County has used eminent domain rarely in the 20 
years that he has been on the Council.  This is just a good tool to help when acquiring 
land for trails. 
A motion was made by Council Member Bradley, seconded by Council Member 
Snelgrove, to support H .B. 133.  The motion carried by a vote of 8 to 1, with Council 
Member DeBry voting in opposition.

A motion was made by Council Member Ghorbani, seconded by Council Member

-S.B. 37 Electronic Cigarette and other Nicotine Product Amendments
Mr. Gary Edwards , Director, Salt Lake County Health Department, stated this bill puts 
an excise tax on electronic cigarettes.  The tax would be used to fund the enforcement, 
education, and some broader substance abuse intervention efforts in public safety.
A motion was made by Council Member DeBry, seconded by Council Member Jensen, to 
support S.B. 37.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
-H.B. 23 Tobacco Retailer Amendments
Mr. Edwards stated this bill clarifies what a tobacco retailer is.  When the federal 
government raised the age to 21, it did not change the federal rule to that same age .  
Utah’s law is tied to the federal rule, which has caused enforcement problems statewide.

-Salt Lake County Inland Port Appointments (not numbered yet)
Council Member Newton stated current statute allows the Salt Lake County Mayor to 
appoint an individual to the Inland Port Authority, but it does not include advice and 
consent from the County Council.  The statute also specifically lists “the director of the 
Salt Lake County Office of Regional Economic Development” to serve on this board .  
She asked that the Council take a position to support amending these appointments to say 
the County Mayor gets two appointments, with the advice and consent from the County 
Council. 
Mayor Jennifer Wilson  stated she would be opposed to any changes to this bill.  This 
is a highly complex issue and she did not want to take the chance of Salt Lake County 
losing its two seats at the table.  Also, she would like more information and discussion 
before any recommendation is made. 
Council Member Bradshaw stated he would support this amendment if the County ’s 
legislative team proceeded with caution.  
Council Member Newton stated she would be willing to not change the one 
appointment relating to the director of the Salt Lake County Office of Regional Economic 
Development. However, she still wanted to include the advice and consent of the Council

A motion was made by Council Member Newton, seconded by Council Member DeBry, 
to add with the advice and consent of the Council and to direct the County ’s legislative 
team to proceed with caution with this change.  If the legislative team felt the Legislature 
would be open to make the change to the Board then to proceed.  The motion carried 
with a vote of 5 to 3 to 1, with Council Members Granato, Bradley, and Ghorbani voting

- Mountain View Corridor from 10800 South going south through 13400 South
- Bacchus Highway U111 through Magna

review.  
Mayor Wilson stated instead of asking for specific projects, the lobby team could just
