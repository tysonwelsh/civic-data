Matt Dahl said the Midvale City Council is being asked to consider the approval of a
special bond election that, if successful, would generate $1 1.2 million to finance a portion
of the costs to construct a new recreation center, including a community pool, and active
transportation trails. lf proposed resolution is approved by the City Council, the residents
of Midvale would vote on the bond on November 21 ,2023.

Due to popular support and on-going interest among residents, the City has sought to
construct a recreation center and pool in Midvale for many years. Midvale's current
recreation center, Copperview Recreation Center, is one of the County's oldest recreation
facilities. lt has served the community for over 40 years, but it is nearing the end of its
reasonable use. A new recreation center with a community pool, increased sports
programming, and community spaces would support residents of all ages and abilities
and build a stronger sense of community.

ln preparation for City Council's deliberation on the ballot proposition, Midvale City hired
Wilkinson Ferrari & Co. (WFC) to engage the community by gathering feedback and
opinions on recreation needs, funding options, and other related information. As part of
that work, an online survey and a focus group were conducted. (The addition of the $1 .2
million to the proposed bond for active transportation trails occurred after the survey was
issued and was not factored into the results but was tested with the focus group.) Between
June 1 and July 9,2023,562 residents responded to the survey. This is the largest
number of respondents for a survey conducted by Midvale City. Among the respondents,
86% responded that they were "very happy" or "happy" about pursuing a new recreation
center and 73o/o werc "very happy" or "happy" about the proposed funding solution (i.e.,
bond proposition). The most requested amenity among the participants was a community
pool. Among those that were opposed to the recreation center or the proposed funding
plan, the increase in taxes was cited as the primary reason for opposition.

A recreation center with the above listed amenities is estimated to cost $40-50 million, an
amount that Midvale would not be able to fund with existing resources. The proposed
bond would have a maximum principal amount of $1 1.2 million and would have a
maximum term of 30 years. $10 million would be used to fund the construction of a
recreation center and $1 .2 million would be used for active transportation trails in the City.
lf the bond is approved by the voters, the City would not issue the bonds until the balance
of the cost for the proposed facilities are funded by other sources. The primary source
that Midvale will seek to fund the balance of the construction cost for the recreation center
is Salt Lake County's Zoo, Arts, & Parks Program (ZAP). ZAP has been used by Salt Lake
County to fund the construction of recreation centers in other communities, most recently
                                               9

in Draper. The new round of ZAP funding will be awarded in 2025. Midvale City staff
believes that the odds of receiving ZAP funding will be improved if we include the
availability of the proposed $10 million in bond funding in our grant application. The
balance of the funding for the trails will be provided through the Federal 2023 Raise Grant.
Midvale City has recently been awarded $6,050,849 through the Raise Grant program
but is required to provide $1 .2 million in matching funding. lf the proposed bond is
approved it will fund Midvale City's matching requirement. lt is estimated that if approved,
the property taxes from the proposed bond would increase by $48.80 per year for the
median residential property in Midvale ($438,000) and $88.74 per year for commercial
properties of a similar value.
