27   2. Presentations and Reports
28   2.1. Valley Visioning Update Presentation- Ryan Beck, Envision Utah
29   Ryan Beck, with Envision Utah was there to present on the Envision Utah project. He stated that
30   they completed phase one of the plan where they gathered public input. They also did a random
31   sample survey and found it to be similar to the public input that was provided. Scenario B and C
32   were the most popular. They will continue to work with city staff as they prioritize and plan for
33   Utah Valley’s future.
34
35   2.2. Review and Discussion of City Naming Rights Policy
36   Marlin Eldred stated that they now have a draft before them to consider for a naming rights policy.
37   He asked if this should be an administrative policy or if it should be in Code. He stated that this is
38   more of a guiding policy to handle naming rights for city assets such as a park bench or playground.
39   He said that anything over a $500,000 commitment would need to be approved by the Council. He
40   wanted to be sure that the Council had the right to refuse a promotion and use discretion as allowed
41   in the policy. The Council reached consensus that this should be an administrative policy and that
42   they would bring it back to the Council for final approval with a Resolution.
43
44   3. Agenda Questions
45   Councilor Condie inquired if they were approving the final design of the cycle track. Brad Kenison,
46   Assistant City Engineer, stated that they will be approving the contract for the company to design
47   it.

169   11. Consideration of Ordinance #52-2020, approval of a Development Code Amendment to
170       Section 12.080, amending the landscape buffer requirement (tabled from the September
171       8, 2020 meeting).
172       Jeff Southard was representing the applicant. He explained that in certain scenarios it may not
173       be necessary to have a buffering requirement so this would allow for the Planning Commission
174       to grant an exception to the buffering requirement if it was in an area that was zoned residential
175       but had a non-residential use. Councilor Albrecht expressed concerns with allowing any
176       exceptions to the buffering requirements. Mr. Southard said that a commercial use next to a
177       residence should have a buffer, but when there is a non- residential use, such as a detention
178       basin, then some buffering requirement may not be necessary. Councilor Koivisto expressed
179       concerns with changing the code. Councilor Hancock expressed concern with the lesser
180       noticing requirements for a code amendment.
181
182       Kori Fabian stated that this would help her to better utilize the property on a site that she has
183       her business located on. She said that in her situation she doesn’t see how this would have a
184       negative affect on the neighbors and believed that this would be beneficial to other areas as it
185       would allow for discretion.
186
187       Councilor Koivisto expressed concerns with changing the code that would affect the entire
188       city, and that maybe a development agreement would work best in a situation like this.
189
190       Mr. Struthers didn’t see it as opening a door that couldn’t be closed because the Planning
191       Commission still has discretion to allow for the exception or not. He stated that this still meets

244   14. Consideration of Ordinance #54-2020 approval of a General Plan amendment, changing
245       the designation on approximately 21-acres located at approximately 2100 North 2300
246       West from NC (neighborhood commercial) and LDR (low density residential) to C
247       (commercial) and MDR (medium density residential).
248       Dave Morton was representing the applicant. He stated that after meeting with residents, they
249       found that the biggest concern was traffic. He said that an access to 2100 North would help
250       alleviate the traffic concerns, so that is important for them to get that access. Also, they would
251       improve 2300 West to add a lane in lieu of density. He said that they sought input from the
252       neighbors. He said their goals were to create a mixed-use development integrating commercial
253       with residential, widen 2300 West, and provide an additional access to 2100 North. He showed
254       the Concept Plan that they presented to the Planning Commission and neighbors. He said they
255       would be willing to cap residential units at 95. He then noted the concerns from the
256       neighborhood which included the types of uses that could be in the Commercial zone, traffic,
257       high density, crosswalk access to North Point Elementary, concerns with additional traffic
258       through the neighborhood, and location of open space. He showed a bubble plan that was
259       produced by a resident, Lisa Foltz. He then presented a new Concept plan after receiving
260       comments from the neighbors.
261
262       Mr. Morton then reported on the traffic study that was conducted by Hales Engineering, which
263       showed the importance of the access to 2100 North. Councilor Koivisto noted that the access
264       to 2100 North is not a guarantee. Mr. Morton stated that they are working toward that access.
265       He said there is a strong reason for the access to be granted because it’s creating a safer
266       environment for this area. Mr. Struthers clarified that the City wants an additional access to
267       2100 North regardless of how the property is developed or the type of uses that end up there.
268       Councilors Albrecht and Koivisto expressed concerns with the access not being finalized yet.
269
270       Mr. Struthers explained that the Neighborhood Commercial nod indicated that the corners
271       could be commercial, but it doesn’t specify the exact acreage of the NC zone.
272
273       There was discussion about widening the road on 2300 West along their property. Mr. Struthers
274       stated that they would require any developer to widen 2300 West and the City would need to
275       reimburse the developer. There was also discussion about where the crosswalk could be added,
276       but that would need to be evaluated.
277
278       Mr. Morton stated that they would be willing to enter into a development agreement that would
279       specify the types of commercial uses that would and would not be allowed.
280
281       Mayor Johnson asked what the density bonus would be for widening and improving the road.
282       Mr. Struthers stated that a rough estimate was done and there is some discrepancy with what
283       the base density would be.
284
285       The Mayor asked for public comment.
