3.3 Update on Salt Lake County ’s Coordinated Response to 
COVID-19. 
The Council may Take Action, Including Votes, on any 
Necessary Legislative Matters Related to the Ongoing State 
of Emergency.
20-0349
Attachments: Staff Report
Presenters: Mayor Jenny Wilson, Jill Miller, Associate Deputy Mayor; Clint 
Mecham, Salt Lake County Emergency Manager; Gary Edwards, Director, 
Health Department (Approx. 30 mins.)
Informational
Mr. Gary Edwards , Director, Salt Lake County Health Department, 
updated the Council on the COVID -19 virus.  He stated there has been a 77 
percent increase in cases over the past 7 days.  The group hit the hardest is 
white males between the ages of 21-40.  There are 3,500 to 4,000 tests 
available per day. The results show that 5 percent of the people tested are 
coming back positive.   
Mr. Clint Mecham , Salt Lake County Emergency Manager, updated the 
Council on the 5.7 earthquake that happened on March 18, 2020.  He stated 
there has been over 550 aftershocks as of this morning.  Damage assessment 
is ongoing with more damage being found then initially thought.   Cyprus 
High School had a wall that partially collapsed that was in an area not 
normally occupied.  The building is still safe for occupancy.  West Lake 
Junior High had a wall collapse that was inside the normal occupancy range .  
This is a major concern and the building might be a write off at this point .  
There was minor damage to Fire Station 202 in Magna, but it can still be 
used.  There were 49 house trailers that slid off their foundations, all but one

3.4 COVID-19 Economic Impact Working Group Update 20-0345
Attachments: File ID 20-0345 Staff Report
Presenter: Dina Blaes, Director of Economic Impact Working Group (Approx. 
15 mins.)
Informational
Ms. Dina Blaes , Director, Economic Impact Working Group, updated the 
Council on the work that is being done relating to the economic impact of the 
COVID-19 virus.  She stated the working group started up two weeks ago; 
its purpose is to: 
1) Meet with experts and people of different interest groups to discuss what 
everyone is seeing as well as to review new policies from the State and 
Federal Government. 
2) Develop a clearing house by collecting information about different 
resources that are available not just for businesses as well as housing.  This 
information is included on the County website.  A business relief hotline was 
launch today to provide small businesses with additional resources that might 
be available.  
3) Coordinate with local municipal economic development directors to see 
what measures they are taking within their municipalities to help lessen the 
impact, such as waiving fees for developments. 
 
4) Look at areas within the small business sectors to make sure that all gaps 
are being filled.  The County is also looking to restructure its small business 
loans to help those businesses affected by the COVID-19 virus.   
Council Member Ghorbani stated she was concerned with the number of 
people that are unemployed and looking at being evicted. 
Ms. Kerri Nakamura , Chief of Staff, Mayor ’s Office, stated the Mayor

The Governor would have to act on any other reasons for eviction.
Ms. Blaes stated the group is also looking at how utility companies are 
going to handle deferred payments.  Once the information is gathered, it will 
be on the County's website. 
Council Member Burdick  asked what was being done with the homeless 
population, including the unsheltered homeless population.
Ms. Erin Litvack , Operations Section Chief, updated the Council on what 
was being done to help the homeless population.  She highlighted the 
following areas:
1) The health care providers for the homeless populations were educated on 
how to best handle the clients that reside in the resource centers. The Health 
Department is working with the medical providers to assign personal liaisons 
to help educate and answer questions relating to the virus.  
2) Starting today, four street teams are going out into the community to 
locate the unsheltered homeless to check on them and educate them as well .  
The teams are made up of experts in many different areas so they can help 
this population.  
3) The County is working with the health care providers to get them the 
medical equipment such as gloves and masks, which they need.  It is also 
encouraging providers to put together their Continuity of Operations Plans 
(COOP).  
 
4) The 4th Street Medical Clinic tests the homeless population for the virus 
and provides quarantined and isolation shelters if needed. 
Council Member Burdick stated he would like an additional update next 
week on the homeless situation as well as what is happening at the jail.
