County Planner Jennifer Strader presented the staff report and indicated the location of the 
property in the Rural Residential Zone.  She noted that the entire property is located within the 
Lower Silver Creek Overlay Zone, which is required to receive a clean bill of health from the 
EPA prior to any development occurring.  The Code Amendment would create a new Mixed Use 
Zone intended for medium- to high-density mixed uses built around mass transit areas that would 
allow for pedestrian development.  The applicant based the density on current zoning, which 
allows for 20 residential units.  The applicant assumed each residential unit would be about 
20,000 square feet, which they converted to 400,000 square feet.  The rezone proposal would be 
for 46 acres, and the remaining property, about 90% of the property, would be left as open space.  
The language for the zone would also address parking, setbacks, height, etc.  She presented plans 
submitted with the application and a conceptual site plan. 
 
Rory Murphy, the applicant, explained that he is working on behalf of the Florence J. Gillmor 
estate.  He stated that the subject property consists of about 455 acres of land divided into four 
parcels and is located off of the Silver Creek Interchange.  It is currently used for grazing cattle 
and growing hay and is bisected by the Silver Creek drainage and the Rail Trail.  It includes 
about 95 acres of wetlands that are impacted by contaminated by mine tailings from upstream 
point sources and are subject to the environmental mitigation planned for the entire Silver Creek 
drainage. 
 
Brooke Hontz, representing the applicant, provided photographs of the site and described the 
location from which the photographs were taken. 
 
Mr. Murphy explained that they would like to have a discussion with the Commission tonight 
and get their ideas about future development of the property.  He stated that they have worked 
with Staff and followed their suggestions in preparing this draft proposal.  Conversations with 
Staff have yielded some promising ideas that could significantly benefit the County, and he 
explained that the site plans in the packet are just a place to start.  Under the current zoning, they 
could divide this property into 20 large-acre lots, but they do not want to do that, and Mrs. 
Gillmor’s estate supports the proposed concept.  He recalled that Mrs. Gillmor was very active in 
Snyderville Basin Planning Commission

local land conservation efforts and donated several large tracts of land.  The estate and 
foundation have a fiduciary duty to the estate and need to have the land perform reasonably 
economically while preserving the bulk of the property for the enjoyment of the public and the 
benefit of the County.  They would preserve the vast majority of the property as open space and 
concentrate development in this one area that is bordered by relatively high intensity uses.  He 
stated that none of the area they propose for development is affected by contaminated soils.  He 
explained that they originally approached Staff with the idea of rezoning this area to industrial.  
Commercial development carries a requirement for affordable housing, so they were really 
looking at a mixed-use development, and there was no Code mechanism to do that.  Staff was 
open to their ideas and suggested that this site might be a good place to solve some issues 
currently facing the surrounding communities.  They suggested that the applicant propose a zone 
that would allow for the type of development they would like, which resulted in the draft 
Development Code language in the staff report. 
 
Mr. Murphy explained that the residential component of the proposed development would allow 
for a wide range of socio-economic residents.  The parking would be underground or 
accommodated by parking structures, and they would not propose big-box retail with a sea of 
parking.  They will try to accommodate commuter parking for mass transit on Highway 248, and 
the entire development would be pedestrian oriented and incorporate a series of hiking trails.  
They would incorporate a supermarket in the range of 45,000 to 55,000 square feet and focus on 
serving the residential areas surrounding the development rather than attracting regional traffic.  
The tax base generated by this project would benefit the County and South Summit School 
District, and he noted that the proposed development is entirely within the South Summit School 
District.  The large size of the property would allow for active open space, such as playing fields, 
and they would work with the Recreation District to incorporate property for recreation uses.  
They would also like to incorporate a parcel for a non-profit partner and are currently working 
with Ballet West to establish an academy.  They would consider a cemetery site if that is a need 
determined by the County.  He believed this could tie into the existing uses to establish a more 
cohesive neighborhood in the east part of the Snyderville Basin and reduce regional traffic trips.
