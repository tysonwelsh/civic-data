  access to a single access, and the building architecture fit with the surrounding residential
  community. He also reviewed applicable Community Policies, indicating the proposed rezone
  will not create isolated properties and edges can be created to provide a screen between the
  building and the residential area. Staff recommendation is for approval of the rezoning to C-
  3/CO so long as the items in the existing development agreement are included in the new
  development agreement for the addition.

  Mr. Paul Kellos, 3440 Ogden Avenue, asked how close the building will be to the property line,

6. Conditional Use Permit, for Waste Transfer Station at 3027 Midland Drive. (Attachment 6)

  Mr. Stratford explained while the current Ordinance does not allow waste transfer stations, the
  applicant had actually made an application in 2014 at which time the use was listed as a
  conditional use in the M-2 zone. Because of that application, this item is being considered
  according to the code which was in effect in 214 relating to waste transfer stations.

  Mr. Tyler Cahoon, representing David Rawson and Recycled Earth stated this item had been
  before the Commission in January and had been tabled pending additional information to be
  provided. Since that time, the owner has conducted a traffic impact study for the area and has
  submitted a new landscaping plan, site plan and operation plan outlining how the waste
  transfer station is anticipated to operate. He stated the owner has reviewed the
  recommendations of Staff and finds them acceptable and is requesting approval of the use
  permit.

  Mr. Montgomery stated this project involves a 10-acre facility on Midland Drive and was tabled
  on January 20th. The operation plan identifies two phases, with the first to be for construction
  of the transfer station building and an additional building as a loading area which would be at
  a lower grade, with future phases to include another building to the west as the need arises.
  The proposed landscaping plan includes the installation of an additional 354 trees as well as a
  25’ landscaped buffer around the property. These items are important as they reduce the
  visual impact to the property and also help clean the air and reduce the potential of wind-
  blown items to escape the site. He reviewed the circulation patterns within the site, stating
  vehicles will enter from Midland Drive and be directed to either the recycle area or the transfer
  station building. A traffic study has been submitted which anticipates with the new Midland
  Drive extension, it will accommodate traffic to the site. He reviewed the building design
  indicating the proposed two-tone building with overhead doors and a roof overhangs on both
  the front and sides and identified where signage is proposed. Building materials should be the
  same on all four sides. The traffic study indicates the level of service for Midland Drive will
  accommodate potential traffic to the site. He briefly reviewed the operation plan, stating the
  scale will remain at is current location as there was no requirement for a setback at the time
  the recycling use was approved and continues as a nonconforming right, but Staff is
  suggesting additional trees be planted to screen the scale area from the view to property to
  the north.

8. Final Group Dwelling Approval, for Riverside 16 Development at 1285 16th Street. (Attachment 8)

  Mr. Chaz Schlang stated he is seeking final approval for this group dwelling project, He stated
  the Commission had previously granted preliminary approval and he has been working with
  City Staff to satisfy the ordinance requirements.

  Mr. Simpson stated this is a proposal for a 21-unit group dwelling project. In granting final
  approval, the Commission is to determine whether the conditions of preliminary approval are
  or can be satisfied and whether the project satisfies the regulations of the Group Dwelling
  Ordinance. He first identified items which are now shown on the site plan, including defined
  walkways, a charging station, street trees and an entry feature with a tree preservation plan,
  and a masonry fence along Harrison Boulevard. He stated there has been concern relating to
  trees on the site which have been removed, but the owner has been working with the City
  Urban Forester who had approved the removal of those trees, and that other work had been
  done do reduce fire hazard potential. The final plan also identifies details for recreational
  amenities as well as fencing details.

  He then reviewed the building elevations, stating entryways need to be better defined, and
  suggested the enclosed front patio be eliminated replaced with an entry feature. In addition, a
  wall projections of 2’ or more need to be shown for 20% of the buildings, and roof variations
  need to be provided. He stated the applicant has now provided an elevation design which
  shows a mix of shed dormers and gable dormers and porch roofs which extend beyond the
  wall projections. Staff recommendation is for approval subject to the following conditions:
  1. The buildings incorporate the required entry porches for each unit by providing porches
      that are open on three sides and approved by Planning Staff;
  2. The front entrances be defined and approved by Planning Staff;
  3. The required 20% glass coverage on the front of the main level of the building be met and
      approved by Planning Staff;
  4. Variations in the rooflines be provided, and approved by Planning Staff
  5. Details of the required plantings around the entry sign be provided on the landscaping plan

9. Public Hearing, Petition to Amend Zoning Ordinance, to remove the restriction of prohibiting
   painting brick in the East Central Community. (Attachment 9)

  Ms. Kennedy Rogers stated this petition was initiated by a code violation letter indicating the
  City does not allow brick to be covered with paint in this community. She stated the owner d
  p[purchased the property in January 2021 with the intent to improve the property. He had
  hired a professional to clean and treat the brick, at which time it was found there were many
  cracks and graffiti on the brick. Mr. Rogers had visited the City website which suggested curb
  appeal might be improved with a fresh coat of paint. As there are no permits required for the
  painting of brick, he proceeded and the brick was painted. After receiving the violation notice,
  he contacted a restoration company to chemically remove the paint. After doing a test patch,
  it was found the exterior of the brick is a soft clay and is the paint removal would compromise
  the integrity of the brick. This request is to amend the code to allow paint to not be removed in
  situations such as this where the brick surface is compromised.

  Mr. Montgomery stated the petition intent is to amend the ordinance to allow exterior brick to
  be painted when the brick has either been damaged or is deteriorating. He showed photos of
  the home owned by Mr. Rogers where brick is chipped and damaged, as well as the test patch
  where the removal of paint was attempted.

  He presented information relating to the history of this regulation indicating in 2005 the East
  Bench Historic District was created after two years of extensive research documenting the
  history of the area. The National District designation encourages the preservation of homes


                                                                                   Page 12 of 18
 by offering historic tax credits for the renovation of homes. Then in 209-2010 the East Central
 Community Plan was adopted after much citizen input. Objectives of that plan indicate the
 character of the historic area should be protected. As a result of the East Central Plan, the
 Zoning Ordinance was amended to create specific design standards for the community which
 are applicable in the R-2EC, R-3EC and R-1-5 zones within the community.

 He identified reasons which may justify brick to be painted as (1) to change the color of the
 building; (2) to cover flaws: or (3) to preserve brick if the hardened surface is deteriorating. The
 current trend is that painting brick improves the curb appeal of homes but is not reason to
 justify painting. Concerns with painted brick are that it affects the exterior materials and
 character of the historic district, it changes contrast of textures between mortar and brick, it
 can speed deterioration of brick if not done properly, additional maintenance is added, and it
 removes the ability to obtain historic tax credits.

 He showed sections of the community and identified there have been some brick homes
 which were painted prior to the regulation being adopted, each being limited to around 25% of
 the overall community. He also presented photos of homes where paint has been removed as
 a result of code enforcement, indicating the removal of paint is costly. He presented
 questions for consideration of the Commission as follows:
• Is the overall intent of the regulation still a valid objective for the East Central Community?
• Are there certain conditions that brick could be covered?
• What should be the course of action when painting is done and removal will damage the
  brick?
• What is an equitable treatment for people who clean brick after receiving notice of violation
  and those who do not?

  He stated he also had contacted the State Preservation Office and found tax credits are only
  available for buildings which were painted prior to being on the National District Register. He
  asked the Commission to consider whether a different standard should be available when the
  intent of painting brick is for its preservation rather than for cosmetic or trendy reasons. He
  also identified other methods to preserve brick such as a clear coat finish which does not
  mask the original brick, replacement of damaged brick.

  Commission discussed colors of brick, the process for applying for historic tax credits. It
  was felt the intent for the regulation is still valid and care should be taken to preserve the
  history of the community and the integrity of the original brick structures. Mr. Montgomery
  discussed the public process for both the creation of the national district and the review of
  the East Central Community Plan, stating both had received much public input. The
  Community Plan Steering Committee felt it important to improve the historic character of the
  area by adopting standard. While there had been some education efforts at that time, as
  homes change hands rapidly, many are now unaware of the regulations. Discussion also
  centered on the limited colors now being proposed for any new construction, indicating there
  is little variety, and many of the colors now as well as the trend for painting brick has no basis
  rather than being a current trend.

  Commissioner Schade indicated the Landmarks Commission had been informed of the

                                                                                    Page 13 of 18
   petition and had expressed concern and opposition to the regulation being changed. They
   voiced that most work is done on weekends when there are no code enforcement officers
   available and police do not respond for code violations. He stated they are very concerned
   that the historic brick homes in the East Central community need to be protected.

   Mr. Montgomery asked the Commission to consider whether there may be certain conditions
   where painting brick is the only solution for the brick to be preserved, and asked if an in-depth
   analysis were to determine the brick is compromised but could be preserved if painted,
   whether there could be an exception to the regulation. Another concern is that while some
   have gone to the expense of removing paint, others continue to pay fines, and the cost is not
   equitable and those that receive citations are never required to comply with the regulation.
   Removal of paint also is not always equitable as some brick has more dimension than others

   Chair Graf suggested some marketing efforts might be improved by involving the real estate

   Mr. Brett Rogers stated he was unaware of the regulation at the time the home was painted,

10. Public Hearing, Petition to Amend Southeast Ogden Community Plan, to remove requirement
  for connection of Navajo Drive at approximately 1500 E. Navajo Drive.

  Mr. Scott Larsen stated this petition is to eliminate the requirement for Navajo Drive to be
  connected to allow vehicular traffic, and while he is the primary petitioner, the petition also has
  been signed by other residents in the community. He described the history of the site, stating
  Navajo Drive has been a dead-end for 50 years, and the area has all been built out except for
  this property which had continued to be an estate and farmland. He stated traffic patters have
  been established and there is no need for vehicular traffic to extend further, but that a
  bike/pedestrian path has been established over time through this property. He desires to
  develop one or two new residential lots on the property and dedicate a walkway through the
  property. He stated as many students use the pathway to walk to school, introducing vehicle

                                                                                    Page 14 of 18
  traffic would create a safety hazard. He felt putting the street through would affect how
  development could occur and its limited length would be costly. He stated he is not intending
  to install a cul-de-sac and felt no additional utilities would be required.

  Mr. Montgomery stated the petition represents 34 households in the area, and the petition is
  to determine whether there is a need for an improved road to connect to the existing portion of
  Navajo. HE felt it is important to retain a pathway through the property to provide pedestrian
  connection to the park and school, He reviewed the development history of the area, stating
  the home was built in 1953 at which time the property had not been annexed into Ogden City.
  Property to the east was developed during the 1970’s and then Stone Mountain to the west
  was approved in 1990. At that time the previous owners were charged with constructing a
  road connection to Navajo if development occurred. Both the Subdivision Ordinance and the
  Southeast Ogden Plan indicate road connections are required as development occurs. In
  determining whether a plan amendment is appropriate, the Commission is to also consider
  what changes have occurred since the Plan was adopted as well as policies in the General
  Plan. He stated the General Plan encourages walking and less vehicle travel, with options to
  disperse vehicle concentration. The intent of the connection was to provide access from the
  neighborhood to the park and school as well as to disperse vehicular traffic. He stated Navajo
  does not provide a connection to other roads in the community, and that while road patterns in
  Shadow Valley are characterized by grades and curves, a connection is needed. Staff feels the
  scale of development might be considered a factor, indicating if limited to one or two lots, the
  development of a complete road might be cost prohibitive due to the existing slope. He
  suggested if more than two lots are to be developed, the road should be extended to connect
  but if two lots or less are developed, a pedestrian easement might be adequate. Staff
  recommends the Southeast Ogden Plan be amended to indicate if two or less lots are
  developed, a pedestrian easement is required, but if more than two lots are to be developed,
  the full street improvements would be required.

  Commissioner Schade asked about whether a cul-de-sac would be required and how the
  pedestrian trail would be developed. Mr. Montgomery responded the trail easement would be
  required with the development of the property, and that some grading would be required at the
  west end to assure it is walkable for everyone.

  Mr. Ian Franklin, Engineering, stated the expectation has been that either the road would

  pedestrian connectivity is necessary and the dedicated walkway would be required to satisfy
  ADA requirements. In addition, sewer and water connections will need to be worked out and
  all work would be required to satisfy Engineering standards.
