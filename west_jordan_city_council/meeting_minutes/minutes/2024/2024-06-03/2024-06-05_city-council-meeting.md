 4. PUBLIC COMMENT
     Chair Jacob opened the public comment period at 6:27 pm
     Comments:
     Paul Zuckerman from the Humane Society of the United States raised concerns about puppy
     mills. He highlighted issues such as lack of transparency about animal origins, absence of
     health records, warranties favoring stores, high prices, and predatory financing. He noted that
     many pets from these sources suffer from emotional and health problems, advocating for
     better consumer and animal protections.

     Amy Motta from Bailing Out Benji discussed a 2021 audit on USDA's handling of complaints
     about breeders. The audit revealed that about half of the complaints were mishandled. Despite
     USDA's claims that breeders meet their standards, most people consider them inhumane, with
     animals lacking proper space and enrichment. The USDA's breeder database is poorly
     managed and often requires manual record searches. While approximately 2,000 mills are
     USDA-licensed, it's believed there are over 10,000 unlicensed, highlighting a significant
     problem.

     Julie Barfuss expressed her disappointment to the council regarding a recent offensive and
     disturbing display at the public works building. She mentioned that she had sent emails to the
     council regarding the issue. Despite it being considered freedom of speech, she felt there were
     several violations and was unhappy with how it was handled, urging the council to do better.

     Chair Jacob closed public comment at 6:36 pm

          Mayor Burton praised the parks department for their beautification work at Pioneer Hall
          and announced plans to redo the parking area. He reported on improvements to the
          7125 S Trailer Court Landscape project, the completion of striping on 7800 South, and a
          major landscaping project at Station 55, thanking the firefighters for their efforts. He
          announced a partnership with UTA for the Western Stampede, where every event ticket
          gets a free ride on UTA, and sought feedback on the initiative. He highlighted community
          events such as food trucks at Ron Wood Park every Tuesday, a "Meet with the Mayor"
          session on June 6, a First Friday Concert on June 7 featuring local artists, and noted that
          city offices will be closed on June 17 in observance of Juneteenth.

           Mayor Burton left the meeting.

       d. City Administrator’s Report
          City Administrator Korban Lee announced a ribbon cutting ceremony for the opening of
          HMart this Friday, expressing excitement for the completion of the project.

           Utilities Manager Greg Davenport informed Council that every year, staff submits a
           report titled the Municipal Wastewater Planning Program (MWPP) Survey to the State
           of Utah Department of Environmental Quality. This report helps communities better
           understand wastewater systems and identify any issues, covering financial aspects like
           master plans, impact fees, and rate studies, as well as collection aspects like pipe
           inventory, capital plans, and overflow incidents.

           Chair Jacob thanked the Mayor and street staff for the newly added stripes on 7800
           South, noting the positive feedback he had received and appreciating their efforts on the
           project.

 7. BUSINESS ITEMS
       a. Resolution No. 24-025 authorizing the pick-up of certain public safety and
          firefighter employee retirement contributions.
          Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck oriented the council regarding the
          resolution authorizing the pick-up of contributions for public safety and firefighter
          employee retirement pensions. This action would increase the previously approved rate
          from 2.59% to 4.73%. The rate will remain in effect until URS requires a pickup rate that
          exceeds the approved rate.

           MOTION:     Council Member Green moved to APPROVE Resolution No. 24-025 authorizing
                       the pick-up of certain public safety and firefighter employee retirement
                       contributions.
                        Vice Chair Lamb seconded the motion.
           The vote was recorded as follows:
           YES:       Zach Jacob, Chad Lamb, Bob Bedore, Pamela Bloom, Kelvin Green, Kent
                      Shelton, Kayleen Whitelock
           NO:
           ABSENT:
           The motion passed 7-0.

       b. Presentation of the Annual Fraud Risk Assessment for FY 2024.
          Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck presented the Annual Fraud Risk
          Assessment, initiated in 2020. The city’s score remained high, missing only 20 points
          due to the absence of an internal auditor function.

           Council Member Green inquired about the necessity and benefits of gaining those 20
           points. Director Steck explained that the city’s score remained high comparable to most
           other cities and while assigning the role to a finance team member or hiring a suitable
           person could cover the points, efforts might be excessive and ultimately not impactful
           for the city.
