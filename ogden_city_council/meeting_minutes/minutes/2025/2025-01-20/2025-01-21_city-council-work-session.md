Ogden Dinosaur Park and Museum Annual Report
          Ogden's George S. Eccles Dinosaur Park is a community gathering space that includes museums, gardens, and art
installations. The 8.5-acre sculpture Park is filled with over 100 life-sized dinosaurs and the museum provides interactive learning
experiences. The Park includes the Elizabeth Dee Shaw Stewart Museum of Paleontology, Ogden Natural History Museum, and a
working paleontology lab. The museum offers regular Science, Technology, Engineering, Arts, and Mathematics (STEAM) events
throughout the year.
          Park Director Mejeur and Education Director Bond used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide the Dinosaur
Park and Museum Annual Report. The presentation touched on 2024 attendance, events and education, marketing and community
outreach, current building projects, grants and future projects, and a proposal to increase admissions rates in 2025. For a copy of
the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting. The Council and Mayor thanked the Park leadership for
the information provided tonight and for their efforts to build a meaningful relationship between the Park and Ogden City.

Proposed Rezone – 150 32nd Street
          Planning Director Brierley used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss with the Council the proposed rezone of
property at 150 32nd Street. David Anderson has submitted a petition to rezone the property – one acre in size – from C-2
(Community Commercial) to R-4 (Multiple-family Residential). The petitioner requests to establish zoning on the property to
allow development of single-family rowhouses. The subject property is vacant and is on the same commercial block as Big O
Tires, Dunk Lee Auto Sales, Penske Truck Rental, a small office building and a Rocky Mountain Power utility substation. The
property abuts the R-1-5 zone to the south, where there are several single-family residential developments with homes built
primarily in the early 1900s. The C-2 zone is a community commercial zone typically established in areas having access to major
streets and a low likelihood of conflict with other uses and should be an edge to residential development. The commercial zoning
in this area (C-2 and C-3) has been established primarily along Wall Avenue from 28th to 36th Streets. There is also a stretch of
C-3 zoning along 31st Street, connecting the commercial corridor along Wall Ave to the commercial corridor along Washington
Boulevard. In November of 2024 the Council approved a text amendment that prohibited new residential dwelling units in the C-
2/CP-2 and C-3/CP-3 zones. The R-4 zone is a multiple-family residential zone that allows for single-family, two-family, and
multiple-family dwellings. The R-4 zoning designation would potentially allow for 25 units on the subject property. The applicant
has proposed single-family rowhouses for the development type. The subject property is part of the Jefferson Community Plan.
The land-use map from the Jefferson Community Plan indicates that the M-1 (Limited Manufacturing) and C-2 zoning are
desirable zoning designations for the subject property. The staff report to the Planning Commission explains that although not
supported by the community plan’s land-use map, rezoning the property to R-4/CO is consistent with certain vision strategies of
the community plan including the desire for more homeownership opportunities. The Planning Division has explained that
approving the rezone with a conditional overlay will make the property more likely for development, and needed public
improvements like sidewalks would be installed sooner. The Planning Division has also explained that the R-4 zoning can help to
provide an appropriate transition and buffer between the existing R-1-5 zoning and the commercial zones in this neighborhood.
On November 6, 2024, the Planning Commission considered the request and recommended that the council approve the proposal
to rezone to R-4/CO. The Planning Commissioner who voted no explained that they felt the project does not meet the General
Plan, any future homes developed at the site would not be buffered from the adjacent commercial developments, the future
development would be an isolated neighborhood, the community amenities of the Jefferson Community are not reachable by foot,
and the area is unsafe for the play of children. There were no public comments about the proposal during the Planning
Commission meeting. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Discussion among the Council and staff centered on whether the proposed development can be harmonious with
surrounding commercial development and the level of detail in the concept plan for the development.

Fiscal Year 2023-2024 Consolidated Annual Performance and
Evaluation Report (CAPER)
         Community Development has requested to present the Consolidated Annual Performance Evaluation Report (CAPER)
for Fiscal Year 2023-2024. The report summarizes the Housing and Urban Development (HUD) funded accomplishments of
Ogden City Community and Economic Development Department for the Fiscal Year (FY) July 1, 2023 – June 30, 2024.
         Project Coordinator Fuentes used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the CAPER for FY24, which reports
on the City’s use of Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) and HOME grants, a summary of federally funded
accomplishments, and key interest points. The presentation also included detailed information and photos of various
purchase/rehabilitation/resale and infill housing projects in the City. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the
information packet for the meeting.
         Discussion between the Council and Ms. Fuentes centered on topics such as guidelines for the use of federal funds,
upcoming updates to the Annual Action Plan and Consolidated Plan, and upcoming projects.

Parks Utilization Study Survey
          Ogden City Recreation Division has contracted with consultants J-U-B Engineers to conduct a parks utilization study.
The purpose of the study is to assess how parks are currently being used and to recommend strategies for optimizing park
utilization. The goal is to help improve the efficiency, accessibility, and overall effectiveness of the City’s park space and
recreational facilities.
          Recreation Director Bridge stated that the Parks Utilization Survey period will end on February 3, 2025; it was launched
on December 20, 2024, and was sent to past recreation participants, Ogden School District families, Golden Hours members, and
area soccer participants. It was also advertised through the City’s newsletter, social media, the Marshall White Center (MWC)
Christmas Lunch, and with the assistance of community partners. Thus far, 516 survey responses have been received. He asked for
assistance from the Council in encouraging residents to participate in the survey. JUB Engineers plans to analyze the data over the
summer months and make a final recommendation and presentation to the Council in the fall of 2025.
          Discussion between the Council and Mr. Bridge centered on the questions included in the survey and the cross-over, if
any, of this survey and the survey specific to the 4 th Street baseball/softball complex.
