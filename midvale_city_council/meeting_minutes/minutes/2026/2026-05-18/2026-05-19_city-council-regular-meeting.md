        A. RECEIVE PUBLIC COMMENT REGARDING AMENDMENTS TO THE
           MIDVALE CITY FEE SCHEDULE AND UTILITY RATES FOR 2026
Mariah Hill said during the budget process and periodically throughout the fiscal year,
staff reviews and updates the municipal fee schedule to ensure the City is collecting
appropriate fees that reflect the actual cost of services provided.

This fee schedule amendment includes changes to the City’s previously adopted drought
rates. These changes can be found on page seven of the attached fee schedule. The
drought fees will continue to be based on the Jordan Valley Water Conservation District’s
Water Supply Availability Level Designation; the City is currently at a Level 2 designation.
The revised structure removes the charge for all Tier 1 water usage and adds additional
clarification regarding effective dates. The City operates two utility billing cycles:
    • Cycle 1: Billed on the 15th of each month
    • Cycle 2: Billed on the last day of each month

To ensure equitable application across both cycles, the drought fees will be effective:
   • Cycle 1: May 15 through October 15
   • Cycle 2: May 1 through September 30

Residents will first see these charges on their June utility bill.

A public hearing on these proposed changes will be held on May 19, 2026. Upon
approval, the amended FY2026 Fee Schedule will be posted on the Midvale City website.

FISCAL IMPACT
The fee schedule is structured to reflect the actual cost of providing services to Midvale
City residents, businesses, and other users. Fees are set at levels sufficient to generate
the revenue necessary to support the City’s Enterprise Funds and to recover certain costs
incurred by the General Fund for Fiscal Year 2026. Selected fee adjustments; however,
are specifically designed to encourage water conservation; if effective, reduced usage is
anticipated to offset higher rates, resulting in no net change to overall City revenue.

        A. DISCUSS REGARDING AN ORDINANCE ESTABLISHING THE
           COMMUNITY CLEAN ENERGY PROGRAM IN MIDVALE
Mayor Dustin Gettel said Midvale City is a member of the Utah Renewable Communities
(URC), a coalition of Utah municipalities and counties working in partnership with Rocky
Mountain Power (RMP) to develop and implement the Community Clean Energy Program
(CCEP). The goal of the program is to provide a net 100% renewable electricity option for
participating communities within the next several years. Midvale joined the URC through
an interlocal cooperation agreement in 2024, allowing the City to participate in program
design and negotiations with RMP. The Program is authorized under Utah’s Community
Clean Energy Act, and Rocky Mountain Power filed the Program application on behalf of
the URC. Following several years of development and regulatory review, the Utah Public
Service Commission (PSC) approved the Program on March 4, 2026.

The Program establishes a new clean energy option (from renewable energy resources)
for residents and businesses served by Rocky Mountain Power. Participation is structured
as an opt-out program, meaning eligible electric customers within participating
communities would be automatically enrolled, with the ability to opt out. The Program
includes an initial cancellation period, during which customers may exit the Program
without incurring a termination fee. After the cancellation period ends, customers may still
opt out at any time; however, a termination fee would apply, as approved by the PSC and
outlined in Program rules.

The PSC approved an initial residential flat rate of $4 per month, which is made up of
$3.88 per month plus a $0.12 per month low-income program surcharge. For all other
(non-residential) customer classes, the PSC approved a volumetric rate of $0.00609 per
kilowatt-hour (kWh) and a $0.12 per month low-income program surcharge. The low-
income surcharge collected from participating residential and non-residential customers
helps fund bill credits for income-qualified customers enrolled in Rocky Mountain Power’s
Home Electric Lifeline Program (HELP). These customers will receive a bill credit equal
to the $3.88 clean energy charge, allowing them to participate in the Program at no cost.
Program rates may be adjusted periodically, but no more than annually, consistent with
standard utility ratemaking practices The PSC indicated that rates are not expected to
increase significantly over time and may decline as administrative and reserve funds are
established.

State law requires that each participating community adopt a local ordinance within ninety
(90) days of PSC approval in order to make the Program available within its boundaries.
The deadline for ordinance adoption is June 2, 2026. If Midvale City adopts the
participation ordinance, the Program would be implemented locally, with customer notices
anticipated in late 2026 and Program charges beginning in early 2027. If the ordinance is

not adopted, the Program would not be available in Midvale, and the City would no longer
participate in the URC.

Staff has prepared a draft ordinance to establish the Community Clean Energy Program
in Midvale City for the City Council’s consideration. The Council has been asked to
provide direction to staff on whether to proceed by placing the draft ordinance on the next

        B. DISCUSSION OF FY2027 PROPERTY TAX IMPACT SCHEDULE.
Mariah Hill said curing the 2026 Utah Legislative Session, HB 236 – Truth-in-Taxation
Amendments was enacted, making several changes to Utah Code § 59-2-919 and 59-2-
924, which govern notice, disclosure, and public hearing requirements when a tentative
budget includes a proposed property tax rate increase, including municipal property tax
increases. These provisions are intended to promote transparency and ensure the public
is provided clear information regarding the potential impacts of a proposed tax rate prior
to final budget and tax rate adoption. Accordingly, the City is required to prepare and
present a Property Tax Impact Schedule that illustrates the estimated effect of the
proposed tax rate on typical residential and non-residential properties and present and
make it available to the public separate from other budget documents.

The Property Tax Impact Statement is included on this agenda as a discussion item to
satisfy the statutory requirement that it be presented at any meeting where the budget is
discussed. Its inclusion ensures compliance with state law and provides an opportunity
for the City Council and the public to review and understand the projected impacts of the
proposed tax rate.

The Property Tax Impact Schedule was adopted by the Midvale City Council on May 5,
2026. This action does not finalize the property tax rate. Final consideration and adoption
