2.     DECLARE AS SURPLUS POLICE SERVICE DOG ‘KILO’, TRANSFERRING OWNERSHIP TO
       DEPUTY BRETT LAWRENSON FOR CARE, AND RELEASING THE UTAH COUNTY
       SHERIFF’S OFFICE OF ALL RESPONSIBILITY FOR AND CLAIM TO KILO
(Track A, 04:55)
Utah County Sheriff James O. Tracy came forward with Deputy Brett Lawrenson and police service dog ‘Kilo.’
Sheriff Tracy explained Kilo has served faithfully for seven years, but has no monetary value at this point and
cannot be traded to another organization because his age and medical condition will not allow him to continue the
work. Sheriff Tracy reviewed highlights of Kilo’s record, including assisting in 101 arrests of criminals and 438
conclusive narcotics searches. Sheriff Tracy pointed out there could be liability for the county if Kilo were adopted
to a non-law-enforcement family that is not trained in handling police dogs. Commissioner Lee asked about the cost
of the canine, and Sheriff Tracy explained it is approximately $10,000 to purchase. This does not include the
service, training, and materials throughout the dog’s career, but those skills are not transferrable to another canine.
Deputy Brett Lawrenson explained he has trained and worked with Kilo since 2010 and the dog will be treated as an
honored member of his family if allowed to go home with him.

           Commissioner Graves made the motion to declare police service dog ‘Kilo’ as
           surplus, transferring ownership to Deputy Brett Lawrenson as outlined in
           Consent Agenda Item No. 2. The motion was seconded by Commissioner Ivie
           and carried with the following vote:

5.    ADOPT RESOLUTION CONSENTING TO THE WEBB ADDITION ANNEXATION INTO
      SARATOGA SPRINGS, UTAH
(Track A, 15:25)
Utah County Deputy Attorney Robert J. Moore explained a legislative update prohibits annexations which create
county ‘islands,’ unless the county explicitly agrees to the annexation. Saratoga Springs City has requested several
annexations which would create islands. These annexations were protested by Lehi City, but the Utah County
Boundary Commission determined approval should be granted to Saratoga Springs as long as the county allows it.
Commissioner Lee noted the applicants in Consent Agenda Item Nos. 5, 6, 7, and 8 have all requested to be
incorporated into Saratoga Springs. Mr. Moore mentioned both Lehi and Saratoga Springs presented to the
Boundary Commission for annexation into their cities, and the Boundary Commission ultimately chose Saratoga
Springs based on the requests of the applicants.

            Commissioner Ivie made the motion to adopt the resolution consenting to the
            Webb Addition Annexation into Saratoga Springs, Utah, with the findings as
            detailed in Consent Agenda Item No. 5. The motion was seconded by

5.      AUTHORIZE COMMISSION CHAIR TO EXECUTE MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING
        BETWEEN THE FEDERAL BUREAU OF INVESTIGATION AND UTAH COUNTY
(Track A, 28:25)
Utah County Sheriff James O. Tracy commented on the purpose of the Transnational Organized Crime Western
Hemisphere Task Force, or ‘TOC-West TF.’ The proposed memorandum of understanding will partner Utah County
Major Crimes to work with the Federal Bureau of Investigation regarding cartels, money laundering, human
trafficking, and other collaborative criminal groups crossing national borders. This makes it possible to share
resources and facilities to remove the criminals from local communities. Commissioner Ivie expressed concern of
federal overreach, and Sheriff Tracy confirmed he remains the chief law enforcement officer of the county.
Commissioner Lee and Sheriff Tracy discussed delegation of responsibilities. Utah County Attorney Jeffrey R.
Buhman noted the agreement is not written to the standard county format, but that is only because it is coming from
the FBI and being sent to hundreds of organizations nationwide.

                 Commissioner Graves made the motion to authorize the Memorandum of
                 Understanding between the Federal Bureau of Investigations and Utah County
                 as defined in Regular Agenda Item No. 5. The motion was seconded by
                 Commissioner Ivie and carried with the following vote:

6.    APPROVE AND AUTHORIZE THE COMMISSION CHAIR TO SIGN A DECISION ON THE
      GRAMA APPEAL FILED BY MARK ALLEN
(Track A, 37:10)
Utah County Deputy Attorney Paul Jones explained Karen McCoy had submitted a GRAMA (Governmental
Records and Access Management Act) request on behalf of Mark Allen for information on mining claims in
American Fork Canyon. The County provided the requestors with a website link with that information, which they
considered inadequate, and filed an appeal for additional documents. The documents were released with redacted
information and Mark Allen subsequently appealed to receive non-redacted paperwork. The non-redacted
paperwork has since been provided to Mr. Allen, and Mr. Jones therefore considers the appeal completely satisfied.
