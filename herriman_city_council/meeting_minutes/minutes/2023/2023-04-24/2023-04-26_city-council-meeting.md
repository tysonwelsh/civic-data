           2.4. Short Term Rental and Accessory Dwelling Unit Presentation – Todd Sheeran, City
                Attorney
      City Attorney Todd Sheeran explained short term rentals could cause negative neighborhood
      relations, although he also acknowledged their convenience. He noted pre-2007, short term
      rentals were called vacation rentals and when AirBNB launched in 2008, it had become a
      billion-dollar a year industry. City Attorney Sheeran showed some examples of AirBNBs
      across the state and outlined the benefits of short-term rentals which included option variety,
      location convenience, price, diversity, tourism opportunities, extra income for property
      owners, and development opportunities.

      City Attorney Sheeran said the negative aspects of short-term rentals included loss of
      residential feel of neighborhood, parking issues, nuisances for neighbors like trash and noise,
      and rent increases. City Attorney Sheeran said there was also a study which called short-
      term rentals’ impact on rent into question. City Attorney Sheeran said short-term rentals
      mostly affected areas with lots of tourism and pulled up statistics which looked at county
      usages of short-term rentals. He said several tourism dense areas have almost a quarter of
      their housing going to short term rentals which can be a huge issue with limited land. City
      Attorney Sheeran said this was less of a problem in Salt Lake County.

      City Attorney Sheeran said in 2017, a bill defined a ‘short-term rental’ and addressed Cities
      and Counties were issuing fines or outright banning short-term rentals. He explained the bill
      gave people the right to list short-term rentals and said the language in the document gave
      the State the right to prosecute people using auxiliary units as short-term rentals. City
      Attorney Sheeran said there were several bills from 2022 legislative session which restricted
April 26, 2023 – City Council Minutes                                               Page 7 of 13


      municipalities and the right to prosecute property owners renting things out short-term. City
      Attorney Sheeran went through a few other bills which failed to pass.

      City Attorney Sheeran said Herriman Code prohibited short-term rentals; however, there
      were still 44 listings within the community. He clarified this did not seem like a problem.
      Councilmember Ohrn commented those people might not know Herriman prohibited short-
      term rentals. City Attorney Sheeran agreed and added there were business tools at the City’s
      disposal to deter from people listing short-term rentals. He thought the group should address
      the General Plan’s language about short-term rentals.

      City Attorney Sheeran said short-term rentals could be compatible with residential Code, but
      the City needed to adhere to strict definitions for terms such as dwelling. He said they also
      needed to address location and how short-term rentals fit into zoning ordinances and
      acknowledged the fact that short-term rentals could be limited based on population and
      development. There could also be occupancy, noise, and maintenance requirements which
      cross reference local business license requirements.

      Councilmember Hodges asked if the Council could restrict occupancy based on Fire Marshall
      recommendations, to which City Attorney Sheeran said they usually abide by two people per
      room.

      Councilmember Hodges said regulating short-term rentals would be difficult. City Attorney
      Sheeran agreed and added there would be a fine balance to decide whether to punish the
      owner or the tenants. The City should also have the enforcement and penalties clearly
      defined. Councilmember Hodges said there are currently no regulations outlined.
      Councilmember Ohrn asked if there had been issues. Deputy Chief of Police Stromberg said
      complaints are received based on parking, trash, and noise. Councilmember Henderson said
      historically, the owner was the individual to be punished.

      Councilmember Ohrn said she believed it was overly restrictive to outright ban short-term
      rentals. She did not necessarily think rentals should be allowed everywhere, but supported
      people making extra money.

      Councilmember Henderson said this warranted a bigger discussion. He told a story from his
      first term in 2017 which involved a big party house and lots of trash and noise.
      Councilmember Shields indicated his neighbors love who throw annual Halloween parties.
      Councilmember Henderson opined there was only so much which could be done, but next
      steps should be discussed.

      Councilmember Henderson suggested the Council look at the structural integrity of the
      neighborhood when rendering a decision and opined local laws to be put in place which
      prohibit short-term rentals in specific areas.

              District Governing Document – Nathan Cherpeski, City Manager

      Hills public infrastructure district governing document. He explained there had been seven
      public infrastructure districts when Olympia was formed and was limited to contract fees. He
      reported the development team requested the council allow for a mil rate as a financing
      option. Any infrastructure upsizing would be at the cost of the City.

      Councilmember Hodges asked if fees were on the same schedule, and City Manager Cherpeski
      explained the property owners paid for these things differently. Councilmember Ohrn asked
      if this covers all or part of the issue. Councilmember Shields expressed this was not an

      procurement policy. She did not like how this could lead to excess engineering costs.
