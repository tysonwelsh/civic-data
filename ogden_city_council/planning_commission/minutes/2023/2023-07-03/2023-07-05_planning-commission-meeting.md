5. Conditional Use Permit/Site Plan, Apartments at 1117 Country Hills Dr.
  Mr. Christopher Huffman indicated the existing office building has been converted into nine residential
  units which are quickly becoming leased, and he is requesting construction of an additional 14 units so
  there will be 23 total new units. He stated he has been working with the HOA of the adjacent multi-
  family projects and has reached some agreement regarding the maintenance of the private access
  road into the area and a maintenance agreement will be recorded.
  Commissioner Safsten indicated at the previous meeting concern had been raised relating to storm
  water and asked how it is being addressed. Mr. Huffman responded he has submitted a new
  geotechnical report which identifies an underground creek, and the intent is to create an underground
  storm system which will tie into existing storm lines. Commissioner Graf asked about which HOA is
  referred to. Mr. Huffman replied the access road provides access to the Secret Cove Condominiums
  and the Country Hills Drive Condominiums as well as the previous office property and the agreement
  involves HOA’s from each project for the future service of the private access road. He stated utilities
  are available for the new units.

7. Proposed Ordinance Amendment, Group dwelling standards
  Mr. Rypien indicated a petition had been submitted asking for some standards in the group dwelling
  code to be amended. As Staff reviewed the existing code, it was determined to review the entire
  chapter along with the items being requested by the petition. He stated several new definitions are
  proposed to be added as well as identifying revised standard which would allow the density of a group
  dwelling to be more compatible with existing standards for apartment buildings. Staff also is
  recommending changes to architectural standards. Commissioner Schade asked about the widths of
  balcony space asking whether the proposed standards would require encroachment into the City right-
  of-way. Other factors being considered are building types, glazing requirements, commercial
  component requirements, as well as individual unit requirements, including accessibility to electric
  vehicle charging stations, tree and shrub requirements and open space requirements. Many items
  simply clarify existing language. Density bonus points also are eliminated and some items listed are
  adopted as standards. Total open space is reduced but standards for gathering spaces are added.
  Commissioner Southwick expressed concern that EV charging stations are mandated, and felt the
  market could better identify when these are needed. Commissioner Shale commented care should be
  taken to assure new development is sustainable, and felt the EV requirement over time would increase
  the value of property. Discussion continued relating to individual charging stations outside and how
  these could be provided in individual attached garage space. Mr. Brierley stated the existing code
  requires one charging station per 20 dwelling units and the proposed ordinance reduces the
  requirement to one per 30 dwelling units, but requires each unit to provide electricity to garage space
  where a EV adaptor can be installed.
   Mr. Mike Bastian, petitioner, stated while he is pleased with most of the items proposed by the
   amendment, there still is some concern which makes developers opt for a single apartment building
   rather than a group dwelling. He expressed opposition to the requirement for a variety of housing
   types and site requirements for front-loaded garages, stating the limit to less than 40% of the front
   appearance of each structure as garage restricts design options for developers. He hoped the
   ordinance would create a level playing field for developers when considering either one building or
   multiple buildings. He supported the commercial component requirement for projects which contain
   more than five acres.
   Commissioner Southwick felt there are items which should have more attention and suggested the
   item be tabled and allow additional discussion and/or revisions. Mr. Tanner suggested the petitioner
   work with Staff and identify items of concern.
   MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Southwick to table action until the August meeting to
           allow Staff to further review the items discussed. Motion was seconded by Commissioner
           Graf and passed unanimously, with Commissioners Aaberg, Blaisdell, Graf, Safsten,
           Schade, Shale, Southwick and Sandau voting aye.
Commissioner Southwick was excused and Commissioner Shinoda arrived at 7pm.

6. Petition to Rezone, property at 557 25th St. from R-3EC to MU
  Ms. Marshae Stokes this petition is to rezone property which had previously been reviewed, and a new
  project proposed which would add four units to the previous design but the proposed project is a better
  fit for the surrounding community. The detention pond has been relocated so as to not be adjacent to
  structures as recommended previously.She indicated she has reviewed the Staff Report and has no
  concerns with its recommendations.


                                                     4
  Mr. Simpson stated a previous action had rezoned this property to MU subject to conditions in an
  associated development agreement which also specified a maximum time period for construction. As
  those conditions were not satisfied within the time frame, the property has now reverted back to its
  previous zone classification. This petition is to again rezone the property to MU with a new
  development agreement. The Commission is to determine whether the zoning is consistent with the
  General Plan, and whether the proposed site plan complies with the Mixed Use zone. The property
  also is within the Adams Redevelopment Project area. He expressed support of the rezoning based on
  the previous determination it conforms with the General Plan but suggested two properties to the west
  be included in the petition so as to not create a small island of R-3EC zoning facing 25th Street. He
  stated these properties need not be included in the development agreement but the action should not
  create an isolated zone. As these properties were not noticed for the rezoning, Staff suggests they be
  considered at the August meeting. He then reviewed the proposed development plan stating the new
  layout has been reviewed by the Fire Marshal and the street design now limits the height of buildings to
  30’. The project shows adequate open space and meets the standards of the MU code. Staff is
  suggesting a walkway be required to connect the new BRT station to the neighborhood and that the
  detention basin be surfaced with grass. He stated the MU zone requires a minimum of three uses, with
  one being residential. He felt commercial spaces and other uses should be better defined. Staff also
  would like to review the building design in detail to assure it meets the character of the surrounding
  area. Staff recommends the timing of the development agreement require a site plan to be submitted
  within 18 months of the final action by the City Council to rezone the property. Staff recommends
  approval of the proposed rezoning and development agreement subject to the following:
  1. The properties at 543 and 549 25th Street be included in the rezoning to MU and return to the
     August 2, 2023, meeting for consideration to ensure legal noticing for the action.
  2. The MU site plan review application for the project be submitted within eighteen (18) months from
     when the rezone and development agreement are approved (if approved) by the city council.
  3. The development agreement concept plans reflect the development standards outlined in the staff
     recommended development agreement.
  Commissioner Blaisdell asked about the building surface where the mural is proposed. Mr. Simpson
  indicated the wall would be stucco and there could be no murals painted on any brick surface.
