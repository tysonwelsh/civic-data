PUBLIC HEARING:

   Mayor Seely opened the public hearing to receive input from the public with respect to the issuance
of electric revenue bonds for the purpose of financing the costs of the acquisition, construction, and
completion of improvements to the electric system of Nephi City. The City intends to issue the bonds in
an amount not to exceed $8,500,000. The City will not pledge any taxes of the City for the payment of
the bonds. Instead, the City will pledge the revenues attributable to the City’s electric system for
payment of the bonds.

  Marcus Keller representing Crews and Associates (Nephi City’s municipal advisors) spoke about his
appreciation for working with Nephi City staff. He stated that he had sent out over 15 requests for bids
to financial institutions across the country. He stated that Nephi City has done a great job keeping their
electric fund healthy. He said that interest rates had decreased 15 basis points in recent days which
mean potentially lower principal and interest payments on the bonds.

   Brandon Johnson representing Johnson Farnsworth (Nephi City’s bond counsel) said he is also happy
to be working with the City and that the final Master Bond Resolution should be adopted at the June 4 th
regular council meeting.

Mayor Seely, seeing no other public comment, closed the hearing at 7:15 p.m.

MASTER FEE RESOLUTION 05-07-2024 - POWER PLANT NATURAL GAS RATE ADOPTED:

  Mr. Atkinson reviewed with the council that the city has been involved with the Utah Municipal Power
Agency (UMPA) to construct a small power plant on the south side of the city near Substation #2. As
part of the project, a natural gas gate station was constructed. The two turbines in the power plant are
fed with natural gas. Since Nephi City is a member of UMPA and knowing that any rates we charge will
ultimately be incorporated in a power bill, staff recommended a utility natural gas rate that would be
specific to this facility as long as it is owned by a power organization affiliated with the city.

cover the city’s cost of operations for the natural gas utility on this facility. Master Fee Resolution 05-

seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

DNR UPDATED LAND EXCHANGE AGREEMENT APPROVED:

   Mr. Atkinson reviewed with the council that for the past several years, Nephi City has been working
with the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources (DNR) on a land swap to potentially expand the golf course.
Things were moving forward, although at a slow pace. Earlier this year, the US Fish & Wildlife Service
(USFWS) had concerns over the Environmental Assessment and whether or not the exchange met the
purposes of the original grant that was used to purchase the DNR parcel. After meeting with the Utah
State DNR staff, a solution was proposed to the USFWS that was accepted.
  The main item that was altered in the new arrangement is that the city would exchange their 40 acres
for a smaller portion of the DNR land that would allow for the golf course expansion while still
maintaining the purposes of the original federal grant. An updated Environmental Assessment is now
being completed as well as an updated Yellow Book appraisal to take into account this change. An
updated exchange agreement is also needed with the DNR to reflect these changes and to extend the
deadlines to allow for the items above to be completed.
   DNR staff sent an updated exchange agreement that has been reviewed by the staff. The updated
agreement reflects the negotiated items from prior agreements as well as the updated acreage swap.
Councilor Skip Worwood moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the updated land exchange agreement
with the DNR. Councilor Travis Worwood seconded the motion. The motion carried unanimously.

900 NORTH FENCE ISSUE DISCUSSED:

   Mr. Atkinson updated the council that last September staff had updated the City Council on a fence
issue at the property located on the southeast corner of 900 North and 200 East. The property owner
was unaware of the property boundaries and had spent the money to construct the fence, staff
recommended that a time frame be given to relocate the fence. At that meeting the Council discussed
giving the property owner 12 months to relocate the fence out of the city’s street right-of-way. Mr.
Atkinson had informed the property owner through email about the Council’s decision. No response
was received from the property owner. Last week, the city’s code enforcement officer followed up with
the property owner and found that a sprinkler system was being installed in the city’s right-of-way as
well. The property owner contacted the City Administrator and following a discussion about the need to
