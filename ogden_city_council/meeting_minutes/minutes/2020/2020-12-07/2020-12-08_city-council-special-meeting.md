Public Comments
          Heath Satow stated that he addressed the Council last week and asked for an update regarding the City's recycling
program; he commented on his concern about recyclable materials being dumped in the landfill and he feels that everyone should
be upset about that. He also asked for clarification of any plans to resume the recycling program. He thanked Council member
Hyer for his comments and response providing the information he had about optional recycling programs, but the Mayor was
defensive and suggested that he had accused him of not caring. He stated that he honestly does not know if Mayor Caldwell cares
or not, and he never said that, but he does believe he clearly does not care about informing anyone of the recycling situation
because he emailed the entire City Council and Mayor three weeks ago about the misinformation and broken links on the City's
website regarding recycling. Apparently, the City's website still has a page indicating the recycling program is on hold and
provides a link to 'read more', but when he clicks on that link, it takes him to a page saying that the desired destination could not
be reached. He stated that he would not be talking about this issue if it had been fixed several weeks ago when he emailed about it.
Not one person from the City responded to his email and the Mayor has never responded to a single email he sent, except for
acknow!edging receipt of one of the emails by way of sending a received receipt. He stated that if any elected official wants to
give people the impression that they actually care, they should respond to emails from the constituents who pay their salaries with
tax dollars. He stated that if nothing has been done about a broken link for several weeks, he cannot believe someone when they
say they care. He asked that the Mayor spend less time complaining about being held accountable and more time earning his
salary.

Mayor Comments
         Mayor Caldwell stated he would love to address something unique that occurred today. United States Attorney General
William Barr recognized Randy Watt as the Police Chief in one of six cities in the nation that has employed the operation "Project
Safe Neighborhoods". If not for COVID-19, the Federal Government would have flown Randy Watt to Washington, D.C. to
recognize him in person. In the past three years since the project was instituted, the City has had a 29 percent reduction in Part
One crime and a 24 percent reduction in violent crime. Chief Watt recognized all the local agencies that contributed to the
program's success and this was a great honor for the Police Department and the dedicated public servants there. He thanked Chief
Watt for his leadership and the entire Department for the service they provide in the community.

Council member Comments
          Council member Hyer stated he had the opportunity to participate in in the ceremony during which Chief Watt and his
Department were recognized; it was an honor to participate and represent the Council. He will send the entire Council the letter
detailing the honor that has been bestowed on the Police Department.
