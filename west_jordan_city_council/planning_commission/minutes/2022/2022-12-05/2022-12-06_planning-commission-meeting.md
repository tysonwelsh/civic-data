2. Google Fiber Hut 142; 7960 South 4000 West; Conditional Use Permit for Major Utility;

P-F Zone; BHC/Robert Vaccaro, P.E. (applicant) [#28958; parcel 21-31-200-043]

Robert Vaccaro, applicant on behalf of Google fiber, joined remotely. He stated the proposal is for a

fiber equipment hut that will serve the entire West Jordan area. The pre-manufactured building is 360

square feet and set on a concrete pad in an existing parking lot island. It will be surrounded by an 8-

foot-tall precast wall to match existing wall enclosing an area 51 % feet by 29 4 feet with a decorative

man gate and an 8-foot-wide solid metal vehicle gate. After construction, the parking lot curb will be

rebuilt, and landscaping repaired. The backup generator runs off a natural gas service only in case of

power outage or during a testing period about once per month. He couldn’t identify any conflicts in this

area that would require special hours of testing, but he stated that Google will work around any special

requirement in that regard. After construction the structure is an unmanned facility that runs 24/7 to

provide internet service.

Mark Forsythe gave an overview of the application. The P-F zone is intended for public utilities,

substations, and government services. This use is classified as a major utility based on the definition

and size. The nearest residential is 260 feet away across 4000 West. Staff did not think that this use

would have any detrimental impact to those residents, and it does not generate dust, smoke, or

vibration, etc. Noise from the HVAC system is typical of a residential unit, and it faces away from the

residential areas. The site will be surrounded with an 8-foot-tall wall. The man door is wrought iron and

it does not allow view to any of the equipment. Security cameras will be mounted on the building, and

gates have automatic locks. He noted a correction that here is one parking space that is being removed

with the construction of the hut. However, a parking study indicated that 191 stalls are required, and

325 stalls are provided.

Staff recommended that the Planning Commission approve the Conditional Use Permit for Google

Fiber Hut 142, located at 7960 South 4000 West in a P-F Zone, with the Requirements of Approval

listed in the staff report.

6. Discussion and Possible Recommendation of Section 13-8-23 — Balanced Housing

Ordinance
Larry Gardner said the planning commission asked about the possibility of creating more multi-family
product in the city during review of the general plan updates. He reviewed the history and purposes of
the Balanced Housing (formerly Cap and Grade) Ordinance. He mentioned that new laws created the
option for accessory dwelling units in the single-family areas for more moderate-income housing
opportunities. Several exemptions were included in the ordinance to meet federal fair housing
regulations and to have more control over where multifamily is located. He felt that the Balanced |
Housing Ordinance has achieved what it was created for, and it allows for exemptions in areas and |
situations where the Planning Commission and City Council feel are appropriate. He noted that the
Planning Commission had previously discussed the possibilities of recommending amendments to the |
ordinance.
He further explained some of the requirements for multi-family in the ROD, which is for existing
commercial property.
Matt Quinney asked for an estimate of how many potential IOZ, ROD, and new P-C properties there
are that could fit within these exemptions.
Larry Gardner said the Southwest Quadrant zone also has a multi-family component. He estimated that
there could be two potential Residential Overlays, three Interchange Overlays, and one or two
Integrated Housing. Any new applications for these districts would come to the Planning Commission
before going to the City Council for a legislative decision.
McKenna Marchant said that all along Redwood Road seemed like a great opportunity for multi-family
development.

Trish Hatch asked how a determination is made regarding adequate public services when these

applications come before them.

Scott Langford said the city is enhancing the analysis of future utility needs with each rezoning or land

use map amendment application. The Public Utilities staff will do an adequate public facilities analysis.

There will soon be a detailed interactive map that will indicate how many equivalent water allocation

units are available for a particular parcel. Any departure from that modeling will have to show how they

will upgrade or enhance the infrastructure to accommodate a proposed development. Eventually there
will be maps for sewer capacities and other services.

McKenna Marchant said if staff is looking at multi-family residential on Redwood Road, it would be

good to consider reserving the ground level floors for commercial space or business offices.

Duncan Murray addressed the procedural issue of recommending a text amendment. The city code

allows the Community Development Department, the Planning Commission, or the City Council to

initiate a text amendment. As a full planning commission with a motion, they can ask staff to get started
on a text amendment. It is possible in the future that City Council may want to review the
recommendation before staff begins work.

MOTION: McKenna Marchant recommended that if staff isn’t already looking at multi-
family residential along Redwood Road, reserving commercial on the ground level,
that they do so. The motion was seconded by Matt Quinney and passed 5-0 in favor.
