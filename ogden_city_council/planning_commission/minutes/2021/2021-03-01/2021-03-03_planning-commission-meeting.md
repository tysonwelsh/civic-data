2. Public Hearing, Preliminary Subdivision Approval, for BDO Plat 36, approximately 400 N 1200 W.
   (Attachment A)

  Mr. Cameron Cook stated this is a request to create seven new industrial lots along the west side of the
  BDO property facing 1200 West. The first lot will have loading docks to the east while other lots will be
  accommodated on the north and south ends of the building due to their limited width. At the request of
  Marriott-Slaterville, there will be no windows or outside lighting on the west side of the buildings.

  Mr. Montgomery stated while property at the BDO can be built on without being subdivided, dividing the
  lots helps define the building space and is helpful in financing of the construction. He stated the
  property being subdivided is entirely within the BDO and in Ogden City, while 1200 West is within
  Marriott-Slaterville. Therefore, no access is proposed from the west, and 400 North will provide the
  primary access. He stated at the time the BDO property was conveyed to Ogden City, a plan was
  created, and one of the goals identified was to integrate with the surrounding communities as much as
  possible. In meeting that goal, it was determined a 40’ buffer area from 1200 West would be
  established to protect the residential area to the west from noise and lights which may be associated
  with the business park. He stated the lots proposed meet the requirements of the MRD zone and no
  remnant parcels are created. Utilities are available from within the BDO property. He suggested the 40’
  “no build” area be identified on the plat to insure the setback remains in the future. While not a part of
  the subdivision review, the applicant has agreed that no doors or windows would be on the west side of
  buildings and loading docks will be either on the sides or east of the buildings. He also recommended
  appropriate trees and vegetation for the 40’ buffer area. Staff recommendation is for approval of the
  subdivision plat as it complies with the regulations of the Zoning Ordinance and Subdivision Ordinance,
  subject to the 40’ no build area be defined on the plat and that the title page be revised to match the
  drawings on the plat. He also noted this area has been cleared from any underlying environmental
  concerns and is available for development.

  Commissioner Schade asked if there might also be a height restriction on these buildings as there is
  currently no height limit in the MRD zone and future development may not be sensitive to the existing
  neighborhood.

  Mr. Kerry Wayne asked if there is a purpose to this meeting as it appears work has already begun on the
  property. He expressed concern about the existing Perry Ditch which runs through the property and
  wanted to be assured water service will continue. He stated many of the industrial uses have overhead
  doors with alarms and hoped these would not be allowed. He also asked if the gate accessing the
  retention pond area would remain and expressed concern with security. He also stated the DDO Reuse
  Plan had guaranteed any future road improvements to 1200 West would be made on the DDO side of
  the road and not infringe on existing residential properties.

  Ms. Shelle Kautzman, 335 S 1200 W, stated she lives directly across the street from Building 7 and
  stated the drawings show windows and lights on the west side of the building. She expressed concern
  with the noise of semi trucks as they maneuver and idle. She also stated residents had been promised
  no new buildings would be allowed west of the existing buildings and felt the proposed 40’ landscaped
  berm is insufficient. He also expressed concern that construction would begin early in the mornings
  and disturb residents.

                                                    [2]
  Ms. Lori Slater Jones. 113 S 1200 W stated notices had been received on Friday and vehicles were on
  site moving dirt on Monday. She also had understood no new construction would be allowed west of
  the existing buildings.

  Mr. Bryce Kautzman, 353 S 1200 W expressed concern with air quality as semi trucks often sit and idle,
  allowing their exhaust into the air. He wanted to know the process of dropping trailers and reloading.

  Ms. Lori Jones, 113 S 1200 W asked if work could be allowed if the subdivision was not yet approved.

  Ms. Tina Evenhuis asked if an environmental impact study had been conducted, expressing concern
  with the potential of lost property value due to noise from the BDO.

As there were no additional public comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Southwick to close
the public hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Safsten and passed unanimously.

  Mr. Stratford, City Attorney, stated the purpose of the meeting is to inform neighboring property owners
  of the proposed subdivision of properties, and is an administrative review to determine whether the
  standards of the Ordinance are complied with. The Planning Commission is to determine whether the
  size of lots are allowed under the provisions of the Ogden Code. Consideration is given as to the lot size
  and whether utilities are available. This review is not for a particular permit wherein the Commission
  could attach conditions which might reduce impacts to surrounding properties. The Commission’s
  authority is limited to whether the subdivision as proposed is compliant with the land use code. He
  stated excavation on site is allowed without subdivision approval.

  Mr. Montgomery stated notice is sent to surrounding property owners in an effort to alert them of the
  proposal to subdivide property, and determine whether it is compliant with the Ordinance and with the
  General Plan, but this review is not for building approval. He stated a building drawing had been
  provided to Marriott-Slaterville City which is inaccurate, stating there will be no openings on the west
  side of any building. Additional screening also will be provided in the area where loading docks are at
  the ends of buildings. He stated the applicant is allowed to develop according to the master plan. He
  suggested the owner might consider limiting hours of construction, but so long as the noise ordinance
  is not violated, the City has no ability to limit the hours. He stated the applicant is responsible to assure
  water rights are not affected and to obtain permission for work on the Perry Ditch. The two-acre
  detention basin is intended to control storm water runoff from the site. He also indicated typical
  weather patterns come from the west and there should be little impact to residents from vehicles, and
  that the use of public roads for these vehicles is allowed. He also stated there is not a requirement for
  environmental impact studies for developments within Ogden City. He stated the effect on property
  value is unknown, and often depends on a particular appraisal, but should not change because of the
  effect of the industrial development.

  Mr. Cameron Cook stated no docks will be allowed on the west side of any of these buildings. While
  many vehicles do have back-up alarms, there are no alarms on overhead doors. There will be no staging
  of semi-truck trailers and tenants would not be moving trailers once they are delivered, He stated
  during construction it is anticipated there will be some night work for concrete work during the next few
  months, but would be completed by May or June of this year. The Perry ditch is anticipated to be piped
  during the next few weeks and the gate to the detention basin will be removed. He stated the building
  next to the Ogden Nature Center is limited to 40’ in height, and other buildings are proposed to be
  between 20’ and 36’ in height. He stated he is unaware of any restriction of the Government relating to
  building beyond the existing structures.

  impact to the surrounding community than the BDO business park. He asked if efforts had been
  required for environmental or chemical cleanup within the site. Mr. Montgomery responded there was a
  requirement for the Federal Government to mitigate any damage to property within the DDO site. Some
  areas are yet to be completely mitigated. Mr. Cook stated Boyer has worked closely with the Army
  Corps of Engineers and stated they are quick to respond if soil contamination is found.

  MOTION:       A motion was made by Commissioner Blaisdell to recommend preliminary approval based
                on the findings there is good cause for the subdivision and the proposed subdivision
                complies with both the Zoning and Subdivision Ordinances. Approval is subject to
                complying with all division review comments, the 40’ no build easement along the west
                property line be identified on the plat, and that the title sheet be revised to include the full
                area being subdivided within the plat. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Safsten
                and passed unanimously, with Commissioners Blaisdell, Boykin, Garner, Safsten, Sandau,
                Schade, Southwick and Graf voting aye.

5. CBD Site Plan., Encircle Youth Resource Center, 2458-2464 Washington Boulevard. (Attachment D)

  Mr. Steve Bingham stated this request is for a new building to provide a resource center for youth. 0

  Commissioner Garner asked if ADA access is available for the three-story structure. Mr. Bingham
  responded ADA parking will be available in the rear to provide access to the building, but there are no
  elevators proposed within the building.

  Mr. Simpson stated this request is for new construction on four existing parcels which have recently
  been functioning as a pocket park since the previous building was removed. The four parcels will be
  combined to allow construction of a new youth facility consisting of 4,200 square feet of interior space.


                                                     [5]
He stated ADA parking is part of the Staff Review and likely will be a requirement to satisfy building code
requirements. The Ordinance requirement for parking is one stall per staff member, and the applicant is
expecting 3-5 staff members. Three parking spaces are available at the rear of the building, with limited
parking available along the street frontage on Washington Boulevard due to the anticipated BRT station
in front of the building. An existing paid parking lot exists to the north where additional parking spaces
might be leased, and the BRT system will provide service which may reduce the need for on-site
parking. The Commission has the authority to reduce required parking if it can be found peak usage is
at different hours, a reduction in parking will not cause a shortage of parking for other uses in the
vicinity and a reduction will contribute to the vitality of the CBD. Staff feels this is satisfied due to the
availability of the paid parking lot to the north and the BRT service being proposed, and recommends a
waiver be granted. He reviewed the site plan and indicated a detailed landscaping plan will be required
to show 10% on-site landscaping. The building design is compatible with the downtown in that it has a
flat roof and a defined base, body and cap are identified. Construction is to be brick and fiberboard with
a concrete base. Utilities for the building are required to be screened from the public view and may be
placed on the roof or on the site so long as it is screened. Staff recommendation is for approval subject
to a reduction of required parking requirements being approved by the Commission and the following:
1. The four (4) parcels associated with the property being combined under Ogden City review and
     approval.
2. The applicant work with Planning Staff to develop a detailed landscaping plan that conforms with
     zoning code requirements and is compatible with the site.
3. Utilities be screened or placed on the site so they are not visible from the public way.
4. The applicant obtaining and satisfying all department staff review comments.

7. Conditional Use Permit, to allow used car lot at 335 Patterson Street. (Attachment F)

  Mr. Robert Kuchinski stated while he is retired, he desires to maintain his auto dealership license. This
  proposal is to use an existing warehouse to clean and repair vehicles which would then be taken to the
  auction for sale. There will be no retail customers coming to the site, and all repair and storage will be
  inside the warehouse building. No vehicles will be stored or displayed on the site.

  Ms. Olson stated this is a commercial property consisting of two existing parcels. The 5,000 square
  foot warehouse building is on the west side of the site and was previously used as auto repair, which is
  a permitted use in the C-3 zone. Because the applicant will be selling vehicles and desires to maintain a
  dealer’s license, the use is classified as a car lot which is a conditional use in the C-3 zone. The building
  will be used to store and recondition vehicles, and no changes are proposed for either the building or the
  property except to stripe some parking spaces on the site. No retail sales will occur on the site. In
  considering a conditional use permit, the Commission is to determine whether any detrimental effects
  are created by the use and whether other provisions of the ordinance are satisfied. While the area is
  zoned commercial, there are several existing nonconforming single-family homes. The site plan shows
  a fenced in area to be used for storage as well as a 15’ strip on the east side of the building. Staff
  recommends the storage area be screened with a 6’ tall screening fence of either wood, vinyl, or chain
  link with interlocking vinyl slats.

  Mr. Kuchinski stated there is no proposed use for the outside area, as there is no intent for more than
  ten cars to be on the site, and the warehouse building could accommodate up to 20 vehicles. He
  stated he is also working with an adjacent property owner to remove debris from the site.

                                                     [7]
Ms. Olson stated if the area is used for storage screening is appropriate. She stated the use proposed
meets the objectives of the Plan, but that some upgrades should be made to comply with all other
zoning regulations by installing two parking spaces and one ADA parking space with an 8’ access aisle,
as well as adding landscaping and pedestrian walkways to connect the entrance of the building to the
street. Staff also recommends the two existing parcels be combined into one parcel so the building and
storage area are on one lot. Staff recommendation is for approval of the conditional use permit subject
to the following conditions:
1. The use of the property is limited to wholesale used vehicle sales.
2. Install a minimum six (6) foot tall solid screening fence constructed of either wood, vinyl or chain link
   with interlocking opaque vinyl around the boundaries of the proposed outdoor storage areas as
   shown on the staff recommended site plan.
3. Install two (2) parking stalls including one ADA stall with a minimum eight (8) foot access aisle along
   the northwest boundary of the property, to meet parking ordinance requirements.
4. Install a six (6) inch parking block for each parking space to provide a barrier between the required
   parking stalls and the neighboring property.
5. Install a minimum fifteen (15) foot landscaped setback between the front property line, parking area
   and the building.
6. Install five hundred and ten (510) square feet of landscaping at the northwest corner of the property
   and one thousand (1000) square feet of landscaping at the northeast corner of the property, in front
   of the building to meet open space requirements.
7. Install a four (4) foot wide sidewalk along the north wall of the building to provide a designated
   pedestrian access from the parking area to the main entrance of the building.
8. Install a four (4) foot wide sidewalk connection between the sidewalk along Patterson Street and the
   main entrance of the building.
9. Complete a lot combination process to combine parcels 04-059-0045 & 04-059-0044 into a single
   lot/tax ID. Once approved, the lot combination shall be recorded at the Weber County Recorder’s
   Office.

Commissioner Safsten asked if there is no outside storage, whether there would still be a requirement

9. Request for Change of Nonconforming Use, at 740 28th Street. (Attachment H)

  As these two items appear to be the same building, but are on separate parcels, they were discussed
  together but voted on separately. Each side of the building is being proposed to be converted to a one-
  bedroom single-family use.

  Ms. Ashlee Bonham stated this is an abandoned building and the desire is to change each parcel to a
  single-family unit in an effort to create low-cost housing in the neighborhood.

  Ms. Corbridge indicated the proposal is to reuse each of the commercial spaces as one-bedroom single-
  family homes. The exterior wall materials will be updated with siding and brick. The Commission is to
  determine the new use does not create more impacts to surrounding properties than the previous use,
  does not intensify the use of the site and does not change the character of the existing building unless
  the change would make the use more compatible with its surroundings and existing zoning. She stated
  the previous use was a barber shop, and the change to single-family would be compatible with the
  surrounding R-2EC neighborhood. The proposed building materials are compatible with the
  surrounding community, and existing materials are deteriorating. The proposed materials do not alter
  the character of the building and are consistent with other building styles and colors in the area. The
  building occupies the entire parcel and there is no ability to add parking or landscaping on the site.
  Because it is on a corner lot, parking has occurred along the street for many years. While the parking
  ordinance requires two parking stalls per unit, it also allows the Planning Commission the ability to
  approve less parking if no parking is available so long as the reductio is not detrimental to the
  surrounding neighborhood. The existing building is considered as having a nonconforming right for
  both parking and landscaping. Staff recommendation is for approval of the change for each parcel to
  become a single-family use and reduce the required parking spaces to zero for each of the units.

  Commissioner Blaisdell clarified there is no space on site for parking. Commissioner Southwick stated

  Mr. Montgomery stated this request is to convert an existing single-family home to a triplex in the NC-2
  zone. While neither use is allowed in the NC-2 zone, the Ordinance allows the Planning Commission to
  preserve historic structures, allowing their reuse by approving uses which might otherwise not be
  allowed so long as other ordinance requirements are either complied with and/or exceeded. In
  considering the exception, the Commission is to consider the character of the surrounding area,
  whether adequate landscaping beyond the minimum requirement is achieved, and whether other
  standards of the underlying (NC) zone are satisfied. The site must exceed the minimum landscaping
  requirement and Staff recommends an additional 440 square feet of landscaping be added at the rear
  of the site. The dumpster needs to be located outside of the landscaped area and be screened, and
  space available for five parking spaces. The damaged sidewalk and approach also need to be repaired.
  Staff recommendation is for approval, subject to the following conditions:
  1. Architectural drawings be submitted showing the three units for approval by Building Services, with
       the building being inspected to create three dwelling units to meet present building codes;
  2. Site plan be revised to show scaled drawing of parking and additional 30x44’ of landscaping;
  3. A financial guarantee be provided for all required landscaping and public improvements;
  4. The trash enclosure be revised with three sides being solid 6’ masonry walls to match the brick of
       the home;
  5. All damaged fencing be removed, and new screening fence provided along the parking lot area
       (chain link is not allowed);
  6. Scaled site plan to show location of 3-6 parking spaces to meet ordinance design standards; and
  7. All damaged sidewalk be replaced.

   Commissioner Schade expressed concern that the front door entrance is not compatible with the
   building and suggested it be removed. Mr. Jensen stated the front door is crucial as access to the
   building as it leads to the second floor. He stated it could be re-framed or painted to make it more
   compatible with the building and surrounding development. Commissioner Blaisdell expressed concern
   that the City had made strides to eliminate multi-family housing from larger homes in the recent past
   and this action seems counterproductive to that effort. Mr. Montgomery responded that this building is
   surrounded by multi-family uses, and it is felt to be more beneficial to preserve the historic structure as
   a triplex rather than allowing it to be demolished and a 5-plex being constructed. He stated the
   Ordinance gives some flexibility in order to preserve historic resources. Commissioner Southwick
   stated as this is limited to the NC area, while it is contrary to the East Central Community, it makes
   sense to preserve the historic structure and allow the density as there are other higher density uses on
   the corner. He stated there is a real need for affordable housing and it makes sense at this location. He
   commented it could be an owner-occupied single-family home with an ADU, and the triplex is only
   adding one additional unit.
