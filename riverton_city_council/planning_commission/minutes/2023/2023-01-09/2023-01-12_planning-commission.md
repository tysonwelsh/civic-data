       A.     GENERAL PLAN AMENDMENT – MODERATE-INCOME HOUSING
              ELEMENT - RIVERTON CITY IS PROPOSING AMENDMENTS TO THE
              MODERATE INCOME HOUSING ELEMENT OF THE GENERAL PLAN,
              SOLELY TO MAKE TECHNICAL CORRECTIONS RELATIVE TO
              UPDATED REQUIREMENTS OF THE UTAH STATE CODE.

Chair Cluff opened the public hearing.

Development Director, Jason Lethbridge, presented the Staff Report and stated that
typically, a General Plan Amendment is broader in scope. Tonight’s correction was more
technical in nature. He explained that all jurisdictions in Utah are required to have a
Moderate-Income Housing Element as part of their General Plan that addresses needs
within the City. Riverton City adopted the current Moderate-Income Housing Element in
2019. The Utah State Code at that time required all cities to select from a list of strategies
to focus on. Riverton City did so and was adopted in 2019. The language of those
strategies was taken directly from Utah State Code. The City Council ultimately adopted
four that were included as part of the General Plan Amendment. Every year, cities are
required to file reports and updates. This year, for the previous year’s report, the State
required that the language in the Moderate-Income Housing Element be updated to reflect
the current language in Utah State Code. House Bill 462 was adopted last year and
introduced changes to that list. The amendments that were introduced into the strategies
were also identified. The State requires cities to update their plans to reflect the current
language.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that they are not amending the plan or changing direction and
2023 is the end of the Moderate-Income Housing Element. As a result, they will be going
through a comprehensive update with the Planning Commission and City Council later in
the year. Tonight’s action was narrowly focused and involves adopting an amendment to
the plan that will be done by ordinance. It specifies that wherever in the existing plan they
refer to the strategies, they will utilize the language from the newly-adopted State Code.
Staff recommended approval with final adoption by the City Council.

There were no public comments. The public hearing was closed.
