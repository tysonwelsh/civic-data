          Presentation on Galena Soo'nkahni Preserve - Wendy Fisher, Director of Utah
          Open Lands

Wendy Fisher explained that Salt Lake County put in the Jordan River Trail and found
archaeological value in this area. She said this area is also an educational area and Utah
schools have been taking field trips to learn about this area.

She explained that over the years there have been threats of development on this property,
however, many organizations have fought to permanently protect this area. She talked about
the many challenges that are along the Jordan River corridor including phragmites which are a
problem they are trying to mitigate.

Ms. Fisher said that one of the reasons she was asked to present was to make the council
aware that there are many great projects to preserve and restore the land around the Jordan
River.

Councilmember Wells asked how many acres were in the Galena preserve.

Ms. Fisher said there were 250 acres.

Councilmember Buroker said that she appreciated the council’s willingness to listen to this
presentation and thanked Wendy Fisher for presenting.

          Discussion Regarding Secondary Metering Project - Mayor Staggs

Mayor Staggs said he wanted to discuss two factors driving his budget regarding remitting the
money back to the state for the secondary metering project. He believes there are a number of
grants that have come into existence and could be used to fund the secondary metering project.
He said that he did not want residents to carry any debt for this project.

Mayor Staggs said that if we give the money back to the state before the end of this fiscal year
then the city would be saving over $1 million per year in debt service over the next few years.
He said that within the next year the city will have the old water bonds completely paid off. He
said the city was getting pressure from contractors and that the council needed to give direction
on whether to move forward with this project or to put this project on hold.

Councilmember McDougal asked if there was a penalty for breaking the contract.

Ryan Carter, City Attorney, said that there are no penalties for breaking this contract because
the contract is on a year to year basis.


          Update on Recreation Master Plan - Trace Robinson, Public Works Director and
          Sheril Garn, Events and Operations Director

Sheril Garn explained that back in 2003, staff had been directed to put together a recreation
master plan. She said that one of the determining factors for this master plan was parks per
1,000 residents and said that the goal in 2003 was to have a park within a half mile of every
resident. Ms. Garn said that the master plan identified a trail system which included sidewalk
and transportation. She said that in order to create the master recreation plan, a survey was
sent out to residents asking them for their input.

Ms. Garn asked the council if they would like to update the master recreation plan and if so,
then how they would like this updated. She suggested that the city could contract with a
consultant, ask the Parks and Trails Committee or the city could send a new survey to
residents.

Mayor Staggs said that he would like to use the Parks and Trails Committee to create a new
recreation master plan.

Councilmember Buroker said she wanted the Parks and Trails Committee to create the plan and
that the city should also consider hiring a consultant.

Councilmember Stewart said he would like to use the Parks and Trails Committee and believes

Discussion Item

          Ranked Choice Voting Discussion - David Brickey, City Manager

Mayor Staggs explained that the council needed to determine whether they wanted to
continue to move forward with Ranked Choice Voting. Mayor said that he would like to see
citizens weigh in on this issue and would rather wait so that they could get a response from
citizens.

Councilmember Wells said that we do not know what the software cost would be because the
$25,000 cost is spread out among all of the cities. He said that his number one concern was
whether Salt Lake County would be ready to do ranked choice voting. He said that now that
they are ready, he is okay with choosing ranked choice voting. He said that he is hesitant as
to whether they would be able to educate the public because people don’t understand ranked
choice voting. He asked what the plan was going to be to educate citizens.

Councilmember Buroker said that cost of the software would be split among all cities
participating in ranked choice voting. She believes that the $200,000 that has been set aside
by the Lieutenant Governor’s Office would be a significant media campaign to educate
citizens.

Councilmember McDougal said that he believes that the longer time frame there is to educate
the public the better.

Councilmember Stewart said that the one thing he feels strongly about is changing the way
someone has the right to vote. He thinks this decision should be made by the citizens and not
the city council. He said that he would like to see a path that would allow the citizens to make
this decision and to possibly put this on the ballot in the fall.

Councilmember McCay said that she supports ranked choice voting and feels the state can
educate the voters.

Councilmember Wells said he believes that the cities choosing ranked choice voting could
work together to put out the information.

Mayor Staggs said that he thinks the decision to do ranked choice voting will place more
responsibility on staff but would like staff to know as soon as possible if the council would like to
continue moving forward with ranked choice voting.

Councilmember Buroker said she doesn’t want residents to get worried because it is a pilot
program. She said that this method of voting is not set in stone for future elections. She said
that after the election was over, residents would be surveyed and would then determine if they
wanted to continue using ranked choice voting. Councilmember Buroker absolutely supports
using Salt Lake County to run the elections.

          Discussion Regarding Text Amendment to Riverton City Ordinance 18.20, Elderly
          Housing Overlay Zone, 18.90 Table of Commercial Uses, and Commercial Zones,
          Addressing Care Facilities, Riverton City, Applicant - Jason Lethbridge,
          Development Services Director

Jason Lethbridge explained the need for discussion regarding the text amendments to
Ordinance No. 18.20. Mr. Lethbridge said that the proposed amendment would remove
“commercial” uses and that the elderly housing overlay would no longer apply to commercial
zones. He said that with the proposed amendment, nursing homes, care facilities and assisted
living centers would be shifted to commercial zones with the exception of commercial office
space.

Mr. Lethbridge said that the text amendment would create “Age Friendly” features and he listed
these features. He said there was concern that this amendment would introduce high density
housing but he thought that the physical requirements would be tailored to the elderly.

Mr. Lethbridge said he wanted a density bonus over the general plan which would make the
underlying zoning consistent with density, development standards would be appropriate to use
and the city would still be able to apply limits/restrictions.

Mr. Lethbridge explained the double bonus density in different zoning areas and said he
believed this would be less confusing. He said that elderly housing restrictions would still apply
and asked the council for their feedback regarding the density bonus.

Councilmember Stewart said that instead of “Age Friendly” he thought that “Mobility Friendly”
would be a better term. He suggested that if the applicant incorporated more of the Mobility
Friendly features then they would be able to receive the double density bonus.

Mr. Lethbridge said that the city can establish appropriate conditions and language could be
added to increase the number of mobility friendly features to receive the double density as
Councilmember Stewart had suggested.

Councilmember Stewart believes that elderly housing continues to be a need in the community.

Mr. Lethbridge clarified that the double density bonus would only be above single family lots.

Councilmember McDougal said he agreed that this is a demographic that is increasing in the
city. He said he is not as worried about the density as he is that the city designate that the
double bonus is only available for single level housing.

Councilmember McCay said that she supports doubling the density and she supports allowing
for two stories so that families can have a bonus room.

Mr. Lethbridge said that he will put some language together that would make the requirements
more specific.

Councilmember Buroker said that she appreciates the hard work and only has one concern that
the city try to comply with whatever requirements have come from the legislature.

Mr. Lethbridge said they are looking through all of the changes from the legislature and will
include any analysis of the changes when he brings this back to the council.
