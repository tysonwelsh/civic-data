GENERAL SESSION

Chair Snow called the Planning & Zoning Commission meeting to order at 6:02 p.m. He
explained how the meeting would proceed. First, the Planning Department would brief the
Commission; then the applicant would speak to the Commission; the item will be opened to the
public for their comments on public hearing agenda items; the Midvale Deputy City Attorney
will then brief the Commission on their role on the given item; and then the Commission would
deliberate and decide.

PUBLIC HEARINGS


1.     Tom Henriod/Midvale Creekview Borrower LLC requests approval for an amended
       conditional use permit to allow an eight-foot-tall fence located at 935 East South
       Union Ave, between the North Union Apartments and the Arlen Jensen property.
       (Wendelin Knobloch, Planner I)

Wendelin Knobloch stated that the applicant requests approval for an amended conditional use
permit to construct an eight-foot-tall fence between the North Union/Creekside Apartments,
which are currently under construction, and the single-family residential lot to the south which is
owned by the Johnson Family.

The original approval identified a six-foot-tall block wall which is now proposed to be replaced
by an eight-foot-tall, molded composite fence (Simtek).

Prior to hearing public comments, the Deputy City Attorney, Garrett Wilcox stated that it has
come to staff’s attention that there may be a potential legal dispute between the applicant and the
adjacent property owner. He stated that the Commissioners role is not to determine who is right
and who is wrong. The Commission is only considering whether the request meets the
requirement set forth in the City’s ordinance.

Arlen Johnson, the property owner of the adjacent property, stated that the prior agreement did
specify that the structure would be a wall and not a fence. He added that as he understands it,
anything over eight feet would require a variance request. He stated that as far as he is concerned,
his arrangement was to change the boundary line in exchange for a ten-foot precast wall. In his
opinion anything over eight feet will not happen because of the hardship clause.

Commissioner Leidtke asked for clarification on what the neighbor was looking for as far as fence
height.

Mr. Johnson stated that he would like whatever height would make it hard to scale, the bigger the
better. He stated that his concern is security and privacy.

Commissioner Erickson clarified that from the applicant’s perspective the height will be eight and
that the height from the adjacent property it would ten feet. She asked if the material on the side
which is eight feet, made it difficult to scale, he would be okay with the proposed request?

Mr. Johnson stated that he never would have signed the agreement if he knew this would have
been the outcome.

Commissioner Liedtke stated that the zoning for this area is either six feet or eight feet and those
are the two options.

Mike Edwards, Midvale resident asked if the fence height restriction is set at a county or city level
and if a text amendment would alter the restriction?

Mr. Wilcox stated that this is a city ordinance and that a text amendment could be proposed and
would have to be approved by the Planning Commission and City Council.

Mr. Henriod stated that if it were allowed, he would put up a ten-foot fence. He also wants to
acknowledge that at the time of the agreement none of the parties knew that there would be an
issue with a ten-foot fence. He stated that he completely understands Mr. Johnson’s concern and
is willing to comply with what the Mr. Johnson is requesting even if that means they go through
the process of a text amendment.

DISCUSSION:

Mr. Garret Wilcox stated that this item is an administrative decision. There is flexibility in
accessing if there are factors that allow for the fence to be eight feet.

Commissioner Liedtke stated that his concern with doing a text amendment to allow for a ten-foot
fence opens the door for much larger fences in this area. For him it sounds like the only way to
resolve the privacy issue is to build a fifty-foot fence.

Mr. Olsen stated that another option is a request for a ten-foot fence which would not go to the
Planning Commission for approval but would need to go to a variance hearing. The request for an
eight-foot fence would only need approval from the Planning Commission. He added that the
variance may still be an option even if the Planning Commission approves the request for the eight-
foot fence.
