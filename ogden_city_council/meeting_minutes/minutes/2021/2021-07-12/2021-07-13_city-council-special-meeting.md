        Proposed Resolution 2021-10 approving the honorary designation of 2nd
        Street from Century Drive to Wall Avenue as Chief Little Soldier Way
                  A memo from Community and Economic Development Department came before the Council to consider a resolution
        regarding a proposed honorary street name designation. The memo stated Anna Keogh submitted a petition to assign an honorary
        street name for a portion of2nd Street between the Union Pacific railroad tracks and Washington Boulevard. The initially proposed
        honorary street name was "Bingham Fort Lane" in honor of Bingham Fort, which was located in that area. The proposal was
        reviewed by the Planning Commission and a recommendation of approval for a shorter distance was forwarded to the Council
        with a vote of seven to zero. As recommended by the Planning Commission, the honorary street name would apply to the portion
        of2nd Street between Century Drive and Wall Avenue, which the Planning Commission f~und to align with the fort's historic
        location more closely. After the initial review by the City Council, the Council requested that the proposal be reviewed by the
        City's Diversity Commission. This review was completed, and the Diversity Commission forwarded a recommendation of denial
        to the Council with a vote of nine to one. The Diversity Commission's recommendation was based on the belief that the proposal
        did not accurately represent and honor the perspectives of all the peoples of the area at the time. At the Council's February 2, 2021
        work session, Council members discussed the proposal and the recmmnendation from the Diversity Commission. Council
        members expressed opposition to the proposal as presented. The petitioner requested additional time to develop an alternative
        proposal that would help address concerns expressed by the Diversity Commission. The proposal was amended to include the
        name of Chief Little Soldier, a prominent leader of the Northwestern Band of the Shoshone Nation. The amended petition
        proposed assigning the honorary name as "Bingham Ft-ChiefLittle Soldier Ln." The alternative proposal was reviewed at the
        April27, 2021 work session meeting. At the Council's May 25,2021 meeting, a vote was taken on the alternative proposal which
        concluded in a 3-3 tie. The tie vote resulted in the denial of the petition. After the May 25 meeting, the petitioner expressed
        interested in proposing further amendments to the honorary name eliminating the Bingham Fort name and proposing the name be
        "Chief Little Soldier Way." At the June 1, 2021 meeting, in accordance with its Rules and Norms, the Council voted to reconsider
        the item. The Council discussed the new proposal at its June 15, 2021 work session and gave direction to place the petition on a
        future agenda for fmal consideration.
                  Council Deputy Director Symes summarized the memo and stated the original petition proposed the honorary street name
        apply to 2nd Street between the Union Pacific railroad tracks and Washington Boulevard. The Planning Commission's
        recommendation for approval shortened the applicable section to between Century Drive and Wall Avenue. The petitioner has
        indicated support for the shortened length and the proposed resolution for the Council's consideration reflects this change. In April
        of2021, the proposal was amended from the original submittal of"Bingham Fort Lane" to "Bingham Ft-ChiefLittle Soldier Ln."
        The amended proposal was reviewed by the Council and denied with a vote of three to three. The Council voted to reconsider the
        item expressing a desire to give the full Council the opportunity to vote on the proposal and to potentially consider the most recent
        proposed name of"ChiefLittle Soldier Way." Subsequently, at the June 15, 2021 work session, the Council discussed the process
        for reviewing and reconsidering the proposal, including the consideration of the most recent proposed name. The City's legal
        department determined that the proposal and the most recent proposed name does not need additional Plarming Cmmnission
        review. The Council expressed an interest in moving forward with the consideration without further engagement of the Planning
        Commission or the Diversity Commission. At the June 15 work session, Darren Parry, Council member and former Chair of the
        Northwestern Band of the Shoshone Nation, expressed support for the proposal. The standards by which the Council reviews and
        approves both standard and honorary street name changes are found in Section 7-1-2 of the City's code. This section of code states
        that the Council may designate an honorary name for a public street in addition to the assigned street name using the same process
        for changing assigned street names. For assigned street names, the Council may approve a change to eliminate duplications, avoid
        confusion that would negatively impact emergency services, or for any other good cause. Honorary street names are not proposed
        to eliminate duplication since they are supplemental to the assigned street name and are always reviewed by the City's engineer
        and emergency service personnel to ensure there is no impact on services. This means that the Council, for the purpose of
        honorary names, would generally approve a petition for an honorary name if there is "any other good cause." The petitioner has
        submitted a petition to assign the honorary street name for this portion of 2nd Street to recognize the contributions of Chief Little
        Soldier and that recognizing him would honor both Chief Little Soldier and the Northwestern Band of the Shoshone who lived in
        the area prior to and during the pioneer settlement of the Ogden area. The newly proposed name eliminates the reference to
        Bigham Fort, which the Diversity Commission indicated may "promote symbols of oppression, dominance, and sometimes
        violence." Ultimately, the decision is up to the Council to detennine ifthere is good cause for the approval of the honorary street
        name.

              A memo from Council staff came before the Council to consider a resolution authorizing the execution of an interlocal
    agreement for the City's participation in the Community Renewable Energy Program (CREP). The memo stated adoption of the
    resolution would allow the City to work with other participating cormnunities, Rocky Mountain Power (RMP) and other
    consultants to develop the rate structure. The Council must also authorize the expenditure of funds in the amount of$35,738 for
    the payment of the Phase One costs. If the Resolution is not adopted, the Council may choose to continue monitoring the CREP
    without official participation or cost until January 31, 2022.                                             .
              Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide
    background information regarding House Bill (HB) 411 and the CREP. The goal of the program is to serve as a model for local
    govermnents to partner with a major utility to deliver a customer choice model. It will provide more renewable options to all
    customers in participating communities who share the cost of the Public Service Commission process that detennines the
    projected renewable rates. If projected rates are agreeable, govermnents adopt ordinances to provide renewable energy to all RMP
    customers in their communities. In communities with ordinances, individual customers can opt out of the program to remain on
)   standard rates. According to HB411, July 2021 is the timeframe for the Council to decide whether to enter into the governance
    agreement and pay Phase One costs. Entering into the agreement gives the City the ability to vote on utility program design.
    January 2022 is the City's last change to decide whether to become a party to the governance agreement; 12 of the initial23 cities
    who signed on to the program have approved the governance agreement and Ogden would be the 13th. By July 2022 the City
    would pay Phase Two costs and in September 2022 a decision would be made by the Utah Public Service Commission regarding
    program application. In December 2022, the Council would decide whether to pass an ordinance regarding involvement in the
    program, after which a request for proposals (RFP) would be published for a renewable energy resource. The purpose of the
    interlocal cooperative agreement is to establish a Cormnunity Renewable Energy Agency and create a decision-making process for
    program design, cost share, allocation, resource solicitation, resource acquisition, and other program issues. Each participating
    cormnunity has one Board seat on the Agency. Ms. Eller-Smith discussed the duties of the Agency and the manner in which
    voting on issues will occur. Pre-implementation costs are estimated to be $700,000, which will cover technical and legal
    consulting, RMP filing costs, and program review costs for state regulators. The minimum fmancial commitment for a community
    to participate is based on their participation percentage of the $700,000 total. This may be paid over two fiscal years. Participating
    communities will also share the cost of reimbursing RMP for noticing customers-an estimated cost of $300,000 for two
    mailings. Once the program is implemented and the renewable resources are on-line, RMP customers will be purchasing
    electricity from RMP as they were before, but rates will be adjusted to reflect the costs associated with the renewable sources.
    These rates may be higher or lower, depending on the market. The tenn of the agreement is 50 years.

Council member Comments
           Council member Hyer stated he feels the need to reply to some of the public comments made; he acknowledged that it is
important to take action now rather than wait 20 years and look back wishing that something had been done sooner. However, in
terms of planting a tree, in this situation he feels the City is 'planting apoplarratherthan an oak'. He believes that the City maybe
going down the wrong road and will waste time trying to nurture the poplar when it would have been better for an oak to be
planted and growing. He heard from several Ogden residents who were very concerned about this project and the lack of
government's willingness to listen to their concerns about this matter. He asked them why they did not speak out publicly about
the issue and they told him that they were afraid because the 'other side' of the issue is ruthless and attacks those who do not agree
with them. He feels that all voices need to be heard, even if they are in disagreement. One side ofthis issue was very organized
and the other side not as much and they were not heard as a result. He stated he was advised to look into what is happening in
Germany, where nuclear plants have been shut down in order to transition to sustainable sources. Power costs there have risen
exponentially. He stated that France has bought into the nuclear idea and their power prices are decreasing. He stated he simply
feels this is the wrong path for the City. He is not a climate change denier, and neither are the experts he has listened to as they
have admitted that the climate is changing. They just do not feel that the type of program Ogden has become involved in is the
remedy to solving those concerns.
