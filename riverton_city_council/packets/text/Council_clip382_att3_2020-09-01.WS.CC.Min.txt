47   direction to staff to find any and all ways the City could expend all the money received. He said
48   it was recommended that the funds be used for law enforcement, which if done, would build a
49   fund balance that would enable RLESA to change their budget year from a calendar to a fiscal
50   year.

 1   City Attorney Ryan Carter explained that in accordance with the Treasury’s Coronavirus Relief
 2   Fund Guidance, the Fund was designed to provide ready funding to address unforeseen
 3   financial needs and risks created by the COVID-19 public health emergency. For this reason a
 4   local government may presume that payroll costs for public health and public safety employees
 5   were payments for services substantially dedicated to mitigating or responding to the COVID-19
 6   public health emergency. He also said that costs incurred for a “substantially different use”
 7   include, but were not necessarily limited to, costs of personnel and services that were budgeted
 8   for in the most recently approved budget but which, due entirely to the COVID-19 public health
 9   emergency, have been diverted to substantially different functions. Mr. Carter said that
10   generally, law enforcement has presumed to have had all of their duties shift as a result of
11   COVID. He said that it makes for a very clean audit if you just take the payroll for law
12   enforcement generally and assign the CARES Act dollars to it.

21   they be added to the Main City Park for citizen recreation and tournament use or if additional

 9       a. Ordinance No. 20-15 - Proposed Amendments to the Riverton City Code, Section
10          18.145.100, Updating Terminology Regarding Accessible Parking
11
12   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained a proposed change in the Zoning
13   Text proposed by Riverton City Staff. He said the proposed change was to a very specific place
14   in the Code and was intended to rectify inconsistencies and update the Code to current
15   standards and terminology. He said that Section 18.145.100 of Riverton City Code contained
16   requirements for “Disabled Parking”. He said that, not only was the terminology outdated, so
17   were several of the standards it listed. In some instances the Code calls to ADA requirements
18   but lists an incorrect or outdated standard. The proposed change updated the wording to reflect
19   current terminology, by replacing “Disabled” and “Handicap” with the term “Accessible” or
20   “Accessible Parking”, which are standard terms for referring to ADA Accessible Parking. The
21   Planning Commission recommended approval of the proposed amendments.
22
23   Mr. Lethbridge explained that on March 12, 2020, the Planning Commission held a public
24   hearing to review the proposed text change. The Planning Commission, following their review,
25   voted to recommend approval of the proposed change and staff concurred with that
26   recommendation.
27

40          Frontages
41
42   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that Monument signs were
43   regulated by Riverton City Code 18.150 “Sign Regulation Ordinance”. He said the table of sign
44   standards described the maximum area, height, density, spacing and set-back for monument
45   signs. He said the density for monument signs limited the approval to “one for sites having only

50   those for pole signs, in particular each eligible development is allowed one sign regardless if the

18       c. Ordinance No. 20-17 - Proposed Amendments to Riverton City Code, Section
19          18.150, Sign Ordinance, Clarifying Language and Updating Zoning Lists
20
21   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that signs or signage in Riverton
22   were governed by Chapter 18.150 “Sign Regulation Ordinance”. The chapter was adopted in
23   2008 with several revisions and clarifications since. The ordinance sets out standards for each
24   type of sign, and designates which type of sign is allowed in each zone. He said the proposed
25   amendment was for correcting contradictions and deficiencies in the ordinance but does not
26   alter the existing character or design requirements in the code. The most significant modification
27   is to the “Table of Permitted Signs” (see 18.150.110). The proposed changes adds the RM-6
28   (Residential Multi-Family 6du/acre) and PCC (Planned Commercial Center) Zones to the table.
29   He said the RM-6 zone signage was assigned the same permissions as the RM-8 Zone. The
30   PCC Zone signage was assigned the same permissions as the CR (Commercial Regional)
31   Zone. Also, in the Table permissions for “Bus Bench” were removed to be consistent with the
32   text in 18.150.120 (2). The amendment also removed a restriction that limited certain monument
33   signs to only a business name and address, but did not refer to specific zone. He said that the
34   Planning Commission recommended approval of the proposed changes, and staff concurred
35   with that recommendation.
36
37   Mr. Lethbridge said that on May 14, 2020, the Planning Commission held a public hearing to
38   review the text change. The Planning Commission, following their review, voted to recommend
39   approval of the proposed change.

 1       d. Ordinance No. 20-18 - Proposed Amendments to Riverton City Code, Section
 2          18.10.010, Updating the Zoning Districts List
 3
 4   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained a change in the Zoning Text
 5   proposed by Riverton City Staff. He said the proposed change was to a very specific place in
 6   the code, and was intended to rectify inconsistencies in the code. Section 18.10.010 of Riverton
 7   City Code contains a list of the zones found in the ordinance. He said that it appeared that in the
 8   past the list was not consistently updated as zoning ordinances were adopted or repealed. In
 9   some cases the list was updated, for instance, newer zones like the PCC and RM-6 appeared
10   on the list. In other cases, by oversight or clerical error, the list was not updated, for instance the
11   RM-14 zone does not appear on the list. He said the proposed change simply removes zoning
12   designations from the list, if the zoning designation no longer exists in the ordinance, and adds
13   zoning designations to the list if the zone exists in the code. The Planning Commission
14   recommended approval of the amendment, and staff concurred with that recommendation.
15
16   Mr. Lethbridge said that on March 12, 2020, the Planning Commission held a public hearing to
17   review the text change. The Planning Commission, following their review, voted to recommend
18   approval of the proposed change.
