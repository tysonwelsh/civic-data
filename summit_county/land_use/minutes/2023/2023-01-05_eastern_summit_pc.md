2. Public hearing regarding Promontory Liberty Ranch at Star Point Final 
Subdivision Plat (1 Lot) and Final Site Plan (40 Units), Parcel SS-51-C, located at 
6680 Promontory Ranch Rd., Promontory, Summit County, UT; Chris Zarek, 
Applicant. Amir Caus, AICP, Senior Planner. (6:20 P.M.) 
 
Amir Caus, AICP, Senior Planner, provided background information regarding Promontory 
Liberty Ranch at Star Point Final Subdivision Plat (1 Lot) and Final Site Plan (40 Units), Parcel 
SS-51-C, located at 6680 Promontory Ranch Rd., Promontory, Summit County, UT.  
 
Chris Zarek, Cowboy Partners, Applicant, provided background on Cowboy Partners, the 
proposed project, and the discussions with the neighbors following the last hearing regarding 
privacy and noise. 
 
Commissioners shared their perspectives on the site plan. Rich Sonntag recused himself from 
the discussion. 
 
Commissioner Wilde opened the meeting for public input. (6:47 P.M.) 
 
Brian Schwartz, a Silvergate homeowner, noted that he is the only true fence line neighbor to 
the project. He had only two days to digest the staff report. He focused on legal issues he 
doesn’t believe are resolved. All the neighbors agree there should be affordable housing in 
Summit County. This area is not zoned for this density. He asked the Commission for help 
understanding the density issue. He was confused by the staff report’s statement that the 
zoning is all good. There are three separate governing items: the AG-80 zoning, the Promontory 
Specially Planned Area (SPA), and the Promontory Housing Agreement administratively 
approved by the County staff. The zoning matrix does not apply here if there is no zoning limit 
involving this project if they don’t violate setback and height limits. To believe staff, one has to 
believe the housing agreement, approved only by the staff, increased the density. Staff can’t 
increase density without approval from County Council. Administrative approval is not available. 
Either the housing agreement did not change the density, or it did, in which case, it was not 
properly approved. The applicant cannot have it both ways. He acknowledged not being a 
lawyer or urban planner but identified as a very concerned. The staff have chosen pieces of 
relevant documents and ignored others. County staff are good people with positive intentions, 
but they have made mistakes. He asked the Commission to table the application and ask staff 
to revisit the density issue and get Council approval if they are going to increase density. He

had additional questions for the applicant—regarding the request to split out land, he wanted to 
know what is to become of that parcel. He would like to know that answer before moving 
forward on the rest of the project. He thanked the Commission. 
 
Pam Strausser, spoke last time and thanked Mr. Zarek for meeting and trying to listen to the 
neighbors. She fears for the unvoiced, the children, living there. There isn’t a school there. 
There is a ton of traffic. The children will be endangered by traffic if they try to ride bikes or roller 
skate. They will spend their lives on buses to get to and from school. South Summit doesn’t 
have before or after school childcare. Parents will have to drive so far to pick up sick children.  
She thanked the Commission for listening.  
 
May Payan (6:57 PM) appreciates everyone being present for the meeting. There are some 
sidewalks around the complex. Children would be safe there. Housing for people with low 
incomes is very important. She provides low-income housing for her relatives. Residents won’t 
look at the layout; what’s more important is paying less rent, then layout and where children can 
play. The concern here is low rent. If more people from the community are seeking that, it is 
more of a concern than play areas. There are great other places to play around the City. Deer 
Mountain residents go all the way to Heber on the bus. She likes the idea and thanked the 
Commission. 
 
Ryan McTish-(7:00 PM) is a homeowner in Silver Gate. He thanked Mr. Zarek for trying to work 
with the community. Have had some time to step back. The process of how everyone got to this 
moment seems kind of broken. The complexity of the rezoning is beyond him. Public opinion is 
held in high regard and often sought. There were changes here without the input of those 
impacted. There is lots of open land around Promontory. He doesn’t know what keeps further 
development from happening around the community without community input. Affordable 
housing is exciting. The community supports it. He thinks the community is just trying to find the 
optimal location for it. There is still room for improvement. As a neighborhood, this brings a lot of 
density to their doorstep. He is concerned about growth. There are several homes just north of 
that property line that are significantly impacted by this development. And he wanted to support 
them. He doesn’t want to give another multigenerational family a reason to move out of Summit 
County. They have two other gates. He asked if this couldn’t be consolidated into one location. 
More dispersed development is favored. It could go across the road by the equestrian barn. Mr. 
McTish asked how extensively the other locations had been studied. It’s so close to the property 
line that he doesn’t know. He doesn’t know what the zoning is to the East. He asked why the 
project couldn’t move to the southeast; why not incorporate this more into the Promontory area 
and put it right at the entrance. He thanked the applicant and the Commissioners, and Mr. Zarek 
for listening. Everyone just needs to find a perfect solution where everyone is happy and 
impacts are minimized. 
 
Commissioner Wilde closed the public input. (7:09 P.M.) 
 
Mr. Zarek responded to some of the Commissioners’ and the public’s comments and questions.  
 
Commissioners continued to comment and ask questions. Helen Strachan, Deputy Attorney, 
responded to some of the questions.

3. Public hearing regarding a recommendation to Community Development Director 
regarding a Low Impact Permit for an accessory dwelling unit within an existing 
attached garage, located on Parcel NS-152-B, 1920 S. West Hoytsville Rd, 
Wanship, UT, Hiram and May Payan, Applicants. - Kirsten Whetstone, AICP, 
County Planner. (7:26 P.M.) 
 
Kirsten Whetstone, AICP, Planner, provided background information regarding the Low Impact 
Permit for an internal accessory dwelling unit within an existing attached garage, located on 
Parcel NS-152-B, 1920 S. West Hoytsville Rd, Wanship, UT.  
 
May Payan, the applicant, explained that it is her sister living in the home.  
 
Commissioners asked questions and stated their positions. Deputy Attorney Helen Strachan 
responded to Commissioners’ questions, explaining the legal procedural background regarding 
the property. 
 
Commissioner Wilde opened the meeting for public input. (7:50 P.M.) 
 
Larry Vernon (7:50 P.M.) owns the land all around this lot. The Payans moved there in 2015. 
They immediately started working on the garage and applied for a permit. People have been 
living there for 6 years. He complained to the County. The County didn’t do anything. He filed 
suit against the Payans. The case went to trial in 2022. Judge Holmberg gave the Payans 90 
days to obtain permits. They did not apply for them. There was a hearing on January 24, 2023 
for sanctions and to determine what else to do. Mr. Vernon asked the Commission to please 
deny the application for the following reasons: the Code requires the Payans to live on the 
property. They don’t. They live in Coalville. Item 5(b)(ii). They are the owners of record; they 
don’t live there. This increases the density in the area. They have one dwelling unit; they can’t 
have two. It might be an accessory dwelling unit, but it is also a dwelling unit. The area exceeds 
the 2000 sq. ft. The Code is for gross square footage. The application should be denied 
because the third district court has ruled that the building is a duplex. They had 90 days to 
acquire permits. They didn’t do it; they didn’t even apply for it. Utah Code Annotated section 10-
9a-511.5 applies. They must live there, and they don’t. So, you have to deny it. The size 
limitations are only for residential area. This is in AG10. 
 
Commissioner Wilde closed the public input. (7:57 P.M.) 
 
Hyrum Payan (7:58 P.M.)- bought the house in 2015. They have had 7 years with harassment 
from Mr. Vernon which has affected his family greatly. They have tried to go to a judge to stop 
him. It’s not just a matter of the house. He has attacked them in different ways. He has trucks 
and family in Summit County. Mr. Vernon has taken them to court. Mr. Payan doesn’t find the 
harassment of the family to be done in a secure manner. It has been 7 difficult years for his 
family. Mr. Vernon greatly affected his family, and they don’t have much time to be wasting. 
They have worked hard. He has been in Utah for 22 years.  
 
Ms. Whetstone and Ms. Strachan addressed the issues raised. 
 
Commissioners provided additional comments. 
 
5 
 
Commissioner Wheaton moved to table the matter until after Jan 24, 2023, when the 
Judge rules. Commissioner Clyde seconded the motion. All voted in favor. (8:12 PM).
