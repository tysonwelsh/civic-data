2.3. Approval of this Meeting’s Agenda.
Council Member Lord moved to AMEND the Agenda to move Public Comment from Item 8 to
Item 5. Council Member Austin seconded the motion.
Council Member Lord recommended that public comments be heard after the Consent Agenda.
Public comments had been moved to the end of the meeting agenda in November and December, but
he prefers to hear those comments early. Meetings can be very long, and he does not believe it is fair
to require citizens to wait several hours to speak.
City Manager, Bruce Kartchner, stated that public comments were moved to the end of the agenda
because other citizens were present to speak to agenda items. As there can be one comment or many
hours of comments during the public period, it could be unfair to those with items on the agenda and
consultants the City pays to be present to make them wait until after public comments. Additionally,
Staff often follows up with citizens after their comments. If the public comment period is at the end
of the meeting, Staff or the City Council has the opportunity to speak with them immediately. Council
Member Lord stated that he had not been to many meetings in the past two years where the public
comment period was lengthy.
Council Member Wilding agreed with Council Member Lord. However, City Council meetings are
typically short, and he likes having the ability to address issues raised during the public comment
period directly with those constituents. He feels their frustration when citizens comment but are not
provided with the opportunity to have a dialogue with the Council.
In response to a question raised by Council Member Crockett, Council Member Lord clarified that he
only proposed moving the current agenda item. The Council could then discuss the matter in more
detail at another time.
Vote on the Motion: Council Member Austin-Yes, Council Member Aston-Yes, Council
Member Wilding-No, Council Member Crockett-Yes, Council Member Lord-Yes. The motion
passed 4-to-1.

4.2 Presentation of the 2024-2025 Fiscal Year Audit Report, Hansen, Bradshaw,

Malmrose & Erickson. (Presenter, Jeff Miles) [Joint presentation of Audit: City
Council, LBA & RDA Board]
Mayor Hall reported that the RDA and LBA Audit Reports would be presented in conjunction.
Jeff Miles from Hansen, Bradshaw, Malmrose & Erickson reported that his firm had issued a clean,
unmodified opinion on the City’s financial statements. The statements presented fairly in all material
respects the respective financial position of the governmental activities, business-type activities, each
major fund, and the aggregate remaining fund information of the City as of June 30, 2025. The report
discusses management and auditor responsibilities and also reports on supplementary information like
budget information and consolidated secondary fund schedules.
Mr. Miles reported that the Management’s Discussion and Analysis on Page 4 of the report was
prepared by City management. At the end of the most recent fiscal year, the City’s assets and deferred
outflows of resources exceeded its liabilities and deferred inflows of resources by approximately $246
million, an increase of $17.2 million over the previous fiscal year. The bulk of the City’s equity is
tied up in infrastructure and capital assets, including the Public Works building and road
improvements.

A key metric is the unassigned General Fund balance as that reflects available discretionary monies.
At the end of the fiscal year, the unassigned balance was approximately $2.9 million, which

no more than 35% of fund revenues. Bluffdale’s total was approximately 19%, down from 33% in

total outstanding debt increased by $17.2 million due to bonds for the Public Works building.

reviewed the document.

e Added specific information regarding secondary water usage and metering, as well as long-

term plans for secondary water usage.

e Added information regarding whether applicants are required to provide their own drinking

water sources and rights.

e Added recommendations for targeted water conservation outreach for high-volume water

users.

e Removed low-flow fixture ordinance recommendation.

Associate City Planner, Ellen Oakman, reported that Rocky Mountain Power approached the City
regarding large trees near transmission and distribution power lines. Their goal is to work with the
City to protect future electrical infrastructure and prevent unnecessary removal of trees due to being
too close to power lines.
The proposed changes addressed street trees near power lines, as well as trees on commercial and
residential properties in general. They also specified that other utility company standards may require
additional landscaping or tree standards. The recommended guidance was to contact each utility
company for their specific requirements when planting in or near utilities or utility corridors.
Ms. Oakman reviewed a graphic and table outlining the proposed required distances and tree size.
e Low-growing trees (under 25 feet at maturity) may be placed adjacent to overhead power
lines.
e Medium trees (25 to 35 feet at maturity) must be at least 25 feet from overhead power lines.
e Large trees (over 35 feet at maturity) must be at least 50 feet from overhead power lines.
Mayor Hall asked if the ordinance addressed existing problem trees and how the City could educate
residents on the changes. For example, her street does not have overhead power lines, so she
questioned whether the new ordinance will affect what she can plant in her park strip. Ms. Oakman
clarified that the standards would apply to all new trees. The pamphlet would be added to the City
website and includes approximately 100 approved trees. Mayor Hall recommended that an article be
included in the next newsletter reminding residents that new trees in park strips must be on the list of
approved trees and stating the reason the rules were put in place.
