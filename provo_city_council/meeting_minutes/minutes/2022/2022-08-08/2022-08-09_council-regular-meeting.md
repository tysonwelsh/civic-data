Public Comment (11:08)

Vice-Chair Handley opened public comment.

Kelly Potter, of Provo, discussed incident on July 7th involving the Police Department. She expressed her
feelings of the officer’s use of unnecessary force and feels the officers need to be held accountable. She
suggested creating a civilian review board to oversee police incidents which would include members
who are not involved in law enforcement and represent minorities and the unsheltered.

neighborhood chairs. She disclosed she wants to hold meetings for residents to vote.

Steven Langle, of Provo, addressed ADU’s and discussed areas that should be excluded from proposed
changes.

Ty Belka, Member of the Party for Socialism Liberation, expressed concern for unsheltered. He proposed
emergency shelter for those most in need.

Melanie McCoard, of Provo, asked if a citizen review board was created several years ago.

Brian Jones, City Attorney, answered by explaining that there is currently an advisory board that advises
the city on matters of the Police Department’s policies and actions.

9.         An ordinance amending the zone map classification of approximately 0.23 acres of real
           property, generally located at 769 W 200 N, from Residential Conservation (RC) to Low-
           Density Residential (LDR) and authorizing a related development agreement. Dixon
           Neighborhood. (PLRZ20210338) (1:42:36)

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2022-32, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Aaron Ardmore, City Planner, presented. He shared that the staff is supportive of the rezone. He added
that the development agreement would prohibit this area from being used for any other purpose.

Councilor Shipley asked about enforceability of development agreements after ownership changes.

Brian Jones shared that the development agreement typically ends when the certificate of occupancy is
issued. He discussed other potential options.

Councilor Fillmore suggested there be a way to create a covenant that runs with the land instead of a
development agreement.

Bill Peperone, Director of Development Services, asked for clarification of the concern. He said the
development agreement solidifies what can be built.

Councilor Shipley said the concern is not necessarily for this property, but for future requests.

Mr. Jones said if there is an ongoing concern, the better solution would be to amend the zone instead of
relying on development agreements.

Councilor Fillmore shared his opinion of it being easier to create a one-page document that runs with
the land instead of changing the zones.

Mr. Peperone suggested that a restriction of use be recorded on the plat itself.

Austin Taylor, of Provo, presented as the applicant. He addressed the concern of council with some
statists. He shared the purpose of the proposed development.

Councilor MacKay asked why it showed eight units in the presentation.

Mr. Taylor responded that the zone allows eight units, though he is not proposing that many. He
continued his presentation. He discussed his desire to leave the existing house and maintain the
architectural look for the new house.

Councilor MacKay asked if a part-time staff member would be sufficient to handle the neighborhood
program while the communications aspect could remain with the full-time position.

Mrs. Tapahe provided some feedback regarding staffing requirements.

Vice-Chair Handley opened public comment.

Dave Knecht, of Provo, shared some ideas regarding the minimum requirement of votes for a
neighborhood chair. He suggested the council stick with the current program with a few changes.

David Wright, of Provo, said neighborhoods can handle their own elections.

Paul Evans, of Provo, shared an example from another city of a functional and structured approach to
the neighborhood program.

Melanie McCoard, of Provo, asked what the quorum requirement would be.

Vice-Chair Handley closed public comment.

Councilor MacKay addressed a comment made by Paul Evans. She shared that votes from neighborhood
meetings would be seen by planning commission and council regarding development proposals. She also
said that the Development Services department has stated that a City Planner would be present at
neighborhood meetings.

Councilor Shipley shared that his view of the changes is a hybrid approach. He believes neighborhoods
should function independently. He expressed the appointment process would be used for
neighborhoods who do not have current representation.

Councilor Ellsworth asked members of the committee about the language around voting.

Councilor Shipley responded and shared his recommendation to leave the opportunity for a group to
straw poll anything. He added that the intention is to have the comments and votes recorded.

Councilor Ellsworth said it does not seem like a straw poll when people’s address and information is
verified. She said at that point, it seems like a very formal vote.

Vice-Chair Handley and Councilor Ellsworth discussed the need to verify people’s address or
neighborhood to vote.

Councilor MacKay added that the benefit of having things recorded is that the numbers would be there.
She gave the example of two people being present at a meeting and both voted yes.

Councilor Shipley said that a straw poll is simply to get a sense of how a big group feels. He agreed with
Councilor Ellsworth that substantive comments would need to be provided in another way.

Councilor Whipple expressed that the change in the neighborhood program does not limit people from
gathering in neighborhoods. She added that the current program leaves one chair to carry a huge
burden. The proposal allows for a board member and city staff to help distribute that burden.

Councilor Fillmore shared his struggle between getting more involvement and engagement from
neighborhoods and not taking away rights of residents. He said that he would like to explore a hybrid
approach that would allow the opportunity for neighborhoods to elect a chair. He said the
neighborhoods without much engagement could have an appointment.

Councilor MacKay said she agrees with Councilor Fillmore’s idea of a hybrid approach.

Vice-Chair Handley discussed his struggle between the program being intended to represent neighbors
or engage neighbors.

Councilor Whipple shared that the idea would be to offer a board position to existing neighborhood
chairs and vice-chairs, which they could decline if they chose to. She explained by doing so it would
allow time to prepare for a viable election to fill the vacant seats.

Councilors Ellsworth, Whipple, and MacKay discussed what a proper vote would be.

Vice-Chair Handley asked if Councilor Whipple was proposing a substitute motion.

Councilor Whipple responded that she is not proposing a substitute motion. She mentioned that she
would prefer to wait to see if the new proposed program will function and solve some of the other
issues the current program has been facing.

Councilor Ellsworth suggested council keep the language as is for now and allow a six-month trial period.
She said it would be helpful to have an outside, unbiased group evaluate how the program is functioning
at that point.

Councilor Fillmore said he appreciates Councilor Whipple’s suggestion and added that he looks forward
to revisiting it again in six months.

Vice-Chair Handley called for a vote.

     Vote:        The motion was approved 5:2 with Councilors Ellsworth, Handley, Hoban, Shipley, and
                  Whipple in favor. Councilors Fillmore and MacKay opposed.
