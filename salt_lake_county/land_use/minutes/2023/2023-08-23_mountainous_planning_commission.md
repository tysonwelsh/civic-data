CUP2023-000897 – Nima Mahak , representing Snowbird Ski Resort, has requested a conditional use 
permit for a minor ski resort improvement for Snowbird Ski Resort. The proposal is for four avalanche 
mitigation towers (Wyssen Towers) that are to be located south of the resort base and just north of the peak 
of Mt. Baldy. Acreage: ~96 acres. Location: 9410 East Little Cottonwood Canyon Road.  Zone: FR-20. 
Planner: Morgan Julian (Discussion Only).   
 
Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District Plann er Morgan Julian provided an analysis of the staff 
report. 
 
Commissioners and staff had a brief discussion about serving by helicopters and construction, with a fair 
share of helicopter use for construction.

Comments: Mr. Mahak said the towers cause a lot of small avalanches instead of a big one. Wyssen Towers 
are used in Europe, have tracked and recorded what have worked well, and use a charge instead of shooting 
or taking a helicopter to the location. 5-meter tower at an angle that holds charges and detonates from there, 
lowers down and sends the charge. Avalanches start high up on the slope and must be installed on steeper 
slopes. Guest and employee safety are a priority. Without these towers, patrols go as a team and ski in the 
morning with runout zones. Easy to get caught in the slide, and unfortunately it does happen. Dangerous to 
go out and manually mitigate avalanche hazards. Another option, which is being phased out by the military, 
is to use a 105 mm howitzer to detonate an avalanche. The Wyssen Towers are a much safer method. The 
last few years and coming years a lot of applicants coming in with these. May end up with 30 to 40 in the 
ski area boundaries. Snowbird works with Alta on avalanche mitigation , and A lta is installing Wyssen 
Towers as well.  Snowbird’s towers need to be done in tandem with them or will no longer be able to shoot 
towards the ridgeline. The weather we get by end of September may provide one good snowstorm, so there 
is a tight construction timeline; urge the commission help streamline so towers can be installed before bad 
weather.  
 
Commissioners and applicant had a brief discussion regarding the need for safety, Army phasing out the 
howitzer, and the mechanics of the tower --snow depth sensor, the charge calibrated to go down a certain 
distance and high off the snow, etc.
