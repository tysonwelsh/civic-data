PUBLIC COMMENT:

Chris O’Neill approached the city council about ongoing concerns in Chapel View Subdivision with
settling and concrete cracking. After initial settling in 2022 a company came in and lifted the concrete.
That caused some major cracking in the concrete. Chris sent an email with photos around October 2023
and was told someone would come look at it, but didn’t know if that happened because he received no
other response. Chris had also reached out to Councilor JD Parady via text and there was some
confusion that he hadn’t known there was a problem. O’Neill had a major crack in some new concrete in
his driveway and one panel of sidewalk in front of his home is lower and fears it may be a tripping
hazard. He came to city council meeting to see if there was any remediation that could be done. He
wanted to request that more information be given in the future that lifting concrete can cause cracking
to raise awareness to those having work done near their homes. Mayor Seely said they would follow up
and thanked Mr. O’Neill for the information.

DISCUSSION ON CENTRAL COMMERCIAL ZONE:

City Administrator Seth Atkinson stated that the Central Commercial zone was discussed in a previous
City Council meeting– specifically, about whether or not to allow drive-through services in the Central
Commercial Zone. There are currently several businesses who have drive-through services for financial
banking uses. If one of these businesses were to change uses to a restaurant, the current zoning does
not allow for a drive-through service. The Council briefly discussed the possibility of changing the zone
to allow for these types of uses.

The Staff wanted direction from the Council on whether or not to allow for these types of uses. The
Central Commercial zone is a true commercial zone with very limited residential uses unlike the hybrid
Combined Use zones. He suggested it may be worth having the Planning Commission discuss the matter
more thoroughly since the Central Commercial zone is similar to the Highway Commercial zone and
would promote economic development in the heart of the city. The Staff recommended requesting the
Planning Commission explore this possibility and forward a recommendation to the City Council for
consideration.

Councilor Travis Worwood suggested the Planning Commission also look at the 5000 ft2 limitation for
commercial development in the same CU1 zone.

Councilor JD Parady was curious about the architectural standards in the Central Commercial area. He
felt things looked a little disorganized in those areas. Mr. Atkinson explained that the city is very involved
up to plat approval, but not much involvement after that. He also stated that the state has banned some
architectural standards.

RESOLUTION 03-18-2025 - MUNICIPAL WASTEWATER PLANNING PROGRAM:
City Administrator Seth Atkinson presented the annual self assessment report for the Nephi City Sewer
System to be adopted. The report helps the water and sewer department identify deficiencies and
problem areas within the City wide sewer system. The City is required to submit this annual report to
the department of environmental quality (DEQ) and more specifically a sub-group of DEQ known as the
Water Quality Board each year for their review. As part of that review DEQ requires that the City
Council review the Municipal Wastewater Planning Program Self Assessment Report for 2023 and
confirm that all appropriate actions have been taken to maintain effluent requirements in accordance
with the Utah Pollutant Discharge Elimination System (UPDES) Permit.

While completing the annual self assessment report it was noted that there are some improvements
that need to be completed. This is consistent with the recent sewer master plan update. There are a
number of capital improvements that will need to be made and rates will need to adjust to fund these
improvements.

It was recommended that the City Council adopt the Resolution 03-18-2025.

Councilor Travis Worwood made the motion to adopt Resolution 03-18-2025–Municipal Wastewater
Planning Program. Councilor Skip Worwood seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.

WORK SESSION ITEMS:

    1.​ Seth Atkinson informed the council of discussion of a fee for water callouts. He said they had
        talked with the irrigation company. The fee would be applied if our guys were called out after
        hours. They were looking at $100/hr on a call out with a one hour minimum. That fee would
        include labor and a truck roll out for the equipment. He said if the council was ok with that
        amount they would bring back a master resolution with that added to it.
    2.​ Mr. Atkinson introduced Brace Carroll who was in attendance to give some input concerning
        cemetery fees. Mr. Carroll stated that they have looked at fees for non-resident rates and
        discovered that they were somewhat low compared to communities north of us. He declared
        that this gives an incentive for people up north to select our cemetery and that reduces the
        amount of space for residents. He had also spoken with Mona and compared our rates to
        theirs. They had just increased their rates. Lisa Brough shared Nephi’s current fee rates and at
        least 2 incidents in the last couple months of people having no ties to Nephi requesting to be
        buried here simply because of our rates. Both Mr. Carroll and Mrs. Brough presented different
        fee structures including rates for residents and non-residents within and outside the county.
        The discussion included the impact these changes would have on existing burial rights and the
        need for staff to track and manage these changes. The council agreed to request a
        recommendation from the staff on new cemetery fees and the potential for additional
        cemetery space.
