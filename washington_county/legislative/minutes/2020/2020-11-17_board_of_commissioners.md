PUBLIC HEARING: COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT BLOCK GRANT (CDBG)

2021 FISCAL YEAR

Chairman Iverson stated that the purpose of this public hearing is to provide citizens with
pertinent information about the Community Development Block Grant program and to
allow for discussion of possible applications for the 2021 funding cycle. The grant money
must be spent on projects benefiting primarily low- and moderate-income persons. The
Five County Association of Governments, in which Washington County is a member, is
expecting to receive approximately $907,269 in this new program year.

All eligible activities that can be accomplished under this program are identified in the
CDBG Application Policies and Procedures Manual and interested persons can review it
at any time. Examples of eligible activities include construction of public works and
facilities such as water and sewer lines and fire stations. Other examples of eligible
activities also include acquisition of real property and provision of public services such as
food banks or homeless shelters. In the past Washington County has received 23 grants
that have supported transitional and affordable housing, community facilities remodels,
purchased meals on wheels trucks and fire trucks, updated water systems, and expanded
fire stations. We handed out our capital investment plan as part of the regional
“Consolidated Plan”. This list shows which projects the city has identified as being needed
in the community.

County Administrator Nicholle Felshaw mentioned that there are several entities that have
expressed interest in the grant.

CONSIDERATION OF RESOLUTION NO. _R-2020-2675, A RESOLUTION
APPROVING A COOPERATIVE AGREEMENT BETWEEN WASHINGTON
COUNTY_AND_ THE UTAH DIVISION OF WILDLIFE RESOURCES FOR
STUDIES, SURVEYS, AND ACTIVITIES TO BE CONDUCTED IN THE
FURTHERANCE OF THE HABITAT CONSERVATION PLAN

Habitat Conservation Plan (HCP) Director Cameron Rognan explained that there are two
previous commitments agreed to in the HCP renewal process. The first is to revise the
translocation plan within two (2) years; and the second is to revise the areas where surveys
are performed and clearances of Desert Tortoises within five (5) years. The Division of
Wildlife Services (DWS) has agreed to assist Washington County in monitoring,
surveying, and safely transitioning or removing the Desert Tortoise.

Commissioner Cox recognized Mr. Rognan for his dedication to the HCP and spearheading
the renewal of the Plan.

Habitat Conservation Plan. Motion seconded by Commissioner
