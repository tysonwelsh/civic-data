Subject: Council Members Sharkey Proposed Amendments to the FY26 Tentative Budget

Description: Reduction of the proposed Storm Water Fee Increase
Proposal Summary:
Each year during our budget process we are tasked with deciding how much of our rising costs
we will ask our residents to bear. While the name of the fee we are asked to increase changes
from budget year to budget year, those who pay the fees don’t. They are our constituents, our
neighbors, those who we have been tasked to watch over. Our Storm Water Fee does need to
be increased. This is clear when considering just the current and upcoming capital needs of the
department. What is also clear is that this Council has been consistent in favoring a steady
incremental approach to fee increases, rather than significant sudden increases. While the
absolute cost of the increase is just $2.50 a month, this calculates as a 42% increase from our
current fee of $6.00. This is a lot to ask our residents to shoulder and a more incremental
approach would soften the impact. The proposed rate is projected to generate $1.7 million
dollars in revenue annually. Over a 10-year period that amounts to 17 million dollars in our
Storm Water Fund. My proposal contains two options with lower rates that would generate
that same 17 million dollars, just over a longer period. For your consideration I have rates that
would generate the same amount of revenue, just over a period of 15 years or 20 years rather
than 10. These are shown in the table below:

If there is support for either of my alternative rates, it is my recommendation that our Public
Utilities staff examine the reduction in revenue and make adjustments to their expenditures
accordingly.
Justification:
Over the past few years this Council has been asked to increase the cost of being a Sandy
resident through tax increases, water rate increases, bulk waste fee increases, etc. Those
increases were difficult and necessary, but they were also prudent and measured. Each time we
have been asked to pass costs on to our residents we have found a way to lower the impact by
adopting a more incremental approach. Once more I am proposing that we continue that
approach. What I have proposed allows our Storm Water Fund to begin needed work on critical
capital projects while we smooth out the impact of rate increases by a much lower percentage.
