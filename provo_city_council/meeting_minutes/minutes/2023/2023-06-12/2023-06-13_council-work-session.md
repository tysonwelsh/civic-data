1.       A training on Council meeting procedures. (23-002) (00:08:30)

Brian Jones, City Attorney, gave a brief training on the procedures for making motions in
Council meetings. He explained the intricacies and what is permitted as far as making motions,
amending motions, and subsequent motions, noting that no more than three motions may be on
the table at any one time. Mr. Jones recommended always restating the motion prior to taking a
vote. Mr. Jones also cautioned against multiple Councilors editing a motion as it was being
made. If a Councilor was still in the formative stages of deciding what their motion should be, he
suggested that the Councilor share an idea, and discuss that idea first. After a motion is made, if
another Councilor asks if a motion included something, the original motion maker should clarify
the intent of their motion and ask if the second still stands. If this new comment was not
contemplated or explicitly included, they should say it was not their intent and the commenter is
welcome to make a substitute motion or amend the motion to that effect. Mr. Jones noted that the
intent is to preserve the formality of the process while also allowing some flexibility to the
Councilors as they are formulating their ideas. Councilor Rachel Whipple shared her thoughts
from the perspective of someone making a motion and asked for clarification about the formal
procedure. Justin Harrison, Council Executive Director, also briefly commented on the nature of

2.      A discussion regarding unencumbered ARPA funds. (23-049) (00:22:21)

Justin Harrison, Council Executive Director, presented. Mr. Harrison gave background
information about the ARPA fund allocations approved by the Council. With the recent
agreements in Congress regarding the debt ceiling, one provision in the Fiscal Responsibility Act
of 2023 was to rescind unobligated ARPA funds. The language is very ambiguous, but there is
the potential for the City to lose any of its unspent or unallocated funds. Staff thought it would be
prudent to bring this to the Council for further discussion and consideration. Council Chair
Katrice MacKay thought it would be most prudent to allocate the funds.

Councilor Shannon Ellsworth thought it would be good to use the unallocated funds for water
conservation initiatives. She suggested implementing smart controllers at City parks. She also
suggested flipping the strips on Center Street where there is turf. Wayne Parker, CAO, shared
some insight about smart controllers in city parks and some of the factors impacting irrigation.
Ms. MacKay asked about the urgency of the ARPA fund allocations. Mr. Parker said they have
spoken with Congressional staff and everything they have heard is about the federal government
reclaiming funds from federal agencies. If certain municipalities have missed the deadlines, then
they will no longer receive the funds. However, if funding has already been allocated to a city,
those dollars are considered encumbered. Ms. MacKay suggested they asked for Parks and
Recreation to come to a Work Meeting. Mr. Parker said they could facilitate that for a future
Work Meeting. Presentation only.

3.      A presentation regarding FY24 tentative budget amendments. (23-008) (00:31:00)

Kelsey Zarbock, Budget Officer, presented. She shared a list of the proposed amendments to the
tentative budget and reviewed each change with more context. Councilor Rachel Whipple asked
about the certified tax rate from the County. Ms. Zarbock explained that the property valuations
had dropped slightly, which impacted the City’s resulting revenue from property tax. Ms.
Zarbock continued with the line-by-line review. Councilor David Shipley asked if there were
usually a $10,000 increase in expenses, would they try to allocate that equally. Ms. Zarbock
explained that for this particular cost, this fits into the General Fund divisions portion of the
chargeback, so it was split across departments. Councilor Travis Hoban asked about how they
typically account for shortfalls in the budget. Ms. Zarbock explained that the miscellaneous
revenue account really is miscellaneous, so they try to avoid using this account to balance the
budget. John Borget, Administrative Services Director, explained they also avoid adjusting sales
tax revenues, as there is a calculation they use to forecast sales tax. Mr. Shipley asked why the
County had two different calculations of valuation, one with revenues increasing and one with
revenues decreasing. Ms. Zarbock explained her conversation with Utah County—there were
some additional data points entered in by the County on Friday related to redevelopment areas
which was what caused the changes. Mr. Shipley asked why the City versus the Library’s
budgets moved in opposite directions. Ms. Zarbock clarified that both budgets moved in the
same direction, just by different amounts. Ms. Whipple said it would be helpful to get a better

4.      A discussion regarding property tax (23-008) (00:48:14)

Justin Harrison, Council Executive Director, presented. Council Chair Katrice MacKay
suggested they move right to discussion as the Council has had a previous presentation on this.
Mr. Harrison said they would look at the library and general operations property tax rates
separately. Councilor Travis Hoban asked if they could briefly review the highlights of each of
the entities. Mr. Harrison reviewed the recommendation from the Library Board. Mr. Hoban
asked if the Council would need to do Truth in Taxation to change the Library rate, which Mr.
Harrison indicated was the case. Councilor Shannon Ellsworth shared some comments as a
member of the Library Board. She thought they needed to have a more comprehensive facilities
plan in order to properly plan for the facility’s capital needs. Mr. Hoban wondered if the Library
has said where specifically they would spend this money. Councilor David Shipley said that
Carla Gordon, Library Director, recently presented and said that every year they are using funds
from their general reserves for facilities needs. Councilor George Handley asked about what the
ask from the Library was. Mr. Harrison indicated that the Library asked for a specific dollar
amount for each year over the next five years. Mr. Handley clarified that he wondered if the
requested increase was commensurate with the typical inflation or cost-of-living increase of 4%.
Mr. Shipley said the Library’s requested amount was under 4% of their total budget. Mr. Shipley
thought they were trying to be very conservative about this. Ms. Whipple thought it was a
conservative solution but it did not solve their issue entirely.

Mr. Shipley noted again that they cannot commit a future Council to this. In the last discussion
he asked how much they would need up front if this Council were to do a larger, one-time
increase. Councilors discussed the possible increase for the Library. Mr. Shipley noted that the
Council could do a one-time increase that was less than $500,000 and due to the Library earning
interest they could still accomplish their goal with a smaller one-time property tax increase. In
response to a question from Mr. Shipley, Brian Jones, City Attorney, said the Council must
notice the maximum rate they would consider adopting for the two different tax rates. Mr. Jones
said it was most helpful for staff to know as soon as possible if there was consensus among the
Council on whether to do Truth-in-Taxation at all. Ms. Zarbock said they needed to alert the
County by June 22 if the Council intends to pursue Truth-in-Taxation and must submit the final
rate by June 30.

Motion:        David Shipley moved to move forward on Truth-in-Taxation on the requested
amount from the Library Board of $100,000. Seconded by Katrice MacKay. Mr. Shipley
rescinded this motion.

Councilors discussed the Truth-in-Taxation process and the other taxing entities in the area. Mr.
Handley suggested the Council consider the amount for public safety so they could consider the
total dollar amount impacting residents. The Council discussed a potential increase to property
tax for public safety. Mr. Harrison reviewed previously presented information about inflation.

Mr. Shipley shared comments—sales tax is strong now, but it may not always be the case, so he
thought it was important to plan for public safety with property tax with a nominal adjustment
each year. Ms. Ellsworth asked if the Council was budgeting for outcomes within the public

property tax increase to specific public safety needs. Ms. MacKay asked how many police
officers were not fulfilled in the budget. Mayor Michelle Kaufusi said that four were requested as

Mr. Shipley noted that sales tax revenue does typically increase with inflation, although the
property tax revenue does not. Ms. Whipple thought the property tax revenue should go up every

tax increase to a public safety officer; it could be an increase simply to account for inflation. Mr.

Shipley shared comments on keeping up with inflation each year with the property tax rate. Mr.
Handley suggested the Council consider the maximum portion for the City as the rate which will
cost the average household $7.80 and the maximum amount for the Library of $300,000. Mr.
Shipley said this was not accomplishing his desired outcome of pre-funding the Library. Ms.
MacKay indicated that Central Utah Water Conservancy District would likely do a 4% increase.

Captain Brian Taylor, Provo Police Department, was present to answer any questions from the
Council. Mr. Shipley summarized the Council’s intent to have some additional funding each year
through Truth-in-Taxation to fund public safety. The Council’s understanding is that even if the
Council added an officer each year for the next several years, there would still be a need as they
would still be short of the ideal number. Captain Taylor said that understaffing hurts their ability
to provide public safety to the community, particularly at the airport and in patrol.

The Council continued their discussion. Mr. Shipley wanted to make this year’s increase as
minimal as possible due to a high increase in property taxes for Provo residents last year. Mr.
Handley restated his motion. Mr. Shipley was not in favor of the 5% increase for the general
operations rate that Mr. Handley was proposing. Mr. Hoban preferred noticing at the lower
levels; he was comfortable with 3% on the City side and $100,000 on the Library side. Ms.
Ellsworth also said she was comfortable with that.

                the City as the maximum. Seconded by Katrice MacKay.

5.      A discussion regarding the FY24 budget adoption process. (23-008) (02:24:04)

Kelsey Zarbock, Budget Officer, presented. Ms. Zarbock reviewed the categories of things that
are addressed slightly differently in the budget ordinance this year. She reviewed the past process
and noted that they have added several of the historically agreed-upon carryover categories into
the budget, in order to ensure that the Council is supportive of those categories and uses of
carryover funds, including equipment maintenance and replacement, ongoing projects, and other
expenses (elections, tuition reimbursements, and indigent defense). Councilor Shannon Ellsworth
asked if this approach satisfies the intent of the former Council Carryovers Committee.
Councilor David Shipley said he understood that this approach (the color-coded chart and the
standard categories) satisfied the intent of that committee. Mr. Shipley noted that the full list of
carryovers is brought to the full Council. Ms. Zarbock noted they may indicate one-time
carryovers (versus carryovers that occur every year) if that information would be helpful.

Ms. Zarbock continued with her presentation. She outlined how the sick leave buy-out process
works for City employees. As they have looked back at several years of past budgets, they
observed that many departments are not utilizing all the sick leave buy-out. This formula was
pretty accurate in the end, although there were several areas in the General Fund that utilized
