Minutes of the regular work session of the Ogden City Planning Commission held August 19,
2020. Meeting was conducted by Chair Graf and began at 5:37pm.
Members Present:      Dave Graf, Chair
                      Jenny Sandau, Vice-Chair
                      Cathy Blaisdell
                      Wesley Boykin
                      Neil Garner
                      Rick Safsten
                      Bryan Schade
                      Rick Southwick
Members Excused:      Jessica Stoker
Staff Present:        Greg Montgomery, Planning Manager
                      Jannette Borklund, Planning Technician
                      Mark Stratford, City Attorney

1. Discussion, Pending Ordinance: Apartments and Group Dwellings in Commercial
   zones
   Mr. Montgomery stated the Planning Staff had become concerned with the recent surge of
   apartment development, stating in 2018 there were 122 new units approved and between
   2019 and the current time, over 1,500 units have been considered. Due to this concern, the
   City Council had adopted a pending ordinance relating to multi-family uses in commercial
   zones, which gives the City 180 days to adopt an ordinance, which is coming due October
   18, 2020.
   He stated there are varieties of reasons why housing units are requested, including the fact
   that Utah is a stable market in terms of housing and employment, land is available and
   affordable, and financing is available for multi-unit projects. While higher density is desirable
   in the downtown, thee projects are coming to other commercial areas as well. He suggested
   some reasons there has been less interest in the downtown might be due to the higher cost
   of property, the additional design standards of the downtown, and the RDA has not been
   actively pursuing residential in the downtown as the River Project is incomplete.
   He stated Staff has analyzed some of the current projects relating to their long-term livability,
   as well as long-term consequences of poorly designed or placed projects, and is considering
   ordinance language to address some of these concerns. He stated the regulations would
   include both apartments, defined as one building with multi-family dwellings, and group
   dwellings, which are identified as more than one building on a lot. Either scenario could then
   be either rented or developed as condominium units. Important items identified by Staff
   include site design, open space, parking and screening.
   He stated while there is an abundance of commercially zoned property, care should be taken
   to assure prime commercial space is not lost. In considering where multi-family projects are
   and are not desirable, the first is consideration of the goals and policies of the General Plan.
   He stated when the Plan was adopted in 2002, areas were identified as potential locations of
   village centers which would be a combination of commercial and high-density residential
   uses. It also states multi-family hosing is more desirable along transportation corridors.
   Village Centers are to be well designed and have a mix of uses. Corridors are typically a
   good location for both commercial and high-density residential, and consideration needs to
   be given as to where commercial, residential or a mix of residential and commercial are most
   appropriate. In identifying particular locations, there is a balance between the creation of

jobs and services needed to support residential development, while residential uses often
are needed to support commercial uses, and both compete for space along transit corridors.
He stated Staff has identified around 74 acres of underutilized or undeveloped property in
Ogden City where multi-family projects could be located. He stated this effort is to develop
standards for development rather than allowing the market to dictate development projects.
Commissioner Southwick expressed concern that there are standards for multi-family
development in high density residential zones which are currently not required for the same
project in a commercial zone and felt both should have the same standards. He also
expressed concern with the balance of rentals verses owner-occupied units, stating some
are approved with the anticipation they will be owner-occupied, but then become rentals. It
was indicated even if a project is subdivided as condominium units, these can still become
rentals. While lot size may dictate building type, with townhomes being developed on
smaller parcels, whether a unit is owned or rented is connected to both the economy and the
intent of the developer. Commissioner Boykin noted a person may favor a rental unit at
various stages of life. Discussion continued relating to demographics of apartment dwellers,
housing options as well as poverty and the choices available. Mr. Montgomery stated there
are becoming more situations where multi-generations live in one house as well as the
potential increased demand for boarding houses due to the cost of developing multiple
kitchens and bathrooms. He felt it is important for residents to have the ability to purchase
and own property, whether they be condominium units or single homes. While the majority
of new construction is anticipated to be rentals, some may return for conversion to
condominiums at a later date if the project is designed so this can occur.
Discussion continued relating to single-use commercial areas and the need for a variety of
non-residential as well as residential uses in relation to identifying where multi-family uses
are most appropriate. It is assumed there is a need for commercial development as tenants
in multi-family projects have a need for nearby services within walking distance. While these
multi-family projects might be desirable along transit corridors, the ability to cross streets
should be considered in their placement. Nearby employment opportunities also should be
considered, including office uses, industrial uses and commercial uses. Mr. Montgomery
indicated some commercial investors wait for density to be established before committing to
a particular area, yet the residential tenants would desire nearby services which may not
exist. Commission discussed the existing retail at both 12th Street and Wall Avenue and the
Newgate Mall in relation to the retail climate and potential for potential housing in these
areas. Commission felt it is important for retail and office space to be on the ground floor in
these commercial nodes, noting a residential structure should have a setback in order that
the tenants have privacy, commercial or office uses can be closer to the street.
Commissioner Sandau stated retail or office on the ground floor with upper story apartments
seems to work well. Mr. Montgomery noted while a vertical mixed use is desirable, based on
recent history in Ogden, the housing fills quickly while the retail might remain vacant for a
time. Commissioner Blaisdell felt the placement and fit of mufti-family is important, and
some existing projects appear to be mis-placed, particularly along 12th Street. She felt the
current standards allow these to be too close to the busy street, and the fact crossing 12th
Street as a pedestrian is impossible.

Mr. Montgomery then discussed the village center concept contained in the General Plan,
stating 5-Points is one area identified as a potential area for the gathering of both
commercial and residential uses. While some of the retail stores have since become vacant,
there is a need to preserve commercial opportunities in this area and allow adequate lot
areas for new commercial uses anticipating a mix of use is more desirable than the area
becoming multi-family, indicating if this were to occur, apartments may not be sustainable as

   buildings, it might also be important to wait for the right project and/or developer.

   The final discussion topic is relating to design standards for multi-family development. Mr.
   Montgomery stated in his developer interviews one had stated that Ogden has a particular
   character and the City can determine to be like everyone else, or to retain Ogden’s character
   and soul. Design standards include setback, open space, building material and density. He
   stated architectural style and amenities should be cohesive with the surrounding uses, with
   buildings closer to the street and parking to the rear. Discussion related to desirable
   amenities for multi-family projects as well as vertical mixed-use amenities which might
   include meeting rooms, office space, on-site recreation uses, etc.
   Commission felt it is appropriate to adopt standards which would require new construction to
   complement existing construction. Standards should be adopted to require a sense of
   community, defined entrances, building material and design. Commission felt the main level
   of new multi-family construction should be a quality material such as brick or stone, while
   upper stories could have less durable materials. They also indicated a front setback should
   be required in order to create privacy for tenants, with landscaping and other details to be
   the same standards as exists for the same projects in residential zones. It was felt there
   should be convenient connections to parking lots and to on-site amenities and green space,
   and a defined front entryway.
   Discussion continued relating to on-site storage, and whether the storage of recreational
   vehicles should be allowed in guest parking areas. It was indicated while several options for
   amenities might be identified in the Ordinance, there should be some ability for the
   developer to select these based on his anticipated tenants.
   Commission also discussed parking standards, and whether these should be defined as a
   minimum or maximum requirement. While the typical parking requirement is two stalls per
   unit, along transit corridors this is reduced to 1.5 per unit. Some developers reduce parking
   in an effort to reduce the cost of the development, while others still provide two stalls per unit
   in an effort to satisfy tenant demand. Commission felt standards are important to address
   entrances, parking and design. Commission also felt these standards should be clearly
   defined in the ordinance. They felt it is important to level the playing field with the same
   standards established for uses in commercial zones as those in residential zones.
   Commission also discussed the change in retail and office use and the ability to work at
   home, stating since the COVID-19 pandemic has begun there is are increased technologies
   and abilities to do more electronically with less personal contact, allowing people to work at
   home and many purchases done electronically, and whether this might affect the future of
   new commercial or office uses.

There being no additional business before the Commission, the meeting was adjourned at
7:35pm.
