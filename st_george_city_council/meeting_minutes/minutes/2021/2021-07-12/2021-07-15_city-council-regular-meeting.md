     (Residential Single Family, 10,000 sq ft minimum lots) to PD-R (Planned
     Development Residential) on approximately 12.24 acres generally located
     on the northwest corner of Gap Canyon Parkway and Divario Canyon Drive
     known as Rilassante at Divario (Divario PA 17). Case No. 2021-ZC-028
St. George City Council Minutes

      Planner Dan Boles presented the request to change the zone from R-1-10 to PD-R on
      approximately 12.24 acres generally located on the northwest corner of Gap Canyon
      Parkway and Divario Canyon Drive known as Rilassante at Divario. He provided a
      PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics: Existing zoning map; Existing
      general plan map; Previously approved site plan (2008); Proposed site plan; and
      Project data table. After being asked by Councilmember Larkin, Mr. Boles explained
      there is proposed to be two- and three-story buildings.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard asked staff about the specific grading to minimize the
      impact of the height of the buildings and what mechanisms does the City have in
      place to make sure the plan is followed as per the commitments made by the
      developer.

      Community Development Director John Willis noted staff can check the finish grade
      when looking at the construction drawings and ask the applicant to provide the
      elevations and finish grade elevations to make sure the developer is trying to
      minimize the impact due to the height of the buildings. After being asked by City
      Manager Adam Lenhard, Mr. Willis commented this proposal falls within the approved
      master plan.

      Councilmember McArthur inquired if the current master plan allows the City to
      change where the density goes.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard explained staff does not believe that is the case.
      According to the agreement, the developer can choose to move the densities;
      however, the City is not allowed to unilaterally make that decision. To this point, the
      developer has told staff they do not intend to move the densities around. He asked
      the developer’s representative if they are willing to move the density to a different
      planning area.

      Applicant Mark Teepen commented they have visited this issue several times. They
      like the master plan as it has been agreed to and they do not feel there is any reason
      to change it.

      Councilmember Larkin repeated what was said at the June 24th meeting – the City
      Manager explained the City is constrained by the legally binding development
      agreement, PA17 has always been identified as a high-density pod since before the
      existing homes were built. Further, per the agreement, the developer has a right to
      bring forth an application showing high density as it complies with the plan. At that
      meeting, the City Attorney advised there is a development agreement in place and
      the developer has come in according to the plan and development agreement and
      noted to put all of the high density into one area makes it not as livable for those
      living in apartments. She is aware that a number of nearby residents are frustrated;
      however, this has been a long time coming.

PRELIMINARY PLAT:
     Consider approval of the preliminary plat for La Entrada, a 20-lot residential
     subdivision located approximately at the intersection of Magatsu Drive and
     Snow Canyon Parkway within the Entrada development.

      Assistant Public Works Director Wes Jenkins presented the preliminary plat for La
      Entrada, a 20-lot residential subdivision located approximately at the intersection of
      Magatsu Drive and Snow Canyon Parkway within the Entrada development. He
      showed and outlined the preliminary plat, noting that there is an existing regional
      sewer line on the west side of the property and several 48” storm drains in the area
      as well that discharge water onto this property. The Wastewater Department will be
      upsizing the sewer line in the current fiscal year. Staff will be working with the
      developer on a cost participation agreement for the sewer and storm drain. The
      applicant is requesting a gate as this is part of the Entrada master plan.
      Additionally, they are requesting to eliminate the sidewalk on one side of the road.
      He noted that the City does not have a sewer easement on this property.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard pointed out Entrada has done well with utilizing
      xeriscape landscaping, he hopes to see that continue. With regards to the remaining
      preliminary plats, the City is working on standards for future development with
      regards to water conservation measures. He hopes that the City and developers can
      approach this proactively and aggressively pursue xeriscape as outdoor landscaping,
      anything that can minimize the use of water.

      Assistant Public Works Director Wes Jenkins outlined the conditions as follows:
      working with staff on a cost participation agreement; relocation of the existing sewer
      line and storm drain line; elimination of the sidewalk; and allowing this to be gated.
      He noted that he is okay with the elimination of the sidewalk.

      MOTION:
          A motion was made by Councilmember Curtis to approve the
