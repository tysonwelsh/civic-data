40   Mayor Buroker emphasized the importance of establishing a clear, long-term vision for Riverton’s
41   future. She noted the General Plan should reflect and support that vision. She referenced recent
42   development tours and stated that her evaluation of projects was influenced less by residential
43   density and more by whether developments promote community connectivity, including walkability,
44   green space, and overall design quality.
45
46   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained the purpose of the discussion was to
47   identify desired community characteristics, values, and development patterns to guide future
48   decisions related to housing, commercial development, and land use. Participants were invited to
49   provide feedback on development projects they found successful or unsuccessful, with emphasis
50   on community character, design, density, and quality of life.

27            Sentinel Ridge Median
28
29   Matt Cassel, City Engineer, discussed traffic mitigation at Sentinel Ridge and nearby intersections,
30   prompted by updated traffic studies associated with the Mountain Ridge development and a 235-
31   unit apartment complex that had not been included in earlier analysis. He explained that the revised
32   2023 study showed congestion and potential intersection failures occurring sooner than previously
33   anticipated, particularly at Sentinel Ridge and 13400 South, as well as at nearby intersections such
34   as Templer and Ledger. Developers had been directed to provide mitigation measures intended to
35   ease, rather than fully resolve, anticipated traffic impacts.
36
37   Councilmembers and staff discussed several mitigation options, including a traffic signal,
38   roundabout, and various median configurations ranging from partial to full closures of left-turn
39   movements. A roundabout was noted by some participants as a preferred option for maintaining
40   traffic flow, though concerns were raised regarding feasibility, right-of-way impacts, and developer
41   acceptance. Other participants expressed concern that signalization or median restrictions could
42   shift congestion to other nearby intersections, particularly given the close spacing of signals along
43   13400 South and Sentinel Ridge. The discussion also acknowledged uncertainty about future
44   regional transportation changes, including the potential influence of the Mountain View Corridor. No
45   final decision was made, and further evaluation in closed session was suggested before proceeding
46   with direction to the developer.

27            Ordinance No. 26-12 – Amending Section 9.20.010(1), Adopting Standards for the
28            Discharge of a Firearm as Part of the Firearm Manufacturing Process, Amendments
29            Proposed by Zev Technologies
30
31   Mr. Lethbridge presented a proposed citywide amendment to Riverton’s code regarding firearm
32   discharge regulations, explaining that current code prohibits the discharge of firearms within City
33   limits except at approved and licensed gun ranges. The proposed amendment, submitted by a
34   business owner, would allow a limited exception for firearm discharge within controlled, enclosed,
35   and licensed facilities for the purpose of manufacturing and testing firearms or firearm
36   accessories. He noted that the language had been reviewed and revised in coordination with the
37   City Attorney’s office and Riverton Police Department to ensure appropriate safety and regulatory
38   controls.
39
40   Mr. Lethbridge further clarified that the amendment would not change existing restrictions on
41   recreational shooting or establish additional allowances for gun ranges, but would narrowly apply
42   to licensed manufacturing and testing operations under strict oversight. Because the proposal is a
43   code amendment rather than a land use action, it was not reviewed by the Planning Commission.
44   Staff recommended approval of the amendment as presented.
45
46   Mayor Buroker opened the public hearing and called for public comments. There being none, she
47   closed the public hearing.
48

12            Ordinance No. 26-13 – Amending Sections 18.90, 18.056.030, and 18.195.060,
13            adopting standards for “Ambulance Service/Emergency Medical Services Facility”
14            in the Commercial Gateway Zone, Amendments proposed by Gold Cross
15            Ambulance
16
17   Mr. Lethbridge presented a proposed text amendment to the City’s commercial use table to allow
18   ambulance services within certain commercial zones, explaining that current code classifies
19   ambulance services under motor vehicle services and does not permit them in the commercial
20   gateway zone. The amendment was initiated in response to a request from Gold Cross
21   Ambulance and would reclassify ambulance and emergency medical services as a conditional use
22   in the commercial regional, PCC, and gateway zones, while prohibiting them in neighborhood
23   professional office and downtown zones, and adding a formal definition for the use.
24
25   Mr. Lethbridge explained that ambulance facilities would be subject to specific conditional use
26   standards, including requirements for indoor vehicle storage, restricted siren use until vehicles
27   enter public roads, no public walk-in services, and site design compatibility with surrounding
28   development. It was noted that these facilities would include staging and operational space for
29   emergency medical personnel, including rest areas during shifts, but would not include patient
30   care. The Planning Commission recommended approval of the amendment, and staff concurred.
31
32   Mayor Buroker opened the public hearing and called for public comments. There being none, she
33   closed the public hearing.
34

36   18.056.030, and 18.195.060, adopting standards for “Ambulance Service/Emergency
37   Medical Services Facility” in the Commercial Gateway Zone, Amendments proposed by
38   Gold Cross Ambulance. Councilmember Haymond SECONDED the motion. Mayor Buroker
39   called for a vote; the vote was as follows: Haymond-Yes, Johnson-Yes, McDougal-Yes, Pierucci-
40   Yes, and Smith-Yes. The motion passed unanimously.
