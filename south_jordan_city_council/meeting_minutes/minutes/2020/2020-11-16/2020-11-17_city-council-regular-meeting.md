Athena Awards. She said she attended the My Care Grand opening with Governor Herbert and

ACM Lewis said the city received an email that Ty Montalvo and the Building Department were

approximately 25 years. He thanked Mr. Montalvo for his efforts and the work he and his team

                  H.2. Presentation: UDOT update Bangerter Highway project(s). (By City
                  Engineer, Brad Klavano)

Marwan Harah, UDOT project manager, reviewed a prepared presentation (Attachment A). He
discussed some difficulties they have ran into, including crews testing positive, the need to
sanitize equipment, and not being able to receive supplies timely. He said the closure for 12600
South will start in February, and will be for 6 months. Both 12600 South and 10400 South will
be closed at the same time, for 3 months.

Mayor Ramsey asked for people’s patience during the project. The road won’t be closed entirely.
This plan will help the project move along faster and be safer.

Council Member Marlor asked when will 9800 South come up on the agenda? Mr. Harah said
they are in the process of trying to get funds to do the environmental document for the entire
Bangerter corridor. There are still 12 intersections that need to be converted to interchanges.
They have seen the benefits of the interchanges, from safety to traffic mobility. He said 9800
South is a top priority because traffic won’t stop for 6 miles in each direction. There is not
enough signal time to clear 60,000 cars at a time, and it will be a bottleneck. 9800 South is one
of the top priorities to get funds.

Mayor Ramsey said the State is considering a transportation bond. She thinks that is the only
way they will get the money to get it done.

Mr. Harah said finishing the interchanges will help take traffic off I-15. Mayor Ramsey said
there are other big projects that need to be done as well.

         J.       Action Item: Resolution R2020-55, authorizing the South Jordan Mayor to sign
                  Addendum #2 to Assignment and Assumption of Purchase and Sale Agreement
                  (Glenmoor Golf Course). (By City Attorney, Ryan Loose)

City Attorney Loose reviewed the background information on this item. In the assignment, there
was a put option to work out the details. It had to be exercised by last year, but neither the city or
other party were ready. They have a lot of planning and other details to work out. They are
asking for a 1 year extension on the put option.

Council Member Zander asked if there is a risk to the city to do the put option? City Attorney
Loose said there is no more risk than they have had since the assignment. If they don’t extend
this, the other party will feel that they have to trigger the put option.

Council Member Shelton made a motion to approve Resolution R2020-55. Council Member
Marlor seconded the motion.

Council Member Shelton said the process of dealing with the Glenmoor golf course was
difficult, but the result has been amazing. He noted Mr. Young’s drive to make it a good golf
course and facility, and noted the dedicated staff. He said it is the premier golf course in the area.
He said there have been a number of top golfers that have been there and been amazed at the
quality of the facility. They could not have asked for a better outcome. He said extending the
option is giving their partner the time to complete the work they need so they can transfer the
development rights to a different property and keep the golf course the jewel that it is. Mayor
Ramsey concurred.

Roll call vote. The vote was unanimous in favor.

         I.       Discussion Item: ADU Ordinance. (By Planning Director, Steven
                  Schaefermeyer)

Planning Director Schaefermeyer said Mayor Ramsey pointed out, correctly, that now may not
be the ideal time to change the Ordinance, considering potential changes coming from the
Legislature. They determined to still move forward with the discussion. He reviewed a prepared
presentation (Attachment B).

Council Member Shelton said he feels using the lot size is a better tool than the zone. Council
Member Marlor concurred.

Mr. Schaefermeyer said it is a requirement for the owner of the home to live on the property in
order to rent out the ADU.

They discussed ADU occupancy limits in surrounding cities. Mr. Schaefermeyer said he
anticipates seeing changes in West Jordan. He noted that there are some cities in the County that
don’t allow ADU’s.

They discussed the number of people living in a house, even if they’re related. Mr.
Schaefermeyer reviewed the definition of families. He said the issue/complaint typically received
is with the number of cars at the residence.

Council Member Shelton asked that staff look for a way to enforce the ADU that is less difficult,
including the total number of people allowed in the ADU and not worrying about the relation.

Mr. Schaefermeyer said the larger the ADU, the more people that can live there and the more
issues they will have with cars/parking. He said the occupancy is tied to the size of the unit.
What is the maximum square footage that they are okay with? He reviewed the size limitations
and asked them to consider if they want to limit the size of the ADU based on the size of the
main dwelling of the home, or limit the square footage of the unit.

Mr. Schaefermeyer reviewed concerns with the appearance of the exterior of the home. They
don’t want the ADU access to be visible from the street.

Mr. Schaefermeyer said he would send the information to the City Council. He asked that they
send questions, concerns, or feedback back to staff.

Mr. Schaefermeyer noted that Daybreak is not part of this discussion. The rules in Daybreak are
different.

 One or more persons related by blood, marriage, adoption or approved foster care, and
