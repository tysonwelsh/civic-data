    2.1. Discussion of the Storm Water Impact Fee Analysis, and Impact Fee Facilities Plan - Jonathan
         Bowers, City Engineer
City Engineer Jonathan Bowers explained Bowen, Collins and Associates had been hired to draft the Storm
Water Impact Fee Analysis and Impact Fee Facilities Plan. He noted he had been working closely with
Engineering Consultant Kameron Ballentine as part of these efforts. Engineer Bowers explained the fee was a
one-time charge on construction to fund the infrastructure based on the impact of the development. The fees
cover only the cost of improvements and could not be utilized to fund maintenance on the system.

Engineer Ballentine explained the actual calculation of the fees are based off of the Impact Fee Facilities Plan
along with the Impact Fee Analysis. The Storm Water fees have been calculated and prepared as follows:
$2,610.84 per acre for the west area (reduced from $3,489.79), $4,088.17 per acre for the Herriman Town Center
area (reduced from $8,041.32), and $2,610.84 per acre for the south area (increased from $1,337.48)

    7.1. Presentation of the 2021 Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District budget and area cleanup
         - Pam Roberts, Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District General Manager
Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District General Manager Pam Roberts thanked the Council for the
opportunity to present on the 2021 budget and area clean-up area efforts. She introduced the approval of the
Administrative Control Boards recent adoption of the 2021 budget and noted the District sustained the base fee
of $17 monthly for garbage collection and the $204 annual assessment for recycling collection and distribution.
Manager Roberts acknowledged the determination to raise the rates for bulk trailer rentals to help offset the full
cost of the service as well as the increase for curbside green waste collections.

Manager Roberts highlighted the modified services offered throughout the COVID-19 Pandemic which delayed
the start and operation of a modified version of the area clean-up program during 2020. Residents had been
given the option to reserve a container placed in their driveway for their use. She noted the program was initially
met with opposition; however, received many compliments from participants after receiving the service.
Manager Roberts presented the results and utilization of the program. One of the biggest benefits of the
modified program was improved efficiencies and safety with no illegal dumping or abuse from contractors and
landscapers which allowed residents to share a container and have room to dispose of their waste.

Manager Roberts indicated the District conducted a satisfaction survey for the modified area clean-up and noted
92.1-percent of Herriman respondents were satisfied with the program.

  8. Public Hearing
    8.1. Public Hearing to allow public input regarding (a) the issuance and sale of not more than
         $28,000,000 aggregate principal amount of water revenue and refunding bonds, series 2021,
         (the “bonds”) and (b) any potential economic impact that the project described herein to be
         financed with the proceeds of the bonds issued under the act may have on the private sector;
         and related matters - Alan Rae, Finance Director
Finance Director Alan Rae explained the parameters resolution for this public hearing had been conducted
during the previous City Council meeting. He highlighted the number of projects the parameters resolution
contemplated funding towards, including:
         North Herriman Water Improvements $ 1,575,000
         East Herriman Water Improvements 12,000,000
         Additional East Herriman Water Improvement 2,500,000
         Juniper Canyon Water Improvements 2,400,000
         Zone 4 Pump Station Upgrades 550,000
         Zone 5 VFD Pump Station 1,100,000

The total bonding amount would be $25,000,000 with interest not to exceed 3-percent, and partially used to
refund two existing Water Revenue Bonds from 2015 and 2017. The proposed term of the bond would not
exceed 21 years. The total payment on the new bonds was estimated to be $1,800,000 annually for the first
eleven years and approximately $1,250,000 for the remaining years. The City did not want to extend the life of
the two refunding bonds, so they would be amortized separately. Refunding Water Revenue Bonds 2015 and
2017 would result in a net present value savings of an estimated 6.761-percent net present value. These bonds
would be water revenue bonds, meaning that the revenue from the water fund would be the security for the
repayment.

Mayor Watts opened the public hearing.

No comments were offered.

Councilmember Smith moved to close the public hearing. Councilmember Ohrn seconded the motion, and all present voted aye.

    10.1. Discussion and consideration of an ordinance amending Title 8, Chapter 5 of the Herriman
          City Code related to sewage disposal and for the purpose of updating the City Code to comply
          with new state and federal requirements - Chase Andrizzi, City Attorney
City Attorney Chase Andrizzi explained the South Valley Sewer District informed the City of changes in state
and federal regulations which have required an update to pretreatment rules. Entities within the service area of
the Sewer District is required to update their pretreatment ordinance prior to January 17, 2021. The proposed
changes deal with substances which are prohibited from being discharged into the City’s sewer system. The
change would instead prohibit the discharge of substances which are listed in the District Rules and Regulations
Section 2.2. This list will be made available on the City’s website.

Councilmember Smith moved to approve Ordinance No. 2021-01 adopting an amendment to Herriman City Code Title 8, Chapter
5 regarding sewage disposal. Councilmember Shields seconded the motion.
