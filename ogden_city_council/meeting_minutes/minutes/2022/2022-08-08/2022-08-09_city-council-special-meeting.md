Proposed Ordinance 2022-39 amending the zoning map to reclassify
property located at 171 Franklin Street and 162-170 35th Street from R-1-5
to CP-3 to expand the Young Ford auto dealership property; AND
Proposed Ordinance 2022-40 vacating a portion of Franklin Street between
Wall Avenue and Lincoln Avenue at approximately 171 Franklin Street

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed rezone of 171 Franklin Street
and 162-170 35" Street came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Curtis Groft, representing the Young
Automotive Group, has filed a petition to rezone three properties to the east of the Young Ford dealership on Wall Avenue. The
properties, which include 171 Franklin Street and 162 and 170 35th Street, are currently owned by the Young Automotive Group
and are zoned R-1-5 (Low Density Residential). The petitioner is requesting these properties be rezoned to CP-3 (Regional
Commercial) to allow for the expansion of the dealership.

An additional memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed vacation of a
portion of Franklin Street came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Mr. Curtis Groft, representing the Young
Automotive Group, has filed a petition to rezone three properties to the east of the Young Ford dealership on Wall Avenue. The
properties, which include 171 Franklin Street and 162 and 170 35th Street, are currently owned by the Young Automotive Group
and are zoned R-1-5 (Low Density Residential). The petitioner is requesting vacation to allow for the expansion of the dealership.

Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memos and stated the Planning Commission reviewed the zoning change
proposal at its February 2, 2022 meeting and forwarded a recommendation of approval to the Council with a vote of 6-0. The
recommendation was made with the following findings:

e [The proposal] will provide a definite edge to development and buffering between types of uses by:
0 restricting vehicle access on Franklin Street (as a requirements of the street vacation permit).
© installing the most intense screening and landscaping along the east property line.
© completing all development processes before expanding.
e Will reflect the prevailing character of the overall district by expanding the rezone to the vacated Franklin
Street right-of-way.
e Will not cut across lots or developments by completing the vacation of Franklin Street adjacent to the property
and combining all of the parcels associated with the car dealership.
e Is supported in the Jefferson Community Plan and the Wall Avenue Corridor Plan.

There was brief discussion among the Council and Mr. Brierley regarding materials that would meet the definition of
‘intense screening and landscaping’ along the east property line. Mr. Brierley identified a table in the City’s land use code that
provides direction regarding fencing, buffering, and screening to ensure compliance with the Planning Commission’s
recommendations.

Council member Choberka asked if the applicant has indicated a desire to further expand their business into the 35"
Street block. Mr. Brierley answered no. Council member Choberka stated she understands any concerns residents may have about
the expansion of the car lot and how that type of use would impact residential use. Mr. Brierley stated that it can be difficult to
determine where to draw lines between uses, but organic growth has occurred and there are many areas in downtown Ogden
where commercial uses have crept outside the boundaries of cores defined in the City’s General Plan.

Relative to the proposed street vacation, Planning Manager Brierley stated the Planning Commission reviewed that
proposal at its February 2, 2022 meeting and forwarded a recommendation of approval to the Council with a vote of 6-0. The
recommendation was made with the findings that there is good cause for the street vacation; that neither the public interest nor any
person will be materially injured by the vacation; and that the vacation is consistent with the General Plan provision by rezoning
the associated properties, restricting vehicle access on Franklin Street, and combining all of the parcels associated with the car
dealership.

There was a brief discussion among the Council and Mr. Brierley regarding a property owner’s responsibility to mitigate
nuisances on their property associated with transient occupancy of an area; Mr. Brierley stated that vacant parcels are more
commonly occupied by transients because there are no other people present to deter them. The applicant has indicated they feel
that this proposal and subsequent development will address the issues the neighborhood has experienced associated with an
increased transient population in this area.

Proposed Resolution 2022-16 amending Goals and Guidelines for the
Fiscal Year 2023 Budget

A memo from Council staff came before the Council to consider a resolution amending goals and guidelines for the
Fiscal Year 2023 budget. The memo stated each year, the Council adopts goals and guidelines prior to accepting the Mayor’s
proposed budget in order to communicate the Council’s expectations for implementation of the City’s budget—the primary policy
document of the City. In approving the budget, the Council strives to ensure that the policies supported by the budget reflect both
the operating needs of the City and the desires of its residents as outlined in the Five-Year Strategic Plan. During discussions
regarding the Mayor’s proposed Fiscal Year 2022-2023, the Council expressed concern regarding the use of excess compensation
and benefit funds resulting from vacancies in authorized full-time positions. After input from the Administration, the Council and
Mayor agreed to a joint resolution amending the financial principles and budget goals and guidelines to limit the use of these
funds. The proposal is to adopt an amendment to the goals and guidelines related to excess compensation and benefit funds as
follows:

Proposed Resolution 2022-17 approving modifications to Council Rules and
Norms
A memo from Council staff came before the Council to consider a resolution updating Council rules and norms. The
memo stated the proposed revisions include changing the electronic stipend amount, establishing a threshold for special day by
proclamation, clarifying language regarding public comment, and changing the term “Closed Executive Session” to “Closed
Session.” Other minor changes to correct typographical errors or ensure consistency among sections have also been made.
Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the memo and stated the City Council has established Rules and
Norms to provide an overview of the Council’s purpose, vision, goals, priorities, expectations, and relationships, and to provide
guidance on how the Council conducts it day-to-day business and meeting. She briefly discussed the proposed revisions as
follows:

e Changing Electric Stipend Amount: The proposal is to increase the electronic equipment amount from $500 to
$1,000.

e Establishing Threshold for Special Day by Proclamation: Language would be added to require a supermajority
of support for a proclamation.

e Clarifying Language related to Public Comment: Language would be added to clarify that a person speaking in
public comment, whether for themselves or others, is limited to three minutes.

e Closed Executive Session Change: At the recommendation of the City Attorney, “Closed Executive Session”
will be changed to “Closed Session” because the Council’s responsibilities are legislative in nature.

e Request to be on the Agenda: An individual or group may request time or be invited to present information
during a meeting. The time is reserved for non-profits or individuals who are presenting information of general
interest of the community. Presentations may not include requests for funding or address issues on which the
Council will act.

e Other miscellaneous minor changes to correct typographical and grammatical errors, inconsistencies or
outdated practices.

“A resolution of the Ogden City Council approving modifications to Council Rules and Norms.”
