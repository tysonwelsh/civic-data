1. Stockyard Exchange building modifications – material to be sent

2. Residential Vacation Rentals
   Mr. Brierley stated there have been some complaints about vacation rentals in
   neighborhoods and their enforcement has been difficult for the City. This discussion is to
   determine whether some modifications to the Ordinance are warranted. While some
   complaints are because citizens feel these are not compatible in neighborhoods, most
   complaints deal with parking throughout the neighborhood and noise. He described the
   types of vacation rentals, indicating owner-occupied requires an owner to live on the
   premises for most of the year, but is unenforceable.

   Mr. Jared Johnson, Building Services Manager, stated there are 18 legally licensed vacation
   rentals in Ogden, but evidence shows over 250 being used. He stated while both VRBO and
   Air B&B require local regulations to be complied with, many are listed without the City’s
   approval. He indicated the City is in the process of contracting a company called Granicus to
   help identify and enforce local vacation rentals. He stated Code Enforcement is unable to
   prove whether the 8-month residency requirement is satisfied. He stated while it is
   acceptable for homeowners to park in the street, problems occur when there is a multitude of
   guests at a particular home and parking trails up and down the street and Code Enforcement
   does not have the tools to identify owners of vehicles. He suggested all parking be required
   to be on site, and that group events or parties not be allowed as a vacation rental. He stated
   if a violation of the Code occurs, the City has the ability to revoke the business license which
   would then prohibit their reapplication for a residential vacation rental for three years. He
   stated he has contacted other cities for ideas on better enforcement and regulation of
   vacation rentals and is suggesting the owner would receive a packet of information which
   identifies the rules for the use, and then requires them to contact adjacent property owners
   who would also have the same information with contact information for both the owner, the
   police and code enforcement. He felt with better standards, these could be a better fit with
   other neighborhood uses, indicating he has met with various cities to determine what works
   and does not work relating to vacation rentals.

   Mr. Brierley indicated Staff is suggesting changes be made to eliminate the eight-month
   residency standard as it is unenforceable, but requiring the owner to prove he is the owner at
   the application level. Other options might be to either eliminate the use in single-family zones
   or to require the owner to be present during rental periods. Commissioner Safsten felt the
   owner or his representative should be on-site during rental times, while Chair Sandau
   suggested seniors or snowbirds might not necessarily be on-site during the entire time
   property is rented.

   Mr. Johnson stated most complaints are related to parking and parties. He requested
   consideration be given to limiting the number of guests to what is approved for over-night
   stay. Commission suggested if this were adopted, the parking along the street also may be
   less of a problem. Mr. Johnson also suggested another option would be to limit parking to
   what is available on the property and prohibit guest parking along the street and indicated the
   parking violation citation would go to the property owner and not the overnight guests. Mr.
   Brierley clarified the existing code limits the number of guests based on the number of
   bedrooms. Discussion continued relating to the original adoption of the residential vacation
   rental regulations and whether it changed over time prior to its being adopted as well as the
   ability of the City to require licensing. Mr. Brierley suggested both simpler ordinance
   language and approval processes may improve the licensing of these. Commission also
   discussed the ability of the City to collect transient taxes for these uses in neighborhoods.

2. Group Dwelling Issues
   Mr Brierley stated Ogden defines group dwellings as being more than one building on a
   residential lot. He stated the term is rather unique to Ogden and may be better defined as a
   housing complex. Commission discussed the definition of apartments and other multi-family
   uses, indicating more than three units requires commercial financing. He indicated other
   states most often have the same regulations whether the multi-family use is one building or
   several buildings on a lot and may identify them as a cottage cluster. He suggested the use
   and definitions of group dwellings might be eliminated from the Ordinance, allowing all
   apartment types to be treated the same. He stated group dwellings have separate
   regulations than other multi-family uses which often are confusing. The existing ordinance is
   inconsistent with locations where these are allowed, and there are locations where they are
   supported by the General Plan, but not allowed by Code. He stated the density is often
   reduced by using the group dwelling standards in relation to other multi-family standards.
   Discussion continued relating to multi-family development, indicating density can often be
   increased due to architectural features and layout of buildings.

   Mr. Brierley identified issues relating to the group dwelling standards for more immediate
   consideration, stating these are required to have a pitched roof while single apartment
   buildings are required to have a flat roof. Group dwellings are required to have 20% glazing,
   and while anticipated it would mean the front of the building, it is not clear where the glazing
   can be located. The existing code also requires both trees and shrubs based on the total
   number of units, and when implemented, it would become a forest when there is a large
   number of units. He suggested this standard be revised to be based on square footage of
   landscaped area and that roof features be identified based on the context of the surrounding
   neighborhood. It was noted some neighborhoods are unique in that there is an eclectic
   variety of architectural styles while other neighborhoods have no particular character.

3. Planning Commission agenda modifications
   Mr. Brierley suggested some modifications be considered to be added to the agenda, including
   placing minutes on the consent agenda, to have items to be tabled listed as under approval
   items, adding new business or public comment at the end of the agenda. Commission felt
   only in extreme situations should they allow public comment at the end of the agenda,
   indicating most items would better be handled by Staff. He also suggested the Commission
   consider agenda items on the third Wednesday in order to relieve some of the items for the
   regular agenda meeting. After some discussion, the Commission determined they would
   prefer one longer meeting and keep the time available for work sessions only, except for
   special circumstances might consider some consent items to be considered on the third
   Wednesday meeting date.

4. Joint City Council work session topics
   A suggestion was made the Commission and Council further discuss the elimination of pole
   signs on Harrison which had been approved by the Planning Commission in 2017 and no
   action has taken place by the Council.

There being no further business before the Commission, the meeting was adjourned at 7:40pm.
