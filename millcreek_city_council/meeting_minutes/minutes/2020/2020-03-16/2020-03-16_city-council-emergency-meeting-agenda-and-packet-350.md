WORK MEETING — 2:00 p.m.
TIME COMMENCED: 2:02 p.m.

Mayor Silvestrini called the meeting to order and led the pledge of allegiance. He said the
emergency meeting was called with the consent of all members of the Council. He wanted to call
an emergency meeting that day since the Council was already gathering for a special meeting
and so they could discuss matters that would make it easier for staff to function during the state
of emergency. The Mayor declared Millcreek in a state of emergency last week when word was
received that there was a possibility of exposure of Millcreek schools to a person who had tested
positive for COVID-19. A state of emergency principally qualifies Millcreek to qualify for
federal and state assistance to offset expenses the City might incur in dealing with an emergency
and attracts members of the community to respond in an appropriate manner to limit the spread
of disease. He encouraged residents to follow guidelines of washing hands and practicing social
distancing. If residents were symptomatic, he encouraged them to call the health care provider
before going to a clinic to allow time for the clinic to prepare to receive them without
endangering other people. Symptoms of COVID-19 are high fever, dry cough, and difficulty
breathing. He said there was no need to hoard supplies from the grocery stores, especially bottled
water since the culinary water system was safe. He said useful resource links could be found on
the City’s website and the United Way had made available their 211 resource to be connected to
virus assistance. The Salt Lake County Health Department declared a health state of emergency

1. Discussion and Consideration of Ordinance 20-11, Ratifying and Confirming the
Mayor’s Emergency Proclamation No. 1 of 2020 Declaring a Local State of Emergency;
Millcreek State of Local Emergency Directive No. 1 of 2020 Concerning Conduct of
Public Meetings During the Emergency; and Millcreek State of Local Emergency
Directive No. 2 of 2020 Concerning Waiving the Requirement for the Presence of a
Quorum at the Anchor Location of a Public Meeting During the Emergency
Mayor Silvestrini said the ordinance would permit the City Council and Planning Commission
to meet by electronic means. The City had developed a way for video conferencing, to stream
live meetings on the City’s website, and for the public to submit written comments via a form
on the City’s website during the meetings. The City was acting pursuant to the emergency
declaration and the two issued directives which allowed for separating people when they come
to a public meeting and allowed for electronic meetings with no public body quorum present
at the anchor location. He said it was important that nobody panic and to deal with the
situation rationally and calmly. Council Member Jackson appreciated the directives. Mayor
Silvestrini said City Hall was open for business though many services could be done online,
and the public could participate in meetings online via the City’s website. He added that
public safety would still be on duty and would respond to calls. Council Member Uipi asked
about allowing staff to flex their schedules. John Geilmann said the City was already allowing
it. Mayor Silvestrini said the City was also in close contact with Unified Command with the
County and other cities in the State.

Council Member Marchant moved to adopt Ordinance 20-11. Council Member Uipi
seconded. The City Recorder called for the vote. All Council Members voted yes. The
motion passed unanimously.

ADJOURNED: Council Member Uipi moved to adjourn the meeting at 2:20 p.m. Council
Member Jackson seconded. Mayor Silvestrini called for the vote. All Council Members
voted yes. The motion passed unanimously.
