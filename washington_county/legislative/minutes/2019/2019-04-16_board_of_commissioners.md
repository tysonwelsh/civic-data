REGULAR AGENDA ITEMS FOR CONSIDERATION:

PRESENTATION: BMX BIKE TRACK: SANTA CLARA CITY

Santa Clara City Councilman Jarett Waite explained that Santa Clara City is in partnership
with the Santa Clara BMX and is seeking funds to construct a BMX track located in Canyon
View Park. The new track is projected to bring in approximately $560,000 in economic
impact to Washington County. The track has a small nominal fee in order to cover
insurance costs for the riders, however, there will be several free events held throughout

the year.

Santa Clara will provide $30,000 in cash and $78,000 in-kind match for the BMX park.
The Santa Clara BMX Track is seeking $158,000 from Washington County to supplement
the remaining costs of the course.

The Commissioners will consider this request at a future Commission meeting.

PRESENTATION: YOUTH COALITION

The Washington County Youth Coalition explained that the Coalition is made up of several
students from various high schools throughout Washington County and work to educate
other youth and the community about drug use and suicide prevention, as well as the
adverse effects of using drugs.

CONSIDERATION _ OF RESOLUTION NO. _R-2019-2452, A RESOLUTION
RECOGNIZING THE WASHINGTON COUNTY YOUTH COALITION FOR
EFFORTS TO REDUCE AND PREVENT SUBSTANCE ABUSE

Commissioner Almquist stated that the Youth Coalition has impacted the community in
many meaningful ways over the past year including: successfully lobbying local Utah
Legislative leaders to support HB 324, which raised the age for obtaining and possessing
tobacco products to 21; obtaining approval of a policy in Hurricane City to prohibit
electronic cigarettes in city parks; manning booths at various events to educate community
members on substance abuse; and providing training in school classrooms and assemblies.

MOTION: Motion by Commissioner Almquist to Adopt Resolution No. R-2019-
2452, A Resolution Recognizing the Washington County Youth
Coalition for Efforts to Reduce and Prevent Substance Abuse. Motion
seconded by Commissioner Iverson and carried by unanimous vote,
with Commissioners Cox, Iverson, and Almquist voting aye.

CONSIDERATION _ OF RESOLUTION NO. R-2019-2453, A RESOLUTION
APPROVING A MEMORANDUM OF AGREEMENT BETWEEN WASHINGTON

COUNTY __AND__THE UTAH _DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH FOR
FINGERPRINTING SERVICES

Washington County Justice Court Director Salote McKenzie mentioned that the Livescan
fingerprinting machine was moved from the Sheriff's Office to the Justice Court in

December 2018.

Ms. McKenzie explained that Washington County and the Utah Health Department
previously entered into the agreement for the County to use the machine in exchange for
health care workers receiving free fingerprinting services. The new agreement will be
renewed through 2024 and health care workers will now pay the same fee as the general
public.

MOTION: Motion by Commissioner Almquist to Adopt Resolution No. R-2019-
2453, A Resolution Approving a Memorandum of Agreement Between
Washington County and the Utah Department of Health for
Fingerprinting Services. Motion seconded by Commissioner Iverson
and carried by unanimous vote, with Commissioners Cox, Iverson, and
Almquist voting aye.

CONSIDERATION OF RESOLUTION NO. _R-2019-2456, A RESOLUTION
AMENDING WASHINGTON COUNTY'S INVESTMENT POLICY

Washington County Treasurer David Whitehead explained that the Washington County
Investment Policy allows the County to invest in other funds, specifically, CD’s and Money
Market accounts. Funds come from the Utah Public Treasurer’s Investment Fund (PTIF)
and are typically used for capital improvement projects and in the event of an economic
downturn. The amendment makes slight modifications in order to conform to the 2019
legislative changes.

CONSIDERATION OF A MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING BETWEEN
WASHINGTON COUNTY UTAH AND DIAMOND VALLEY ACRES WATER

COMPANY

Ms. Felshaw explained that Washington County receives a small portion of funds from the
RAP (Recreation, Arts, and Parks) tax. Diamond Valley currently has a public park which
is owned by the Diamond Valley Acres Water Company. This agreement will allow
Diamond Valley to apply for RAP funds with the understanding that the park will remain
open and available for public use.

and carried by unanimous vote, with Commissioners Cox, Iverson, and
