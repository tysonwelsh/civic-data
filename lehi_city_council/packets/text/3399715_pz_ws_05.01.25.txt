30   2) Discussion of updates to Conditional Uses
31
32   Both the residential and nonresidential tables of uses have been gone through by the planning
33   department with help from legal. A few conditional uses remain, but the majority have been
34   removed, and supplementary code has been added to Chapter 12 to assist with clarification.
35   Changes have also been made to Chapter 39 and other various places in the code. The project is
36   larger than initially planned.
37
38   There are currently twelve conditional uses in the residential table. Planning has cut it down to
39   five: electrical substations and power transmission lines; commercial campgrounds; commercial
40   recreation facilities; kennels; and commercial greenhouses.
41
42   Commissioner Everett asked why kennels were included on the list. Staff explained that they are
43   currently governed by municipal code, which would have to be changed before anything can be
44   changed in the development code.
45
46   Legal believes that the city will need to allow uses of juvenile group facilities and rehab and
47   treatment facilities to comply with the Utah Fair Housing Act, but they are still looking into it.
48   Most of the remaining conditional uses are situations that don’t show up very often. Oil and gas

50   have been split into large and small capacity, and child daycare has been added as a permitted use.
51   Commissioner Everett asked if there were size limits for daycares. Currently they are only allowed

55   Commissioner Eyre asked why campgrounds and nursing homes are allowed in the TH-5 zone.

60   Kim Struthers suggested adding another category for HOA amenities like pools and clubhouses as
61   the ‘recreational activities’ label doesn’t currently cover them.

63   The nonresidential table of uses has been cut down to eight: communication facilities tower; non-
64   municipal electrical substations and power transmission lines; public and private utility buildings
65   and other above ground structures; wind energy facilities; large auto service and repair;
66   commercial entertainment facilities; commercial recreation facilities; and contractor’s storage
67   yard.
68
69   Lots of supplementary code will have to be added to make up for the lack of conditional uses.
70
71   The plan for upcoming months is to have legal continue researching state standards for
72   communications facilities and towers, to update chapters 12 and 23, to add supplementary
73   requirements of approval, and to create a separate chapter (12-C) for supplementary requirements
74   so they are easier to locate.
75
76   Commissioner Eyre clarified that people can still come in and ask for development agreements or
77   to change the code.
78
79   Commissioner Lockhart asked if existing conditional uses that get changed to permitted will
80   remain conditional use, or if they will change to permitted use, and if the city would notify
81   individuals of the change. Curtis explained that there wouldn’t be much impact as the conditional
82   uses in effect already had approval. Struthers and Curtis said they would check with legal, but that
83   to the best of their understanding if a conditional use swapped to permitted use it would be
84   classified as legal non-conforming.

86   3) Bike-Ped Update Presentation
87
88   Mike West presented. The text is currently several hundred pages. He would like to get it down
89   to 30 pages (without pictures) for readability’s sake.
90
91   Gap analysis is being conducted and will extend to other cities to see where connections are. The
92   department is mainly concerned with directness and destination access. The public is majorly
93   concerned with the safety of biking in Lehi (70% of citizen responses mention it). The vision
94   statement of the plan is: Lehi is safe and comfortable for families and individuals of all ages and
95   abilities to walk, bike, and roll to their desired destinations.
96
     Lehi City Planning Commission                    2                                        May 1, 2025
 97   The goals for the next 5-10 years include: updating the development code; adding 20% of
 98   missing sidewalks along developed roads; enhancing safety and comfort by adding lighting to
 99   the rail trail and analyzing lighting needs for other trails; focusing on improving access to key
100   destinations; adding an additional 11 miles of paved trails and 12 miles of on-street bike
101   infrastructure; creating design requirements for pedestrian-friendly community gathering places;
102   completing murals an public art at 3 trail under-crossing; and completing two or more segments
103   of the recreational trail network.
104
105   Commissioner Everett asked if bike repair stations are a part of the bike/ped plan. West said
106   there are a couple in the city, and that Law Tigers provide a few every year. It is not currently a
107   goal of the plan, but they are mentioned in the appendix.
108
109   Commissioner Roberts asked why these are the specific goals chosen. West explained that this
110   was the core of what was researched and what was determined would work best for the city. He
111   clarified that it is not in order of priority.
112
113   Included in the bike/ped plan are station area plans, visionary paths, and non-type specific on-
114   street facilities. Currently Lehi has 17.4 miles of bike lanes, 1.8 miles of separated bikeways,
115   and 75.8 miles of paved trails. Most of the funding comes from development, MAG, UDOT,
116   Utah County and the city itself. Lehi has a higher amount of trail per 10,000 citizens than most of
117   the surrounding cities. Bike path usage generally matches traffic flow. One upside of putting in
118   separated bikeways is that they choke crossing points and make them safer for children and other
119   pedestrians.
120
121   The delineators being used to separate bikeways are being damaged somewhat, but the city’s
122   ultimate plan is to eventually install concrete separators which will provide more protection. City
123   staff has done research on many different designs.
124
125   Next steps include meeting with the public works and administrative departments, meeting with
126   neighboring cities, finalizing the bike map and text, releasing those to the public, and starting the
127   adoption process.
