Joint Resolution 2024-2 approving the naming of the “Spencer Fox Eccles
Pioneer Stadium” and authorizing and approving a sponsorship naming
agreement with the George S. and Delores Dore Eccles Foundation
         A memo from the Public Services Department regarding a proposed sponsorship naming agreement for the Ogden
Pioneer Stadium came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the proposal is to adopt a joint resolution approving
a sponsorship naming agreement with the George S. and Dolores Dore Eccles Foundation. Under the terms of the agreement, the
Ogden Pioneer Days Foundation will recognize the recent contribution of $800,000 to construct improvements to the rodeo
grounds as well as the millions of dollars donated over the course of more than forty years—since 1982—to fund the Ogden
Pioneer Days rodeo and improvements to the stadium. In return, the City will grant naming rights for the stadium to the
Foundation for a term of 75 years or until such time as the stadium is no longer used as a public stadium space. The stadium
would be known as the Spencer Fox Eccles Pioneer Stadium.
         Parks and Cemetery Manager Stewart summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize
the terms of the naming agreement as well as to present a rendering of the sign that will be erected at the stadium, and which is
patterned after the iconic arch sign on Washington Boulevard and 17 th Street near the stadium. He also discussed the project
currently underway to renovate and update the stadium and thanked the Ogden Pioneer Days Committee, the Ogden Pioneer Days
Foundation, and the George S. and Dolores Eccles Foundation for their ongoing support of the Pioneer Days celebration and
rodeo.
           Ogden Pioneer Days Foundation Chairman-elect, Steve Handy, thanked Mr. Stewart, the George S. and Dolores Eccles
Foundation, and Ogden Pioneer Days Foundation Chairman Alan Hall for their efforts to preserve and improve the stadium over
the last several years; the entire Pioneer Days Foundation has a great love for the Ogden community and its fantastic Pioneer Days
Celebration, which was first established in 1934.
           Rodeo Director Rick Davis stated that the Ogden Pioneer Days Rodeo has elevated itself to one of the premier rodeos in
the nation; there are 786 professional rodeos nationwide and last year the Ogden Pioneer Days Rodeo was ranked number 20. This
year, it will improve to the number 15 ranking, which means it is a top tier rodeo and it will be broadcast worldwide. It draws
contestants from all over the world. He has been involved with the Foundation for 34 years and over that period of time the Eccles
Foundation has been such a great supporter of and donor for the Stadium; in fact, Spencer Eccles’ mother, Ms. Fox, was
represented as Miss Ogden and Miss Utah in the Wild West Show in Ogden. She rode her horse onto the steps of the Utah capitol
building to invite the Governor to come to Ogden in 1922 to see the show.
           Council members expressed their gratitude to the Eccles Foundation, the Ogden Pioneer Days Foundation, and Mr.
Stewart and the Ogden City Parks staff for their ongoing support of the annual Pioneer Days celebration. Mayor Nadolski echoed
the comments of the Council and thanked everyone for their collaboration and efforts that led to this opportunity.

Proposed Resolution 2024-5 delegating to certain officers of the issuer the
authority to approve the final terms and provisions in connection with the
issuance and sale of not more than $82,100,000 aggregate principal
amount of subordinated sewer, water, and storm sewer revenue bonds; and
related matters
           A memo from the Management Services Department regarding the funding mechanism for the 36-inch Canyon Waterline
project came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the proposal is for the Council to consider adoption of a
resolution for the final bond documents for the financing of the replacement of the Canyon Waterline. The project will involve
several funding sources and will include proposed water rate increases to pay for the debt service. The project, Capital
Improvement Plan (CIP) Project Number WU003, is estimated to cost approximately $100 million and includes not only the water
line in the Ogden Canyon but the 36-inch concrete lined steel pipe running under Pineview Reservoir from the City’s wellfields as
well. The proposed funding sources include $34.3 million from a State Revolving Loan Fund, $42.6 million from a federal Water
Infrastructure Finance and Innovation Act (WIFIA) loan, $10 million from State appropriations, and $12 million from City
American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funding. The bond parameters resolution is required as the City is incurring debt and must
comply with state bonding requirements.
           Public Services Director Anderson summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the
need for the project; the waterline was originally constructed in 1935 and plays a critical role in supplying water to Ogden City.
The presentation also included historical photos of the waterline to illustrate the manner in which the infrastructure is aging and
the risk of failure. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
           Brief discussion among the Council and Mr. Anderson centered on the City’s 2008 Water Master Plan, which projects
growth and water needs for the City through the year 2030. This Master Plan has informed the City’s capital planning efforts and
identifies projects that are needed due to growth as well as to reduce the loss of water through aging waterlines. Mr. Anderson
stated that the original waterline lasted 90 years, which is fantastic considering construction methods in the early 1900s; the new
36-inch waterline is expected to last even longer than 90 years based upon improvements of construction materials and methods.
           There was also discussion about the route of the new waterline and the City’s evaluation of all optional routes in an effort
to lessen the impact the project could have on Pineview Reservoir.
           Fred Philpot of Lewis, Robertson, & Burningham (LRB), the City’s Financial Advisor, then used the aid of a PowerPoint
presentation to provide an analysis of the City’s water utility fund, which will be one of the funding sources for repayment of the
bond that will be issued for the project. The City’s rate structure model includes the provision of funding for major capital
infrastructure; the model includes programmed general rate increases of four percent over the next five years, as well as a blended
inflation adjustment increase (BIA). The BIA is calculated yearly based upon a formula that weights the western region consumer
price index (CPI) and the American City and County Commodity Channel Index (CCI). The current recommendation is to
maintain these inflationary increases as well as consider a rate increase to maintain fund balances. For a copy of the presentation
in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
           Nate Robertson, also of LRB, then summarized the bond parameters as follows:
              •     Discount – 3%
              •     Term – 40 Years
              •     Rates – Not to exceed 6%
                          o State Revolving Loan Fund – 1%
                          o WIFIA Loan – 5%
                • Maximum Par Amount - $82,100,000
                • Total Estimated Debt Service
                          o State Loan - $39,965,000
                          o WIFIA Loan - $75,060,000
           Mr. Robertson noted that both the bond and the WIFIA loan will be closed at the same time, but the WIFIA loan will
have zero percent interest rate for five years.
            Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2024-5, entitled:
                    “A resolution of the City Council of Ogden, Utah (the “Issuer”), delegating to certain officers of the issuer the
                    authority to approve the final terms and provisions in connection with the issuance and sale of not more than
                    $82,100,000 aggregate principal amount of subordinated Sewer, Water and Storm Drain Revenue Bonds, Series
                    2024 of the Issuer; authorizing and approving the execution of a subordinate General Indenture of Trust, a First
                    Supplemental Subordinate Indenture of Trust, a Second Supplemental Subordinate Indenture of Trust, a WIFIA
                    Loan Agreement, and other documents that may be required in connection therewith; authorizing the taking of
                    all other actions necessary to the consummation of the transactions contemplated by this resolution; and related
                    matters.”
           Chair Richey then opened the public hearing.
           Laura Lewis stated she is seeking clarification on exactly how this project and its associated debt will impact her utility
bill for the next 30 years; she understands the annual four percent increase will remain constant, but the blended inflation
adjustment will fluctuate from year to year. She noted the City Council annually transfers approximately $3 million out of the
Water Utility Enterprise Fund to the City’s General Fund and she asked if some of that money could be used to help finance this
project in the future.
           Joseph Bauman stated this project actually started in 2009; in 2012 when the water treatment plant was built in the
Canyon, a portion of the 42-inch line was replaced and Ogden City allocated money to band aid and repair the 36- and 42-inch
lines in the Canyon. He stated his question was why this project did not start back in 2009 when the problems with the waterlines
were known and construction and material costs were much lower than they are now. This would have reduced the burden on the
taxpayers and utility customers of the City. It also does not seem prudent to him to pursue a bond without an understanding of the
actual interest rate for the bond as well as the utility rate increases that will be needed to fund it. He is not opposed to the project,
but he feels the City needs to know all the details related to the debt that is being issued.
           Jeff Humphrey stated he is the General Manager of Pineview Water Systems, which oversees the Pineview Dam and
operations for Ogden River Water Users. He stated he has been ‘left in the dark’ about this project and he did not know it was this
far along in the planning stages. He understands the waterline is in bad shape and needs to be replaced, but there is a lot of other
aging infrastructure throughout the County and if Pineview Water Systems is going to be forced to raise its water rates, he needs
to have a full understanding of the reasoning behind the increases as well as be involved in certain aspects of the project related to
drawing down the level of Pineview Reservoir. He would like Pineview Water Systems and the Ogden River Water Users entities
to be more involved in the project as it moves forward.
           Linda Sanchez stated she believed the City already had funding set aside for this project. One of the things that does
concern her is that the City has approved projects like the Ya Ya Foods construction in Business Depot Ogden, which will require
more water, without having secured the water sources before now. She asked if commercial entities pay the same water rates as
residential users. She noted she knows that the project must be done, but she is concerned about the financial planning for
significant infrastructure projects. Rate increases will have an impact on many residents, typically the elderly population of the
community, and it is being proposed in the same year as a property tax increase.
           There being no further persons appearing to be heard, COUNCIL MEMBER GRAF MOVED THE PUBLIC
HEARING BE CLOSED. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER HYER, ALL VOTING AYE.
           Chair Richey stated that Ms. Lewis’s understanding of the rate increases is correct; the general increase is programmed at
four percent for the next five years, but the BIA will adjust annually and is expected to be in the range of three percent. The
Council expressed a desire to receive a model from LRB that illustrates what a resident’s utility bill could be in the next five to 10
years based upon the rate increases and the bond debt.
           Chair Richey then invited Mr. Anderson to address Mr. Bauman’s comments about the timing of the project. Mr.
Anderson stated that in 2008 a large waterline at the mouth of the Canyon broke and the northern end of the City did not have
water for approximately three days; since that time, the City has built a four-million gallon and one-million-gallon water tank on
the north end of the City and replaced an existing water tank at 36 th Street with a five-million-gallon tank. A 24-inch waterline was
also replaced in the Canyon and all of these projects, as well as upgrades to the reservoirs, had to be completed before the 36-inch
waterline could be replaced. The project planning is systematic, and it is necessary to consider how to keep all lines in service
during a project like this. Chair Richey then asked Mr. Anderson if the Pineview Reservoir drawdowns are planned for the winter
months at the end of the irrigation system. Mr. Anderson answered yes; he added that he believes Mr. Humphreys is new to his
position, but he has sent all the environmental documents regarding the project to all water entities that will be impacted. He
added he is happy to sit down with Mr. Humphreys to answer his questions and discuss timing of the project. He then addressed
Ms. Sanchez’s comments about the Ya Ya Foods project; they are actually paying consumptively for the water they use, and this
is a great thing for the taxpayers. The City has been working tirelessly to ensure that there is source water available to sell and
being able to sell it to Ya Ya Foods has generated revenue to help pay for the improvements and keep water rates lower. He
concluded that commercial entities also pay a higher amount for water than residential users and they are subject to a tiered rate
system based upon their usage.
          Vice Chair White then addressed Mr. Bauman’s comments about the unknown interest rates for the bond. She noted that
the resolution that has been proposed tonight will set a maximum interest rate and other parameters for the bond. Chair Richey
also referenced Ms. Lewis’s question about utilizing the annual enterprise fund transfer for this project and he asked Mr. Philpot
to respond. Mr. Philpot stated that option was considered in the models that were prepared regarding the bond; if the money were
to stay in the enterprise fund in order to assist in funding this project, the property tax implications for residents would actually be
higher because the annual transfer is used to supply the General Fund and if that money is not available, the City must raise other
funding sources. Council member Graf noted that it is important to understand that the rate models that have been adopted by the
City include sufficient cash-on-hand to continue to operate the utilities in the event of an emergency.

Proposed Ordinance 2024-18 to rezone property located at 605 North
Jackson Avenue and 685 Rear North Jackson Avenue from R-1-6 to R-1-5
for development of single-family residential lots
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed rezone of 605 North
Jackson Avenue came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Ogden City Community Development has
submitted a petition to rezone 6.9 acres from R-1-6 to R-1-5. The City purchased 6.5 acres from the school district as surplus
property and seeks to establish zoning that would allow slightly smaller single-family lots than the current zoning allows. A
portion of the 6.9 acres (approximately 0.40 acres) is in the process of being purchased from the school district. The Planning
Commission held a public hearing for the proposal on May 1, 2024, and recommended approval of the petition.
          Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to orient the Council to
the location of the property and to identify current land use of surrounding parcels; the zoning designation that has been proposed
will meet the General Plan objectives of expanding homeownership opportunities and facilitate residential in-fill redevelopment
and rehabilitation – in some cases at higher densities. He concluded the Planning Commission held a public hearing for the
proposal on May 1, 2024, and recommended approval of the petition. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the
information packet for the meeting.
          Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2024-18, entitled:
                    “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Zoning Map of Ogden City, as adopted by Section 15-3-3 of
                    the Ogden Municipal Code, to reclassify as Single-Family Residential Zone (R-1-5) property heretofore
                    classified as Single-Family Residential Zone (R-1-6), generally located at 605 and 685 rear North Jackson
                    Avenue, and providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final
                    passage.”
          Chair Richey then called for public input regarding the proposal.
          Linda Sanchez stated she is happy that the City is pursuing a zoning designation that will provide for single-family
dwellings on the property.
          No additional persons came forward to be heard
          ON A MOTION BY COUNCIL MEMBER HYER AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER MYERS,
ORDINANCE 2024-18 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – COUNCIL
MEMBERS BLAIR, CHOBERKA, GRAF, HYER, MYERS, VICE CHAIR WHITE, AND CHAIR RICHEY. VOTING
NO – NONE.
          Vice Chair White stated that the original layout for the development of the property included a cul-de-sac, and she
encouraged Planning staff and the developer that will ultimately be building the project to consider alternative layouts. Council
member Hyer agreed and stated there is a desire from residents in the area for more connectivity and a through-street will help to
accomplish that.

Proposed Ordinance 2024-19 to adjust the CP-1, 12th Street Corridor
Overlay, and R-3 zone boundary at 981 12th Street and 1245 Monroe
Boulevard to allow a property line adjustment between the two properties
         A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed rezone of 981 12th Street
came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Jeremy Krause has submitted a petition to amend the zoning
boundary between two parcels so that the zoning boundary follows the property line. The properties involved are the Dylan’s
restaurant property on the corner of Monroe Boulevard and 12th Street and the property directly south of Dylan’s which has R-3
zoning on the entire property.
         Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
history of the property as well as the implications of the zone change; for a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the
information packet for the meeting. Discussion among the Council and Mr. Brierley centered on parking and setback requirements
for the Dylan’s Restaurant and preliminary site plan documents that have been submitted for the proposed improvements. He
noted the Planning Commission held a public hearing on this proposal on March 6, 2024, and recommended approval of the
proposal subject to the following conditions:
             1. The petitioner completing the small subdivision plat process to have the property line between 981 12th Street
                  and 1245 Monroe Boulevard follow the new zoning boundary lines.
             2. The existing row of parking stalls be shifted out of the required 10’ wide landscaped setback along west
                  property line and the new property line/zoning line, and also the required 20' wide landscaped setback along
                  Monroe Boulevard, which will need to be completed under a site plan review process.
             3. The dumpster for Dylan’s Restaurant be placed in screened enclosure out of the setbacks along the streets and
                  be approved through a site plan review process.
         For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
         Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2024-19, entitled:
                  “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Zoning Map of Ogden City, as adopted by Section 15-3-3 of
                  the Ogden Municipal Code, to reclassify as Neighborhood Commercial Zone and 12 th Street Corridor Overlay
                  Zone (CP-1 / 12th CO) property heretofore classified as Multiple Family Residential Zone and 12 th Street
                  Corridor Overlay Zone (R-3 / 12th CO), by shifting of the property line between 981 12 th Street and 1245
