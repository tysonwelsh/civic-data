CONVENE ELECTRONIC MEETING – Public Welcome and Chair Opening Statement.
Chair Marianne Ricks called the Meeting to order at approximately 6:45 p.m. and read the
Commission statement.

PUBLIC HEARINGS
1.      Residential Subdivision – “Weyburn Retreat” Concept Plan (PUD) – 1306 East
        Spring Lane (RM).
Chair Ricks reported that since the last meeting statements have been received from the
applicant, Jeff Kruckenberg, and members of the public. The requested traffic study was also
conducted. There was some question as to the validity of the traffic study since it was conducted
during the pandemic where traffic levels were much lower than normal. Commissioner
Bradshaw commented that Traffic Engineer, Dr. Joseph Perrin, recognized the issue and took
data from different months to provide averages.

A question was raised as to why Dr. Perrin used the 1300 East and 3900 South intersection.
Community Development Director, Paul Allred stated that that was his reference point and he
has the associated data. The total amount of traffic generated from the site was not significant so
traffic counts were provided for both Spring Lane and Van Winkle Parkway. It was determined
that the project will produce approximately 151 cars per day at the maximum or 9.44 cars per
unit. Because 16,000 cars per day travel on 1300 East, the traffic generated by the site is small.
In the staff report, Mr. Allred listed the most salient points from the traffic study, including
percentages. He explained that the traffic study was less about the amount of traffic and more
about safety and visibility.

Chair Ricks observed that with traffic studies a letter grade is generally assigned to the
intersection as a whole although one portion may not carry the same grade. Mr. Allred pointed
out that this project will produce very little traffic although there will always be some
inconvenience associated with turn movements during peak times. He suggested the
Commission refer to the General Plan.

Chair Ricks commented that one of Dr. Perrin’s recommendations was to lengthen the storage of
the turn lane by 25 feet. She asked if doing so will improve traffic flow. Commissioner Carter
did not expect it to change the grade of the intersection. The contribution from this project to
additional wait times will be 1.3% in the morning and 1% in the afternoon. He commented that
the consideration of traffic generation needs to take place when the property is zoned so that
maximum uses are modeled. Potential remedies were discussed. Commissioner Bradshaw
commented that they cannot prevent a project from moving forward if it is pursuing the proper
zoning regardless of the traffic.

Mr. Allred reported that he and City Traffic Engineer, Tosh Kano visited the site together and
presented several concerns to the Planning Commission who continued the matter to allow for a
traffic study, which determined that the traffic impacts of the project are minimal. It was
suggested by Dr. Perrin that the private lane be striped and Mr. Kano deferred to Dr. Perrin.
The traffic study was explicit and indicated that a middle turn lane is not needed on Spring Lane.
Dr. Perrin did not recommend that Tobermory be widened.

A question was raised as to why the existing entrance into the church was being eliminated.
Mr. Allred explained that when he, Mr. Teerlink, and Dr. Perrin met and outlined issues raised
by the residents and staff, Dr. Perrin determined that the proximity of the church entrance to
Tobermory will create conflicting turn movements. In addition, there is a grade problem to the
west that would push the access closer to the corner, which was not recommended.

Commissioner Lloyd reported that the entrance has existed for a very long time and she did not
believe that the development of twin homes will make it infinitely more dangerous than it has
been historically. Commissioner Mackin disagreed since the entrance into Tobermory and all of
the individual homes is a very narrow road with single car access.

Mr. Allred explained that the uses are significantly different and Tobermory was expected to
have significantly more parking whereas the church had sporadic busy periods. Road width
issues were discussed. Mr. Allred verified that the current road complies with the Code and is 20
feet wide including the drivable curb. Possible options were discussed including widening the
road to the width of the proposed parallel parking.

Mr. Allred explained that under the PUD ordinance, the use is conditional, which means that
conditions can be imposed to mitigate potential negative impacts. Because the project is narrow
and does not allow for stacking in the driveways, the applicant will be required to provide guest
parking.

Comments received from citizens were acknowledged. One comment was that the lane is 16 feet
wide of asphalt. The two feet of concrete with a raised bumper on each side is what the Fire
Department would drive over. There was some question as to whether 16 feet is a reasonable
width for two cars or trucks to use regularly and allow for pedestrians on either side. It was
noted that there is no sidewalk. City Planner, Jonathan Teerlink acknowledged that there will be
problems in that section under that scenario.

Chris Layton reported that Dr. Perrin’s study acknowledges potential conflicts and claims that it
is more dangerous with two entrances than with one improved entrance. Mr. Allred commented
that the residents have indicated that the church entrance could be used to reduce the impact of
traffic onto Tobermory. Dr. Perrin was asked specifically to study the width and indicate if it
was problematic. It was noted that the City paid for the traffic study. Mr. Allred reminded the
Commission that they are now at the conceptual stage, which provides the developer with
direction to move forward. He confirmed that Dr. Perrin answered the outstanding questions.
Mr. Allred explained that the concept plan does not grant final approval. The matter will have to
come back to the Planning Commission for a Conditional Use Permit and preliminary plat
approval.

Commissioner Layton was concerned about traffic and safety. He referenced vehicular safety
pertaining to the drive width as well as pedestrian safety and the lack of a sidewalk. He
suggested that the Commission list recommendations for the developers to consider as they work
toward preliminary approval.

Mr. Allred explained that the concept plan requires the developers to show the roads that are
public and private and the widths. A potential motion could specify that private roads be at least
20 feet wide with an adequate turning radius. In response to a comment, Mr. Allred clarified that
their parking will never be allowed on Tobermory Lane or the private lane within the proposed
development. Guest parking issues were discussed. It was noted that the applicant will be
required to provide eight guest parking spaces.

The residents would like the Spring Lane entrance to be used for the proposed development to
keep Tobermory as it is. If the traffic study shows that that is not the safest approach,
Tobermory will need to be widened to make it safer. The possibility of the developer placing a
sidewalk where the parallel parking currently exists was mentioned.

Road width issues were discussed. Mr. Allred commented that part of the problem is that
Tobermory was not built out front on Spring Lane. At the time, the expectation was not for the
church to be sold and vacated. There was some question as to whether the Tobermory HOA is
willing to help pay to widen the road. Commissioner Carter commented that there is a
contractual arrangement between the property owner and Tobermory residents in the form of an
easement. He agreed, however, that the road needs to be wider to proceed with the proposed
development.

Chair Ricks opened the public hearing.

Jeff Kruckenberg from Alan Kruckenberg Construction was willing to discuss all options with
staff to create a quality development. The overriding staff concerns were traffic and the outcome
of the traffic study. The results of the traffic study showed that the existing access to the church
should not be used as it creates safety issues and that Tobermory Ridge Lane should be used for
all access into the proposed subdivision. Three guest parking stalls were shown along the
western edge of Tobermory Way, which meets the requirements of the RM zone with a PUD
overlay. Mr. Kruckenberg expressed a willingness to work with staff and the Planning
Commission to resolve the problem. With regard to widening the road, Mr. Kruckenberg stated
that the road to the west of the entry to the intersection of Spring Lane and Tobermory Way

some width gained to the east to help widen the road. One pedestal is a ground sleeve that is

Commissioner Bradshaw asked Mr. Kruckenberg about installing a sidewalk along the east side

Peggy Schultz Beck gave her address as 1311 Tobermory Way and stated that she is a 4 ½ year
resident and serves on the HOA board. She commented that there has never been a problem with
the right/left turn using the church entrance onto Spring Lane. The problem is for those coming
out of the Weyburn Retreat. It is very difficult for those traveling north, south, east, west, and
backing onto Tobermory Way, which is a private lane with a right-of-way that is maintained by
the HOA. She pointed out that they are a new HOA and do not have the money to widen roads.
Ms. Beck commented that the best option is to use the existing church entrance. She pointed out
that the traffic study was done on March 24 when traffic was very light.

Adam Haver gave his address as 1323 East Tobermory Way and stated that widening the road on
the east side will benefit both communities. At one time a sidewalk was shown where the
parallel parking is located. He commented that a sidewalk would be very beneficial. He hoped
to focus on solutions going forward. He was in favor of sharing the entrance with stipulations
including widening Tobermory and adding a sidewalk. Mr. Haver clarified that he would
support widening the road on the east side at the turn into the new development since that is
where the great increase in traffic will occur.

Ryan Haver gave his address as 1327 East Tobermory Way and concurred with the previous
public comments. He acknowledged that the developer has rights but Mr. Haver had safety
concerns. He did not consider the traffic study to be valid due to the circumstances surrounding
COVID-19 with there being little or no traffic at the time the study was conducted. He felt that a
compromise would be beneficial to everyone and agreed that widening the road beyond the
proposed development should not be required of the developer. The developer, however, should
be responsible for widening the road in the area of the development, which is the area covered by
the easement. He commented that it would be ideal for the Tobermory community for the only
existing entrance to be used by the new project.

Fred Dickerson gave his address as 1315 East Tobermory Way and commented that utilizing the
entrance off of Spring Lane makes the most sense. The two entrances have existed for four or
five years with no notable problems. He did not consider the left turn/right turn issues addressed
in the traffic study to be of concern.

Kristine Goddard gave her address as 5061 South Oban Court and stated that she has lived in
Tobermory for nearly five years. When she first moved to the City of Holladay, the church was
active and there were meetings in the evenings and on Sundays. She was not aware of any
problems with people coming and going from the property. Her preference was for the existing
Spring Lane/church property entrance to be maintained and used as the primary access for the
Weyburn Retreat development. She understands this would require Mr. Kruckenberg to make

Doug Beck, a five-year resident, gave his address as 1311 East Tobermory Way. He agreed with

In response to a question raised, City Planner, Jonathan Teerlink stated that within the
Tobermory development there are 24 individual units. Mr. Allred explained that Tobermory
Way is located on the developer’s property but there is an easement that allows Tobermory
residents to use it.

Commissioner Layton commented that both accesses are on the applicant’s property so
Mr. Kruckenberg has the right to use either of them. Chair Ricks stated that the right depends on
how the easement is written and whether it is exclusive or shared access. Mr. Allred clarified
that it is non-exclusive access.

Mr. Kruckenberg responded to questions raised and stated that their intent, beginning with the
demolition of the existing church, is the installation of the horizontal utilities. He noted that they
will use the church access to keep the traffic off of Tobermory Way during construction. The
existing access would be used for demolition and the extension of utilities into the proposed
subdivision. Concerning the easement and access onto Tobermory Way, the easement is non-
exclusive and can be used for all 40 residents. He is also allowed to place an amenity behind the
guest parking where the existing access is located. Mr. Kruckenberg reported that he moved the
buildings as far to the east as possible to mitigate the steep slopes.

The location of the 8 guest parking spaces was identified. Commissioner Holbrook commented
that he would be inclined to sacrifice some of the green space to address the parking off of
Tobermory elsewhere in the project, allow for the road to be widened, and include the location of
the guest parking stalls. He was pleased that the current access will be used for construction to
keep traffic off of Tobermory Way and not impact the residents.

Ways to reduce the slope were discussed. Chair Lloyd suggested that the development be shifted
to the west to make it more buildable. She preferred to see a sidewalk and road widening to
parallel parking. Commissioner Mackin commented that it is unlikely that anyone will park on
Tobermory as it is already so narrow. Possible conditions of approval were discussed.

Commissioner Mackin did not support burdening the existing undersized road with an additional
16 units, which equates to a 60% increase. She suggested that the existing road be utilized.

Commissioner Layton considered the current site plan to be a much better solution. He
supported widening the road, constructing sidewalks that are contiguous to the curbs along the
length of the road, and providing a transition at Weyburn Retreat. He supported utilizing and

Commissioner Bradshaw recommended they share the road and improve it to serve all of the

There were no further public comments. The public hearing was closed.

Commissioner Carter moved to approve the Conceptual Subdivision proposal from Jeff
Kruckenberg from Alan Kruckenberg Construction for the Weyburn Retreat located at 1306
East Spring Lane based on the following:

Findings:
      1.       Sufficient information has been received and reviewed by the city to show
               compliance with the requirement for conceptual subdivision approval.

       2.      A traffic study has been performed to examine capacity, safety and other
               concerns related to the proposed development.

       3.      As noted earlier in the report, the traffic study directly addresses many of the
               concerns and concludes that the subdivision street and access plan as proposed
               is appropriate given the level of proposed development. The study also includes
               a few recommendations to improve upon the plan such as striping Tobermory
               Lane and banning parking on the south side of Spring Lane with red curbs and
               signs. These recommendations should be specifically implemented as conditions
               of any future approval of the subdivision and conditional use permit.

       4.      Unified Fire Authority indicates support for the proposal.

       5.      Any corrections, edits to the concept plan as identified by City staff.

The applicant was encouraged to work with staff to resolve the following:

       1.      Parking issues on Tobermory Way.

       2.      Return the road and fencing to its current condition.

       3.      Road widening.

       4.      Sidewalk issue.

       5.      Utilize the current church access for construction.
