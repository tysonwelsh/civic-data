OPENING CEREMONY:
James Boyd, CAPSA Chief Development Officer offered the opening ceremony and led
the audience in the pledge of allegiance.

Mr. Boyd stated that October is Domestic Awareness Month. Last year 2,429 individuals
were helped by direct services. He added that 1 in 3 women will be affected by domestic
violence and 1 in 5 men in our State. Because victimization occurs behind closed doors,
we often do not see it nor are we aware of it.

CAPSA is an emergency homeless shelter for victims of domestic and sexual violence.
Our shelter is unique in the State of Utah because it is the only shelter that is a zero turn
away shelter meaning no residents of Logan, Cache, or Rich County will be turned away
if they qualify for services. Last year $80,000 was spent on hotels to make sure no victim
was turned away.

Also unique to our shelter is that CAPSA owns their own housing for individuals who
don’t qualify for housing due to mitigating circumstances. CAPSA owns 21 homes and
helps individuals get back on their feet. Occupants can reside in these homes for up to
two years. CAPSA also helps 20 families by subsidizing their rent within the community.
Last year, CAPSA provided direct services to 1,452 individuals who were Logan
residents.

The Council thanked CAPSA for all they do to support those in need. They added that as
a community, we should strive to put CAPSA out of business by supporting programs
that help reduce domestic violence.

Mr. Boyd also thanked the Logan City Police Department and their partnership with
CAPSA, which is truly saving lives.

Once an annexation petition has been submitted to the City according to UCA 10-2-405,
the Municipal Council needs to either accept the petition for further consideration or
reject the petition due to specific deficiencies. If the annexation petition is accepted by
the Council, the City Recorder will certify the petition and then it will be referred to
Community Development for distribution to city agencies, affected entities, and the
Planning Commission for review & recommendations. Staff recommended the Municipal
Council accept the annexation petition.

The proposed annexation request has tentatively been scheduled on the Planning
Commission's November 14, 2024 hearing agenda, the Council's November 19, 2024
workshop agenda, and the Council's December 3, 2024 hearing agenda.

If the Council rejects the petition, staff will then follow the process outlined in UCA 10-
2-405(3)(a)&(b) and direct the applicant to address any deficiencies with their annexation
petition and then have them resubmit once corrected.

Consideration of a proposed resolution establishing a Credit Card Convenience Fee
– Resolution 24-41 – Richard Anderson (1:06:31)

Finance Director Richard Anderson addressed the Council regarding the proposed
resolution. He explained that the City of Logan takes credit card payments for various
City services. The Finance Department has administratively established a limit on credit
card payments for the purpose of controlling credit card fees and customers have
expressed a willingness to pay a credit card convenience fee for those transactions in
excess of the limit. Mr. Anderson proposed that a credit card convenience fee for credit
card payments over the administratively determined payment limit be established as
follows: Credit Card Convenience Fee = 3 %.

Chair A. Anderson asked if the three percent fee is just for credit card payments over the
administrative limits or for any credit card usage.




                                                                                    8 | Page
             Logan Municipal Council Minutes ~ Logan, Utah ~ October 15, 2024
Mr. Anderson replied that going forward the 3% will apply to any transaction made with
a credit card for over $3,000. Currently, there is a $3,000 administrative limit on credit
card transactions with the City.

Councilmember Johnson inquired if the 3% fee would affect utility bills.

Mr. Anderson answered the 3% fee will not affect utility bills under $3,000 but over
$3,000 the convivence fee will be applied. He did caution that with time and as credit
card fees grow, the City will have to evaluate whether to continue to cover credit card
fees for any amount.

Chair A. Anderson asked who determines the administrative limit.

Mr. Anderson responded that the finance department determines the administrative limit
in regard to fees.

Councilmember Johnson expressed concern that the wording of the resolution doesn’t
specify that the fee would only be applied to bills over the administrative limit.
The proposed resolution will be an action item and public hearing at the November 5,
2024, Council meeting.
