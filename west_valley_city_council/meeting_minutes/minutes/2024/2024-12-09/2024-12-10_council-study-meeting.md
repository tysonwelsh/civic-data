RESOLUTION 24-177: APPROVE THE EXECUTION OF A COOPERATIVE

OF FORESTRY, FIRE, AND STATE LANDS
Dan Johnson, Public Works Director, presented proposed Resolution 24-177 that would
approve the Execution of a Cooperative Agreement Between West Valley City and the Utah
Division of Forestry, Fire, and State Lands.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       West Valley City applied for and received a Jordan River Cooperative Weed
       Management Area Grant to reimburse the City for work to fight phragmites and other
       invasive species along the Jordan River in specific locations for a period of three
       years. The areas of work include phragmite stands near 3900 South and the Pioneer
       Crossing Park, and additional maintenance work at the Cottonwood Pond at 3300
       South. Phragmite mitigation will be completed using the recently purchased Marsh
       Master amphibious vehicle. Work will be completed in phases, corresponding with
       the appropriate times of year to effectively treat phragmites.

       Work also includes certain Russian Olive and Tamarisk tree removals to be
       completed by the Fire Department wildland fire crews and the Public Works
       Department.

       The City will work with a private group, Tree Utah, to revegetate the treated areas,
       with the intent to eliminate the invasive species in the longer term.

COMMUNICATIONS
A.  PROFFESIONAL STANDARDS REVIEW BOARD ANNUAL UPDATE
    Eric Bunderson, City Attorney, stated that the Professional Standards Review
    Board (PSRB) plays a vital role in the city by reviewing police activities, including
    all uses of force, vehicular pursuits, and internal affairs investigations. The board,
    composed of dedicated citizens, provides quarterly reports to city officials.
    Key points from the latest report:

              Data Reviewed: There were 702 uses of force this year, defined broadly to
               include actions like drawing a weapon or using a taser.
              Purpose: These reviews help identify trends and potential issues, including
               problem officers.
              Findings: Most cases were compliant with policy, while a few were not.
               The PSRB made recommendations in these cases, which the police chief
               reviewed for potential discipline.
              Disciplinary Actions: Recommendations often resulted in measures such
               as reprimands, additional training, suspensions, or officer resignations
               before final discipline.

        The PSRB does not directly discipline officers but plays a critical advisory role to
        ensure accountability and adherence to policy.

        The Mayor and Council had no further questions or concerns.

   Councilmember Whetstone stated that housing is a huge problem but noted that

   imposing legislation with an expiration date.

   Brandon discussed the complexities of housing policy in Utah. He highlighted how
   Utah's unique demographic trends, such as historically high birth rates, are shifting
   with sub-replacement fertility rates for the first time. While the state continues to
   grow, much of this growth now stems from in-migration rather than natural
   increase. Brandon indicated that increasing housing inventory might attract more
   migrants, which, while beneficial, doesn’t address housing affordability because
   demand rises in tandem with supply. He referenced Governor Cox's perspective
   that Utah shouldn't compromise its appeal as a state. However, this dynamic creates
   a persistent affordability challenge. Brandon drew comparisons to declining urban
   centers like Detroit or Cleveland, where housing oversupply due to population
   decline led to affordability but under undesirable circumstances. He cautioned that
   decisions that seem suitable today may not align with future demographic and
   economic realities. Brandon underscored the long-term planning challenges
   associated with balancing population trends, housing policies, and affordability.

A.   POTENTIAL FUTURE AGENDA ITEMS

        Councilmember Huynh expressed concern about rental practices in the city,
        highlighting a distinction between large apartment complexes and individual
        landlords. He noted that apartment complexes typically perform thorough
        background and employment checks on prospective tenants, ensuring a smoother
        operation with fewer issues. In contrast, single-home landlords often forgo these
        checks, creating opportunities for problems such as drug-related activities and
        other issues that negatively impact the community. Councilmember Huynh
        suggested involving the police department to conduct background checks for
        landlords, with landlords paying for the service, similar to private background
        check providers. This system could help "screen out bad apples" and make the
        city safer. He emphasized that the goal isn’t to label individuals as "bad people"
        but to address behaviors that harm the community, ensuring that all renters meet
        basic standards.

        Councilmember Huynh raised concerns about the lack of utilization of the
        Residential Sustainability (RS) zoning, introduced in 2020 to promote zero-
        energy housing developments. He noted that the RS zoning was initially targeted
        for smaller parcels of land, such as pockets of 4-10 acres, with the intent to make
        the city a leader in sustainable housing in the valley. Councilmember Huynh
        observed that, over the past four years, only one property, located at 3200 West
        and 3724 South, has adopted the RS zoning. He expressed frustration that no
        other pocket developments have followed suit, questioning whether the zoning
        was being applied selectively to one property or whether the city remained
        committed to its sustainability objectives. He asked for clarification on whether
        the city intends to continue promoting RS zoning across other developments or if
        the initiative has lost momentum, given the apparent lack of broader
        implementation.

        Mayor Lang stated that RS zoning is something that is requested by the
        developers so the City can’t control that. She indicated that staff could perhaps
        provide a report as to why people aren’t applying. Mayor Lang stated that there
        are county facilities that may provide background checks so the burden doesn’t
