                                       DISCUSSION ITEM – ZONING MAP ANALYSIS




  5.          12Z20 - Discussion of a Zoning Map Analysis for the Area South of 4800 South, North of 5400 South, East
              of Canal Street and West of the Jordan River. (Jim Spung AICP/Senior Planner)


5.1            Mr. Spung presented this item. The City of Taylorsville has initiated a zoning map analysis for the
        area identified in this application. The purpose of this study is to better understand the current built
        condition to help inform future policy.

        5.1.1       Several lots in the study area were created under the County’s jurisdiction or were established
                    using an older set of development regulations. The development regulations have been amended
                    over time resulting in the creation of some legal nonconforming lots. A “legal nonconforming lot”
                    is a lot that does not meet the minimum lot area and/or dimensional standards in the zoning
                    district in which it is located but was compliant at the time the lot was created. A legal
                    nonconforming lot is sometimes referred to as “grandfathered.”

        5.1.2       Due to the large scale of the study area, Staff has decided to divide the research into a few
                    different phases. The first phase includes two neighborhoods with a relatively large number of
                    legal nonconforming lots. The legal nonconforming lots in the two identified neighborhoods are in
                    the R-1-10 zoning district, however, several lots are between 8,000 and 10,000 square feet.
                    Additionally, several of these lots have a lot width less than 80 feet, which falls below the
                    minimum required lot width in the R-1-10 zone.

        5.1.3       In order to reduce the number of nonconforming lots in this area, Staff recommends revising the
                    zoning designation from R-1-10 to R-1-8. This proposed change would reduce the minimum lot
                    size required from 10,000 square feet to 8,000 square feet and reducing the minimum required lot

                frontage from 80 feet to 70 feet. This proposed amendment would help align the zoning
                designation with the actual built condition of the area. These changes would not alter the existing
                character of the area because several lots have already been built to the R-1-8 zoning district
                standards. These two neighborhoods are designated as “lot-density residential” in the City’s
                General Plan, a change from R-1-10 to R-1-8 is consistent with this General Plan designation.

        5.1.4   Staff’s analysis also considered how the recommended changes to lot size and lot width would
                impact the potential creation of new lots (i.e., subdivisions). Since the area is primarily built-out,
                there are already very few opportunities for new lots to be formed. Lots that are currently eligible
                to be subdivided would still be eligible after the proposed changes. Additional research is required
                to determine if any existing lots that meet the minimum area and frontage requirements for a
                subdivision would actually be eligible after considering the other residential design standards (e.g.,
                utilities, access, geological constraints, easements, floodplains, etc.). Staff is not aware of any
                detrimental impacts of the proposed change from R-1-10 to R-1-8.
