MAG Small Area Population Projections Tim Hereth, MAG (2:10) Tim Hereth presented the Small Area Population Projections 2020-2050 Online Map Application. The population forecast is updated alongside the update of the Regional Transportation Plan (RTP). Each four-year RTP cycle shows more growth in Utah County. The forecast uses the year 2019 as a base to start. The projections go to 2050 to align with the RTP and are used to update the RTP, the traffic forecasts, and air quality conformity. The draft population forecast was presented to the MPO TAC in fall 2022 for feedback and comments. Seven communities gave valuable feedback. All feedback was incorporated into the inputs of the Real Estate Market Model which is the primary tool for producing the population projections. Staff recommends that the MPO TAC recommend to the MPO Board approval of updated population and employment projections. Brad Kenison moved to recommend to the MPO Board approval of the small area population projections as presented. Mary DeLaMare-Shaefer seconded the motion. Approve Highway, Transit, and Active Transportation Maps (14:17) Every four years, the Regional Transportation Plan (RTP) is updated to align with the changing growth trends of Utah County, comply with federal and state regulations, and promote the transportation challenges present in the region. The RTP includes a listing of capacity projects that improve and expand the regional highway, transit, and active transportation networks. The MPO uses regional land use and travel models co-developed with MAG, WFRC, and the state to identify congested areas where proposed highway and transit capacity projects can keep people and goods moving throughout the region. Approve Air Quality Conformity (33:32) Before TransPlan50 can gain final approval, the plan is analyzed for conformity with Air Quality rules and requirements; namely, that the vehicle emissions produced do not exceed the emissions budget approved by the EPA. The Conformity Determination Report is that analysis. FHWA must concur with the MPO finding that the Regional Plan conforms to federal air quality regulations. After FHWA concurs, TransPlan50 can be considered approved and eligible projects funded in the first 10 years of the plan become eligible to receive federal funding. The TransPlan50 Conformity Determination Report demonstrates that construction of the proposed funded projects within the plan and the associated vehicle emissions conform to current EPA regulations. Staff recommends that the MPO TAC recommend to the MPO Board approval of the draft Conformity Determination Report for the 2023 TransPlan50 - Regional Transportation Plan, citing that the plan does conform to all federal air quality regulations. Gordon Haight moved to recommend to the MPO Board approval of the draft Conformity Determination Report for the 2023 TransPlan50-regional Transportation Plan, noting that the plan conforms to all federal air quality regulations. Andy Spencer seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. c. Financial Plan (44:15) This plan cycle, the MPO will not create a 40 plus page professional document for TransPlan50. Transit Signal Prioritization Study - B ob Allen, MAG (1:08:37) Michael Sheffield, WGC reported that Transit Signal Priority (TSP) is an operational strategy that facilitates the movement of transit vehicles through traffic-signaled controlled intersections. This will provide extra green time to transit vehicles and modify timing as bus is approaching to extend green or provide early green light signal. The final product will be a guidebook for cities on how to create and implement TSP. Project Objectives include: 1. Prepare for TSP implementation across multiple regional and local jurisdictions 2. Understand how TSP can be applied to all transit modes to improve person throughput and reduce roadway congestion 3. Explore methods for TSP to support future connected vehicle technology development 4. Develop principles for TSP conditions and route prioritization MAG Staff recommends that MPO TAC become aware that the UTA and UDOT adopted MPO Technical Advisory Committee March 27, 2023 Page 5 platform/technology has been established to assist cities by supporting improved safety and the interoperable deployment of TSP and signal preemption throughout the region. Safe Streets For All – Update - Jim Price, MAG (1:19:47) The Safe Streets for All (SS4A) is a multi-year USDOT program to assess and address both regional and local roadway safety issues. The program provides $1 billion nationwide which is a two-part program. 1. Complete a Safety Action Plan, which will identify high crash rate areas and seek to address these through safety-oriented programs and projects. 2. Apply for implementation funds MAG received a $1.25 million SS4A Safety Action Plan grant. This effort will be MAG wide and take a deep dive into crashes and other data to identify critical programs and projects. MAG will administer the program funds in cooperation with FHWA. Next Steps: 1. Staff are currently working with FHWA representatives to execute a Grant Agreement that will provide access to the planning funds. 2. Organize a steering and oversight committee to work with staff to finalize the project Scope of Work, select a consultant team, provide feedback to the consultant and liaise with TAC and the MPO Board. 3. Develop a comprehensive Safety Action Plan, identify projects, and create project concepts and cost estimates to prepare for implementation grant applications. The goal is to complete this effort by late spring/early summer of 2024 to allow for Implementation Grant applications during FY24-26. FHWA anticipates taking applications each September. MAG Staff seeks MPO TAC volunteers to serve on the Project Steering Committee. Staff suggests a small number of volunteers from each of the geographic quadrants in order to achieve equitable representation from the urban, suburban, and more rural areas of Utah County to create a representative and workable group size.
