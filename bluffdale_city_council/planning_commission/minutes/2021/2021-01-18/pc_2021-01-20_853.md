2.     Training and Open and Public Meeting Laws, Staff Presenter, Todd Sheeran.

City Attorney, Todd Sheeran, provided the training, which was facilitated by a PowerPoint
presentation. He first reviewed the definition of "open meetings" and reported that a meeting is the
convening of a public body to discuss public business in an open setting. Due to the COVID-1 9
pandemic, the Utah State Legislature has allowed electronic meetings to take place without an
"anchor location" if there is a substantial safety risk. He clarified that a meeting is not a chance
gathering of members of the public body. A quorum must be present to conduct the business of the
body. In the case of the Planning Commission, that is three or more members.

Mr. Sheeran next reviewed the cautions that need to be exercised when sharing emails between
individuals or group emails. Also, when a meeting has adjourned, it is essential that there is not a
"meeting after the meeting." Chance meetings of members of the public body are permitted;
however, no City business can be discussed or detennined. He noted that per the Government
Records Access and Management Act ("ORAMA"), a legal requirement can be imposed to retrieve
all emails, whether they are made on the City email system or private emails. He stressed that all
City business should be conducted using City email addresses. He gave an example of citizen
complaints regarding a school board that allegedly exchanged inappropriate emails. The exchange
of emails can potentially constitute a violation of the Open and Public Meeting Laws and be subject
to a ORAMA request for all of the emails that are exchanged on an issue.

With regard to closed meetings, Mr. Sheeran stated that it would be extremely unlikely that the
Planning Commission would have a legitimate reason to hold a closed meeting. He enumerated
reasons for which a closed session can be held and when a closed meeting would be prohibited. A
Closed Meeting violation constitutes a Class C Misdemeanor. A motion needs to be made to go
into and come out of a closed meeting. A public record needs to be made of an action to go into a
closed meeting. The subjects discussed must remain confidential. Any action taken must be part of
the open meeting.

Mr. Sheeran showed a video demonstrating a meeting that gets out of control due to public protests.
The Chair and Commissioners need to decide how to handle such a situation. He showed a video
illustrating how an out-of-control protester, or protesters, can be dealt with. If a meeting gets
completely out of control, the Chair can end the meeting and continue the remaining agenda items
to the next meeting so that they do not have to be renoticed.

Mr. Sheeran next reviewed the potential for conflicts of interest that might exist among members of
the public body or City staff. He explained that a person who has a conflict of interest must declare
it but that person can legally still vote. He noted that it might be more ethical for the person to
recuse himself/herself from the vote. Despite one's best efforts to avoid a conflict of interest, the
public might have a different perception of said conflict of interest.

Mr. Sheeran would provide the Commissioner members a sheet summarizing Robert's Rules of
Order to help with the parliamentary process used during meetings. He explained that the Planning
Commission is not legally obligated to follow Robert's Rules of Order; however, they are a good
template to follow.

3.     CONSIDERATION AND VOTE on a Proposed Site Plan Application for Covent
       Garden Consisting of 4.94 Acres located in the HC Heavy Commercial Zone at 14664
       South 855 West, Brix.ton Partners, Applicant (Application 2020-33). Staff Presenter,
       Ellen Oakman.

Associate City Planner, Ellen Oakman, reviewed the site plan and identified the location of the
subject property. She noted that 855 West is currently a private road, but the intent is for it to be
dedicated as a public road to provide greater flexibility for the surrounding property. The proposed
use of the subject property is compatible with the zoning. The site plan includes four buildings,
which are designated as Buildings A, B, C, and D. The applicants are also in the process of
preparing a condominium plat nearby. The front of the buildings will face the parking lot. The
landscaping includes a utility corridor, so the trees at that location cannot exceed 12 feet in height.
The site plan was detennined to meet the City's landscaping requirements. Ms. Oakman next
reviewed the design and street elevations. The colors, materials, textures, and rooflines will be
varied, and they will include canopies.

Chair Cragun asked about the timing of the construction of the buildings and the dedication of 855
West to become a City road. City Plmmer/Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell, stated
that the plans are being prepared to mobilize the project. Several entities have committed to the
site, so things are progressing forward, ostensibly at the same time.

Chair Cragun invited the applicant to address the Planning Commission.

Matt Kelly from Brixton Partners was thrilled with the project and ready to get going. The
buildings will be beautiful and will have phenomenal tenants. With regard to the road, Mr. Kelly
stated that it is imperative to have it completed about the same time as the project, if not sooner.
They are committed to upgrading it to qualify as a public road. They hope to break ground within
two weeks of approval. The completion goal for turning the buildings over to the tenants and
owners in September of this year.

Mr. Crowell read an email he received from Shivam Shah that he asked Mr. Crowell to read.
Mr. Shah thanked the City for being such an outstanding partner. Brixton Partners has other
projects they will be bringing to the Planning Commission. Of the 24 units being developed, 18
units have been pre-sold to companies that will enhance the economy of Bluffdale.

Chair Cragun reported that the item involves an administrative action and will not include a public
hearing. The Planning Commission serves as the Land Use Authority for the application.

Stephen Walston moved to approve the Covent Garden Site Plan Application 2020-21, subject
to the following:

Conditions:

Mr. Crowell briefly reviewed the Economic Development Meeting that took place the previous
week. Long-range transportation and transit planning issues were discussed as well as the Wasatch
Front Regional Council's ("WFRC") Long-Range Regional Transportation Plan, which includes
long-range road projects and transit planning, extending to the year 2050. A variety of studies were
underway regarding Southwest Salt Lake County and transit along the prison site. This planning is
important for the economic development of Bluffdale.

There is still no plan for a bus route in Bluffdale although many innovative ideas were discussed.
The RDA was discussed briefly, along with the Design Ordinance for site plans. The Long-Range
Transportation Plan still includes the potential for a FrontRunner stop in Bluffdale sometime in the
future. It will be based on the desire of the citizens and available funding. The location could move
from its current proposed location near the new middle school.

Mr. Crowell reported that the next City Council meeting will include the issues of senior housing.
