2. Public Hearing and possible action regarding a Conditional Use Permit at 
Building G of the Park East II Business Community Commons for a new 
manufacturing and brewing location for the Park City Brewery; 4554 N 
Forestdale Drive, #41; PEIIBCC-41; Scott Ray, applicant- Sean Lewis, County 
Planner  
 
Planner Lewis said the Park City Brewery is proposing to use all of Building G for a 
brewery and warehouse.  He pointed out that a brewery is not listed in the Use Table; 
however, the Director has determined that a brewery is similar in nature and fits under

the category of heavy manufacturing.  Traditionally bars, taverns, and pubs are listed as 
a Conditional Use Permit (CUP) in the Service Commercial Zone.   
 
Planner Lewis said none of the building’s exteriors are being changed.  This is an 
application is for the use of the building as a brewery and a tap room.  Staff 
recommends that following a public hearing, the Commission votes to approve the CUP

Commissioner Cooke verified that heavy manufacturing is allowed under the CUP.

tasting room is another use that would require a second CUP, if so, are two CUPs 
needed.  Planner Lewis answered that whenever there is more than one use being 
sought, the more stringent process applies, but only one application is needed.  In this 
case, the CUP approval approves both uses. 
 
Commissioner Harte asked if a future Planning Director may decide that this use is not 
heavy manufacturing.  Planner Lewis answered that the CUP runs with the land, which 
means the use can continue in that location in perpetuity.  If the Commission wants to, 
they could write a Code amendment to allow breweries in the Service Commercial Zone.  
If that were done, it would come before this body and the County Council.   
 
The public hearing was opened.  No comments were made and the public hearing was closed.

FINDINGS OF FACT 
1. PCBC Lot 19, LLC is the listed fee title owner of Parcel PEIIBCC-41.   
2. Parcel PEIIBCC-41 is 0.331 acres in size. 
3. Parcel PEIIBCC-41 is located at 4554 North Forestdale Drive #41. 
4. Parcel PEIIBCC-41 is located within the Service Commercial Zoning District. 
5. Both Heavy Manufacturing and Bar, Tavern, or Club are listed as  Conditional 
Uses in the Service Commercial Zoning district. 
6. Heavy Manufacturing is defined in the Snyderville Basin Development Code as: 
“The converting of raw or partially processed materials into a product used for 
further processing or distribution.  Examples of heavy manufacturing include 
lumber and paper processing or distribution.  Example of heavy manufacturing 
include lumber and paper mills, sewage treatment plants, stone, clay, glass

product manufacturing, asphalt,  and concrete batch plants, and similar 
operation.  These uses may be conducted partially or wholly outdoors and 
usually create noxious byproducts such as dust, fumes, hazardous waste 
products, noise, vibration, and glare.”     
7. Bar, Tavern, or Club is defined in the Snyderville Basin Development Code as: 
“An establishment serving alcoholic beverages for consumption on the premises.  
The term “bar” shall also include taverns, discotheques, nightclubs, private 
liquor clubs, saloons, and other similar uses.   
8. The Park East II Business Community commons Condominiums has been 
granted a building permit to begin construction of all buildings within the 
development. 
9. Adjacent uses include: warehousing, auto repair, and gun club.   
10. The building will be used for the production, packaging warehousing, and 
distribution of products made by the Park City Brewery.  The applicant also 
proposes to have a tasting or tap room that will be open to the public.   
11. This proposal puts a large scale manufacturing building in the center of the 
existing light industrial zoning district and development intended for such uses.  
The inclusion of a taproom, is complementary to the primary industrial use, and 
will add a quasi-mixed use element to the Park East II development parcel.   
12. Snyderville Basin Development Code provisions regarding building massing, 
architecture, lighting, etc. were addressed during the condominium approval 
process.  There are no proposed changes to the exterior of the building, existing 
landscaping, or parking areas, which have previously been found to be in 
compliance.   
 
CONCLUSIONS OF LAW 
1. The proposed use of Building G for Heavy Manufacturing and as a Bar, Tavern, 
or Club is compliant with the Snyderville Basin General Plan. 
2. The use conforms to all applicable provision of the Snyderville Basin 
Development Code.

WORK SESSION 
 
1. Discussion regarding possible amendments to Chapters 1 and 2 and the 
RR/MR/HS sections of Chapter 3 of the Development Code- Ray Milliner, Principal 
Planner and Jennifer Strader, Senior Planner   
 
Planner Milliner said he and Planner Strader have been working to correct 
inconsistencies, update, add references, and make other needed corrections to the 
Development Code.  Tonight they ask the Commission to discuss Chapters 1 and 2 and 
the first half of Chapter 3.     
 
Planner Milliner said Staff is recommending the language for Chapter 1 be simplified.  Much 
of the language is more appropriate to include in the General Plan.  They have eliminated the 
Statement of Purpose.  In its place, they have inserted the Title, Authority, Purpose and 
Applicability.  
 
Chapter 2 is a user guide.  It will help a layman with things such as understanding how to 
read a zoning map and how to find their property on the map.  The intent of this chapter is to 
make the processes simpler and easier to understand.   
 
Planner Milliner said they would also like to discuss the first section of Chapter 3.  This 
chapter contains the different zoning districts.  They are proposing to eliminate the use table.  
In its place, they will have the permitted uses at the beginning of each chapter.  Tonight, they 
are asking for feedback from the Commission.   
 
Vice-Chair Stevens opened the time for overall comments among the Commission.

Commissioner Cooke said he appreciates Staff’s effort to make this simpler for the lay 
person.  Many people tell him they don’t understand how to even begin.  He is uneasy about 
deleting the General Plan language from the document.  It feels like a piece of the soul of the 
Code is being removed.   
 
Commissioner Fine asked if they need all of the zones that are currently on the books.  
Should there be less?  Should there be more?  Planner Milliner said as they grow, there will 
most likely be a need for more zones.     
 
Commissioner Harte asked if a use is not listed, is it prohibited.  Or is there a process an 
applicant can go through that would allow it.  Would that process be overly burdensome?  
Can some of the current uses be removed?  If some uses were deleted, the Commission’s 
hand would not be forced when a CUP comes to them.   
 
Attorney Brackin said the Commission can add or take away uses.  The current language is 
that unless a use is specifically allowed, it is prohibited.  She advised that not be changed.  If 
a use is removed, the only way to get it back is through a Code amendment, which is a fairly 
cumbersome process.  The Commission needs to weigh if this would be a better process than 
a Conditional Use Permit.   
 
Planner Strader said they want to be sure that each use has a proposed definition.  Most of 
them do, but some definitions need to be cleaned up.  The language should be concise.  Staff 
will make sure the Commission has a copy of the definitions.  Planner Milliner said that any 
capitalized word means it has been defined.   
 
Commissioner Dickey said he likes removing the General Plan language, but the document 
should begin with a strong statement.  He likes both the Use Table and having the uses in 
each section.  He suggested that hyperlinks be added.  Vice-Chair Stevens agreed.  She 
added that the County’s website is often difficult to maneuver through.

Commissioner Fine asked the reason behind the update for the Development Code.  Planner

realized the commercial areas in these zones were disappearing.  It is taking years to rezone 
some of the areas back to commercial.  When a use is eliminated, they need to be sure this is 
not a use they will never want in the area.
