  Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
  information as follows:

         On 9/17/2019, the City Council adopted a 6-month temporary land use
         regulation or moratorium (Ordinance # 19-38) that prohibited rezone
         applications to the RM (multiple unit dwelling residential) zone as well as
         ordinance revisions pertaining to multi-family housing. Prior to the
         moratorium adoption the City had received several applications to change
         the criteria used to determine whether a property is eligible to be rezoned to
         the RM zone as well as several rezone requests to the RM zone. The
         moratorium was passed to allow time for the City to consider where
         additional multi-family housing should be allowed as well as the standards
         under which it should be allowed.

         Following the adoption of the moratorium, the Council held two discussions
         during their study meetings on 11/12/2019 and 12/17/2019. During the later
         meeting a draft of several ordinance revisions was presented for discussion
         to the Council. The revisions incorporated standards used in recent
         development agreements for multi-family housing and changes to the RM
         zone eligibility criteria. The Council then directed staff to start the formal
         ordinance revision process.

         After reviewing the ordinance revisions, the Planning Commission
         recommended some changes described below:
              Zone change requests to the RM zone should be allowed on
                properties with access and frontage on a street with a planned
                right-of-way width of at least 80 feet. The density of such
                properties should be limited to 7 units/acre.
              The three light rail stations that were added to the proposed
                ordinance should be removed.

         Since the initial direction for this ordinance came from the Council, the
         proposed ordinance does not incorporate the Planning Commission’s
         recommendations.

RESOLUTION 20-25: AWARD A CONTRACT TO BLACK & MCDONALD
LIMITED FOR CERTAIN LED STREETLIGHT IMPROVEMENTS
Erik Brondum, Public Works, presented proposed Resolution 20-25 that would award a
contract to Black & McDonald Limited for certain LED streetlight improvements.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       The project will change out 5,775 existing non-LED street light cobrahead fixtures,
       tear drop lamps and post top lamps to LED fixtures and lamps.

       The Public Works Department retrofits street lights to LED while performing
       maintenance on the street light system. This has resulted in slow progress in
       reducing the energy usage and costs of the system. Energy studies indicate that
       changing to LED street lights is one of the shortest returns on investment for energy
       savings.

       The project will result in significant energy savings to the City, which will pay for
       the bond over a ten (10) year period.

Councilmember Lang asked if the City will be working with Rocky Mountain Power. Erik
replied yes and noted that the City will be receiving a $100,000 automatic rebate from this
project. Mayor Bigelow asked what the approximate return in savings timeframe will be.

to install the lights. Erik replied that he would estimate completion in August of this year.

CONSIDER APPLICATION S-3-2019, FILED BY DANIEL HERZOG,
REPRESENTING DAI, REQUESTING FINAL PLAT APPROVAL FOR THE
SUNSET HILLS SUBDIVISION- PHASES 3-5, LOCATED AT 6600 SOUTH
AFTERGLOW LANE
Steve Lehman, CED, presented proposed application S-3-2019, filed by Daniel Herzog,
representing DAI, requesting Final Plat Approval for the Sunset Hills Subdivision- Phases 3-
5, located at 6600 South Afterglow Lane.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       Daniel Herzog, representing DAI, is requesting final plat approval for the last
       phases of the Sunset Hills Subdivision. The subject property was rezoned in May
       2006 from the A-1 zone to the R-1-10 zone. The overall project received
       preliminary approval in August 2006.

       The final plat consists of 94 lots on 35 acres. This equates to an overall density of
       2.6 units per acre. Lots range in size from 8,000 square feet to 18,000 square feet.
       The average lot size is approximately 9,800 square feet.

       Access to the subdivision will be gained from 6600 South and from other stub
       streets platted as part of phase 2 to the east. An additional sub street will be platted
       within this phase to provide access to the west. All streets within these phases will
       be dedicated to a 54-foot width to include curb, gutter, sidewalk and asphalt.

       As with previous phases in Sunset Hills, staff will recommend that the developer
       coordinate grading and drainage with the City Engineering Division. The grading
       and drainage plan will need to reflect methods of lot grading and retaining if
       necessary.

       As part of the preliminary plat, a soils report was submitted. The report stated that
       no ground water was encountered to a depth of 15 feet. To staff’s knowledge, no
       ground water has been encountered for any of the existing homes constructed to
       date.

      The developer will be responsible to coordinate the availability of all utilities for
      the subdivision. With regards to water and sewer, the developer and KID are
      coordinating the construction of two new water tanks to service this subdivision.

      There are a few parcels within the boundary of the subdivision. Parcels A&B will
