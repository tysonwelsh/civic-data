2.     ADOPT (OR DENY) AN ORDINANCE TO AMEND SECTIONS 1-6-B, 1-6-C, 2-2-A, 3-3, 3-15, 3-51,
       AND 5-2 OF THE UTAH COUNTY LAND USE ORDINANCE TO ADD BUILDINGS AND
       FACILITIES FOR THE REPAIR OF AGRICULTURAL EQUIPMENT AND AGRICULTURAL
       VEHICLES AS A PERMITTED USE IN THE RESIDENTIAL AGRICULTURAL (RA-5) ZONE,
       AND TO AMEND SECTION 3-30-D RELATED TO HOME OCCUPATION BUSINESSES
(Track A, 13:30)
Bryce Armstrong of the Utah County Community Development Office summarized the commissioners had
requested an ordinance be created to allow farm equipment repairs to take place in the Residential Agricultural (RA-
5) Zone; currently, vehicle repair is only allowed in neighborhood commercial, industrial, and highway services
zones. Mr. Armstrong reviewed the acreage requirements, the length of time a vehicle can be stored on property, the
definition of ‘agricultural’ equipment, home offices, and vehicle sizes. Commissioner Lee asked about parking
space requirements, and Commissioner Graves commented on the need for additional room for certain vehicles.
Peggy Kelsey of the Utah County Community Development Office came forward to address the differences between
home occupation and repair services – they are exclusive of each other, so vehicle repair is not held to the same
noise restrictions as a business office.

(Track A, 28:10)
Commissioner Lee asked whether there could be unintended consequences for other businesses as a result of this
ordinance. Bryce Armstrong talked about permitting commercial use in residential areas, and mentioned
compatibility concerns voiced by Santaquin City and Payson City.

Benjamin resident Dan Johnson thanked the Board of Commissioners, the Utah County Planning Commission, and
the Utah County Community Development Office for addressing this issue. He had concerns about the 90-day limit
for vehicle repairs on the property, but is satisfied that the Board is considering extending the time frame to six
months. Mr. Johnson reiterated his gratitude for Peggy Kelsey.

                 Commissioner Graves made the motion to adopt the ordinance to amend the
                 Utah County Land Use Ordinance to add buildings and facilities for the repair
                 of agricultural equipment and agricultural vehicles as a permitted use in the
                 RA-5 Zone, and to amend Section 3-30-D related to Home Occupation
                 Businesses, with the changes as discussed in Regular Agenda Item No. 2: editing
                 the last sentence of Section 3-30-D-2 to read “and one trailer.”; and setting the
                 limit at “six months from date of intake.” The motion was seconded by
                 Commissioner Ivie and carried with the following vote:

4.     APPROVE THE RECOMMENDED LIST OF PROJECTS PROPOSED BY THE MOUNTAINLAND
       ASSOCIATION OF GOVERNMENTS REGIONAL PLANNING COMMITTEE TO USE THE
       COUNTY TRANSPORTATION SALES TAX FUNDS, PROJECTED FOR THE 2019 & 2020 YEARS
       (Continued from the June 27, 2017 Commission meeting)
(Track A, 33:15)
Utah County Public Works Director Richard Nielson came forward with Andrew Jackson and Shawn Eliot of the
Mountainland Association of Governments. Mr. Nielson reviewed there are two new projects for the Board to
consider utilizing county funds: the widening of Elk Ridge Drive, and Phase 3 of the Provo Lakeview Parkway –
numbers 20 and 22 on the 2016 TIP Project Selection Funding List, respectively. When Commissioner Graves
explained why he would like to see the number 12 Orem roundabout removed from the list, Mr. Eliot clarified this is
a separate project from the roundabout identified by Commissioner Graves.

Andrew Jackson answered a question which had been posed during the previous Commission meeting, letting the
Board know it is possible to partially approve the list. Since the full list includes 33 projects, with everything
numbered after item 18 categorized as “To be funded if additional funds become available,” Commissioner Lee
suggested all items under that heading be removed entirely. Commissioner Lee explained this would be to prevent
re-prioritizing projects without additional study by the county. Richard Nielson pointed out any excess county funds
would need to come back to the Board for review before re-allocation.

(Track A, 39:55)
Andrew Jackson compared the situation to a hypothetical request from a Public Works Office. If Public Works
plans for the purchase of a truck within a budget, but later another project becomes more important, the department
could theoretically take the same funds for the higher priority. However issues can arise and the office is directed to
either purchase the truck or allow another department to use the funds; more often than not, the first office will
choose to obtain the vehicle even if it is not urgent rather than lose the money entirely. Mr. Jackson noted this can
happen between the approval process and construction of road projects identified on this list. He gave practical
examples by addressing the Murdock Canal Trail and the Provo Airport Westside Connector. Mr. Jackson and the
commissioners discussed the prioritization process, incentives, and city road plans.

(Track A, 49:30)
Commissioner Lee noted if the cities choose to move forward with the $3,000,000 expense for a ‘free fare zone’ as
part of the Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) project, the county portion of those funds would be in jeopardy. Commissioner
Lee asked for it to be clearly stated in the record that he was assured the project would be a success based on models
throughout the entire BRT process, but now that construction is 30-40% complete it has been decided “the
modelling stinks.” It seems that ridership goals cannot be reached without giving services away for free, and that is
in addition to the already extensive subsidization from other entities such as the Utah Transit Authority (UTA) and
Provo City. Andrew Jackson noted the success of BRT is relative – it can be based on revenue, reduced traffic, or
overall environmental impact. Commissioner Lee reiterated he is opposed to a free-fare zone; the Board will not
authorize the Utah County contribution for that purpose if the cities approve it. Commissioner Graves mentioned
while UTA may keep individual residents off the roads, they are still causing wear which must be repaired by the
cities.
