BUDGET OVERVIEW
Danyce Steck presented an overview of the FY 2020/2021 Proposed Budget. She explained
changes from previous proposed budgets presented to the Council: increased transparency;
work and performance measures; color coded fiscal years; 5-year plans; and new funds.
Changes yet to be implemented include: increased communication; key performance
indicators to other cities; long-term financial plan; and citizen’s budget.

Ms. Steck presented General Fund revenue and expense assumptions pre and post COVID-
19, and explained that West Jordan anticipates a 15% loss in sales tax revenue, with the
following recommended General Fund adjustments in response:

       Revenue
   •   Transfer in 5% utility revenue from utility funds

       Expense

            •    Reduction in force
            •    Extended life on police vehicles to 5 years
            •    Transportation sales tax funding Streets Department

            •    Use of reserve funds

       Ms. Steck presented a schedule of upcoming budget meetings and public hearings, with
       adoption of the FY 2021 Final Budget and certification of the tax rate scheduled for June
       24, 2020. Ms. Steck showed ending fund balances and a General Fund summary, and
       answered questions from the Council.

       Responding to a question from Councilmember Green, Ms. Steck pointed out that property
       tax is the only revenue source over which the City has direct control. Councilmember
       Green commented that either the budget needs to be cut by 8%, or property tax needs to
       increase by 20% for the City to break even, at a time when many citizens are unemployed
       and struggling financially. Councilmember Green expressed support for cutting $3 million
       from the budget.

       Responding to a question from Councilmember Whitelock, Ms. Steck explained that the
       General Fund cannot be subsidized by transfers from other funds. Councilmember Jacob
       spoke of the danger of being a bedroom community with not enough sales tax revenue.
       Chair McConnehey expressed concern regarding actions that would be necessary next year
       if something is not done this year. He suggested a 12-15% increase should be considered
       for FY 2021. Councilmember Worthen agreed that a property tax increase will be
       necessary, but said she would not support one this year considering the stress citizens have
       already dealt with this year. Councilmember Jacob said he would support a 3-5% property
       tax increase this year.

       The Council thanked Ms. Steck, the Mayor, and Staff for their dedication and many hours
       spent preparing the budget.

       NEW BUSINESS AND UNFINISHED BUSINESS
       The Council reviewed new business items that will appear on the next Council agenda.

    c. MASTER TRANSPORTATION PLAN UPDATE
Nate Nelson explained proposed amendments to the City Transportation Masterplan to allow
for changes to the 6400 West and 6700 West alignments between Wells Park Road and Old
Bingham Highway. The two streets would be combined south of Wells Park Road to a new
street called “6500 West”. The proposed change to the street network would allow large
developments to progress in the area and maintain traffic connectivity in the area. The streets
that connect Wells Park Road to Old Bingham Highway in this industrial area would be
constructed by the adjacent developers according to the West Jordan City development code.
Mr. Nelson answered questions from the Council.

IX.    WORKSESSION
    a. MJ-PROJECT 1 – GENERAL PLAN LAND USE MAP AMENDMENT AND
       ZONING MAP AMENDMENT
Mr. Langford explained there were two proposed map amendments associated with the
application. The applicant was proposing to amend the future land use map on approximately
9 acres from Parks and Open Land to Light Industrial on property located at approximately
6835 West New Bingham Highway. This amendment would remove the parks and open land
map designation from a no-name wash area. The property in this wash area was farmed for
decades and appears to be more of a low spot in the farm land rather than a defined wash.
However, it is still a drainage area and would have to be appropriately dealt with during site
plan approval. The applicant also requested to Rezone approximately 60 acres of property
from A-20 agricultural zone to M-1 manufacturing zone.

The subject property’s surrounding zoning and land uses were as follows:

                Future Land Use                  Zoning      Existing Land Use
 North          Medium Density Residential       A-20        Agriculture
                and Community Commercial
 South          Light Industrial                 M-1         Warehouse under construction
 West           Light Industrial                 A-20        Agricultural
 East           Light Industrial                 A-20        Agricultural

          Findings of Fact
  A.      The proposed amendment conforms to and is consistent with the adopted goals,
          objectives, and policies set forth in the City General Plan.
  B.      The development pattern contained on the land use plan inadequately provides the
          appropriate optional sites for the use and/or change proposed in the amendment.
  C.      The proposed amendment would be compatible with other land uses, existing or
          planned, in the vicinity.
  D.      The proposed amendment constitutes an overall improvement to the adopted general
          land use map and is not solely for the good or benefit of a particular person or entity.

  F.      The proposed amendment is consistent with other adopted plans, codes and

        Section 13-7D-7(A): Amendments to the Zoning Map
Prior to recommending approval of the proposed amendment to the Zoning Map, the Planning
Commission made the following findings:
    1. The proposed amendment is consistent with the purposes, goals, objectives, and
        policies of the City’s General Plan.
    2. The proposed amendment would result in compatible land use relationships and would
        not adversely affect adjacent properties.
    3. The proposed amendment would further the public health, safety and general welfare

    4. The proposed amendment would not unduly impact the adequacy of public services
