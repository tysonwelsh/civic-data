Logan Municipal Council                   Logan, Utah                         May 13, 2025

Minutes of the meeting of the Logan Municipal Council convened in a budget workshop
Tuesday, May 13, 2025, in the Logan Municipal Council Chambers located at 290 North
100 West, Logan, Utah 84321 at 5:30 pm. Logan Municipal Council Meetings are
televised live as a public service on Channel 17 and the City of Logan YouTube channel
at: https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCFLPAOK5eawKS_RDBU0stRQ

Council Members present at the beginning of the meeting: Chair Jeannie F. Simmonds,
Vice Chair Mike Johnson, Councilmember Amy Z. Anderson, Councilmember Mark
Anderson and Councilmember Ernesto López.. Administration present: Mayor Holly H.
Daines, Finance Director Richard Anderson and City Recorder Teresa Harris.

Chair Simmonds welcomed those present. There were approximately 6 in attendance at
the beginning of the meeting.
Overview of General Budgetary Issues – Richard Anderson, Finance Director (5:55)
Finance Director Richard Anderson addressed the Council, the projections for fiscal year
2026 are for sales tax to remain relatively flat. There is a proposed property rate increase
of 3%. In the Parks & Recreation Department, there is an additional full-time position
moving from the park & rec fund to the general fund. There are two full-time positions
added to EMS, due to the increase in calls for service. The enterprise fund does depend
on charges for services collected.
Insurance costs only went up 9%, which is a welcome change compared to 29 %, 15 %,
and 7 % in the three previous fiscal years. There is a 4 % Pay for Performance increase
with the focus going to the lower-paid employees. Employees will be rewarded for their
performance, but there is a target amount for all employees.
A CIP (Capital Improvement Program) scorecard can be used to track spending in major
funds. There is a targeted amount for fund sustainability. There is no exact target,
however, we track how much we are putting toward improving services. We also
compare where we are in a relationship and where we have been. There are several years
to compare, starting from 2013. There has been significant and sizeable progress in many
funds. In the funds, we are not making progress, either new funds or services have
changed, such as in the electrical or environmental departments. It also has to do with
how capital is categorized vs. maintenance. Significant amounts are being put toward
maintenance for electric and environmental.
The fund reserve policy and which was adopted by the Council the "fund target policy" in
order to operate efficiently. Currently, we are meeting all target levels for reserves. We
are also putting away capital for future capital projects. However, currently, there are not
sufficient funds for a future capital funds project. One of them is a public works building,
which we are working toward.

Chair Simmonds asked if the sales tax issue is related to increased commercial shopping
availability.
Mr. Anderson responded that it may be part of it, but it is also the end result of COVID.
The government pumped money into the economy, and sales taxes went up. Most of the
funds have worked their way out. The hope is rather than a recession, the economy will
remain flat as it has been. However, the tariffs will play a role in the economy.
Chair Simmonds asked if the City is still subsidizing the EMS enterprise fund.
Mr. Anderson answered that the EMS enterprise fund is subsidized partially by the City
in terms of use of the building, but operation costs are wholly covered by the enterprise
fund.
Councilmember A. Anderson requested confirmation that insurance is fully funded.
Mr. Anderson confirmed that insurance is fully funded. The insurance is bid out every
year and is reviewed each year.
Councilmember M. Anderson inquired what the focus should be for the target reserves.
Mr. Anderson replied that the electric fund needs the reserves.
Mayor Daines added that the electric funds were full, but funds have gone down due to
ongoing capital projects.
Vice Chair Johnson asked if the electric fund was the only concern.
Mr. Anderson clarified that the sewer treatment 9-20 fund is the next concern, followed
by environmental.
Chair Simmonds inquired if the 4th bio-reactor was included as part of the budget or is
separate.
Mr. Anderson responded that the estimate is high for the 4th bio-reactor, and currently,
there is not sufficient funding for completion. The concern is whether to borrow from a
fund or a bond to cover the cost.
Councilmember A. Anderson asked how much the library still needs to pay back.
Mr. Anderson answered that the library owes $1.8 million.
Fraud Risk Assessment – Richard Anderson (17:25)
Mr. Anderson gave a report on the Fraud Risk Assessment. The total score of the City is
355 out of 395. This puts the city in the high-low or very-low category for fraud risk. The
City focused on creating a policy that was required by the State. The assessment could be
more tailored, but overall, it is still a good fraud risk assessment.

Consideration of a proposed resolution approving Member Contributions to the Utah
Retirement Systems Contributory Retirement Plan FY 2025-2026 – Resolution 25-22
– Ambrie Darley, Human Resource Director (24:29)

Human Resource Director, Ambrie Darley addressed the Council regarding the proposed
resolution. He stated that the City participates in the Utah Retirement Systems for all
eligible employees. The City contributes to the Utah Retirement Contributory Retirement
Plan as allowed. The City formally agrees to fund (“pick-up”) 100% of the required
employee contribution for all eligible Tier 1 members who are required to contribute to
the Utah Retirement Systems Contributory Retirement Plan for the 2025-2026 Fiscal
Year (effective July 1, 2025) according to the applicable Utah Retirement Systems
Retirement Contribution Rates. Tier 2 members are responsible for the employee
contributions that are required for the 2025-2026 Fiscal Year (effective July 1, 2025).

Chair Simmonds inquired how many employees are tier-1.

Ambrie Darley replied that less than 50% of employees are tier-1. A number of tier-1
employees are retiring or have retired.

The proposed resolution will be an action item and public hearing at the June 17, 2025
Council meeting.

Consideration of a proposed ordinance adopting Salary Schedules for Logan Elected
and Executive Municipal Officers FY 2025-2026 – Ordinance 25-09 – Ambrie Darley,
Human Resource Director (26:20)

Human Resource Director, Ambrie Darley addressed the Council regarding the proposed
ordinance. He presented the following:

Utah Code Ann. § 10-3-818 provides that elected and executive municipal officers in
municipalities may only receive compensation for services, including compensation
increases, pursuant to ordinance enacted by the legislative body following a public hearing;
and, the City Council has held the necessary public hearing and has determined to adopt
the following compensation schedule for Logan City’s elected and executive municipal
officers.

SECTION 1: Elected Officer Salary Schedules Adopted. FY2025-2026 Logan City
Elected Officer Salary Schedules are hereby adopted as follows:
   A. “Elected Officers” are as defined under Utah Code Ann. §10-3-1303(5).
   B. Elected Officer wages shall be identified, reviewed, and adjusted annually in
       accordance with the same procedures applied to all other City employees in the
       budget process.

   C. Pursuant to Utah Code Ann. §10-3-818, if the City Council decides that the
      compensation or compensation schedule for Elected Officers should be adopted,
      changed, or amended, it shall set a time and place for a public hearing.
   D. The City Council shall hold a vote upon the proposed Elected Officers'
      compensation or compensation schedule and shall require a majority vote to
      establish any change in the annual compensation of Elected Officers by
      ordinance.

SECTION 2. Compensation and expense for members of the Logan City Council shall
be as follows:

   A. Each Municipal Council member shall be paid an annual salary of $20,152.43.
   B. Each Municipal Council member shall receive a $300.00 vehicle allowance per
      year for travel expenses incurred while on City business.

SECTION 3. The compensation and expense for the Mayor, who serves as the elected
chief executive officer of the City, shall be as follows:

   A. The Mayor shall be paid an annual salary of $124,693.40.
   B. The Mayor shall receive a $6,000.00 vehicle allowance per year for travel
      expenses incurred while on City business.
   C. The Mayor shall also be provided with those employee benefits generally
      provided to other full-time employees of the City.

A list of all salaries was also included in the ordinance and available on the City’s
website at loganutah.gov.

Councilmember M. Anderson asked if the City is becoming comparable to other

Vice Chair Johnson requested that a comparison be presented to the Council of Council

Chair Simmonds also requested that a percentage be presented on raises for Councils in

The proposed ordinance will be an action item and public hearing at the June 17, 2025
Council meeting.

Logan City Budget Transfers Fiscal Year 2026 – Richard Anderson (34:07)

Finance Director Richard Anderson addressed the Council regarding the proposed Budget
Transfers for Fiscal Year 2026. He referred to the following, which has been publicly
noticed. An estimated number of non-taxable properties is that 60% of the properties in
Logan are non-taxable as they belong to the County, Church, University, Federal, Parks,
Non-Profit Organizations, and so forth. Due to the high concentration of non-taxable
properties, transfers are an option for the City as the entities in question do pay utility fees.
If transfers go away, the estimate is that the public would have to pay twice for every dollar
needed. Property taxes would have to be drastically increased to cover the amount of almost
150%, which would only apply to the 45% who pay property tax, not the 60% who are
non-taxable entities.

Noted is the construction of a new Public Works Facility and the exchange that will occur
thereafter.

Chair Simmonds requested confirmation that if the City does not elect this process, the
process to bond will be an entirely different process.

Mr. Anderson confirmed that the process for a bond would be an entirely different process.
