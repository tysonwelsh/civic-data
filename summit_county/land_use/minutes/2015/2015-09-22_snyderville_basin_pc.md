Commissioner Klingenstein made a motion to forward a positive 
recommendation to the Summit County Council for the proposed 
amendments to Section 10-8-11:  Preservation of Historically Significant 
Structures in the Snyderville Basin Development Code as shown in the staff 
report dated September 22, 2015, with the follow findings of fact and 
conclusions of law contained in the staff report: 
Findings of Fact: 
1. Section 10-8-11 of the Snyderville Basin Development Code 
encourages the preservation of historic structures by allowing the use 
of the structure and/or associated property to be converted to a new 
use complying with the Low Impact Permit criteria in Chapter 3 of 
the Development Code, even though these uses are not specifically 
permitted in the applicable zone district. 
2. Section 10-1-1 D of the Snyderville Basin Development Code states 
that, “the intention of the County is to assure the managed, proper 
and sensitive development of land to protect and enhance these 
desired qualities and the lifestyle that exists.” 
3. The current language in Chapter 10-8-11 of the Snyderville Basin 
Development Code is not consistent with the stated intention of the 
Snyderville Basin Development Code Section 10-1-1 D. 
4. Policy 5.3 of the Snyderville Basin General Plan states that a 
comprehensive Heritage Amenities and Cultural Arts Plan should be 
adopted in the Basin that provides specific provisions for the type, 
amount, and manner in which heritage preservation will be 
incorporated into a development project. 
5. No comprehensive Heritage Amenities and Cultural Arts Plan has 
been adopted by Summit County.

6. The lack of regulation concerning the allowed uses associated with the 
regulations in Section 10-8-11 of the Snyderville Basin Development 
Code and the lack of guidance provided by a Heritage Amenities and 
Cultural Arts Plan constitute a substantial risk to the public’s health, 
safety, and welfare that should be addressed through an amendment 
to the Development Code. 
Conclusions of Law: 
1. The amendment is consistent with the goals, objectives, and policies of 
the General Plan. 
2. The amendment will not permit the use of land that is not consistent 
with the uses of properties nearby. 
3. The amendment will not permit suitability of the properties affected 
by the proposed amendment for the uses to which they have been 
restricted. 
4. The amendment will not permit the removal of the then existing 
restrictions which will unduly affect nearby property. 
5. The amendment will not grant special favors or circumstances solely 
for one property owner or developer. 
6. The amendment will promote the public health, safety, and welfare 
better than the existing regulations which the amendment is intended 
to change. 
The motion was seconded by Commissioner Barnes and passed unanimously, 
7 to 0.

Director Putt introduced the first discussion item regarding development pressures in the 
Snyderville Basin/Quinn’s Junction area.  He presented and discussed a current project map that 
includes the Boyer Tech Center, the Canyon Corners project, the Canyons-Park City Mountain 
Resort interconnect, the East Canyon Ranch/Woodside development, the Hyatt House Hotel, the 
Nevis Newpark condominiums, Promontory, brisk single-family home construction, and Silver 
Creek Village.  He noted that the County Council recently adopted a new Snyderville Basin 
General Plan, and they will not upzone above base density or incentive base density until they 
complete the re-write of the Development Code.  Director Erickson added that at least that much 
additional development will be coming from the Wasatch County side as well. 
 
The Planning Commissioners discussed the impacts of traffic related to these development 
pressures and how they might be addressed.  They discussed what they might be able to do to 
help each other with these issues.  Commissioner Peck asked what could be done to incentivize 
the resorts to help get their guests out of cars.  Commissioner DeFord believed that could be 
done through technology and the visitor experience.

Loomis with Mountainlands Community Housing Trust reviewed some of the affordable housing 
options and noted that Park City targets almost exclusively an 80% of AMI, which targets the 
wrong group.  Director Erickson suggested that the two Planning Commissions hold a joint work 
session to address this question to be sure one entity is not operating differently than the other

commented that they are concentrating on 1960’s technology with buses and trains, and someone

and City transportation specialists are working on forming a transportation management
