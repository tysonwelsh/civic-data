D. THOUGHT OF THE DAY - Commissioner Harvey
      Chair Jenkins mentioned a couple of items of interest to area citizens. Gondola coming into North Ogden: The
      only information that the Commission had was that just prior to this item being presented for approval at the
      committee hearing in Washington, D.C., it was pulled. Landfill at Promontory Point: This issue is officially not in
      the county’s purview; the State approves landfills, and the landfill has been approved. The county would save
      $600,000/year to send its garbage there but has a 5-year contract with Republic Services of which there is a 1½ year
      remaining, and it appears to be an enforceable contract.
E. PUBLIC COMMENTS:
   --Randy Winn, of North Ogden, feels that he speaks for a lot of area residents who are very concerned about the
   gondola, and they oppose it. He requested that the Commission pass that knowledge along.
   --Lynn Satterthwaite, of North Ogden, opposes the gondola and said that he spoke with two of the landowners,
   Mr. Hall and Mr. Barker, whose land is in unincorporated Weber County, close to the City’s green waste facility,
   and whom he feels could possibly support the gondola and are willing to sell. He feels that the gondola could be
   approved by the Forest Service and the county and asked the Commission that if there is anything to this issue that
   they look out for the impacts to the City and keep the citizens involved. After speaking with Mr. Barker,
   Commissioner Froerer believes that the specific land owned by Mr. Barker would have to be annexed into the City
   because the county would not have the ability to service that particular site without the annexation. He feels this is
   really a North Ogden/Ogden Valley issue, and from feedback he has received, it is not well received from either
   side, and it may be a far point down the road as this would need Forest Service and congressional approvals.
   --Meg Sanders, of North Ogden, said that she attended six meetings with James Coleman and Mountain Capital
   Partners, a meeting at the Governor’s Office and another in Logan, and asked what the Commission had decided
   about designated roadless rules because this was their responsibility, that Mr. Coleman was taking 3,600 acres of
   not only Forest Service land but that he wants North Ogden and Weber County land, and she wanted to know dates
   of when the item was pulled and who had informed the commissioners of the pull out. She found out the item was
   pulled from someone at the Standard Examiner. Commissioner Froerer asked if it was true that there would have
   been no reason for the county to review or have that discussion prior to the Forest Service approval because without
   their approval it was a moot issue since Forest Service approval had not occurred. Mr. Wilkinson stated that was
   correct, that any prior action would simply be speculation on the county’s part about what might happen. Ms.
   Sanders said that Randy Marriott has 200 acres that he wants to annex into North Ogden. She found out on the
   county’s website that people had to submit a letter of petition by June 12 and asked if people had not been aware of
   this would they not have a say with the Commission. Commissioner Froerer said that the annexation would have
   gone through North Ogden not the county and Mr. Wilkinson said that was correct. Chair Jenkins said that 4-5
   options relating to the roadless rules issue had been looked at and Mr. Wilkinson added that when Sean Harwood,
   Uinta-Wasatch-Cache National Forest District Ranger, had presented he had stated that it was out of the county’s
   hands. The State will ultimately make the decision, regardless of the county’s recommendation.
   --Julie Sanders, of North Ogden, referred to a previous Commission meeting on water issues. She owns land in
   Western Weber and referred to development there, expressing concerns about water because she said that Utah is
   the second driest state. She said that the Commission will have to consider water shortages and algae blooms.
   Additionally, the road to the Promontory Point landfill runs very close to her property and she is concerned with it
   becoming a path of garbage with the wind blowing, etc., and that government might not take care of the garbage.

H. COMMISSIONER COMMENTS: Commissioner Froerer invited Weber County Sheriff Ryan Arbon to present on the
   VIPS (Volunteer in Police Service) program that he instituted. He said that these volunteers help in the Sheriff’s
   Office in the day-to-day duties and save taxpayers a substantial amount of money. Sheriff Arbon enacted this
   successful program that is designed to supplement law enforcement activities and helps build a better relationship
   with the community. He gave a great example of a volunteer mother and daughter that patrolled from 9 p.m.-6 a.m.
   and helped Ogden City with traffic control. Commissioner Harvey referred to the “Extreme Makeover: Home
   Edition” of two homes that just occurred in Ogden stating that the reception with the Ogden Convention Bureau
   was very warm towards the TV producers and Wadman Corporation stepped up and said that they would build the
   houses without pay. He said that Wadman was extremely conscientious on the project. One of the houses was a
   cement slab on Friday and finished the following Thursday). Chair Jenkins spoke about Commissioner Harvey’s
   attention to detail. He is a subcontractor who pointed out a missing outlet and it was then handled within a short
   time. Chair Jenkins told those present that they are grateful to be their commissioners. He asked county staff to
   stand and introduce themselves, and then invited the public to address any additional comments and concerns with
   any of the staff.
