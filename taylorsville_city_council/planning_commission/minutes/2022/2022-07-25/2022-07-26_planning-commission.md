3.1      This item was presented by Karyn Kerdolff.             The applicant is requesting a Non-Administrative

4.6      DISCUSSION:
         4.6.1     Commissioner Quigley            said in looking at the image, the 457 required parking stalls seems
                   very generous. That parking presently in Sorenson Research Park, which has true office buildings,
                   is under-utilized. Looking at this scenario, he wondered if there was any flexibility allowed to
                   eliminate a few of the parking stalls. Background shows they are anticipating 304 employees,
                   which would leave room for 150 visitors. His question was if the applicant anticipated having that
                   many visitors and 304 employees on the site. He was aware that there have been several
                   discussions on the Planning Commissions that in the past they have over-parked sites, which seems
                   to be going away. If some parking spaces could be eliminated here, they may be able to eliminate
                   some of the encroachment within the 100’ distance on the trail, particularly on the east end. There
                   are 25 stalls right at the top of this site that could be eliminated if the parking requirement could
                   be reduced somewhat.             Mr. Spung answered that under the table of parking requirements,
                   office space requires five spaces and warehouse space requires one space per one thousand
                   square feet of building area. So, the parking provided on site actually is at the minimum
                   requirement. There is some language in the Code that does allow the Planning Commission to
                   reduce that number based on certain things. One of which is if the applicant were to demonstrate
                   that alternative modes of access could be provided. Because this project is along a major bike and
                   pedestrian corridor, they could justify reduction of parking because of its proximity to that major
                   bike-way arterial in the valley. He was not sure however, what the percentage number would be
                   without analysis/study. In other cities across the nation, proximity to a transit stop, may allow
                   vehicle parking reductions of up to 10%. Where this would be strictly bike/pedestrian and given
                   the land use, he was not sure what an appropriate reduction would be, but the applicant can
                   certainly address this possibility. One of the items Staff mentioned was distribution of parking on
                   the site. While it does make sense to eliminate as much parking here as possible to preserve the
                   area, it would impact the number of parking stalls available for those tenants on the east side of
                   the project. Staff’s opinion, without re-working the whole site, is that it is probably not attainable
                   at this point. Perhaps parking is an area that could accommodate a little bit of flexibility.
                   Commissioner Quigley ‘s thought was that with the proximity to Mason’s Landing Apartment
                   Complex, there is the likelihood of some of those employees living there and walking to work on
                   this site. He still thought that reducing the parking requirement would alleviate some of the
                   problem and would like that looked at. Mr. Spung said that can be addressed with the applicant.
                        A lot of projects that are speculative in nature, tenants are looking for a specific number of
                   parking stalls. Generally, it is good to defer to the market as to what parking demands would be,
                   but the Planning Commission would have the authority to adjust that slightly if necessary, based
                   on the Code.
