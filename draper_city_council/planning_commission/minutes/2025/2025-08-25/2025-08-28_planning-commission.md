       F.      Public Hearing: Goodrich Pointe Land Use Map and Zoning Map Amendments.
               (Legislative Item)
               On the request of Alan Prince, representing the Crescent Irrevocable Trust, a Land Use
               Amendment from Residential Low/Medium Density to Residential Medium Density
               and a Zoning Map Amendment from RA-1 (Residential Agricultural) to R4 (Single-
               Family Residential) for approximately 8.7 acres, located at approximately 11775
               South 700 West. Known as Applications 205-0109-MA and 2025-0105-MA. Staff
               Contact: Maryann Pickering, (801) 576-6391, maryann.pickering@draperutah.gov.

Ms. Pickering presented the Staff Report and indicated that the subject property is on the western
edge of Draper and has direct frontage on 700 West. The Aerial Map was reviewed, indicating two
current lots on the property, one of which has been developed. The applicant requested to change the
Land Use designation from Residential Low/Medium Density to Residential Medium Density and the
zone from RA1, which has a one-acre minimum lot size, to R4, which has a one-quarter-acre
minimum lot size. A future subdivision is planned for the property, and that subdivision plat would
be submitted for review at a later date. Ms. Pickering noted that the Staff received two written
objections to the request that were emailed to the Planning Commission.

The applicant, Alan Prince of MP&G, LLC, stated that they have been working on the project for
over three years. He presented a preliminary site plan and reported that the property is a consolidation
Draper City Planning Commission Meeting
August 28, 2025
Page 6

of three separate parcels totaling approximately 8.7 acres. Future Lot 2 contains an old rental home
that faces away from the road and typically houses several families at once. He believed the
neighboring property owners would be pleased when the structure is removed.

Mr. Prince indicated that they requested the higher-density zoning to match the property immediately
to the south. To accomplish that goal, they needed a zone that could accommodate the frontages,
setbacks, and lot sizes the irregularly shaped property could support. The project would include two
phases. Rebecca Goodrich Watson will continue to live in the existing home along the creek; her
property will be subdivided, and her daughter will have a home on the new lot. Including those lots,
there would be a total of 14 lots. The current RA1 zoning allows two units per acre, and the total
achievable density for the property is 16 lots. He noted that if Ms. Watson were to sell her property
in the future, it is possible that two additional lots would be created.

Regarding a concern expressed in a written objection, Mr. Prince clarified that no homes would have
frontage on 700 West, and a masonry wall would be constructed on that frontage. They intend to
dedicate approximately one-half acre along the creek for the trail and maintenance, and two 25,000-
square-foot lots will face the creek.

Chair Adams opened the public hearing.

Rebecca Goodrich Watson stated that she lives on the subject property and will retain a large parcel.
Her parents purchased the property in 1968, and she returned to the family home in 2018 to care for
her mother and brother. She is selling five acres to Mr. Prince. There will be two accesses to 700
West. The sale will benefit her and her family and create a great new addition to the community. She
respectfully asked that the project be approved.

Jan Sharp stated that she lives on 700 West and is part of the West Crescent Irrigation Company. She
did not object to the request but wanted to ensure that the developer was aware that the irrigation
ditch running through the property needed to be protected.

Greg Wilding stated that his company is working with Mr. Prince on the development. They have
met onsite with the irrigation company and already have plans in place for maintaining service
throughout the property. All downstream users will continue to have access.

Michael Bautner stated that he works at the butterfly farm across the street from the proposed
development. He was not opposed to the development but requested that the City and property owners
save and protect a small portion of land for wildlife, especially along the creek and canal. They often
use the milkweed on the property to feed monarch butterflies that are raised on the farm. Milkweed
is the only plant that monarch butterfly caterpillars can eat. It was once abundant across Utah, but
due to habitat loss, monarch butterflies are on the verge of being declared endangered. The patch of
milkweed on the land is likely one of the largest remaining milkweed sanctuaries in Salt Lake County,
and wild monarch eggs have been found under the leaves. Many other types of butterflies, bees, and
other pollinating insects, as well as birds, also forage and nest on the property. Wildlife will suffer if
the land is turned into suburban homes with no consideration for them. He requested that a small
section of the land along the canal be protected.

County Flood Control, and Draper City all have claims there. They will work with the Staff to

       I.      Public Hearing: City-Initiated Preschool, Limited Text Amendment Request.
               (Legislative Action)
               On the request of Draper City for a Text Amendment to Draper City Municipal Code
               Section 9-3-040 in order to adjust the number of children allowed in a Preschool,
               Limited Use. Application Number 2025-0189-TA. Staff Report by Jennifer
               Jastremsky, 801-576-6328, jennifer.jastremsky@draperutah.gov.

Ms. Jastremsky presented the Staff Report and indicated that the Text Amendment would change the
definition of Preschool, Limited Use to increase the number of children allowed. The use is a home-
based business that is allowed, subject to a CUP. The current code allows up to 10 children per
session, with two sessions per day, as well as one non-resident employee. However, the Utah
Department of Health and Human Services allows up to eight children with one caregiver and 16 with
two caregivers. The amendment would allow up to 16 children to bring DCMC into compliance with
State rules.

In response to a question, Ms. Jastremsky reported that the preschool’s drop-off and pick-up plan,
parking plan, and other relevant details would be reviewed as part of the CUP application. The State
requires a certain amount of space per child.

Commissioner Nixon stated that 16 children is a large number, but she understood that it could be
frustrating for a preschool owner to be told by the State that their license allows 16 children, but the
City only allows 10. She was pleased that the City regulations would be brought into alignment with
the State.
Draper City Planning Commission Meeting
August 28, 2025
Page 14


Chair Adams opened the public hearing.

Andrew Bates stated that his daughters attend the preschool that requested the change, and he was
present to express his support. He believes the conversation should be around how to encourage more
home-based preschools because they are great community-building opportunities and are usually
woman-owned businesses.

Angie Willey stated that her son attends the same preschool, and her two-year-old daughter is on the
waitlist. The preschool owner, Jennifer O’Neal, has a five-year waiting list because she has proven
to be a great teacher.

Joycelyn Glover stated that she lives in Sandy and two of her grandchildren attend Ms. O’Neal’s
preschool. One of her daughters drives from Woods Cross to bring her child to preschool, which
speaks to the amount of care and love the children receive. It is important to keep City regulations in
line with the State to avoid confusion for small business owners.

Jennifer O’Neal stated that she and her partner operate a preschool. She thanked the Staff for their
assistance with the amendment and noted that preschools are licensed as schools, not daycares.

There were no further public comments. The public hearing was closed.

City Planner Todd Taylor introduced Marianne Stuck of Design Workshop, who presented the final
Draper Station Area Plan (“SAP”). Per House Bill 462, cities are required to develop an SAP for a
one-half-mile radius around public transit stations. The Draper SAPs will guide future development
to ensure that it conforms with the City and community’s vision for the area.

SAPs were developed for the Vista, Kimballs Lane, and Crescent View stations. Each plan’s overall
goals were:

   •   Create a vision for the two TRAX Station Areas and one FrontRunner Station Area.
   •   Increase the availability and affordability of housing.
   •   Promote sustainable environmental conditions.
   •   Preserve the character of surrounding neighborhoods, especially in the Kimballs Lane and
       Crescent View areas.
   •   Increase transportation choices.

   •   Enhance access to opportunities.
   •   Involve key stakeholders, including the Utah Transit Authority ("UTA"), Utah Department of
       Transportation ("UDOT"), Metropolitan Planning Organization (“MPO”), property owners,
       business owners, and residents.

The plan process began in February 2025 and is scheduled for adoption by the City Council on
September 2, 2025. Public engagement in the process included small group meetings in March 2025
and a design charrette in April 2025. Two alternative plans were developed for each station after the
design charette was presented at an open house in June 2025 and through an online survey. The final
SAPs were then created. Ms. Stuck then reviewed the plans.

The Vista FrontRunner SAP focused on the following principles:

   •   Activate the public realm.
          o The community indicated that the area feels disconnected. It includes both established
              and new neighborhoods and an employment center, but lacks places where people can
              gather and connect socially.

   •   Build a new east-west pedestrian connection.
          o Many comments focused on how disconnected the two sides of the rail line were, and
              how hard it is for people working in the employment center to the east to get to the
              station platform.

   •   Incorporate small-scale retail.
          o A series of Master Area Plans have been approved for the area. The SAP considered
              options for including small-scale retail, such as small corner stores, services, and
              restaurants to provide everyday amenities for residents and visitors, including
              residents of the over 2,500 vested units contained in the Master Area Plans.

   •   Create a green space network along Vista Boulevard.
          o This would make Vista Boulevard more pedestrian-friendly, perhaps including a linear
              park on the east side and a series of smaller pocket parks and community spaces.

   •   Integrate adjacent uses to the station area.
           o Connections could be created to employment centers, neighborhoods, the Jordan
               River, and the trail system, and bring cohesion to the area and enhance connectivity.
