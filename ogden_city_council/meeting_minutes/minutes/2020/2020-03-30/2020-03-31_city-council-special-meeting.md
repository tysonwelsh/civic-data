         A memo from Council staff cmne before the Council to consider a resolution regarding electronic meetings procedures.
The memo stated the Council will consider adopting a resolution establishing meeting fonnat and public input procedures for
electronic meetings. The City Attorney and Council staff recommend adoption of a resolution the outlines rules and procedures for
conducting electronic meetings and taking public comment.
         Council Executive Director Eller-Smith stated Utah's Open and Public Meetings Act, Utah Code§ 52-4-101 et seq.
(OPMA) provides that public bodies may hold meetings electronically if the public body has adopted a resolution authorizing
electronic meetings, if certain noticing requirements are met, and provisions are made to allow the public to monitor and
participate in the proceedings of the meeting from an anchor location. These public monitoring and participation requirements of
OPMA tend to promote the gathering of members of the public and members of a public body in a single location. Under the
current state of emergency due to novel coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) federal, state, and local authorities have
recommended that individuals limit public gatherings and that individuals experiencing symptoms of COVID-19 self-isolate to
prevent and control the continuing spread of the disease. Out of concern for the health of public officials and members of the
public, on March 18, 2020 Governor Gary R. Herbert issued Executive Order 2020-5 (Order), suspending the enforcement of
certain provisions of Utah Code§ 52-4-101 et seq. The Order allows any public body governed by OPMA to hold an electronic
meeting regardless of whether the public body has adopted a resolution, rule, or ordinance governing the use of electronic
meetings until such time as a resolution, rule, or ordinance can reasonably be adopted. Furthermore, a public body that convenes
or conducts an electronic meeting is not required to:
               1. Post written notice at the principal office of the public body or at the building where the meeting is to be held;
               2. Post written notice at an anchor location;
               3. Establish one or more anchor locations for the public meeting;
               4. Provide space and facilities at an anchor location to allow the public to physically attend and monitor the open
                    portions of the meeting; or
               5. If comments from the public will be accepted during the electronic meeting, provide space and facilities at an
                    anchor location where the public may physically attend, monitor, and participate in the open portions of the
                    meeting.

             If electronic meetings are held, the public body must provide for the following:
                  1. Provide a means by which interested persons and the public may remotely hear or observe, live, by audio or
                        video transmission the open portions of the meeting;
                  2. If comments from the public will be accepted during the electronic meeting, provide a means by which
                        interested persons and the public participating remotely may ask questions and make comments by electronic
                       means in the open portions of the meeting; and
                  3. If the public body has not adopted a resolution, rule, or ordinance governing the use of electronic meetings,
                        adopt as soon as practicable a resolution, rnle, or ordinance.
              The Council's adopted Rules and Norms do provide for electronic meetings and therefore meet the requirement for
    having adopted "a resolution, rule or ordinance governing the use of electronic meetings." However, the Rules and Norms do not
    specify in detail how such meetings will be conducted or how the public may monitor and participate. Ms. Eller-Smith concluded
    by providing a brief overview of the procedures outlined for conducting electronic meetings.
              Chair Choberka thanked Ms. Eller-Smith and members of Council staff for reacting so quickly to the evolving pandemic
    situation and preparing processes that will allow for the City Council to continue to conduct its business.
              Council member White stressed that people do not need a computer or internet connection to participate in a Zoom
    meeting; individuals can use any phone to call in to the meeting and listen to the business being conducted.
              Chair Choberka introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2020-3, entitled:
                        "A resolution of the Ogden City Council providing that the Council may convene and conduct electronic
                       meetings in accordance with the Utah Public Meetings Act(§ 52-4-207) and Executive Order 2020-5 issued by
                        Governor Gary R. Herbert, temporarily suspending Council Rules and Norms relating to City Council meetings
                        and establishing Rules, Nonns, and Procedures for electronic meetings."

Joint Proclamation declaring April 1, 2020 as Census Day to encourage
Ogden residents to participate in the 2020 Census
         A joint proclamation declaring April 1, 2020 as Census Day came before the Council for consideration. Council member
Hyer read the joint proclamation, after which COUNCIL MEMBER HYER MOVED TO APPROVE THE JOINT
PROCLAMATION. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER WHITE, ALL VOTING AYE.
         Chair Choberka then noted the framed proclamation will be sent to the Complete Count Committee, which is comprised
of representatives of several community partners. She invited Diversity Affairs Officer Felix and Treasurer Johnson to address the
Council. Ms. Felix stated that she has been monitoring the community's Census response and it appears to be increasing by two
percent per day, for a total of 36 percent thus far. She thanked the Council for adopting the joint proclamation encouraging
participation and she provided the phone number and website that residents can access to participate. Ms. Johnson echoed Ms.
Felix's gratitude to the Council and Mayor for their support of the work that has been done by several community partners for the
past 18 months to encourage participation in the Census.
