Proposed Zone Text Amendments to Multi-family Residential Design
Standards in Residential Zones

Planning Manager Brierley explained Ogden City Planning Division is proposing design standard changes for new multi-
family residential development in residential zones. In 2020, the Planning Division conducted an extensive study researching
design standards for multi-family residential development in commercial zones and significant changes were made as a result. The
2020 changes were specific to multi-family design standards in commercial zones and did not apply to residential zones. As a
follow-up to those changes, the current proposal is to update the design standards for multi-family residential development in
residential zones. An initial work session was held on October 11, 2022 to review the proposed changes. At that meeting, Council
members requested additional information on options for electric vehicle (EV) parking standards. Mr. Brierley then used the aid of
a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the benefits of EVs; the increase in the global electric car stock from 2010-2021; and the
availability of infrastructure needed to support EVs. He summarized current EV charging requirements for the multi-family
developments in the C-1, C-2, and C-3 zones, group dwellings, and in the Nine Rails Creative District zone. EV chargers are
required for any development size over 17 units. The MakeOgden Plan calls for one EV charging station for every 50 parking
spaces for all uses in downtown Ogden. Requiring EV charging for apartments with 17 plus units would be consistent with other
zoning requirements and help to meet future EV needs. A garage with a 120-volt outlet is sufficient, though 240 volts would be
better.

Discussion among the Council and Mr. Brierley centered on the specifications for EV charging stations and the
availability of electricity infrastructure to serve an increase in charging stations; uniformity of vehicle charging adapters; the need
to receive input from the Sustainability Commission on the matter; the current use of existing EV charging stations in the

ConPlan and the Annual Action Plan. She then provided an overview of the CAPER, focusing briefly on available funding;

Proposed Fiscal Year 2022-2023 Annual Action Plan Amendment #1

Community Development Project Coordinator Fuentes explained that as a requirement to receive federal funding from
the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD), the City’s Community and Economic Development Department
prepares and submits to the Council what is known as a five-year Consolidated Plan (ConPlan) and an Annual Action Plan. The
Annual Action Plan (AAP) is used to outline how federal Community Development Block Grant funds, HOME funds and other
federal and non-federal program funds will be used by the City in the upcoming fiscal year. When the Annual Action Plan is
submitted to HUD each year, specific dollar amounts for program income funds and carry-over funds are estimated for the
remainder of the fiscal year. Part way through the fiscal year, after the specific dollar amounts are known or when program
income from loans and other programs are received, an amendment must be made to the plan to update the Annual Action Plan
budget. Often when the mid-year amendment is proposed, changes in funding levels within certain programs and line items are
proposed as well. The amendments proposed with this mid-year update include the program income amounts as well as the carry-
over amounts for the current fiscal year. The update also increases the budget for the Own in Ogden program from $250,000 to
$500,000, the Small Business Loan program and the Quality Neighborhoods program. She briefly expounded on the AAP goal
changes that align with the proposed funding/budget amendments. She concluded the public comment period for the proposed
amendment ends on December 5 and staff recommends scheduling a public hearing for December 6, 2022.

There was brief discussion among the Council and Community Development staff regarding the lending terms for the
small business loan program; Ms. Fuentes indicated the small business loan program should be considered to be complimentary,
rather than competitive with, typical SBA lending options.

Proposed Purchase and Sale Agreement with Union Pacific Railroad for
Purchase of Union Station Property
Community Development Deputy Manager Smith used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the proposal

to enter into a purchase and sales agreement with Union Pacific for the Union Station property; the proposal is to purchase 806
acres of land at a price of $5.5 million in order to accomplish the following:

1. Preserve ownership/control of Union Station

2. Enhance Union Station as a cultural anchor of the community.

3. Facilitate the creation of targeted jobs.

4. Create a unique and vibrant downtown experience.

Mr. Smith discussed the history of the Union Station land dating back to 1976 when the building was conveyed to Ogden

City viaa ground lease with Union Pacific. In 1989, Ogden City purchased the Shupe Williams Building and Property, which was
later destroyed by a fire in 2006 and the property was sold to the Union Station Foundation in 2007. The Union Station
Foundation was created in 2004 and began managing the facility at that time. In 2017, the City started negotiating with Union
Pacific for the purchase of the land and in 2018, Union Station Foundation’s management of the facility ceased, and that
responsibility returned to the City. The City and the Union Station Foundation jointly commissioned the Union Station Project
Charter that same year. In 2020, Ogden City adopted the MAKE Ogden Downtown Master Plan and Ogden OnBoard Plan and in
2021, planning efforts for the Union Station campus commenced. He provided an overview of pre-development activities for the
facility, including architectural studies focusing on the museum needs and the MAKE Ogden Downtown Master Plan; he
presented images from the MAKE Ogden Plan that are conceptual in nature and provide ideas for how the west side of the Union
Station site could look upon revitalization and redevelopment of the area, with preservation of the historical elements of the Union
Station and museums as an anchor. Additionally, the Ogden OnBoard Plan includes conceptual planning elements for connection
to the Union Station site from 21% to 27" Streets. Finally, the Union Station Framework Plan was developed in 2022 in
conjunction with the Union Station Foundation and Utah Transit Authority (UTA); critical success factors of the Plan include
creating an economic engine, building a dynamic neighborhood, honoring local history and culture, and leaving a legacy. Four
dilemmas were identified in the process to develop the Plan: Wall Avenue improvements, vacant and underutilized parcels,
underutilized transit station, and use restrictions. Those performing the study to inform the Plan examined other railyards that have
been revitalized and made part of significant redevelopment projects, including Santa Fe, New Mexico; Haymarket in Lincoln,
Nebraska; The Boise Depot in Boise, Idaho, and The Gulch in Nashville, Tennessee. He then noted that tonight, the Agency is ata
critical juncture in terms of acquisition of the Union Station property; once that has been finalized, all parties can move forward to
engage in strategic visioning and designing the Union Station Campus with economic sustainability, education, history and culture
preservation, and transportation as guiding elements.

Community and Economic Development Director Cooper then noted that in 2015 and 2016, additional studies were
commissioned by the Union Station Foundation relating to the master plan and programming of the facility and strategic planning
from 2016 to 2021. He stated throughout all the studies, there is a recurring theme relating to the need to preserve the history of

of the facility transitioned from the Union Station Foundation to the City in 2018 and he highlighted three important points from

Fact finding on the Community Renewable Energy Program (CREP)

In preparation for upcoming decisions related to the Community Renewable Energy Program (CREP), the Council will
receive a review of the Program and the City’s involvement to date, information on technical details of the Program, and the
City’s current sustainability efforts.

Council Executive Director Eller-Smith reviewed key dates in the CREP process, past actions taken by the City relative
to the CREP, upcoming decisions to be made, and costs associated with the CREP to date and in the future. At present there are 81
participating in communities and each community is represented as part of the Community Renewable Emergency Agency
(CREA). Additionally, Rocky Mountain Power (RMP), the Office of Consumer Services, and the Division of Public Utilities will
be involved in administering the program. The governance agreement between the City and the Agency was executed in July of
2021; from that point the Agency began working to create a utility agreement, low-income plan, and model ordinance for the City
to consider. The model ordinance is the document the Council would need to adopt if the decision is made to stay in the program.
To date, the City has paid $72,000 to participate in the program; if the model ordinance is adopted, the City will begin providing
the required notice to all utility customers at an estimated cost of $30,000.

Mr. Cain addressed the question regarding bundled RECs; this is a market force used to calculate or assign a value to an

the market value of it and sell it instead to someone else. The REC can then be retired or expired and green value can be claimed.
RECs can be bundled together and sold to produce a renewable source.

Kim Buttschardt thanked Mr. Lowder for his presentation and stated it is important to recognize all of the great things
that have been done in terms of sustainability efforts. She stated she is the owner of one of the first businesses that signed on to
participate in the CREP; the future of the environment is a real concern for her children and their friends, and she thanked the
Council for their careful consideration of the matter.

Mark Stevenson agreed with Mr. Murray and many of the claims he made but indicated that he supports the City
remaining involved in the CREP because this is an opportunity to make a decision that result in future improvements in terms of
carbon emission production. He stated the program is complicated and there is a great deal of uncertainty, but a majority of the
City’s residents responded to a survey and indicated they want to be part of the program and are willing to pay for it.

Heath Satow also expressed his support for remaining in the CREP; Ogden has done some great things in terms of
sustainability and needs to continue to lead by example.

Jenny Gnagey stated that she was one of the four researchers that participated in development of the public survey
regarding the CREP; 68.5 percent of Ogden residents are willing to pay some amount for renewable energy. The median amount
they were willing to pay was 9.3 above current energy costs. The average household pays $80 per month, meaning that at least
half of residents are willing to pay approximately $7 more per month for energy.

There were no additional persons appearing to be heard.

Several Council members thanked everyone who was involved in gathering and presenting information tonight; Chair
Nadolski thanked the residents for their graceful participation in tonight’s discussion, even though this can be an emotional issue.
