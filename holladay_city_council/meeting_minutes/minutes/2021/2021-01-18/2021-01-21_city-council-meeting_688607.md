IV.    Public Hearing on Proposed Rezone for Property Located at 4545, 4549, and 4551 South
       Highland Drive from R-2-10 Zone to RM.
Senior Planning Advisor, Paul Allred informed the Council Members that the Planning Commission
voted 5-to-2 in favor of the rezone. There had been a lot of public comment both for and against the
rezone during the Planning Commission public hearing.

Mr. Allred reviewed the staff report. One of the items stated that the proposed rezone met the
recommended zoning for the location in the General Plan. Additionally, the rezone did not conflict
with the goals of the General Plan and supported the overall goal to create additional housing within
the community.

Council Member Petersen stated that the property is 0.95 acres in size and zoned R-2-10. She
wondered what the difference was between the current zone and the proposed zone. Mr. Allred
explained that the R-2-10 Zone allows eight units per acre. Since there was not a full acre of land, it
would allow for approximately seven units. The RM Zone allows for 16 units per acre. This would
equal approximately 14 units on 0.95 acres. Mr. Allred added that in the RM Zone, there is a 40-foot
allowance over one acre. Since there was not a full acre of land, the development would be limited

Council Member Durham asked about the heights of the current homes on the property. Mr. Allred

V.      City Manager Report – Manager Chamness.
Ms. Chamness reported that the Legislative Session began on Tuesday, January 19, 2021. For the
last several years, the City of Holladay benefitted from having a member of the Council on the
Executive Board of the Utah League of Cities and Towns. Changes were made to the composition of
the Board last fall and the City of Holladay was no longer represented.

Ms. Chamness reported that there was a referendum bill that staff would continue to follow as well
as a bill related to animal services that would move the function from municipal to county-wide. Staff
would also follow a bill that could change the amount allowed to be in the Fund Balance reserve. The
latter would increase the Fund Balance reserve from 25% to 35%. Council Member Petersen
wondered what the benefit would be of increasing the Fund Balance. Ms. Chamness explained that
it would provide additional insurance in the event of an unexpected event, such as an earthquake.
Council Member Durham asked about the billboard legislation. Ms. Chamness reported that it had
been the subject of conversation but there had not been any movement. Community opposition to
electronic billboards had been expressed.

        a.      Review of Emergency Operations Plan – Julie Harvey.
Ms. Harvey reported that she serves as the Municipal Emergency Management Planner for Holladay,
Midvale, and parts of the MSD. The Emergency Operations Plan outlined actions to be taken by the
City of Holladay in cooperation with other agencies (Salt Lake County, the State of Utah, and Federal
agencies) and private organizations to respond to emergencies or disasters. Ms. Harvey explained
that the plan consists of the following three parts:
    • Part I: Basic Plan;
    • Part II: Functional Annexes; and
    • Part III: Hazard Annexes.

The Basic Plan provided an overview of the City’s approach to emergency operations. It identified
emergency response policies, described the response organization, and assigned tasks within the City
of Holladay. The Functional Annexes outlined and highlighted the key functional area operations,
such as mass care and damage assessment. It provided general oversight and guidance as to how the
City would respond and take care of those functions. The Hazard Annexes outlined the primary
hazards that face the City, such as severe weather, earthquakes, and wildfires. It provided information
on which Functional Annexes may be critical in the response. Ms. Harvey shared an overview of the
Emergency Operations Plan Contents related to Part I, Part II, and Part III.

Ms. Harvey requested that the City Council review the plan within the next two weeks so it could be
adopted by resolution at the next City Council Meeting. Council Member Durham asked if the
community had been involved in the development of the plan. She noted that the Emergency
Operations Plan could be shared on the City website to obtain public comments.

               • Proposed Rezone.
Council Member Durham wondered if traffic studies had been conducted. He commented that many
residents had questions related to the egress and ingress on Highland Drive. Mr. Allred reported that
the ordinance requires a traffic study for any project that proposed enough units that 100 trips would
be generated during the peak hour. The project was well below that threshold. Council Members
Petersen and Durham were interested in what Dr. Perrin would have to say. Council Member
Fotheringham noted that it is a residential proposal and would not create the same number of trips as
a commercial proposal. He felt it would be fairly mild in terms of the intensity of use.

Council Member Petersen believed that a lot of the trees are cottonwoods and noted that there could

traffic volume. Since there is not a site plan showing where the driveways would actually be, he felt

the developers could already more than double the current density within the R-2-10 Zone. She was
not opposed to an RM Zone but felt it was important to remain consistent with decision-making. In
the past, she had voted against certain rezones because the developers were already able to increase

                • Proposed Holladay Crossroads Zone.
Mr. Allred reviewed the Summary Report The first issue related to the proposed eight-foot fence
height. He explained that there may be unintended consequences of an eight-foot fence, such as
blocking views of the canal. Mr. Allred noted that the zone could allow the Planning Commission to
increase the fence height if it was desired by the neighbors. Mr. Allred reported that the City standard
for fences is six feet in both commercial and residential areas. Council Member Gibbons believed
the ordinance should mirror the language in the ORD Zone.

The trash receptacle location was discussed. Mr. Allred noted that certain regulations have the
potential to drive building design and have a negative impact on perimeter development. He
suggested that the rules be the same as in the Holladay Village Zone, where there cannot be a dumpster
within 50 feet of an abutting home. Mr. Allred noted that there are a few homes on the north side of
the Holladay Crossroads Zone on 6060 South. Those homes do not have the canal buffer and the
Holladay Village Zone rules would work well there. He added that the canal would provide a buffer

of between 60 and 66 feet elsewhere. Council Member Gibbons believed the Holladay Village Zone
language would work.

Mr. Allred noted the height of the buildings will be a subjective decision for City Council. There will
be future discussion related to floor heights, roof heights, and the overall height of a building in the
future. The red-line document presented by Council Member Gibbons stated that “The proposed

       c.      Update on Skate Park RFP – Holly Smith.
Assistant to the City Manager, Holly Smith reported that funding for the Skate Park had been
approved. A request for proposal (“RFP”) had been issued the following day with a deadline of

December 18, 2020. Four proposals were received and forwarded to the Selection Committee, that
consisted of Council Members Petersen and Durham and City Staff. Committee Members reviewed
the proposals. The proposals were scored individually. All of the Committee Members chose Spohn
Ranch as the best proposal. Ms. Smith shared some of the highlights from the proposal: Spohn Ranch
offered a two-year warranty; they provided a thorough approach to the project as well as a detailed
list of deliverables; and they had a slightly more aggressive schedule than the RFP had asked for.

Ms. Smith reported that Spohn Ranch would be partnering with two local firms. G Brown Design
would be used for some of the architectural design work and Perigee Consulting would be used for
project engineering. Spohn Ranch has built projects in Utah before and the City was confident that
they would deliver a quality Skate Park to the community. Ms. Smith explained that there would be
a Stakeholders Group that would share suggestions and be part of the final design process. The
Stakeholders Group would be joined by the Selection Committee. Once the pre-final design was
completed, it would be brought to City Council for review.

The design process was expected to begin the beginning of February and would take approximately
one month to complete. Once the final design is ready, it will be presented to the City Council.
Construction would likely begin in late April and have a 12-week timeline and could open by mid to
late July.

Council Member Petersen wondered how the Stakeholders Group would be determined. Ms. Smith
suggested that she prepare a list of those who had expressed interest as well as the members of the
Selection Committee. She clarified that the Stakeholders Group would be more of a roundtable
discussion, where the consultant would take all feedback and work it into the final design. Council
Member Fotheringham suggested Jeffrey Conrad from the Holladay Arts Council. Council Member
Fotheringham also mentioned the importance of appropriate branding throughout the Skate Park and
the rest of the council agreed.

        e.      Discussion on Proposed Targeted Residential Picketing Ordinance
Ms. Chamness reported that over the last nine months, there have been a number of instances along
the Wasatch Front where people have picketed outside the homes of elected and appointed officials.
In response to those gatherings, several communities looked into ordinances that would restrict
picketing at primary residences. Mayor Dahle asked that the item be brought to the City Council for
discussion. There was a nearly two-decade-old ordinance passed by Salt Lake City and more recently,
ordinances in Orem and Lehi that limited picketing at homes to further than 100 feet from the property
line. Ms. Chamness wondered whether this was something Council Members were interested in
pursuing.
