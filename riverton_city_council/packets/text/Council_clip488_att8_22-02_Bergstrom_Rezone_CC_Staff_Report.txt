On December 9, 2021, the Planning Commission held a public hearing to review this
application for rezone. Minutes from that meeting are included below. The Planning
Commission voted to recommend approval of this application, with the following
recommended motion:

I move the City Council adopt Ordinance 22-02, amending the General Plan to Medium-
Density Residential and changing the zoning to R-4 (Single-family residential, 10,000-sf lots),
on 0.50-acres of land located at 1933 W 11800 South, as shown in Exhibit “A”.


Background

This is a public hearing for a General Plan Amendment and Rezone of 0.50-acres located on an
existing parcel on the south side of 11800 South at 1933 West.

The property has an existing house, and the applicant would like to split the property into two
lots and build a new house.




The subject property is currently zoned RR-22, which is a single-family residential zone half-
acre lots and animal rights. Area zoning is a mix of RR-22, R-3 (14,000-sf) and R-4 (10,000-sf)
with R-4 touching the subject properties east boundary, RR-22 touching the south and west
boundary.

       B.     REZONE, A REQUEST FOR A GENERAL PLAN AMENDMENT AND
              REZONE OF APPROXIMATELY 0.50-ACRES LOCATED AT 1933 WEST
              11800 SOUTH. APPLICANT-KEVIN BERGSTROM.

Chair Cannon opened the public hearing.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that there are two rezones on the agenda
and they are in close proximity, but they were noticed separately and will be heard separately
in the meeting. The Bergstrom rezone is located at 1933 West 11800 South. The area the
applicant is requesting the rezone for is 0.50 acres to divide into two ¼ acre lots, keeping
the ½ acre zoning for the rear of the property. Mr. Prestwich described the location and the
zoning map. The current zoning in the area is mixed, including R-22, R-3, and R-4. The
General Plan map shows potential developers and landowners what the City has intended
for specific areas in the City. The Bergstrom property is right on the border between low
and medium-density residential zoning. Mr. Prestwich explained that the General Plan and
the Zoning Map usually correspond with each other but it is not necessary. The applicant is
requesting a change to the General Plan to Medium-Density Residential, changing from R-
22 to R-4. The current General Plan would allow for R-3 zoning, but not R-4. The main
difference between the different zones is the lot width. Zone R-22 requires 150 feet of
frontage, while R-4 requires 80 feet. The other concern is animal rights. R-22 has animal
rights, while R-3 and R-4 do not.

The applicant, Kevin Bergstrom reported that the current home on the property is 100 years
old. It is owned by his father and his uncles own surrounding property. The family’s long-
term plan would be to make improvements to the original structure and build a home on the
lot behind that be created by this rezone.

Richard Silva indicated that he has been a neighbor in the Green Haven subdivision since
2017. He had concerns with safety and ingress and egress to the property due to the road
width. He asked if there would be sidewalks and room for emergency vehicles. He
presented the Commissioners with information that he had gathered.

Commissioner Cluff asked about the questions Mr. Silva raised regarding safety and roads.
Mr. Prestwich explained that this proposal will come before the Commission two more times
to address specific issues.

There were no further public comments. Chair Cannon closed the public hearing.

Commissioner Cluff moved that Planning Commission recommend APPROVAL of
Application PLZ-21-4010, for 0.50 acres of land located at 1933 West 11800 South,
thus amending the General Plan to Medium-Density Residential and changing the
Zoning to R-4 (Single-family residential, 10,000-square foot lots). Commissioner

       C.     REZONE, A REQUEST FOR A GENERAL PLAN AMENDMENT AND
              REZONE OF APPROXIMATELY 4.07-ACRES LOCATED AT 2356 WEST
              11800 SOUTH. APPLICANT-BEN HOWELL.

Mr. Prestwich described the location of the subject property and the details of the application.
Notice was sent out to the neighbors within 1,000 feet of the proposed rezone. The parcel
is at 2356 West 11800 South. It is 726 feet on the back parcel and 190 feet along Midas
Creek. When the parcel was developed in 2012 through 2015, a canal crossing was
discussed, and it was decided by the Planning Commission and City Council that a
connection should not be made. The Ivory development includes one-quarter acre lots and
north of Midas Creek are one-third acre lots and zoning. The current zoning is one-half and
one-third acre. There are two parcels in the area that allow for animal rights. The zoning is
harmonious but the lot sizes and use varies.

Some neighbors were concerned about the irrigation rights and Staff assured them that it
would be handled with a site plan. The current zoning is RR-22 with animal rights. The
General Plan anticipates change in the area. The current General Plan designation calls for
low-density or R-3 zoning. The applicant is requesting a change to medium-density which
would allow for R-4 zoning. The differences between R-3 and R-4 are lot size. R-3 includes
is 14,000 square-foot lots while R-4 is 10,000 square-foot lots. Lot widths for R-3 are 90
feet and R-4 is 80 feet. Staff had seen multiple layouts for the property without anything
coming to fruition. It is a difficult shape and creates issues for development. Occasionally,
developers will ask for R-4 zoning because they need a narrower frontage, which may or
may not be the case in this situation. This application has received numerous comments
from neighbors.

Chair Cannon opened the public hearing.

The applicant, Ben Howell, explained that several options have been considered and the
R-4 zoning would make the width to depth ratio better without adding a lot of homes. He
had spoken to some of the neighbors and addressed some of their concerns. The maximum
number of lots that would border Midas Creek is two, regardless of the zoning, based on the
shape of the parcel. He mentioned that some of the neighbors with water rights have
concerns and he would make sure they are addressed.

Commissioner Brown asked why the applicant is requesting R-4 instead of R-3 zoning.
Mr. Howell stated that the 90-foot width requirement for R-3 makes for an awkward layout
with extra-large side yards that potentially use excess water. The development lays out
better as R-4 zoning and will ultimately only add an additional two units.

Commissioner Cluff asked what the square footage of the lots would be. Mr. Howell
explained that the minimum would be the required 10,000 square feet, but several of the lots
will be closer to 13,000 to 14,000 square feet. There will be a maximum of 15 homes in the
development.
