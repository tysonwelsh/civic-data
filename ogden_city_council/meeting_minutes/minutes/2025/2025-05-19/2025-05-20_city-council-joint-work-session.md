        The purpose of the Joint Work Session was to discuss the proposed Fiscal Year 2025-2026 budget for the Community
and Economic Development and Fire Departments, and the Redevelopment Agency; discuss proposed Hazardous Environmental
Conditions Amendments; discuss Council/Board business; and hear Council/Board comments.

Agenda Review

scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Proposed Ogden City Fiscal Year 2025–2026 Budget
         Community and Economic Development Department Budget and Capital Improvement Projects: Community and
Economic Development Executive Director Johnson used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the proposed Fiscal
Year 2025-2026 budget for his Department, which includes Economic Development, Union Station, Planning, Community
Development, Building Services, and Cultural Services (Formerly Arts, Culture and Events) – all of which are funded in the
General Fund – and the Airport Enterprise Fund, Major Grant Fund, Miscellaneous Grants Fund, and the Tourism and Marketing
Accounts of the City. Mr. Johnson offered a comparison of the Fiscal Year 2024-2025 adopted budget and the Fiscal Year 2025-
2026 proposed budget, noting General Fund program funding will decrease by 4.38 percent, Airport Enterprise funding will
increase by 0.77 percent, Major Grants funding will increase by 6.27 percent, and Miscellaneous Grant funding will increase by
2.20 percent. The presentation offered information regarding the structure and staffing of the Community and Economic
Development Department and projects to be funded in Fiscal Year 2025-2026. The presentation also identified Capital
Improvement Project (CIP) projects for several divisions of the Department; the total cost of Community and Economic
Development, Airport Enterprise Fund, and RAMP CIP projects is $45.7 million, with $1.26 million requested in the Fiscal Year
2025-2026 budget. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
         Throughout Mr. Johnson’s presentation he engaged in discussion with the Council regarding Airport operations, the
scope of various projects included in the proposed budget, and changes to staffing in the Department.
         Fire Department Budget and Capital Improvement Projects: Fire Chief Mathieu used the aid of a PowerPoint
presentation to summarize the proposed Fiscal Year 2025-2026 budget for his Department, which includes Administration,
Emergency Management, Medical Services, Operations, Paramedics, Prevention, and Training. An increase in wages and benefits
of $482,400 is the most notable change from the previous fiscal year budget. He offered a comparison of the Fiscal Year 2024-
2025 adopted budget and the Fiscal Year 2025-2026 proposed budget; the general budget will increase 7.5 percent, or $482,475
and the Medical Services Enterprise fund will decrease 1.6 percent, or $211,525. The presentation also identified Capital
Improvement Project (CIP) projects for the fire facilities improvements and major equipment in the Department; the total CIP
request for FY 2025-2026 is $1,735,000, and is broken down as follows:
             • Project FI021 – Fire Facility Maintenance and Replacements, $1.5 million to be funded by the Medical Fund
                  balance

                  Revenue
             • Project FI023 – Fire Station Exhaust System Replacement, $180,000 to be funded with BDO Lease Revenue.

Proposed Hazardous Environmental Conditions Amendments
          Fire Marshal Brown explained that the Fire Department is proposing amendments to Ogden Municipal Code § 11-6-3
Hazardous Environmental Conditions. The proposed amendments make the following changes:
               • Adds new definition for “hazardous areas” and removes definitions that are being replaced. Hazardous areas are
                   defined as:
                         o Mountainous, brush covered, forest-covered, or dry grass-covered areas, including wooded areas
                              along the Ogden and Weber River Parkway.
                         o Areas within 200 feet of waterways, trails and canyons or similar areas.
                         o Wildland urban interface areas where structures meet or intermingle with undeveloped wildland
                              including all areas east of Harrison Boulevard from the north city boundary to the south city boundary
                         o A limited area outside these defined hazardous areas to facilitate a readily identifiable closed area.
               • Simplifies definition of “ignition source”.
               • Removes current prohibition on use of ignition source.
               • Adds new section establishing Fire Restriction Levels to allow the Fire Marshal to determine and declare the
                   fire restriction level for hazardous areas to reflect the risk of fire ignition in those areas based on the currently
                   existing environmental conditions:
                         o Yellow – Elevated Conditions. Prohibits aerial fireworks and any ignition source in non-landscaped
                              areas, areas that are not continuously attended, or without a means of extinguishment. Allows small
                              open-flame fire pits, outdoor appliances (grills, etc.), and ground level fireworks and sparklers.
                         o Orange – Critical Conditions. Prohibits any open flames, all fireworks, and any ignition source in non-
                              landscaped areas, areas that are not continuously attended, or areas without a means of
                              extinguishment. Allows the use of fire pits and outdoor appliances if used in areas that are landscaped,
                              continuously attended, and equipped with a means of extinguishment.
                         o Red – Severe Conditions. Prohibits use of all ignition sources in designated hazardous areas.
               • Makes technical changes to noticing requirements to align with state statute.
          Mr. Brown concluded he and Fire Department Administration are requesting the adoption of this ordinance prior to the
2025 fire season.
          Discussion among the Council centered on the potential for public confusion relative to the color-coded system; and
dangers associated with private fireworks in residential areas – particularly near the mountain benches.
          Mr. Brown then provided the Council with information regarding House Bill (HB) 48, legislation intended to oversee
wildfire risks associated with wildland urban interface property. Counties are required to take certain actions related to wildland
urban interface property and assess fees to municipalities; the revenues of the fees will be deposited into the Wildland-Urban
Interface Prevention, Preparedness, and Mitigation Fund. Municipalities are also required to adopt wildland urban interface
building code standards. Mr. Brown stated that there are many unknowns related to the fee structure as well as the areas of the
City and County that will be identified as wildland-urban for purposes of mitigation efforts.

Proposed Redevelopment Agency Fiscal Year 2025–2026 Budget
          Finance Director Sorensen and representatives of the Community and Economic Development Department used the aid
of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the proposed Redevelopment Agency budget for Fiscal Year 2025-2026; the
Administration is requesting the Board adopt the proposed Fiscal Year 2025-2026 Redevelopment Agency budget totaling
$16,850,075. There is a decrease of $1,393,650 or 16.91 percent over the Fiscal Year 2024-2025 budget. Reductions in the total
budget are the result of the expiration of two districts, Fairmont and Lincoln, and from a reduction in environmental remediation
expenditures in Trackline. The Redevelopment Agency is funded primarily through tax increment received from the various
redevelopment districts or project areas. Contributions from Business Depot Ogden lease revenue are also included in the
Agency’s revenue. A contribution from the Agency to the General Fund covers services provided to the Redevelopment Agency
by city administrative staff. The Redevelopment Agency Administration has a total operational budget of $331,500, of which
$282,025 is for city consulting services, $22,475 is for insurance, and $15,000 for travel and education. The Administration has
provided a spreadsheet summarizing the revenues and expenditures of the various Redevelopment Agency areas in more detail.
Finally, Administration is proposing use of $4,850,050 in Business Depot Ogden Lease Revenue to fund prior commitments made
in existing project areas. Business Depot Ogden funds are proposed to be used as follows:
              • CBD Mall - $250,000 debt service/reserve/capital
            • Kiesel - $780,300 for Union Station Land Purchase three of seven
            • 25th Street - $500,000 for Interfund Loan related to the Central Business District
            • Continental - $3,019,750 for WonderBlock Debt Service
            • No area - $300,000 for Loan Repayment for St. Anne’s property purchase
        For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
        Discussion among the Board and Redevelopment Agency staff centered on the City’s ability to repay debt in the event of
an economic downturn; the status of developments in various Redevelopment Agency project areas; and staffing adjustments in
the Community and Economic Development Department related to Redevelopment Agency activities.
