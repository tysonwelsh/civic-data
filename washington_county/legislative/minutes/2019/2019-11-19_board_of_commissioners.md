PRESENTATION: ALZHEIMER'S ASSOCIATION

Mike Miller explained that adults with Alzheimer’s disease often don’t have the
appropriate resources. People that are susceptible to the disease are encouraged to engage
with others and remain active in order to keep brain activity stimulated.

Kim Boyer stated that while entities within the community exist, they are often nonprofit
and struggle to provide the necessary resources. It is the hope of the Alzheimer’s
Association to have a unified care center within the community where people in need of
help can go for resources.

PRESENTATION AND OPEN DISCUSSION FOR PROPOSED TAX INCREASE
FOR THE WASHINGTON COUNTY WATER CONSERVANCY DISTRICT

Washington County Water Conservancy representative Ed Bowler explained that
Washington County’s growth is projected to increase by 226% by year 2065 and it is the
responsibility of the Water District to ensure that all residents of Washington County have
continuous access to water during that growth.

Property tax is one of the main revenue sources for the Water District used to pay for water
infrastructure. Other services funded by property taxes include the development of new
water supplies, and public safety initiatives.

The proposed tax increase is 0.000042, which is approximately $7.57 annually based upon
the county’s average home value of $329,000. The Water Conservancy District’s
maximum allowable limit is 0.001 which does not require a public vote.

PRESENTATION: PRELIMINARY BUDGET 2020 WASHINGTON COUNTY
BUDGET

Washington County Clerk/Auditor Kim Hafen presented the proposed 2020 budget. There
are no projected changes to the Utah State Retirement (URS) rates and healthcare rates.
There was minimal change to the general fund, which increased by approximately
$500,000 dollars. The increase comes mainly from property tax growth of new homes and
the quarter option sales tax.

One of the main changes with the general fund includes the transferring of the patrol
portion of public safety into the municipal services fund. The overall budget of the Sheriffs

Office is approximately $20,000,000.

The final budget will be presented at the December 17, 2019 Commission Meeting.

PUBLIC HEARING: CREATION OF AGRICULTURAL PROTECTION AREA IN
PINE VALLEY

Chairman Cox opened the public hearing and invited any interested parties to speak.
Washington County Community Development Director Scott Messel explained that an
agricultural protection area allows property owners to utilize property for specific
agricultural purposes. The private property owners have requested than an agricultural
protection area be created over their combined 288.38 acres located in Pine Valley, UT.

Clerk/Auditor Kim Hafen stated that he enjoys the sights and smells of agriculture.

Seeing no one else come forward, Chairman Cox closed the public hearing.

CONSIDERATION _ OF RESOLUTION NO. R-2019-2532, A RESOLUTION
APPROVING THE DISSOLUTION OF THE ROCKVILLE/SPRINGDALE FIRE

SPECIAL SERVICE DISTRICT

Hurricane Valley Fire Chief Tom Kuhlmann stated that the Rockville Springdale Fire
Special Service District has been contracting with the Hurricane Valley Fire District for
the past seventeen (17) months. As a result, the standby fee previously paid by the RSFSSD
will no longer apply and the HVFD will provide all emergency services to the residents of
Rockville and Springdale.

Mr. Kuhlmann clarified that in the event that the HVFD’s equipment could not provide the
necessary services, a mutual aid agreement with surrounding communities is in place in
order to provide the best service possible.

Chairman Cox commended the HVFD for their efforts to provide the best service possible
to those areas of the County.
