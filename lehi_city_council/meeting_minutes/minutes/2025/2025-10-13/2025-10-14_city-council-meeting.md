2.3) Discussion of Shift Differential Pay Policy

Lehi City Council Meeting                     1                               October 14, 2025
Police Chief Paul presented a proposal for implementing shift differential pay for police officers.
He explained that over a year ago, the department shifted from a rotation model to a bid shift
system based on seniority to have more officers available during peak call times. However, this
resulted in newer officers consistently getting less desirable shifts. The proposed policy would be
a 5% additional pay for graveyard shift officers and 2.5% additional pay for evening shift
officers. The chief noted this approach was standard among other agencies and would help with
officer wellness, work-life balance, recruitment, and retention.


2.4) Quarterly Financial Report- Dean Lundell, Finance Director

Dean Lundell presented the quarterly financial report, highlighting the city's financial
performance. Sales tax revenue showed moderate growth of 3 -4%, coming in at $25.2 million
compared to the budgeted $23.7 million. Property tax was nearly on target, with $16 million
collected against a $16.1 million budget. Total revenue reached $65.7 million, exceeding the
budgeted $59.6 million, which allowed for a $6.5 million transfer to capital projects. The city's
unassigned fund balance ended at $24.5 million, slightly above the 35% cap. Lundell broke
down the fund balance into three key components: a 5% floor reserve of $4 million, a $11.6
million rainy day fund for revenue fluctuations, and an $8.9 million risk management reserve for
potential disasters. Enterprise fund cash reserves varied, with the culinary and water funds
maintaining healthy reserves of about 618 days, while the pressurized irrigation and sewer funds
had lower cash reserves. The power fund is recovering from a challenging 2023, and Joel's team
is currently conducting a new rate study. Class C gas tax revenue was also reported as healthy,
based on gallons consumed rather than dollar value.

5) Consideration of Ordinance #59-2025, approving a Development Code Amendment to
     Chapter 37, Connectivity Standards.

Kim Struthers presented a proposed amendment to Chapter 37 connectivity standards to add
flexibility when road connections to local streets would push traffic volumes over acceptable
levels. This amendment arose from discussions about the Timpanogos Highway/Morning Vista
retail project.

The amendment would allow alternative methods (maintaining bicycle and pedestrian
connectivity) instead of requiring vehicular connections when traffic volumes would be
problematic. While the current developer chose not to use this flexibility, the amendme nt would
be available for future projects.

9) Consideration of Ordinance #61-2025, approving the Starter Home Attainable Residential
     (SHAR) ordinance creating an opportunity for the development of small single -family
     homes.

Brittney Harris, city planner, presented the SHAR ordinance, which had undergone extensive
development over 14-15 months. The ordinance aimed to create opportunities for small, single -
family homes priced around $400,000 with deed restrictions for owner occupancy.
Key features included: 1.5 density bonus in R1-12 and smaller zones, deed restrictions requiring
owner occupancy, development agreement process giving council discretion, no ADUs allowed
on SHAR lots, and a goal of providing affordable homeownership options.

Mayor Johnson noted discussions with congressional representatives about housing affordability
and the need to prevent outside investors from buying up residential properties for rentals.

Mayor Johnson asked for public

Laura Stevens argued that a $400,000 “starter” home is not affordable for many young adults,
questioned how prices and affordability could be enforced over time, and worried about citywide
impacts on property rights and transparency.

Kaylynn Osborne raised concerns about stacking overlays (she already lives in a TOD area), the
150% density bonus, and the risk that “single-family” units could still function as multi-family.

Jonathan Madsen shared negative experiences with deed-restricted communities in Arizona,
warning about HOA “enforcers,” conflict, and investor control if the City lacks clear
enforcement authority.

James Harrison called the proposal half-baked, doubted that such tools truly create affordability,
and cautioned against reshaping Lehi’s character with an underdeveloped ordinance.

Rachel Freeman explained that, given Lehi’s AMI and typical new-graduate incomes, the
proposed “attainable” units would not actually be reachable for many younger households.

Shannon Topham questioned whether higher density has ever really lowered prices in Utah,
suggesting it often drives them up instead.

Emily Stenbridge objected to adopting code the City and public do not fully understand,
especially with current strain on roads, schools, and services.

Evan Howlett rejected the framing of $400,000 as a realistic starter price and warned that both
