3. Public hearing and possible action regarding a Code Amendment to Section 10-2-
4 Rural Residential Zone of the Snyderville Basin Development Code– Ray 
Milliner, Principal Planner    
 
Planner Milliner said Staff is requesting the Commission consider a Code amendment to 
the rural residential (RR) zone.  Staff is requesting that clarifying language is inserted 
into the Code.  The purpose of the amendment would be to clarify setback language 
regarding lots where the property line extends to the center of the road.   
 
Currently, the Code language says the front setback is 30 feet; however, there are 
situations where the lot line extends into the road.  This creates confusion on where to 
measure from.  Planner Milliner showed examples of how this amendment would be 
applied.  He said that Staff recommends the Commission conduct a public hearing and 
forward a positive recommendation to the County Council.   
 
The public hearing was opened.  
Martina Nelson said she is a surveyor.  The map being displayed was one she created.  
She thanked the Commission for putting this language in the Code.  She asked how 
long before the language will take effect.  Planner Milliner told her the County Council 
is scheduled to review it the next day.  If approved, it will be in effect immediately.           
 
The public hearing was closed.  
 
Commissioner Dickey made a motion to forward a positive recommendation to the 
County Council as outlined.  Commissioner Fine seconded the motion.  All voted in 
approval.     
FINDINGS OF FACT 
Snyderville Basin Planning Commission 
March 12, 2019 
Page 3 of 15 
 
1. In October 10, 2014, the Summit County Council amended the Snyderville Basin 
Development Code creating exceptions to the setback rules. 
2. As part of that approval, setback requirement Number 8 of the Rural Residential 
(RR) zone was removed.   
3. Setback requirement Number 8 addressed building setbacks in cases where the 
property lines extend to the center of the road. 
4. Because setback requirement Number 8 was removed, the County has 
encountered instances were a lot line extends into the center of the road, and 
created confusion as to where the setback should be measured.   
5. Reinsertion of this language would clarify how the setback is measured and 
eliminate any confusion and make the rules in the RR zone consistent with those 
in the MR and HS zones.  
6. One component in the goal of Chapter 2 of the Snyderville Basin General Plan is 
to maintain the neighborhood character.   
7. The proposed amendment will ensure that new properties are consistent with 
the surrounding properties nearby.   
 
CONCLUSIONS OF LAW 
1. The amendment is consistent with the goals, objectives and policies of the 
General Plan.   
2. The amendment will not permit the use of land that is not consistent with the 
uses of properties nearby.  
3. The amendment will not permit suitability of the properties affected by the 
proposed amendment for the uses to which they have been restricted.  
4. The amendment will not permit the removal of the then existing restrictions 
which will unduly affect nearby property.   
5. The amendment will not grant special favors or circumstances solely for one 
property owner or developer. 
6. The amendment will promote the public health, safety and welfare better than 
the existing regulations for which the amendment is intended to change.

2. Discussion regarding possible amendments to the Snyderville Basin Development 
Code crating a Neighborhood Mixed Use zoning District (NMU-1); Henry Sigg, 
Representative- Ray Milliner, Principal Planner    
 
Planner Milliner said this is an application for a Code amendment to create a 
Neighborhood Mixed Use zone.  Henry Sigg is the applicant.  He is requesting the 
Planning Commission consider adopting the proposed amended Code language.  It 
would allow properties to be rezoned NMU in the future.  Mr. Sigg has an application for 
a project in Silver Creek near Home Depot.  If this application is successful, he will apply 
to rezone his development project to the NMU zone.   
 
Planner Milliner said he would like review the NMU zone that is being proposed by Mr. 
Sigg.  Afterwards, the applicant requests time to further outline his proposal.  Planner 
Milliner said the language being proposed at this meeting is the same language that was 
proposed by Staff at earlier meetings.  Commissioner Cooke said he noted some 
substantial language changes from the versions presented earlier to the Commission.     
 
Planner Milliner addressed the rezoning eligibility:   
1. The land must be featured on the future land map as a mixed use.  There are a 
number of properties scattered throughout the Basin with this identification.   
2. The properties must currently be located in one of the following zones:  
Rural Residential (RR)  
Community Commercial (CC)  
Service Commercial (SC)  
Neighborhood Commercial (NC)  
Town Center (TC)      
He identified the areas with these designations.    
3. The maximum size would be up to 30 acres.  There are many areas that would be 
eligible for this.   
4. The property must be adjacent to an existing commercial, mixed use, or 
institutional/civic development.  
5. The property must be next to an existing transit system, or have plans integrating 
into the system.
