ATTACHMENT 1
                                  BUNDLE OF RIGHTS TRAINING
                          PLANNING COMMISSION MEETING 4/9/2025
                         PRESENTED BY SHAUNA TALBOT, CITY PLANNER


Real property -land plus appurtenant (attached) structures) have certain “rights” that belong to
the person who owns title to the property in “fee simple” (holding full rights).
The various rights can be broken down into component parts, and can be shared, or reduced by
various means. All rights held together is called ownership in “fee simple”.
Why is it called a bundle of rights?
Long ago in history when land ownership was transferred from one party to another they would
physically hand over a handful of soil, or a bundle of sticks from a tree growing on the property
representing ownership changing hands. Over time this metaphor has been used to represent
the type of “rights” a person who is the “owner of record” (listed as the owner once recorded in
the county recorder’s office) of a property owns.
Some of the “rights” available to “hold in fee simple” are…
   1. Right of POSSESSION – The right to own and occupy the property.
   2. Right of CONTROL – The right to determine how the property is used, including what
      activities can take place on it subject to zoning regulations, HOA rules, and other laws.
   3. Right of EXLUSION – The right to prevent others from entering or using the property
      without permission, also known as the right to exclude others.
   4. Right of ENJOYMENT – The right to use and enjoy the property lawfully, including
      activities like inhabitation and resource extraction, not just to the land but also to center
      of earth and reasonably above airspace to infinity, but within legal constraints.
   5. Right of DISPOSITION – The right to transfer ownership of the property, either
      temporarily (as in a lease) or permanently (through sale, inheritance, or gift).
   6. Right to TRANSFER – The right to transfer ownership of property by selling it, giving it to
      someone else, or transferring it to an heir, or abandoning it.
   7. Right to DESTROY – The right to destroy property as long as they don’t violate laws.
Restrictions and limitations on some of the rights can come about through various means:
   1. Possession can be limited by:
         a. Tenancy
         b. Subtenancy
         c. Lien (trust deed – mortgage)
         d. Lien (property taxes))
         e. Lien (contractor’s)
