1. Discussion regarding possible light industrial zoning in Browns Canyon area– 
Planning Department Staff  
 
Community Development Director Patrick Putt said a member of the public, Paul 
Trunnell, spoke with him about some possible light-industrial service-commercial uses 
in the Browns Canyon area via the process of leasing.  During the conversation, it 
became apparent to him that the use of leases would probably not get Mr. Trunnell 
where he wanted to be.  This seemed more like a land use and zoning issue.   
 
Director Putt said he suggested to Mr. Trunnell that given the fact the Commission is in 
the process of reworking the Code and talking about possible adjustments to the zoning 
maps, it might be helpful to have him speak to the Commission.  Mr. Trunnell planned 
on being in attendance to express his thoughts to the Commission, but has not yet 
arrived.

Director Putt said the purpose of this meeting is to have a discussion to consider if some 
light industrial uses in the Browns Canyon area might be appropriate.  Would a 
community need be met?  Would a rezone help to organize some of the existing uses in 
the area?   
 
Director Putt asked Planner Sean Lewis to give a brief background of the pertinent 
history for the benefit of the Planning Commission.  Planner Lewis said in 2008, Staff 
received several conditional use permit (CUP) applications in the Browns Canyon area 
for excavating.  These were from companies that wanted to process dirt and resell it as 
top soil.   
 
Planner Lewis said Staff received an application from Jeff Hisler and High Mountain 
Construction in 2008.  There were two or three of these types of applications at that 
time.  The Planning Commission decided to add a line under the rock quarry use in the 
Use Chart to allow “the filtering, sifting, and processing of soil.”   
 
At that time, the Planning Commission asked Staff to look at this as a potential rezone.  
The feeling among the Commission was that this would be calling the area what it is.  
Planner Lewis displayed a graphic of the zoning of the area in August 2009.  He added 
there hasn’t been much change since that date.   
 
Planner Lewis identified the different properties that comprised the proposed rezone.  
The property owners were Jeff Hisler, Russ Witt, and Paul Trunnell.  The Commission 
thought rezoning these three properties would be a case of spot zoning.  They thought it 
would be more effective to rezone the entire length of High View Road, which runs 
down the middle of the properties and ends at the Friends of Animals.        
 
Planner Lewis said in August of 2009, letters were mailed to the neighboring property 
owners to request feedback on the proposed rezone project.  Three property owners 
indicated they did not want to be included.   
 
In November 2009, the Planning Commission asked Staff to include the other quarries 
located in Browns Canyon.  A slide was displayed to indicate the land that would be 
included.  Letters were sent to the quarries and the other local property owners.  There 
were some who wanted to opt out.  Most of the concerns centered on an increase in 
property taxes.   
 
In January 2010, a field trip to the site was taken.  Three options were considered:    
1. To rezone the three parcels included in the application.   
2. To rezone all properties along High View Road, excluding the properties that the 
owners didn’t wish to be included.   
3. To rezone all rock quarries.

Planner Lewis said the Planning Commission decided that a Light Industrial Zone 
should be created.  During the February 3, 2010 meeting, Staff was directed to evaluate 
how the proposed rezone might affect property tax evaluations.  Staff was also directed 
to contact Z-Rock Quarry, which is on the north side of Browns Canyon, to see if they 
would like to be included in the rezone. 
       
Planner Lewis said on March 23, 2010, a public hearing was held.  He showed a slide 
that displayed the possible rezone of the properties.  Nine members of the Community 
spoke.  Some were in favor and some were opposed.  At that time, the Commission 
voted 5 to 1 to recommend approval to the County Commission.   The opposing vote 
was from Mike Brown.  He expressed concern about Code language, not the potential 
rezone.

application.  The Council did not feel the area had the infrastructure nor was it the right

time, a Light Industrial Zone has been created.

3. Planning Commission round-table discussion regarding 
reasons/justification/criteria for applying zoning designations– Planning 
Department Staff 
 
Director Putt said this item was labeled as a round table discussion to talk about the 
factors that go into making zoning decisions.  The subcommittee decided this should 
come back to the entire Commission in order to make sure the subcommittee needs to 
know how to proceed.  He created a discussion list based on the subcommittee’s 
conversation.  He thought it would also be beneficial to bring the State Code language to 
the meeting.     
 
Director Putt said the purpose of the last four meetings was to receive public feedback.  
Now, he would like to check in with the Commission on the approach they are going to 
use moving forward.  The current plan is to take the information provided by the public 
and go back to the subcommittee level and use that information to create a proposed 
map.   
 
Director Putt emphasized this will not be a final map, but a map they can take to the 
public hearing process.  Staff is aware from the state statute that the Commission is the 
body that prepares and recommends the zoning map, the zones associated with it, and 
the Development Code.  After a public hearing, a recommendation will be handed to the 
County Council.  At that point, the County Council will hold one or more public hearings 
on the matter.

Director Putt said the approach they are taking is to allow the subcommittee to 
generate the map.  When the subcommittee work is done, that map will be brought to 
the Commission for review and adjustments.  Then, that map will be brought to the 
public.  He assumes the public will have changes, revisions, and edits.  By the time the 
map is handed to the County Council it will very likely not be the same map that they 
started with.   
 
Director Putt proposed an idea on how to keep track of the public’s edits.  He proposed 
they annotate the map.  They can apply as much ink to it as they want to reflect the 
comments received both from the public and from the Commission.  When the public 
hearing process is completed, they will have both the written comments and a set of 
visual comments.  These will be helpful to create the map that will be sent to the County 
Council.  Additionally, they will not only present the map to show Council, but how they 
got to that point.   
 
Director Putt said this way of going forward is predictable, it is measureable, and they 
will have a record of what the public wants.  He said he wanted to make it clear the 
Commission will ultimately recommend a map to the County Council.  It will then be in 
their hands.     
 
Director Putt distributed the list of possible rezoning factors that might influence their 
decisions to either zone or rezone.  This is not a comprehensive list.  He also distributed 
the State Code language.  He read Paragraph A.   
 
Director Putt said when they look at these two lists, the rezoning factors created by the 
subcommittee generally fall into the categories outlined by the State.  They want the

conjunction with the Development Code.
