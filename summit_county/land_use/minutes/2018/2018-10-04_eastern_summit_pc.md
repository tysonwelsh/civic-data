Attorney Strachan stated that for any Conditional Use Permit (CUP), under State Law 
there are allowances for conditions to be placed on the CUP to protect the health, safety 
and welfare of residents. There are preemptory federal laws that pertain to these type of 
telecommunication towers that are specific as to how local government can regulate these 
types of towers. Local government cannot unreasonably discriminate, cannot regulate the 
placement, construction, or modification of the tower. If the tower meets the requirements 
under federal regulations, then the health considerations that might be brought up cannot 
be considered. If there is substantial evidence to deny the application, then the Planning 
Commission may do so, but the denial must be very specific to the concerns the 
Commission may have. Aesthetic concerns are really not a consideration according to Code. 
Mitigation of aesthetic concerns can be included in Conditions of Approval. 
Liz Walker, the applicant on behalf of Skyway stated that the tower would be more 
representative of a real pine tree than what is shown in the Staff Report, with fuller, denser 
branches. The applicant has worked hard with Staff to earn the recommendation of 
approval. The infrastructure proposed is in response to the high demand for cellular 
services in the area. More wireless infrastructure is being required due to the way people 
are using their cellular devices. There is an average of nearly four wireless devices per 
person now and people expect connectivity with their cell phone. The wireless industry has 
a great impact and huge benefit on the economy.

Planner Milliner shared the background. There was a discussion about the limitations of 
lighting of buildings at a previous meeting and Planner Milliner was instructed to present 
specific language to the Planning Commission for a possible recommendation to add the 
lighting ordinance to the Code. The specific language would specify how buildings are lit in 
Eastern Summit County. This ordinance would not apply to indoor lighting, but exterior 
lighting only. Existing outdoor lighting will be allowed. This ordinance would apply to any 
new outdoor lighting, including any changes made to existing lighting. There will be a 
review of exterior lighting plans for compliance through the Planning Department.  
There was a discussion about full cut-off lighting. Commissioner Sonntag stated that full 
cut-off is restrictive. As long as the lighting source is obscured, a lit translucent piece 
should be allowed for a light fixture. Commissioner Sargent stated he likes the full cut-off. 
Anything below the cut-off shield is annoying. With so much visible light source, people 
cannot see the stars anymore. 
Commissioner Wheaton asked if there are time restrictions for lighting at sports facilities.

o’clock would be a good time.
