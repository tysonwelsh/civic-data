   1.2. Review of Agenda Items – Planning Staff
The commission held a detailed discussion regarding the single agenda item on the table, which was
the conditional use permit for monument signs intended for Zions Bank. Planner Howa elaborated
on the situation, pointing out that there was a disagreement between the applicants and the staff
over the interpretation of the ordinance concerning the calculations for sign size. He indicated that
the staff had given a recommendation for approval, contingent upon the applicants meeting the
standards as interpreted by the city.
Planning Director Maloy provided further context, shedding light on the historical and procedural
background. He explained that there had been specific administrative referral guidelines established
for handling monument signs in the past. These guidelines served as an internal procedure to
streamline the approval process for signs that adhered to the pre-established benchmark. However,
Maloy clarified that any projects not fitting neatly within those guidelines, such as the current case
with Zions Bank, would be escalated to the Planning Commission to be tackled as a conditional use
permit. This methodology ensured thorough scrutiny and alignment with city ordinances, giving the
commission clear oversight on cases with potential gray areas in interpretation.
The commission took time to consider how best to address the intricacies of the ordinance and how
it applied to the proposal, weighing the history of consistent application against the specific details
of the current case.
   1.3. Review and discuss Permitted and Conditional land uses in Agricultural Zones – Sheldon
        Howa, Planner II
The primary focus was on discussing the appropriateness of current land use regulations,
particularly concerning the allowance of commercial businesses within agricultural zones.
According to Planner Howa, the ongoing issue revolves around an applicant who wishes to operate a
commercial business in an agricultural zone, which is permitted as a conditional use under current
ordinances. The commission intends to evaluate whether this aligns with the intended use of
agricultural areas and if such uses are compatible with surrounding residential zones. They plan to
review the criteria currently set forth in the ordinance, which requires a certain amount of land for
a business to qualify under a conditional use permit, and to ensure that these uses do not disrupt
the nature of agricultural or residential areas.
   1.4. Review and discuss Land Use Development regulations, standards, policies, and best
        practices for commercial and residential uses – Michael Maloy, Planning Director

Planning Director Maloy presented the addition of commercial subdivision amendments, allowing
for these to be administratively reviewed. He noted the need to consider dedications of real
property for public utilities like storm retention basins and water infrastructure. The commission
expressed a preference to review such dedications, citing potential community impacts. The
discussion emphasized the necessity and possible expansion of administrative practices, while
remaining open for future review.

Community Development Director Thomas provided an overview of Public Infrastructure Districts
(PIDs) and their implementation in Herriman City. He explained that PIDs are used to finance public
improvements such as parks, water improvements, and roads. He mentioned that the Auto Mall
development has a PID with a mill levy which is doubling the property taxes for those lots.
Community Development Director discussed the differences between PIDs and impact fees, noting
that PIDs allow developers to access municipal bond markets and control their own destiny in terms
of infrastructure development. He also mentioned that the city maintains improvements after they
are completed and warrantied.
The commission discussed various aspects of PIDs, including:
   1.   The length of time PIDs can collect assessments (up to 30 years)
   2. The potential for developers to inflate costs
   3. The auditing process by the state
   4. The possibility of excess funds at the end of a PID's life
   5. The need for more experience and knowledge about PIDs in Utah
The commission expressed interest in learning more about PIDs and potentially having a training
session with an expert in the future.

Planner Howa presented the conditional use request for two monument signs for Zions Bank,
located at the intersection of Herriman Boulevard and Herriman Main Street. He described the
proposed signs, noting that one smaller sign would be placed along Main Street and a larger sign
along Herriman Boulevard.
He explained that the larger sign, measuring about 13 feet long, 6 feet high, and 1.5 feet deep, had a
total square footage of just under 84 square feet, including an electronic message board. He noted
that the base would be constructed with brick to match the bank building. He then detailed the city's
interpretation of the ordinance, which includes the support structure in the calculation of the sign's
square footage. Based on this interpretation, staff determined that the proposed larger sign
exceeded the 64 square foot maximum allowed by ordinance.
The applicant, Kevin Anderson from Impact Signs, addressed the commission. He argued that
according to the city's own definitions, the sign area should only include the portion encompassing
the actual sign, not the structure supporting it. He stated that based on this interpretation, the sign
meets all ordinance requirements with a sign area of 54 square feet.
Applicant Anderson expressed frustration with the planning department's interpretation and
communication during the review process. He emphasized that his company always strives to
design signs that fall within city ordinances to avoid issues for their customers.
Commission members discussed the interpretation of the ordinance and the city's history of
applying it consistently. Commissioner Jacobson noted that they had always included the support
structure in the calculation and didn't want to deviate from that practice.
Commissioner Rypien inquired about the possibility of having a process for applicants to request
approval for larger signs. Chair Powell noted that currently, there is no such process in the
ordinance.

5. Chair and Commission Comments
Commissioner Rypien suggested considering a process for applicants to request exceptions or
variances for sign sizes in the future. He expressed his appreciation for unique, architectural signs
and felt that there could be exceptions to the current strict interpretation.
Chair Powell agreed that it would be beneficial to clarify the ordinances to ensure clear
interpretation in the future. He suggested scheduling a future work session to review the sign
ordinance and potentially consider modifications or exceptions.
Planning Director Maloy acknowledged the suggestion and noted that while the city has worked
hard to encourage coordinated site plans and favor monument signs, the commission could review
and potentially recommend modifications to the ordinance, subject to City Council approval.
