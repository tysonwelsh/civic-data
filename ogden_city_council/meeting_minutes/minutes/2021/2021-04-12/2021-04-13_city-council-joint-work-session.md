amendments; fee amendments include the following:
               •    Landing fees -12,500+ pounds ($.75 > 1.25 per thousand pounds)
               •    Fuel Flowage ($.07 > .09 per gallon)

           Council member Hyer inquired as to the manner in which landing fees are collected by the City. Mr. Williams indicated
they are generally collected from the fixed-base operators that are servicing an aircraft. Airport Manager Garrett added that fixed-
base operators serve as proxy to the Airport, and they retain 35 percent of the landing fee amount and remit the balance to the
City. However, fees are only collected from aircraft that are based at the airport; most of the aircraft are transient in nature and
they are not charged a landing fee unless they park at the de-icing ramp or can be contacted by an Airport staff while they are at
the facility.
          Mr. Williams then noted Administration proposes expansion of the duration of ground leases; the current ordinance
provides for hanger construction lease terms of 15 years for a private hangar owner, 20 years for a commercial operator, 25 years
for a fixed-base operator, and a possible five-year renewal. Airport staff has determined these are insufficient lengths to attract
significant airport development and the Airport is in need of a new standard. Staff has looked to the Federal Aviation Authority
(FAA) guidance to determine appropriate adjustments and they found the following:
               •    Sufficient Years to Amortize Tenant's Capital Investment.
               •    May offer ground rent below fair market value to facilitate amortization.
               •    30-40 years recommended, over 50 years requires FAA approval.
               •    End of Lease -Airport exercises Reversionary Rights.
                         o Airport takes possession of land and improvements.
                         o Rent the land and any improvements for fair market value.
          Airport staff recommends that for new construction, the ground lease should be sufficient to amortize a tenant's capital
investment, or a 40-year maximum term. At the end of the lease, the Airport can exercise reversionary rights by taking possession
of the land and improvements and renting the land for fair market value through a facility lease. For a facility lease after a ground
lease expansion, improvements would be owned by the City through reversion or other action. Leases will be at fair market value
determined by commercially reasonable methods and may vary by age, size, location, fmish, condition, etc. Preference will be
given to prior ground lease or facility lease tenants. The proposed ordinance amendments are consistent with lease agreements;
ground can be leased for a term of years (five to 25), and at the end of the lease, the landlord takes possession of the premises via
reversionary rights. A tenant may remove improvements within 60 days after the end of the lease term and if the tenant fails to
remove the improvements, ownership of such improvements will revert to the Airport as the landlord. Tenants will have the
benefits of their lease agreements. These proposed ordinance amendments are consistent with FAA guidance and Mr. Williams
presented an example of this type of adjustment at the Shreveport Airport. At that installation, the FAA found violations of grant
assurances whereby tenants were given endless ground leases, the airport failed to exercise reversionary rights and take possession
ofland and improvements, and the airport failed to charge fair market rent at the end of a lease term. Due to these conditions, the
FAA ordered a corrective action plan for the Shreveport Airport. Additionally, the proposed ordinance and lease amendments are
consistent with State law; Mr. Williams referenced a Utah Supreme Court case concerning Price Development and Orem City.
The Court's ruling was that "public property is held in trust for the public and may not be disposed of other than "in good faith
and for adequate consideration." (i.e., fair market value); and "Utah's constitution and statutes must be satisfied, and they require
that the public's money and property be carefully tended, lest public and political pressures persuade local government to transfer
it into private hands without receiving a fair net benefit in exchange." Based on this case law, the City should exercise its
reversionary rights, with that action being held in trust for the public. Mr. Williams emphasized that the second court opinion
regarding the public trust was most striking to him; in speaking with the current and past Airport Managers, he has learned that
most of the decisions they made in regard to lease terms was that they were worried about the public and political pressure from
tenants at the Airport. The Court has specifically cautioned Airports to not give in to political and public pressure; rather, the City
is responsible to protect the public trust by exercising contractual reversionary rights.
          Council member Hyer stated it is important to note that all of the tenants at the Airport are different from one another;
some are individuals while others are businesses. One thing that may be concerning to the City is the potential for these
amendments to result in a class action suit, but it is his understanding that would not be an option in this situation. Mr. Williams
stated he will not provide a firm legal opinion on that matter during this meeting, but he did communicate the City's legal counsel
could defend the City's position with strength if the suit were based upon changes resulting from the ordinance or lease
amendments. He stated there have been rumors of a threatened lawsuit, but he advised the Council to make decisions based upon
FAA guidance, state law, and case law rather than on public or political pressure. The City Attorney's Office will handle legal
challenges as they come about.

              A memo from Community and Economic Development Department came before the Council to consider a resolution
    renewing the Quality Neighborhoods Initiative Program. The memo stated the City's Community and Economic Development
    Department is proposing the City Council, Mayor and Redevelopment Agency renew the Quality Neighborhoods Initiative. The
    Quality Neighborhoods Initiative was frrst adopted in November 2015 as a way to coordinate various community and economic
    development programs and strategies into a consolidated vision designed to help stabilize and revitalize neighborhoods in Ogden.
    The Initiative uses several programs, including the City's five-year Consolidated Plan and Annual Action Plans, the City's
    Redevelopment Agency, and other city and state programs, to establish a coordinated pattern of public investment into the overall
    health of the community. The program uses several funding sources, which include Community Development Block Grants, BDO
    lease revenue, state and other local funding sources. An update to the BDO lease revenue appropriation is addressed in a separate

    expound on the proposal to renew the Quality Neighborhood Initiative. He discussed the evolution of the Initiative relative to
    funding sources and neighborhood focused strategies. The purpose of the Quality Neighborhoods Initiative is to create healthy
    neighborhoods with a broad range of housing options reflecting the demographic, ownership, and value ratios of the community.
    The philosophy of the initiative is that a strong core builds on strength and enhances the City's image. The five main guiding
    principles of the Initiative include:
                   6. Concentrate resources and target interventions to establish and encourage investment in the components that
                       make up a Quality Neighborhood.
                   7. Focus on creating communities of choice by promoting and supporting an environment of unique homes with
                       general market demand.
                   8. Identify and build on strengths by focusing on areas exhibiting a higher degree of Quality Neighborhood

                       the unique character and value of legacy neighborhoods.
                   9. Build confidence in the market by maintaining critical momentum through appropriate velocity and scale of
                       in vestments.
                   10. Overcome impediments to success by establishing a strong declared intent to the public that the City will take
                       steps necessary to assure quality projects are supported.
