20   Culinary Water
21       Installation of a reverse osmosis system on the City’s Green Well to improve water
22          quality that will meet or exceed the quality of water provided by the Jordan Valley Water
23          Conservancy District (JVWCD). Salt Lake County has signaled interest in contributing $3
24          million toward the project in exchange for a special water rate for the Riverbend Golf
25          Course. This investment, combined with a contribution of $650,000 from the City using
26          ARPA funds, will allow the City to re‐engage the Green Well. This innovative project
27          could supply nearly half of the City’s culinary water needs, reducing our dependency on
28          JVWCD water, and allowing us to keep culinary water rates low for the foreseeable
29          future.
30       The City has budgeted funds to replace aging water lines on 1830 West and 11800
31          South.
32
33   Pressurized Irrigation (Secondary Water)
34       $3 million ($1.5 million this year and $1.5 anticipated next year) of ARPA funds have
35         been budgeted to install secondary (pressurized irrigation) water meters on the
36         remaining two-thirds of connections without a meter. The City anticipates the State of
37         Utah paying for 70% (approximately $8.8 million) of the total cost of this project using
38         funds the Utah State Legislature set aside in House Bill 242 for this purpose. The City
39         has already made application for those state funds. It’s likely this project will span the
40         next two years.
41       Proposing the City make significant investments to improve pressurization and filtration
42         of the secondary irrigation water system. $850,000 has been budgeted for the addition of
43         a pump station, pump repairs, and improved filtration.
44       Currently in negotiations to purchase the balance of secondary water shares the City will
45         need through build‐out. Proposing paying for those shares over a 5‐year period, paying
46         20% each year starting in Fiscal Year 2022‐2023.

 8            Administrative Law Judge Discussion
 9
10   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, explained he would introduce the new administrative code that
11   would affect code enforcement violations. This code would provide one central location for the
12   City’s options for pursuing a code enforcement problem and would put an administrative law
13   judge in place to enable the City to streamline things for most types of cases.
14
15   Mr. Carter said the remedies are threefold: the ability to issue a civil citation and fine, the ability
16   to abate nuisances, and the ability to proceed in a classic fashion and have the case heard
17   before the Riverton Justice Court judge. Under these three approaches, the City will have
18   various options to go after code enforcement issues. The most common remedy would be to
19   issue a civil citation and have a person come before an administrative law judge which can
20   happen quickly.
21
22   Mr. Carter believes the City should step up the administrative enforcement of these issues,
23   abate the nuisances and assess liens on the property. He would like to bring this back for
24   approval at the next City Council meeting. The City would need to hire an administrative law
25   judge and he believes the code enforcement officers are capable of handling these cases. He
26   asked the Council to look at the fees which are similar to fees in surrounding cities.
27
28   Councilmember McDougal asked about the fee schedule and whether multiple fees would be
29   assessed if there are multiple charges. Mr. Carter responded that there are penalties for each
30   charge. However, he explained that the City would consider what it would take to abate the
31   problem and this is not a fixed fee. The civil fee is for administrative enforcement and then the
32   City would charge for actual costs to abate the problem.
33
34   Councilmember McCay asked how the new fee structure compares to the original fee structure.
35   Mr. Carter responded that there were no fees set out for administrative fines in the old code.
36   This is the first time that the City has had these fees and these fees were determined by looking
37   at surrounding cities. Mr. Carter explained that one of the provisions in the new code would
38   deny an applicant seeking development of a property if they have outstanding code
39   enforcement violations.

21            Ordinance No. 22-14 – Cardenas Rezone, Rezone of approximately 2.62-acres
22            located near 1768 West 13400 South from RR-22 to R-4. Applicant-Juan Cardenas,
23            with Del Sol Properties
24
25   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained this was a public hearing for a
26   Rezone application brought by the applicant for 2.62-acres located near 1768 West 13400
27   South. The application includes four (4) properties that form an “L”-like shape with frontage on
28   Redwood Road and on 13400 South. The applicant included a preliminary site plan which
29   shows the two existing homes facing Redwood Road remaining, while the underused rear
30   portions of the property would be accessed by a new private lane coming from 13400 South
31   Street. The preliminary site plan is only conceptual, and would be subject to further review,
32   applications and city approvals prior to the land being subdivided or any construction starting.
33
34   Mr. Lethbridge said the subject property is currently zoned “RR-22” which is a single-family
35   residential zone with half-acre lots and animal rights. All the adjoining properties are also zoned
36   RR-22, although many of them are less than half-acre, something that is common in older areas
37   of Riverton. The General Plan, which was adopted in August 2020, designates the entire area
38   as Medium Density Residential. This designation allows for 10,000-sf lots or larger, and the
39   requested R-4 Zone is consistent with the General Plan.
40
41   Josh Black, Civil Solutions Group, representing Juan Cardenas, explained that the rezone
42   extends to Redwood Road and includes the existing homes. Mr. Lethbridge agreed that the
43   rezone extends to Redwood Road.
44
45   Councilmember McCay asked if the 2.62 acres include the existing homes’ lots. Mr. Black
46   replied that it does include the existing homes and the new subdivision is about 1.75 acres.
47
48   Juan Cardenas, applicant, explained that they are trying to improve the neighborhood and make
49   it look better. He knows that lots are very expensive and he would like to be able to give his
50   children lots to build their own homes.
