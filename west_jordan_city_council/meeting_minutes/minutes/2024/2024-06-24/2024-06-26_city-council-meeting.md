 5. PUBLIC HEARINGS
       a. Ordinance No. 24-26 Copper Rim update to the Master Development Agreement and
          Major Variation to the Preliminary Development Plan located at approximately 7516
          South Copper Rim Drive.
          Matt Dean with CW Land Company, applicant, said he presented a proposed density
          reduction to the Committee of the Whole in 2023 that was not well received. He said the
          applicant had listened to feedback from the Committee, and said he believed his proposal
          that evening represented a much better land plan. Mr. Dean explained the proposed
          update to the Master Development Agreement (MDA) and variation to the Preliminary
          Development Plan (PDP) for Copper Rim:
             • The applicant seeks to remove the 204 age-restricted unit designation from
                 Copper Rim Phase 9. He said the applicant conducted several economic studies for
                 over a year and a half and found that age-restricted units were unfeasible
                 economically because of low rental rates and low for-sale prices. He said the “pool”
                 of renters and buyers for a 55-plus development was very shallow.
               •   The applicant seeks a major density reduction overall from 878 units to 731 units
                   (17% reduction from 2021 approval), resulting in approximately 3.5 density units
                   per acre overall. He emphasized that a major density reduction represented a
                   sizeable developer concession.
               •   The proposal would free up culinary water capacity back to the City (147 ERCs).
               •   The applicant proposes increased public open space (18.3%).
               •   The applicant proposes Phase boundary line adjustments and minor density shifts
                   for northern Copper Rim, providing two points of access for all phases and
                   accommodating correct lot widths.
               •   Preservation and protection of historic dry wash.
               •   The PDP update would clean up inconsistencies.
               •   The 2018 Phase 9 exterior loop road was not feasible. The applicant plans to move
                   the connection point to the east, creating safer intersection conditions.

           Senior Planner Ray McCandless believed the proposed plan had better connectivity and
           was a better land plan overall. Mr. McCandless noted that both staff and the Planning
           Commission recommended approval.

       d. Resolution No. 24-026 Enterprise Funds Transfer
          Ms. Steck said authority for an enterprise fund transfer was referenced in Utah Code 10-
          6-135.5. She explained what was involved in an enterprise fund transfer, and presented
          the proposed Enterprise Fund Transfer for FY 2025. A 3.5% property tax increase was
          proposed with the FY 2025 Budget. If the Enterprise Fund Transfer were removed from
          the FY 2025 Budget, an additional 13% property tax increase would be necessary.

           Ms. Steck explained and outlined allocated costs (reevaluated and adjusted annually):
              • Water Fund $3,641,885
              • Sewer Fund $1,368,915
              • Solid Waste Fund $751,594
              • Storm Water Fund $1,346,984
              • Total Admin & Overhead Cost $7,109,378

           Chair Jacob opened a public hearing at 7:56 pm.

           Comments:


City Council Meeting Minutes                Wednesday, June 26, 2024                           Page 7
           Chris McConnehey, West Jordan resident, was not a fan of the utility transfer and would
           rather see an increase in property taxes.

           Chair Jacob closed the public hearing at 7:58 pm.

           Vice Chair Lamb shared background of the Enterprise Fund Transfer in West Jordan, and
           said he had not realized when he voted in favor six years ago that the transfer would
           continue forever. Vice Chair Lamb said he wanted to find a way to be able to stop doing
           the transfer, and suggested raising rates in each fund. He said he would vote against the
           Enterprise Fund Transfer.

           Council Member Green said he agreed with Vice Chair Lamb, but suggested the only way
           to solve the problem would be to eliminate $2.5 million from the City budget. He said he
           would not vote to increase property taxes 13% in the meantime. Until difficult policy
           discussions took place regarding new-growth money and property tax increases, the
           Enterprise Fund Transfer was needed in the budget. Council Member Green said he felt
           the transfer as proposed was the fairest option for now.

           Council Member Shelton appreciated comments from Chris McConnehey and Vice Chair
           Lamb. He said he would vote in favor of the transfer as a method to collect compensation
           for services provided by the City to taxable and non-taxable entities. Council Member
           Shelton expressed the opinion that it was an ugly, shifty way to collect tax, and suggested
           in the future a way could be found to turn it around.

           Council Member Whitelock would continue to support the transfer. She said close to 25%
           of property in West Jordan did not pay property tax, but did use services provided by the
           City. She expressed the opinion that the transfer was a straight-forward tax that was more
           equitable to the residents.

           Chair Jacob believed all the Council comments were correct, with differences in
           philosophies. He said he was in favor of the Enterprise Fund Transfer.

           MOTION:      Council Member Shelton moved to APPROVE Resolution No. 24-026
