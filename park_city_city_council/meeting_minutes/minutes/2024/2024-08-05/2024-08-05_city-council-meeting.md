Regional Park and Ride Study:
Carl Miller, Summit County Transportation Director, and Alex Roy, Senior Transportation
Planner, presented this item. Miller indicated that a study was conducted last fall to
determine how park and ride lots in the community could be used more efficiently. He
reviewed the usage per lot and listed available spaces: Kimball Junction 39, Jeremy
Ranch 69, Ecker Hill 358, and Richardson Flat 742. Miller stated over 1,100 participants
responded to the park and ride survey, including residents, workforce, and skiers.
Amenities they wanted to see at the lots included shelters and restrooms.

Roy stated they looked at some alternatives for expansion and consolidations. The
distributed system would involve many small or medium-sized lots along the corridor.
The consolidated system would have fewer but larger lots at key gateway locations
along the corridor. The hybrid system would include characteristics of both the
distributed and consolidated systems unique to their travel origins. He proposed having
big lots at the SR248 and SR224 junctions and then smaller lots along the corridors.

Miller stated some things they could do to implement the plan included continuing to
collect data to ensure there was still demand for park and ride lots and making sure the
lots were flexible to accommodate events such as the Olympics. They would work with

Short-Term Rentals Study:
Jamie Johnson, Park City Board of Realtors CEO, presented this item. Johnson stated
she was part of the Western Mountain Resort Alliance. The report presented was a
breakdown of three different markets within the Alliance. She noted the study was
funded by the National Association of Realtors. Jack Greacen, AEG Policy Advisors in
Tucson, Arizona, stated the study needed to break down the metrics of the city,
economy, and affordability. The study did not include Summit County, Utah, or Park City,
but that was in the plan for the future. He stated Summit County had 22% nightly rentals
and vacation homes comprised 34% of the county. He indicated nightly rentals
generated jobs and had a large economic impact. There was a 51% occupancy rate for
nightly rentals in Park City.

Vice Chair Hanson was excused at 10:55 a.m.

Council Member Ciraco asked for the size of nightly rentals since the hotel fee versus
nightly rental fee was quite different. Greacen stated most nightly rentals were two or
three bedrooms.

Greacen stated the impact on the price of property had less than a 1% impact. He
stated if there weren’t short term rentals, there would still be empty homes in the
community. There wasn’t affordable housing in this community, but nightly rentals were
not the reason for this. It was the nature of a resort community. He noted regulations put
on nightly rentals reduced property values. He recommended that government be
cautious where it concerned nightly rentals.
