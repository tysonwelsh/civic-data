1. 3° Quarter Report from Salt Lake County Animal Services
Carrie Sibert highlighted the Outreach Department and said the third quarter features the
largest events. The quarter ended with a 95% save rate in Millcreek. They provided 212
microchips, 90 FVRCP, 119 Bordatella, 163 DHPP, and 126 rabies vaccines. They also sold
2,316 licenses. Average response times for priority calls were 23 minutes for priority 1 calls
32 minutes for priority 2 calls, and 61 minutes for priority 3 calls. Sibert went over the
statistics from the quarter which included 661 total calls. The employees of the month were
Megan Allred in July, Matt Assaf in August, and Kim Karren in September. She also
highlighted the volunteers of the month, Traci Harrison for July, Lindsey Wood for August,
and Ellen Grove for September. The biggest events of the year were the Spayghetti and No
Balls fundraiser and the Petapalooza adoption event. She said 918 animals were sent to
rescue that may not have had another option. Programs at the shelter include volunteer work
and the TNR feral cat program. The Mayor thanked her for her report.

3. First Reading
a. Rezone from R-2-10 to R-1-21 at 2254 Alva Circle

Blaine Gehring said the applicant, Laurel Osborn, and Wallace Fetzer own two lots at the
end of Alva Circle (Lots 8 and 9 of the Keller Circle Subdivision). They have added
additional property to both lots in the past and are now seeking to rezone two parcels to
the east and add them to their properties. The two parcels had duplexes on them which
have been demolished. In order for a lot line adjustment to occur to reconfigure and
enlarge Lots 8 and 9, the two former duplex lots need to be rezoned to R-1-21 to match
the zoning on the adjusted Lots 8 and 9. The East Mill Creek Community Council and
Planning Commission unanimously recommended approval of the rezone. Mayor
Silvestrini asked if the applicant had any comments, she said no. Council Member
Jackson asked about the public’s response. Gehring said he received two emails; one in
favor of the rezone and one against it. Council Member Uipi asked about the manure
odors referenced in the staff report. Gehring said the opposition to the rezone was for fear
of increasing the number of animals on the property, but that would not take place
because there is a conditional use permit for the property on the number of animals
allowed.

2.2 Discussion and Consideration of Ordinance 18-64, Amending Code Section
5.08.040 Adopting an Updated Fee Schedule to Provide that Rental of One or Two
Residential Dwellings will Pay a Reduced Business License Fee in the Amount of $60
per Year

Mayor Silvestrini said the Council has received concerns from Millcreek residents about
rental properties that are not properly maintained. The landlord either temporarily fixes
the problem but lets it lapse or does not address problems at all. The Council adopted an
amendment to the business licensing code to require a license for rentals. He said
surrounding cities require a license for rentals. This ordinance will give code enforcement
a tool to revoke the license if the landlord fails to keep the rental up to compliance with
code. He said the original fee was $120, the Council is now proposing to lower the fee to
$60 per unit.

Council Member Marchant moved to approve the rental fee on one or two residential
dwellings to $60 which is a reduction from what it has been. Council Member Jackson
seconded. Mayor Silvestrini asked for public comment. There were no comments. Council
Member Jackson said this fee was reasonable based on feedback received. The City Recorder
called for the vote. The motion passed unanimously.
