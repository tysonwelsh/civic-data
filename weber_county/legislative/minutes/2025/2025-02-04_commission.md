    2. APPROVE A CONTRACT WITH OGDEN CONTEMPORARY ARTS FOR CURATION AND
       OTHER ARTWORK RELATED SERVICES AT THE WEBER/MORGAN CHILDREN’S JUSTICE
       CENTER.
         Rod Layton (CJC Director): This is a big deal. We have done a lot of work on this project. I had a phone
         call with someone who, as a child, had been through the CJC years ago when they were nine years old. She
         mentioned the only things she remembered were wall paper and size of the building. She did not remember
         any of the people. Another person mentioned remembering the color of the roof and rooms. They did not
         remember anything about who worked the case or other details. I did not realize the importance of those
         details. One day I got a call from Scott Mendoza and he was fired up about artwork in the building. As you
         know, we have run over our budget due to soil issues. I’ve learned that the first thing you do is cut things
         like artwork. But we know those things matter. The artwork, the color on the walls will help reduce that
         trauma.

         Venessa Castagnoli (Ogden Contemporary Arts Executive Director): When Scott came to me for this project
         and gave me a tour of the building, I was in tears. What’s most important is how we are engaging with our
         community. We did a similar project for YCC. We try to make a space that is inclusive and inviting and a
         place where it is ok to share those feelings. We will fill this space with meaningful artwork. A selection
         committee will help us with these decisions during the curation process. After the artwork is done, we will
         continue to provide art projects for staff, parents, and children.

         Scott Mendoza (Operations): I recently had the pleasure to meet them. There was a presentation given by
         Chris Crockett and Lauren Thomas to the Board.

         Colby Dustin: My part time job for the last 13 years has been for the Friends Board of the CJC. This is a
         win/win... We spent a lot of money taking care of an old building. We look forward to spending a lot of
         money taking care of the program.

         Mr. Layton: Friends of CJC has always owned the buildings we have used. This is a transition to Weber
         County owning the building. This is a big deal as it allows our funding to go toward the program. Vanessa
         talked about doing projects for the staff. They see 4-5 interviews each day. They need things in place to
         keep them healthy. As for the contract, we thought it would be better to have the contract between Weber
         County and the OCA. Friends of CJC will provide the funding but managing a complex contract will be
         done by the County.

         Commissioner Harvey: For Mr. Hatch, when the Friends money comes into the County, it’s held in a special
         fund so they remain separate, correct?

         Ricky Hatch (County Clerk/Auditor): Correct, those funds are held separately, including the interest gained,
         to ensure those funds end up where intended.
