3, 2022 at 6:00 p.m. in the Herriman City Council Chambers, 5355 West Herriman Main Street, Herriman,

1. 6:00 PM - Work Meeting (Fort Herriman Conference Room)
Chair Berbert called the meeting to order at 6:01 p.m.

    1.1. Review of City Council Decisions - Michael Maloy, City Planner
Planning Manager Spencer informed the Commission that Commissioner Heather Garcia was approved as
a full member of the Commission, and no longer an alternate. Council also agreed to begin accepting
applications for alternate Commissioners. The Reserves at Sky Ranch, rezone and the General Plan were
discussed in the work meeting. The amendment to allow churches in the C-1 zone was discussed, but no
final decisions were made. The Council requested staff to conduct a thorough analysis of parking studies,
analysis, and standards. They requested PowerPoint presentations be removed from packets, to reduce

   1.2. Review of Agenda Items – Planning Staff
The Commissioners confirmed the applicant for item 4.1 was aware the City Council would review the item
and make the final decision regarding the rezone to C-1.

    1.3. Discussion of potential amendments to the Home Occupation Ordinance, which includes
         daycares and preschools, Planner I
Planner I Talia Wright reviewed the potential amendments to the home occupation ordinance, preschools
and daycares. The current code did not restrict the number of customers at one time, and defined group
instruction as three or more students. She requested feedback asking if the City should specify the number
of customers allowed. Recent applications have requested all adult classes, which would create more cars
parking in the area. Special home occupations in agricultural zones, allowed two additional employees, two
additional employees. Special home occupation on lots greater than one acre allowed four additional
employees. She asked if the city should change the allowed number of employees. She identified the
different home occupation groups. Recent problems included parking issues and provided examples of
onsite parking, which could cover up to twenty-five percent of the rear yard. One acre lots allowed up to
seventy percent of the entire lot for parking. Nearby city statistics were provided, generally one or two
employees were allowed. Commissioners discussed the appropriate yard locations for parking, allowable
vehicle totals, and potential street parking problems.

                              Commissioner Jacobson arrived at 6:24 p.m.

Commissioners debated acceptable levels of onsite and street parking. HPD Deputy Chief Cody Stromberg
added street parking was difficult to regulate and enforce. Commissioners cautioned against adding too
many restrictions, especially for those with no customers. They discussed allowing three additional
employees maximum with onsite parking.

    1.4. Discussion of conditional use standards, Planning Director Michael Maloy
City Planner Maloy informed the Commission that current standards were included in the packet. He
requested what kind detrimental impacts should be removed or added from the current list. Current
conditional use standards addressed noise, health concerns, and environmental issues amid others. City
Attorney Andrizzi defined standard suitability for use and stated codified standards were necessary for
enforcement. Commissioners were in favor of specific standards to address detrimental impacts.

The Commission adjourned the work meeting by consensus at 6:58 p.m.

    3.1. Request for Approval for the Conditional Use of a Soil Processing Operation for Huskie’z
         Landscaping, Inc at 16476 & 16536 S Redwood Road in the M-1 (Manufacturing) Zone.
         Applicant: Zachary Hinckley (owner)
         Acres: ± 2.84
         File Number: C2020-020
Planner Sheldon Howa presented the proposed conditional use request, which had been tabled by the
Commission at the January 6, 2022 meeting with a request for more information. He identified the location
of the soil processing operation. He stated no mining would take place, and contract drivers only would
access the location. The applicant would use the site for soil processing only, no buildings were planned,
and no retail customers would be onsite. The applicant would maintain a twenty-foot landscaping buffer
along MVC. The site had no utilities, and water tanks and/or trucks would be utilized for dust mitigation.
Additional water may be accessed from the Welby Jacobs canal. The applicant would provide portable
lavatories and hand washing stations. The applicant requested a waiver of strict compliance to reduce the
landscape requirement from fifteen to twelve and a half percent, which reduced the amount from 20,600
to 16,068 Square Feet (SF). UDOT had no issues with the applicant using the property but required
completion of the permitting process. They also required the access be widened from twenty-one to
twenty-five feet. The conditions of approval were reviewed. Commissioner Jacobson recommended the
unused area by the canal be left in a natural state, and allow native plants to count toward the landscaping
February 3, 2022 Planning Commission Meeting Minutes                               Page 4 of 6
requirement. Eliminating the need for the landscape reduction waiver. Further expansion would require
additional approval from the Commission.

Owner Zachary Hinckley approached the podium and offered to answer questions. He stated steep land
would be preserved in its natural state. Soil screening took place a couple days each week and would not
be done on windy days, to limit dust in the area. He offered the opinion the masonry wall was not
necessary, due to the elevation, but they would comply with all Commission requirements.

Commissioners recommended preserving the native landscape on the west side of the canal and allowing
native plant areas count towards the landscape requirement. Storage wall materials and locations were
debated. City Attorney Andrizzi stated the Commissioners should identify impacts from the conditional use
and the most reasonable solutions to mitigate the detrimental impacts. Commissioners debated the
fencing requirements, and agreed a masonry wall should only be required on the east and south sides of
the storage unit. They agreed chain link fencing would be adequate on the west side.

Requirements:
   1. Receive and agree to all recommendations from other agencies.
   2. The use shall be operated in a manner that eliminates unnecessary dust, noise, and odor, including,
       but not limited to, covering trucks, hoppers, chutes, loading and unloading devices and mixing
       operations, and maintaining driveways and parking areas free of dust. This shall also include having
       a water truck/storage permanently on the site.
   3. The site shall not be mined, operations limited to import/export of materials.
   4. Provide an access agreement from UDOT for access from Redwood Road and comply with all
       applicable regulations and requirements for improvements. Which shall include permitting and
       widening the throat of the access to twenty-five (25) feet.
   5. Six (6) foot masonry wall required around all storage areas, including material bins, with the
       exception of the west fencing, which may be slatted chain-link.
   6. Overall site landscaping shall be installed at a minimum of twelve and one half (12.5) percent and
       comply with Herriman City’s Water Efficiency Standards. Existing native vegetation shall be preserved.
   7. Hours of operation are limited to 7:00 am to 6:00 pm, Monday through Saturday.
   8. No signs approved with this request.


Commissioner Powell MOVED to approve item 3.1 Request for Approval for the Conditional Use of a Soil
Processing Operation for Huskie’z Landscaping, Inc at 16476 & 16536 S Redwood Road in the M-1
(Manufacturing) Zone, with eight staff recommendations and amendments to # 5 to include the language
- except adjacent to the canal/west side, which may be chain link, and add to #6 the overall site landscaping
shall be a minimum of 12.5 percent, and there would be a preservation of the existing, natural topography
and vegetation west of the canal, and that would be the condition to allow this variance. Commissioner
Jacobson SECONDED the motion.
