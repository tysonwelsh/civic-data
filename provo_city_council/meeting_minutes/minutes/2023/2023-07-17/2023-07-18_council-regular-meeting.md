Aaron Ardmore, Planning Supervisor, presented. He provided a quick overview of the property and
proposed project.

Councilor Handley asked if Mr. Ardmore could provide the updates that were added to the development
agreement. He also asked why the changes did not persuade the staff to change their opinion.

Mr. Ardmore said the staff does not believe this project complies with the general plan. He then
discussed the revised development agreement.

Councilor Handley asked why there is concern over the boundary lines.

Mr. Ardmore responded that staff felt like in this case it was too far of a change, as it would essentially
double what was imagined for this corner. He added that there are additional concerns over not being
able to guarantee owner occupancy.

Councilor Ellsworth asked if the desire for this mixed used was to accommodate affordable housing. She
said that density typically yields more affordable housing.

Mr. Ardmore said that he believes it is a desire for housing on the west side where there is not a lot of
growth happening. He added that the market right now is not affordable at all but without some kind of
guaranteed deed restrictions, staff does not think the density would help with affordability.

Chair MacKay invited someone from Public Works to address the sewer capacity on the west side.

Gary Calder, Director of Water Resources, responded. He said in 2017, the process began for designing
the West Side sewer, and by 2018, Horrocks Engineering had produced a comprehensive 300-page
document for state approval. The initial plan was for four units per acre, but upon the Council’s
suggestion, it was revised to allow a maximum rezoning of six units per acre. This influenced the sewer
trunk line design to accommodate up to six units per acre. He added that he still prefers the original four
units per acre due to safety considerations. He also explained the west side faces issues of infiltration
due to high groundwater, and the likelihood of groundwater entering the sewer line would increase
over time. While not an immediate problem for the new sewer line, it would become one eventually. He
said increasing zoning in the area would lead to reallocating zoning from other locations, implying
potential denials. The design of the sewer system had factored in these considerations for the west
side's future. With significant work and financial investment, including an upcoming lift station, there
was not a feasible way to revert these changes unless a new design was proposed.

Councilor Whipple said there are competing factors to preserve open space and add affordable housing.
She said she believes by increasing density in some areas, it prevents being able to build on others which
accomplishes both goals.

Mr. Calder added that changing the density of the area would significantly impact the sewer system,
even though it's a substantial line. He said this could lead to a situation like the past few years where
we've had to advise people to wait while constructing the trunk line.

Chair MacKay said she fears there will be issues with parking.

Chair MacKay invited the developer to present.

Steve Turley discussed why he feels like his project complies with the general plan highlighting sections
of the Southwest Area Plan and the Southwest Neighborhoods Plan. He also addressed concerns that
were brought up regarding parking.

Councilor Whipple asked why owner occupation requirements were not included in the plan.

Mr. Turley said he was not able to get funding for the project if owner occupancy was required.

Jordi Barrett, of Hales Engineering, was invited to answer questions related to parking.

Chair MacKay opened the item for public comment.

Becky Bogdin, of Provo, noted that even established designations could change, referring to Geneva
Road's future as a five-lane highway. Ms. Bogdin cautioned against assuming a permanent status for
streets, as the state could potentially take control for connectivity purposes. She mentioned limited
sewer connections were a concern, and she posed a scenario wherein allocating these connections for
increased density without proper justification could lead to issues. Ms. Bogdin also pointed out that the
commercial space might not be available immediately on the west side, where commuting is common.
She questioned the feasibility of expecting only two drivers per apartment complex in such a setting.

Tanner Bennett, of Provo, expressed his support for the project, especially the increase in high-density
housing on the west side. As someone planning to remain in Provo after graduating from BYU, he found
projects like this appealing. He highlighted the project's consideration for parking and its potential
transformation into a town center, indicating that substantial thought had gone into the planning. He
recognized the rising demand for affordable housing, particularly among students. He wondered if
measures could be taken to prevent the property from being sold to investors, given the affordability
crisis Provo was facing. He proposed the idea of forming a homeowner’s association (HOA) that would
restrict unit sales to a single entity or individual. He said he believes this might prevent the apartments
from being sold to entities that could further escalate rates, particularly for students and others in
similar circumstances.

Rick Cox, of Provo, shared his support for the project and said there needs to be more dense
development on the west side to bring in better services.

Dave Lewis, of Provo, echoed the concerns of Councilor Shipley regarding parking. He shared his overall
support for the project.

Councilor Hoban thanked the development team for their dedication to this project. He shared the
reasons why he is not in support of this project as is including owner occupancy and parking. He said he
struggles to overlook the staff's evident concerns regarding the issue at hand. While he acknowledges
the proposal's favorable aspects, he also acknowledges the presence of potential risks. He emphasized
the significance of the staff's recommendation in his decision-making process.

Councilor Ellsworth pointed out the frequent dismissal of comparable projects and recognized the west
side's longing for a grocery store, underlining the project's closeness to a potential site. While she
inclined towards the staff's suggestion, she also expressed reservations regarding moving the goal posts.
She stated that Councilor Whipple's viewpoint on mixed-use resonated with her, particularly in less
developed areas like this in the city. She said she hopes to find a solution because this project could
bring diversity, affordability, and commercial possibilities.

Councilor Handley found it questionable to outright reject the project. He noted the absence of a
guarantee for the desired density in the broader area. He recalled his initial concern about lacking
commercial centers in the West Side plan. He emphasized the importance of creating such centers to
prevent residents from constantly crossing the city for services. He underscored the importance of
keeping people local by offering essential services nearby. He said he was not fully convinced that the
project posed a significant risk, considering the designated density for the area and the uncertainties
about achieving it elsewhere. He felt comfortable with allowing an extra acre for the project and was
willing to support it, deeming the potential rewards to outweigh the perceived low risk.

Councilor Fillmore expressed gratitude for the insightful discussion and thoughtful contributions from
staff and the council. He acknowledged Mr. Turley's efforts to address the council's concerns from the
previous meeting and appreciated the progress made, though he believes there is still room for further
improvement. He mentioned his liking for the commercial mixed-use concept on the west side to add
density. He shared his concerns about parking, safety on Geneva, sewer capacity, and staff reservations

10.         A resolution instituting a Fire Restriction Order. (23-052) (00:36:47)

       Motion:      An implied motion to approve Resolution 2023-32, as currently constituted, has been
                    made by council rule.

Brian Jones, City Attorney, presented. He explained the issuance of a fire restriction order. He noted that
this year’s wet winter had delayed the implementation of this order, but the subsequent growth in the
foothills during the wet period has led to potential hazards as the region has dried out during the hot
summer. He said the restriction, beginning on Thursday, effectively bans fires outside of designated fire
rings or pits in improved campgrounds and residential fire pits in the foothill area. He said this measure
aims to empower the fire department in preventing wildfires and the resulting damage.

Councilor Ellsworth shared her appreciation for the Fire Department. She added that this restriction will
keep a lot of homes safe.

With no other council discussion, Chair MacKay called for a vote.

       Vote:        The motion was approved 6:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Handley, Hoban, MacKay,
                    Shipley, and Whipple in favor. Councilor Fillmore excused.

12.         A resolution amending the bylaws of the Redevelopment Agency of Provo City Corporation
            (23-045) (00:40:23)

Brian Jones, City Attorney, presented. He explained that the need for changes to the RDA bylaws arose
due to outdated address details following the move to the new city hall. In a prior board meeting, a
motion had been passed to remove the section concerning the executive committee. Seizing the
opportunity, the staff conducted a comprehensive review of the bylaws. They eliminated outdated
language, aligned certain aspects with regular Council practices—such as agenda item inclusion—and
made other minor adjustments. Mr. Jones characterized the changes as not extensive, but they
encompassed a thorough revision of the bylaws, with the updated version provided to the members.

Board Member MacKay asked about the historical aspect of the RDA, where the leadership used to align
between the council and the RDA. She said it seems there could be benefits to reverting to that
arrangement, possibly starting next year rather than immediately.

Chair Ellsworth said it could be in the bylaws, but it does not have to be. She said the council and RDA
board could vote for the same leaders if they wanted to. She added that there were many things that
the board proposed to be added to the bylaws, but they were not written per the advice of legal. She
noted that there are benefits to keeping them separate.

Chair Ellsworth asked for a motion on this resolution.

       Motion:      Board Member Whipple made a motion to approve the resolution as written. Board
                    Member Handley seconded.

Chair Ellsworth called for a vote.

       Vote:        The motion was approved 6:0 with Board Members Ellsworth, Handley, Hoban,
                    MacKay, Shipley, and Whipple in favor. Board Member Fillmore excused.
