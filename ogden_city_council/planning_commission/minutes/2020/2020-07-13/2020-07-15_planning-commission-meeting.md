1. Discussion, Pending Ordinance: Apartments and Group Dwellings in Commercial zones

  Mr. Montgomery stated the Commission and Staff are in the process of developing language to
  address multi-family uses in commercial zones due to the pending ordinance restricting new
  development. He stated there are now approximately 1,800 units either under construction or with
  valid building permits in commercial zones. A new ordinance is being considered which would identify
  what regulations are appropriate for these uses, as well as to identify areas where multi-family uses
  are or are not appropriate. While it is acknowledged there is more than adequate commercially zoned
  property, some areas are better suited for commercial use rather than multi-family. This discussion is
  to consider how to identify what commercial areas should contain commercial uses as well as what
  areas might better be suited for apartments. He stated he has had some preliminary discussions with
  Bill Knowlton, and invited him to address the Commission from a developer’s perspective.

  Mr. Bill Knowlton stated he had begun meeting with City Staff in June 2019 anticipating a multi-family
  residential project on Washington Boulevard and anticipates submitting an application in August. He
  stated he is optimistic about the future of Ogden being a good location where residents can live, work
  and play.

  He introduced his development company and stated they have developed approximately 6,300
  affordable housing units, consisting of both moderate and low income units using tax credits available.
  Most projects have a mix of income levels, with some units being market rate and others identified as
  affordable. The company purchases property, develops projects and maintains them over a period of
  time rather than flipping them to a second or third party.
He stated developers are looking for the highest and best use for property, considering first if a project
is physically possible, legally permissible, and then financially feasible. Consideration also is given to
its size, shape topography. He stated when considering whether legally permitted, consideration is
given to its current zoning as well as potential zoning and to what may be permitted. Then they
determine whether the cost of development justifies the risk.

He identified existing housing projects in Murray, Midvale and Layton, indicating the Ogden project is
anticipated to be a four-story affordable housing project market to seniors over the age of 62. In
considering location, a driving force is required parking standards and transportation options, with
other consideration to magnets such as schools, employment, connection to surrounding
neighborhoods and existing infrastructure, as well as nearby amenities such as parks, recreational
centers, sporting events, library, theater, etc. He stated factors for a successful project are often
based on market studies, identifying whether there are sufficient people seeking rental units in order
to cover the development cost, and indicated one of the previous projects were completely leased
within ten days of their opening. He stated his company develops Class A properties, with amenities
available such as a pool, gym and spa and a mix of market rate and affordable units are needed. In
obtaining financing, most investors require a market study to identify the lowest cost per unit in
relation to both the cost of the land and the cost of development. He stated the cost to develop a
parking structure is often less desirable than surface parking, but more property is needed, which
might then increase the cost.

Developers then determine the approval process for projects and the certainty of government
regulation, avoiding conditional use requirements as well as form-based regulations, indicating public
meetings often add time to the approval process which adds to the cost. He also stated construction
costs have escalated during the past few years. Other factors to attract tenants include proximity to
activities, crime rates, security, quality of development, on-site amenities and proximity to transit and
job opportunities. Tenants are then retained by a well-built and well maintained project that is designed
with a logical layout, with quality building materials, good lighting and ventilation, soundproofed walls
and quality work in cabinetry and plumbing. Amenities are designed for the target tenant, with
elevators in each project which is more than one story in height. He stated elevators are oversized in
order to accommodate furniture being moved. Management teams are well trained and consideration
given to the aesthetics of a location, including having a pleasing appearance, odors and limited sound
interruption for tenants.

He then described types of buildings, stating most are done in a wrap-around design, allowing open
patio space to be available as a courtyard. These often have parking built on the lower levels with
apartments wrapped around the parking targeted for high density. The podium design may contain
one to two levels of parking with units above the parking, or surface parking with one building designed
for high density. The garden style is often limited to three stories and has interior courtyards and open
breezeway access. Buildings are designed based on the target demographics and market support.

Chair Southwick asked Mr. Knowlton to define what is considered affordable housing and whether
government subsidies are used. Mr. Knowlton stated his market depends on low income tax credits
and not typical government subsidies. He stated the most desirable amenities include a clubhouse,
 meeting space, fitness center, computer business center, wifi, balconies and natural lighting, dog
 parks, open space courtyards and a 24/7 mail center where packages can be delivered. Challenges
 include property entitlements, changing standards and limited government regulation. He agreed
 Ogden’s standards for development should be improved with uniform development standards for
 market and affordable development projects, indicating creating a green community with energy star
 certificate requires a better finished project which will stand the test of time. He encouraged the City
 to consider enterprise green communities as a standard for development indicated buildings would
 then be better constructed and better managed.

 Mr. Steve Graham stated the key to longevity is maintenance and good management. He stated
 developers often build a project and then leave the management to the tenants or third party which
 may or may not be effective. He stated when a property is sold time and again, it affects the tenants,
 and their quality of life. He suggested apartments might have a better longevity if there were a
 requirement for a 50-year land use requirement by requiring ongoing maintenance to equal that of the
 original as-built standard. He discussed financing, stating rent needs to be sustainable, and the model
 of a mix of incomes utilizing Utah Housing tax credit programs provides a means to subsidize the
 lower rents as well as maintaining quality units. He detailed the tax credit program and how it works
 with market rate rents. He felt local governments should create a partnership with developers, and
 assure the rules do not change, indicating regulations increase the price of development.

 Commissioner Safsten asked how parking needs are assessed. Mr. Knowlton responded reduction of
 parking is most often based on the availability of mass transit as well as the ability of transit to get to
 where jobs are. Mr. Hooper Knowlton stated the target market for the project is also a consideration
 as student housing would typically each own a vehicle while seniors may be more able to share one
 car or depend on mass transit. He stated the ability to provide adequate parking often affect the
 financing of a project. He stated the accommodation for cars is often viewed as more important than
 that of people.

 Mr. Bill Knowlton stated the Ogden Washington project is anticipated to create 82 jobs and will source
 local subcontractors as well as local maintenance personnel.

 Commission thanked Mr. Knowlton and his team for the information provided, indicating it will be
 helpful in crafting an ordinance.

2. Discussion, Make Ogden CBD Plan

 Mr. Montgomery stated this discussion is to review the relevancy of the goals and strategies for the
 Design Guidelines in the Make Ogden document. He asked the Commission to determine whether
 proposed goals make sense, indicating while the consultant has many good ideas, the Commission
 is more familiar with Ogden and he wants to make sure the goals make sense and whether the
 allotted time frame is realistic, indicating there may be some which would require partnerships with
 other entities.

 Commissioners suggested some objectives may not be realistic, suggesting parameters might be
 given for progress towards a particular goal. It was felt standards should be in the Ordinance, and
  should be simple and understandable. Standards should be stated up front and not added at the end
  of the review process. Commission expressed concern about the recommendation about another
  layer of review for historic buildings and felt this should be left to the Landmarks Commission. It was
  indicated the document recommends wayfinding signs which already has begun, and reduced fares
  for mass transit, which is already free in the downtown circulator and is anticipated to be free for the
  first few years of operation of the BRT line. Commission felt it is more important to finish what has
  been started rather than to identify new projects. Commission expressed the document suggests to
  Historic 25th Street and questioned why and how. It also recommends adopting an open space plan
  which already exists which also implements the parks and recreation master plan. Commission also
  discussed the recommendation of relocating the FrontRunner Station to Union Station, indicating
  this should be done as a single overall project for the area surrounding Union Station to also include
  the connection of the 24th Street Viaduct with Wall Avenue.

There being no additional business before the Commission, the meeting was adjourned at 7:45pm.
