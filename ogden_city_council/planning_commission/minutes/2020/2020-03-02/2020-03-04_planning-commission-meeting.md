  Mr. AJ Pepper, representing Eco-Site, stated while the original monopole was approved as a stealth
  tower, it was built with an array and Staff had contacted them and given options to either reduce the
  bracket length and request an amended conditional use permit or to reconstruct the pole as it was
  approved. He stated the pole has now been corrected by three arrays with shorter brackets and
  antennas are closer together and is requesting it be approved. He clarified it is a slimline pole.

  Mr. Troy Benson, representing Verizon, stated the proposal is for a co-location at the 45’ height on
  the existing monopole by installing 12 8’ antennas and 12 radio heads to be mounted behind the
  antennas. All ground equipment will be accommodated within the existing compound.

  Mr. Stratford clarified the pole had been approved and when Verizon approached Staff, their review
  indicated the construction of the pole differed from what was approved. Eco-Site has addressed



                                                   3
changes made to bring the pole into compliance with its initial approval, and the application at this
time is only for the new co-location for Verizon.

Ms. Olson stated the property is in the R-2EC residential zone and the main concern is the visual
appearance of the pole to the surrounding residential community. She reviewed the history of the
pole, stating approval had ben given in November 2018 for a 60’ slimline monopole with antennas to
be mounted close to the pole with no brackets. Permits were then issued in March 2019 and the
pole was constructed. In October 2019 Staff found the constructed pole was not the slimline design
with stealth antennas as was approved. Since that time, efforts have been made to bring the pole
into compliance with the approved design. In February 2020 Eco-Site proposed to remove the
bracket and to reduce the arm length of the antennas and bringing them close together. Verizon
now desires to locate on the existing monopole with antenna support arms, and equipment cabinet.

She reviewed the conditional use standards indicating as the pole is in place, the installation of the
new antennas will have little impact to surrounding land uses. Because of the residential area, there
is some visual impact created by the new use, but it will provide a needed service for the
surrounding community by creating better coverage. She suggested the array brackets be reduced
in length to match the existing array and the number of panels be reduced from 12 to 9. Staff
recommendation is for approval for the installation of nine 8’ antennas, removal of the 4’ microwave
dishes and the brackets be reduced to 6’. She stated while the co-location satisfies the land use
ordinance as it uses an existing structure, Staff is concerned about its visual appearance in this
residential neighborhood and has suggested conditions of approval to mitigate those visual
impacts.

Commissioner Blaisdell asked if there will be an opportunity for a third carrier to utilize space on this
monopole. Ms. Olson responded the initial tower approval indicated it would accommodate up to
three carriers. She clarified her recommendation is for approval as submitted, subject to reducing
visual impact by reducing the arm length to 6’, reducing the number of antennas to 9 and to remove
the microwave dishes.

Chair Southwick asked if there is concern about the diesel generator being so close to the school.
Ms. Olson responded the generator would be used only during power outages and should not affect
the daily workings of the school. Mr. Benson stated it would be tested monthly and there are
additional sound barriers within the enclosure.

4. CBDI Site Plan, for new mixed-use building at 144 Historic 25th Street. (Attachment E)

  Mr. Pierre Langue stated this request is for a new mixed-use building in the historic district which
  will accommodate 49 residential units at market rate prices. The ground floor would provide 3,000
  square feet of commercial space. He stated the design of the structure is to fit with the existing
  historic district by providing three stories along the 25th Street frontage and additional two stories to
  be stepped back from the street frontage. He stated the building would create more activity in the
  downtown by adding both residential and commercial space.

  Mr. Spencer stated the design of the building had previously been approved by the Landmarks
  Commission and it is before the Planning Commission to determine whether it fits with the
  character of the downtown as well as to consider whether required parking can be waived or
  reduced. The property is now vacant and the request is for the construction of new commercial
  space with apartments on the upper levels which would encompass the entire site, leaving no
  parking available for tenants or for the users of commercial space within the building. The applicant
  is seeking for a waiver of parking cue to existing parking on nearby properties. The design also
  accommodates a walkway for pedestrian access between 25th Street and the parking area in the
  rear.

  He stated the use is compliant with the CBD Plan as it adds population and vitality to the downtown.
  The proposed walkway encouraged pedestrian activity and provides a connection between the
  Street and the rear ally. Staff is recommending the applicant work with Staff to create a better alley
  deign and adding landscaping features. He stated while there is some ongoing discussion a new
  parking structure may be added to this block, the request for waiver of on-site parking is to be based
  on existing conditions. He reviewed findings which would allow the Commission to approve a
  parking waiver.

  He then reviewed the proposed architecture and stated the step-back of the upper two stories
  reduces the impact to the street frontage. He stated while the Landmarks had approved the design
  to be historically compatible with the District, they had directed the applicant to work with Staff in
  the selection of a brick color, indicating he favored the more traditional brick color. He stated the
  proposed building height is within the standards of the 25th Street District and the CBDI zone for 25th
  Street. He stated both front and rear entrances should have a covered entry which could be
  accommodated by adding a roof feature or by indent in the entrances to create a covered entry. He
  also suggested windows be added to the west side of the building in order to add variety to the
  walkway.

  He stated traffic and parking will need to be addressed stating the proposed 49 units creates a need
  for a minimum of 74 parking spaces. He stated there are 239 existing parking space on the north
  side of he uses, which accommodates both 25th Street and other use within the block. He stated
  there is a greater demand for the use of this parking area during evening hours, which is likely when
  most tenants would also need parking. The Ordinance allows a parking waiver or reduction upon
  Planning Commission approval if it is found parking is available within a 500’ radius, the reduction

5. Proposed Expansion of Nonconforming Use to allow drive-thru window or parking in setback for
   existing market at 809 28th Street (Attachment F)

  Mr. Brent Bateman, representing Stop & Go Market, stated in December 2019 the Commission had
  reviewed the existing nonconforming use and had revoked the approval of its expansion as
  requested. The conditions attached at that time would likely cause the business to shut down due
  to reduced income. Since that time, he has met with City Staff and has now made an application
  which would satisfy many of the City’s requirements while allowing the business to stay open. The
  applicant has proposed two options for consideration by the Commission. Option 1 would retain the
  drive-thru window and add landscaping and on-site maneuvering as recommended by Staff. Option
  2 would remove the drive-thru window but would add parking as well as proposed landscaping. He
  stated the owner desires to retain the drive-thru window and prefers Option 1. The owner is willing
  to upgrade the site with landscaping, to move the dumpster and to contain lighting to the site as
  well as to change the ingress/egress patterns as recommended by the City.

  Mr. Simpson stated the request is for the expansion of a nonconforming use of a commercial
  building in a residential zone. He reviewed the history of the property stating it was used as an
  accessory use to a gas station when zoning was adopted in Ogden City in 1951. In 1971 a change
  of use was approved to allow the demolition of an adjacent house and the construction of a car
  wash. Over time it became a two-bay car wash with a gas station, and then in 1995 approval was
  given by the Planning Commission to enclose one car wash. Although the request at that time
  spoke of a drive-up window, it was not shown on the site plan and it is unclear as to whether the
  drive-thru window was approved or not. Several conditions of approval were attached at that time,
  many of which have not been enforced. Since that time, the additional car wash bay has been
  enclosed without permits and violations have been identified. He clarified the previous action of the
  December action which revoked the 1995 expansion approval and then a new site plan was
  proposed which eliminated the drive-thru window, added parking, and limited hours of operation. A
  time period was added to bring the property into compliance as June 1, 2020.




                                                  7
He reviewed the application now before the Commission, stating the first option includes the drive-
thru window, widening the approach on Monroe increasing hours of operation and the time period
for improvements to be completed by August 31, 2020. The second option is to increase on-site
parking to seven stalls and would close the drive-thru window but again extend the hours of
operation an hour beyond that requested for Option 1.

He reviewed recommendations of the City Engineering Division relating to access onto Monroe in
light of the anticipated addition of bicycle lanes as well as on-site stacking regulations. He reviewed
other commercial markets in residential areas, showing the availability of parking, drive-thru
windows and stacking of vehicles. Other departmental concerns relate to the enclosure of the
second car wash bay without permits or inspections and whether it may be unsafe. The impact of
drive-thru traffic to the surrounding neighborhood had been identified as a concern when
considering the effect of the use to the neighborhood.

expanded. These include whether new nonconformities are created, whether it meets the standards

7. Consideration of Capital Improvement Plan 2020-2025 (Attachment H)

  Mr. Montgomery stated the Capital Improvement Plan is reviewed annually by the Planning
  Commission to assure its compliance with the General Plan. He explained the CIP is the process of
  identifying the use of City funds for both repairs and upkeep of existing assets as well as potential
  new projects. It is a means to prioritize where the money is spent as well as implementing the
  General Plan. Each City department defines their needs, and these are reviewed and prioritized by
  the City administration. The review of the Commission is to determine its consistency with the
  General Plan, and if not consistent, items cannot be funded. The Planning Commission is not to
  authorize projects but may ask the Council to consider other items which might have been identified
  in the planning process. While it is yet unknown what amount of funding will be available, the CIP is
  a ranking of items which the administration is most important for the use of potential funds. The
  Commission has no authority to reallocate funding proposed.
