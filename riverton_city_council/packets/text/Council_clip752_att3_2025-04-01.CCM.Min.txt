37          Business License Code
38
39   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, explained that the changes to the business license ordinance focus
40   on modernizing the application process without making substantive policy changes. The
41   revisions aim to streamline procedures, require online applications, and close loopholes—such
42   as businesses applying under alternate DBAs—to ensure more accurate information is
43   collected. He emphasized this is a practical update intended to improve efficiency within the
44   business license division.
45
46   Mr. Carter highlighted that much of the current update involves removing outdated and unused
47   portions of the business license fee schedule by deferring entirely to the annually adopted City
48   fee The alcohol licensing section has been cleaned up by removing duplicative references to
49   state law, which posed a risk if state laws changed. The City will now simply require proof of
50   state compliance for off-premise and restaurant liquor licenses, which are the only two
51   categories being emphasized. Provisions the City never had the resources to enforce, such as

 1   certain proximity rules or extra enforcement measures, have been removed.

 3             Animal Code
 4
 5   Mr. Carter explained that the proposed changes to the Animal Control code address
 6   longstanding enforcement challenges and modernize procedures to improve public safety. A
 7   key issue was overlapping administrative processes and unclear authority when animals
 8   attacked people or were involved in rabies-related impounds. These situations were
 9   complicated by legal ambiguities, especially regarding the seizure and holding of animals
10   pending trial. To resolve this, the updated code simplifies seizure authority by explicitly allowing
11   animal control officers to seize animals involved in attacks and, if owners do not cooperate, to
12   apply for a search warrant.
13
14   Another significant update clarifies that animal owners are liable when their animals attack or
15   are at large, even if someone else had custody of the animal at the time. The definition of
16   “animal under restraint” was also revised. Previously, vague language allowed owners to argue
17   that radio shock collars qualified as restraint. The revised definition eliminates this loophole,
18   requiring physical leashes or containment. Radio collars are no longer accepted, as they are
19   often unreliable and create public perception and safety issues. This policy change is supported
20   by Animal Control staff and the Police Chief.
21
22   Mayor Staggs asked for clarification regarding whether animal control officers have discretion in
23   impounding a dog after an attack, referencing a previous Council decision that may have
24   mandated impoundment after a single incident.
25
26   Mr. Carter responded by citing the current language, which states that an animal that bites or
27   attacks a person or another animal may be seized by the department without a court order. If
28   the animal control officer is unable to obtain the animal voluntarily, they shall pursue a search
29   warrant. He explained that recent complications in a municipal court case—such as confusion
30   over notice to the owner and requirements for written orders—led the City to shift its approach.
31   The City decided to standardize the process by using search warrants for seizure, which is a
32   routine legal mechanism already used for other types of evidence.
33
34   Mr. Carter emphasized that the City has the capacity to care for animals while a case is pending
35   and stated that allowing the return of an animal that has already attacked could present a public
36   safety risk. The decision to keep the code language as “may” rather than “shall” was to allow
37   discretion in cases where the injury was minor or not immediately apparent. He indicated that
38   discretion should depend on the severity of the incident and the determination of the animal
39   control officer. The primary duties of animal control include verifying rabies vaccinations and
40   determining if there is an ongoing threat.

 7             Ordinance No. 25-08 – Amending Section 18.90.010 (Table of Commercial Users)
 8             clarifying allowed commercial uses, Amendments proposed by Riverton City
 9
10   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, discussed updates to the table of
11   commercial uses, which aimed to clarify and better define certain categories without significantly
12   changing permitted uses. Key updates included defining assisted living facilities, nursing homes,
13   and residential care facilities as conditional uses in all commercial zones; creating a new
14   category for building trades (such as electricians, plumbers, and HVAC) to address recent
15   questions, with restrictions on vehicle and equipment storage; explicitly including dance studios,
16   gyms, and martial arts studios; and clarifying the definition of bus terminals to differentiate them
17   from bus stops. These changes were recommended for approval by the Planning Commission.

31             Ordinance No. 25-10 – Amending Riverton City Code Title 6, Chapter 5 to Revise
32             Administrative Provisions of the Animal Control Ordinance, Amendments
33             Proposed by Riverton City
34
35   Mayor Staggs allowed citizen comment on this discussion/action item:
36
37   Jill Walker raised concerns about the proposed animal control ordinance, particularly the
38   provision allowing someone to kill a dog if they reasonably feel threatened, noting the inclusion
39   of barking, growling, or baring teeth as justification and questioning why Riverton’s code would
40   expand beyond what Herriman and South Jordan allow. She also noted that Riverton’s three-
41   day hold period is shorter than South Jordan and Herriman's five-day policy and argued that five
42   days is more reasonable for owners to locate lost pets. Lastly, she requested clarity on who is
43   held criminally responsible when a dog is loose—the person who let the dog out or the
44   registered owner—and suggested that "dog at large" should be treated as an infraction, in line
45   with 2022 Utah legislation.
46
47   Shauna Walker expressed strong concerns about the proposed changes to the code,
48   particularly regarding provisions that allow someone to kill a dog if they feel threatened. She
49   cited the Fifth Amendment and the right to due process, arguing that allowing a person to kill a
50   dog based on subjective fear—such as barking, growling, or baring teeth—effectively makes
51   them judge, jury, and executioner, and violates constitutional protections.
     Page | 6 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1
 2   Councilmember Pierucci voiced concern about the proposed language in the animal control
 3   ordinance that would allow someone to kill a dog if they feel threatened. While he acknowledged
 4   the intent—to permit self-defense during an actual attack—he felt the current wording may go
 5   too far, particularly by including behaviors like repeated barking, growling, or baring teeth as
 6   justification. He suggested revising the language to narrow the scope strictly to situations
 7   involving protection of life.
 8
 9   Mr. Carter clarified that the proposed ordinance change includes a reasonableness standard,
10   which would prevent misuse of the provision allowing someone to kill a dog if they feel
11   threatened. He explained that simply barking behind a fence would not legally justify lethal
12   action, as a reasonable person would not perceive that as an imminent threat. He emphasized
13   that the ordinance aims to address those rare but serious circumstances and aligns with
14   broader legal standards for self-defense. He stressed that this ordinance does not significantly
15   change legal protections but provides clarity for edge cases where immediate defensive action
16   might be necessary.
17
18   Councilmember McCay expressed appreciation for residents who shared their concerns and
19   echoed those concerns regarding the proposed language allowing for the killing of a dog under
20   certain circumstances. She also questioned the current three-day hold for impounded animals,
21   suggesting it be extended to five days to align with neighboring cities and provide owners a fair
22   chance to recover their pets, particularly given the emotional attachment many have to their
23   animals.
24
25   Councilmember McDougal argued in favor of the proposed ordinance language, emphasizing
26   that it is a protection for individuals to act in self-defense before an attack occurs. He stressed
27   that no person, including children, should have to wait to be harmed before defending
28   themselves, and likened the provision to existing self-defense laws for human threats. He
29   clarified that the ordinance does not authorize someone to harm a dog simply for barking behind
30   a fence.
31
32   Councilmember Buroker shared a personal experience of being bitten by a dog, expressing
33   empathy for how frightening that can be. She emphasized that it is the City's responsibility to
34   ensure residents feel safe when using public sidewalks and trails. She concluded that if the
35   ordinance helps achieve that goal of protecting the public, then she supports it.
36
37   Councilmember Haymond echoed previous comments, stating that most people are generally
38   reasonable and that provoking a dog behind a fence would clearly show unreasonable behavior,
39   with the dog essentially acting in self-defense. He noted that although the ordinance allows any
40   person to kill a dog under certain circumstances, in reality, most people aren't carrying weapons
41   or tools that would enable them to do so. He expressed comfort with the ordinance as written,
42   believing it properly allows individuals to defend themselves or others if truly necessary.
43   Councilmember McDougal MOVED that the City Council adopt Ordinance No. 25-10 –
44   Amending Riverton City Code Title 6, Chapter 5 to Revise Administrative Provisions of
45   the Animal Control Ordinance. Councilmember Haymond SECONDED the motion. Mayor
46   Staggs called for discussion on the motion. Mayor Staggs called for a roll-call vote. The vote
47   was as follows: Buroker-yes, Haymond-yes, McCay-no, McDougal-yes and Pierucci-no. The
48   motion passed with a 3 to 2 vote.
