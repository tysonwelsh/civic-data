I. Welcome and Introductions — Mayor Silvestrini
Mayor Silvestrini called the meeting to order and welcomed the legislators. He said he
would like to talk to them about what they see and what the City sees with the upcoming
legislative session. One of the principle issues of concern is planning the future with
housing while recognizing the need to accommodate an ever-increasing population. He
said the City needs to accommodate housing, so staff needs to work with the legislature
on planning with transit because one does not make sense without the other. Introductions
were then made by staff, City Council, and legislators.

ii.

Distribution

Mayor Silvestrini said the Governor’s message talks about broadening the
base of sales tax to possibly include services because the economy is
changing with more internet sales. There also is an issue about the
distribution. He said it is a struggle educating residents about revenue
sources. Cities run on sales tax, property tax, and fees. All of Millcreek’s
property tax pays for police services. The City used to be in the Salt Lake
Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA) taxing district to pay
for police services but withdrew last year, so the City levied a property tax
to pay for those services. Mayor Silvestrini said sales tax is vitally
important to Millcreek. He said if the distribution formula changes, there
will be winners and losers. The 1% local option sales tax is based 50% on
the point of sale and the other 50% is a portion based on population.
Millcreek is the tenth largest city in the state, so it has a healthy population
and sales tax base. He asked the legislators to be careful with the
distribution formula.

Senator Iwamoto asked how the Mayor felt about the Governor’s budget
broadening the base of sales tax. Mayor Silvestrini said in his career as a
lawyer, he used to think that was an awful idea because it would increase
the already high cost of legal services for people, but as the economy
changes, it may be appropriate to tax some of those things. Representative
Carol Moss said she has spoken to small business owners and they are
concemed about the affect it will have on them. Mayor Silvestrini said he
does not know the answer. Ashley Spatafore, the City’s lobbyist, said it
may exclude legal and medical services. She said the Utah League of
Cities and Towns said when the distribution compromise came up, it was
the cities that brought it forth. Senator Gene Davis said Florida has had a
service tax and Utah should look at the issues they have run into. He said
we are in a service society, not a retail society.

Mayor Silvestrini said if they could broaden the base and lower the rate, it
may make it fairer. Senator Davis said if you look at the general fund
overall, he does not think they could lower the rate. The State’s fund base
is sales tax. Council Member Jackson said in a robust economy, people are
willing to pay for services, but services slow down in a bad economy.
Mayor Silvestrini said we need to stay away from zoning for dollars.

firefighters. In 1971, it was 50% fire and allied funds and 10% of life
insurance funds allocated into this account. The account also pays for fire

Senator Mayne: Public Safety Planned Retirement

Spatafore said UFA had 17 retirements at one time and so they have been
running on mandatory over-time for over a year now. UFA will not start
training until someone retires. The bill will allow a six-month period for
the retiree to stay on staff and work while someone is being trained; a

c. CRA Legislation
i. Senator Bramble: Tax and Revenue Adjustments
Spatafore said Senator Bramble has sponsored a bill to identify a baseline
for what tax increment is assessed off of and to make sure Salt Lake
County is assessing that the same way other counties are.

ii. Representative Coleman Bill (TBD)
Spatafore said there is a percentage that goes to affordable housing in
community reinvestment areas. She said Representative Coleman would
modify an individual track for economic development that would get rid
of set aside affordable housing. Senator Davis said setting it aside is very
important. Spatafore said the Affordable Housing Commission is trying to
expand how to deal with affordable housing on a state level and to not lose
current funding. Mayor Silvestrini said there are two philosophies on
affordable housing: one is if you increase the supply, you increase the
affordability across the board because of the supply/demand problem; the
other is a subsidy which is beyond cities’ control. He said we need to work
on a solution to make housing more affordable.

iii. Representative Winder Bill (TBD)
Mike Winder said he is working on restaurant alcohol proximity
restriction amendments. Current law states that there must be 300 feet
walking distance from the door of a business serving alcohol to certain
public properties and 200 feet from the door to the property line. He said
he is proposing to adjust the proximity to 200 feet to the building of the
church or school but to the property line with parks. He said this will
benefit restaurants.

d. Municipal Water (Representative Coleman)
Council Member Jackson said Millcreek does not have control over their water
rates. Mayor Silvestrini said Representative Coleman is running a bill that
requires cities that sell water outside of their jurisdictional boundaries to be more
accountable and he supports that. Salt Lake City Council has refused confirming
Mayor Silvestrini to a seat on their Public Utilities Advisory Commission though
their Mayor appointed him. He said there should be accountability to the surplus
customers. Spatafore said this bill was part of a task force and has passed as a
committee bill; it seems like there is support.

f. Municipal Boundary Adjustments
Mayor Silvestrini said Millcreek residents ask him about getting Brickyard Plaza
back into the City. He said the connection from Salt Lake City to Brickyard Plaza
is a power line easement, so the only way you can get from Salt Lake City to
Brickyard without going through Millcreek is if you are an electron. Mayor
Silvestrini said 3300 S. and Highland Dr. and 1300 E. is the commercial nexus of
Millcreek. Mayor Silvestrini said by not having the property, it is an impediment
to good planning. He has spoken with Salt Lake City about giving it back to
Millcreek but letting them keep the revenue for the next 10 years. Salt Lake City
told him to put the city center elsewhere. Mayor Silvestrini said there is
consideration for legislation stating that if three sides of an area are surrounded by
a jurisdiction, that the city the disconnection is from cannot protest the
disconnection. This would help property owners in Brickyard Plaza disconnect
from Salt Lake City without its objection. Winder said it would be a high bar, like
80% with an acreage termination, and it would be up to the property owners.
Senator Davis gave a brief history on the area and that the property owners
needed the utilities that Salt Lake City provided at that time. Mayor Silvestrini
said if the property owners want to disconnect, they should be able to do it. Mayor
Silvestrini said he spoke with Salt Lake City representatives and they were in
opposition of a boundary adjustment.

V. Brief Update on Millcreek since Incorporation in 2017 — Mayor Silvestrini

a. City’s General Plan
Millcreek City Council is open to the idea of accessory dwelling units, but they
wanted to do something in conjunction with the general plan which will be
adopted soon. They are concerned about not imposing burdens on existing
neighborhoods, so the units need to have off-street parking. Mayor Silvestrini said
they are a good way to increase density that does not have a demonstrable effect
on the neighborhoods.

Brian Stillman said the implementation of an owner being required to live on the
property is a good way to control rental-type problems. Francis Lilly said the
planning department gets about 2 inquiries a week about accessory dwelling units.
He said people are looking for housing and looking for a way to stay in their
housing. Sandy and Murray have experience with the units.

b. City Center Plan
Mayor Silvestrini said the City terminated the blight study because it was what
the public wanted. He said Millcreek does well on public engagement.
Representative Moss said the word blight is unfortunate and density has become a
dirty word. The City is working on a city center master plan.

c. CRAs Being Created in Millcreek
Mayor Silvestrini said the City has adopted two other CRA’s, Olympus Hills and
Canyon Rim Commons.

He said the City has also launched a Promise Program that is similar to that in
South Salt Lake. There are three Title 1 schools in the City. Millcreek has
launched a day time reading program with volunteers at Millcreek Elementary.
The goal is to serve the whole community in all the schools; a community wide
program designed to build bridges with resources. Millcreek is trying to address
causes of problems rather than treating symptoms. Council Member Marchant
mentioned that the Read Today program has been going on longer than the City
has been around.
