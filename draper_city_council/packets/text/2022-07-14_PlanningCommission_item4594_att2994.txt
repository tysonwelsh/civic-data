BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY
This application is a request for approval of a Deviation for approximately 0.95 acres
located on the north side of 12300 South, at approximately 293 East 12300 South (Exhibit C
& D). The property is currently zoned CR. The applicant is requesting that a Deviation be
approved to eliminate side perimeter landscaping standards.

The Conditional Use Permit and Site Plan for a convenience store and gasoline sales was
approved in June of 1994. In 2001, the applicant obtained approval to add an additional gas
pump to the site. Draper City is currently in the process of widening the intersection of
12300 South and 300 East. With that widening they will be purchasing some land from the
applicant and reducing the amount of landscaping along 300 East. Per Draper City
Municipal Code (DCMC) Section 9-6-080, the site will still be considered conforming to
landscaping standards in regards to the reduction due to public action.

ANALYSIS

General Plan and Zoning. The Land Use Map of the General Plan calls for the Community
Commercial land use designation for the subject property (Exhibit E). This category is
characterized as follows:




The property has been assigned the CR zoning classification (Exhibit F). According to DCMC
Section 9-8-020 the purpose of the CR zone is to “provide areas where a combination of
destination oriented business, retail commercial, entertainment, and related uses may be
established, maintained and protected to serve both residents and nonresidents of the city.
Typical uses in this zone include large scale, master planned commercial centers with outlying
commercial pads, big box stores, and offices.” The property abuts the CR zone to the north
and west and the CC (Community Commercial) zone to the east and south.

Deviation. The existing property does not conform to current landscaping standards found
in DCMC Section 9-23-090. (Exhibit G). This section requires at least 10-feet of landscaping
along all perimeter property lines. There is no landscaping along the western property line.
The site did not include landscaping in this area when built in 1994 and is considered legal
nonconforming in regards to perimeter landscaping. The applicant is seeking to redevelop
the site in the future and is asking for a deviation to DCMC Section 9-23-090 in order to



Holiday Oil Landscaping                                             App. # VAR-129-2022
Deviation Request                             2
keep the western property line as it is today and not install the 10-feet of landscaping that
would otherwise be required with their redevelopment (Exhibit H). The applicants request
letter can be found in Exhibit I of this report. No application for an amended site plan has
been submitted to the City for review at this time. An application for an amended site plan
will be required and compliance to DCMC will be required with the submission, subject to
any amendments to the perimeter landscaping in this request.

The western property line is located 28-feet away from the existing convenience store
building. That allows for two-way traffic around the building. A minimum of 24-feet is
required under DCMC Table 9-25-2. If a 10-foot landscape area is required along the
western property line, it would reduce the drive aisle on the western side of the existing
building to 18-feet wide, which only allows for one-way traffic. The applicant’s concept plan
attached in Exhibit H shows the proposed building located 17-feet away from the western
property line, with no landscaping provided. A 10-foot landscape area would reduce that
drive aisle to 7-feet and the minimum width for a one-way traffic drive aisle is 13-feet.

Landscaping Modification. DCMC Section 9-23-030 allows the Planning Commission to
approve deviations to strict compliance to the landscaping requirements in Chapter 23 of
the DCMC based on making the findings as outlined in the Criteria for Approval section of
this report.

Criteria For Approval.
The criteria for review and potential approval of a landscapign deviaton request is found in
Section 9-23-030(B) of the DCMC. This section depicts the standard of review for such
requests as:

         1. Is consistent with the purpose of this chapter and any applicable master plan or
            ordinance;
         2. Reflects a design that conforms to the requirements of this chapter to the greatest
            extent possible and offers alternative methods for addressing the landscape
            requirement for which a deviation is being requested. Alternative methods may
            include additional fencing, berming and plant material than what would otherwise be
            required;
         3. Will not adversely affect neighboring property owners or residents;
         4. Will not adversely affect sight distance or otherwise diminish public safety;
         5. Is justified by site constraints; and
         6. Is of high quality and integrates aesthetically with the design of the primary buildings
            on site and established streetscape.

To Whom It May Concern:

Holiday Oil is applying for a deviation to code 9-23-030 (see below) concerning the landscape buffer
requirement for commercial sites. Draper City is condemning our frontage along 300 East under Eminent
Domain to widen the right of way by adding a turn lane. Holiday Oil would like to construct a new
updated C-Store and increase the size of the fueling canopy with an exception to the current landscape
buffer requirements on both the east and west boundaries of the possible in connection to the
condemnation.

The site improvements will greatly improve the traffic flow especially during peak hours. The current
western boundary doesn’t have any landscape buffer and we would like the new site improvements to
keep the current western boundary as it currently sits. We feel our site meets the deviation requirement
and justified by site constraints under 9-23-030:(B)(5).

In exchange for cooperation with the Draper’s condemnation request, Holiday Oil is asking for:
    1) Building permit to improve the site with a new c-store larger canopy
    2) The landscape buffer exceptions on the east and west boundaries of the parcel

9-23-030: DEVIATIONS FROM STRICT COMPLIANCE:
        A. Deviations Authorized: Since site conditions and development constraints vary greatly among sites, the
            planning commission is authorized to approve landscape plans that deviate from strict compliance with
            the provisions of this chapter if it makes the findings required in subsection B of this section.
                 1. Such a deviation may include the use of water wise materials in lieu of otherwise required
                     materials.
                 2. Any proposed deviation from the requirements of this chapter shall be:
                           a. Clearly identified on the proposed landscape plan; and
                           b. Accompanied by a written description of the proposed deviation showing how w the
                               purpose and intent of this chapter will be mt by the proposed plan.
        B. Findings Required: The planning commission may authorize a landscape plan deviation only if it finds
            the deviation:
                 1. Is consistent with the purpose of this chapter and any applicable master plan or ordinance;
                 2. Reflects a design that conforms to the requirements of this chapter to the greatest extent
                     possible and offers alternative methods for addressing the landscape requirement for which a
                     deviation is being requested. Alternative methods may include additional fencing,
                      berming and plant material than what would otherwise be required;
                 3.   Will not adversely affect neighboring property owners or residents;
             4. Will not adversely affect sight distance or otherwise diminish public safety;
             5. Is justified by site constraints; and
             6. Is of high quality and integrates aesthetically with the design of the primary buildings on site
                and established streetscape. (Ord. 1132, 2-17-2015)
