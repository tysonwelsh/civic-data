The purpose of the work session was to review the agenda for the City Council meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.;
receive an update regarding the Marshall White Community Center; discuss agenda management software; discuss Council
business pertaining to the Council member Initiative Process; and hear Council comments.

Agenda Review

Chair Nadolski and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council meeting
scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Marshall White Community Center Update

Public Services Director Lowder, Recreation Division Manager Bridge, and VCBO Architects representative Tippets
provided an update on the development of plans for anew Marshall White Facility. Future development and use of the Marshall
White Facility will include:

e Providing a safe place for individuals and families to gather.

e Honoring the legacy of Marshall White.

e Increasing the size of the building to provide additional recreational and community event programming, one or
more aquatic features, fitness studios, and all-abilities access.

Mr. Tippets stated he is in the process of finalizing construction drawings for the project, which will be available for
public bid in January of 2023. There will be a 60-day window for bidding and negotiations, with a contract award to be made in
April. He then reviewed the conceptual drawings for the facility, highlighting the pool, locker room, administration offices,
recreation programming space, outdoor programming space, classroom space that can be separated into three individual spaces or
used as one large space, kitchen, multifunctional spaces and opportunities to honor the legacy of Marshall White.

The Council engaged in discussion with Mr. Tippets regarding mechanisms that will be used to control access to the
outdoor programming spaces; optional uses of the multifunctional spaces; and participation of the Marshall N. White Center
Advisory Committee and other stakeholders in the design process.

Discussion then shifted to options for financing the project; Chief Administrative Officer Johnson stated that
Administration would prefer to not bond for more than $15 million for the project and has explored opportunities for raising funds
for the project through a capital campaign process. Based upon the current draft of the construction plans, the project could cost
approximately $29 million, and costs will only continue to increase in the coming months. The group participated in a

Agenda Management Software

Information Technologies (IT) Customer Account Manager McClelland used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
discuss a proposal to implement agenda management software in the City. PrimeGov was selected after IT and Council staff
received presentations from several vendors and Council staff is requesting Council’s input on the meeting management module to
determine if this module is of interest to the Council. He discussed the City’s current process for creating and publishing agendas,
as well as synchronizing agendas with YouTube video playback. Optional features provided by PrimeGov include providing
personal notes and scripts prior to a meeting, and displaying agenda items on a television, displaying pre-registered speakers and
times, and displaying motions and votes during a meeting. He presented screenshot images of the appearance of the PrimeGov
meeting viewer program screens. The Council engaged in discussion with Mr. McClelland and Council staff regarding the manner
in which this type of tool will increase public accessibility and participation in open meetings of the Council and the potential for
the tool to reduce staff time associated with agenda preparation and meeting administration; they concluded to support the
purchase of the PrimeGov base package at this time with plans to revisit the issue in the future to determine whether to add
additional modules.

Council Business

Council member Initiative Process: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the Council initiative process,
which is defined in the Council’s Rules and Norms document. At present, a Council member can complete a form that will
generate an email to Council staff regarding the initiative, but she would like to propose a more in-depth process that would
increase the level of involvement of the Council member submitting the initiative. She presented an outline for the process
adjustments she is recommending; for legislative processes, a Council member would meet with the Council Executive Director
regarding the issue they would like to address. During this meeting, there would be a focus on why a change is needed and the
benefits of the proposed change. Council staff would then complete a preliminary review of the initiative, including a fiscal and
legal review, after which the issue could be placed on a work session agenda for review and discussion with the entire Council.
For an administrative issue, the same process would be followed, but before commencing the initiative process, the Council
member would be encouraged to discuss proposed administrative changes with the Mayor.

The Council discussed the proposed process adjustments and offered additional suggestions for minor changes to the
process; there was a focus on whether to implement an expected timeline for an initiative or an additional meeting between
Council staff and the sponsoring Council member after the fiscal and legal analysis of an issue has been completed. Ms. Eller-
Smith stated she will prepare a draft proposed initiative process for the Council to consider in a future work session.

The meeting adjourned at 5:54 p.m.
