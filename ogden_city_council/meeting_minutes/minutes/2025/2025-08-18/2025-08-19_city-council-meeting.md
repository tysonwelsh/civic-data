Recognizing Chief Mathieu’s four decades of dedicated service to the
Ogden City Fire Department
         A memo from Council staff regarding recognition of Chief Mike Mathieu for decades of dedicated service to the Ogden
City Fire Department came before the Council for consideration. Council member Blair read the certificate of recognition issued
by the Ogden City Council and Mayor Nadolski and presented Chief Mathieu with the certificate.
         Chief Mathieu thanked the Mayor and Council for recognizing him; it has been a privilege to serve in the community of
Ogden and to serve under four separate Mayors during that time. He indicated the Fire Department is a team that he considers
himself fortunate to have been a part of; however, there are two people that he must thank specifically. He recognized his wife
Tina Mathieu, who served 911 Weber Area Dispatch and is now the Executive Director of Utah Communications Authority
(UCA); she was creative and innovative during her Dispatch service, and she has made a big difference through the way she
serves the community. He thanked her for her passion and her commitment to public safety. He also thanked his Office
Administrator, Michelle Scrip; she has been a key partner to him during his time as Fire Chief. She was a great partner, confidant,
and counselor to him and has helped him to manage and maintain the Department in a professional manner.
         Mayor Nadolski thanked Chief Mathieu for his comments and again expressed his gratitude to Chief Mathieu for his
service and dedication to Ogden City. The City owes him a great deal for his leadership and developing a credible Fire
Department. He then noted he has reached out to the three living Mayors under which Chief Mathieu has served and asked them to
provide comments on their experience with Chief Mathieu. He read each of the statements provided along with his statement
presented the comments provided by former Mayors Caldwell and Godfrey along with three keys to the City.
         Council members echoed the gratitude expressed to Chief Mathieu and also recognized his wife for her support of Chief
Mathieu throughout his career. They wished him well in his retirement and commended him for his leadership. Chair White
commented on Chief Mathieu’s legacy of courage, commitment, and integrity and thanked him for all he has done.

Proposed Ordinance 2025-26 amending the Ogden Municipal Code by
revising definitions, removing aeronautical activities minimum standards,
removing airpark minimum standards, and making technical changes
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed Ogden Municipal Code Title
8 amendments came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated City Administration is proposing to make significant
changes to Chapters 1, 4, and 8 of Title 8 of the City’s code as part of a larger effort to update the City’s airport regulations.
          Community and Economic Development Executive Director Johnson summarized the memo and referred to discussion
of the proposed amendments over the course of three different work sessions, including tonight’s work session. He acknowledged
there have been several questions asked by the City Council and the public and Administration is committed to continue working
to answer the questions and address concerns that have been raised. He noted that the changes to Title 8 are to be reviewed and
considered over several phases. It is anticipated that the process will take approximately six months and there will be opportunities
for involvement from stakeholders, the Council, and the public. Changes to Chapters 4 and 8 related to minimum standards are the
most pressing and will be reviewed first. Additional phases include further changes to definitions; leasing policies; rents, rates,
fees, and charges; other rules and regulations; and additional development standards.
          Council member Richey applauded Administration’s efforts to make changes that will give the Ogden Hinckley Airport
the best opportunity to be successful. Council member Graf agreed and thanked Mr. Johnson, Airport Manager Condie, and other
members of City Administration for their willingness to answer questions the Council has had about this proposal. He recognizes
the current issue is a remnant of the past form of government of the City and he believes that these efforts to realign the policies
that govern the airport will empower Administration to make decisions that will benefit the Airport and the City. He looks forward
to the Airport becoming the economic engine it can be. Council member Blair echoed the comments made by Council members
Richey and Graf; he thanked Administration for their flexibility and willingness to work through the concerns the Council has
expressed.
          The Council then discussed the matters that were raised during the work session this evening and contemplated whether
they could amend the ordinance tonight before adoption, or if it would be better to continue consideration of the ordinance to a
future meeting to give Administration time to amend the ordinance document. City Attorney Williams reiterated that the process
to amend and update Title 8 will be ongoing and he feels the Council can adopt the proposed ordinance tonight as written, with
direction to incorporate proposed edits into future iterations of the ordinance document. The Council accepted Mr. Williams’
suggestion and discussed the timeframe for considering additional proposed amendments to Title 8.
          Chair White introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2025-26, entitled:
                    “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Ogden Municipal Code by amending Section 8-1-1 to revise
                    definitions; by amending Chapter 4 of Title 8 to remove aeronautical activities minimum standards; by
                    amending Chapter 8 of Title 8 to remove airpark minimum standards; to make technical changes; to
                    appropriately revise the index; and by providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon
                    posting after final passage.”

Proposed Resolution 2025-15 modifying the Rules and Norms of the Ogden
City Council
          A memo from Council staff regarding proposed Council Rules and Norms amendments came before the Council for
consideration. The memo stated during a Council strategy session in April 2025, the Council expressed interest in updating its
Rules and Norms to give the Chair discretion to adjust time limits for public input, in order to help manage meetings more
efficiently. In addition to this proposed change, Council staff have proposed further updates to the Rules and Norms, including
language addressing the use of employee images on social media, clarifying that the use of media recordings is not permitted
during the public comment portion of meetings, and an addition to the categories listed on an agenda.
          Council Policy Analyst Burton summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
proposed amendments. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Chair White introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2025-15, entitled:
                   “A resolution of the Ogden City Council approving modifications to Council Rules and Norms.”
          ON A MOTION BY VICE CHAIR HYER AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER BLAIR, RESOLUTION
2025-15 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – COUNCIL MEMBERS
BLAIR, CHOBERKA, GRAF, MYERS, RICHEY, VICE CHAIR HYER, AND CHAIR WHITE. VOTING NO – NONE.

Public Comments
          Gregory Allen expressed concerns about homelessness and blight in Ogden and noted he is seeking ways to get involved
in community improvement. He finds Ogden to be a challenging place to live for a reformed felon or homeless individual; these
people cannot even go to a City park and use restroom facilities, and he does not believe that is fair. He is planning to meet with
Council member Choberka following tonight’s meeting to discuss his concerns further.
          Kerry Wayne questioned the Ogden Bend land exchange from 2011 and the City's support for Lotus Development. As an
Ogden City taxpayer, he cannot understand the City’s willingness to trade land with developers that only benefit the developer and
not the City. He suggested that the City’s efforts be redirected to programs that can benefit the youth of Ogden. No-cost leases are
not good for Ogden City.
          Laura Lewis recognized a City employee in the Parks Department for the assistance he offered to her recently. She then
criticized the City Council’s handling of public comment time; she asked them to reconsider their action to approve amendments
to the Council Rules and Norms document and called for consistency and fairness in public meeting procedures.
          Scott Jensen voiced concerns about a recent fire on 18th Street; communication between the Fire Department and property
owners/residents living near the property involved in the fire was lacking and this is very concerning to him. He has notified the
City of safety issues with the property long before the fire occurred and those issues were not addressed. He is very disappointed;
he lost a structure and several pieces of equipment due to the fire, and he is now forced to put his life on hold to address those
matters. He asked the Council to explore options for addressing these types of situations in the future.
          Heath Satow addressed the proposed amendments to Title 8 regarding the Ogden Airport; he is concerned about ceding
more authority to the Mayor’s Office and noted that the implications of the proposed amendments have not been discussed
enough. He encouraged the Council to work to strength the role of advisory bodies and promote transparency for those groups.
The Council must retain meaningful oversight and urged them to discuss the proposal with outside legal counsel. He also
expressed concern about the changes to the Rules and Norms document regarding limitations on public input during public
meetings. Based on the changes, the Council will only be reducing the amount of time spent on public comments when there are a
lot of people at a Council meeting. This is the worst time to impose restrictions because people rarely show up to Council
meetings and if they do show up, it means that they care deeply about the issue they are intending to hear or address. It is very
important to give these people their full three minutes. If the Council cuts public input time short, residents will feel disregarded.
He urged the Council to reconsider this change.
          Teresa Bramwell shared a recent experience with homeless individuals on her property; she was disappointed by the
response from 911 Dispatch, who dispatched the Fire Department and paramedics. After the paramedics were able to rouse the
individuals, they quickly jumped up and left her porch and started walking towards her. They quickly left her property, at which
time she called the Police Department again and this time she received a response. However, she is unsure whether the individuals
were taken into custody. She felt the Police should have come to her house upon receiving the first call and the initial response
was not useful. President Trump signed an Executive Order on July 21 called “Ending Crime and Disorder on America’s Streets”
and that order deals with homeless people, drug use, and mental illness and she feels Ogden could use an increased focus on those
matters.
          Chair White referred to the comments made regarding the amendments to the Rules and Norms document and noted the
Council has studied this issue at length. Council member Graf agreed and stated he is sympathetic to the concerns that were
expressed; the Rules and Norms document is a living document that can be changed in the future if deemed necessary. He also
noted that the changes do not prohibit residents from reaching out to Council members directly and he noted he is willing to speak
to any resident of the City on the telephone with no time limits. He also answers every email he receives, and he does not feel the
public’s access to elected officials is limited.
           Chair White then addressed Mr. Jensen’s comments about the 18th Street Fire and noted that Chief Slater addressed the
Council tonight during their work session regarding the Fire Department’s efforts to address the issue. She also commented on the
Police Department’s response to Ms. Bramwell’s situation and deferred to Mayor Nadolski to discuss dispatch protocols.
           Council member Choberka stated she wished to address Ms. Bramwell’s comments as well; she is assuming that the
reason the Fire Department was dispatched is that the dispatcher believed the homeless individuals were in danger or needed
medical support. She apologized that Ms. Bramwell had a negative interaction, but feels the response was appropriate.
           Council member Blair addressed Ms. Lewis’ and Mr. Satow’s comments about public comment rules; he acknowledged
that it takes a great deal of courage for residents to attend and speak at a Council meeting, and he does want to hear from them. He
believes that the changes to the Rules and Norms are appropriate, and the changes will only be exercised when warranted; the
change gives the Chair another tool to effectively manage public meetings of the Council, and he does not believe that it will be
abused.
           Mayor Nadolski stated that Council member Choberka’s assessment of the response to Ms. Bramwell’s call for service
was accurate.
           Chair White recognized Kirk Nigro from Rocky Mountain Power (RMP) and asked him to provide comments regarding a
recent increase in power outages in the community. Mr. Nigro stated RMP has implemented enhanced safety settings for areas
with elevated fire risks; if there is a trip in an area that is at risk of a fire, the system will shut down to avoid sparking a wildfire.
Safety has been prioritized above reliability of power. He knows this can be frustrating and the protocol will be evaluated
regularly; efforts are also underway to clear areas where there is an increased risk of wildfire. He apologized that the situation has
the potential to impact residences and businesses, but safety is a top priority for RMP. The Council engaged in brief discussion
with Mr. Nigro regarding the causes of specific power outages and anything the City can do to assist RMP in their efforts. The
Council requested increased and improved communication between RMP and their power customers in the event of an outage.
Mr. Nigro briefly summarized the current notification methods employed by RMP and expressed a willingness to participate in
public workshops to discuss these issues directly with the public.
           Council members then discussed options for ensuring backup power generation in the event of a power outage.

Mayor Comments
