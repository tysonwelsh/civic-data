OPENING CEREMONY:
James Boyd, Chief Development and Marketing Director at CAPSA provided the
opening ceremony and led the audience in the pledge of allegiance.

Mr. Boyd addressed the Council and appreciated the opportunity to speak before the
Council. The month of April is Sexual Assault Awareness Month. Many members of the
community recognize CAPSA as a domestic violence center. However, CAPSA is also a
state-recognized rape recovery center for the community. CAPSA is a dual agency that
has the unique opportunity to serve victims of sexual assault and domestic abuse.

A recent case involved a female college student who was sexually assaulted by her
landlord. She no longer felt safe returning to her apartment. The apartment and landlord
invoked fear and trauma in the victim. CAPSA is able to provide housing, shelter, and
rape crisis casework support and clinical therapy. Combined together, these two services
provide a unique opportunity for healing. Tragically, this is only one of many stories of
individuals that CAPSA helps support.

In the last three months, January through March, CAPSA has provided support services
to 130 individuals who have suffered sexual assault. In addition, 250 individuals where
sexual assault was the secondary cause for them seeking services. CAPSA has responded
to 15 Code R events at the hospital. These are forensic sexual assault exams for adults,
where advocates respond at any time of day to collect evidence that may be used in future
prosecution.

With April being Sexual Assault Awareness Month, the community is asked to start by
believing. At CAPSA, we witness the transformational power of believing daily.
Survivors are able to bravely share their stories and experiences. The first response to the
assault significantly impacts a survivor’s healing journey. Negative responses can deepen
trauma and deter survivors from seeking further help. Thus, contributing to the chilling
statistic that 74% of sexual assault victims will never report the assault to law
enforcement. In contrast, research has found that victims who are supported, including

Logan River Project Update – Zan Murray, J-U-B Engineers (10:50)

Zan Murray with J-U-B Engineers addressed the Council.

Mr. Murray said the needs and challenges of the Logan River Watershed Plan are the
protection of water rights, limited resources, deteriorating infrastructure, and the need for
culinary upgrades. At present within the Logan River Watershed Plan, Logan is at the
environmental stage.

Currently, there is a 30-day public comment period for the notice of intent to prepare an
environmental impact statement. During the environmental assessment over 800 public
comments were received. The comments received will be used as part of the plan.
Additional information and potential alternatives can be found at
https://bit.ly/LoganRiverWatershedProject.

Chair A. Anderson asked if any content was similar or consistent among the public
comments received.

Mr. Murray responded that similar comments were categorized and complied together.
One of the categories was related to the Little Logan portion of the river. The Little
Logan is part of the Crockett Avenue canal system used to channel the irrigation water to
the southern part of the city. The water in the canal is fed into the system by being
diverted from the river. There is a desire by the public to continue to preserve the canal
system. Provisions have been made as a result to continue to permit water to be
maintained and channeled through the canal. Public feedback received will permit the
plan to be formed and meet the needs of the community.

Another ongoing item is the question of what to do with the canals. Canals can be a
challenge to maintain. As part of the plan, the flood conveyance is being reviewed to
mitigate issues. Some of the alternatives are canals being lined or piped to carry flood
water and be kept clean.

Councilmember López requested confirmation that one of the projects is to potentially
increase the flow of water into the Logan River.
Mr. Murray confirmed that this is correct. At the Crockett water diversion, they have the
right to the initial flow to put into the canal system. Recognizing there is value in keeping
water in the river for the fishery and habitat. The project will make provisions to allow 25
sec/feet of water to go past the diversion and continue down the river.
Councilmember Simmonds inquired if there are individuals or entities that can divert water
directly from the river in the service area.
Mr. Murray answered there are several irrigation companies that divert water from the
river. He cannot say for certain whether there are individuals who have the right to do so.
If there were it would be in the lower reaches as the river heading west, individual

agricultural/water irrigation rights. Typically, irrigation companies are the primary
diversions in the upper portions of the river.
Councilmember Simmonds asked how many irrigation companies are vested in the Logan
River within the Crockett Avenue umbrella.
Mr. Murray replied under the umbrella of the Crockett Avenue diversion, there are ten
irrigation companies. He briefly introduced seated in the audience, Gordon Colson, the
president of the Irrigation Company.
Councilmember Simmonds requested confirmation that at the end of the environmental
assessment, the alternatives found will be carried forward to the next phase, though the
alternatives have the potential to change during the environmental impact statement.
Mr. Murray responded during the initial environmental assessment, there were twenty
alternative approaches created. The alternatives created were put into a matrix and were
ranked according to meeting the standards of key criteria based on information gathering
including public comments. All the alternatives created are still applicable in the matrix
but may change based on further feedback received.
Councilmember Simmonds inquired between the environmental assessment and impact
statement what additionally needs to be accomplished prior to funding.
Mr. Murray answered that within an environmental assessment, a primary alternative
option can be selected and moved forward. An economic analysis is done on the specific
alternative along with biological studies in regard to the flora, fauna, and wetlands. In an
environmental impact statement, multiple alternatives are reviewed in a detailed manner.
The other prominent alternatives are reviewed in the same detailed fashion as the primary
alternative option. Currently, there are three alternatives. The first alternative is the "no
action" alternative meaning taking no action at all beyond patchwork. The second
alternative is diverting water from the first dam to pressurize the system. The third
alternative is to pressurize and pump water out of the Crocket Avenue diversion.
Councilmember Simmonds asked if there is a possibility that other alternatives may rise to
the top of the matrix or are the mentioned alternatives continue to move forward for an
additional study.
Mr. Murray replied it is probable the mentioned alternatives will move forward, but there
is no guarantee as further feedback received from the public may change that.
Gordon Colson, President of the Irrigation Company added for clarification that the
mentioned alternatives address most of the concerns of the public comments received at
this time. If something better can be done, it is their wish to do so. The canal system has
run through the city for over 100 years. The desire is to look equally that far forward to
ensure the canal system will be here for years to come. It will be at least another year before
the final compilation will be completed.
Councilmember Simmonds added that the completion of the Logan River Watershed Plan
may be further extended as the plan and selected option depends on funding with the
exception to do nothing.
Mr. Murray clarified that even doing nothing requires funding as patchwork must be
implemented to maintain the current infrastructure.

WORKSHOP ITEM:

Consideration of a proposed resolution to approve Library Meeting Room Fees –
Resolution 24-09 – Karen Clark, Library Director (41:15)

Library Director Karen Clark addressed the Council regarding the proposed Library
Meeting Room Fees.

Councilmember Simmonds asked if there is a restriction on food not being brought from
home when using the community rooms.

Ms. Clark answered there is no such a policy at this time.

Councilmember Simmonds expressed concerns about community rooms becoming a
location to host a potluck. She recommended a requirement that the food being provided
is catered or provided by another food service provider, both of which would have a food
handler’s permit.

Ms. Clark said the request/condition can be added as part of the meeting room policy to
have a requirement of a food handler’s permit.

Chair A. Anderson asked if whole meals applied to the library space or the community
room.

Ms. Clark explained whole meals apply to the library space. Patrons are discouraged
from bringing meals like burgers, breakfast cereal, and so forth. Water bottles are
permitted and encouraged along with drinks that have lids; some small snacks are
permitted. The intent is to keep the library looking new, clean, and nice.

Chair A. Anderson said small community groups tend to share the food of their
community. If the regulation is made to prohibit community food, it would prevent these
smaller groups from sharing food.

Councilmember Simmonds clarified her stance, that the food does not need to be catered
but should be prepared by someone with a food handler's permit.

Councilmember López inquired how such requirements could be enforced, and to whom
would the permit be shown.

Councilmember Simmonds replied that the community group would need to show their
food handler’s permit for preparing food and do so at the time of reservation to the
library staff.
