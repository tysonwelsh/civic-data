GPZ-7-2023
Chris Clifford
5143 W 3500 S
General Plan change from Mixed Use and Large Lot Residential to Small Lot Residential
and a zone change from A to RM
4.34 acres

This application was continued by the Planning Commission during the public meeting on
August 9, 2023.

Chris Clifford has submitted a General Plan/zone change application for three parcels totaling
4.34 acres located at 5143 W 3500 S. The property is currently zoned A (Agriculture, minimum
lot size ½ acre) with a General Plan designation of Mixed Use for approximately the north 230’
and Large Lot Residential (2 to 3 units/acre) for the rest of the property. At this location, the
Mixed Use designation includes Office and High Density Residential (12 to 20 units/acre). The
proposed zone is RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling Residential) and the proposed General Plan
designation is Small Lot Residential (4 to 7 units/acre).

Surrounding zones include A to the south, west, and a portion of the east and R-1-8 (Single Unit
Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 8,000 square feet) to the north and the remaining east
portion. Surrounding land uses include single family homes to the north, south, and east and
single family homes and a six-plex to the west. The north parcel includes a home that, according
to the Salt Lake County Assessor, was built in 1900. The two parcels to the south are vacant.

At the time this report was prepared, staff had received one email from an adjoining neighbor
who is opposed to this application. The email is included with this report.

Development Proposal

As shown in the attached concept plan and rendering, the applicant is proposing to demolish the
existing home and develop a 21-unit planned unit development (PUD) of twin homes and one
single unit dwelling. The proposed density is 4.8 units/acre. The streets would be public and
utilize the 44’ right-of-way allowed in Section 7-13-705 for planned unit developments (PUDs)
with attached units.

The Fire and Public Works Departments will require a cul-de-sac at the south end of the
property. In addition, the City’s ordinance requires 4.75 parking spaces for single unit dwellings
and twin homes dwellings. The applicant is aware of both of these requirements.

Last year, Mr. Clifford submitted a General Plan/zone change application on the same three
parcels along with an additional parcel to the south. The General Plan request was the same
(Small Lot Residential); however, the zoning requested was RS (Residential Sustainability). A
table comparing this previous proposal with the current proposal is below.

In reviewing this application, staff suggests that there are two primary questions to answer:

   1. Is this the right location for a twin home development?

   2. Does the quality of the project further City goals?

These two questions are explored below.

Question 1: Is this the right location for a twin home development?

RM Zone Conditions
According to the City’s zoning ordinance, zone changes to the RM zone can only be considered
by the City if the following four conditions are met:

   1. For properties without existing Structures which are proposed to be developed, the
      property must be a minimum of one acre and the Density must not exceed 12 units per
      acre. For properties with existing Structures proposed to be redeveloped, the property
      must be a minimum of 20,000 square feet and the Density must not exceed 20 units per
      acre. For properties that are west of Redwood Road and within one-half mile of the West
      Valley Central light rail station, the Decker Lake light rail station or the Redwood
      Junction light rail station, the Density may exceed 20 units per acre;

   2. The property shall meet any one of the following four requirements: (i) have access and
      Frontage on a Street with a planned Right-of-way width of at least 80 feet (for properties
      under two acres) or 100 feet (for properties two acres or larger) as indicated on the Major
      Street Plan; (ii) be adjacent to existing multifamily residential development on two sides;
      or (iii) be located west of Redwood Road and within one-half mile of the West Valley
      Central light rail station, the Decker Lake light rail station, or the Redwood Junction light
      rail station; or (iv) if the property is vacant, was vacant as of January 19, 2021, will be an
      exempt housing development under 24 CFR 100.304 or successor regulation with a
      density no greater than seven units per acre, and have access and Frontage on a Street
      with a planned Right-of-way width of at least 80 feet;
   3. A Development Agreement must be proposed with the zone change application that
      addresses Dwelling Unit sizes, exterior materials, architecture, Landscaping, and project
      Amenities; and

   4. A traffic impact study prepared by a registered traffic engineer is required for projects
      with 100 or more Dwelling Units.

On the first and second conditions, the property has access and frontage onto 3500 South, which
has a planned right-of-way width of 115’. On the third condition, the applicant’s development
agreement standards are addressed later in this report. The fourth condition does not apply
because the project is under 100 units.

In the General Plan, the north 1.5 acres of the subject property includes the option of High
Density Residential, which has a density range of 12 to 20 units/acre. The remaining 2.8 acres of
the subject property is designated as Large Lot Residential, which has a density range of 2 to 3
units/acre. The table below summarizes the number of units anticipated in the General Plan at the
low and high ends of the density ranges for the subject property.

In summary, for a project to comply with the existing General Plan designation, the number of

As pointed out earlier, zoning in the surrounding neighborhoods is R-1-8. Typical density within
the R-1-8 zone is around 4.2 units/acre. The proposed density of the project of 4.8 units/acre is
closer to the typical density within the R-1-7 zone.
Question 2: Does the quality of the project further City goals?

As mentioned earlier, the proposed density of this project is somewhat higher than the density in
the surrounding neighborhoods. For PUDs, the City’s ordinance allows for a density increase;
however, any increase in density must be compensated by increased amenities and improved
design which are proportional to the density increase. The sections that follow address dwelling
unit size, exterior building features, and amenities.

Dwelling Unit Sizes
The City’s ordinance sets the minimum size for single unit dwellings, twin homes, and two unit
dwellings at 1,000 square feet. This ordinance amount is limited by State law unless a
development agreement requires a larger amount. The applicant is proposing a 1,650 square foot
minimum area for rambler units and a 2,400 square foot minimum for two-story unit. All units
would include basements. For comparison, the City’s ordinance sets the minimum size for a
three-bedroom townhome at 1,400 square feet.

Exterior Building Features
The applicant is proposing the following exterior building design features:
    primarily fiber cement siding exteriors with 10 to 20% of the exteriors as brick or stone,
    a minimum roof pitch of 6:12, and
    variations in designs and setbacks.

Amenities
The applicant is proposing the following project amenities:
    a pavilion placed in the common area,
    a playground, and
    a walkway around the perimeter of the property.

Staff Suggestions

In addition to the standards proposed by the applicant, staff recommends these additional
standards along with a revision to the applicant’s standards.

      Number of units: The number of units shall be no more than 21.
      Fencing: Perimeter fencing along the west, south, and east sides shall be 6’ tall vinyl. The
       perimeter fence along the north side shall be 6’ tall, pre-cast concrete in the same color
       and pattern used by the City along 4100 South. An entry feature shall also be included
       along 3500 South. A 5’ wide area of landscaping shall be included between the sidewalk
       and precast concrete wall along 3500 South.
      Architecture: At least one alternative front elevation shall be developed and built for at
       least 6 of the proposed units. The alternative front elevation shall be reviewed and
       approved by the Planning Commission during the subdivision plat review. Brick or stone
       shall be used on at least 20% of the exterior of all homes. There shall be at least a one-
       foot setback difference between twin home units within the same building.
      Right-of-way dedication: 17.5’ shall be dedicated along the entire frontage of 3500
       South.
      The right-of-way width for the extension of Kiowa Way shall be 54’ to accommodate
       parkstrip and sidewalk on both sides of the street.

C-16-2023
Shoppes at Lake Park Ph 4 (TGI
CORP.) 2893 S. 5600 W.
C-2 zone, 3.01 acres

The applicant, TGI CORP., representing the Shoppes at Lake Park Ph 4, is requesting
conditional use approval for the final phase of a shopping center at 2893 South 5600 West. The
zoning for this property is C-2, General Commercial and the General Plan designation is
General Commercial. The surrounding zone is C-2 on the west and south. The zoning to the
north and east is RM, multi unit dwelling residential. The surrounding uses include the Shoppes
at Lake Park development to the south (Burlington, Ross, Kohls and Michaels etc.) and west
(retail and restaurant uses including Buffalo Wild Wings) and Highbury Apartments to the north
and Highbury Townhomes to the east.

This property is located within the Shoppes at Lake Park that has been developed in several
phases under C-35-2003, C-5-2006 and C-15-2016. The current and final proposal is for two
retail tenant spaces that will finish off the anchor building for the center. One tenant is
approximately 10,000 sqft (Pad H) and the other is 42,000 sqft (Pad J). The building will be
constructed of stucco with a brick veneer wainscot and column features to tie in with the
original design concept for the building. This design shall also comply with the West Valley
City Commercial Design Standards so staff recommends that the north façade provide relief
along the wall elevation as well as the roof plane, similar to what was done along the south side
of the center, to break up that façade.

This site was originally planned as a building lot so there is adequate parking to accommodate
this use and development. All interior parking lot landscaping will be maintained and updated
per the original design concept for the interior islands (C-5-2006). There is existing
landscaping in the parkstrip on the north side along Highbury Pkwy that will remain. The 20’
landscape setback on the subject property must also contain trees as outlined in the previous
approvals (C- 5-2006) and the West Valley City Code (one tree every 30’ of frontage).

This overall shopping center has existing improvements for the primary pedestrian corridors.
There is an east-west connection out to 5600 West located by Ross and this phase will complete
the north-south connection for the center out to Highbury Pkwy. There is also an existing
masonry wall along the east property boundary adjacent to residential so no additional
screening is required as part of this application.
Only wall signage is proposed with this application and is subject to the West Valley City Sign
Ordinance (Title 11). If dumpsters are provided for this building they must be located within a
6’ masonry enclosure that is constructed to match the building and meet the location
requirements outlined in 7-2-118.

C-17-2023
Mountain View
Flex 5653 S 6055 W
M Zone (2.64 acres)


The applicant, Davis Warnock, is requesting conditional use approval for office/warehouse
flex buildings with outside storage, located at 5653 S 6055 W. The property is zoned M,
Manufacturing and the West Valley City General Plan designates this area as Light
Manufacturing. Zoning to the north and west is M, while to the south it is R-1-8. The
parcels to the east are in Kearns and are zoned for single unit dwellings. Adjacent land uses
include self-storage units to the north, a precast concrete plant to the west, and single unit
dwellings to the south and east.

This property is the only remaining vacant parcel along 6055 West. The applicant is
proposing to build four office/warehouse flex buildings, which include a total of 29 units.
Each unit will be approximately 1,500 sq. ft., and will include a small office space and
bathroom, with the remainder of the building open for various uses. The applicant is also
proposing a small outside storage space for each unit behind the buildings. With the current
configuration of the buildings, Bldg. D will not be allowed to have overhead doors in the
back, or an outside storage area behind the building, as a 20’ landscaped setback is required
next to residential.
There will be one access point for the project from 6055 West. With the proposed layout of
the project, the Fire Department requires an additional access point, but the parcel does not
have enough frontage to allow two access points as outlined in Title 7, 7-9-107. The
applicant is proposing to install one emergency-only vehicle access near the south end of the
parcel. The access will not include a curb cut and will only be used for emergency purposes.

Parking for the project is proposed along the front of the buildings, except for the areas in
front of the overhead doors. Based on the current proposal and square footage of uses,
there are 10 more parking spaces proposed than what the code requires. As different
tenants and businesses occupy the buildings, the on-site parking will be monitored through
tenant improvement permits and/or business license reviews.

The applicant’s proposal includes four metal buildings. Warehouse uses are not required to
meet the Commercial Design Standards (7-11-300P), but office uses are. The most
emphasis on architectural design and relief shall be placed on the street-facing building
elevations.
These sides shall be treated as primary facades and shall meet all requirements for primary
facades as outlined in 7-11-300P. The applicant is proposing to place 10’ tall CMU walls
adjacent to the buildings on the street-facing facades, to act as screening for the outside
storage areas. Typically, that tall of a fence would not be allowed, but in this instance, it
acts as an extension of the building. The applicant should incorporate the wall into the
building

design, which may help in meeting the commercial design requirements. The front side
elevations of the buildings shall be treated as secondary facades and meet all regulations as
outlined in 7-11-300P, except for the masonry requirement. The front building elevations
do not need to include 35% masonry, but should meet the building relief, design, and roof
requirements as outlined in the code.

The applicant is proposing small, outside storage areas behind the buildings for materials
and equipment storage. The storage areas will be screened behind buildings or walls. The
applicant is proposing to use gravel for surfacing, which is acceptable if vehicles are not
stored there.

Five percent landscaping is required for the site. The east, south, and west boundaries of the
parcel require 20’ of landscaping. The east and south sides adjacent to residential shall
provide 1 tree every 30’. All landscaped areas shall include 50% coverage of live plant
material. This project shall continue the park strip tree plantings that have been established
on the street to the north, which is one tree every 30’. The tree variety shall match what
exists.

The applicant is proposing to remove the berm along the east property line that had been
used historically for screening next to the residential properties. A 6’ tall masonry wall is
required on the east and south sides due to the adjacent residential uses. There is an existing
wall along the south parcel line, and the applicant is proposing to install an 8’ tall wall along
 the east property line.

 The applicant has not provided a sign plan for any proposed signage. All future signage
 needs to comply with the regulations in Title 11.



 Staff Alternatives:

 Approval, subject to the resolution of any concerns raised at the public hearing as well as
 the following:

              1. The site shall be constructed per the approved plan.
              2. There shall be no overhead garage doors on the back of Building D.
              3. Access to the site shall be reviewed and approved by the Public
                 Works Department and the Fire Department.
              4. Parking on the site shall meet the requirements of 7-9 of the West Valley
                 City Code.
              5. The side building elevations that are street-facing shall be considered primary
                 facades and shall meet the Commercial Design Standard requirements
                 outlined in 7-11-300P. The front building elevations shall be considered
                 secondary facades and shall meet all regulations outlined in 7-11-300P,
                 except masonry shall not be required.
              6. All outside storage areas shall be screened from public view and shall not be
                 located within any required landscaping or parking areas. Gravel is an
                 acceptable material for surfacing if the storage is for materials and equipment.
                 Vehicle storage is not allowed.
              7. There shall be a minimum of 5% landscaping on the site. 20’ of landscaping
                  shall be provided along south, east, and west property lines with 1 tree
                  spaced every 30’ along the east and south property lines. All landscaped
                  areas shall include 50% live plant material.
              8. Street trees shall be provided every 30 feet in the park strip, and the variety

              9. A 6’ tall masonry wall is required along the east and south property lines.
                  An 8’ tall wall is allowed with a building permit.
              10. Signage shall comply with the West Valley City Code and a building
                  permit shall be obtained.
              11. Shall meet the requirements of all affected departments and agencies.
              12. Subject to review upon valid complaint.
