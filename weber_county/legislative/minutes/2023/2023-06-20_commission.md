D. THOUGHT OF THE DAY: Commissioner Froerer: Yesterday we celebrated a holiday new to the state of Utah. I
ran into a number of people that did not understand the purpose of the Juneteenth holiday. It is a celebration of the
final emancipation. It took two years after January 1st, 1863 adoption of the Emancipation Proclamation to reach some
confederate-held states such as Texas. On June 19th, 1865, Union soldiers marched into Texas and emancipated 250k
slaves.

    5. APPROVAL OF RESOLUTION 29-2023 APPOINTING MEMBERS TO THE GRAMA APPEALS
       BOARD.

         Craig Brandt (Clerk/Auditors Office): Section 2-13-7 of the Weber County Code establishes a three-member
         appeals board to hear and decide appeals of decisions made by Weber County’s chief administrative officer
         for GRAMA appeals. The board is to be comprised of one member that is an employee of the county, and
         two members of the public, at least one with professional experience requesting or managing records.

         Roger Larsen’s retirement created a vacancy for the County’s representative on the board. Currently, James
         Ebert and Nathan Carroll are board members from the public. Nathan has since joined the County in the
         Public Defenders office and it is proposed, and he is willing, he represent the County on the GRAMA
         board. James Ebert is willing to continue serving on the board.

         It is proposed Noel Zabriskie be appointed as the second member from the public. Some background on Mr.
         Zabriskie:
               BA & Masters from BYU.
               Served as Ogden School District Superintendent from 2003-2011.
               Serves on multiple boards, including Ogden School Foundation Board.
               He’s an avid Scouter and a great singer (according to Ricky Hatch).

         In summary, two proposals are being made for the GRAMA Appeals board:
             1. Appoint Nathan Carroll to represent the County on the board
             2. Appoint Noel Zabriskie as a board member from the public.

    5. Action on public hearing:

         I2: Approval of Ordinance 2023-21 of the proposed amendment to the Weber County zoning map, changing
         certain property from A-2 (Agricultural) to M-T (Manufacturing and Technology), and allowing for the uses
         permitted within that zone and subject to the regulations associated with that zone, together with a
         development agreement with Black Pine LLC. File.
         Chair Froerer: I am comfortable with reducing from 300 foot easement down to 100 foot talked about. This
         will necessitate a change in our ordinance. As terms for the fence, I am not quite as solid giving carte
         blanche acceptance as this will be used to update the ordinance and we do not want to set a precedence. We
         do not want to put burden on future commissions to have to use taxpayer dollars to build a fence. I propose
         putting it in the development agreement to say that a fence if a fence is needed, will be required under
         developer’s expense.

         Commissioner Bolos: I did some homework yesterday while running on a trail, I saw some developments
         with and without fences. Fences really get in the way of the open views of the river and trail. Some fences
         have graffiti on them, some fences are tall. I do not think we need to require a fence. I am ok if a property
         owner wants to install a fence. If I lived along the river, I would put up my own fence for my own security.
         It’s a right property owners should have. It speaks both to the aesthetics and the property owners rights. I
         don’t think it’s our right to tell them how to protect their property.

         Commissioner Harvey: I think this gives latitude. I like what Commissioner Bolos said. I think this
         establishes a foundation without burdening this pioneering group out there.

         Chair Froerer: I contacted friends in cities farther south and some require fencing and some do not. Three of
         the four cities I talked to said if they had it to do over again they would require fences from a public safety
         concern. I don’t want to tie the hands of future Commissions 10-15 years down the road.

         Commissioner Bolos moved to approve Ordinance 2023-21 of the proposed amendment to the Weber County
