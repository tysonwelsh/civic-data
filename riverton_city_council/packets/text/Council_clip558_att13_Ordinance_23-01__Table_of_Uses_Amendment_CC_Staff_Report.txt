The Planning Commission held a public hearing on December 8, 2022, and voted to
recommend the following motion:

I move the City Council adopt Ordinance 23-01, updating and consolidating Section 18.90.010,
Table of Commercial Uses, as described in Exhibit "A" herein, with the following addition;

    1. Keep 'Landscaping Services' as a permitted use in the C-R Zone.

 Background

This is a public hearing for a proposed Text Amendment to adjust Riverton City’s Table of
Commercial Uses. This proposal does not make substantial changes to current policy or
practice. The goal of this proposal is to make it easier for customers, City officials, and city staff
to identify land uses in the City’s Commercial Zones. To achieve this goal staff identified these
objectives:
    1- Place all Commercial Zones in one Table of Uses,
    2- Eliminate categories in the Table of Uses that overlap, are defined elsewhere or are no
       longer relevant, and
    3- Add categories to address frequent questions or ongoing issues.
A copy of the proposed changes is attached, and a detailed explanation of each change is found
below.

 Explanation of proposed changes

    1. Consolidate two table of uses by adding unique uses from the PPC Table of Uses to the
       existing “Table of Commercial Uses”.

    2. Add Land Uses Permissions for the PCC Zone to existing categories unique to the Table of
       Commercial Uses.

          c. Pet boarding, associated with animal hospitals/veterinary clinics.
          d. Pet boarding, nonmedical.

    7. Add “Chiropractor and massage therapy” to the Table of Uses. This was previously
       considered “Beauty Services” or “Medical clinic” which frequently creates confusion with
       potential business owners, landlords and agents. Proposed permissions match Beauty
       Services.

    8. Remove “Construction Services” because it is covered elsewhere. This has been a point
       of frequent confusion because it overlaps with other definitions. A construction office is
       allowed under “Professional Office”. Some people assume “Construction Services” implied
       it was okay to store vehicles or supplies outdoors.

    9. Remove “Drive-through windows for food services”. The Table of Uses includes a
       category for “Drive-through windows” with similar permissions.

    10. Rename “Electrical, gas, and water utility” to “Utility, including electrical, gas, water, and
        communications, excluding cell-towers and wireless communications”. Keep the use as
        conditional in all zones. Remember cell towers and wireless communications are
        governed by existing city ordinance RCC.18.175 “Wireless Communication Towers”.

    11. Combine the table entries for “Hotel” and “Motel” under a single category “Hotel, motel”.

    12. Change “Landscaping Services” to Conditional in the C-R zone to be consistent with the
        PCC.

    13. Expand description of “Nurseries (plants)” to “Nurseries (Plants), wholesale and retail only”
        and change permissions to Conditional in the C-G Zone.

    14. Consolidate the overlapping uses of “Medical clinics- outpatient only” and “Physicians,
        dental, and other professional medical offices – outpatient only” into one category “Medical
        and dental offices and clinics, outpatient only.”

    15. Remove “Recreation” from “Recreation or fitness center/health club”.

    16. Combine sit-down and drive-through restaurants into a single category “Restaurants” with a
        note to see the entry for Drive-throughs. (see #9 above)

    17. Expand “Storage and warehousing into two categories 1- Storage and warehousing,
        standalone, and 2- Storage Units.

    18. Add category for Vehicle and Equipment storage, outdoor, not accessory to a primary use.

    19. Add category for Vehicle and Equipment storage, outdoor, accessory to an approved
        primary use.

    20. Expand “Wedding Reception Center” to “Reception Center”

 Recommendation

The Planning Commission in their hearing on December 8, 2022 to consider this item, voted to
recommend approval, with one addition. They recommended keeping “Landscaping” as a

permitted use in the Commercial Regional Zone. The reason staff’s proposed draft changed that
use from Permitted to Conditional is that such uses can include significant outside storage of
equipment and materials, which can potentially create impacts to surrounding properties, both
commercial and residential. The Conditional Use permit process allows for an additional level of
review to consider what if any screening, buffering, or other mitigation may be necessary to
minimize such impacts. Staff recommends keeping the existing draft language, with
“Landscaping Services” as a conditional use in the Commercial Regional zone.

      E.     CODE AMENDMENT – PLZ-22-5004, RIVERTON CITY IS PROPOSING
             CHANGES TO ITS LAND DEVELOPMENT AND ZONING CODE IN TITLE
             18.90.010 “TABLE OF COMMERCIAL USES.”

Mr. Prestwich reported that the above item was a legislative recommendation. Staff
presented Code Amendments to the Commission for consideration. The amendments
related to the Table of Commercial Uses. The Planning Commission recommendation would
be forwarded to the City Council, who would make the final determination. He explained
that the amendments would add clarity to the ordinance. The Table of Commercial Uses
was not the easiest for residents and developers to understand on their own. Some
amendments were proposed to address that. There was a Table of Commercial Uses for
the five commercial zones and a separate table that was largely identical in a different
section of the Code. This created confusion so the desire was to combine those.

The other issue was that there were a lot of overlapping categories. This also created a lot
of confusion. Mr. Prestwich explained that similar categories were combined to reduce that
list. Some uses had also become outdated and ceased to be meaningful in the code. There
was a detailed list and explanation included in the packet. It outlined all of the proposed
changes. There was also a sample Code with the corrections.

Commissioner Park believed the amendments would add clarity and make the language
more user-friendly. He wondered if there were any substantive changes. Mr. Prestwich
denied this. There were no substantial changes. However, the Planning Commission could
recommend additional changes to the City Council. Commissioner Breinholt asked if the
maps would change. Mr. Prestwich reported that the maps would stay the same. It was
only the table that would be altered so everything was in one place.

Chair Cluff wondered why landscaping as a use was conditional in the C-R Zone rather than
permitted. Mr. Prestwich was not certain why that change had been made. It may have
been done to match some of the previous decisions. The Commission could recommend
that it be permitted in that zone if that was desired by other Commissioners.

Chair Cluff opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public hearing
was closed.

3.     DECISION ITEMS

       A.     FINAL/DETAIL PLAN - “MOUNTAIN VIEW VILLAGE PHASE 4,” PLZ 22-
              8029, A FINAL PLAN FOR A COMMERCIAL DEVELOPMENT TO BE
              LOCATED AT APPROXIMATELY 4300 WEST 13400 SOUTH. APPLICANT
              – LANCE BLACKWOOD, RIVERTON CENTERCAL.

Mr. Prestwich reported that the applicant, Lance Blackwood, submitted a Final/Detail Plan
related to Mountain View Village Phase 4. This was the final phase of the development.
Mountain View Village was a separate and special zoning district inside of the SLR Zoning
District. As a result, Mountain View Village operated under the SLR Master Development
Agreement and zoning. Mr. Prestwich believed the Commission was familiar with Mountain
View Village. Phase 1 was finished, Phase 2 was almost completed, and Phase 3 was
approved previously. Construction for that phase would start in Spring 2023. As for Phase
4, the canal ran along the east boundary and the internal roads were all private roads.

Mr. Prestwich shared a street view of the area that looked northwest from 13400 South. He
pointed out that there is a sidewalk in place and that sidewalk would continue along the front
of Phase 4. The canal was identified. It acts as a soft boundary. The Site Plan was
reviewed. There was a private drive that came in with a right-in-right-out. The public
sidewalk would continue along the frontage to the crosswalk. There would be ADA access
to all the shops. Mr. Prestwich pointed out the main entrance and parking locations. The
first building had the potential for a drive-thru window with plenty of stacking. There were
