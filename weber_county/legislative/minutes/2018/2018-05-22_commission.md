    3. APPROVAL OF REQUEST FROM RIDGE UTAH DEVELOPMENT TO REDUCE IMPACT FEES.

         Chad Meyerhoffer, of County Engineering, stated that according to the county’s ordinance that allows this fee
         adjustment, the Ridge Utah Development requested reduction of the impact fees relating to the construction of a
         pool house, pool, and Jacuzzi in The Ridge Townhomes Phase 3 (on parcel number 22-344-0011), located at
         approximately 5300 East Moose Hollow Drive, Eden. Mr. Meyerhoffer felt comfortable with this reduction to the
         actual impact of the building for a total of $809.38 rather than to the entire property. He responded to Commissioner
         Ebert’s question stating that this item is only for the storm drains; there is no reduction for the roads.
         Commissioner Gibson moved to approve the request from Ridge Utah Development to reduce impact fees;
         Commissioner Ebert seconded.
         Commissioner Ebert – aye; Commissioner Gibson – aye; Chair Harvey – aye
     4. CONTRACT WITH MATHEW SHECHTER FOR AN ADMINISTRATIVE LAW JUDGE FOR EMPLOYMENT APPEALS.

        Sarah Swan, County Human Resources Director, stated that the county has been working on this item for a number

        Commissioner Ebert – aye; Commissioner Gibson – aye; Chair Harvey – aye

G. PUBLIC COMMENTS:

        Judge Trent Nelson, employee of Roy City as the justice court judge, has been the judge for the Roy/Weber
        County Justice Court for about a year. He is the judge for Roy, unincorporated Weber County, West Haven and
        Hooper. There has been a long standing relationship with the county and he wished to give a state of the judiciary
        system. He outlined the various types of criminal cases that he presides over. Some of the challenges facing the
        court include the huge impact from the increased filings from all jurisdictions. In the past 5-6 years they had
        averaged close to 8,000 filings/year, and in the last year they have increased to 9,000/year, a historical jump.
        Court time has increased 32% and is quite a challenge to defendants, the parties, etc., and he has worked to spread
        out the court time so people do not have to wait so long and to improve customer service. Judge Nelson is working
        hard to help people improve. He is a strong advocate of people taking responsibility and being held accountable
        for their actions so that they do not perpetuate the negative impacts to families and communities. There has also
        been a huge turnover in the court staff. He noted that one court efficiency measurable is how long a case takes
        from the filing to when there is a disposition. When he started with the Roy court, the figures were around 70-
        74/% of cases being disposed of within the recommended time. The goal set by the State Judiciary is 95% and
        they are now at 87% and continue to improve. He said that the county had petitioned to change its affiliation with
        Roy Justice Court and they are somewhat in limbo structurally in terms of hiring. Chair Harvey expressed
        appreciation for the state of the judiciary and requested that he continue to give reports. Judge Nelson plans to
        do so once a year.
