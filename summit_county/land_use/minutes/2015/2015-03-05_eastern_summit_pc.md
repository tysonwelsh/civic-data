1. Discussion regarding possible updates to the Eastern Summit County Zoning 
Maps from Henefer to Coalville – Planning Department Staff  
 
Community Development Director Patrick Putt welcomed the public.  He said he 
wanted to receive the public’s input on the initial mapping.  This is an informal work 
session.  He invited the public to gather around the table to review the maps.   
 
Director Putt outlined the new zone districts that are being considered: 
 
1) An Agricultural zone (AG-1).  This zone allows one unit per acre.  This zone may 
become the replacement for the highway corridor (HC) zone.  They don’t want to create a 
zone that has unintended consequences such as non-conformity.     
2) The rural residential zone (RR) is one unit per one acre.  Director Putt said the difference 
between this zone and the AG-1 zone is that the RR zone will have a more restrictive list 
of agricultural uses.  The minimum lot size might be as small as .75 acre.   
3) The residential subdivision (RS) zone would be a single family development with little to 
no agricultural uses.  This could be as much as three units per acre.   
4) The recreation commercial (RC) zone.  This would require a rezone.   
5) The AG-6 zone.  Potentially this could be applied to a combination of the HC zone and 
the AP-40.  In Eastern Summit County, 60% to 75% of the AP-40 zone is non-
conforming in size.  This is a transitional zone that might help to solve some of 
nonconforming problems.   
   
Director Putt said the question is how they should move forward to map these zones.  
They want to recommend to the County Council a base map that would be the starting 
point for zoning.  The Commission is considering that the AG-1 become the replacement 
to the highway corridor (HC) zone.  Another question is where to apply the AG-6 zone 
and where would the AG-40 zone remain.     
 
Director Putt said under this proposal, there would be an opportunity for property 
owners to rezone to some of the new zones.  Possible evaluation criteria might be the 
following:    
• What is the existing parcel size or operation? 
• Are the parcels located in areas that have an existing development pattern?   
• Are the parcels in areas that have a property size pattern?   
• Will this help to eliminate the non-conforming lot size?

Director Putt said he is happy that Mayor Johnson from Coalville is present.  Mayor 
Ovard from Henefer had previous commitments.  He explained the State requires 
municipalities to have annexation areas to accommodate possible future growth.  This area is 
called an annexation declaration area (ADA).  These lands may or may not come into the 
cities at some point.  Until they are taken into a city’s jurisdiction, they are still part of the 
unincorporated County.  The towns are required to identify the ADA boundaries.  There must 
be a long-range land use map associated with the ADA.   
 
Director Putt said for this mapping exercise, Staff has indentified where these zones are 
located and what the municipalities have projected for the area.  The County wants to be in- 
sync with the municipalities.  The County doesn’t want to compete or surprise the 
municipalities.
