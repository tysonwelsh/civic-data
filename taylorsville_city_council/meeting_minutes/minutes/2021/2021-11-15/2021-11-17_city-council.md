Councilmember Armstrong indicated that he had missed the meeting following a recent fatal auto-
pedestrian accident but asked that the city consider improving lighting at all the main

Councilmember Burgess had heard from a constituent who expressed concern about transients

plan for addressing the ongoing problem. After some discussion it was found that the TVPD was
actively working on the issue and their efforts appeared to be effective. Mayor Overson explained
that the Jordan River Commission had representation from each city along the river and were well
aware of the problem.

In response to a question from Mayor Overson, Chief Brady Cottam responded that the city
received funding from the Division of Wildlife which was earmarked for enforcement near the
river. The work typically took place in the summer months which was when the problem was at
its peak. Taylorsville was taking care of problem activity within its borders.

Councilmember Harker asked if the city would be hosting a holiday event the following month.
Mayor Overson responded that there would be an open house during business hours on December
9th to honor community and business leaders. The councilmembers would soon be receiving
invitations.

5.      PLANNING MATTERS

        5.1      Public Hearing – To Receive Public Comment regarding Needs for the
                 Upcoming 2022-2023 Plan Year of the Community Development Block Grant
                 (CDBG) Program – Ken Donarksi

       Ken Donarski reminded the council that the city contracted with him to consult on the
Community Development Grant (CDBG) program and HOME funds. He explained that this public
comment period was an opportunity for service providers who sought CDBG funding from the
city to explain the funding needs they saw within the city and possibly to illustrate the ways in
which the Taylorsville community benefitted from those services. He explained that the following
day there would be a training opportunity to help service providers understand the application
process and pre-applications could begin the following Friday.

Chair Cochran asked about the funding timetable. Mr. Donarki responded that pre-applications
could be submitted from November 19th through December 12th. The formal application period
was December 13th through January 13th, and the applications would then be presented to Mayor
Overson for her recommendations. Those recommendations would then be presented to the council
for ratification.

Chair Cochran opened the public hearing comment period at 7:01 p.m.

Andres Calderon of Assist Inc. explained that his group had been assisting Salt Lake County
residents since 1969. Assist Inc. provided emergency home repairs, accessibility design assistance
and community design programs. Their organization provided small construction grants for
households with significant need of critical home repairs such as replacing leaky roofs, broken
sewer lines and non-functioning furnaces. Accessibility modifications provided dignity and
independence to those with physical disabilities and for the aging population. Over the last fifty
years, such interventions represented over $20 million in community investment/assistance. Since
2000, 923 projects had been performed in Taylorsville.

Nick Whittaker explained that Utah 211 (part of United Way of Utah) was a state-wide health and
human services referral system that connected people to a variety of necessities. During FY20-21,
the agency had 4,182 interactions with Taylorsville residents. The most common needs these
residents had were housing, shelter, utilities, employment, healthcare, income and food. Mr.
Whittaker asked that Utah 211 be allowed to continue serving Taylorsville residents with
continued funding through the CDBG program.

There were no additional public comments and Chair Cochran declared the public hearing closed
at 7:08 p.m.

7.      OTHER MATTERS

        7.1      Ordinance No. 21-17 – An Ordinance of the City of Taylorsville authorizing
                 the Creation of a Community Advisory Board for the Taylorsville Police
                 Department – Jay Ziolkowski

       Jay Ziolkowski expressed thanks to Chief Cottam for presenting the report from the
Transition Team at a previous city council meeting.

Mr. Ziolkowski explained that the team had known from the beginning that it was important to
have a Community Advisory Board for the Taylorsville Police Department. They had initially
hoped to have the Board in place when the department began operations on July 1 but realized that
it was best to spend more time on its creation. They now sought to have the board in place by
January 1, 2022. It would consist of five members who would provide advice and review pertaining
to incidents involving pursuits, and use of force, as well as on internal investigations. This was in
line with “best practices” at the national level. Each board member would receive training, have
the opportunity to participate in ride-a-longs and be made aware of policies, procedures, funding,
etc.

As noted in Ordinance No. 21-17, an administrative policy had been created which would remain
adaptive as changes in law enforcement occurred.

Councilmember Cochran asked Mr. Ziolkowski to describe the application process for serving on
the board. Mr. Ziolkowski responded that several community members had already reached out to
the city and expressed interest in serving on the board. There was also a group of community
members who had been highly recommended and who had helped create a strategic plan for the
new police department. Those people would be contacted, and a general notice would be posted
for anyone interested in serving on the new board. Appointment to the board would be made by
Mayor Overson with the advice and consent of the city council.

Councilmember Harker inquired how often the board would meet and whether they would present
their findings to the city council. Mr. Ziolkowski responded that the board would meet monthly
and that the intent was for the board to make presentations to the council on both a quarterly and
an annual basis.

Councilmember Barbieri asked for a brief overview of what the city hoped to accomplish by
having a Citizen Advisory Board. Mr. Ziolkowski explained that the idea was to ensure that the
community had the opportunity to review the types of hot topics that often ended up in the news.
The city sought to dispel the belief that law enforcement issues would be covered up and instead
to increase transparency. Ideally, board members would understand the dynamics of what it meant
to work in law enforcement but perhaps had a different perspective. It was hoped that the board
members would be able to provide feedback to Chief Cottam and the city administration from a
community member’s perspective regarding the appropriateness of officers’ actions.

Councilmember Barbieri asked if the intent was to have the Advisory Board share its information
with the public or would the information be kept between the board and city leaders? There was
an expectation that certain matters would be kept private (such as an officer’s name) but the intent
was for the board to share their knowledge of the police departments’ training, funding and
policies.

Chair Cochran asked about pre-requisites for serving on the Citizen Advisory Board and wondered
if any groups would be excluded from serving on the board. In response, Mr. Ziolkowski indicated
that members would simply need to be willing to attend training and be genuinely engaged with
the community. City Attorney Tracy Cowdell clarified that board members would need to undergo
background checks since they would have access to sensitive information. Additionally, he
suggested that elected officials not serve on the board since it should be autonomous from policy
makers.

Councilmember Burgess was happy to see checks and balances in place.
