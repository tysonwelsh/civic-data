SUMMARY
This application is a request for approval of a zoning map and land use map amendment
for approximately 9.81 acres (see Exhibit B) located at approximately 12197 S. 300 E. (see
Exhibit C). The site has a land use designation of Neighborhood Commercial and is

Residential High Density and rezone the entire property from A5 to RM2.

BACKGROUND
The subject property has historically been utilized as agricultural or farming land since at
least 1978 when the City incorporated. There have been previous applications to rezone
the property and the most recent one was in the fall of 2020. The fall 2020 application was
for a commercial zoning, and there were concerns expressed about that request from the
public. The applicant has submitted this application for residential zoning based on those
concerns raised.

General Plan and Zoning. The Land Use Map of the General Plan identifies the property as
Neighborhood Commercial (see Exhibit E). This category is characterized as follows:




The property has been assigned the A5 zoning classification, which allows both residential
and agricultural uses (see Exhibit F). The purpose of the agricultural zones per Draper City
Municipal Code (DCMC) Section 9-8-020 is “to preserve and protect agricultural lands and
related activities, to permit activities normally and necessarily related to agricultural
production, and to prohibit land uses that may undermine continued agricultural activity.”

A Map Amendment is a matter committed to the legislative discretion of the City Council
and is not controlled by any one standard. However, in making an amendment, the
Planning Commission should consider the following factors in Section 9-5-060(E) of the
DCMC in making its recommendation to the City Council:

        1.      Map Amendments:
                a.   Whether the proposed amendment is consistent with goals, objectives
                     and policies of the city's general plan;
                b.   Whether the proposed amendment is harmonious with the overall
                     character of existing development in the vicinity of the subject
                     property;
                c.   Whether the proposed amendment is consistent with the standards of
                     any applicable overlay zone;
                d.   The extent to which the proposed amendment may adversely affect
                     adjacent property; and
                e.   The adequacy of facilities and services intended to serve the subject
                     property, including, but not limited to, roadways, parks and recreation
                     facilities, police and fire protection, schools, stormwater drainage
                     systems, water supplies, and wastewater and refuse collection.

STAFF RECOMMENDATION
Staff recommends that the Planning Commission review the request, receive public
comment, and make a recommendation to the City Council based on the findings listed
below and the criteria for approval, or denial, as listed within the staff report.

The findings for a positive recommendation are as follows:

    1.      That DCMC Section 9-5-060(E) provides the approval standards for amending
            Draper City’s zoning map.
    2.      That this map amendment does not compromise any of the five factors listed in
            DCMC Section 9-5-060(E)(1).
    3.      This land use map amendment proposes a land use designation that has less
            intensive traffic uses and therefore would result in a reduced traffic impact to
            the surrounding area than the current commercial land use designation.
    4.      The proposed residential land use and zoning category will provide a buffer from
            the existing commercial uses to the south and the existing single-family
            residential to the north.

The findings for a negative recommendation are as follows:

    1.      That this map amendment compromises one or more of the five factors listed in
            DCMC Section 9-5-060(E)(1).

Engineering and Public Works Divisions Review.
1.     The adequacy of facilities and services intended to serve the subject property,
       including but not limited to roadways, parks and recreation facilities, police and fire
       protection, schools, storm water drainage systems, water supplies, and waste water
       and refuse collection. Other than noted below, we are not aware of any
       inadequacies of the facilities intended to serve this property.
       a.     The increase in density from one residential unit per five (5) acres zoning to
              up to approximately twelve units per acre constitutes significant increase in
              the amount of traffic to and from the subject area. One single family
              residential structure and associated agricultural uses constitutes up to nine
              daily trips and two peak hour trips, based on industry standard vehicle trip
              tables. Typically, in the RM2 zone, residential density can be similar with just
              over one peak hour trip per unit and a similar number of vehicle trips per
              day. This increase in traffic would be added to 300 East, 12200 South, and
              other surrounding streets where there exists significant traffic congestion at
              the present time. Traffic issues in the area begin in the AM peak time and
              extend throughout the day through the PM peak travel time. Due to a
              private school in the area, extensive retail, and dining businesses, there are
              additional peak travel times throughout the day, like at lunchtime, end of
              school, etc. Extension of 12200 South between 300 East and 450 East is on
              the city’s master transportation plan. Expansion of 12300 South in the area
              is a future Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT) project.
       b.     Connectivity with the subject parcel to a public right-of-way is on 300 East or
              a future 12200 South extension through the site. There is an arterial street
              just south of the site owned by UDOT, 12300 South. Specific access locations
              will be evaluated with any site plan or subdivision submittal. Access locations
              will be required to meet Draper City Standards as outlined in our Master
              Transportation Plan and according to UDOT standards. A Traffic Impact
              Study will be required with a site plan or subdivision application based on the
              proposed use and projected traffic volume.
       c.       There are existing storm drainage facilities available fronting the subject
                area, but due to geographic limitations, may not be feasible to connect.
                Provisions for onsite storm drainage will need to be addressed with any
                subdivision or site plan application, and shall comply with the provisions of
                the development requirements within the Draper City Municipal Code.
       d.       Sanitary sewer facilities will be provided by South Valley Sewer District. Any
                subdivision application will require a commitment to serve from the Sewer
                District that facilities are adequate to provide service for the proposed uses.
       e.       Drinking water facilities will be provided by WaterPro. Any subdivision
                application will require a commitment to serve from the WaterPro that
                facilities are adequate to provide service for the proposed uses.
