       A.     Public Meeting: Danner I-ADU Deviation Request.
              (Administrative Action)
              On the request of Karen Danner, representing Draper Danner Family Trust, a request
              for approval of a Deviation from strict compliance with the geographical restrictions
              for the creation of an Internal Accessory Dwelling Unit (I-ADU) on approximately
              0.19 acres, located at 1177 E. Hickenlooper Way. Known as application 2024-0419-
              VAR,        Staff       Contact:     Nick      Whittaker,      (801)      576-6522,
              Nick.Whittaker@Draperutah.gov.

City Planner, Nick Whittaker, presented the Staff Report and stated that there are two types of
Accessory Dwelling Units (“ADU”) allowed by Draper City Code. They include Internal Accessory
Dwelling Units (“I-ADU”), which are attached to the primary structure, and Detached Accessory
Dwelling Units (“D-ADU”), which are separate from the primary structure. The vicinity, aerial, land
use, and zoning maps were displayed. The property’s Land Use Designation is Residential Medium
Density. Mr. Whittaker noted that the Deviation was required because the subject property is in the
I-ADU Prohibited Area.

The Site Plan and elevations were reviewed. The I-ADU would be in the basement of the home with
a separate entrance from the main structure, and the proposed parking is located to the side and in
front of the third-car garage.

In response to a question, Planning Manager, Todd Draper, stated that the prohibited areas have streets
that are narrower than the minimum public street width or are private streets where the City would
have no control over on-street parking. The City was only allowed to require one parking spot for an
I-ADU under State code, and there was a concern that additional vehicles would be parked on the
narrow streets. Deviations are allowed for homes in the prohibited area that have a second parking
stall available for the I-ADU. As long as the stalls for the I-ADU do not impede the two required
parking stalls for the primary dwelling, the property complies with Code requirements.

The applicant, Karen Danner, stated that they purchased the home 30 years previously and always
planned to have a basement apartment. They began working on it in 2010 but were unable to complete
it at that time. Her husband had since passed away and she now had the resources to finish the I-
ADU. She reported that her neighbors approve of her plans for the I-ADU.

It was clarified that it was a public meeting, not a public hearing, and was noticed as such.

In response to a question regarding parking, Mr. Draper clarified that the Planning Commission was
considering the Deviation request only. If the property were not in the prohibited area, the application
would not have come before the Planning Commission. Mr. Whittaker had reviewed the application
and determined it to be in compliance with the provisions for accessory dwelling units.

Motion: Commissioner Nixon moved to APPROVE the Deviation request from strict
compliance with the geographical restrictions for I-ADUs, as requested by Karen Danner
representing the Draper Danner Family Trust, Application 2024-0419-VAR, based on the
following:

Findings for Approval:

       1.      The lot or parcel on which the primary dwelling is located is at least six thousand
               square feet (6,000 ft²);

       2.      No D-ADU exists on the property;

       3.      The proposed I-ADU can meet all of the requirements of DCMC sections 9-31-
               030 and 9-31-045, including other applicable requirements of chapter 9-31 before
               issuance of an ADU permit; and

       4.      At least two (2) parking stalls meeting the requirements of 9-31-045(B)(2) are
               provided for use by occupants of the I-ADU.

Second: Commissioner Fowler seconded the motion.

Vote on Motion: 5-0 in favor.

                 (Legislative Item)
                 On the Request of Brandon Preece of Gilson Engineering, representing Vanguard QA,
                 LLC, a request for a Zoning Map Amendment from the RA1 (Residential Agricultural,
                 40,000 ft² min. lot size) to the RA2 (Residential Agricultural, 20,000 ft² min. lot size)

                 Known as Application 2024-0340-MA, Staff Contact: Nick Whittaker, 801-576-6522,
                 nick.whittaker@draperutah.gov.

Mr. Whittaker presented the Staff Report and stated that the property is comprised of three parcels.
The current Land Use is Residential Low-Medium Density. The current zoning is RA1, which
requires a 40,000-square-foot minimum lot size, and the proposed amendment would rezone the
property to RA2, which has a 20,000-square-foot minimum lot size. Both zones have the same
description in the City Code. Zoning and aerial maps and a property photograph were reviewed.

In response to a question from Commissioner Nixon, it was confirmed that the access road to the west
provides access to a residence south of the subject property and that the property will retain access
rights to the private road.

The applicant, Brandon Preece, stated that the rezoning request is the first step in a planned
subdivision. Plans have been completed and will be submitted for Staff review once the rezoning is
approved. An access agreement is in place with the property owner to the south to develop the dirt
road into a paved access road.

Chair Adams opened the public hearing.

       F.      Public Hearing: New Union LLC. Home Occupation Conditional Use Permit
               (Administrative item)
               Item to be continued to the February 13, 2025 Planning Commission Meeting.
               On behalf of Quinn Edwards, representing New Union, LLC. a request for approval
               of a Home Occupation Conditional Use Permit to operate a home-based business
               office that sells and ships art prints to private buyers on approximately 0.72 Acres
               located at approximately 12785 South 1300 East. Known as application 2024-0389-
               USE, Staff Contact: Nick Whittaker, 801-576-6522, nick.whittaker@draperutah.gov.

The above item was continued to the February 13, 2025 meeting of the Planning Commission.

Community Development Director, Jennifer Jastremsky, provided an update on items discussed at
the January 7, 2025, City Council Meeting. The Mercer Mountain Development Agreement
Amendment was approved as presented. The rezoning request for the Suncrest Reception Center was
denied, as they found that the proposed zone did not fit the neighborhood. Because the property is
surrounded by a residential neighborhood, they believed that such an intense commercial use was not
appropriate for the area. The public turnout was similar to the Planning Commission meeting. The
Commissioners expressed surprise at the denial.

In response to a question raised, Ms. Jastremsky stated that she is unsure of the owner’s plans moving
forward.

Chair Adams asked if Staff believed public clamor influenced the decision. City Attorney, Traci
Gundersen reported that public clamor can be considered in zoning change requests. Ms. Jastremsky
added that State law gives the Council broad authority on the decision. Differences between the
responsibilities and authority of the Planning Commission and City Council were discussed. The
Planning Commission has more limited authority because if an item meets the criteria, it must be
approved. The possibility of appeal and potential future uses of the property were discussed.
Commissioner Nixon stated that it was not the Planning Commission’s job to determine its highest
and best use, but rather to determine if it makes sense from a planning perspective and meets the
required parameters. Chair Adams clarified that the Commission could not approve or deny an
application based solely on whether it was the highest and best use of the property, but they could
provide that input to the City Council. He gave the example of the applicant who wanted to install a
hedgerow in place of a solid fence and indicated that it was an allowed use but not a practical one.
He spoke against the use in the hope that the comment would carry weight with the City Council.

Ms. Jastremsky reviewed the City Council’s findings for the denial of the zoning map amendment as
follows:

       1.      It is not consistent with surrounding uses, which are predominantly residential or low-
               impact commercial.

       2.      This amendment would adversely impact adjacent properties.

       3.      The presumption has not been overcome, and the current existing use is sufficient.
