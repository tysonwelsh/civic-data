PUBLIC COMMENT PERIOD
Jim Vesock reported attending the Salt Lake County Council’s recent budget meeting. He
noted the meeting was lengthy and shared his appreciation for West Valley’s finance staff,
particularly praising Finance Director Jim Welch for providing clear, accessible budget
information—something he found lacking in the County's presentation materials. Jim also
shared concerns raised during the County meeting about increased security for public
officials. He noted that Mayor Jenny Wilson now has a security detail due to rising threats,
and that County Council members also have access to similar protection when needed. He
expressed sadness that the current social climate requires such measures for those serving
the public. Regarding WestFest, Jim said he attended for several days and generally thought
it was a well-organized and enjoyable event, with improved layout and more shade.
However, he relayed concerns from vendors about poor sales, vendor redundancy, and
issues with music volume near food booths. He suggested the WestFest Committee
consider limiting similar vendor types to help support better sales outcomes in future years.

Cheneil Hill, representing SAFE (Stand Against Fear and Exploitation), introduced the
organization as a Utah-based 501(c)(4) nonprofit made up of survivors, advocates,
families, and citizens focused on protecting the vulnerable and demanding accountability.
While one of their current campaigns targets sex offender registry reform, their broader
mission includes addressing systemic failures in handling abuse across institutions such as
government, religion, and education. SAFE also advocates for victims of domestic
violence, elder abuse, trafficking, and institutional cover-ups, and supports families
affected by preventable tragedies. Cheneil emphasized that SAFE is not a single-issue
group but a movement for justice and community empowerment. She requested the
Council’s support—not in the form of funding or public endorsements, but in collective
backing to demonstrate to lawmakers that communities across Utah demand stronger
protections. She concluded by thanking the Council for their service and expressing
appreciation for their work on behalf of the community.

B.     ACCEPT PUBLIC INPUT REGARDING APPLICATION GP-2-2025,
       FILED BY WEST VALLEY CITY, REQUESTING A GENERAL PLAN
       CHANGE TO PROVIDE A COMPREHENSIVE UPDATE TO THE MAJOR
       STREET PLAN
       Mayor Lang informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular Council
       Meeting scheduled June 24, 2025 in order for the City Council to hear and consider
       public comments Application GP-2-2025, Filed by West Valley City, Requesting the
       GP-2-2025, Filed by West Valley City, Requesting a General Plan Change to Provide
       a Comprehensive Update to the Major Street Plan

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

              The Major Street Plan defines street alignments and right-of-way widths for
              existing and proposed major streets within the City and is referenced several
              times in the City’s Zoning Ordinance. The City first adopted a Major Street
              Plan in August of 1980 as Ordinance 80-05. Since that time there have been
              numerous revisions made to this Plan as the City has grown and changed.

              Staff is proposing a comprehensive update to the City’s Major Street Plan
              based on travel demand modeling for the year 2050 performed by the
              transportation consulting firm Parametrix. This effort was undertaken for
              the reasons listed below:

                  •   Staff wanted to verify that the street right-of-way widths
                      recommended in the Major Street Plan were still supported by the
                      latest travel demand modeling.

                 •   Staff is working on several updates to the General Plan to comply
                     with State code requirements that must be met by the end of 2025.
                     While making these State mandated changes like adopting station
                     area plans and a water element, staff wanted the rest of the General

                 •   Staff is also preparing an update to the City’s transportation impact

 CHANGE TO AMEND SECTION 7-2-112 OF THE WEST VALLEY CITY
 MUNICIPAL CODE TO PERMIT A MOBILE FINANCIAL INSTITUTION
 AS A TEMPORARY USE
 Mayor Lang informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular Council
 Meeting scheduled June 24, 2025 in order for the City Council to hear and consider
 public comments regarding application ZT-2-2025, Filed by Granite Credit Union,
 Requesting a Zone Text Change to Amend Section 7-2-112 of the West Valley City
 Municipal Code to Permit a Mobile Financial Institution as a Temporary Use

 Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
 information as follows:

    The applicant submits this request based on the following considerations:

        1. Continuity of Financial Services: When a financial institution
           undergoes remodeling or reconstruction, community members who rely
           on in-person banking services may experience significant disruption.
           Allowing a financial mobile branch ensures uninterrupted access to
           essential banking services, such as deposits, withdrawals, loans, and
           financial counseling.
        2. Alignment with Existing Temporary Land Use Permits: The
           ordinance already permits mobile medical units and temporary
           structures for construction and sales offices. Financial mobile branches
           serve a similar public benefit by maintaining community access to
           critical financial resources during construction.
        3. Economic and Community Stability: Access to financial services
           plays a vital role in maintaining economic stability for individuals and
           small businesses. Allowing temporary mobile branches helps customers
           avoid financial hardship by ensuring they can continue managing their
           banking needs locally.
        4. Regulated and Temporary Use: The proposed amendment maintains
           reasonable land-use regulations by requiring financial mobile branch
           permits to be renewed every six months while construction is ongoing,
           ensuring they remain temporary and are not used indefinitely.

 Mayor Lang opened the Public Hearing. There being no one to speak in favor or
 opposition, Mayor Lang closed the Public Hearing.

CONSENT AGENDA
A. RESOLUTION 25-86: APPROVE A CERTIFICATION OF ELIGIBLE
   EMPLOYEES TO THE DEPARTMENT OF PUBLIC SAFETY
   Mayor Lang presented proposed resolution 25-86 that would approve a Certification of
   Eligible Employees to the Department of Public Safety.

   Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information
   as follows:

       The Local Public Safety and Firefighter Surviving Spouse Trust Fund provides
       financial support to agencies by reimbursing the cost of continued health coverage
       for the surviving spouses and children of Public Safety Officers and Firefighters
       who die in the line of duty.

       For the upcoming fiscal year, the contribution rate is $100 per eligible employee.

       Participation in the Trust Fund is mandatory for all agencies employing one or
       more Public Safety Officers or Firefighters eligible for Utah Retirement System
       service credit under Title 49 of the Utah State Retirement and Insurance Benefits
       Act, unless exempt under Utah Code Section 53-17-201(3).
     MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING JUNE 24, 2025
                           -7-

       Effective May 7, 2025, two statutory changes were enacted by the Utah
       Legislature.

           •   Vision and dental premiums are now eligible for reimbursement by the
               fund.
           •   Reimbursement for health, dental, and vision insurance now begins on the
               date of the line-of-duty death, eliminating the previous requirement that
               the employing agency cover the first 12 months of premiums.

       These changes enhance the scope of support provided to the families of fallen
       public safety employees and reinforce the state’s commitment to those who serve
       and sacrifice for their communities.
