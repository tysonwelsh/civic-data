Mayor Silvestrini called the meeting to order at 5:05 p.m. and welcomed all those in attendance as well

Discussion with John Miller, Salt Lake County, Millcreek Engineer, Engineering Updates and Discussion
Regarding an Interlocal Cooperative Agreement with Salt Lake County for CATNIP (County-Wide Active

Transportation Program)

Mr. John Miller, Millcreek Engineer, updated the Council on some details regarding some project he is
working on. He indicated they painted on Jupiter Drive; however, there was an another accident on
Jupiter Drive. He stated he was curious to get the police report to see if the painting was the purpose of
the accident.

Mayor Silvestrini explained the painting of Jupiter Drive narrowed the lanes of traffic, and it was a traffic
calming strategy.

Council Member Jackson inquired what the sign said. Mr. Miller said the sign is a flashing sign and the
engineers will be able to get data from the speed. Mayor Silvestrini added the City will be able to get a
lot of different of data. The neighborhood discussed a lot of different strategies, and they are trying to
get a solution. The painting of the street was the cheapest strategy to try first.

Mr. Miller explained he forgot Millcreek is its own entity now as well as forgetting to geta signed
interlocal agreement with the City. The interlocal is an agreement that will address calming the streets
throughout the entire City. He added he will be preparing mini reports and he will give the Council the
reports at a future date. In addition, it may require funding requirements.

Mr. Miller noted he has been working on Upland Drive with the schools and communities. He informed
the Council they do not want to do the project until school is out of session; they do not want to impact
the children’s path to school. They will start the project as soon as school is out, and the goal is to be
completed before school is back in session in the fall.

Council Member Marchant addressed a street by a park from 700 East to 500 East. He noted one of the
neighbors bought his own speed signs, which he realized is not legal; however, he wondered what the
City could do about the situation. Furthermore, there are cars parked on the streets and that becomes a
one-way street.

Mr. Miller inquired if the neighborhood is suggesting no parking on the street. Council Member
Marchant indicated that some had suggested speed bumps; however, he believes there are less
expensive options. Mr. Miller indicated they would document what the data is and get the counts
together.

Council Member Uipi addressed the Upland project. She said she would like the information six months
in advance to go to the Community Councils so they can be consistent. Ms. Lund stated she has been in
touch with Skyline High School and she thought it might be time to start that process. Council Member
Uipi noted it is not time to change the design, but it is open for comment and time to work with the
community.

Mr. Miller addressed CATNIP. He informed the Council the County received money from the State for
transportation. The Council applied for it prior to Millcreek being incorporated, and the interlocal is the
paperwork to make sure the City and County is all documented for the bike land project. He added it is
under budget; he just needed a recommendation.

Item 1. Matt Gardner, Presentation and Discussion Regarding an Emergency Cache at Rosecrest
Elementary

Council Member Jackson prefaced the agenda item. She explained he asked Mr. Gardner to address the
Council regarding the emergency cache at Rosecrest Elementary. She indicated the Rosecrest has about
3,500 homes in the area. She pointed out not everyone has checks, and they thought it would be a good
idea to have an online payment option.

Mr. Gardner informed the Council he is a software engineer, and he writes financial software for
computer systems. He went on to say that he looked at various payment options and he thought a
Venmo account is the best option. An individual will only need a phone number or a phone number
through Google Voice that can receive voicemail and other people can text you with their information.
Mr. Gardner explained the process of utilizing Venmo.

Council Member Uipi said Venmo is very similar to PayPal and someone will oversee the account.
Council Member Jackson added it is an App that does not charge a fee. Mr. Gardner said he setup a test
account and verified in order to withdraw money, the account holder has to verify it’s the owners
account; however, to deposit money, the account owner does not have to verify the account.

Mr. Gardner indicated he is willing to work with Linda Milne and Council Member Jackson to set the
account up for the City. Mr. Gardner suggested if the City wanted to set up an official City Venmo
account, the City will want a dedicated device to utilized.

Mr. Gardner said as far as he is aware, there is nothing that said a City agency cannot use Venmo.
Council Member Marchant suggested the payments go into the savings account and then the money can
be transferred.

Mr. Geilmann clarified if the money/donation is for the school. Council Member Jackson indicated the
donation is not for the school. The school is only allowing the City to utilize the location to store the
resources.

Mayor Silvestrini stated the City would need to go through the process of setting up an account. He
suggested setting up an Enterprise account for the deposit of donations for emergency services. Council
Member Jackson indicated their goal is to have it set up by next week.

Council Member Uipi indicated she was okay with setting up the account with Venmo; however, she
thought they needed to setup a policy or standard operating policy for how they setup APPs, who will
handle it, how they handle donations, etc. Mr. Geilmann said he would speak to a few different
financial officers, and make sure the City passes the required tests for the City’s auditors.

Item 2. Discussion of New Items for Subsequent Consideration

Mayor Silvestrini said an item that needs to be revisited in October is a revision to the interlocal
agreement with SLVLESA. He explained the SLVLESA Board moved the agreement to their next meeting
in October because they wanted to presented to the Metro Townships.

Council Member Uipi pointed out October 9, 2017, is Columbus Day, and she thought they needed to
talk about not meeting on that date. Mayor Silvestrini agreed; the Council will not meet that night.

Mayor Silvestrini indicated the Council might need to discuss financing options during the Council's
October 2, 2017, meeting.

Mr. Brems informed the Council the “Camping Ordinance” is ready for the Council’s review. Mayor
Silvestrini the ordinance might be useful for Unified Police to have a tool to be able to prohibit camping
on public property in Millcreek. However, the City will want a permitting process to allow organizations,
such as, Boy Scouts, to allow them to use public space. He added that Unified Police does recommend
that the City adopt an ordinance in the event it is needed, and it can be used.

Mayor Silvestrini suggested the Council might want to consider an ordinance for signs to encourage
individuals to give their resources to organizations instead of panhandlers. He thought they should
come up with a list of organizations and put some emphasis in the City’s newsletter.

Council Member Jackson requested an update on the City’s General Plan process from the City’s
consultants. Mayor Silvestrini indicated he could do that; he said he and some other individuals went to
Envision Utah, and they received some information, which he shared. Council Member Catten added
they had a hard time getting individuals there west of Highland Drive.

Mayor Silvestrini informed the Council they might be receiving some additional ordinances from the
Planning Commission. In addition, he would presume they are working through the development
agreement process. He noted during the last Planning Commission meeting, there was some discussion
regarding a development agreement, and the developer was willing to do an agreement, and the
Planning Commission want to do the agreement after approval of the site. Mayor Silvestrini explained a
development agreement is negotiated between the Planning staff and the applicant, and a commitment
from the developer is obtained before it goes to the Planning Commission so they accommodated. He
went on to say, that way the Planning Commission knows what the City wants to see; the development
agreement is reviewed by City staff; and finally, the Planning Commission makes a recommendation to
the City Council. He stated | think we do want to clarify that to the Planning Commission.

Council Member Marchant indicated Wasatch Front Waste & Recycling is scheduling a fee increase, and
Pam Roberts would like to come before the City Council to give a proposal. He suggested October 2,
2017. Council Member Jackson noted it is good for the City to be informed.

Mayor Silvestrini said the Finance Officer with SLVLESA will be stopping in on October 16, 2017, to
address the Council. He said SLVLESA is proposing an increase; however, Millcreek’s residents will not be
assessed the increase. He explained the City will have to make a recommendation to SLVLESA’s Board.
The cities that are withdrawing will ratify the remaining cities vote.

In addition, on October 16, 2017, Ben Horsley, Granite School District, wants to come before the Council
and make a presentation regarding the Schoo! District’s proposed bond for renovations to Skyline,
Evergreen, and Oakridge schools.

Council Member Marchant discussed parking on public streets for weeks/months at a time. He said the
City does not have an ordinance that addresses this issue. He questioned if the City could do something
about large vehicles and trucks on the street. Mayor Silvestrini indicated there are cities that have an
ordinance that prohibits a vehicle from parking on a street for more than 24-hours at a time. He said he
thought an ordinance that required vehicles to be moved periodically would suffice.

Council Member Uipi inquired if there has been conversations with the vehicle owners. Council
Member Marchant said there has been, and they just move the vehicle around the corner. Council
Member Uipi expressed concern about writing ordinance for resident complaints.

Mayor Silvestrini stated that individuals would not be happy; however, they should not be allowed to
park for long periods of time on public streets. The issue should be forwarded to legal counsel. Council
Member Uipi addressed safety hazards as the City approached the winter months and snow plowing.
Mr. Brems indicated he would bring back some suggestions for the Council’s review. He noted he
thought the City will need a recommendation from their traffic engineer first to make a
recommendation on an ordinance. Mayor Silvestrini stated he thought there are standards for that.

item 3. Discussion of items on Regular Meeting Agenda
With no further business coming before the Council during their Work Session, the meeting adjourned

at 6:41 p.m., and Mayor Silvestrini indicated the Council would recess into their break and reconvene
back into their Regular Council meeting at 7:00 p.m.
