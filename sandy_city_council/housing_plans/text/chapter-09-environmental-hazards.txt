Introduction and Background

There are hills and ridges within Sandy City which because of their physical dominance constitute
significant natural topographical features and comprise a large part of the natural open space and
scenic resources of the community.

It is desirable to require in these areas an alternative approach to traditional and conventional flat land
practices of residential development, to keep grading and cut and fill operations consistent with the
retention of the natural character of the hillside and ridgeline areas, and to preserve the predominant
views both from and of the hillside and ridgeline areas.

The retention of scenic hillsides and ridge lines in as near a natural state as is feasible consistent with
the  rights  granted  by  law  to  property  owners  to  develop  their  properties  is  important  to  the
community¶s aesthetic qualities and will preserve a desirable visual identity of the City.

Each hillside property has its own unique characteristics, including but not limited to topography,
tree-cover, and visual impact.  The guidelines in this general plan are intended to provide flexibility
in the treatment of development if individual properties as indicated by their uniqueness rather than
to provide a fixed set of strict standards applicable in the same manner to all properties.  In this way
each property can be developed to its full potential consistent with land use constraints and other
applicable land use regulations.

Finally, it is the intent of the General Plan to provide for development on hillsides within Sandy City
in such a manner as to protect the public health, safety, and welfare by minimizing the potential of
such  development  to  cause  or  contribute  to  landslides,  erosion  and  flooding  or  the  aesthetic
degradation of the City¶s natural environment and scenic backdrop.

PURPOSE 

The purpose for this element of the General Plan is to:

        1.      Preserve  significant  features  of  scenic  hillsides  and  major  ridge  line  areas  in
                essentially their natural state.

        2.      Keeping the semi-rural qualities of the City by preserving its open and uncluttered
                natural topographic features.

        3.      Minimize water runoff and soil erosion problems incurred in adjustment of the terrain
                to meet on-site development needs.

SLOPE

This General Plan should affect all hillside areas of the City of Sandy, as defined in the Sandy City
Development Code.

STRUCTURES

There are various types of structures property owners can locate upon their lot.  For this reason it is
important to define each type of structure.  The Sandy City Development Code defines structures,
dwelling structures, accessory structures, and fences as follows:

Structures             Anything constructed or erected, the use of which requires location on the
                       ground or attachment to something having location on the ground.  A building
                       is included in this definition.  Any structure two feet or above in grade shall
                       meet all underlying zoning requirements.

Dwelling Structures Any structure used for human occupation that has been constructed to comply
                    with all building codes of the City of Sandy, or the building codes established
                    by the National Manufactured Housing Construction and Safety Standards
                    Act.

Accessory Structure An incidental subordinate building customarily incidental to and located upon
                    the same lot occupied by the main use or building, including detached garages.

Fences                 The  term  fence  shall  include  any  tangible  barrier,  an  obstruction  of  any
                       material, a line of obstacles, lattice work, screen, wall, hedge, or continuous
                       growth  of  shrubs  or  trees  with  the  purpose  of,  or  having  the  effect  of
                       preventing passage or view across the fence line.

All fencing materials should be of dark vinyl coated chain link, and erected in such a manner as to
preserve the hillside integrity.  Extreme care should be taken to not remove existing vegetation, and

VEGETATION

The intent of the General Plan is to preserve existing hillsides in a natural state.  Planting vegetation
will stabilize slopes, help reduce erosion, and enhance the natural beauty and qualities of hillsides
within Sandy City.

While every new development in Sandy City should seek to minimize the impact of construction on
hillsides, some disruption is probably inevitable.  This holds true of property owners desiring to
landscape their own lots as well.

Correcting and damage done in these processes will require revegetation; and this should, to the
greatest extent possible, re-create the earlier character of the site, using indigenous plants and trees
that are native to the area.  New plants should blend in with the existing landscape; so that several
years hence, all traces of the disruption will have disappeared.

WATERING METHODS

When considering which is the best way to water a steep or sloped hillside, consideration should be
given to the erosion factor.  Depending upon the type of planting used for a given hillside, the best

method for watering is short frequent cycles.  This method prevents water from saturating the area
and running off the hillside.

Once plant material begins to take root, watering times can be decreased.  This will allow the plant
to develop a deeper root system decreasing the constant need to water.  It is a good rule of thumb to
water for a period not to exceed one year, before decreasing the watering time.

One of two watering systems should be used to water hillside areas.

Option 1.        Installing a temporary irrigation system to water a hillside for a period of one to three
                 years.

The most economical and effective way to temporarily water a hillside or flat area would be to install
an above ground watering system.  If the area is relatively small a low gallonage type nozzle would
be adequate.  If the area is larger, a rotor type head is best.  This method will provide irrigation needs
to plant materials early in existence.  Once the material is deeply rooted and established, irrigation
needs will decline.

Option 2.        Installing a permanent irrigation system.

If  a  permanent  irrigation  system  is  desired,  the  owner  should  start  from  the  beginning  and  plan
carefully  the  type  of  landscape  to  be  used,  and  design  the  irrigation  system  to  provide  the  best
coverage for the various areas and or plant materials.

When installing a permanent irrigation system care and consideration should be taken to ensure that
the proper materials are being used.  This will prevent future disturbances of hillside areas, if proper
materials are used from the beginning.

Watering methods for either of these options should remain the same.  Watering with short frequent
cycles will assist in controlling the erosion process of hillsides
