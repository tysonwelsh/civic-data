        1.2      Mayor’s Report

     Mayor Kristie Overson reported that there had been a ribbon cutting at Delirio Pizza recently.
Their pizza was so good that the City ordered several pizzas the day before in celebration of
National Pi Day.

Wall replacement on the east side of Redwood Road was expected to begin in the next few weeks.
The UTA bus stop at 5100 South was temporarily out of service during the project, leaving the
closest available stop at 4835 South.

The purpose of the Redwood Road project was to improve the appearance of Redwood Road and
particularly to increase traffic flow and safety.

Mayor Overson pointed out that the Council had an item on the agenda regarding funding for
pickleball courts. It was clear that work was already underway at the courts by both the Senior
Center and Vista Park. She was very excited about the opportunity to partner with Salt Lake
County and make these improvements.

The Mayor, Kris Heineman and the Youth Council recently participated in the Youth Council
Conference at Utah State University in Logan, UT. Two of the young people made a presentation
titled Your Mental Health Matters to over 200 other Youth Council members from across Utah.
The kids were very engaged and had an opportunity to strengthen their relationships with one
another as well as those from other cities.

Demolition had recently begun at the old Kmart building at 5400 South Bangerter Highway. It was
very exciting to see a building that sat vacant for eleven years finally begin to come down as we
moved closer to the much-anticipated Volta project.

Taylorville’s Building Official Steve Porten had recently received a great letter from a resident
who expressed how much she had appreciated the great customer services she experienced from
the Building Department and in particular, Kathy LeMay.

Chair Knudsen added his appreciation to the Building Department staff as well as those working
throughout the city for the benefit of the residents.

       3.2       Taylorsville-Bennion Improvement District – Mark Chalk

        Mark Chalk, General Manager of Taylorsville-Bennion Improvement District displayed
satellite photos comparing last years’ snow pack to this year’s. The difference was very evident.
Alta Ski Resort had 643” of snow as of one week ago. He also presented information regarding
how snow contributed to water levels. For example, the ratio of rain to snow could vary greatly
from as low as 1:4 to higher than 1:20. Still, Utah’s precipitation was still below average, and it
needed a very wet Spring for that to change.

Councilmember Cochran asked how much of the precipitation would stay local and how much
would be diverted to places like Lake Powell and Lake Mead. Mr. Chalk explained that it would
depend on which basin the precipitation fell into. For example, the Great Basin would feed local
reservoirs along the Wasatch Front and then eventually into Utah Lake and the Great Salt Lake.
Other basins flowed to the Colorado River and then to Lake Powell, etc. It would take a significant
amount of moisture over several years to make up for the fact that Lake Powell was too low by at
least 180’.

Councilmember Cochran also inquired how likely it was that there would be serious Spring
flooding when all the snow melted. Mr. Chalk responded that depended on how rapidly the outside
temperatures increased.

Chair Knudsen thanked Mr. Chalk for his report.

        City Attorney Tracy Cowdell mentioned that the current state law did not allow the city to
        restrict fireworks due to highly effective lobbying by fireworks industry representatives.
        There was a very narrow exception to that based on what the fire marshal recommended.
        Despite this, Salt Lake City had attempted to limit them and were sued by manufacturers
        and distributors with the industry. In short, municipalities were very limited by what the
        law allowed. He recommended that the city pay close attention to any recommendations
        made by the fire marshal in the coming months.
