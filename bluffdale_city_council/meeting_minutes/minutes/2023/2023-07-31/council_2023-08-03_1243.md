Administrative Services Director, Bruce Kartchner, reported that the Truth in Taxation Hearing is
a step in a budgetary process that is only required when there is a proposed tax increase and is
mandated by State law. It is done after the budget has been discussed for a number of months,the
numbers adjusted, and a Tentative Budget passed. The hope was that it would be passed as a final
budget tonight. All ofthe numbers have been discussed at great length including the proposed tax
increase. The hearing is specifically for a Certified Tax Rate for a New Fire and Police Protection
Fund that is not part ofthe General Fund.

Mr. Kartchner reported that last year the City Council approved a tax increase in the General Fund
because there were specific needs consisting of fire improvements in fire services as well as
additional costs that were incurred due to a specific, above average,increase in the Police contract
that was entered into the previous October. Last year, the tax increase was approved by the City
Council. When it was discussed at public hearings, many residents specifically stated that they did
not support the tax increase in the General Fund because they could not guarantee that it would be
used for Fire and Police Services.


Once money goes into the General Fund, it becomes fimgible and you cannot specify one dollar
from another. As staff reviewed what other cities are doing, one of the mechanisms created by
State law was that a specific fund could be created within the City that property taxes could be
collected for. The law specifies that any taxes collected in the fund can only be spent for the
specific services identified. In this case, they can only be used for Fire and Police services.


Mr. Kartchner reported that there is an ongoing staffing problem in the Fire Department where
firefighters are called back to their home agencies and have to work when they are scheduled to
work in Bluffdale. During COVID,firefighters were taken out ofservice after being exposed and
forced to quarantine. Issues also occur around holidays when there are staffing shortfalls. When
that happens, Bluffdale's part-time firefighters, who all have a different home agency, cannot be
relied on since they may have to work at their home agency.

Last year, the intent was to add additional full-time firefighters mid-year and then on July 1,2023,
they planned to add more. When the referendum removed the tax increase funding, the City had
to re-budget and the plans were delayed. The need has not gone away and staff is presenting the
proposed change in configuration. There is also a continuing need in Public Safety for both Police
and Fire where there is a greater demand for personnel than there are people available. It was
noted that fewer young people are getting into police services. As a result, police wages continue
to increase at a rate that is greater than the Consumer Price Index ("CPI") and other general City
increases. As a result, those wage increases are likely to continue into the future.

Mr. Kartchner reported that the proposed budget includes additional funds to hire six full-time
combat firefighters to fill two of the six positions the City has. Additionally, all firefighters
received a 5% wage increase that was part ofthe proposed budget. The Police Contract with the
Saratoga Police Department also includes above average market driven wage increases and they
have specifically targeted certain promotion-based increases.

Mr. Kartchner next addressed the question many have regarding why taxes are increasing. He
explained that the State ofUtah backs into the rate and many feel that when the value oftheir home
increases,the City receives more taxes as a result. Because ofthe way the State calculates property
taxes, that does not happen. The State of Utah specifically has a law that specifies that local
governments will not get a windfall of revenue as a result of the revaluation or equalization of
property taxes. He explained that when property values rise, property tax rates must fall. For the
City as a whole, property value increases do not change the amount received. The only increase
the City receives each year is on growth or estimated growth, which is calculated every year.

Mr. Kartchner reported that the rate does not change relative to inflation but relative to values. For
example, when looking at the 2022 tax dollars paid for one specific property, $534.40 was paid.
Even though the value of the property increased slightly compared to the previous year, for 2023
with no budget change, the amount decreases by $86.66. That means that commercial properties
increased more than residential properties in Bluffdale this year. The reverse happened last year.
The calculation and discussion for Truth in Taxation addresses the impact of the proposed tax

increase. If the increase is approved, the 2023 change will be $119, If compared to 2022, the

Council Member Kallas clarified that in general, property taxes do not increase unless they are
raised,the property owner is noticed,and a Truth in Taxation Hearing is held. The rate may change
automatically but the changing rate is intended to keep the dollar amount level. If an entity, such
as a City Council, needs to hire more firefighters the City has to approve it and hold a similar type
of hearing, which increases the percentage. Mr. Kartchner stated that that is in aggregate.
Individual properties, depending on how they increase or decrease relative to other properties in
the taxing umbrella, can increase or decrease without a change. In aggregate, it remains flat year
after year dollar wise.


Mr. Kartchner reported that the increase is just for the Fire and Police Protection Fund. Prior to
the proposed increase, the current tax rate in the Fire and Police Protection Fund was zero. The
proposed tax increase increases it to .000260, which is the New Certified Tax Rate. Notices were
sent out by the County Assessor and included the General Fund and Fire and Police Protection
Fund Rates in a single rate. When invoices are received by the Treasurer, the two amounts will be
split out to show the amount of the tax increase specific to the Fire and Police Protection Fund.

Mr. Kartchner reported that prior to any changes, the General Fund was at .000976, which is a
reduction firom last year. The rate last year was .001161. It decreased slightly from last year
because property values in aggregate increased so the rate went down. There was no proposed
change, so there will not be a Truth in Taxation Hearing related to that fund. The total, however,
changes in aggregate from .000976 to .001236. The County also specifies the average home price
for the City of Bluffdale. It is not a number the City calculates and provided by the County. The
average number for this year is a $694,000. For the average homeowner, the impact of the
proposed tax increase will be $8.27 per month or just over $99.00 for the entire year.


In summary,as demanded by residents, the tax increase is only used for Fire and Police Protection
Services by creating a Special Fund for the Fire and Police Protection Services. The Council also
has staff redirect all property taxes that the City collects into the Fire and Police Protection Fund.
