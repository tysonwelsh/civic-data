30   2) North Lehi Station Area Plan Presentation and Discussion
31
32   Christie Richman presented the current North Lehi Station Area Plan.
33
34   The current plan covers both developed and undeveloped land. The undeveloped land has several
35   different owners, and some of the existing plans for it include business offices and a freeway
36   access point. It is in close proximity to the State Flight Park Recreation Area, Shadow Ridge
37   Park, as well as existing trail infrastructure. One goal is to create a new gateway into Lehi City.
38
39   Feedback from analyzing the area and from community engagement events have resulted in the
40   following project vision: to establish and maintain a unique hub of neighborly activity, easily
41   accessed by people of all ages. Commissioner Eyre had concerns over focusing on small-scale
42   neighborhood amenities when the ultimate goal is to encourage more people to use the area.
43   Christie Richman explained that the development will be more of a transition area between a
44   regional hub and a transit supportive environment.
45
46   The preferred layout currently places mixed-use areas close to the station, with small lot single
47   family homes or townhomes further away. Several plans for mixed-use blocks were presented,
48   showcasing varying development intensity. The station will be a central hub connecting the

49   Village Green, the Village Center, and existing residential areas. The building plan places an
50   emphasis on sustainability and accessibility. Smaller blocks and narrower roads will assist in
51   this. Firefighters will conduct their business from intersections, rather than from the wider roads
52   present in other parts of the city. Commissioner Everett asked if narrow streets would prevent
53   snow removal. Richman was uncertain, as she doesn’t know the size of the city’s equipment, but

55   private, and thus not the city’s responsibility to plow.
56
57   Commissioner Everett asked about potential integration into Thanksgiving Point and the silicon
58   slopes. Richman explained that the goal of this area is not to create competition, but to create an
59   interim space for people working in those places. Commissioner Everett clarified that he is
60   worried about creating an island, and asked if planning was going to review the general plan to
61   put more connections in. Richman and the Planning Department explained that the rail itself is
62   going to connect to The Point. It will also connect to the Draper frontrunner station.
63
64   There will be a public hearing for the plan at the next Planning Commission meeting (March 13),
65   and it will then proceed to the City Council.

69   3) Hospital/2100 North Station Area Plan Presentation and Discussion
70
71   The commission reconvened at approximately 6:30.
72
73   Jordan Swayne presented the area plan, with assistance from Ray Whitchurch and Jonathan
74   Young.
75
76   HB462, which passed last year, requires cities to create plans for a half mile radius around any
77   light rail stations, and a quarter mile from any BRT (bus rapid transit) stations. The four goals of
78   the legislation are to (1) create affordable housing around transit stations, (2) encourage
79   environmentally sustainable infrastructure, (3) provide access to opportunities, especially places
80   to live, work, and play, and (4) find ways to enhance a multimodal operation of streets.
81
82   The combined area plan encapsulates two stations, South Triumph Station, located at the
83   hospital, and the 2100 station. South Triumph Station is about seven years out, with the
84   development of the second station being further out. 2100 will be connected to Provo, potentially
85   by a BRT line. UTA is considering a shuttle that would connect South Triumph Station to the
86   Lehi Frontrunner station. The gap between the two stations is still being discussed by
87   UDOT/UTA.
88
89   Commissioner Eyre asked how the removal of parking space for the area plan would be
90   addressed, as the Young Living Campus is a central part of the plan, and currently requires a lot
91   of parking. Swayne explained that much of the surface parking would be consolidated into
92   parking structures. He is also hopeful that societal shifts towards remote workability will be
93   utilized by companies in order to regulate the number of cars in an area at any given time. He
94   explained that the development will be market-driven, as no part of the plan is dependent on the
95   other, aside from the train stations.
96

 97   Commissioner Everett shared concerns about the tensions between walkability and maintain
 98   traffic flow. Luke Seegmiller said that 1200 West will need to remain an arterial road. Jordan
 99   Swayne doesn’t think it will affect the plan too much. Aerial pedestrian bridges have been
100   suggested as a solution, in addition to the two planned places for pedestrians to cross.
101   Commissioner Everett is still worried about isolating the northern half of the development from
102   the southern half of the development, as they will be separated by five lanes of traffic.
103
104   The majority of the planned streets will be residential style with 25 mph speed limits. Other
105   streets are designed to give a ‘village’ style look with more amenities and green space. There
106   will be dedicated bus lanes to South Triumph Station. Additionally, the development features a
107   disentangled multi-use path that is separated from streets, but still connects to different areas in
108   the community in order to provide a safer non-car transportation experience.
109
110   The plan is divided into seven different planning districts. The two closest to the stations will be
111   oriented around the stations to emphasize and encourage public transit. Other districts include a
112   health and wellness shopping district, an innovation district for startups and rental spaces for
113   businesses, a commercial corridor district that will carry groceries and other essential goods, and
114   two transit supportive districts.
115
116   Commissioner Everett is concerned about the east side of Lehi which will remain unwalkable.
117   Jordan Swayne clarified that the area plan is not designed to take larger connections into
118   consideration. The commission acknowledged this but thinks that the larger connections are
119   important to consider anyway. Commissioner Jackson explained that it is much harder to
120   rearrange infrastructure once it’s been put it. Ray Whithouse suggests planning and maintaining
121   corridors so that infrastructure won’t have to be upset to build more connecting rails.
122   Commissioner Jackson also shares his opinion that the only way to improve public transit usage
123   economically is to make public transit just as easy as driving.
124
125   There isn’t much room for residential housing north of 1200 W, so the majority of the
126   development will be commercial and retail. There will be some mixed-use and low density
127   housing south of 1200 W. Open spaces will be integrated into the designs of the districts, with
128   the areas near the hospital being more plaza-like, and those in the commercial districts having
129   outdoor seating for eating.
130
131   Commissioner Eyre asked if the South Triumph Station would allow a track crossing. Mike West
132   explained that it’s vital to have a track crossing there to enable access. Luke Seegmiller asked
133   about the timeline of the development. Jordan Swayne said there is no current deadline, but that
134   the estimate is seven years. Various factors like the construction of The Point and the Olympics
135   may accelerate construction.
136
137   This item will come before planning commission at the second meeting in March. There will be
138   more information about implementation at that time.
