BOARD OF COMMISSIONERS OF UTAH COUNTY, UTAH
Public Meeting

March 12, 2025

2/Page

He is amazing at what he does. He always has a smile on his face, a quick retort
for everything, and a great rapport with almost all the inmates. Just the other day
there was an issue in Canyons 1 with an inmate not wanting to leave after being
released. Deputy Z. Wall was asked to go and talk with said inmate. On his way to
the inmate's cell, he stopped and chatted with more than a handful of inmates,
just because he walked past, and they stopped him. After arriving at the inmate's
cell, it was just a matter of a few minutes before the inmate was walking down the
hall and released. Z. Wall did what a few other deputies could not do because of
how he is viewed by the inmates.

Deputy Wall is also part of DRT, which requires dedication. That requires copious
amounts of training, getting dressed in a hot vinyl suit to enter a hazardous cell to
remove an inmate, and always being ready for the unknown backup call. Deputy
Wall is not afraid to do what it takes to get the job done.

Deputy Wall is part of the FTO crew. He takes this very seriously. He wants to
make sure that every new deputy is well-trained and up to date on policy and
procedure. He spends a lot of time in Canyons 1, our lockdown unit, helping the
newer deputies to feel confident in working that unit (it can be a little
intimidating). Deputy Wall's experience at the prison has come in handy on the
FTO crew. He is very knowledgeable , easy to talk to, bounce ideas off of, and
helps when asked.

Not once has he ever told me no when I have asked for his help. Deputy Wall is a
big presence in the jail, and he should be recognized for it.

For this and a slew of other reasons, I want to nominate Deputy Zach Wall for
Utah County Employee of the Month.

Redistricting Parameters

Each new school district will have 7 seats

Districts should be substantially equal in population

Districts should be as contiguous and compact as practicable

Efforts should be made to follow city and precinct lines whenever possible

Where possible, the boundaries should maintain communities with shared
interests, such as economic, cultural, or geographic similarities
