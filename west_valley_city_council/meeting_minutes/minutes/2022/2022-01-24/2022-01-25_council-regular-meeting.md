ORDINANCE 22-02: AMEND SECTION 1-2-107(3) OF THE WEST VALLEY
CITY MUNICIPAL CODE TO UPDATE ADMISSIONS FEES FOR THE WEST
VALLEY CITY FAMILY FITNESS CENTER
Wayne Pyle, City Manager, discussed proposed Ordinance 22-02 that would amend Section
1-2-107(3) of the West Valley City Municipal Code to Update Admissions Fees for the West
Valley City Family Fitness Center.
   MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING JANUARY 25, 2022
                          -4-

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       The WVC Family Fitness Center has seen a significant increase in expenses since
       its last fee increase 10 years ago. The proposed membership and drop-in admission
       fee increases outlined below will help cover upsurges in maintenance costs,
       personnel and benefits, property insurance and overall cost of services. Staff
       compared membership fees with other similar facilities and found that the proposed
       increases remain comparable to most nearby recreation facilities. With these
       changes, it is recommended that the Centennial Outdoor Pool be included in the
       memberships.

       It is proposed that all membership rates be increased by 15% apart from senior
       membership rates which will be raised 5%. This reflects a monthly membership
       fee increase of:
             Family (up to six members): $4.58 per month
             Adult Couple: $4.17 per month
             Adult: $2.58 per month
             Senior Couple: $0.83 per month
             Senior: $0.67 per month
             Student: $2.08 per month

       Drop-in admission fees have a proposed increase by a range of $0.25 – 1.00
       depending on the age category.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Lang there were no further questions from members of the City
Council, and she called for a motion.

       Councilmember Harmon moved to continue Ordinance 22-02 to study potential

of an Interlocal Cooperation Agreement, a Park Easement Agreement, and a Perpetual

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       West Valley City owns a water meter north of the Utah Cultural Celebration Center
       that is used very little. Salt Lake County will be building and maintaining Pioneer
       Crossing Park and have asked for this agreement allowing them to utilize the
       existing water meter and water line. By utilizing existing utilities there will not

RESOLUTION 22-14: ADOPT THE STATE LEGISLATIVE AGENDA OF WEST
VALLEY CITY FOR THE 2022 UTAH STATE LEGISLATIVE SESSION
Wayne Pyle, City Manager, presented proposed Resolution 22-14 that would adopt the State
Legislative Agenda of West Valley City for the 2022 Utah State Legislative Session.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       As the second largest city in the state it is imperative that the City has a significant
       presence at the state legislature. The City enjoys a particularly strong presence and
       maintains that presence each year through the process of setting guidelines for
       representation and executing on those guidelines.

       Due to the city’s unique position, it has been essential to maintain this presence and
       to proactively work on legislative issues throughout the year. Each January, the
       Council sets forth the guidelines for this work. These guidelines are found in the
       attached resolution. The guidelines allow staff and contractors to adequately
       represent the City on any topic in a constantly changing environment and provide
       protection to the City from any misunderstanding of the City’s position.

       Due to the extremely dynamic nature of the legislative session, staff must be clear
       about the direction of the City Council. This resolution assists in that effort.

       Of the various issues that will be discussed during this legislative session, of
       particular interest are policing, land use, emergency operations, taxation, funding,
       local control, economic development matters, and transportation funding.

Councilmember Harmon asked where he can find the list of items the City is pursuing.
Wayne replied that there is no specific list and noted that there is a general guideline listed
in the issue paper. He noted that the Council will be receiving an updated bill tracker each
week that details the City’s interest in various bills. Councilmember Harmon stated that he
would like to have a specific list as he discusses this issue with citizens.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Lang there were no further questions from members of the City
Council, and she called for a motion.

       Councilmember Fitisemanu moved to approve Resolution 22-14.

       Councilmember Nordfelt seconded the motion.

    RIGHT OF ENTRY AND OCCUPANCY AGREEMENT WITH AND FROM
    THE SALT LAKE VALLEY MENTAL HEALTH BOARD,
    INCORPORATED FOR PROPERTY LOCATED AT 4980 WEST 4700
    SOUTH
    Wayne Pyle, City Manager, discussed proposed Resolution 22-18 that would
    authorize the City to Enter into a Right of Entry and Occupancy Agreement With
    and From the Salt Lake Valley Mental Health Board, Incorporated for Property
    Located at 4980 West 4700 South.

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

             The Salt Lake Valley Mental Health Board, Incorporated parcel located at
             4980 West 4700 South is one of several parcels affected by the 4700 South
             Reconstruction Project (4000 West to 5600 West). This project is being
             constructed in cooperation with Kearns Metro Township. This project will
             reconstruct the pavement, curb, gutter, sidewalk and decorative street
             lighting on both sides of 4700 South and 4715 South to improve safety and
             ride quality of 4700 South and 4715 South. Proposed compensation for the
             purchase of the Warranty Deed, Perpetual Easement, Temporary
             Construction Easement, Public Utility Easement and improvements is
             $40,900.00 based upon the appraisal report prepared by Van Drimmelen
             Associates, Inc. The Right-of-Entry and Occupancy Agreement will allow
             construction to move forward while negotiations with the property owner
             continue. Funds in the amount of $40,900.00 will be deposited in escrow
             pending the final resolution of the acquisition. If further negotiations are
             unsuccessful eminent domain action will be required.

             The project is being funded under the Surface Transportation Program
             (STP) with federal funds. Kearns Metro Township executed the Federal
             Aid Agreement with UDOT. West Valley City has signed an Interlocal
             Agreement with Kearns Metro Township. The city is responsible for 6.77%
             of all project costs, including right of way, within West Valley City
             jurisdictional limits. The total value for the Warranty Deed, Perpetual
             Easement, Temporary Construction Easement and the Public Utility
             Easement is $40,900.00, making the West Valley City share of the
             acquisition $2,768.93.
