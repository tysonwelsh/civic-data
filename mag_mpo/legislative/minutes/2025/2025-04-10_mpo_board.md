Wasatch Choice Vision Update (00:06:07) Dan Wayne presented an update on the Wasatch Choice Vision, focusing on the Utah County portion of the project. He explained that they are working on updating the future land use element needed for the travel demand model and regional transportation plan due in 2027. Dan Wayne highlighted that they have completed the goals and strategies, acquired local general plan land use data, and met individually with each local government in Utah County. The team has now prepared a map-based comment tool that allows local governments to review the compiled data, add comments, make corrections, and edit features. The tool is divided into three maps: centers (neighborhood, city, urban, and metropolitan), other land uses (employment, industrial, retail, etc.), and parks and open space. Dan Wayne emphasized that this is a first-cut version, and the maps will go through substantial modifications before being performance tested in the travel demand model. The next steps include technical analysis, presenting results to local governments, and potentially modifying the maps before bringing them back to the MPO Board for final approval and use in the regional transportation plan. 2025 General Session - Legislative Summary (00:13:24) Cameron Deihl and Mayor Troy Walker provided a comprehensive review of the recent legislative session. Mayor Walker highlighted the unique nature of Utah's legislature, which is predominantly Republican and passed 589 bills. A significant focus was on the Beehive Development Agency bill, which would have given a new agency extensive land use authority, potentially allowing for projects like nuclear power plants in local communities. Mayor Walker emphasized the League of Cities and Towns' critical role in negotiating and moderating legislative proposals. He praised the league's staff, particularly Cameron Deihl, for their expertise, organization, and ability to navigate complex legislative processes. The mayor noted that while they didn't stop all problematic bills, they were successful in killing some ADU (Accessory Dwelling Unit) bills, preventing others from advancing, and improving some legislation. He stressed the importance of being at the negotiating table and not simply being a "League of No," but instead offering smart, compromise-driven solutions. Cameron Deihl added context about the legislative process, noting that 959 bills were introduced, 582 were passed, and this was the highest number of introduced bills ever. He emphasized the league's approach of working collaboratively with legislators and seeking to be partners rather than opponents. Page 2 of 5 TIP Modification Eagle Mountain/Saratoga Springs Park and Ride - Additional Funding (00:33:34) Bob Allen presented a TIP (Transportation Improvement Program) modification request for UTA (Utah Transit Authority). He explained that FTA (Federal Transit Administration) recently changed its policy regarding fund obligation, requiring funds to be used within five years. MAG had previously awarded UTA federal funds for bus stop improvement projects and a historic Rail Trail feasibility study. The funds need to be obligated quickly. The request involves transferring previously awarded funds to two park and ride lot projects in Saratoga Springs and Eagle Mountain. For the Eagle Mountain location, UTA wants to modify the project scope to add a hawk signal crossing Pony Express Parkway to improve pedestrian access and safety, which would add $260,000 to the project. The Saratoga Springs location requires redesign due to wetlands, and they don't yet have a new project estimate. Bob Allen noted there was initially some controversy at the TAC (Technical Advisory Committee) meeting, with a motion by Lehi that failed and a motion from UTA that passed was later rescinded. He emphasized that UTA has since coordinated with local mayors and believes everyone is now in alignment with the proposed changes. Mayor Kurt Ostler moved to approve adding funds from PINs 19098, 17052, and 18986, totaling $1,339,274, to the Eagle Mountain and Saratoga Springs Park and Ride Lots Project and modifying the scope to add a HAWK signal. Mayor Jim Miller seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. Corridor Preservation - Springville 2600 West (00:42:28) Calvin Clark presented two corridor preservation requests. The first was for Springville's 2600 West roadway, involving five property owners with vacant land. The project aims to create a future five-lane major roadway, with Lakeside Land Partners donating right-of-way for a "ribbon road" to initially connect existing road segments. The total corridor preservation request for this project is $1,660,800, with the Utah County corridor preservation fund having approximately $10.5 million unobligated. The second request was for Saratoga Springs' Mountain View Corridor, with an appraised land value of $960,000 and estimated closing costs of $10,000, totaling $970,000. Calvin noted a unique situation with this request, as they could not obtain a standard voluntary seller certification due to the property owner's non-profit status. After consulting legal counsel, they determined they could make a one-time exception to the typical procedure, emphasizing this would not change existing policy. The unobligated fund balance after this request would be around $8.8 million. Mayor Carolyn Lundberg moved to approve Springville City’s Corridor Preservation request for $1,660,800 for the presented properties for the 2600 West Roadway. Mayor Denise Andersen seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. Road Functional Classification Data Update (00:53:35) Matthew Silski presented the road functional classification data update, a comprehensive effort done every 10 years in conjunction with census data and required by the Federal Highway Administration (FHWA). He explained that this classification system determines federal funding eligibility for road projects, specifically for Transportation Improvement Program (TIP) funds. MAG created a map-based comment tool for proposing updates, with the last comprehensive update occurring in October 2013. The tool allows cities to review and provide feedback. Matthew Silski noted they have already received over 100 comments from nearly 10 cities. The map includes guidance from federal highways on how roads should be classified, ensuring consistency across the county and state. Matthew Silski emphasized this is a once-in-a-decade opportunity, but cities can still request additions through the TAC if a regional road project is proposed. The timeline includes a first draft due to UDOT by the end of April, summer collaboration with UDOT and city staff, and final board approval in October/November, with the goal of submitting the final system to Federal Highways by December 29th. Jurisdictional Cash Assessment Increase for FY2026 (00:59:25) LaNiece Davenport presented the jurisdictional cash assessment increase for fiscal year 2026. As the MPO is responsible for administering transportation planning work, they receive about $1.5 million annually from the federal government with a required 6.77% local match. She requested an additional $50,000 from local governments in Utah County, increasing the assessment from $50,000 to $100,000. The proposed increase would help meet the match requirement, which is approximately $111,000 annually. A chart in the packet showed how the increase would impact each jurisdiction, with cities like Orem and Provo seeing the largest increases. LaNiece explained that in previous years, they had been using project money to fill the funding gap. Michelle Carroll added context, noting that the executive council had been working on budget adjustments and that it had been several decades since they last reviewed local jurisdictional cash assessments. The goal is to ensure they are meeting required matches with local dollars. Mayor Matt Packard recommends that the MAG MPO Board approve an increase in the Jurisdictional Cash Assessment for FY2026 from $50,000 to $100,000 to help cover the 6.77% match for the federal planning monies we receive.
