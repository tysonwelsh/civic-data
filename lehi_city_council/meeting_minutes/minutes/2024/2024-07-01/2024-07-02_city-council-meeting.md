2. Discussion Items
   2.a. Fraud Risk Questionnaire Report- Finance Department
   Dean Lundell presented on the city’s fraud risk assessment that is required by the Utah State
   Auditor. He noted the fraud risk hotline that was created. Overall, the city received a very low
   risk assessment.

   Item 2.c was heard next.
   2.c) Discussion of PacifiCorp transmission rates- Lehi Power Department
   Joel Eves, Lehi Power Director, explained that PacificCorp, the transmission provider, is facing
   over $8 billion in lawsuits related to wildfires in Oregon in 2020. PacificCorp was found to be
   grossly negligent for failing to turn off transmission lines despite being asked to do so due to
   fire risk. As a result, PacificCorp has implemented a 45% rate increase on transmission costs
   starting June 2024, which equates to around a 2.8% increase in Lehi City's overall power costs.
   UAMPS, the power consortium Lehi belongs to, is legally challenging this rate increase and
   seeking changes to the accounting rules to prevent such costs from being passed on to customers
   in the future. The city is monitoring the situation closely but does not anticipate the need to raise
   rates for customers at this time.

   2.b) Discussion of the Moderate Income Housing Element- Planning Department
   There was extensive discussion around the city's goals for Moderate Income Housing.
   Councilors expressed concerns that recent mixed-use projects have lacked the desired

   Overall, the conversation highlighted the need to balance regulations, infrastructure, community
   needs, and market forces to foster sustainable, affordable housing options and neighborhood
   revitalization.
