The following are the minutes of the Planning Commission Meeting of the Herriman Planning Commission.
The meeting was held on Thursday, January 7, 2021 at 6:00 p.m. in the Herriman City Council Chambers,
5355 West Herriman Main Street, Herriman, Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting, as required by law, was
posted in the City Hall, on the City’s website, and delivered to members of the Council, media, and interested
citizens.

Presiding: Chair Andy Powell

Commissioners Present In Person: Brody Rypien and Heather Garcia

Commissioners Present Electronically: Andrea Bradford, Adam Jacobson, Jackson Ferguson, Lorin
Palmer, and Joy Kaseke

Commissioners Excused: Chris Berbert

Staff Present In Person: City Planner Michael Maloy, Interim City Manager Wendy Thomas, HPD Deputy
Chief of Police Cody Stromberg, Deputy City Recorder Wendy Thorpe, Communications Specialist Mitch
Davis, and Community Development Director Blake Thomas

Staff Present Electronically: Planner II Sheldon Howa, Engineer II Josh Petersen, City Engineer Jonathan
Bowers, Assistant City Manager Tami Moody, and City Attorney Chase Andrizzi



Chair Andy Powell called the meeting to order at 6:03 p.m.


Assistant City Manager Tami Moody presented the 2021 Legislative Priorities. They focus on local control,
public safety, housing, building, economic development, infrastructure, parks, trails, and recreation. The
primary goals for housing were moderate-income housing, and Accessory Dwelling Units (ADU) support. The
City supports ADUs in the proper zones. HB0098 would impact multiple building aspects including permitting,
inspections, design standards and was not supported by the City, as it was currently written. It would allow
developers to opt out of using the City for inspections and plan reviews and remove design guidelines and
design requirements. The City was supportive of gravel pit regulations. Code enforcement legislation had been
discussed but bills have not been introduced yet. Economic development addressed job creation, infrastructure,
and sales tax revenue. Herriman City hoped to increase sales tax revenue to make up for a declining number
of building permits. The economic development map displayed zoning for areas not yet developed. Proposed
updates included Main Street widening from 6200 West to 7300 West, 6000 West widening and improvements
from 12600 South to 13100 South, 6400 West new construction from 12600 South to 11800 South, widening
of 11800 South from 6000 West to 7000 West, and 7300 West new construction from 13100 South to the
north City boundary. Funding verification from last year’s session for the construction of 6600 West across
Rose Creek needed confirmation as that funding was placed on hold, due to the COVID-19 pandemic. New
transit lines would also be needed to reduce congestion from major arterial roads. On demand by via has been
an appreciated program. The southwest section of the valley has the most growth in the state, due to population
growth. Funding assistance has been requested for water conservation and aging infrastructure replacement
studies. The City supported HB13 requiring water testing in childcare centers if cities would not be responsible
for conducting the testing.

Federal Legislation items being followed include Army Compatible Use Buffer (ACUB) and Readiness and
Environmental Protection Integration (REPI), transit & transportation funding, grants for education, trails
funding, secondary water, and efforts to resolve contaminated soils issues. Additional items of focus included
annexations, affordable housing, and zoning reform. Commissioners requested regular Legislative updates
from Assistant City Manager Tami Moody. City Planner Maloy stated there had been a great deal of local
interest in many current legislative items. Commissioner Lorin Palmer asked how the Commissioners could
express their concerns to the Legislature. City Planner Maloy recommended coordinating with City staff. City
Attorney Chase Andrizzi said they should be mindful of speaking on behalf of their own personal interests and
not as a City representative. He recommended for Commissioners to communicate their comments to staff.
Assistant City Manager Tami Moody stated the Legislators may accept comments electronically due to
COVID-19. She encouraged listening to the sessions online and making her aware of issues. Legislative open
houses will begin in a couple weeks, and she offered to provide the schedule to the Commissioners.
Contaminated soils cleanup was a large expense, and the City was looking at funding options.


City Planner Michael Maloy informed the Commission that City Council remanded the Valhalla development
back to the Commission, with the change from half acres to A-1 zoning. The subdivision, which was not on
for approval tonight, was likely to be divided into separate cul-de-sacs to reduce the grade. City Planner Maloy
revisited the situation at the previous meeting when the applicant expressed frustration with not being able to
re-address the Commission, however the record was reviewed, and he had addressed the Commission before
the Public Hearing. Technically it was not a procedural due process error. Commissioner Brody Rypien
requested for commercial building design standards to be reviewed in a future work session.
The work meeting adjourned by consensus at 6:40 p.m.


Chair Andy Powell called the meeting to order at 7:01 p.m.


Commissioner Brody Rypien led the audience in the Pledge of Allegiance.


Full Quorum Present.


No conflicts were offered.


Commissioner Adam Jacobson moved to approve item 2.4 approval of the Minutes for November 19, 2020, Planning Commission
Meeting. Commissioner Brody Rypien seconded and all voted aye.

Chair Andy Powell reviewed the Public Hearing guidelines.




City Planner Michael Maloy explained one of the conditions of approval for the planned development required
ten-foot-wide trail access to Arches park and the open space to the south. However, connections were not
designed and constructed in compliance with the required condition. During park development it was
determined the trail was not as wide as the Commission requested. Further, the City stated the material should
be concrete, but the Commission requested asphalt. The overall trail connectivity objective had been met, but
the size and material did not match the Commission conditions. Smaller setbacks and open space along the trail
were deemed acceptable, given the narrow area and anticipated usage. The HOA was responsible for
maintenance. Access easements were not required initially but may be added to ensure maintenance and access.

Applicant Brandon Bennett was present online and offered to answer questions.

Community Development Director Blake Thomas clarified the bridge and other sidewalks in the area were five
feet wide.
Amended Requirement:
  10. Provide and maintain a concrete trail connection to Arches Park to the north and Midas Creek trail to
       the south, and record a survey describing a public easement in favor of (and in a form acceptable to)
       Herriman City to ensure perpetual public access to the trails.

Commissioner Brody Rypien moved to approve item 3.1 file number C2020-096 Conditional Use Amendment for Anthem Sky
Townhomes regarding the location and design of required trails in a Planned Unit Development with an additional #10 to record
a survey that shows public easement to ensure perpetual public access to the trails. Commissioner Heather Garcia seconded the
motion.

The vote was recorded as follows:
Commissioner Andrea Bradford             Aye
Commissioner Lorin Palmer                Aye
Commissioner Jackson Ferguson            Aye
Commissioner Adam Jacobson               Aye
Commissioner Heather Garcia              Aye
Commissioner Brody Rypien                Aye
The motion passed unanimously.




City Planner Maloy stated the applicant previously requested a rezone from FR-2.5 Forestry Recreation (2.5
acre minimum) to A-.50 Agricultural (half-acre minimum). The City Council remanded the request back to the
Commission requesting one acre lots instead. The Commission had the option to modify the request at a lower
density zone or add zoning conditions within their recommendation to the City Council.

Applicant James Horsley spoke in person and stated they were happy with the one-acre sized lots. He offered
to answer questions.

Chair Powell opened the public hearing.

Deputy Recorder Thorpe read the following comment from Rebekah Brinkerhoff:
There is a hillside overlay zone in this property but also greater than 30% slope that lies outside of that zone. In
the previous meeting with the planning commission a slope heat map was presented. I did not see this heat map
in the packet for tonight. I believe any decision made today, careful examination of this heat map and the
intended lot proposals is crucial. Lot #10 will wholly be built on a 20-30% slope and butt up to the 30% and
greater slope. Excavating and cutting deeply into the mountain, putting in a 40-foot retaining wall does not
reflect the natural setting and respect unique site constraints. In previous meetings Mr. Horsley stated he is just
helping Val and his kids get these lots to build on. Lot #11 is reserved for Val’s son in law but lot # 10 is for
Mr. Horsley himself and lot #9 is being sold through the builder Gordon Milar. If the purpose is to give Val a
place for his family to build a home, they already are able to add the other home under current zoning along
with allowing the future home to be built under the regulations an FR zone would have. In giving up the precious
zoning we are not preserving and protecting this hillside. There is no benefit to the city, there is no park, there
is no trail access. If this rezone is allowed it could also be used as a precedent for other similar rezone requests,
continuing building into our hillside. Ultimately this rezone request does not conform with and goes against our
general plan for our city, ordinances, and our city code. I respectfully ask for you to not support this rezone
request.

Deputy Recorder Thorpe read the following comment from Shilo Smith:
These 3 lots are holding the whole subdivision back. Can we (as in the owners of the other lots) get our homes
going? I'm wasting money on rent each day that passes by. Please and thank you!

Kody Crowley spoke representing a group. He expressed appreciation to the Commission for hearing their
concerns last time. They support the development if drainage and safety concerns were addressed. They also felt
it should be part of the bigger picture and done appropriately. He stated the neighborhood felt heard.

Kelly Cabot stated she was retired law enforcement from Florida and chose Herriman because of green space.
She was concerned the proposal would eliminate mandated green areas and negatively impact wildlife. Thirty
percent slope was too high. The steep roadways would create unsafe driving conditions and cause tragic human
injury.

Chair Powell closed the public hearing.

Commissioners requested the heat map. City Planner Maloy stated the heat map was from the subdivision
presentation. The Commissioners clarified the building limits on slopes steeper than thirty percent and identified
the areas where improvements would not take place.

Community Development Director Blake Thomas reviewed the overlay zone and the proposed cul-de-sac
location. He said this area included more than 250 feet which was more than enough distance to safely grade the
land. Chair Powell reminded the Commission the subdivision approval was not part of tonight’s zoning request.

Commissioner Garcia was opposed to changing it to one acre. She agreed with residents to keep the hillsides
clear and not put houses against the mountain. She said the development did not contribute to the community.
Commissioner Rypien agreed with her concerns. He requested more training from the planning department to
identify which areas should remain open space. Commissioners discussed the lot sizes in the area, and some
agreed the one acre lots were a good transition between the quarter acre lots and the hillside.
Commissioner Adam Jacobson moved to recommend approval to City Council of item 4.1 file number Z2020-060 Rezone
Property from FD-2.5 (Forestry Recreation) to A-1 (Agricultural – 1 acre minimum) for the proposed Valhalla Subdivision
(Public Hearing). Commissioner Jackson Ferguson seconded the motion.

The vote was recorded as follows:
Commissioner Andrea Bradford               Aye
Commissioner Lorin Palmer                  Aye
Commissioner Jackson Ferguson              Aye
Commissioner Adam Jacobson                 Aye
Commissioner Heather Garcia                No
Commissioner Brody Rypien                  No
The motion passed with a vote of 4 to 2.
