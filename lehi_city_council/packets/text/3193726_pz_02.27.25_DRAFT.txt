343   3.6) Public hearing and recommendation of Lehi City’s request for review of a
344   Development Code amendment to Chapter 12-B, removing the requirement that property
345   owners cannot purchase property from the City.
346
347   Jacob Curtis presented the item. The purpose of the amendment is to keep options open to the
348   city for property that has been encroached upon. Currently the city is not allowed to sell property
349   which limits its options when working with citizens who have encroached on city property.
350   Curtis mentioned that the City Council is in favor of the change.
351
352   Commissioner Jackson said that as the council is already in favor, he feels as though this is more
353   of an academic exercise than anything else. He thinks that it is shortsighted for the city to prevent
354   itself from selling property and that making this change would create a freer market.
355
356   Commissioner Jackson opened and closed public comment.
357
358   Commissioner Everett expressed that he liked the idea of the city having the ability to sell land
359   but worries that it’s being put into place as a result of specific instances of encroachment in the
360   city rather than as a proactive measure.
361
362   Commissioner Eyre asked about the intent behind the stricken clause. Kim Struthers that it was
363   added about a year ago in response to development in Traverse Mountain. In his opinion, the city
364   council want to have the option as it could be beneficial for the city.
365
366   Commissioners Eyre and Jackson discussed wording and suggested rewriting the clause to
367   explicitly add the option for the city. Craig Chambers explained that it’s not necessary to write
368   specifically that the city can sell property.
369
370   Commissioner Everett was troubled and felt that extreme actions taken by citizens wouldn’t be
371   treated seriously by the city if the option was made available. Craig Chambers assured him that
372   citizens that encroach are not getting away with anything. He knows this because of his work on
373   the cases.
374
375   Commissioner Everett suggested bringing cases like these to auction.
376
377   Jacob Curtis emphasized to the commission that this is mainly an amendment in the grading
378   chapter of the city code.
379
380                   Motion: Commissioner Kunze moved to give a positive recommendation to the
381                   Lehi City Council, finding that the proposed development code amendment is in
382                   conformance with the purposes, intent, and provisions of the general plan and its
383                   various elements. Commissioner Everett seconds.
384
      Lehi City Planning Commission                     8                                  February 27, 2025
385   Craig Chambers requests that Commissioner Kunze add a recommendation for the City Council
386   to look at the language of the clause. Commissioner Everett agrees. He expresses that he doesn’t
387   like the way the change has come about. He fears losing open space as a public amenity.
388
389                   Amended Motion: Commissioner Kunze moved to give a positive
390                   recommendation to the Lehi City Council, finding that the proposed development

401   3.7) Public hearing and recommendation of Lehi City’s request for review of a
402   Development Code amendment to Chapter 12 to update buffer and screening requirement
403   for non-residential zones adjacent to residential zones.
404
405   Jacob Curtis presented the item. This amendment was reviewed at the January work session. The
406   intent behind the amendment is to beef up the buffer and screening requirements. The larger
407   changes focus on including fencing specifications for use and zones next to residential zones, as
408   well as adjustments to landscaping clauses.
409
410   Commissioner Everett asked if drive isles are for planting boxes or for ingress/egress. He asked
411   for clarifying language to be added to the clause. Kim Struthers and Jacob Curtis agreed to look
412   at it.
413
414   Commissioner Eyre asked about potential exceptions in the landscaping clauses. He wants to
415   make as few exceptions as possible.
416
417   Commissioner Jackson opened public comment.
418
419   Matt Berg commented online and came to speak in person as well. He shared his concerns over
420   the compatibility section being deleted. He lives against a large commercial development and has
421   been able to use the clause in the past to argue against it and attain something more desirable for
422   his neighborhood. He admits that it is a highly subjective clause. He asks that the city clarify it
423   rather than delete it entirely.
424
425   John Madsen, Matt Berg’s neighbor, was also present. He has been before the city council many
426   times. He also advocates for the maintenance of the compatibility assessment clause. He
427   mentioned that in the past he has missed opportunities to provide feedback to the city on certain
428   decisions and has ended up with fencing issues on his property due to it. He appreciates the
429   clarified fencing amendments for this reason. He stated that he understands that making the
430   compatibility clause concrete will likely take a number of public hearings and meetings, but he
431   thinks it would be worthwhile. He would like to avoid taking all of it out in one go.
432
      Lehi City Planning Commission                    9                                 February 27, 2025
433   Commissioner Jackson closed public comment.
434
435   The commission thanked Berg and Madsen for their concise and applicable comments.
436
437   Commissioner Eyre asked the planning division why the compatibility clause is being removed.
438   Jacob Curtis explained that it’s a very surface level clause that defines nothing and is nearly
439   impossible to enforce. Craig Chambers further elaborated that as it stands it mainly opens up the
440   city for claims, as it’s too open of a clause. He suggested sending a recommendation to the city
441   council to look into it further. Commissioner Everett asked if there could be an intent statement
442   in the code that addresses compatibility. Chambers said that the best way to do it would be
443   through specific buffering guidelines in the code. Curtis suggested putting the intent statement
444   into the following section of the code that discusses buffering. Commissioner Eyre clarified that
445   the suggestion from the city’s legal team is to remove it. Chambers confirms that that is correct,
446   but that the next sections should still be investigated so that the intent can be enforced, rather
447   than existing as fluff in the code.
448
