Proposed Resolution 2021-6 adopting the Annual Action Plan for the period
July 1, 2021 through June 30, 2022 and directing that this document be
submitted to the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development
         A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the Fiscal Year (FY) 2022 Annual
Action Plan (AAP) came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated City Administration is proposing the Council
adopt a resolution that approves the AAP and budget for FY 2022, which is the second year of the five-year Consolidated Plan
(ConPlan) period. The amounts in the AAP budget as approved by the Council are incorporated into the Major Grants section of
the City Budget.
         Deputy Community Development Manager Smith summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation
to provide additional information regarding the FY21-22 AAP. The AAP year is July 1, 2021 to June 30, 2022 and is the
implementation plan for the second program year of the five-year ConPlan. It is the mechanism by which the City receives
Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) funds. The Community Development Block grant (CDBG) budget for the
FY22 AAP is $3, 129,408; 80 percent of the CDBG funds are aimed at benefiting low to moderate income (LMI) persons and 20
percent is for program administration. Programs funded with CDBG funds include:
               •   Infill housing- $150,000
               •   Target area public improvements - $250,000
               •   Own in Ogden - $250,000
               •   Emergency Home Repair- $40,000
               •   Quality Neighborhoods- $1,330,873
               •   Business Information Center (BIC) - $55,000
               •   Small Business Loan Program- $395,000
               •   Microenterprise Loan Program- $405,000
               •   Special Economic Development Projects- $100,000
               •   Administration- $403,534

              Vice Chair White inquired as to the manner in which the goals of the AAP are defmed; she asked if they are directed by
    HUD. Mr. Smith stated that many goals are crafted to meet the guidelines set forth by HUD to ensure that the City is eligible to
    receive grant funding. Vice Chair White asked if the City is required to provide an explanation to HUD in the event that goals of
    the AAP are not met. Mr. Smith answered yes but noted the City has been successful in meeting those goals each year and plans to
    continue to meet those goals.
              Council member Stephens asked what becomes of money that is unspent in any given AAP year. Mr. Smith stated it can
    be carried forward to future years utilizing methods defmed by HUD. Council member Stephens asked if there is a cap on the
    amount of money that can be carried over from year to year. Mr. Smith answered yes; the cap is 1.5 percent of the City's annual
    entitlement amount.

    AAP, HUD has a cap of $270,000 for the maximum sales price of a home for LMI individuals.

          Redevelopment Agency (RDA): Deputy Director Cooper used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the
proposed RDA budget for FY2021. The purpose of the RDA is to contribute to the responsible and sustainable growth of Ogden
City by elevating the quality of life in all neighborhoods through the facilitation of a wide range of housing, employment, and
wealth creation opportunities. Essentially, the RDA is intended to enhance livability by creating opportunity. Success of the RDA
is defmed by making a great City by doing the following:
               1. Creating a Distinct Image
               2. Creating a Central Marketplace or Distinct Downtown
               3. Creating a Reliable Economic Base
               4. Creating a Compact Dense Building Pattern
               5. Creating a Concentration and Variety of Interesting Housing
               6. Building an Understandable and Attractive Street System
               7. Developing Comfortable Parks, Plazas, and Open Space
               8. Creating a Sense of Permanence
               9. Creating a Soul
               10. Creating Visionary Leadership
          Greatness is measured by the City's prosperity, promotion, product, people, programming, and place. Immediate
challenges facing the RDA include relevancy given that the City's economic competition is fierce, employment centers are
shifting, and strategic alignment is important. Additionally, the RDA must consider resource allocation and important updates to
City codes. A long term challenge the RDA faces is sustainability; this includes balancing neighborhoods, furthering the
availability of quality/proximate development, ensuring expenses are not outpacing general fund revenues, addressing
poverty!homelessness issues in the community, and wealth creation/opportunity. Opportunities for achieving relevance and
sustainability include:
               •    Continuous investment in meaningful ways
               •    Leverage scarce resources
               •    Amplitude and Velocity
               •    Key Partnerships
               •    Wealth Creation/Opportunity/Jobs
