first to respond to emergencies. In those situations, he ensures affected residents are supported and
connected with resources such as the Red Cross. Chief Headman also recognized several of Mr.
Blinzinger’s recent accomplishments, including organizing and facilitating a FEMA Integrated Emergency
Management Course (IEMC) this past August, which brought together approximately sixty city
employees for a week-long training. He further acknowledged Mr. Blinzinger’s efforts in updating the
city’s Emergency Response Plan and his service as a liaison officer during the Willard Peak Fire in Ogden

Public Comment

Chair Garrett read the public comment preamble and opened the public comment period.

Katie Volpe addressed the Council, noting she has lived in Provo for nearly five years and cares deeply
about the animals in the community. She asked the City to update its ordinances to align with Utah’s
Community Cat Act by ending the impoundment and euthanasia of healthy community cats and
adopting a Trap-Neuter-Vaccinate-Return (TNVR) approach. Ms. Volpe shared that she became aware of
the issue while working as a letter carrier, frequently encountering stray cats and small colonies
throughout the city. She expressed disappointment that the Best Friends Animal Society TNVR pilot
program was withdrawn due to lack of agreement between the City and Police Department, despite
broad community support. She also noted that Utah County remains the only county in the state not to
achieve “no-kill” status, with a local cat save rate of about 56%, and urged the City to take action to
change that.

With no other comments, Chair Garrett closed the public comment period.

      Motion:       An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2025-59, as currently constituted, has been

Jessica Dahneke, City Planner, presented. She reminded the Council that in August they received the
initial petition and passed a resolution to consider it for further review. The current action was to
approve the annexation ordinance. Ms. Dahneke explained that the area is identified as Area 7 in the
City’s Annexation Policy, noting that approval would remove one of the few remaining unincorporated
“islands” within Provo. If approved, the annexed area would be designated as an Agricultural (A1.40)
zone rather than Open Space, consistent with the City’s annexation policy and general plan. She
emphasized that while no development proposals have been discussed, any property owner wishing to
develop in the future would need to apply for a rezone to allow higher density, which would return to
the Council for consideration.

Councilor MacKay asked how long an annexation typically takes for the applicant.

Ms. Dahneke explained that the process varies depending on required notices and certifications. It
begins with pre-noticing, followed by county notification and certification, then Council consideration
through a resolution. After a mandatory 31-day waiting period, the ordinance returns for final approval
before being certified by the state. She noted that annexations generally take around six months to
complete, barring unusual circumstances.

Chair Garrett referred to the staff report and noted that the annexation includes a portion of Foothill
Drive, which he found surprising as he believed all city roads were owned by Provo City. He recalled that
the property had originally been acquired through eminent domain for the road and asked for
clarification on ownership and maintenance.

Ms. Dahneke confirmed that a portion of Foothill Drive had remained county property, though the City
maintained the roadway. She explained that the annexation would incorporate that section into the
City, ensuring all portions of Foothill Drive within the area are included.

Chair Garrett opened public comment.

Sharron Memmott, of Provo, reported on the neighborhood meeting where the annexation proposal
had been presented. She thanked Ms. Dahneke for clarifying the zoning designation and expressed
appreciation that any future request for increased density would still be required to go through the
standard review process.

Chair Garrett closed public comment. With no other council discussion, he called for a vote.

      Vote:         The motion passed 5:0 with Councilors Bogdin, Garrett, Handley, MacKay, and
                    Whipple in favor.

Melia Dayley, Council Policy Analyst, presented. She reviewed the proposed amendment to the
definition of “owner occupancy.” She explained that the issue arose during the Bannock Drive ADU
discussion, prompting a staff and legal review to determine whether any conflicting language or
loopholes existed. The review found none, though some portions of the code caused confusion.
Ms. Dayley noted that the Planning Commission reviewed the amendment on September 24 and
recommended denial (7–1), citing the need to further study the definition and the effect of ADUs on
housing affordability. She stated that a zoning code rewrite is underway to address definitions for clarity
and that, while the language can be confusing in parts, it is sound when read as a whole.

Councilor Handley stated that he found the amendment somewhat difficult to follow and sought
clarification on why the Planning Commission voted against it. He asked staff whether removing the
language would make the code clearer or create challenges for applicants in situations similar to the
couple involved in the other case.

Ms. Dayley responded that the amendment does not change City policy but simply clarifies the wording
to prevent confusion. She explained that when read together, the existing clauses do not create a
loophole related to tax documentation, but the language has been difficult for both staff and applicants
to interpret. Removing the section is intended to simplify the code, consistent with the goals of the
ongoing zoning rewrite. She added that the Planning Commission’s denial appeared to stem partly from
broader concerns about Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs) and housing affordability, as referenced in the
General Plan, and from discomfort with the existing wording of the definition.

Chair Garrett confirmed with staff that Development Services is in the process of a zoning code rewrite
and asked how soon the Council might see these types of clarifications addressed through that effort
compared to acting now.

Ms. Dayley responded that she was unsure of the exact timeline for the zoning code rewrite but offered
to find out and provide an update to the Council by email.

Chair Garrett opened public comment. With none, he invited a council discussion.

Bill Peperone, Director of Development Services, clarified that the zoning code rewrite referenced by
Ms. Dayley is part of a comprehensive review of the City’s code. He stated that staff had hoped to bring
it to the Council by the end of the year but that it will likely be ready in the first quarter of next year.

Councilor Whipple stated that she supported revisiting the amendment because the existing language
had caused confusion by allowing an owner to claim status without being an occupant. She explained
that the code appeared to have been written to allow multiple owners, requiring only one to reside on
the property for an Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU), consistent with related language in the City Code.
She noted, however, that in practice the City has long interpreted the ordinance to require all owners to
occupy the property in order to qualify for an ADU. Councilor Whipple said that while she personally
understood the Planning Commission’s concerns about housing affordability and would favor allowing
one owner to occupy the property, she recognized that there was not broad support for that approach.
Therefore, she supported removing the confusing section to ensure the code is clear and consistent with
current enforcement.

Chair Garrett called for a vote.
