         2.1    Discussion of the Public Works Building. (Staff Presenter, Shane Paddock).

Public Works Director, Shane Paddock discussed the replacement of the Public Works Building. He
reported that the current Public Works Building was built in the mid-1970s. At that time, the building
codes were very different than they are currently. Two years ago, there was an earthquake in
Bluffdale. An Inspector was brought in to look at the Public Works Building and there were a few
structural cracks on the exterior. It was determined that the building was still acceptable to use but
was not up to code standards. The telemetry in the building was aged and the HVAC system was also
dilapidated. Mr. Paddock shared an aerial image of the current Public Works Building that is on
approximately 0.9 acres.

The Engineering and Public Works Departments recently went through a Division of Water Quality
inspection, which was a lengthy three to four-day inspection. City infrastructure was inspected as
well as the storm drains. The inspection looked into compliance as well. For instance, whether

records were up to date, what was done with spent oil, and how vehicles are washed. Mr. Paddock
identified a sto1m drain on the aerial image, which was in a bay inside the Public Works Building.
The Division of Water Quality was still working on the inspection findings. At the moment, the City
is not allowed to use the vac truck until the final inspection is done. The reason was that the VOC
comes off of road salts, oil, and gas from vehicles and ends up on the road. This temporary restriction
impacted both the vac truck and street sweeper.

Mr. Paddock reported that the Division of Water Quality is not allowing the City to wash any large
equipment at the current Public Works Building because there is not a containment system in place.
He explained that being able to wash and maintain the vehicles is especially important during the
winter months since road salt can impact the functionality of the vehicles. He had spoken to some
neighboiing communities over the last several weeks and a city to the south reported that they were
recently fined $375,000 from the same inspection. Another city was fined over $400,000. It was very
serious, and he did not know what would happen in the future.

Currently, some of the City equipment is exposed. Mr. Paddock explained that the boom truck should
be inside, but it sat outside for most of the winter because there was not enough space to store it
appropriately. He displayed images of the yard and the current salt containment shed. The salt
containment shed is not compliant because no salt materials are allowed to leech off and go into the
storm drains. It all needs to be contained on-site. Mr. Paddock explained that some equipment is
stored at the Rodeo Arena. There had been vandalism in the last year, even though the Rodeo Arena
is locked at night. It was a concern that there are unprotected City assets. Mr. Paddock displayed an
image of the Parks Building at the arena. It used to be storage only for Parks equipment, such as
tractors used for the arena. He discussed fleet maintenance and explained that during the winter
certain pieces of equipment cannot be stored outside.

Mr. Paddock reported that in 2018, there were 13 individuals in Public Works and there are now 26
due to growth. The intention was to also have nine seasonal workers. He shared an image of the
Public Works Building parking lot. Due to the number of workers, as well as the seasonal employees,
creative solutions are needed. The current Public Works Building is at full capacity. He added that
Redwood Road is becoming busier and more dangerous. Mr. Paddock shared a video illustrating his
traffic concerns.

An overlay of the current Public Works Building against the property over on 3 800 West was shown.
He shared an image of the site that the City plans to build on. Mr. Paddock explained that there had
been back and forth discussions with an architect about the new Public Works Building. There would
be 60 days for the completion of construction documents. Currently, those documents were
approximately 85% complete. Future steps included the preparation of the bid documents and bid
proposals. The actual build would be anywhere from 14 to 16 months. He noted that prices are
fluctuating at the moment and there have been substantial increases.

Administrative Services Director, Bruce Kartchner discussed the bond and funding. He stated that
the City is still working to determine if it is possible to find less expensive funding. The municipal
rates are currently increasing. An analysis was conducted, and he did not believe a new Parameters
Resolution would be needed. As long as there are no increases in costs, he believed the project could

Council Member Kallas referenced the $16 million expenditure in the Local Building Authority
("LBA") and wondered why it was listed. Mr. Kartchner explained that a separate entity owns and
built the building and rented it back to the City. City Hall was also part of the same LBA. The
General Fund each year makes a payment to the LBA to cover the bond payment. Council Member

does not know what the final bond will be. However, there are times when the bondholders will
require there be a cash reserve equal to one payment. That had been accounted for and would be
handled within the budget.

       2.2    Discussion Relating to the New City of Bluffdale General Plan. (Staff Presenter,
              Grant Crowell).

City Planner and Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell shared updates related to the City
of Bluffdale General Plan. It was discussed during the previous Work Session. Since then, the
consultants had double-checked some of the data and a few of the map exhibits were updated. He
shared a list of the changes made since the last meeting, which included updates to the Housing
Framework map and node name updates. Additionally, Utah County was added to certain maps, such
as the Open Space and Trails Map. The City square miles were confirmed, and the infographics and
data points were clarified. Mr. Crowell noted that not every data point was released but there were
certain estimates. The intention was to use as many data points as possible from the 2020 census.
Hyperlinks were added to the document and other minor edits were made.

Mr. Crowell asked that the Council discussions begin with Section 4.2 - Future Housing
Considerations. Council Member Crockett noted that Very Low-Density Housing was described as
a one-acre lot or larger. Low-Density Housing is located between Redwood Road and the railroad.
Below that, it stated that Detached Accessory Dwelling Units ("DADU") are not currently allowed
by Code. She noted that those lots would likely not have room for a detached garage and wondered
if that language should be moved from the Low-Density Housing Section to the Very Low-Density

Council Member Hales asked about the cottage clusters, duplexes, and triplexes that were listed under
Low-Density Housing. Council Member Crockett believed it made sense to list those housing types
there based on the legal definition of Low-Density housing. Council Member Hales believed that
having several homes on one lot is not necessarily Low-Density Housing. He asked that cottage
clusters, duplexes, and triplexes be moved to the Medium-Density Housing Section instead.
Mr. Crowell explained that those were examples of housing types that could occur. If the example
diagrams were confusing they could be removed from the General Plan. Council Member Crockett
pointed out that the City needs a variety of housing types. It is important to keep them. Council
Member Kallas believed the examples were just ideas to consider.

Mr. Crowell wanted the document to be something the City Council would be willing to pass. If it is
controversial to reference DADUs or cottage clusters, he was open to shifting the language or
removing the language as necessary. Mayor Hall asked that the ADU information be moved from
Low-Density Housing to Very Low-Density Housing. There was further discussion regarding the
placement of the language as well as the intention of the language. Council Member Aston pointed
out that many residents have those types of ADUs already. Council Member Hales did not believe
the laws should be ignored simply because some people broke the rules. Council Member Kallas
believed the intention was not to change policies. The General Plan was simply outlining certain
possibilities within Low-Density Housing areas.

Mr. Crowell explained that the General Plan document could be changed so that type of information
is either removed or made broader. If the Council was not interested in seeing cottage clusters listed
that housing type would not need to be in the document. Some communities tried to create policies
with their General Plan, however, if the Council was not ready to do that certain discussions could be
deferred. Council Member Crockett was fine leaving the ADU information in the Low-Density
Housing Section because she understood the points made by Council Member Hales. It was
appropriate to leave the information where it is until a policy decision is made.

The Housing Density and Water Conservation Section was discussed. Mayor Hall noted that the
section addressed a great deal about storm drains but not much about conservation. She stated that
there was a Water Conservation Plan and wondered if that would be linked in the Appendix.
Mr. Crowell explained that the Water Conservation Plan was referenced in the Resiliency Chapter of
the General Plan. Having the adopted Water Conservation Plan included in the Appendix would
likely meet the new statute, which was to have a Water Conservation Plan in the General Plan by
2025. The Housing Density and Water Conservation Section referenced higher-density housing and
explained that this type of housing would have a different impact on the water supply.

Council Member Crockett believed there needed to be housing types for all ages and stages of life.
That meant that people who love living in Bluffdale could continue to live in Bluffdale throughout
