Study Session

      Wasatch Front Regional Council – Wasatch Choice Vision Update
      Community Development Director Jennifer Jastremsky explained that the
      Wasatch Front Regional Council (WFRC) had requested Draper City to review
      the Wasatch Choice Vision Land Use Plan and provide any proposed updates.
      Ms. Jastremsky presented the current land use plan map, outlined staff-
      recommended modifications, and responded to questions from the City
      Council.

      700 East Storm Drain Fund Scope Change
      Deputy Public Works Director Robert Markle reported that the Lone Peak
      Culvert Reconstruction Project was completed under budget, leaving a surplus
      of $218,000 from the amount originally allocated from the Storm Water Fund.
      Mr. Markle stated they would be transferring the remaining funds to the 700
      East Storm Drain Project.

      Discussion: Private Road Solid Waste
      Deputy Public Works Director Robert Markle outlined challenges the City faced
      in providing garbage and recycling services on private streets. These included
      limited access, lack of turnaround space, absence of recorded easements

      permitting City access, a higher rate of accidents, and increased liability. Mr.
      Markle presented two potential solutions proposed by staff:

      •   Contract with a private hauler, with the City managing can delivery and
          billing at the private rate (approximately $3–$6 more per month); or

      •   Continue City-provided services, with residents required to wheel their
          cans to the public right-of-way.

      Mr. Markle stated that staff recommended the private hauler option. He noted
      that of the 461 private streets in Draper, 73% were already serviced by private
      haulers. A majority of the City Council expressed support for proceeding with
      the private hauler option.

      Legislative Update/Ethics and OPMA Training
      City Attorney Traci Gundersen provided an update on the recent Legislative
      Session, including bills likely to impact Draper, and answered questions from
      the Council.

      Council/Manager Reports
      Councilmember F. Lowry reported that residents on Canyon Breeze Lane had

6.d   Public Hearing: Ordinance #1645 approving the Farmer's Residence
      Development Agreement for approximately 0.32 acres of property located at
      approximately 902 E. 12650 S. within Draper City
      Community Development Director Jennifer Jastremsky said the subject
      property was within the Neighborhood Commercial land use designation and
      within the RA1 Zone (one-acre residential). She said the property had
      historically been used for residential, and the previous home on the property
      was built in 1907. Ms. Jastremsky said the city had been unable to identify
      who owned the adjoining private road (900 East).

      Ms. Jastremsky said the property owner proposed to widen the private road
      from 21 feet to 24 feet, which would help with emergency access, and
      requested a 12-foot setback along the 900 East property line (18-foot
      reduction) to build a single home and detached garage. The proposed
      development agreement would prohibit accessory dwelling units (ADUs) on
      the property, and existing on-street public parking would be preserved. Ms.
      Jastremsky said the Planning Commission reviewed the request and
      forwarded a positive recommendation.

      Ms. Jastremsky answered questions from the Council about setbacks,
      maintenance responsibility, and indemnification with regard to the private
      road. Mayor Walker asked about indemnification in the case of bankruptcy.

      Mayor Walker opened a public hearing, and closed the public hearing seeing
      no one come forward.

     Responding to a question from Mayor Walker regarding ownership of 900 East
     and City liability, Vince Rampton, attorney for the applicants, said he was
     confident the existing line of trees flanking 900 East would help establish
     ownership of the property within that line. He said the proposed widening of
     the private road would benefit the city.

     Councilmember F. Lowry asked what would happen if the proposed access
     along 900 East were deemed to not belong to the applicants. Mr. Rampton said
     the applicants would need to acquire access either by prescriptive use or
     acquisition. Ms. Gundersen read aloud a portion of the indemnity clause, and
     expressed confidence that the language would both protect the City and
     provide flexibility for the property owners.

     Councilmember F. Lowry pointed out language in the proposed agreement did
     not specify the applicant would be responsible for maintenance of the road in
     perpetuity. City Manager Mike Barker responded staff were confident the road
     was not a City road. The Council and staff discussed potential scenarios.
     Councilmember F. Lowry said he liked that the setback along 12650 South
     would be the standard 30 feet, and said he liked that the property would be
     developed.

     Councilmember F. Lowry moved to approve Ordinance #1645.
     Councilmember Vawdrey seconded the motion.

     A roll call vote was taken. The motion passed unanimously.
