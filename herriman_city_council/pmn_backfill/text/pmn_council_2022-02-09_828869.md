approved, consolidated all of the residential units to one area. Manager Spencer offered a supportive

to the Herriman housing market, and he thought the planned development looked very interesting. He felt the

moving forward. Mayor Lorin Palmer thought it might not be an appropriate time for a negotiation, and thought
the request should move forward, since the development was beneficial for the City and he wanted to see the
project move forward.

    2.3. Discussion regarding a proposed amendment to Title 10 pertaining to accessory structure
         regulations (File No. Z2021-054) - Clinton Spencer, Planning Manager
Planning Manager Spencer presented the updates for accessory structures. He recalled the amendment had
originally been presented in the January 12, 2022 meeting, and the Council requested some changes. He stated
an exception had been made for corner lots, and any building less than eight feet tall which was adjacent to the
street had a setback within one foot of the property line. If the building was taller than eight feet, it would have
to meet the setback requirement of the home. He briefly elaborated on the changes, and offered some scenarios
in which the corner lot exception would apply. Manager Spencer also reviewed additional exceptions in regards
to agricultural uses, and stated greenhouses would not have to meet the architectural requirements for the
accessory structures. He also clarified requests for exceptions could be handled by staff, and applicants would
not have to go through the Planning Commission. He lastly stated the amendment had changed the definition
of a pie-shaped lot to be an irregular lot rather than a regular lot, even though there were many lots with a similar
shape throughout the City. He asked if there were any questions or comments about the updates.

Councilmember Hodges asked why the Planning Commission had determined a pie shaped lot should be
irregular, and Manager Spencer replied people bought those lots since the rear lot was a little bit larger, and so if
people wanted an exception made about units in a pie shaped lot they would have to apply for a building permit.

Councilmember Shields appreciated the clarification of the additional height requirements, and commented he
no longer felt it was an arbitrary number for the setback requirement. Mayor Palmer agreed these requirements
put them on par with other cities, and he felt this amendment would offer additional options to residents which
were currently not available.

Mayor Pro Tempore Ohrn asked why galvanized metals were banned in the amendment. Manager Spencer
replied the galvanized metal would not fit the aesthetic of residential development, and noted it was a value
decision. Mayor Pro Tempore Ohrn thought galvanized metal was a useful material, and did not think the
February 9, 2022 – City Council Minutes                                         Page 4 of 10
aesthetics of a neighborhood was more important than function. She wanted to allow the galvanized materials
to be used as well, and she also did not think the exterior color of the accessory structure should be controlled.
She pointed out barns were often brightly colored, and did not detract from the aesthetic of agricultural areas.
Councilmember Shields agreed the requirements for materials and colors felt overly restrictive. He did point
out it would be hard to delineate between people who wanted to build a barn as opposed to an accessory
structure. Manager Spencer commented the agricultural exceptions were more for height and size, and not
necessarily color. He added if the Council wanted to lessen the restrictions, they could be conducted. Mayor
Pro Tempore Ohrn felt the requirements were an overreach of property rights, and wanted to have the
requirements reworked. City Manager Cherpeski suggested pilots easily became disoriented from the sun glare
from galvanized roofing, which could pose a safety hazard. He said there were non-reflective surfaces which
could also be utilized. Mayor Pro Tempore Ohrn said if there was a safety consideration, she had no problem
with banning galvanized metal, but she did not agree it should be banned on a purely aesthetic basis.

The Council determined this issue would be presented at a future meeting for consideration.

    2.4. City social media roundtable - City Council
City Manager Cherpeski indicated this item had been a request from the Council to discuss how they wanted
items from their partner agencies to be shared on social media. He said the City used a variety of social media
platforms, and they were required in some cases to share the content of their partners. Mayor Pro Tempore
Ohrn discussed that she had been upset to see a shared post which claimed four free COVID tests were available
for each household, and said this was misleading since tests and vaccines were funded by taxpayers. She did not
want Herriman to continue propagating a myth which things from the government were free, when they in fact
were not. She said they needed to be sensitive to the kind of messages shared with the community, and thought
it was important to educate people.

Councilmember Hodges agreed with the principle of Mayor Pro Tempore Ohrn, but noted he did not want to
confuse people and make them think they had to pay for those four COVID tests. He asked about the degree
of flexibility the City had in what content they chose to share with their community, and Councilmember
Henderson agreed they needed to consider if they were supporting propaganda or sharing information. Mayor
Pro Tempore Ohrn said her only issue with the post in question was in regards to the word “free.” She said she
had no other concerns with the content shared, and thought it was important that people knew COVID tests
were available. She opined the Council had a responsibility to keep in mind what kind of messages were being
shared to the residents. Mayor Palmer felt the City had done a good job in being politically neutral in the content
shared on social media, and he did not think there were any problems with the way staff handled social media.
He did not think any changes needed to be made.

covered by an MDA. He said the staff wanted direction from the Council if they would like the requests to be

Councilmember Henderson clarified City Planner Maloy wanted to know if the applications should be processed
individually as they came in, or together under the MDA. City Planner Maloy confirmed, and elaborated
generally, the development agreements included what the land use plan would be, as well as which zoning tools
which would be appropriate for those land uses. He said in this case, the Council would hear the General Plan

Mayor Palmer agreed the project should be kept together. Mayor Pro Tempore Ohrn concurred, and felt they

     7.2. Consideration to Modify the Landscape Design Standards - Jonathan Bowers, City Engineer
City Engineer Jonathan Bowers explained there were two ordinances in the land design standards which had
come up in question recently. He said the two items included live plant requirements for lawn designs, and
artificial turf and plantings. He said they did not have a proposal that day, but had some items for consideration
to get direction as to how they should proceed with the changes.

City Engineer Bowers stated currently, the requirement was to have 75-percent live plant material coverage in
the park strip, and clarified this requirement was at plant maturity, not at the time of installation. He commented
the requirement in the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District was 50-percent, and they had received some
petitions to reduce their live plant material requirement down to 50-percent as well. He shared images which
showed what both 75-percent and 50-percent coverage looked like on park strips. He said they had adopted the
water efficiency standards in light of the drought, and he anticipated there would be a reduction there if they
reduced the standard. He also discussed it would reduce the capital cost. He said artificial turf offered an
alternative to live plant material would not require water, although the current ordinance stated artificial turf
February 9, 2022 – City Council Minutes                                          Page 8 of 10
could not be used to meet the open space requirements. City Engineer Bowers looked for direction from the
Council as to how they should proceed.

Councilmember Shields asked if the policies clarified if the live plants had to be native or water wise. City
Engineer Bowers explained there were materials allowed, and they did recommend drought-resistant plants be
used, although it was not specified in code. Councilmember Shields did not feel the reduction would be a big
deal, but he felt it would be beneficial to require water wise plants be utilized, and have the requirement reflected
in the code.

Councilmember Hodges also felt the reduction would yield substantial cost savings in installation and
maintenance, and felt it would be great to adopt the lower standard. However, he seconded the idea to increase
the standard of the plants which could be used. Councilmember Henderson suggested a requirement be put in
place for water circulation.

Mayor Pro Tempore Ohrn discussed the shade canopy was created with 75-percent plant material at full maturity,
and elaborated the shade canopy helped to keep moisture in the ground, which would then help conserve water.
She believed 50-percent live plant material would still be sufficient. She also noted there was a house bill which
was currently being discussed in the State legislature was pertinent to water wise plants and conservation
requirements, which might make the decision for them in the event that it was passed.

Councilmember Shields asked how the calculations for 50-percent were done, and City Engineer Bowers
explained the requirement was per contiguous park strip, so they could determine if the plants should be
clustered on one of the strips, or spaced out along the entire strip. He also noted they based the coverage off
of plant maturity.

Mayor Pro Tempore Ohrn commented she did not want to see grass on park strips anymore, and discussed the
importance that everyone, including the City, developers, and residents, get behind water conservation so homes
could continue to be constructed.

The Council consensus was to move this item forward.

    7.3. Update on the Safe Walk Route to the New Elementary School located within the Hidden
         Oaks development - Jonathan Bowers, City Engineer
City Engineer Bowers stated they had ongoing construction along Main Street, which was anticipated to go until
summer 2023. He explained the construction came into conflict with a safe walk route for the new elementary
school, and he offered some options for the Council to consider, including the creation of a temporary safe walk
route or bussing the children each day. He commented the school district was open to discuss any options, but
noted their preference was to create a temporary safe walking route, rather than temporarily bussing the students.

Mayor Pro Tempore Ohrn stated she had reached out to Representative Daryl Robinson to discuss the option
of getting a bus for the affected students, but she did not believe this plan had been finalized, and was still
looking for a better solution. She commented the temporary safe walking route would require tat the elementary
students walk through a remote area, which she felt was unsafe.
February 9, 2022 – City Council Minutes                                             Page 9 of 10

Councilmember Shields agreed the route might be unsafe, but also recognized there would be a financial impact
to the City to construct a new temporary walking route, and felt the money could be better spent bussing the
children. He noted if it was a financial burden for the school district, they could have a conversation with them
to find solutions. Deputy Police Chief Cody Stromberg agreed the walking route was not ideal, and there were
active construction sites along the route as well. He noted his feeling the route was much safer than using the
Main Street route while under construction, and said they would have to pay to create a physical barrier between
the traffic and the construction if they wanted the children to use the specified route. He said since the temporary
route was a City-owned trail, it would need to be updated and maintained at some point in the future anyway,
so this would just accelerate the timeline.

Councilmember Henderson clarified these solutions would only be needed for the upcoming school year, and
also clarified they would not remove the temporary trail after it was no longer needed as a route for the students.
City Engineer Bowers explained most of the portions of the temporary safe walk route were already existing
access roads, and clarified it would not need an excessive amount of work to get it into usable shape. He
explained a section would not be maintained after it served its purpose for the elementary students, and the rest
of the path was an already existing gravel road. Councilmember Henderson confirmed they would staff crossing
guards, and Deputy Police Chief Cody Stromberg replied they would have crossing guards posted at the
intersections, and have the temporary route patrolled by an officer during the time it was used by the students.
City Engineer Bowers also noted a large section of the road construction on Main Street would actually be done
before the start of the school year, but it would still be unwise to have the students walk down Main Street while
they finished the project.
