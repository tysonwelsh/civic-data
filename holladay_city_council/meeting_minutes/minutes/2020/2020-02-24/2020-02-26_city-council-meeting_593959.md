III.    Appeal of Final Site Plan Approval for Lot D of Holladay Hills (former Cottonwood Mall
        Site).
Mayor Dahle read a statement setting forth the procedures to be followed and the background of the
request. Following presentations by the applicant and staff, the Council will have the opportunity to
ask questions. No public comment was to be taken. Mr. Steele’s appeal focused on the specifics of
the Site Development Master Plan (“SDMP”) for a project that is the governing plan for the
properties. He expected the Council to struggle with the issue of the 90 feet allowed in the zone and
the five stories. Mr. Steele noted in his appeal that the overall merits of the proposed development
are not at issue and asked that the arguments focus on the issue at hand. Procedural issues were
discussed. It was noted that any final decision must be made in an open and public meeting by a
motion from the Council. That was expected to occur tonight with a formalized written decision to
follow. Four votes of the Council were required to overturn the site plan approval and to sustain the
appeal.

Ryan Steele - 4876 Colony Dr. He is here to settle a legal question regarding the first block to be
developed on the Holladay Hills site. He pointed out that impact on the community and property
rights are at issue. The determination to be made was focused on whether a seven-story building is
permitted where the 2007 SDMP clearly allows only four or five stories. Mr. Steele pointed out that
the role of the Council is to apply the law as-is. The simple question was whether the site plan
complies with the SDMP. He intended to show that it very clearly does not.

Mr. Steele acknowledged the development team who he was confident will do great work on the
site. He explained that the SDMP is explicitly clear in limiting most of Block D to four stories with
a portion limited to five. The Open District, in which Block D is located, is limited to five stories.

He referenced page three of the presentation, which specifically limits height to a maximum of five
stories. Page five includes primarily four-story uses for Block D with the eastern edge showing
fifth story uses. No uses beyond those heights are contemplated anywhere on the site. The
Building Massing and Heights Plan does not contemplate any stories beyond four or five. The Site
Parking Plan on page 8 clarifies the intent that the four stories envisioned is the maximum above
grade and because parking is in the “donut hole” of Block D, it is where the buildings are not. That
clarifies that the intent was never for the number of stories to be on top of stories of parking.

Referencing the conceptual drawings on page 7, Mr. Steele observed that none of the buildings is
above five stories. The site plan for Block D calls for seven stories with two levels of above-ground
parking sitting below five stories of residential. He learned from the developer, Steve Peterson that
the five-story restriction can be on top of parking but their plan deviates from this interpretation.
On the east side of the building, there are five stories of residential above retail. He recognized that
the developers are satisfying the 90-foot height requirement. The SDMP requires it and limits the
buildings to four or five stories. By adhering only to the 90-foot requirement it alters the intent of
the SDMP. The western edge of Highland Drive was limited to four stories and they effectively
instituted a graduated building height envelope.

Mr. Steele stated that the floor area ratio has also changed by more than 10%. In the site plan, there
is a 35% increase in the square footage of the residential area over the maximum from the SDMP.
The retail is within the accepted limits. The plan includes pads for future retail placement and
accommodates more than is proposed and exceeds the maximum. In terms of precedent, in a
conversation with Mr. Peterson, he indicated that his history with Millrock is guiding his
interpretation of the height. However, it is guiding his notion that height trumps stories when the
reality is that Millrock was a completely different situation. A recent Supreme Court ruling was
that approval of the SDMP is a legislative act. That matters tonight because it specifies that this is a
law governing the site. Also, in their opinion they noted that because Holladay has RM/U zoning,
the document serves as the zoning ordinance to fill in the gaps. There is no procedure addressing an
increase to seven stories. Mr. Steele stated that in the absence of that it has to be satisfied.

In terms of flexibility of the plan, there is a notion in the RM/U zone that the SDMP is intended to
be a flexible guide. He argued that flexibility is already included in the SDMP. There are choices
of uses that can go on the site of which one is parking. There are also minimums and maximums
for parking, density, and commercial square footage other than areas where the SDMP specifies that
the limit is a maximum of five stories.

Mr. Steele stated that deviating from the SDMP on the first block puts the development and the
ADL at risk, which specifies that the SDMP must be followed. He saw no reason to put a $122
million agreement at risk in the very first block. It potentially exposes the City to challenges and
alters the outcome of the 2018 vote. Mr. Steele remarked that the developers have stated repeatedly
to the Planning Commission that they intended to comply with the SDMP. He stated that approving
a seven-story building deviates from the letter and spirit of the law for the site.

Mayor Dahle referred to ambiguity in the SDMP that references 90 feet of building height and all
agreed to it. He questioned the ability to have human-scale development. The primary question
was why a group of architects and City Council Members prepare a document that allowed for 614
residential units on a site with little or no single-family. The vision was that there would be a lot of

multi-family stacked above retail on the site, which is called for in the SDMP. He questioned why a
group of architects, developers, and City Council Members would restrict development to five
stories and then allow heights of up to 90 feet. The math behind fitting 614 residential units in the
development as it is conceived does not work. It was clarified that the 614 units do not have to go
in Block D and can be placed elsewhere on the site. Mayor Dahle commented that the situation

objection in 2007 but a plan was prepared that accommodated just that. The Mayor’s opinion was
that the SDMP is a development guideline and the 90 feet is the limiting restriction rather than the
five stories.

There was some question about the “/” in the language of the SDMP. Matt Durham referred to
Section 6.112 of the Chicago Manual of Style, which states that a “/” signifies alternatives and is
also used for alternative spellings or names. Some were of the opinion that “/” means “and”. There
was some question about the interpretation as it applies to this application. Mr. Steele noted that
only approval of the site was being appealed. He pointed out that whatever is decided tonight will
likely set a precedent for the rest of the development.

Steve Peterson - 6147 South Verness Cove. He thanked the Council for their service. He
commented that the Cottonwood Mall site has always been considered a key source of revenue and
is important to the financial viability of the City. Last September, Millrock Capital and Woodbury
Corporation purchased the site from the Howard Hughes Corporation and branded it as Holladay
Hills. They listened to each stakeholder who was interested in discussing their vision and plan.
Their goal was to gain support from all community members, which some considered unrealistic.
They hired the original author of the SDMP and invited them to attend a design charette to
understand the entitlements to fully comply with the SDMP that was written in 2007. They invited
community stakeholders to attend the meeting and received input and feedback. The original author
of the SDMP led the design charette.

Mr. Peterson reported that they invested tens of millions of dollars in the site and spent hundreds of
thousands of dollars in architectural engineering fees as part of the approval process. He stressed
their commitment to the project and stated that they listened, discussed, and received input and were
incorporating as much as they are able into the development. They understand that the number one
priority of the residents is to achieve more meaningful green space. They fully comply with the
entitlement requirements within the 2007 SDMP. Block D conforms 100% with the SDMP
guidelines.

Mr. Peterson stated that the main question was what supersedes height or number of stories.
Resolution 07-51 states the intent for height should be the maximum rather than the number of
stories. The SDMP also illustrates six and seven-story buildings and clearly shows that that was the
intent. A rendering was schematically designed in 2008 by General Growth Properties that clearly
shows the intent of keeping the height under 90 feet but with over seven stories. Once the SDMP
was approved buildings were designed by General Growth that exceeded five stories.

Mr. Peterson referenced the Land Use Plan regarding use of stories there are examples of layout that
are not site plan specific and is form-based document. They included graduated building envelopes
with two-story buildings along Riverwalk Drive creating a human scale on the west side of the
block and walkable trails along the riverfront. The taller buildings are envisioned on the interior of

the site as shown on page six. Mr. Peterson stated that the appellant brought up a question about the
Floor Area Ratio (“FAR”) but it is not in the SDMP, which ties it to districts. Section 13.65.080
(b)(2) addresses the proposed modification of residential density or intensity and specifies that in
the open they have to remain within the FAR parameter. They have investment-backed
expectations and at great expense complied with all of the notices. They are in full compliance with
the site plan approvals required for Block D. The SDMP functions the same as the City’s General
Plan in guiding future growth in larger communities. It is intended to be a comprehensive yet
flexible guide. The plans and renderings submitted for Block D were available during the
neighborhood meetings and public hearings throughout the process and they followed the
conceptual, preliminary, and final plat submissions.

Mr. Peterson fully understood that the maximum building height is 90 feet in the Open District.
Maximums and minimums were established in the SDMP for building height, parking stalls,
setbacks, open space, square footage, and mixed-use allocations. In addition, there are permitted
land uses by district set forth on page 3 and 12 blocks are set. They were careful to understand the
approvals and what is included in the SDMP and ADL and have complied with those requirements.
Mr. Peterson recognized how important the project is for the community and was willing to get
involved at great personal risk. He hoped that they can unite and move forward with the
development of Holladay Hills.

Council Member Gibbons identified potential conflicts with the height and the maximum number of
stories. Mr. Peterson did not consider the SDMP language to be ambiguous after speaking to the
author. He stated that the restrictions are based on the Building Code. Type 3 construction is stick
frame that does not allow for five stories. The limitation on the number of stories is tied to
residential units rather than height. Mr. Peterson also stated that height based on the resolution for
the adoption of the documents clearly specifies that the intent was for height to be the intent of the
maximum. He did not see the conflict. He also pointed out that if there is a discrepancy the
landowner is favored by the law. Mr. Peterson stated that they did a great deal of research before
investing in the property and were certain of the intent before they proceeded.

In response to a question raised, Mr. Peterson stated that five stories refers to their understanding
based on expert feedback. He explained that they cannot exceed five stores on Type 3 construction.
General Growth intended to construct Type 3 buildings for residential. Mayor Dahle agreed that it
is ambiguous when there is a reference to stories in the document. It is also corroborated in a
statement that they speak specifically to height but do not mention stories. It seemed apparent that
it is a height limited development and not a story-limited development. Council Member Peterson
pointed out that the general development guidelines are to be used when working through the
document.

Todd Godfrey, City Attorney commented on the legal aspects of the issue and stated that it was a
clear principle of law before the statute was adopted as part of the common law of the State. It has
always been the case for cities that if there is ambiguity in a regulation that governs an application,
it has to be construed in favor of the property owner. The new statute makes it stronger and now
uses language that specifies that unless an application is clearly prohibited by the regulations, it has
to be construed in favor.

Paul Allred, Community Development Director reported that in terms of the Planning Commission’s
decision making, the Technical Review Committee (“TRC”) met on the application. They reviewed
it at length and met with the applicants on several occasions. Staff felt from the beginning that the
application met the requirements and allowances of the SDMP. During the public process, there
was not much concern expressed about height or number of stories at the Planning Commission
level. He characterized the Planning Commission discussion and stated that there was not much
addressed about stories versus height. Staff never considered that stories would supersede height.
His experience with the development of the SDMP was that it was never intended for five stories to
be the maximum with 90 feet. He stated that it is unlikely that that would be built, especially in
mixed-use buildings. Staff recommended approval to the Planning Commission and they found
compliance based on their reading, understanding, and interpretation of the SDMP. There were
concerns with the civil drawings after the Planning Commission approval in that there was a
significant amount of engineering that needed to be done. The architecture, parking, and land uses
were deemed to comply. The Planning Commission made it clear that it will be important for each
subsequent phase to track how they comply with the minimums and maximums. It was noted that
staff reads the document more flexibly than the Appellant does.

In response to a question raised, Mr. Allred stated that five stories refers to residential and could
refer to the number of residential stories over parking below, which was proposed. He reviewed the
document in great detail to ensure that they were interpreting it correctly. He acknowledged that
there are some ambiguities in the document. Staff’s interpretation was that it complied and worked
well on Block D. He stated that it is not a controversial issue for the community as a whole. Mr.
Allred stated that the argument is an issue of perspective.

Mayor Dahle’s opinion was that the ambiguity is eliminated after reading the statement on the front
page that states that it is intended that height is the maximum stated. If additional height is
requested by the developer, they must first appear before the Planning Commission and City
Council.

Council Member Fotheringham struggled with making a determination but appreciated having
context with regard to how the stories and height fit together. He felt better prepared to make a
decision.

Council Member Quinn reported that State law specifies that to interpret a regulation it has to favor
the applicant. Mr. Steele did not challenge State law and stated that if they compare the four and
five-story requirements to the parking plan they are in different places. There is a notion that five
stories on top of something else is the correct interpretation. He explained that there are very
clearly four and five-story regulations and nothing limiting it to five stories for stick frame
construction. Their own plan is in conflict as well because there are places where it is limited to
four with five stories of stick construction on top of two stories of parking.

Mr. Steele’s interpretation was that the 90-foot limit applies but the five-story limitation still
appears seven times throughout the document.

It was noted that the plan was approved and the drawings submitted later. The recession hit and the
project was put on hold. It was Council Member Petersen’s opinion that the drawings represent
General Growth Properties’ vision for what they wanted to have on the property. Mr. Godfrey

stated that the plans were presented by Mr. Peterson are not as approved drawings but what was
submitted based on the approved SDMP close to the time that it was approved. Mayor Dahle stated
that if it is a public document it is appropriate for it to be presented but it should be clarified that it
was never approved by the Council. If the SDMP was approved in 2007 and there was a
parenthetical reference to five stories in the document. He saw no reason why the developer that

Mayor Dahle considered it to be a commonsense issue and felt that the reference was to dwelling
units. He did not consider it to be a difficult issue.

Council Member Fotheringham struggled with the sensibility of the issue given that so much effort
was placed in the document. He also understood that there are terms of art and context assumed in
the SDMP and architectural plans. He was comfortable moving forward and making a decision.

Council Member Gibbons appreciated Council Member Fotheringham’s position and agreed with
him. Council Member Gibbons stated that what they were being asked to provide an interpretation
of the 90-foot/five-story issue. His opinion was that there are two different uses of that language in
the SDMP. On pages one and six it refers to a maximum 90-foot height (five stories). On page 3, it
refers to 90 feet/five story maximum. While the isolated phrases alone seem ambiguous, they
should first look at the entire document to resolve ambiguities. He commented that a 90-foot, five-
story structure makes no sense. Also, the common usage of a “/” means “or” and not “and”. Last,
they might be convinced as put forward by Mr. Peterson is that the reference to stories may refer to
dwellings, which was a persuasive argument. They also might look at the building massing and
heights plan, which clearly shows seven stories above ground. If there is an irreconcilable
ambiguity, they can look at other factors. If the Council is unable to reconcile an ambiguity they
shall interpret and apply the land use regulation to favor the land use application in which case they
would have to rule in favor of Holladay Hills. He personally did not see an ambiguity when looking
at the entire document.

Council Member Durham did not feel there was ambiguity and did not see how the 90 feet/five
stories worked. He saw examples where there were building above seven stories and while
Mr. Steele’s presentation was very persuasive initially, after looking at the document as a whole he
stated that the document should be looked at as a whole rather than specific sections. The idea that
makes the most sense is that if the height does not exceed 90 feet the applied for use will be
consistent with the SDMP. He also agreed that reasonable minds could differ and if an ambiguity
were found, State law directs them to rule in favor of the use applied for.

Council Member Quinn stated that she does not have a background in land use and appreciated the
various explanations. While Mr. Steele’s presentation was compelling, she was not convinced.

Council Member Fotheringham moved to reject the appeal with a written decision to be prepared
for the record and for a signature. Council Member Petersen seconded the motion. The Council
roll call vote was as follows: Council Members Petersen, Durham, Fotheringham, Quinn, Gibbons
and Mayor Dahle in favor. The motion passed unanimously.
