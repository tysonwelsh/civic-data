     MINUTES OF WEST VALLEY CITY PLANNING COMMISSION STUDY MEETING
                              MARCH 19, 2025

THE WEST VALLEY CITY PLANNING COMMISSION MEETING MET FOR STUDY SESSION ON
WEDNESDAY, MARCH 19, 2025 AT 3:34 PM IN WEST VALLEY CITY HALL, 3600
CONSTITUTION BLVD, SUITE 220, WEST VALLEY CITY, UTAH. THE MEETING WAS CALLED
TO ORDER AND CONDUCTED BY CHAIR MATTHEW LOVATO.
THE FOLLOWING MEMBERS WERE PRESENT:
     Mathew Lovato, Chair
     Martell Winters, Vice Chair
     Darrick Porter, Commissioner
     Harold Woodruff, Commissioner
     Rob Durfee, Commissioner

PLANNING DIVISION STAFF PRESENT:
     Steve Pastorik, CD Director
     Brock Anderson, P&Z Senior Planner
     Jody Knapp, P&Z Zoning Administrator
     Steve Lehman, P&Z Planning Manager
     Julie Davis, P&Z Administrative Assistant

LEGAL DEPARTMENT PRESENT:
     Brandon Hill, Assistant City Attorney

PRESENTATIONS:

1) GPZ-3-2025 Petition by CLARK CONSTRUCTION & SERVICES, LLC requesting a General
   Plan change from Large Lot Residential (2 to 3 units/acre) and General Commercial to Low
   Density Residential (3 to 4 units/acre) and a zone change from A (Agriculture, minimum lot
   size ½ acre) to R-1-8 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 8,000 square feet).
   The property is located at 5990 West 3500 South on 1.99 acres for the zone change and 3.4
   acres for the General Plan change. Staff – Steve Lehman at 801-963-3311 or
   steve.lehman@wvc-ut.gov.

   Steve Lehman presented a proposal to build 11 single-family homes, with unfinished basements, and
   three-car garages. The concept plan includes a new road connecting to existing Timeron Drive, with
   the end of the cul-de-sac designed to potentially extend eastward in the future. The developer is
   requesting exceptions for lot frontage, garage requirements, and rear yard setbacks on some lots. A
   development agreement would outline housing standards, landscaping, and fencing requirements.
   The city is considering selling a strip of land down the middle for storm drainage, contingent on
   efforts to consolidate nearby driveways to improve intersection safety on 3500 South.

2) GPZ-2-2025 Petition by WASATCH GUARANTY DEVELOPMENT, LLC requesting a
   General Plan change from General Commercial to Mixed Use and a zone change from C-2

   acres. Staff – Steve Pastorik at 801-963-3545 or steve.pastorik@wvc-ut.gov.

   Steve Pastorik presented a proposal for redeveloping the Valley Fair Mall site. The redevelopment is
   part of a broader trend of adding residential units to mall sites to address vacant retail space and
   housing shortages. The plan involves demolishing underutilized or vacant spaces from the original
   1970’s mall structure and adding 620 apartments across four phases, along with 80,600 square feet
   of new retail space.

   The proposal aims to keep the current 3,000 parking stalls with 577 of those parking stalls being
   structured parking spaces. The sharing of parking spaces between residential and commercial users
   is still being determined. Commissioner Winters raised a concern about the potential for property
   managers charging parking fees. Steve replied that parking fees are prohibited in residential areas.

   Commissioner Winters discussed the potential benefits of revitalizing the space by introducing many
   residents but expressed concerns about potential traffic issues and sought to understand any potential
   downsides. Chair Lovato agreed that traffic could be a significant concern and suggested exploring
   alternative solutions to mitigate this issue. They also discussed the potential for increased transit
   availability and the need to balance the number of apartments in the area.

   A traffic impact study was conducted, examining 17 intersections under various scenarios.
   The Commission discussed the benefits of the frontage road (2400 West) for easy access to the site
   and the challenge of leaving the site due to one-way southbound traffic on 2400 West.
   Commissioner Winters inquired about the notification process for nearby residents more than 300
   feet outside of the mall. Attorney Brandon Hill clarified that only addresses within 300 feet of the
   proposal property need to be notified and advised sticking to the ordinance for consistency.

   Chair Lovato expressed interest in the quality of the mixed use apartment products built in West
   Valley, particularly the occupancy rates and turnover rates. He also inquired about the performance
   of similar projects developed by the same company, seeking to understand what makes a successful
   venture. Steve agreed to research this and report back.

   Chair Woodruff raised questions about water and sewer capacity, which Steve agreed to investigate.
   The Commission discussed the need for more detailed information about traffic recommendations,
   project phasing, unit sizes, and project performance. Steve confirmed that the staff report would
   include detailed development agreement suggestions, including unit sizes, amenities, and parking.

There being no further business with the West Valley City Planning Commission, the meeting was
adjourned at 4:34 PM.
