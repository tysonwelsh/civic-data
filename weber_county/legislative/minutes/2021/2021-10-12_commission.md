2. APPROVAL OF A WEBER COUNTY POLICY REGARDING PUBLIC INFRASTRUCTURE
DISTRICTS.

Chris Crockett (Attorney) provided background on state creation/approval of PIDs and Weber County’s
process to development PIDs. PIDs exist to fund infrastructure and have authority to create bonds and levy
taxes. State statute directs county legislative body to determine whether a PID will be created; county policy


to be developed to guide decisions on PIDs. Couple of months spent developing Weber County PID policy;
few examples available as starting points. Weber County team, led by Commissioner Gage Froerer, included
County Treasurer John Bond, Community Development Director Sean Wilkinson, County Comptroller Scott
Parke, and representatives from Engineering and Planning created county PID policy with feedback from two
work sessions with County Commissioners. Developers are required to present a petition and abide by
disclosure requirement to land owners within proposed district with their 100% approval. Fee associated with
petition/application to assist the county with number of items to review. Minimum of $10M proposed
infrastructure costs to qualify for PID.

Commissioner Froerer: Very pleased with end product proposed today. Protects the public and gives
developer opportunity to use PID to help develop projects in Weber County. Pleased with disclosure policy.

Commissioner Froerer moved to approve Weber County Policy regarding Public Infrastructure Districts;
Commissioner Jenkins seconded.
Commissioner Froerer — aye; Commissioner Jenkins — aye; Chair Harvey — aye

APPROVAL OF THE FIRST READING OF AN ORDINANCE OF THE COUNTY
COMMISSIONERS OF WEBER COUNTY AUTHORIZING RESTRICTIONS ON EXPRESSIVE
ACTIVITY.

Chris Crockett (Attorney) shared Bill of Rights, especially the First Amendment, makes us stand out
throughout the world; as citizens we have right to speak out without fear of punishment. Supreme Court has
allowed, through interpretation of First Amendment, ‘Time, Place, and Manner Restrictions’, which are content
neutral restrictions, on expressive activity. Utah State Legislature passed a statute Expressive Activity
Regulation by Local Government Act: if local government wants to regulate expressive activity in any way, it
has to be done through Ordinance or policy; they cannot be related to ‘viewpoint suppression’, has to be content
neutral, has to be narrowly tailored, and means are to be provided for public redress of grievances. The
proposed Ordinance is first step to approve policy outlining Time, Place, Manner Restrictions. First reading
for Ordinance, final reading next week with greater details provided.

Commissioner Jenkins moved to approve the first reading of an Ordinance of the County Commissioners of
Weber County authorizing restrictions on expressive activity; Commissioner Jenkins seconded.
Commissioner Froerer — aye; Commissioner Jenkins — aye; Chair Harvey — aye
