                                     BRIEFING SESSION —6: 00 P. M.


The briefing session began at 6: 03 p. m. when a quorum was present. Planner Terryne
Bergeson provided an overview of agenda items No. 3 and No. 4.


Regarding Agenda Item No. 3, she reminded the Commissioners that they had heard this
item previously ( on March 12, 2024) and had approved the following motion with a vote
of 5- 2: " I move that we approve File#      1S124- SI- RA= 000420- 2024 request fora reasonable
accommodation to allow the limit of the paved area in the front yard, excluding the
driveway providing access to the garage, be increased from 20% to 54. 1% for the property
located at 4729 South Turnberry Cove in Taylorsville, Utah, subject to the findings and
conditions in the staff report."


However, upon receiving the approval letter, the applicants realized that the submitted
and approved site plan had not been adjusted to show the correct curb cut; therefore, a
wider drive approach was not included in the request. The applicants had now submitted
a new application for a reasonable accommodation request, which would be presented in
the regular meeting. They were requesting an exception to the standard driveway width
to allow the existing, wider drive approach to remain.

Ms. Bergeson explained that she had mentioned to the applicants, who had not been
present at the March 12, 2024 meeting, that commissioners had several questions that
evening      which      had   gone   unanswered.   Therefore, the applicants were present that

            Bergeson - Planner I)




 IR Ms. Bergeson explained that Brad Reynolds Construction had built a home for Todd
and Rosita Jasper at 4729 S Turnberry Cove. During regular onsite inspections, city
inspectors noticed deviations from the original approved site plan relating to the width of

the drive approach and the amount of poured concrete at the property. The Jaspers
submitted a reasonable accommodation application requesting that the Planning
Commission allow an exception to certain city standards that would accommodate the
special needs of their daughter. The applicants were instructed to submit documents to
support the request, which included a site plan and a written explanation. The documents
were included in a staff report and presentation reviewed by the Planning Commission on
March 12, 2024.


This type of application did not have any requirements for public noticing or a public
hearing.

As stated in the summary, the Planning Commission approved the previous request
relating to increased      paved area ( File#   1S124- 000419- 2024). However, the request did
not include an exception to driveway width. The applicants had expressed that a wider
drive approach had been installed to accommodate the transport van for their daughter.
The request was submitted with an explanation ( Ex F) as well as a new, dimensioned site
plan that illustrated the existing distance from end to end of the approach, measured in a
straight- line was 38. 1   feet wide ( Ex H). An onsite measurement was taken, and the curve
       the approach ( at the street, flare to flare) was 40. 5. The width exceeded the
width of

maximum established in Section 13. 21. 030( A): " Driveways shall be provided for all

residential building lots. The drive approach for the driveway shall be a minimum width of
twelve feet ( 12')
                       and shall not exceed the maximum width of thirty-five feet ( 35)..."
Taylorsville Land Development Code designated the Planning Commission as the
decision- making authority for reasonable accommodation requests.

See staff report for further details regarding the analysis. Approval of the request would
not impact the number of dwelling units and had no impact on the low-density residential
designation. The current General Plan identified the need for housing that met the needs
for residents of all income levels, age groups, and abilities" in Goal 7. 3.

Chair Wilkey invited the applicant to address the Planning Commission.

Todd Jasper            explained    that his daughter   used   UTA to   get   to and from   school.   He
explained that it was best for the bus to be able to load and unload her in the driveway
itself to avoid vehicles parked on the streets, snow in the winter, trash cans on garbage
pick- up day, etc. It also meant his daughter was exposed to the elements for less time
when   loading and unloading her took place during inclement weather. Mr. Jasper
explained that he and the builder were initially unaware of the width restriction, which was
how they ended up in this situation.

Commissioner Russell asked how long he had lived in the house. He explained that they
hadn' t yet closed on the home.
