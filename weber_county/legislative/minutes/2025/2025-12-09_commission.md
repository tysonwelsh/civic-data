         Charlie Ewert (Planning): There is a property owner in the Liberty area that wants to change from A3 to A1
         zone. A3 is 3 acres minimum lot size and A1 is 40k square feet minimum or about an acre. There are half
         acre lots in a cluster development across the street and one acre lots to the east. And there is a subdivision to
         the south with 1.5 to 2.5 acre lots. The developer’s concept plan is almost a mirror image to a neighboring
         subdivision. The Ogden Valley Planning Commission recommended denial of this application.
         Commissioner Barber was not against letting the new city make a decision on this in lieu of the County. My
         original recommendation was to deny based on the increase of rights. The Commission has three options:
             1. Approve the rezone as requested
             2. Approving this would result in an upzone and increased development rights. The Ogden Valley
                  General Plan states we will do everything we can to not increase the net overall development
                  potential that existing zoning has. In this case, the upzoning would be probably be supportable if the
                  developer transfers rights to other developments. One opportunity for betterment is community
                  pathways. The county has in its long term plans to build a ten foot wide paved pathway east along
                  the power line corridor at the Wolf Creek development. It would be great if the developer took the
                  bull by the horns and worked with Rocky Mountain Power to put in the first leg of that pathway and
                  stubbed into their development. Then in the future the new city could look at using impact fees to
                  finish building the project.
             3. Deny the request and ask the applicant to work with the new city.

    5. Public Comment:
          a. Jeanie Wendell, Liberty, UT: I live on a dead end street of 16 homes and we live at the bottom of
               this proposed area. We are monitoring our wells weekly to gather data before a new 2000 foot well
               is used to see if it impacts our water supply. The developer wants to use smaller lots before we know
               whether the underground aquifer will recharge itself. I would ask that you not consider this at all.
          b. Jan Fullmer, Eden, UT: We have issues in Ogden Valley with water and sewage. Having homes
               there under three acres was not allowed by Weber-Morgan Health Department. I n the late 1990s
               they said no more homes with a well and septic system on one acre. The minimum is three acres
               with the possibility of increasing to five or six acres. While the County is looking into feasibility of
               pumping sewage out of the area, that still leaves the issue of water. Hopefully, someday down the
               road the water and sewage issues will be addressed. But please let that be planned by the new city.
          c. Earl Snider, 4100 North (south end): One of the most important staples of the Ogden Valley General
               Plan is the guidance that zoning density increases should not occur unless it substantially advances
               another provision of the plan. This principle appears repeatedly woven through the land use chapter
               rural chapter policies in the community's long view of growth and management. Further, it directs
               the existing densities towards village areas. This helps the community guide growth rather than
               allowing rural spiral into agricultural or undeveloped lands. The General Plan is explicit that
               increases in residential density outside of the village areas should occur only through TDRs. The
               rezoning from AV3 to A1 creates additional residency residential potential without increasing the
               rural character elsewhere in the valley. The proposal does not include clustering, open space
               preservation, or rural land protections recommended by the Plan. The Ogden Valley General Plan
               has a strong recommendation against increasing density unless doing so advances other plan goals in
               a substantial way. Applying this recommendation, the proposed rezone is found to be inconsistent
               with the General Plan. Based on the application of the decision criteria the section 102-5 and the
               General Plans directives for growth and density, the staff cannot make a positive finding that this
               rezone is consistent with land use policy for the Ogden Valley. That's the summary from the
               proposal prepared by your own Planning Commission. One of the things they haven't taken into
               effect yet is the new well for Nordic Valley. The exclusion zone will extend into the proposed
               changes. How many of the 21 lots will be excluded because they will be in the exclusion zone for the
               County? Water is always an issue, always will be until it's proven otherwise. So, my
               recommendation would be to deny it at this point because there are just too many unknown variables
               and it does not fit into the Ogden Valley master plan.
