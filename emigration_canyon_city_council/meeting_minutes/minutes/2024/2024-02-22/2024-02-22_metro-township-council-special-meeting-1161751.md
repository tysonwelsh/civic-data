Emigration Canyon Community Council Reorganization
Bill Tobey, Chair of the Emigration Canyon Community Council, stated that the
meeting focused on the challenges the Community Council is facing, particularly in trying to get a
complete slate of officers for the upcoming year due to a shortage of active members. There is a
possibility of reorganizing the Community Council under the Metro Township Council to help
address these challenges. Key considerations included avoiding unnecessary management
responsibilities for the Metro Council and managing potential liabilities from volunteers operating
within the Township government.

Deputy Mayor Hawkes stated the comittee would still need to adhere to rules
regarding recorded meetings, meeting minutes, and other procedural requirements, which might
differ from the standard practices of the Metro Township Council.
Zach Posner, Emigration Canyon Community Council, stated that a committee
not involved in policy decisions can meet under different requirements.
Mr. Tobey stated the Community Council would primarily handle tasks and
responsibilities, with most members performing specific jobs. Any significant decisions,
particularly those involving expenditures, would require approval from the Metro Township
Council.
Deputy Mayor Hawkes stated there could be separate committees, such as a
firewise committee or a communication committee, to handle specific areas.
Mr. Tobey stated expressed concerns about the limited number of people working
on firewise and wildfire safety initiatives, noting that these tasks are more extensive than they
initially appear. Currently, only two individuals are involved, and with one of them leaving soon,

the workload will fall to just one person. The Metro Township might be able to hire an employee
to take on a significant portion of the work through grant opportunities. Ideally, a firewise team
would consist of three or four people, with the possibility of expanding volunteer involvement in

Council Member Harris asked what the key activities are that the current
Community Council oversees.
Mr. Tobey stated there are challenges in managing communications, which
include tasks such as producing newsletters, maintaining the website, and, at one point,
developing a social media presence. Currently, the communications effort has dwindled to just
two people: one focusing on content generation and the other handling the technical aspects.
A resident, Jeff Carlston had approached the Community Council with a proposal to collaborate
on community programming, specifically focused on education and development around local
history. The Community Council responded positively to the idea. Mr. Carlston also offered to
contribute $5,000 in book profits towards these community activities.
Deputy Mayor Hawkes stated there is an outdoor recreation grant available;
however, the deadline is approaching soon. She suggested that this grant could be used to match
funds for a community program. The program could be tailored to specific needs.
Mr. Tobey stated that the conversation regarding Jeff Carlston's proposal is still in
its early stages. While the Community Council responded positively to the idea, no further
planning or discussions have taken place. This represents another potential area where
volunteers, in collaboration with the Metro Township, could contribute positively to the community.
Canyon cleanups are another important initiative that should be considered.
Fire safety has consumed the majority of their time and effort. He acknowledged that there is
significant potential for expanding communications efforts if there were more capable individuals
available. The lack of progress in communications is disappointing.
Mayor Smolka stated it was proposed that separate accounts could be created
under the supervision of the MSD to manage funds. Specifically, accounts could be established
for distinct purposes, such as one for firewise activities and another for the Historical Society.
Mr. Posner asked whether the proposed changes, which would transform the
organization into a city, would affect the creation of separate accounts for managing funds. They
mentioned not being certain if the legislation had passed but noted that it could impact the current
arrangements.
Deputy Mayor Hawkes stated any website associated with the subcommittees
must include a privacy statement detailing how information is collected, shared, and used. The
requirements include disclosing how personal information is handled and ensuring transparency
about its use, including prohibitions on selling such information.

Another issue raised was regarding insurance coverage. It was suggested that, for meetings, it
would be important to keep records of when and who attended to address any potential issues
that might arise. The speaker was uncertain about how these practices would impact insurance
rates.
Mr. Tobey stated if a subcommittee were formed under the Metro Township, the
Community Council would need to dissolve as a nonprofit corporation and surrender its status as
an independent legal entity. The subcommittee would then operate as a voluntary committee
under the Metro Township's jurisdiction.
Council Member Harris suggested reaching out to the Association of Community
Councils Together (ACCT) to get guidance on transitioning from a nonprofit organization to a
voluntary committee under the Metro Township.
Mr. Tobey stated most of the questions that have arisen so far have relatively
straightforward solutions. However, if any complex issues arise, they could be addressed with the
help of the ACCT. While cash assets might be rolled under the Metro Township, other assets
require consideration. Specifically, the absence of a communications team and the potential need
for someone to manage newsletters after his departure. They noted having a MailChimp account
that costs $30 per month and inquired whether the Metro Township would find it useful and be
interested in taking it over.
Deputy Mayor Hawkes stated she did not believe the Metro Township would need
MailChimp. Resources are also available through the Utah League of Cities and Towns, which
can provide assistance with these questions. The speaker planned to reach out to them, as they
also have access to staff who might offer additional support.
Council Member Pinon suggested exporting all contacts from the MailChimp
account to ensure that the information can be retained and used, regardless of whether the
account is transferred to the Metro Township.
Mr. Tobey stated there are useful pieces of content on the current website,
including a wildfire safety orientation article. Is there a need to maintain a separate website if the
organization is incorporated into the Metro Township. He suggested that it might be more practical
to extract and transfer valuable content from the existing site.
Deputy Mayor Hawkes asked Mr. Tobey to email her what he recommended
keeping on the website.
Mr. Posner offered assistance with the migration process if needed. Once the
majority of content has been transferred, they proposed setting up a redirect from the old website
to the Metro Township Council website to ensure that all traffic is directed appropriately.
Paul Brown, Emigration Canyon Community Council, stated the state registration
has been renewed for the year, which required a small financial fee. He saw no issue with closing
the checking account and writing a check to the Metro Township Council to settle the finances.
Finding volunteers for various tasks, such as Firewise initiatives, remains a challenge, whether
for a Community Council or the Metro Township Council. Subcommittees would need to be
