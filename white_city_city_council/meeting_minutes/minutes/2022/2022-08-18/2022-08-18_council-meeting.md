                                  Special Workshop Meeting

Moderate Income Housing Planning Requirements

              Erin O’Kelley, Planner, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD),
stated the White City Planning Commission held a public hearing for the Moderate Income
Housing Plan on July 28, 2022. The Planning Commission discussed the following strategies:

   -   Strategy (E) Create or allow for and reduce regulations related to, internal or detached
       accessory dwelling units in residential zones.
   -   Strategy (B) Demonstrate investment in the rehabilitation or expansion of infrastructure
       that facilities the construction of moderate income housing.
   -   Strategy (I) Amend land use regulations to allow for single-family room occupancy
       developments.
   -   Strategy (U) Develop a moderate income housing project for residents who are disabled
       or 55 years old or older.
   -   Strategy (W) Create or allow for, and reduce regulations relate to multi-family residential
       dwellings compatible in scale and form with detached single-family residential dwellings
       and located in walkable communities within residential or mixed-use zones.
   -   Strategy (X) Demonstrate implementation of any program or strategy to address the
       housing needs of residents of the municipality who earn less than 80% or the area median
       income, including the dedication of local funding source to moderate income housing or
       adoption of a land use ordinance that requires 10% or more of new residential
       development in a residential zone be dedicated to moderate income housing.

              Paul Ashton stated the Council has the option to adopt more than five strategies.

               Council Member Little stated the reason the Council must address the Modern
Income Housing Plan is because the legislature has determined that at least five strategies need
to be adopted for the township to be eligible for funding through various grant programs. The
Council will need to make progress on all strategies that are adopted.

                Council Member Perry stated he does not see a reason why five strategies
cannot be adopted even if part of the strategy is that the Council is going to educate itself and
research it for a year or two.

                Council Member Little stated the real difference between adopting three and five
strategies is that the township will not get priority consideration for funding if it does not have five
strategies.

               The Council reviewed the strategies and agreed upon the following:

   -   Strategy (E) Create or allow for and reduce regulations related to, internal or detached
       accessory dwelling units in residential zones.
   -   Strategy (B) Demonstrate investment in the rehabilitation or expansion of infrastructure
       that facilities the construction of moderate income housing.
   -   Strategy (O) Apply for or partner with an entity that applies for state or federal funds or tax
       incentives to promote the construction of moderate income housing, an entity that applies
       for affordable housing programs administered by the Department of Workforce Services,
       an entity that applies for affordable housing programs administered by an association of
       government established by an interlocal agreement until Title 11, Chapter 13, Interlocal
       Cooperation Act, an entity that applies services provided by a public housing authority to
       preserve and create moderate income housing, or any other entity that applies for
       programs or services that promote the construction of preservation of moderate income
       housing.
   -   Strategy (I) Amend land use regulations to allow for single-family room occupancy
       developments.
   -   Strategy (U) Develop a moderate income housing project for residents who are disabled
       or 55 years old or older.
   -   Strategy (X) Demonstrate implementation of any program or strategy to address the
       housing needs of residents of the municipality who earn less than 80% or the area median
       income, including the dedication of local funding source to moderate income housing or
       adoption of a land use ordinance that requires 10% or more of new residential
       development in a residential zone be dedicated to moderate income housing.

White City Metro Township Code Amendments

               Erin O’Kelley, Planner, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD),
presented White City Metro Townships Title 18 – Subdivisions and Title 19 – Zoning code
updates. She reviewed the following revised chapters, definitions, parks and open space zones,
agricultural zones, single-family residential zones, medium and high density residential zones,
and commercial zones. As the code continues to be revised, she will bring the draft document to
the Council for its review and feedback. After she receives the feedback, she will edit the draft to
reflect the Council’s recommendations.
