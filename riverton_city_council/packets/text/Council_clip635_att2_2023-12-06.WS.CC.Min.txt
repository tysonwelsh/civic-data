14            The Point Update
15
16   Alan Matheson, Executive Director of the Point provided an update on the development project.
17   This presentation is included in the packet.
18
19   Mayor Staggs asked what the cost was to relocate the prison. In response, Mr. Matheson said
20   the cost of relocating the prison was estimated to be around $800-900 million, and the funding
21   for the relocation is expected to be covered by revenues generated at the Point of the Mountain
22   site. The model for the disposition of property at the site involves long-term ground leases,
23   typically lasting 99 years. Developers will lease parcels, build on them, and generate revenue
24   from the leases. This approach allows the state to maintain control over the development,
25   ensures a long-term revenue stream, and provides the opportunity for taxpayers to share in the
26   increased value of the property over time.

31            Utah Legislative Auditor General’s Report No. 2023-16: “A Performance Audit of
32            Utah Housing Policy”
33
34   Mayor Staggs criticized the Legislative Auditor's report for undermining local land-use authority
35   and asked the Council to discuss. He raised concerns over the legislature's inclination to exert
36   control over municipalities' land-use decisions. He emphasized the significant progress made by
37   local governments, citing 24 legislative efforts in the past years impacting land-use authority. He
38   expressed the intent to draft a resolution outlining concerns and potential rebuttals.
39
40   Councilmember McCay expressed support for drafting a resolution in response to the
41   Legislative Auditor's report. She suggested sending the resolution to legislative members,
42   including senators and representatives in Salt Lake County and northern Utah County. She
43   emphasized the positive role of municipalities in providing zoning for housing but noted
44   challenges such as interest rates, land prices, and construction material costs beyond their
45   control.

48   negative impact of increased government involvement when decisions are distant from citizens.

50   collaborative effort involving other cities would be most effective.

26            Ordinance No. 23-16 – Adopting Section 6.05.425, Feeding Wild Birds/Fowl,
27            regulating the feeding of wild birds/fowl within Riverton City, Ordinance proposed
28            by Riverton City
29
30   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that the ordinance aims to address
31   concerns about birds congregating and creating nuisances. The proposed regulation limits the
32   feeding of wild birds to containers on private property. In the front yard, only suspended
33   containers are allowed, placed at least five feet off the ground. In the rear yard, ground-level
34   containers are permitted, but they must be at least 10 feet away from the property line. The
35   ordinance does not affect feeding ducks or birds along waterways or in parks. Residential
36   chickens and other farm animals allowed under existing ordinances are also unaffected.
37
38   Councilmember McDougal expressed concern about allowing the feeding of ducks or birds in
39   public parks or along waterways. His argument was that altering the feeding patterns of wild
40   animals in these public spaces could lead to waste issues, negatively impacting the enjoyment
41   of these areas for all residents.
42
43   Councilmember McDougal asked about the enforcement aspect of the proposed ordinance,
44   expressing concerns about how it would be implemented. In response, Mr. Lethbridge said the
45   code enforcement officer or animal control would investigate reported violations and issue
46   warnings to educate residents about the ordinance. If voluntary compliance was not achieved,
47   citations could be issued, and legal action might be pursued.
48
49   Mayor Staggs asked if wild fowl was included in any previous ordinances. Mr. Lethbridge said it
50   was not and this would be a new section in the code.
51

 6   Mayor Staggs called for public comments;
 7
 8   Sharla Lenare expressed frustration over the delay in addressing the issue of feeding ducks and
 9   wild birds in the area, noting that the Salt Lake County Health Department regulations already
10   covered many aspects of the problem. She emphasized the harmful effects of feeding wild
11   animals, including diseases, overcrowding, and aggressive behavior. She questioned why the
12   City was still working on enforcement measures three years later when the health department
13   regulations were already in place. She suggested comparing the City's existing regulations with
14   the county ordinance to ensure comprehensive coverage.
15
16   Richard Ludlow expressed concerns about the issues caused by ducks in the neighborhood,
17   emphasizing the safety risks associated with ducks running into the road and the impact on
18   wildlife behavior due to feeding. He highlighted instances of ducks being hit by cars, creating a
19   safety hazard. He explained the natural migratory patterns of wild ducks and the negative
20   consequences of feeding them, leading to the birds becoming domesticated and staying in the
21   area. He appreciated the Council's attention to the matter and Councilmember McDougal's
22   efforts in addressing the issue.
23
24   Kurk Weidauer provided additional insights into the challenges posed by the ducks in the
25   neighborhood. Describing a three-year-long issue, he emphasized the continuous pattern of
26   ducks flying over their houses, causing damage to properties, including homes, cars, and
27   outdoor furniture. He expressed gratitude for the Council's attention to the matter and hoped for
28   enforcement measures to alleviate the ongoing problems caused by the ducks.
29
30   Mayor Staggs called for additional public comment, there being none, he closed the Public
31   Hearing.
32
33   Mayor Staggs invited staff to provide comments, especially regarding concerns raised by the
34   public about Salt Lake County Health Department's regulations.
35
36   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, clarified that the motivation behind creating the ordinance was the
37   county's perceived failure to follow through with the enforcement of their own ordinance. He
38   emphasized that, until this point, the City lacked an ordinance that would empower Riverton City
39   to take enforcement actions. This gap in enforcement authority prompted the City to establish its
40   own ordinance to address the issue effectively.

45   public parks. He said the rationale behind this suggestion is to prevent public spaces from
46   being affected by individuals feeding wildlife, ensuring that all residents can enjoy these areas
47   without interference.
