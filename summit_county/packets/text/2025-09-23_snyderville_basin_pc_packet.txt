Proposal 
The Snyderville Basin Planning Commission approved a Conditional Use Permit (CUP) for the 
uses of “Bar” and “Manufacturing, Light” in Suite 105 on Parcel PCBC-3 (May 13, 2025 Minutes). 
An additional space within the same building (Suite 104) became available and the applicant is 
proposing to expand the Bar and Light Manufacturing use into the additional tenant space.  
3
2 
 
Vicinity Map 
 
 
Background 
Parcel PCBC-3 is located within the Park City Business Center Subdivision, which was recorded 
on July 18, 2007 (Entry Number 819783). The Subdivision is subject to the Park City Business 
Center Development Agreement (Ordinance Number 563). 
 
Currently, the site contains an existing building with a breezeway connecting to a second 
building. The buildings were approved in two phases. The first phase was approved in 2018 via 
a Low Impact Permit for “Office, General.” The second phase was approved in 2022 via a Low 
Impact Permit for “Office, General.” The proposed Brewery is located within phase 2 of the site.  
 
The previously approved space in Suite 105 (see May 13, 2025 Minutes) consisted of 
approximately 4,329 square feet. The new proposed space (including both Suite 104 and 105) 
consists of approximately 7,363 square feet total. The previously approved floor plans are 
attached as Exhibit B. The applicant proposes to shift the taproom into the main level of Suite 
104 and expand the brewery area to the entire main level of Suite 105.  
 
The previous approval included approximately twenty (20) seats within the Taproom. The 
expansion proposes approximately thirty-two (32) seats within the Taproom area. The 
4
3 
 
proposed plans for the main level of the additional space also include administrative/office 
area, two cold box rooms, a product storage area, and a merchandise display area. The 
proposed plans for the second level (mezzanine) of the additional space include storage, mill 
room, and grain & canning supply rooms. 
 
The proposed brewery and taproom are subject to Utah State Code Title 32B, Alcoholic 
Beverage Control Act. In Utah, the Utah Division of Alcoholic Beverage Control (DABC) regulates 
the sale and service of alcohol. The applicant will be required to obtain applicable licenses from 
Utah DABC prior to operating the brewery or taproom.

Findings of Fact: 
1. On May 13, 2025, the Snyderville Basin Planning Commission approved the original 
Conditional Use Permit (CUP) for the uses of bar and light manufacturing within Suite 
105 of an existing building on Parcel PCBC-3. 
2. On August 15, 2025, the Community Development Department received a complete 
application for an Amendment to the CUP in order to expand the Uses into Suite 104. 
3. Parcel PCBC-3 is owned by Topmark Investments. 
4. Parcel PCBC-3 contains 0.84 acres and is located in the Service Commercial (SC) Zoning 
District. 
5. Parcel PCBC-3 is located within the Park City Business Center Subdivision, which was 
recorded on July 18, 2007 (Entry Number 819783).  
6. The Subdivision is subject to the Park City Business Center Development Agreement 
(Ordinance Number 563). 
7. Currently, the site contains an existing building with a breezeway connecting to a 
second building. The buildings were approved in two phases.  
a. The first phase was approved in 2018 via a Low Impact Permit for “Office, 
General.”  
b. The second phase was approved in 2022 via a Low Impact Permit for “Office, 
General.” The proposed Brewery is located within phase 2 of the site. 
8. The applicant is proposing a Brewery and Taproom/Tasting Room to be located within 
an existing building at 4175 N Forestdale Drive.

b. Similarly, Taproom/Tasting Room is not specifically listed in 10-2-10, however, 
the CDD has determined that the proposed Use is similar in nature and logically 
fits within the description of a Bar.

approximately 4,329 square feet. The new proposed space (including both suite 104 and 
suite 105) consists of approximately 7,363 square feet total.

Proposal 
 
The applicant is requesting a Master Planned Development (MPD) for the Junction Commons, a 
19-building mixed use redevelopment project located at 6699 N Landmark Dr (Parcel FSE-1), 
Kimball Junction, Summit County, UT.  The applicant is proposing to Rezone Parcel FSE-1 from 
the Town Center Zone to the Neighborhood Mixed-Use 1 (NMU-1) Zone. 
 
The project includes the redevelopment of the upper portion of the property into a residential 
project while the lower portion of the property would be developed with additional infill 
development.  
 
The upper portion of the property is currently developed with ~98,000 sq. ft. of retail space. 
The applicant is proposing to demolish ~82,000 sq. ft. of the existing retail and replace it with a 
10-building residential project which includes 433 units (391 apartments and 42 townhomes) 
and a ~7,200 sq. ft. amenity/clubhouse space within one of the three apartment buildings. One 
of the buildings is proposed to be mixed use and would contain ~7,300 sq. ft. commercial. The 
applicant is also proposing two parking structures in the upper portion of the property. 
 
The lower portion of the property is currently developed with ~230,000 sq. ft. of retail space. 
The applicant is proposing to infill the parking area with a 9-building mixed-use project with 
ground floor commercial (~54,000 sq. ft.) and upper floor residential (77 apartments).  
 
The applications/processes involved in this project include; 
 
• MPD-Rezone to Neighborhood Mixed-Use 1 (NMU-1) Zone 
• MPD-Conditional Use Permit 
• MPD-Low Impact Permit 
• Development Agreement 
• Potential Final Subdivision/Condo Plat (for any for-sale units)

Background 
 
Junction Commons (formerly Outlets Park City, formerly Tanger Outlets, formerly the Factory 
Stores at Park City) was originally built in 1991 with 208,669 sq. ft. through a Class II Permit (a 
previous version of the development code). Based on the 1990 Class II Permit file, it does not 
26
3 
 
appear that there were community benefits, and the review was based on infrastructure and 
design criteria. 
 
In 1999 the Summit County Board of County Commissioners approved a 92,567 sq. ft. addition 
to the center through the Factory Stores Park City Specially Planned Area and Development  
Agreement provisions of the Snyderville Basin Development Code. Based on the 1998 Specially 
Planned Area and Development Agreement file, the community benefits included a partial 
Snyderville Basin Special Recreation District trail improvement (~650 feet), seating areas within 
the site, and preservation of existing parks and open space areas. 
 
In 2014, the Summit County Council approved a SPA designation for a 23,500 sq. ft. retail 
addition through the Tanger Outlets Specially Planned Area and Development Agreement. 
During the 2014 review, the Planning Commission and Summit County Council required public 
benefits as part of the Development Agreement process which resulted in a cumulative 
financial contribution to the Peace House project.  
 
According to the Summit County Assessor’s Office, the total square footage today is 322,804 sq. 
ft. The existing uses include soft-good retail (~70 tenants), management offices, and a small 
restaurant space.  
 
The 1999 Development Agreement has expired, while the 2014 agreement for the 23,500 sq. ft. 
retail addition is still in effect (2024). The 1990 Class II Permit for the original portion of the 
development does not have an underlying Development Agreement. 
 
In June of 2024, the Summit County Community Development Department received an 
application for a rezone to the NMU-1 Zone.  
 
The Planning Commission held a pre-application work session for this project on April 9, 2024. 
During the meeting the applicant went over the history of the property and introduced the 
project. During the meeting the Planning Commission provided consensus that the overall 
project vision was acceptable.  
 
The Planning Commission held pre-application work sessions on April 9, 2024 and August 27, 
2024 where they confirmed that the redevelopment and the overall massing were acceptable 
but that they wanted to confirm that the affordable housing requirements were being met.  
 
The Planning Commission also held a work session on February 11, 2025. Since the Planning 
Commission had three new members that did not have background on the project, Staff and 
the applicant requested to;

• Create a Mixed-Use Neighborhood states; Mixed-Use neighborhoods are designed

Analysis: Junction Commons was developed as an auto-dependent retail center. The 
applicant is proposing to redevelop the project as a mixed-use development with 
significant portions of retail and commercial. The applicant is proposing internal and

to the rest of the mixed-use neighborhood. COMPLIES AS CONDITIONED 
 
• Create a People Oriented Built Environment states; Establishing a more traditional

pedestrians rather than the movement of vehicles. Traditional streets are not 
dominated by large parking lots, blank building walls, service areas, and utility 
infrastructure. Creating buildings with a continuous active edge, with doors and 
windows opening onto the street, and parking located behind and/or underneath the 
buildings can help encourage comfortable pedestrian activity. The goal should be to 
accommodate vehicles in an environment built for people.

built environment. While the applicant has not provided architectural details, the

the area for pedestrians and encourage neighborhood uses rather than destination

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
The Junction Commons Project represents a transformative redevelopment of 37.3 acres in the 
Kimball Junction area of Summit County, Utah. This Master Planned Development (MPD) with 
Neighborhood Mixed-Use 1 (NMU-1) zoning demonstrates comprehensive sustainability 
through environmental stewardship, social equity, and economic viability. The project converts 
an existing single-use outlet mall and oversized parking areas into a mixed-use, pedestrian-
focused, transit-oriented community that aligns with Summit County's sustainability objectives 
and the Snyderville Basin General Plan.
PROJECT OVERVIEW 
• Location: Kimball Junction, Summit County, Utah
• Total Area: 37.3 acres
• Zoning: NMU-1 (Neighborhood Mixed-Use 1) with MPD overlay

• Commercial Space: Reduction from 318,986 SF to 301,360 SF
