25          1.1       Project GPA-21-001 – A Public Hearing to Receive Comments and Make a
26                    Possible Recommendation to the City Council on a City-Initiated Proposal to

31   Senior City Planner, Andrew Hulka overviewed the Business Meeting agenda, which included a
32   Planned Unit Development (“PUD”) for Doverhill Drive. The item was continued at the
33   December 1, 2021, Planning Commission Meeting. There was a Staff recommendation to
34   continue the item until the next Planning Commission Meeting. The applicant was making
35   progress and there would be further updates soon. Mr. Hulka noted that there would need to be a
36   motion to continue the item during the Business Meeting.
37
38   The second item on the Business Meeting agenda was related to the Mid-Valley Active
39   Transportation Plan. Mr. Hulka reported that it was introduced to the Planning Commission in
40   October 2021. There had been a lot of support for the plan overall. The recommendation from
41   the Commission at that time was to ensure that all of the Master Plans were coordinated and
42   contained the same information. Since that Planning Commission Meeting, the consultants had
43   made updates to ensure that the plan was consistent with the Parks, Trails, and Open Space Plan.
44   Thomas McMurtry from Avenue Consultants was present to share a brief presentation via Zoom.

 1   Mr. McMurtry shared a PowerPoint presentation with the Commission. He stated that the Mid-
 2   Valley Active Transportation Plan included the following cities:
 3
 4       •   Cottonwood Heights;
 5       •   Holladay;
 6       •   Midvale;
 7       •   Millcreek;
 8       •   Murray; and
 9       •   Taylorsville.
10
11   The idea behind the Mid-Valley Active Transportation Plan was to have a more coordinated effort
12   for active transportation routes, trails, and bicycle lanes. It would ensure that the Utah Department
13   of Transportation (“UDOT”), the Utah Transit Authority (“UTA”), the Wasatch Front Regional
14   Council (“WFRC”), and other regional partners are involved in the planning process.
15   Mr. McMurtry shared a list outlining those involved. He noted that Matthew Taylor was the
16   Cottonwood Heights representative who helped to coordinate and lead the plan initially. However,
17   when he left, Mr. Hulka became involved in the process with Avenue Consultants.
18
19   Mr. McMurtry reported that there had been a long process to develop a unified plan. There were
20   exercises to develop the vision and goals. Ultimately, the vision statement was as follows:
21
22       •   Working together on a connected active transportation system for all ages and abilities.
23
24   Mr. McMurtry stated that Avenue Consultants collected data from every traffic signal in the valley,
25   which allowed them to determine where there are higher concentrations of pedestrians.
26   Mr. McMurtry reported that the intent was to have that data to plan for the best facilities, including
27   bicycle lanes, trails, shared roads, signage, and striping. A bicycle tour was conducted to look at
28   the existing infrastructure and a brainstorming event also took place. There was also an online
29   map tool that allowed residents to state where they wanted to see connections and trails.
30
31   There was a process to screen and prioritize all of the information. Mr. McMurtry reported that
32   there were over 1,000 active transportation ideas across all six cities. Ultimately, a list was created,
33   and additional public outreach was conducted online. He shared some of the feedback from the
34   community involvement process. For instance, the following question was posed:
35
36       •   If the ideal walking and bicycling facilities were available, how often would you use them?
37
38   Mr. McMurtry shared the results from Cottonwood Heights residents. 46% of residents said they
39   would walk daily if the ideal facilities were available and 38% would walk a couple of times per
40   week. 27% of residents said they would bicycle daily if the ideal facilities were available and 40%
41   would bicycle a couple of times per week. There was a clear desire from residents to have those
42   facilities available for walking and bicycling. That was true throughout the Valley, but particularly
43   in Cottonwood Heights, where the percentages were higher than some of the other cities.
44
45   The final map of recommended projects was shared. Mr. McMurtry reported that there were over
46   240 total projects. The projects specific to Cottonwood Heights were also shared with the

 1   Commission. He noted that several changes were made since the last time information was shared
 2   with the Commission. For instance, a project was added on Siesta Drive to make sure there was a
 3   shoulder byway identified. Additionally, Creek Road was upgraded to add a multi-use path. A
 4   multi-use path was also added on the north side of I-215. It would not impact the UDOT corridor
 5   but would be within their right-of-way.
 6
 7   Mr. McMurtry stated that the Mid-Valley Active Transportation Plan website included the final
 8   report for Cottonwood Heights. The final report included detailed maps and all of the information
 9   collected throughout the process. Chapter 8 – Implementation and Funding included the associated
10   costs for each of the projects on the list. There was also a section related to funding because it was
11   important to provide tools to accomplish the suggested improvements. The section identified
12   possible funding sources. Mr. McMurtry added that the Governor’s Office of Planning and Budget
13   recently came out with their recommended budget to the Legislature. He noted that last year, the
14   Legislature gave $32 million for active transportation projects across the State. The Governor’s
15   budget included $46 million in the current year.
16
17   Chair Coutts asked if the Mid-Valley Active Transportation Plan would be enacted once each City
18   approved. Mr. McMurtry explained that there were individual plans for each participating city.
19   The hope was that all six cities would adopt their plan. Cottonwood Heights would not be held
20   back if other cities chose not to adopt their plan. Once the individual plan is adopted by
21   Cottonwood Heights, there would be a documented plan in place and the City could work toward
22   getting some of the projects completed.
23
24   There was discussion regarding Wasatch Boulevard. Mr. McMurtry noted that Wasatch Boulevard
25   is a difficult corridor and a lot of feedback had been received about the area. There were a lot of
26   competing interests. Some cyclists prefer to be on the roadway while others prefer a trail. As a
27   result, the plan showed both, with bicycle lanes on the roadway and a trail on the east side. The
28   trail stopped at 3900 South and the bicycle lane extended beyond 3300 South. Commissioner
29   Mills asked about the Gravel Pit area. Mr. McMurtry explained that the exact alignments were
30   not known but the idea was that at some point there would be opportunities within that area.
31
32   Chair Coutts asked for clarification on neighborhood byways. Mr. McMurtry reported that a
33   neighborhood byway shared the same space as vehicles and was ideal for low-speed areas. He
34   shared photo examples and noted that signs or stencils on the pavement could be added to let
35   residents know that bicycles are permitted. The Commission discussed Fort Union Boulevard.
36   Mr. McMurtry reported that the intent was to do a buffered bicycle lane. It could be done without
37   impacting any of the businesses, homes, or properties along the corridor. Chair Coutts wondered
38   how those plans tie into the Fort Union Area Master Plan. Mr. Hulka explained that the plan was
39   considered during the Mid-Valley Active Transportation Plan discussions. Mr. McMurtry also
40   noted that the existing Bicycle and Trails Master Plan was used as a starting point and that
41   information was incorporated into the Mid-Valley Active Transportation Plan.
42
43   Mr. Hulka stated that the Parks, Trails, and Open Space Plan was officially adopted. The Mid-
44   Valley Active Transportation Plan is a long-range plan, but once the plan is in place, it would be
45   possible to move forward and start to realize the vision. Chair Coutts wondered if additions could
46   be added to the Mid-Valley Active Transportation Plan once it is adopted. Mr. Hulka explained

38          3.2     Project GPA-21-001 Overview of Project – A Public Hearing to Receive
39                  Comments and Make a Possible Recommendation to the City Council on a
40                  City-Initiated Proposal to Adopt the Cottonwood Heights Portion of the Mid-
41                  Valley Active Transportation Plan as an Addendum to the Cottonwood
42                  Heights General Plan.
43
44   Mr. Hulka reported that the Mid-Valley Active Transportation Plan is a regional active
45   transportation plan that includes six cities, including Cottonwood Heights. It had been in progress
46   for over one year. There had been a lot of public involvement and it was introduced to the Planning


     Cottonwood Heights Planning Commission Meeting – 01/05/2022                                        6
 1   Commission for consideration in October 2021. At that time, feedback was received from the
 2   Planning Commission to incorporate the goals and vision of the existing long-range Master Plans.
 3   Staff worked with consultants to accomplish that, and several updates were made.
 4
 5   Thomas McMurtry from Avenue Consultants shared a presentation via Zoom related to the
 6   Cottonwood Heights portion of the Mid-Valley Active Transportation Plan. He identified himself
 7   as the Consultant Project Manager for the plan. The Mid-Valley Active Transportation Plan was
 8   led by Wasatch Front Regional Council (“WFRC”) and included the following cities:
 9
10      •   Cottonwood Heights;
11      •   Holladay;
12      •   Midvale;
13      •   Millcreek;
14      •   Murray; and
15      •   Taylorsville.
16
17   Mr. McMurtry reported that 1 ½ years had been spent working on the plan. He shared a slide, that
18   outlined City Staff, Steering Committee Members, Key Collaborators, the Project Team, and the
19   Consulting Team. 51 individuals were listed as Key Collaborators, which allowed for a broad
20   mixture of input from interested parties. Mr. McMurtry stated that a unified vision across
21   organizations had been established. Ultimately, the vision statement was as follows:
22
23      •   Working together on a connected active transportation system for all ages and abilities.
24
25   Four individual goals were established, which included the following:
26
27      •   Prioritize safe routes for all users;
28      •   Complete a connected backbone network;
29      •   Collaborate for public and multi-city commitment; and
30      •   Improve access to key origins and destinations.
31
32   Mr. McMurtry shared a map that outlined the existing active transportation conditions in
33   Cottonwood Heights. He noted that there are 28 miles of existing active transportation facilities
34   within the City boundary, which he believed was the most of the six cities involved in the plan.
35   The planning process also looked at safety improvements. There were quite a few bicyclists and
36   pedestrian fatalities, but none were within Cottonwood Heights. Mr. McMurtry explained that the
37   existing facilities had been examined. He noted that it was also important to consider the different
38   types of facilities that would best suit Cottonwood Heights in the future.
39
40   The Mid-Valley Active Transportation Plan website was discussed. The Cottonwood Heights tab
41   at the top of the webpage included the final report for Cottonwood Heights as well as the appendix.
42   Mr. McMurtry reported that the appendix included all of the public comments as well as a copy of
43   the Bikeway Selection Guide. The latter was used during the planning process.
44
45   Mr. McMurtry stated that several brainstorming sessions were conducted to determine possible
46   projects. There were also over 650 online participants who submitted comments and suggestions


     Cottonwood Heights Planning Commission Meeting – 01/05/2022                                       7
 1   related to active transportation connections. All of the comments and suggestions were evaluated
 2   based on criteria that were developed and based on the vision and goals. There was a detailed GIS
 3   exercise to rank each idea to create a complete network. Mr. McMurtry reported that there had
 4   been a lot of feedback from the public and over 900 completed online surveys. All of the survey
 5   results from Cottonwood Heights were available to view on the website.
 6
 7   Approximately 240 projects across all six cities were developed. Each project was individually
 8   numbered and cataloged. Mr. McMurtry explained that they considered how the projects cross
 9   municipal boundaries and connect to a broader system. For example, Project One was 3900 South
10   through Millcreek, which then bent down to 4100 South through Taylorsville and then went into
11   West Valley City. Even though that project went through several city boundaries, it had one
12   project number. The projects specifically related to Cottonwood Heights were shared.
13
14   Mr. McMurtry reported that the final project list had an accompanying table in the final report. It
15   included details of the recommended projects as well as the accompanying project costs. He
16   explained that the idea behind the Mid-Valley Active Transportation Plan was not to complete the
17   suggested projects quickly. This was a long-range plan, and it would take decades to accomplish.
18   As a result, the list needed to be as comprehensive as possible.
19
20   Several changes were made to the project list since the last time information had been shared with
21   the Commission. For instance, a project was added on Siesta Drive to make sure there was a
22   shoulder byway identified. Creek Road was upgraded to add a multi-use path and a multi-use path
23   had also been added on the north side of I-215. It would not impact the UDOT corridor, however,
24   Mr. McMurtry noted that it would be within their right-of-way. There were some sample drawings
25   related to facility types included in the final report. Mr. McMurtry discussed Fort Union Boulevard
26   and noted that the report contained additional information related to the west side.
