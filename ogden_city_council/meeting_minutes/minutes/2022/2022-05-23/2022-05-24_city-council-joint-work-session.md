Community Renewable Energy Program (CREP) Survey Results
Dr. Katharine French-Fuller, Director, and Jennifer Gnagey, Community Research Fellow, in representation of Weber
State’s Center for Community Engaged Learning, presented the results of the Community Renewable Energy Program (CREP)
survey conducted in February and March of 2022. The purpose of the survey was to determine what residents and business owners
in Ogden are willing to pay to for electricity from renewable sources and to determine whether residents and business owners in
Ogden think Ogden City should join the CREP. Throughout the presentation, Ms. French-Fuller and Ms. Gnagey engaged in
discussion with the Council and Council staff regarding highlights of the report, including: survey response rate; willingness to
pay (WTP) rates among varied demographic groups; likelihood of certain residents to opt out of the CREP; factors contributing to
CREP decisions; the qualifications for a business to participate in the survey; and takeaways from the survey. The following
conclusions can be drawn from the survey:
e A majority of Ogden residents are willing to pay 9.3 percent more for renewable energy.
e Close to an equal amount of residents think the City should join CREP (45 percent) or are unsure about the City
joining CREP (46 percent).
e If energy cost increase by 10 percent, about 20 percent of residents are likely to opt out.
o Many residents are not sure if they would opt out if costs exceeded their willingness to pay.
e A large majority (70 percent+) of residents think businesses, residents and local governments should be doing
more to seek out renewable energy.
e A majority of Ogden businesses are willing to pay 6.9 percent more for renewable energy.
A majority of businesses (61.1 percent) think Ogden City should join CREP.

Closing discussion among the Council and Council staff centered on the timeline for continuing discussion and/or taking

City Fiscal Year 2021-2022 Budget Amendments

Storm Sewer Utility Grants: Comptroller Stout explained Administration is proposing an amendment to the FY22
Budget to recognize and appropriate grant funds from the Dumke Foundation and the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources to the
Storm Sewer Master Plan Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) Project SU078 — Weber and Ogden River Restoration. The proposed
budget amendment also transfers remaining funds from SU079 Weber River Restoration to SU078 Ogden and Weber River
Restoration Project.

Chair Nadolski indicated that he will likely need to recuse himself from discussing and acting on the grant from the Utah
Division of Wildlife Resources. Council member Choberka added she will investigate whether she also needs to recuse herself
given her relationship with the Dumke Foundation.

Redevelopment Agency Fiscal Year 2021-2022 Budget Amendment

Swift Environmental Expenses: Comptroller Stout noted that there is a City and Redevelopment Agency (RDA) budget
amendment for this project; in the City budget, Administration is proposing an amendment to the FY22 budget to recognize and
appropriate funds from BDO Retained Earnings for transfer to the Redevelopment Agency to cover additional environmental
remediation costs at the Swift Building. The discovery of a leaking underground storage tank that has the potential to contaminate
the river requires unanticipated site work. The cost of the Environmental Protection Agency remediation efforts is not included in
this budget amendment.

There was brief discussion among the Council and Community and Economic Development Director Cooper regarding
development plans for the Swift site and the City’s ability to recoup EPA remediation costs from parties responsible for the
contamination.

Fiscal Year 2022-2023 City Budget - Departmental Budget Review
Police: Police Chief Young used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the budget proposal for his
Department including administration, investigations, support services, uniform police and the Weber Morgan Narcotics Strike
Force. He identified notable proposed changes to the Department’s budget for Fiscal Year (FY) 2022-2023:
e Eight percent increase to employee compensation;
e Employee step increases; and
e A budget reduction in small tools and equipment to help fund additional positions.
There are currently no capital improvement projects proposed for the Police Department in FY2022 and the proposed
Weber Morgan Narcotics Strike Force budget does not have any changes when compared to the FY2021 adopted budget.
There was discussion among the Council and Chief Young regarding the impact that past wage/benefit increases have
had on recruitment and retention efforts of the Department as well as ongoing market trends relative to police officers; decreased
crime rates in the community; and partnership with various community groups in an effort to impact at-risk youth in the
community.
Management Services — Information Technology (IT) Internal Service Fund, Fleet and Facilities Internal Service
Fun, and Capital Improvement Projects: Management Services Director Brown used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
summarize the budget proposal for her Department including Management Services General Fund, Fleet and Facilities,
Information Technologies, Risk Management Internal Service Fund, and non-departmental general funds associated with
Management Services. She identified notable proposed changes to the Department’s budget for Fiscal Year (FY) 2022-2023:
e Organization structure/staffing changes;
e Employee compensation increases; and
e Cost savings when compared to FY2021 adopted budget.
There are two Management Services Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) projects included in the FY23 budget:
e FLO007~— Facilities Improvements, General Fund. This project includes Public Works building roof replacement,
Public Works building fire alarm panel, Municipal Building rehabilitation, and generator replacement funding.
The total cost for this project in year one is $850,000.
e FL039— Safety improvements for City Facilities, Business Depot Ogden (BDO) lease revenue. This project
includes ballistic improvements for the Public Works lobby and the second floor of the Municipal Building.
There was discussion among the Council and Ms. Brown regarding the multiple services provided by her Department to
all other Departments of the City; strategic planning efforts, support for diversity affairs events/programs, major projects and
successes in the Department.

Proposed Salary Schedules: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith explained the proposed salary schedules shift
current ranges to accommodate the proposed cost of living adjustments (COLAs) for Sworn Fire (14 percent), General Employees
and Officers (13 percent) and Sworn Police (8 percent). The total cost of the wage and benefit changes for all funds, including
proposed new positions, is $13,874,975. The proposals are based upon the results of the Evergreen Compensation Study, which
have been presented to the Council. To implement proposed new salaries, four salary schedules are proposed: Non-Merit Special
Employees, Merit Employees, Classified Employees within the Police Department, and Classified Employees within the Fire
Department. Additionally, Administration has proposed a 15.26 percent increase for all elected officials; this includes the two
percent provided to all general employees in April 2022 plus an additional 13 percent as proposed in FY2023.

Vice Chair Lopez inquired as to the Mayor’s proposed salary, to which Ms. Eller-Smith reported $154,178. Vice Chair
Lopez stated that he is not in favor of an increase for elected officials, especially for Council members. He would like for action
on the elected officials salary schedule to be separated from the salary schedules for all other employees as he would like to vote
in opposition to the Council’s salary increases but does not want to vote no on increases for other employees. This led to
discussion among the Council regarding options available to them for considering action on the various salary schedules; they
concluded to take separate actions on the salaries for elected officials and all other City employees and directed Council staff to
consider adjustments to the Council’s total compensation that are reflective of the recommendations made in the Evergreen study.

Council Business

Marshall White Facility — Next Steps: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith reported Council staff has received the
conceptual drawings for the Marshall White Center (MWC) project and the Mayor’s proposed budget includes a $2 million
Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) Project for the design of the facility. The total estimated budget for the project is $23 million.
Council staff needs direction from the Council regarding their desired next steps for keeping the project moving forward. The
Council and City Administration discussed opportunities for adjusting the design recommendations from the architect and the
need to dialogue with the public regarding certain components of the project. There was agreement upon the need to proceed as
quickly as possible to take advantage of current funding sources that will not be available in the future and to avoid continued
inflation of costs for this type of project.
