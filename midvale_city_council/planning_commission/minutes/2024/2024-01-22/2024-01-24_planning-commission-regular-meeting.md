PUBLIC HEARINGS

1.   Midvale Bingham Junction Station Area Plan.

      In 2022 the Utah State Legislature passed HB 462 which, among other things, requires
      municipalities with fixed rail guideways (commuter and light rail) to prepare and adopt
      station area plans (SAP) covering a half mile radius of each fixed rail station. SAP’s may
      be unique to their jurisdiction and setting; however, key aspects are to include strategies
      to increase the availability of affordable housing, promotion of sustainable environmental
      conditions, enhancements of access to employment opportunities and increase of
      transportation choices and connections.

      Midvale has three stations within its boundaries: Midvale Ft. Union, Midvale Center, and
      Bingham Junction. The Midvale Ft. Union and Midvale Center Station Area Plan was
      adopted by the City Council on January 16, 2024. The Bingham Junction Station Area
      Plan is ready for recommendation and adoption.

      Design Workshop, the consultant group commissioned to prepare the Bingham Junction
      SAP will lead the discussion and presentation at the January 24th Planning Commission
      meeting. They will highlight recommendations for the plan and proposed implementation
      steps.

      Staff recommends approval.

APPLICANT:

Design Workshop, hired by WFRC, introduce the project and the purpose for the combined city
proposal with West Jordan. The project goals are also discussed at this time. Please see attached
presentation provided by consultant.

The project was initiated a little over a year ago. The studies began and the design concepts were
created. Community Engagement was a huge part of this project, including surveys and Council
Workshop meetings. In July, a preferred concept was selected, and this is now the final review.

Maximizing the use of the UTA property is key for this plan to function properly and for
development to thrive. There is also another privately owned lot, BMC, which can be
incorporated into the plan. Contracts between UTA and private companies will be required to
make the necessary transportation connections.

Open space is also a requirement of the new plan. Revitalizing the old park area into an activated
space is a big goal for this project.

Affordable Housing requirements mandated by the state will play a large factor in this project.
The city is looking into the HTRZ funding program to initiate this development in these transit-
oriented areas.

Implementation is an important part of this project. Code Amendments, new (TIF) district
funding resources, developer relationship formation, contractual agreements between parties
involved (i.e., UTA, BCM, Midvale City) will all be required to move forward with fewer
setbacks.

Policy changes, suggestions regarding density, height of buildings, type of buildings, and
setbacks are all listed on the consultant’s presentation (attached).

Implementation & Policy Recommendations
Implementation & Policy Recommendations
The proposed changes to current zoning in the Bingham Junction Station Area include a small area around
the station and therefore we recommend amending the current Junction at Midvale overlay (JM) and
creating one or two new subareas within the existing overlay to accommodate the new mixed-use and
office land
uses.

As part of the proposed office land use east of the TRAX station lies outside of the existing overlay, we
recommend amending the JMO overlay boundary to include the new proposed office land use.

For the new mixed-use area proposed between the new office use and 7000 S, a new overlay could be
created to avoid amending existing zoning districts. This area is currently zoned as Clean Industrial (CI)
and Main Street Form-Based Code (MS-FBC).
