I.    Welcome and Call to Order – Mayor Dahle.
Mayor Dahle called the meeting to order at approximately 5:30 p.m.

II.     Lighting and Art Gallery Update.
Sheryl Gillilan, Art Council Dir., reported that all of the lighting tracks are up and the light fixtures
continued to be installed. The goal was to turn the area into a gallery to better accommodate
visitors. The gallery name had not yet been selected.

III.    Plaza Discussion.
        a.      Public Art.
Ms. Gillilan was researching ways to bring public art to the City of Holladay. She reported that the
cost will range from $150,000 to $200,000. $175,000 will be given to the artists and $25,000 will
be retained in contingency. She described the process for selecting an artist and stated that the
timeline will take until the fall of 2021. There was discussion about the location and media of the
art.

Council Member Fotheringham expressed a desire to attract people to the art. Council Member
Petersen sent an email stating that she does not support removing the plaza stage. Council Member
Gibbons commented on the iconic nature of Mt. Olympus in Holladay. Council Member Durham
recommended asking the community for descriptors.

The City Council determined that the art will be large scale; fit in the project site; stand out in the
urban setting; be iconic; reflect the City of Holladay’s landscape and history; appeal to people of all
ages; encourage civic pride; reinforce the plaza site as a gathering place for residents, visitors, and
customers; and be safe, durable, and easily banked in the public setting. The site will serve as the
staging area and the selected artist will leave the stage in its current state. The proposed subject
matter will be the artist’s interpretation of trees and creeks with no water feature.

                b.       Furniture.
It was acknowledged that there were numerous questions about the furniture. It was determined to
be possible to use a theme method to select the furniture. Possible options included contemporary
style furniture; a traditional/contemporary mix using mixed metal, wood, and stone or wrought iron
that is removable.

An idea was presented involving the installation of bollards around the Plaza that could double as
bicycle parking. Additional bicycle racks will also be installed. Mayor Dahle suggested eight to
nine bollards be installed. The City’s logo with an “H” would also be used. There was discussion
about attaching chains across the bollards with reflective signs to warn drivers not to enter.

        c.     Skateboarding on the Plaza
Assistant to the City Manager, Holly Smith, reported that she and City Manager, Gina Chamness
visited the Plaza to identify areas for signage prohibiting skateboarding. Three signage location
options were presented to the Council. Mayor Dahle suggested installing two different signs.

It was reported that approximately $35,000 was budgeted for landscaping that could be used for
sculpture gardens or skate plazas for skateboarders as well as visitors. The cost of the proposed
designs varied greatly. Mayor Dahle suggested the budgeted funds be used to prepare design
concept plans.

        d.    Ongoing Maintenance
Mayor Dahle reported that he spoke to Ms. Chamness about finances for the maintenance of the
Plaza space. It was determined that there are planter boxes and copper kitchen planters in need of
maintenance. It was recommended that funds be spent to improve the Plaza before the summer
season.

IV.    Update on Sustainability Efforts – Samantha DeSeelhorst, Sustainability Analyst.
Sustainability Analyst, Samantha DeSeelhorst, described the various projects she was working on.
They included the following:

   •   A document involving HB-411 was being prepared in coordination with other communities.
       A rough draft was to be presented to the Council in June. Mayor Dahle stated that the
       matter will be discussed further at the March 31 Council Retreat.

   •   With regard to the Sustainability Action Plan, Ms. DeSeelhorst provided updates on Electric
       Vehicle chargers in the Village near Burton Lumber.

   •   An effort was underway to ban plastic bags and involved studying data and researching the
       concept. Council Member Gibbons was very interested in the issue and was in
       communication with other jurisdictions regarding their programs. Mayor Dahle mentioned
       that the issue was discussed at the recent League of Mayors Meeting and needs to be a
       County-wide effort.

V.     Holladay @20 Update.
An update was provided on revenue options being developed. It was noted that the revenue
problems are threefold and include stormwater compliance, road quantity/quality, and general City

VI.    Discussion on Terrace at City Hall.
Mayor Dahle commented that when City Hall was completed, there was a proposal to install a
Terrace. The City budgeted $300,000 for the project that has not yet been spent. The comment was
made that now may not be an optimal time to spend money on City Hall as there is insufficient
funding for City roads. It was recommended that the funds not be spent this budget year. Ms.
Smith commented that City Hall has ADA accessibility issues that also need to be addressed. The
matter was to be addressed further as part of the budget discussions.

VII. Discussion on Holladay Boulevard Speed.
A meeting was held recently with Olympus High School officials regarding speeding. City
representatives asked what the City can do to help. The school officials indicated that it would be
helpful for the City to install more lighting and programmable 20 MPH school zone flashers where
students cross the street. The Principal would be submitting written requests to the City for
consideration. Council Member Gibbons stressed the importance of providing the necessary
funding to ensure school safety.

Mayor Dahle reported that there is a 35 MPH speed sign between City Hall and 3900 South where a
resident has complained about speeding. The current speed limit on Holladay Boulevard is 40
MPH. Council Member Gibbons indicated that he held two Mini Town Halls Meetings where he
addressed traffic concerns with residents. He suggested the speed limit be reduced in certain areas.
It was noted that the Council was unanimously in favor of and has precedent to reduce the speed
limit. The City Council’s recommendation was to be forwarded to the City Manager.
