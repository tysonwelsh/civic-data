27   WORK SESSION – 6:30 PM
28
29   Call to Order
30
31   Mayor Staggs called the Work Session to order at 6:30 PM and conducted a roll call.
32   Councilmembers Buroker, McCay, and McDougal were present. Councilmember Pierucci joined
33   via Zoom and Councilmember Haymond was excused.
34
35   Discussion Items
36
37          Project List Review
38
39   Kevin Hicks, City Manager, reviewed ongoing projects and provided brief updates. He reported that
40   the development adjacent to the skate park remained under review and that the developer was
41   currently focused on other projects. An update was provided on the Welby Canal Trail easement,
42   which was reported as substantially complete, with remaining pedestrian signal work expected to be
43   bid in the near future. He noted that additional trail connections would be coordinated with the
44   planned UDOT bridge project. He also provided updates on the 10-acre park design process, the
45   proposed pump station near 11800 South intended to improve water pressure, progress related to
46   the Redwood Road RDA tax increment financing effort, and the planned expansion of secondary
47   water storage at the 4200 West equalization pond.
48
49   Mayor Staggs provided an update regarding the skate facility, noting that the option to remove the
50   existing facility and begin with a clean slate had been contemplated. It was explained that a
51   subcommittee could be established to evaluate potential new locations and funding sources for a
     Page | 2 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1   future skate facility, and that additional discussion would occur at a later time. He reported that
 2   Land Reserve had proposed a concept for the regional park that had been previously shared with
 3   the Council, and that follow-up meetings were anticipated. He discussed progress related to
 4   Dutchman Lane, explaining that the proposed connection between Dutchman Lane and Old Liberty
 5   Way could potentially improve ingress and egress near the 12600 South area. He reported that
 6   design work was being completed in-house and that a construction timeline was not yet available.
 7
 8   Mayor Staggs provided additional updates regarding Community Development Block Grant (CDBG)
 9   funding, the Salt Lake County golf course and pool facilities, and ongoing coordination with Salt
10   Lake County related to potential improvements funded through the ZAP tax bond. He also noted
11   that the City continued to own approximately 1.5 acres of surplus property on the west side of the
12   City, which may be considered for a future request for proposals pending Council direction.
13
14   Finally, Mayor Staggs reported on progress related to a sidewalk project, noting that eminent
15   domain had been authorized at the previous meeting and that steps were underway to serve the
16   affected property owner.
17
18   Councilmember McCay requested an update on the proposed sidewalk along 2700 West near
19   Bangerter. Mr. Hicks responded that the project had been inadvertently omitted from the project
20   list, and would be added back. Councilmember McCay also asked about bike lanes along 13400
21   South near 3200 West and the lack of a pedestrian crossing between 2700 West and 3600 West,
22   noting several near accidents involving children. Cary Necaise, Public Works Director, stated that a
23   formal crossing was anticipated as part of a future canal trail project associated with the Utah Trail
24   Network.
25
26   Councilmember Pierucci asked for an update on the equalization pond design, specifically
27   regarding prior discussions about balancing water storage capacity with public amenities such as
28   trails or fishing access. Mr. Necaise responded that final design concepts had not yet been selected
29   due to additional geotechnical work required to evaluate options, including consolidating the pond
30   into a single larger basin or maintaining two ponds with added beautification.
31
32   Councilmember Pierucci further requested that previously discussed neighborhood traffic-calming
33   measures, including the reinstallation of speed bumps, be added to the citywide project list, noting a
34   specific location on Shaggy Peak Drive and acknowledging other locations previously identified by
35   Council. Mr. Hicks indicated the item was already planned for an upcoming work session and
36   agreed to add it to the tracking list. He also inquired about the status of traffic signal delay
37   coordination with Salt Lake County. Mr. Necaise reported that a pilot study was underway at the
38   intersection of 3600 West and 12600 South, which would be monitored for several months, with
39   results to be reported back to the Council upon completion.
40
41   Mr. Necaise provided an update on the Jordan Basin Improvement District project along 13400
42   South. It was reported that Newman plans to complete all pipe installation by approximately mid-
43   January, with trench repair and final paving to occur later when weather conditions permit. Roads
44   are expected to be fully open during the Christmas and New Year holidays.
45
46   Adjournment of Work Session
47
48   Councilmember McDougal MOVED to adjourn the Work Session. Councilmember Buroker
49   SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for a vote; the vote was as follows: Buroker-Yes,
50   Haymond-Yes, McCay-Yes, McDougal-Yes, and Pierucci-Yes. The motion passed unanimously.
51   The Work Session adjourned at 6:47 PM.

 3   Call to Order
 4
 5   Mayor Staggs called the Riverton City Council Meeting to order at 7:01 PM and conducted a roll
 6   call. Councilmembers Buroker, McCay, and McDougal were present. Councilmember Pierucci
 7   joined via Zoom and Councilmember Haymond was excused.
 8
 9   Prayer / Pledge of Allegiance
10
11   Josh Lee offered the invocation and Chief Taylor led the Pledge of Allegiance.
12
13   Citizen Comment
14
15   Mayor Staggs called for public comments;
16
17   Jason Richman spoke in opposition to closing the skate park, citing personal experience as a
18   former user and as a parent, and expressed concern that the public had not been adequately
19   informed of documented safety issues. He stated that no significant injuries had been observed
20   during the park’s 20-year history, questioned the conclusions and cost estimates in the safety
21   report, emphasized the park’s value as a positive youth amenity, supported additional skate
22   facilities if funded, and offered to volunteer professional services to repair the existing park at no
23   cost, urging the City to retain the facility in its current location.
24
25   Ida Allen spoke in support of retaining the skate park, noting its visual compatibility with the
26   surrounding park and its importance as one of the limited recreational options for children in the
27   area. She expressed concern that development and growth were occurring too quickly without
28   sufficient consideration of Riverton’s identity or youth amenities.
29
30   Hannah Richman stated support for keeping the skate park.
31
32   Mayor Staggs called for additional public comments; there being none, he closed the Citizen
33   Comment period.
34
35
36   Mayor and Council Reports
37
38   Councilmember Pierucci explained he was unable to attend the meeting in person because he was
39   representing Riverton City at the Colorado River Water Users Association conference in Las Vegas
40   in his role on the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District Board of Trustees. He noted that a
41   significant portion of the City’s water supply originates from the Colorado River and that the
42   conference was timely due to ongoing high-stakes negotiations regarding the future of the river and
43   an approaching deadline to renegotiate.

41            Public Works Operational Budget Discussion and Resolution No. 25-62 – Approving
42            the Removal of the Riverton City Skate Facility
43
44   Councilmember McDougal MOVED that the City Council approve Resolution No. 25-62 –
45   Removal of the Riverton City Skate Facility. Councilmember Pierucci SECONDED the motion.
46   Mayor Staggs called for discussion on the motion. Councilmember McCay stated concerns about
47   approving a budget-related action without clear dollar amounts, noting that the resolution did not
48   include specific costs. She emphasized the importance of allowing newly elected
49   Councilmembers, who had engaged extensively with residents, to weigh in on the issue. She
50   reported personally visiting the skate park, observing cracks and maintenance needs that she
51   believed could be repaired, and suggested that the matter be deferred until more concrete cost
     Page | 5 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1   estimates were available. She recommended tabling or denying the item and allowing the next
 2   Council to make a decision with more complete information.

 5   McDougal-yes, and Pierucci-yes. The motion ended in a tie, 2 to 2.
