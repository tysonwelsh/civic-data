Presentations

       Wasatch Front Regional Council

Ted Knowlton, Wasatch Front Regional Council (WFRC), presented an overview of its role as a
regional governmental organization responsible for long-term transportation planning and funding
across multiple counties and cities, including Riverton. WFRC is currently developing a 30-year
Regional Transportation Plan through 2055, which serves as the foundation for future roads, transit,
and trails projects. For Riverton specifically, three key transit concepts were highlighted: extending
the TRAX Red Line toward Mountain View Village and eventually along Bangerter Highway to
connect with FrontRunner and the Blue Line; implementing Bus Rapid Transit along Redwood
Road; and enhancing bus service along 12600 South. WFRC emphasized that implementation
depends on ridership demand, supportive land use, and available funding, and encouraged
Riverton to incorporate these concepts into its own transportation master planning to strengthen
future funding opportunities and long-term coordination.

Mr. Knowlton also presented an overview of the Utah Trail Network and the Beehive Bikeway
system, which together form a statewide and regional vision for connecting communities through
trails for recreation and transportation. These networks integrate existing trails like the Jordan River

Discussion Items

         Budget Kickoff Discussion

Nick Geer, Administrative Services Director, presented an overview of the budget process,
emphasizing a more flexible and collaborative approach than in past years. He outlined the required
timeline, tentative budget by May and final adoption by June, but noted the goal is to complete the
budget earlier and improve transparency through better communication. He explained that Council
will review revenues, operations, debt, and capital projects in stages, with departments directly
presenting their five-year needs during work sessions in late summer and fall. The intent is to give
Council clearer insight into priorities, improve long-range planning, and adopt a more conservative,
data-driven budgeting philosophy that avoids committing to projects before revenues are secure.

Mr. Geer highlighted sales tax as Riverton’s primary revenue source, making up about 43% of total
tax revenue and funding most general operations, with police and fire property taxes together
accounting for most of the rest. Sales tax is economically sensitive and reported with a two-to-three-
month delay, making forecasting challenging. While current year collections are slightly below last
year, large months like November and December are still pending. There was discussion among
Councilmembers and staff about the importance of diversifying the tax base, budgeting
conservatively, prioritizing projects based on confirmed cash flow, and preparing for potential
legislative changes affecting both sales and property taxes.


         Timberline Development Discussion

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, presented a proposed development along 13400
South and Redwood Road, where a developer is seeking to rezone the currently commercial
property to higher-density residential. Key concerns raised by the Planning Commission and public
focused on road connectivity, as two older neighborhood roads currently stub into the site without
modern curb, gutter, or sidewalks. The original plan proposed full roadway connections, but this
raised issues about traffic, parking spillover, and infrastructure impacts on long-established
neighborhoods. In response, a revised concept was shared that would close those road
connections, provide only pedestrian and utility links, and instead create a new primary access
point from Redwood Road, while still preserving future public access for adjacent undeveloped
land.

The Council generally expressed support for this revised road layout, noting it better protects
existing neighborhoods while still allowing connectivity through pedestrian paths. They emphasized
the importance of minimizing disruption to 40-year-old communities, reducing traffic conflicts on
Redwood Road, and keeping maintenance costs low by favoring simple concrete pathways over
landscaped strips.

Representatives from Fieldstone, the developer, confirmed the project would be an HOA community
due to the private road, with the HOA responsible for maintenance and snow removal, and
emphasized their desire for Council guidance on closing the two road connections so they can
proceed with the revised concept. It was noted that the public has not yet seen the new road-

         Sentinel Ridge Median

Mr. Lethbridge explained that increasing development along Sentinel Ridge Boulevard has created
significant traffic and safety issues, leading engineers to require a center median between 13400
South and 13800 South to restrict left-turn movements and reduce conflicts. The median design is
not optional and is based on traffic studies, with the goal of improving safety while still allowing
development to proceed. The median will prevent certain direct turns, meaning residents may need
to travel up to half a mile to reroute. Emergency services have reviewed the design and confirmed
it will not impede response times.

Council discussion focused on how the median should be treated aesthetically and practically. They
strongly opposed landscaped medians due to long-term maintenance and water costs, favoring
concrete or low-maintenance designs instead. While some supported incorporating public art or
decorative concrete, possibly tied to Mountain Ridge High School, to give the area identity, there
was broad agreement that any design must minimize future costs and disruptions. Council
emphasized the importance of installing the median quickly so new residents understand the traffic
patterns from the start.

         City Council Meeting Agenda Review

Kevin Hicks, City Manager, explained that work sessions will be heavily focused on budget
discussions over the next few months, with enough topics and feedback to support two extended
sessions per month through at least June. Agendas will be flexible and “flooded” with items to allow
in-depth dialogue with staff and department heads, including a standing item for open questions if
time allows, so discussions don’t feel rushed and Council can stay informed and transparent.

backfilling that same day. An estimated 350,000 gallons of water, just over one acre-foot, were lost,

authority to act; however, most of the signage in question has been on private property and outside
City control. Residents were encouraged to continue reporting concerns so staff can verify each

         Resolution No. 26-07 – Approving an Amendment to the Riverton City Personnel
         Policies and Procedures Manual

Mr. Geer presented proposed updates to the City’s personnel policies and procedures manual. The
first change involved the observance of Juneteenth, recommending that the City observe the
holiday on the actual date of June 19, rather than adjusting to a nearby Monday, to align with
normal holiday closures and reduce public confusion. The second change pertained to the
catastrophic leave program, proposing that employees be allowed to voluntarily donate sick leave in
addition to vacation and comp time to support coworkers experiencing catastrophic events. He
noted that the program is used infrequently but is intended to provide assistance when needed.
