Planning Update
          Planning Manager Montgomery provided the Council with an update regarding Planning activities in the City; he first
reviewed the current staffmg levels of the Planning Division, which has experienced a great deal of turnover recently. This has
made it difficult to accomplish some of the Division's goals. He then reported on efforts to codify the Make Ogden Plan and
revising the Central Business District (CBD) ordinance; he has selected a consultant to aid in those efforts and hopes to be done
with that project by the summer of2022. A consultant will also be hired in March of2022 to oversee the rewrite of the entire
zoning code. He briefly touched on a project to evaluate parking trends in 0 gden' s downtown; a consultant has been studying the
issue and they have updated their results three different times, the fmal time being in late 2020 and the results were presented to
the Council in December 2020. The recommendations from that consultant will inform some of the zone code amendments. He
then discussed grants that the Planning Division has received that can aid in various planning projects, such as creation of a
historic district. Finally, he noted that the matter that has consuming a great deal of the Planning Division's time over the past
several months is residential development, and more particularly, multi-family housing projects throughout the City. He presented
a map illustrating the areas in which multi-family development is allowed and indicated there are three zoning designations in the
City in which the majority of multi-family housing is being built. He presented a report of residential projects by status and project
type. In conclusion, Mr. Montgomery engaged in discussion with the Council regarding the timeline for completing updates to
various community plans. The Council thanked Mr. Montgomery and his staff for the great deal of work they do for the City and
for the information provided this evening. Council Executive Director Eller-Smith added that there is a joint work session with the
Planning Commission scheduled in the near future and many of the topics reported on during this update can be discussed in
greater depth in that meeting.

Proposed Rezone at 1698 Kiesel Avenue
          The owner of 1698 Kiesel A venue is proposing to rezone his property from CP-2 (Community Commercial) to R-1-6
(Single Family Residential). The prope1ty used to have a house on it, but it was demolished, and the property was rezoned to CP-2
to allow for a small parking lot to be built. The property owner would like to remove the parking lot and build a residence on the
property.
          Planning Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the proposed rezone
application; he presented a map to orientthe Council to the location of the subject property and briefly discussed the history of the
property. The property fronts Kiesel Avenue, which has a single-family character. It is also a local street, which supports R-1-6
zoning. Staff found that the proposed zone change, and subsequent project, will strengthen the core of the single-family
neighborhood. He concluded the Planning Commission voted to approve this item based on the following fmdings:
               1. The request is consistent with the policies of the general plan.
               2. The rezone is consistent with the Mountain View Community Plan.
               3. The zoning reflects the character of Kiesel A venue.
          The Planning Commission had some discussion about what to do with the existing commercial parking on the property,
and it was noted that most people who visit the business along 17th Street park on the road, so the commercial parking on this
property may not be necessary. One commissioner voted against the rezone, the reason being that he felt the commercial on-site
parking should not be removed.

Fiscal Year 2021-2022 Budget Amendment- Police Salaries: and
Fiscal Year 2021-2022 Salarv Schedule Amendment
          The Administration is proposing an amendment to the FY22 Budget to implement Phase 3 of the Evergreen
Classification, Compensation, and Benefit study for sworn police officers. The change will implement full penetration, meaning
that officers will be moved to their correct steps within their respective ranges. Officers will also be eligible to move to the next
step on their anniversary date. The Administration believes these changes are necessary to remain competitive in the current hiring
market. The cost of the early Phase 3 implementation is almost $2.5 Million. Funding is proposed to come from sales tax and
General Fund fund balance. A separate related proposal to amend the Range Placement Table for sworn officers increases starting
police office pay from $21.39 to $24.80, changes step progression, and adds additional steps for police officers and master police
officer positions.
          Police Chief Young used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the proposed adjustments to the Police
Department (OPD) salruy schedule, which will also require a budget amendment. He discussed several factors that have
contributed to the current police hiring environment; following Ogden City's actions to adjust wages for police and other
employees in the City, Salt Lake City police announced a raise in pay. The new pay scale is $26.93 to $39.29 per hour which
went into effect on June 27. Salt Lake City currently has 80-100 vacancies. Additionally, West Valley announced a raise in pay
with the entry level starting at $27.15 starting and topping out at $41.90. This was effective the first week of July to avoid
massive loss of bodies to Salt Lake City. West Valley has 25- 30 vacancies. These increases caused a ripple effect throughout
the Salt Lake Valley which spread to Davis and Weber counties. Layton Police Department raised their top end to $41.19;
Kaysville moved their top end to $41.29; Riverdale gave every officer a two (2) step increase, and Weber County gave all
employees a $2.00 an hour increase. This drastic shift in compensation left OPD approximately $6.00 an hour below at entry
level and $9.00 an hour below at the top of the pay scale compared to other medium and large sized Utah police agencies. The
tight employment market in Utah combined with the national atmosphere regarding policing has led to a shortage of qualified
applicants both nationwide and in Utah. There are more than 600 police officer vacancies across Utah and Utah POST is only able
to train about 200 annually at the primary campus and about 200 more at satellite campuses. During the months of January, July,
and August of this year, OPD had reduced the number of vacancies drrunatically- 19 new/rehires. If the Police Department is
unable to change its pay scale immediately, that trend will shift significantly in the opposite direction. As of September 27, 2021,
OPD has a total of27 vacancies, which include:
               •    10 Unfilled Sworn Vacancies
               •    11 Officers in POST/training
               •    2 Officers on Light Duty Status
               •    4 Officers on Military Leave
          OPD has experienced a significant reduction in new applicants since the pay changes were implemented in other agencies
~long the Wasatch Front. Lateral applicants have withdrawn their applications at OPD in favor of Salt Lake City and West Valley.
The Weber Fraternal Order ofPolice undertook an anonymous survey of OPD officers and found that22 stated they had applied at
another agency and 45 others were considering applying. OPD had one officer submit their resignation to take employment with
Riverdale. Background investigators have visited in reference to three other officers.
          Deputy Comptroller Sorensen discussed the implementation of the Evergreen Compensation Study; in the Fiscal Year
(FY) 2022 budget, police officers were brought to closest step and next step, for a fiscal impact of$1,382,775. The total runount
added to FY22 Budget to partially implement the Evergreen Study for police, fire, and general employees is $3,211,800. Phase 3-
or full implementation- of the study is scheduled to occur in FY23; the fiscal impact for police is $711,000, $302,225 for fire, and
$1,162,000 for general employees.

          Human Resources Manager Judkins noted that Administration has developed a new pay plan proposal to address the
current crisis. This includes immediately increasing the police salary structure to be competitive with the market; and expansion of
the police officer position steps to reflect the step structure of surrounding agencies - 12 steps. She offered a range of pay
comparison:
               •    Current Police Officer     $21.39/hour- $32.20/hour (adopted July 2021 from Evergreen Study)
               • Proposed Police Officer $24.80/hour- $37.63/hour
               • Increases to Sergeant, Lieutenant, and Division Commander ranges also included
          The impact of this proposal in the FY22 budget is $2,484,100. The pay increases would be implemented retroactive to
July 1, 2021 and employees will be placed in the next step in their new pay plan.
          Mr. Sorensen stated that the FY23 fiscal impact will be $4,281 ,650; this includes the amount added in FY22 as well as
the full penetration of the Evergreen Study for police, fire, and general employees.
          Comptroller Stout discussed optional funding sources for the FY22 increases; Administration proposes the use of
increased sales tax ($990,950) and fund balance ($1 ,493, 150). It will be necessary to identify funding sources for the FY23 fiscal
impact of$4,281,650.
          Vice Chair White inquired as to why the City's fund balance has such a high balance. Chief Administrative Officer
Johnson stated that it was previously much higher but has been spent down a bit. The reason for the current balance is that the City
just went through a period of much higher sales tax growth than expected; however, he feels that is temporary and should not be
relied upon as an ongoing funding source. This led to brief discussion about the status of the City's General Fund and
opportunities for identifying ongoing funding sources for increased Police Department wages given that the sales tax trends are
likely temporary. Mr. Johnson stated that he feels that it will be possible to identify an ongoing funding source for Police
Department wages, but it may be difficult to do the same for the needed wage increases in other Departments of the City. There
was also a discussion about opportunities for providing additional incentives and benefits to police officers to make their overall
compensation package richer. This included a brief focus on education funding for officers or housing assistance.
          Council Executive Director Eller-Smith then noted that Council staffhas included an action item on the October 19, 2021
meeting agenda responsive to Administration's proposal. There were no objections from the Council for proceeding in that
manner.

Fiscal Year 2021-2022 Budget Amendment- Airport Grant. Sewer Master
Plan Update. FY21 Unused and Surplus Funds, Etc.
         The Administration is proposing an FY22 budget amendment to increase the FY2022 City budget by $6,007,725. The
proposed budget amendment recognizes FY22 revenues from grants, donations, other goverrunental agencies, and retained
earnings ($2,451,325) and appropriates those funds for various uses. The proposal also includes recommendations for use of
departmental General Fund and Medical Fund FY21 carryover ($3,556,400). Normally, surplus funds would be encumbered prior
to June 30. Any budgeted funds not expended or encumbered during the fiscal year become available for re-appropriation in the
next fiscal year.
         Deputy Comptroller Sorensen presented Administration's proposed revenue and appropriations in the FY22 budget; he
referenced schedules to the proposed ordinance to amend the budget that details the increased revenues and revenue sources in the
General Fund, Capital Improvement Project fund, Airport Fund, Sanitary Sewer Fund, and Information Technology fund. He then
discussed the manner in which these revenues will be appropriated and briefly discussed the projects that will be funded if the
proposed budget amendments are approved. Administration proposes that the Council schedule a public hearing and take action
on the proposed budget amendments.
         There was brief discussion about the rebranding project and the Council asked for clarification from Administration
regarding prioritization of the placement of the updated City brand on various City facilities or replacement of signs.
         Council Executive Director Eller-Smith noted that the public hearing has been scheduled for October 19,2021.
