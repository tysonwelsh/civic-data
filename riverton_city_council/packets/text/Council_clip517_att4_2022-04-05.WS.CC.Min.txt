40          Town Days Fireworks Display Location
41
42   Sheril Garn, Director of Events and Operations, explained that the Council had decided during
43   the Strategic Planning Meeting to have fireworks at two different locations during Town Days.
44   The added cost for the second show would include overtime for City staff, lights, restrooms and
45   fencing.
46
47   Councilmember Stewart said that he would choose Monarch Meadows or Western Springs Park
48   as a location for the second show.
49
50   Councilmember McDougal said that he appreciated the work done to present these options to
51   the Council. He said that Town Days had been something unique to Riverton City that residents

 1   Councilmembers discussed language in the Strategic Priorities about maintaining enterprise
 2   fund balances.
 3
 4   Mr. Hicks explained that when referring to maintaining fund balances in culinary, secondary and
 5   sanitation funds, the Council should use “days on hand.” The bond rating agencies recommend
 6   382 operating days on hand. He thinks this is extreme but feels 270 is a better goal.
 7
 8   Mr. Hicks reported that at the end of fiscal year 2023, Fund 51 will have 136 days on hand and
 9   will need $800,000 to get to 180 days on hand. Fund 53 will finish fiscal year 2023 with 140
10   days on hand and will need $400,000 to get up to 180 days on hand. Because there are no
11   assets in Fund 55, there are no days on hand.
12
13   Councilmember McDougal said he would support working towards 270 days on hand. In
14   response, Mayor Staggs said the Council should be aware of consequences such as fee
15   increases when trying to maintain days on hand. Mayor Staggs also commented that the City
16   delivers the service, has 150-180 days on hand, and maintains a $4 million fund balance in
17   Fund 10 and a $7 million fund balance in RDIIF. He believes that having general fund balances
18   at a certain level is fine, which could support the enterprise fund balances.
19
20   Councilmember Buroker appreciates having standards but doesn’t want to structure the fund
21   balances so tightly that the Council doesn’t have autonomy to make decisions every year. She
22   would like to soften statements and use “work towards” as the language so that it isn’t finite.
23   Councilmember Wells said he agrees with Councilmember Buroker and would like to use “work
24   towards” and have small increases over time.
25
26   Councilmember Stewart commented that having 270 days on hand would mean that the City
27   could function for 270 days without collecting any additional fees. There has never been a
28   moment in his life where cities have stopped collecting fees and he has never seen it drop
29   below an 80% collection rate. He is more comfortable with 180 days and does not feel that 270
30   would be an attainable goal. Once the City hits 180 days then they can reassess.
31
32   Councilmember McCay also liked the language “working toward” and said she would like to
33   exclude sanitation from the 180 days on hand goal. In response, Mr. Hicks said that sanitation
34   was already excluded because it does not have assets. If sanitation were to be brought in-
35   house, he recommended that the City have 180 days on hand in this fund.
36
37   Councilmember McDougal asked for clarification on bonding. Mr. Hicks replied that there are
38   two ratings for bonding: franchise and sales tax revenue bonding and water revenue bonding.
39   The water revenue bond rating includes pledged revenue from Fund 51 and 53 combined.
40
41   Councilmembers agreed to 180 days on hand for the enterprise fund balances.
42
43   Mayor Staggs explained that in his five-year budget the County would pay $3 million for the
44   Greenwell and the State will contribute to the secondary water meters. He does not see any
45   other major capital projects that would require bonding. Mr. Hicks agreed and said that major
46   water line projects are anticipated, but those will be paid out of RDIIF. He also anticipates a
47   property tax increase for RLESA and isn’t sure what will happen with sanitation when it goes out
48   to bid in the fall.
49
50   Councilmembers discussed language in the Strategic Priorities relating to debt per capita.
     Page | 8 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     March 1, 2022




 1   Mayor Staggs explained that the City has $35 million in total debt, equating to $800 debt per
 2   capita.
 3
 4   Councilmember McCay said the City’s goal should be to reduce debt over time. Since it will be
 5   19 years until all debt is paid off, she would like the Strategic Priorities to state that the City will
 6   be debt-free in 20 years and work towards a debt per capita of $700. In response, Mr. Hicks
 7   explained that if the Council adopts a rigid rule about debt per capita, he worries that if
 8   something catastrophic happens, the Council will need to do truth in taxation or raise fees.
 9
10   Councilmember McCay suggested wording that says “work toward becoming debt-free in 20
11   years.” Councilmember Stewart suggested that the City only incur debt when it is absolutely
12   necessary.
13
14   Councilmember Buroker cautioned about putting definitive numbers in the priorities. She wants
15   to have the freedom to continue to improve the City when opportunities arise. She doesn’t think
16   the Council should have any sense of shame that they aren’t a fiscally prudent city already. She
17   would support $1,000 debt per capita. Mayor Staggs agreed with Councilmember Buroker.
18   Councilmember Wells also agreed and said he wants to be careful not to box into an amount.
19   He commented that if the City explores self-providing sanitation, it could mean that the City has
20   to incur debt and buy sanitation trucks.
21
22   Councilmember McDougal said that the reality is that the Strategic Plan is a governing
23   document but it has flexibility. He likes a firm number because it expresses the Council’s
24   attitude about budgeting. He believes the City took on unnecessary debt over the years and
25   this frustrated residents. He explained that it is the attitude that matters and he suggested that
26   the Council reduce debt and focus on the essential needs of the City. He is comfortable with
27   $1,000 or $850 debt per capita.
28
29   Mayor Staggs suggested the objective read “lower debt per capita and ultimately become debt-
30   free.” Councilmember McCay said the goal should be more specific and attainable. She also
31   recommended removing the item about “exploring self-providing sanitation.”
32
33   Councilmember Buroker suggested that the City should use unplanned revenue to reduce debt.
34   Councilmember Stewart agreed and said that 25% of new unforecasted revenue should be
35   allocated to reduce debt. Mayor Staggs suggested that it read “reducing callable debt.”
36   Mr. Hicks clarified that callable debt would need to be refinanced. The City has a duty to the
37   bond holders to pay based on the schedule that was established.
38
39   Councilmember Stewart recommended that the percentage of unforecasted revenue be placed
40   in a fund balance and then used to refinance the debt at the appropriate time. All
41   Councilmembers agreed that 50% of new unforecasted revenue would be placed in a debt
42   service fund.
43
44   Councilmembers discussed whether to remove the language about exploring self-providing
45   sanitation. After discussion, the decision was made to leave the language in there.
46
47   The Strategic Priorities were adopted by all Councilmembers. The final document will be
48   included in the packet.
