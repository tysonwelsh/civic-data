ORDINANCE NO. 21-02-01                                                 DATE February 8, 2021

       AN ORDINANCE OF THE KEARNS METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL ADOPTING
       THE FEE 2021 FEE SCHEDULE

                                          RECITALS

               WHEREAS, Utah Code §10-2a-14(3) provides that a Salt Lake County ordinance
will remain in effect as an ordinance of the Kearns Metro Township (“Kearns”) until the metro
township council amends or repeals the ordinance;” and

               WHEREAS, Kearns contracts with the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services
District (“MSD”) to provide planning and development services; and

                WHEREAS, in addition to Kearns, the MSD provides planning and development
services to the metro townships of Copperton, Emigration Canyon, Magna, and White City as well
as unincorporated Salt Lake County; and

               WHEREAS, Section 3.42.040(A)(1) of the Kearns Metro Code stated that all “fees
to the charged by [a Kearns] office or agency shall be proposed by the agency each year as part
of the annual budget processes,” and

             WHEREAS, Section 3.42.040(A)(3) states that “[u]pon review and approval of fees
by the…council, the fee schedule shall be placed on file with the …clerk;” and

              WHEREAS, the MSD board approved the attached 2021 fee schedule for the
planning and development services the MSD provides; and

              WHEREAS, the Council desires to adopt the attached 2021 fee schedule.

            NOW, THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED BY THE KEARNS METRO TOWNSHIP
COUNCIL that effectively immediately;

   1. The attached 2021 fee schedule is adopted pursuant to Section 3.42.040 of the Kearns
      Metro Township Code; and

   2. Staff are instructed to file this resolution and the attached fee schedule with the Kearns
      Metro Township Clerk; and

   3. Staff are instructed to post this resolution and the attached 2021 fee schedule to the
      Kearns Metro Township website and to the “Municipal Resolutions Book” within the
      Kearns’ Municode page; and

Continuity of Operations Plan (COOP) Discussion

                Mayor Kelly Bush stated the purpose of the Continuity of Operations Plan
(COOP) is to ensure the continuity of work if she is not able to follow through with her
responsibilities. The Council needed to adopt a resolution once all paperwork is filled out and put
into place.

                Chief Wade Russell, Unified Fire Authority (UFA), stated the COOP is a
succession plan for the township in the case of an emergency. It lays out distinct lines of
communication. The COOP covers areas such as the need for an annual review, multi-year
strategies, program management, training and drills, direction, control and coordination. Another
piece is setting forth a budget to cover the cost of these things. This plan is a good kick-off plan
for the Kearns Metro Township. Changes to the plan will need to be made going forward.

              Mayor Bush stated before the resolution can be adopted, there is a ton of
paperwork that needs to be filled out

Legislative Update

                   Mr. Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, reviewed the
following bills:

     HB 98 Local Government Building Regulation Amendment

                Mr. Bracken stated this bill that would allow developers to hire their own inspector
to do building inspection, plan reviews, and certificates of occupancy. It is a bad bill and opposed
by everyone outside of the development community. Leadership has put the bill on hold, hoping
that a compromise can be reached.

     HB 82 Single Family Housing Modifications

               Mr. Bracken stated the bill requires accessory dwelling units (ADU) be a permitted
use in any single family zone. Kearns already has an ADU ordinance, so this bill will not have a
big impact.

     SB 61 Outdoor Advertising Amendments

                  Mr. Bracken stated there are two bills that deal with this issue. The bill would
allow an existing sign to be changed to an electric sign regardless of location. This could have a
big impact on surrounding areas. It would take away the ability for the Council to regulate an
existing sign. The other bill takes away the Council ability to do anything with billboards and if it
interferes it is liable for the actual loss of business revenue. He does not think these bills will be
approved.

     HB 271 Home Child Care Amendments

                Mr. Bracken stated this bill would increase the number of children that could be in
a day care before the owner would have to get a State Day Care License. If the threshold is
increased, it would place a bigger burden on Kearns in making sure that all requirements are meet
before it issued a license.

     SB 58 Metro Township Amendments

             Mr. Bracken stated this bill would allow townships to impose the Municipal Energy
Sales and Use Tax. It has passed the Senate and is now in the House.

     HB 154 Use of Force Revisions

                 Mr. Bracken stated there is a lot of concern with this bill because it would place
an undue burden on police officers by possibly putting them in a position where they could be
subject to liability. The bill has not made any progress.

     HB 284 Minimum Wage Amendments

                   Mr. Bracken stated he just wanted the Council to be aware this bill is being
considered.

Resolution

                Mr. Bracken stated one challenge that he and Mayor Bush are having at the
legislation session is that they are often asked to opine on bills within a short time frame, thus not
having the opportunity to hold a discussion with the Council. He is proposing a resolution that
allows Mayor Bush to take a position of concern on any legislation that would limit local control or
anything that threatens public safety. The other part of the resolution is if there is a bill that does
not affect the two reasons listed above, then the Mayor would send an email to all Council
Members asking for their position on a bill, letting each Council Member know when she would
need to hear back from them. If any Council Member has an opinion different than the others, no
position would be taken until the Council can discuss it.

               Council Member Peterson stated he would rather receive a phone call since he
only checks his email once or twice a day.

             Mr. Bracken stated an email would provide a written record. He suggested the
Mayor could call Council Member Peterson about a bill and asking him to respond through his
email.

                 Council Member Butterfield asked if this resolution was only for this legislative
session or if it was ongoing.

                Mr. Bracken stated the resolution would be in effect anytime there was a
legislative session including special and interim sessions.

              Mayor Bush stated the resolution is for the bills that a quick decision needs to be
made. All the other bills would be brought before the Council for discussion.

               Mr. Bracken stated he could add the following to Section 2 of the resolution:

and Kearns’ legal counsel acting under the direction of the Mayor, to act on behalf of the Council
in discussing, responding to questions or comments about and engaging with legislators,
governmental entities, and others regarding legislation in accordance with principles set forth in
Section 1.”

Basically what this is saying is when there is insufficient time for the Mayor to get the Councils

RESOLUTION NO. 2021-02-02                                                  DATE: February 8, 2021


A RESOLUTION OF THE KEARNS METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL ADOPTING A PROCESS
TO GOVERN KEARNS’ PARTICIPATION IN THE LEGISLATIVE PROCESS WITH THE UTAH
                              LEGISLATURE

                                            RECITALS

                WHEREAS, the Utah Legislature considers numerous legislation each year that
could impact the Kearns Metro Township (“Kearns”) and its residents, public safety, and Kearns’
ability to regulate, govern, or oversee important issues within its jurisdiction, including but not
limited to Kearns’ land use authority, police powers, and other local government issues; and

                WHEREAS, due to the potential ramifications that such legislation may have on
Kearns’ and its ability to govern its own affairs, Kearns’ needs to be prepared to take prompt action
to either support or oppose legislation; and

               WHEREAS, due to the speedy nature of the legislative process, the Mayor and
Kearns’ legal counsel are often asked to support, oppose, or otherwise opine on legislation on
behalf of Kearns before the Kearns Metro Township Council (“Council”) can meet to take a
position on pending legislation; and

              WHEREAS, Section 2.04.160(B) of the Kearns Metro Township Code permits the
Council to adopt a resolution “for policy declarations and proposals” that “may be used to exercise
Council authority in matters of statements of policy and communication;” and

               WHEREAS, to protect Kearns’ interests, the Council desires to provide the Mayor
and Kearns’ legal counsel with principles and instruction to guide them in responding, reviewing,
and acting on legislation.

                NOW, THEREFORE, be it resolved by the Council that when the Utah Legislature
is in session, including but not limited to general, special, and interim sessions, the following
provisions will apply effective immediately:

                                                 9
DATE         MONDAY                    FEBRUARY                                              8, 2021


     1.       Position of Concern. The Council takes a general position of concern on any
     legislation that would: (A) adversely impact public safety within Kearns; or (B)
     adversely impact Kearns’ ability to regulate, govern, exercise its jurisdiction, or that would
     otherwise negatively impact local control, including but not limited to legislation affecting
     Kearns’ land use authority, police powers, and autonomy.

     2.      Legislative Communications. Where there is insufficient time to coordinate with
     the Council, the Council authorizes the Mayor, and Kearns’ legal counsel acting under the
     direction of the Mayor, to act on behalf of the Council in discussing, responding to
     questions or comments about, and engaging with legislators, governmental entities, and
     others regarding legislation in accordance with principles set forth in Section 1 above.

     3.      Official Legislative Positions. For legislation that is not subject to the principles set
     forth in Section 1 above, the Mayor will propose a position on the legislation and any
     related recommended actions to the Council. If possible, the Council will meet as soon as
     is reasonably practicable to consider the Mayor’s recommendations and take action as
     needed. Provided, however, that the Council recognizes that it may not be able to meet
     before a response from Kearns is required on certain legislation, in which case the Mayor
     will notify each Councilmember via email or, if possible, by telephone as soon as is
     reasonably practicable of: (A) the legislation at issue and how it affects Kearns; (B) the
     Mayor’s proposed course of action; (C) why a response from Kearns is needed before the
     Council is needed; and (D) a reasonable deadline given the circumstances for
     Councilmembers to express any objections to the Mayor’s proposed course of action. If
     no objections are received by the requested deadline, the Mayor and legal counsel, acting
     under the direction of the Mayor, may execute the Mayor’s proposed course of action. If a
     timely objection is received, neither the Mayor nor legal counsel may act on the legislation
     and Kearns will be deemed to be neutral on the legislation until such time as the Council
     can meet to discuss the legislation and take an official position.

     4.      The Mayor or legal counsel will provide a report on any actions they may take
     regarding any legislation at the next possible Council meeting, and the Council will ratify
     any positions taken by the Mayor pursuant to Section 3 above or otherwise address
     objections raised pursuant to Section 3.
