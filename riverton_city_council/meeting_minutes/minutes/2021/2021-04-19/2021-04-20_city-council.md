          Update on Green Well Project – Mayor Staggs

Mayor Staggs said that he had invited Total Water Management who was awarded the RFP for
the Greenwell project to give an update.

Mitch Curtis, Total Water Management, said that they are in Phase 1 of the Greenwell treatment
project. He said Phase 1 is final project planning and a key component is seeking to get a
Jordan River discharge permit. He said the study establishes the volume and what they can
discharge at the Greenwell. He said they have been able to do testing of membranes and
establish performance and recovery rates. He said they had compiled that data and presented
that to the water department and are moving forward with final economics for review of the
treatment proposition. He said the permit has been finalized and submitted to the state. He
said that this project will receive public comment within the next month or two and then there will
be a 30 day period where the comments will be evaluated.

Mayor Staggs asked Mr. Curtis to send the electronic documents to the City Council.

Mr. Curtis shared the Riverton City Update documentation. (Documents included in the packet)

Mayor Staggs said that he has been working with Salt Lake County and others to try to get
funding for the Greenwell project. He said the County is looking at congressional approval of
funding to pay for this project or they will use their relief funds to pay for this project. He
believes this could be a win, win scenario if the city could get this Greenwell for no cost and the
Greenwell would give the golf course water and the City would find a way to distribute that water
for Riverton residents. He said this option would be considerably less than the conservancy
district and there would be a great amount of savings. He said the City would not have rate
increases for the next five years if Salt Lake County funds this project. He said the TDS
hardness and selinene levels are the concern but even option 3 will bring the City in line with the
conservancy district.

Councilmember Buroker asked Mr. Curtis to clarify that in order to get to the current taste of the
water you can only recover 50% of the water.

Mr. Curtis said from a taste standpoint, option 3 would be fine with a 70% recovery.

Mayor Staggs said that in order to get the chloride rate to every resident equal to the
conservancy district then there would only be a 50% recovery rate. He said that to get the
residents water that is comparable then there would be a 68% recovery rate. He said the
amount of water that would spill off into the Jordan River would be 600 gallons per minute. He
said that it is naturally flowing at 300 to 400 gallons per minute. He said this is a decision the
Council would need to make later in the process. He believes that Option 1 would treat the
water way too much. He said the actual cost of the unit would be $2.5 to $2.7 million dollar
range to get the technology in place.

Councilmember McDougal said that he had two questions. He said that if the County funds this
project then the City would purchase the system outright and just be worried about warranty and
maintenance. He said his second question was whether the City feels confident that this well
has depth given the current drought environment.

          Stormwater Utility Ordinance Discussion – Ryan Carter, City Attorney

Ryan Carter, City Attorney said that he had prepared a fairly lengthy memo regarding
Stormwater. He said he wanted to drive home the point that this is an important program that is
federally mandated and regulated. He said that while recognizing the importance of the
program it has now become a discussion as to how the City should build the Stormwater fee.

Mr. Carter said that in order to safely discharge the water to the Jordan River, the pollutants
coming from residential and commercial property into the Stormwater must be managed. He
said the City has been charging one fee for one unit of measurement. He said that there are
higher concentrations of pollutants coming from commercial and industrial units then from
residential units. He said that the Stormwater ordinance would provide a fee structure for
amount to charge residents compared to commercial businesses. He said with the
recommendation residents would be $1 less per unit of measurement and commercial would be
$1 more.

Mayor Staggs said that the hard surface unit (HSU) in his budget will amount to an 85%
reduction in their Stormwater fee. He said that from the ERU, the city will push that $5 to
culinary and sanitation. He said that this idea came about some months ago and he charged

          Broadband Discussion – Mayor Staggs

Mayor Staggs said he invited representatives from EntryPoint Networks to try to dust off the
broadband plan done 2 years ago.

Jeff Christensen, EntryPoint Networks, said that the country is awake to networks and
broadband infrastructure being essential. He said that it used to be more of a fringe
conversation but the tides have changed and there is a lot of emotion involved with this topic.
He said that EntryPoint started in Idaho and now has five cities replicating what they did in
Ammon, Idaho. He has been working with several cities on broadband and one city that has a
population of 800,000.

Mr. Christensen believes the entire country will get fiber in the next 10 years. He said that it will
cost over $100 billion and that cable will get replaced by fiber. He said the question the City
needed to answer is whether they are going to own their own fiber or whether they want
someone else to own it. He said that by using the COVID money, Riverton City would have a
real opportunity to implement phase 1 of the plan which would connect the City’s infrastructure.
He said there is a lot of pressure on material and construction crews but thinks with a $1 million
to $1.5 million budget, the City could make a significant backbone structure, He believes that
fiber is the biggest avenue to make change within a city. He said that the City is currently
spending $75,000 per year already and his company could help build a robust network that
Riverton’s IT department would manage.

          Ordinance No. 21-10 - Amendment to Riverton City Ordinance 18.120, Elderly
          Housing Overlay Zone, 18.90 Table of Commercial Uses, and Commercial Zones,
          Addressing Care Facilities, Riverton City, Applicant

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director said the amendments to this ordinance puts
nursing homes, care facilities and assisted living centers into commercial zones and requires
a conditional designation. He said the number of rooms/beds is determined in the site plan
approval and is subject to constraints including parking. He said that individual ownership of
the living space/rooms is not permitted.

Mr. Lethbridge explained that the elderly housing overlay would only apply to residential
zones. He said that the amendment would have the mobility/age friendly features that would
be required, one feature per unit of density. He said that the density bonus will double the
