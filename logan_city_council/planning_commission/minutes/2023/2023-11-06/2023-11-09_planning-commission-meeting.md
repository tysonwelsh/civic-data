PC 23-066 KNM Properties [Conditional Use] Kristie Israelsen/Kristie Israelsen & Nathan D.
Israelsen, authorized agent/owner has requested a Conditional Use Permit to operate a six-bedroom
short-term rental of the entire home located at 273 North 1430 East in the Suburban Neighborhood
Residential (NR-4) zone; TIN 07-152-0711 (Wilson Neighborhood).

STAFF: Russ Holley, Planner reviewed the request for a six-bedroom short-term rental for up to 14
individuals located at 273 North 1430 East in the Cliffside area. R. Holley reviewed the proposed
parking plan and floor plan for the short-term rental and went over the requirements for a short-term
rental and the process of obtaining a business license.

R. Holley reviewed the history of the City Council’s decision to approve short-term rentals throughout
the City in neighborhood zones with a Conditional Use Permit and said that Conditional Use Permits,
by law, cannot be denied, but that conditions can be placed on the property that would mitigate any
impacts to the neighboring properties.

Commissioner Peterson asked about short term rentals following the City’s occupancy limits. R.
Holley said that short term rentals are allowed two individuals per bedroom plus an additional two,
they do not have to follow the city’s residential occupancy.

PROPONENT: Nathan Israelsen said they recently purchased the property and are willing to work
with the neighborhood to try to make this short-term rental a positive change to the neighborhood.
Mr. Israelsen spoke about another short-term rental property they own and how they have not had
any issues. Spoke about how they are very selective with who they rent to and how they will have
an active noise monitoring system in place that will notify them if the noise decibel rises over 70 for
more than 10 minutes.


Kristie Israelsen said they use the Airbnb and Verbo sites, which both have very strict safeguards in
place regarding who can rent the property. They also have a ring doorbell to monitor how many
people enter the home.

Marival Tanner said she has had an Airbnb for 5 years and has not had one complaint. Said that
most people who rent with Airbnb are not typically in the home for their entire stay.

PUBLIC: Darlene Dilley spoke about her concern with Airbnb’s located in neighborhoods.

Lance Hill read some of the CCNRs and said that Airbnb’s are not allowed in their HOA and this
short-term rental would be a violation.

Jim Chambers spoke about the amount of snow in the winter and not being able to park in the cul-
de-sac.

Holly Hill Spoke about the history of the home being a rental property and how it was not well taken
care of and mentioned having to file a lawsuit to enforce the HOA rules.

Doug Johnson, the current HOA president said this short-term rental will change the dynamics of the
neighborhood and is unacceptable.

J.R. Ochsenbein spoke about the type of community they have in this neighborhood, how there is
only one way into and out of the area, and his concern with the security of the homes in the area.

Jerry Wuthrich spoke about the City passing the ordinance allowing short-term rentals without
concern for the residents.

Dan Everton asked what violations would cause the Conditional Use Permit or Business License to
be revoked and what ordinance it is under. Spoke about other areas where an entire community
was developed for short-term rental properties.

Jeannie Simmonds said if the HOA prohibits this type of use, what happens if the city approves it.
Ron French asked what the 16 conditions are that have been placed on this conditional use permit.
Carol Chambers asked about the trees that will be removed for parking and read some nearby short-
term rental listings that allow up to 99 guests for events and parties, and refer guests to park on the

street.

Julie Hill asked about guests using the fire pit in the backyard and spoke about a concern with her
cat being safe outside.

Marci Love said that allowing this use would devalue the adjacent properties and the neighborhood.
Bryan Larsen spoke about the value of the neighboring homes decreasing.

Pauline Wuthrich asked how close short-term rentals can be to each other.

Valerie Beck asked how residents were notified of the code change allowing short-term rentals.

Commissioner Heare said that all meeting agendas are posted on the City’s website and bulletin
board in City Hall, as well as published in the Herald Journal, and on Social Media.


Kyle Rodemack asked how the City would enforce a violation at a short-term rental.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Heare stated that short-term rentals are allowed throughout the city
in residential zones as a conditional use. If an HOA has restrictions on short-term rentals, It is up to
the HOA to enforce their rules.

R. Holley said that if a short-term rental has more than three nuisance violations, their conditional
use permit and business license will be revoked. The Land Development Code allows short-term
rentals to be no closer than 500 feet from each other. A project notice gets mailed to homeowners
within 300 feet of the project site, the meeting agenda is posted on the City’s website, the Utah legal
Notice site, City and Library bulletin boards, the local newspaper, and social media.

Mohammed Abdullahi, City Attorney read Municipal Code Ordinance 5.15.120.B.

In the event of three or more violations of this chapter or the Logan City Land Development Code
committed by an owner or guest, or any combination of the two, within a twelve month period, the
City may, depending on the nature or extent of the violations, proceed with revocation of the short
term rental license for any short term rental property in accordance with the provisions of Chapter
5.02 of the Logan Municipal Code (Ord. 22-08, 5-3-2022).

Commissioner Hear read the 16 conditions of approval proposed for this project. M. Abdullahi said
that the City cannot enforce any HOA provision. The HOA would have to enforce its provision
through the court system.

Commissioner Lucero stated that condition number ten should include an inspection of the fire pit
with the fire inspection.

Commissioner Doutre said that if the neighbors see violations, they should report the information to
the city. Commissioner Peterson said that any existing short-term rentals that are violating

occupancy or parking may not be licensed with the city and citizens should report these locations to
the city to be addressed.

PC 23-056 100 North Historic Project Area — Continued from the October 12" meeting. [Zone
Change] Reed Bullen Jr, authorized agent, and property owners are requesting a Zone Change from
Town Center-2 (TC-2) to Traditional Neighborhood Residential (NR-6) of the eight properties located
at 107, 109, 131, 139 East 100 North and 156, 172, 178 East 100 North; TIN 06-066-0008; -0007; -
0005; -0004 and 06-067-0020; -0021; -0022, -0033 in the Adams Neighborhood.

STAFF: Russ Holley, City Planner, reviewed the request for a rezone of eight properties located in
the Historic District along 100 North between 100 and 200 East from Town Center Two (TC-2) to
Traditional Neighborhood Residential (NR-6). The Future Land Use Plan (FLUP) shows this area
as Town Center. Staff typically recommends compliance with the FLUP for consistency and
predictability which are both important for planning. At the rate Logan is growing, downtown is going
to expand to match the FLUP. Staff is recommending the zoning stay as is.

Commissioner Doutre asked how being in the Historic District would affect the future development
of this area. R. Holley said that any of the buildings in the historic district could be demolished. The
Historic Preservation Committee strongly discourages demolition, they want history to be preserved.
Any exterior remodel or new development has to go through the Historic Preservation Committee for
approval to ensure that the proposed plan is compatible with the historic district with similar materials,
massing, and fenestration.

PROPONENT: Reid Bullen, representing seven property owners with their proposed request to
change the zoning of their properties, spoke about the history of the neighborhood and the homes
in the area. Mr. Bullen read the following portion of the Adams Neighborhood Plan, “The plan aims
to protect the unique design and architecture characteristics of the existing housing stock and
promote the appropriate preservation of historic structures that showcase the unique historic
character of Logan City”. And said that their homes are the definition of historic structures in that
statement and that they are not requesting any change to the future land use plan, only the current
zoning.


PUBLIC: Barbara Fiechtl who owns one of the homes in the project area said, they do not want to
lose the historic nature of the existing homes and want to avoid the homes being demolished for new
commercial buildings to be built.

Jane Nicholson who owns one of the homes in the project area said, she doesn’t want to lose these
historic homes, once they are demolished, they cannot be rebuilt.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Doutre said there is good intention behind this request and asked if
it would be beneficial to change the allowed uses in the Town Center Two zone or to require the
planning commission to review any proposed use change within a historic structure or any alteration
to a historic structure.

R. Holley said that a permitted use or an interior remodel would not require Historic Preservation
Committee approval. Any project proposing new development or changes to the exterior of a building
within the Historic District has to go through The Historic Preservation Committee for approval.

The Commission discussed looking at the allowed uses in the Town Center zones to see if they can
be more compatible with the historic district.

MOTION: Commissioner Peterson made a motion to forward a recommendation of denial to the
Municipal Council on a zone change for PC 23-056 with the findings for denial as listed below.
Commissioner Croshaw seconded the motion

FINDINGS FOR DENIAL

1. The Logan City FLUP identifies the area as Town Center (TC).

2. The surrounding zoning is TC-2.

3. The TC-2 zone is appropriate because of the surrounding mix of commercial, multi-family and
single family residential land uses.

4. Current TC-2 zoning aligns with the goals of outer core downtown as a transition between high
density commercial downtown and single-family zoning farther east.

5. A rezone to a lower residential use is contrary to the long-term overall goals and visions
expressed in the General Plan and current zoning.

Moved: E. Peterson Seconded: R. Croshaw Approved: 6-0

Yea: Heare, Croshaw, Guth, Lucero, Peterson, Doutre Nay: Abstain:

PC 23-068 Bear River Mental Health Receiving Center [Zone Change] Beth Smith/DI Mac LLC &
Zulu Rentals LLC, authorized agent/owner has requested a zone change of a 2-acre parcel located
on the northeast corner of 1950 North and 200 West from Commercial (COM) to Public (PUB) for a
Behavioral Health Crisis Receiving Center; TIN 04-080-0044 in the Bridger Neighborhood.

STAFF: Russ Holley, Planner reviewed the request to rezone a 2-acre vacant parcel from
Commercial to Public, located at 1950 North 200 West to build a Mental Health Receiving Center for
the Bear River Health Department. A Receiving Center is a community facility open 24/7 and staffed
by therapists, nurses, and counselors to provide treatment for individuals in mental health or
substance abuse crisis, and will accept anyone for assessment, no matter their crisis, and are
relatively new to the state.

PROPONENT: Beth Smith said that the Receiving Center is needed to provide individuals with the
best response to crises related to mental health. Without the receiving center, individuals end up in


the hospital, the emergency room, or jail which are not the best options to provide the appropriate
care for many of them.

PUBLIC: Dean Quayle who owns the residential property to the west said they have no plans to
develop any more single family homes in the area. Mr. Quayle said he is in support of the facility,
but not at this location, there is no public transportation close and 200 West is only wide enough for
one way traffic.

David Zook, Cache County Executive said that when you look at traditional health care, there is
currently a robust system in place for the valley. They are trying to create a just as robust system
for mental health care. There has been great progress made with the implementation of 988
(equivalent of 911) and the mobile crisis outreach team (equivalent of paramedics), and they are
only missing the receiving center, which is the equivalent of an Instacare or Emergency Room to get
patients the care they need right away.

COMMISSION: Commisstener Peterson said that there is no denying the need for this facility, but
asked if this is the right location due to accessibility issues and lack of public transportation.

Commissioner Lucero said that mental health care is health care, and health care facilities are
permitted in commercial zones. Beth Smith said tht the nearest bus stop is two blocks away on
1800 North, but they do not anticipate many patients coming in by buss. Individuals will typically be
dropped off, brought in by police, or walk in.

R. Holley said that a 200 West as a collector street should be 80 feet wide, the existing 200 West is
approximately 15 to 20 feet wide. Streets are built along with the frontage of a development, if the
area does not get developed then a CMPO would step in to finish the street as needed.

Commissioner Doutre said she doesn’t have any concerns with rezoning to public.

Commissioner Guth asked about other locations they have tried to get. Beth Smith spoke about
several properties they looked into but were not able to obtain. When this current property became
available they were quick to offer because the location is close to Main Street, has close access to
1000 West to get to Box Elder County, and it’s less than five minutes from their continuum of care
team.

Commissioner Lucero said the benefit of this type of facility far out ways any of the small risks
associated with rezoning the property to public and it being sold and used for something else.

MOTION: Commissioner Lucero made a motion to forward a recommendation of approval to the
Municipal Council on a zone change for PC 23-068 Bear River Mental Health Receiving Center
Commissioner Doutre seconded the motion with the findings for approval as listed below.

2. Most of the surrounding existing land uses to the east are commercial in nature. The areas to
