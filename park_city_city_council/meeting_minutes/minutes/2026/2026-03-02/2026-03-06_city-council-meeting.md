Park City Senior Center by Matt Lee, Park City Economic Development Project
Manager:
Matt Lee, project manager with the Park City Economic Development team, gave an
update on the Park City Senior Center with the backstory, the parcel history, current
status, and anticipated budget and timeline. Mayor Dickey stated that the aim of this
joint meeting was to give the Summit County Council an update on progress and to
open it for questions.

Council Member Hanson asked for clarification on the target square footage and Lee
responded that the direction was to target 13,000 sf with design flexibility up to 15,000
sf pending final funding. Council Member Toly pointed out that they didn’t understand
how much building support and circulation was needed in a 13,000-sf building. They
would be losing about 1/3 of the building to that use and not for any programmable
space so that was why they were considering going up to 15,000 sf. Lee added that the
smaller you get, the bigger the portion your building support would be, such as
mechanical and electrical rooms, elevators, hallways, stairs, etc., and circulation goes
up.

Chair Harte asked for context of what they currently had versus this proposal. Lee
answered that the current footprint was 3,200 sf and a lot of that was dining room space
which was a flexible space. The improvements include a larger kitchen and dining room,
and the multi-purpose room would be a significant increase for programming i.e.,
exercise, games, etc. City Council Member Toly compared the existing senior center to
a house, and this new center is going to be more of a campus or facility.

Mayor Dickey pointed out that right now everything happens in the same space and the
Council asked to combine the dining space with more activity space, but there are a lot
of staff requirements to convert the space back and forth between the food service and
activity space, especially as they anticipate growing the food service in the future. The
Council did shrink that multi-purpose space and Lee added that there is a lot of
intentionality with putting the kitchen, dining room and lounge space on the ground
floors for better accessibility and putting the fitness room and multi-purpose space on
the 2nd floor.

Council Member McKenna asked if there is a plan to rent out the space to other
community needs when it is not being used by the seniors. She also volunteered that
she is the senior services liaison with Council Member Hanson this year. Lee responded
that they were not considering renting the space out at this point. Council Member Toly
added that more of the discussion has been making the parking lot multi-use for the
Park City Film Series rather than the building itself.

Council Member Hanson asked about the timeline and Lee answered that if they started
construction in spring 2027, they estimate it would be finished in late 2028. Council
Members Hanson and Armstrong asked for the estimated number of seniors in 2028
2029. They wondered how many members would be in this building when it is complete

Recycle Utah and Solid Waste Status Briefing by Luke Cartin, Park City Land and
Environmental Sustainability Director, and Janna Young, Deputy County Manager:
Council Member Toly noted the cost for a restaurant to participate in the program is
substantial on top of already paying for trash and recycling. This adds an extra burden,
and every little bit matters for small businesses, so the bags and the cost to businesses
is something she would like to look at for small businesses.

Council Member Miller commented that the most powerful message for her was how
much more she would have to pay when the landfill is full and she suggested that more
specific numbers would be a powerful tool for education. Council Member Hanson loved
the idea of requiring participation and thought that the County should do that also.
Council Member McKenna asked about plans to include multi-family housing and Cartin
answered that the idea is to get 80% of the brick & mortar first and then tackle that next.

Olympic Observer Program Debrief by Jodi Emery, Acting City Manager, and
Shayne Scott, County Manager:
Acting City Manager Jodi Emery and Summit County Manager Shayne Scott presented
what they observed, what they learned, and what they brought home from Cortina, Italy
at the 2026 Olympic games. Council Member Toly noted that at the biathlon, they threw
an Oktoberfest type party after the event and what they did well was the vibe.

Council Member Hanson pointed out that they stayed 45 minutes away and she thought
about other areas outside of the nucleus of the events in Park City. She wanted to
ensure the citizens and communities in the eastern part of Summit County are included
and involved in the 2034 Olympics. Mayor Dickey continued that there is a shift from the
Salt Lake City, Utah Games to the Utah 2034 Games as a big theme and how to get the
whole state activated.

Council Member Ciraco was in Bormio, Italy, where the Men’s Alpine events were held,
and it was very quiet. Every December Bormio hosts a World Cup skiing event and
there were three times the amount of people at the World Cup than they had for the
Olympics. Just having one event with days in between caused people to come in and
out daily rather than staying the whole time. Council Member Zegarra thought
consideration should be given on how to make these the most inclusive games we can
for the visitors and locals.
