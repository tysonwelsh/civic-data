      Planner Dan Boles presented he request to amend the Boulder Creek Commons PD-C
      (River Crossing Phase One) zone for phase one of the proposed commercial
      development to be known as River Crossing Phase One on approximately 12.02 acres
      located on the southeast corner of River Road and 1450 South. He provided a
      PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics: Aerial map; Land use map;
      Zoning map; Site plan; Site plan (after Planning Commission meeting); Conceptual
      landscape plan; Conceptual landscape plan (after Planning Commission meeting);
      Building C; Building D (West); Building D (East); Building E; Building F; Building G;
      Approved use table; Site photos; and Materials board. Parking is adequate;
      however, the Planning Commission was concerned with how the stacking would
      work. When the request came before the Council to remove the limit of drive thrus
      earlier this year, the Council wanted to make sure there is plenty of room for
      stacking; therefore, there is a requirement for stacking of five (5) vehicles for each
      drive thru. Staff has reviewed the plans and made sure that requirement is being
      met. The Planning Commission asked that the applicant provide an exhibit showing
      the stacking will be met, he showed the exhibit. Additionally, the Planning
      Commission was concerned with the two entry ways between buildings G and H;
      therefore, the applicant has removed one of these entry ways to allow for additional
      stacking for buildings G and H. Another change made at the Planning Commission’s
      suggestion is that the entry point at the southwest corner of the property be
      constructed with Phase 1. After being asked by Councilmember Larkin, Mr. Boles
      noted buildings E and F will face 1450 South.

      Jason Brice, architect for the project, explained they were careful to keep the sides
      of the buildings facing the streets aesthetically pleasing. Building F is a great
      example of how the building will look from the front of the building as well as from
      the street.

      Mr. Boles explained that most nearby residents are okay with the project. The
      developer has worked with those that had concerns. After being asked by
      Councilmember McArthur, Mr. Boles spoke about double stacking. Staff is
      comfortable with the proposed stacking.

      Councilmember McArthur commented he is concerned that stacking for this project
      will be similar to that of Red Rock Commons.

      Mayor Randall stated she has spoken with the developer; they do not want to have
      issues with stacking.

      MOTION:
          A motion was made by Councilmember Curtis to approve the
                    ordinance amending the Boulder Creek Commons (River Crossing
                    Phase One) PD-C zone for phase one of the proposed commercial
                    development to be known as River Crossing Phase One on
                    approximately 12.02 acres located on the southeast corner of River
                    Road and 1450 South with the conditions from the Planning
                    Commission that 1) The southernmost entry point is included in phase
                    1; 2) There is adequate stacking; and 3) The entry points between
                    buildings G and H is reduced to one (1).

AMEND ATKINVILLE INTERCHANGE AREA COMMERCIAL ZONE/ORDINANCE:
    Consider approval of an ordinance amending the Atkinville Interchange Area
    Commercial zone for a conceptual site plan, elevations, and colors and
    materials to be known as SunRiver Patio Furniture on approximately 5.06
    acres located south of Nighthawk Drive, west of Pioneer Road and east of
    Sand Piper Drive. Case No. 2021-ZCA-062

      Planner Carol Davidson presented the request to amend the Atkinville Interchange
      Area Commercial zone for a conceptual site plan, elevations, and colors and
      materials to be known as SunRiver Patio Furniture on approximately 5.06 acres
      located south of Nighthawk Drive, west of Pioneer Road and east of Sand Piper Drive.
      She provided a PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics: Aerial map;
      General plan map; Zoning map; Atkinville Interchange Area zone plan; Site plan;
      Parking requirements; Traffic improvements; Pedestrian circulation, bike parking
      electronic, vehicle charging station; Landscape requirements; Buffer requirements;
      Lighting requirements; Elevations; and Planning Commission recommendation. After
      being asked by Councilmember Larkin, Ms. Davidson spoke about the architecture
      facing Sand Piper Drive.

      Applicant Collins Stuart stated that he has met with the residents on three (3)
      occasions.

      Mayor Randall noted the Council visited the site. She explained SunRiver residents
      are concerned with access on Sand Piper after which she mentioned that the
      residents have formed a committee. This committee has been really production and
      understands the topography and that Mr. Stuart needs an entrance off of Sand Piper
      for trucks. Other concerns were regarding trash pickup, deliver times, and lighting.

      Mr. Stuart explained the committee the residents formed has been helpful. Although
      there are a lot of different opinions, residents are concerned about safety. He noted
      that elevations are extremely unique on this property. In the end, his goal is to see
      that traffic turns right when exiting the property. Residents want to avoid traffic
      heading south to Sand Piper, moving to the direction of the neighborhood; he
      agreed. He will install signage to keep traffic from heading south on Sand Piper.
      Regarding hours for deliveries, he will control the hours as to not to disturbing the
      residents. Additionally, he will limit trash pick up to the hours of 8:00 a.m. to 5:00
      p.m.

      Councilmember Larkin noted another of the concerns from the residents is that
      people would use the back access when the rest of the area is developed. She asked
      about installing a gate so that the only access is commercial access.

      Mr. Stuart stated that he has not spoken to the residents about limiting this access
      to commercial use. He indicated that the entrance from Sand Piper is not visible as
      someone enters the property. He has spent a lot of time thinking about the access;
      he uses the same scenario at his other stores. Both of them have access points from
      the rear of the building; however, he utilizes the entrance and exit points from the
      main road as it is visible. They are making sure to share access points with the
      property to the south. As the property is developed, the idea is to make the
      property the most appealing, attractive, visible, and safest to enter the property.

      Mayor Randall asked if the Planning Commission approved hours of deliver and trash
      pickup, etc. Ms. Davidson stated they did not. Mayor Randall indicated she was told
      by the committee that they are okay with the hours of delivery being between 7:00
      a.m. to 6:30 p.m. except for in the hotter months (June, July, August, and
      September) which will be from 6:30 a.m. to 6:30 p.m. and trash pickup between
      8:00 a.m. and 6:00 p.m. Mr. Stuart agreed with those hours.

      After being asked by Councilmember Curtis, Mr. Stuart stated if there is a problem,
      residents could call him. He is currently working with the residents regarding the
      height of the wall.

      Ms. Davidson noted the maximum wall height is 6’4”.

      Councilmember McArthur commented the Council has received more input on this
      item and this project than any other project this year. The Council has discussed
      this request, visited the property, and heard the concerns from the residents. He
      thanked Mr. Stuart for working with the residents.

      MOTION:
          A motion was made by Councilmember Curtis to approve the
                    ordinance amending the Atkinville Interchange Area Commercial zone
                    for a conceptual site plan, elevations, and colors and materials to be
                    known as SunRiver Patio Furniture on approximately 5.06 acres
                    located south of Nighthawk Drive, west of Pioneer Road and east of
                    Sand Piper Drive with the stipulations discussed tonight with regard to
                    hours of operation in terms of trash removal, delivery services, and all
                    other considerations including the right turn only out of the property.
      SECOND:
          The motion was seconded by Councilmember Smethurst.

      10 to PD-C on approximately 15 acres located east of 3050 East Street, north of 750
      North to be known as Zion Regional Medical Center. She provided a PowerPoint
      presentation covering the following topics: Aerial map; General plan map; Zoning
      map; Area concept plan; Site plan; Hospital; Future medical office; Traffic and
      utilities; Parking; Use list; Landscaping – buffer – pedestrian routes – EVCS conduit
      – bike parking; Elevations; Renderings; Materials board; and Recommendation from
      the Planning Commission. She noted the map included in the packet has been
      updated to amend the access between Walmart and Home Depot; she provided a
      new map. There is no power supplied to the property currently; therefore, Ms.
      Davidson suggested approval include a condition that no building permit for vertical
      structures will be issued until the substation is complete and all necessary easements

      are in place for the substation. It is anticipated that the substation will be complete
      in approximately a year and a half after the Energy Services Department gets the
      okay to build. Staff has discussed this issue with the applicant.

      Councilmember McArthur noted his biggest concern is regarding traffic. He asked
      Public Works Director Cameron Cutler to speak about the access to the north. The
      City Manager from Washington City approached him and stated it is crucial to have
      the road through the Walmart and Home Depot. He wants to make sure both St.
      George and Washington City are happy with the ingress/egress.

      Public Works Director Cameron Cutler outlined the traffic movement to the north. He
      noted there is a lot of traffic at 3050 East and Telegraph. The proposed driveway to
      the west, going to 850 North helps with the left turn movement coming out of
      Walmart, greatly improves the intersection. When the MPO modeled this driveway,
      they estimated about 2,500 cars daily. This is not a high number when comparing
      the numbers to a through movement. Washington City will still need to obtain the
      right of way on the Home Depot site. He noted that Mr. Sandberg owns a lot of the
      property to the east and does not want a roadway going through his property. He
      then spoke about future proposed roads in the area, including the proposed
      interchange 11. After being asked by Councilmember McArthur and Larkin, Mr.
      Cutler spoke about the north ingress/egress and how it will impact the parking in this
      area.

      Councilmember Larkin noted people try to avoid the Green Springs intersection. She
      is concerned that drivers turn left onto 850 North and traffic gets backed up; she
      asked if a round-a-bout would help. Public Works Director Cameron Cutler noted
      2,500 cars is not much on a regular road. For example, Indian Hills Road has about
      7,500 cars per day. He indicated that a round-a-bout was not modeled, but he could
      have that done.

      Brian Hales, a traffic engineer for the project, stated the queue length for the
      intersection is 275 feet for the right turn pocket. They have added a lot of elements
      and are looking at a future signal to the south to help traffic flow out of the
      development.

      Developer Jared Cox stated they only heard about the substation condition a couple
      of hours ago. They have always known about the substation would be required for
      this area, but this is the first they heard the building permit would not be issued until
      the substation is complete. Their construction duration will be about eighteen
      months and the substation would be about eighteen months. They envisioned there
      would be an overlap period so the substation would be completed prior to the
      hospital being completed. Waiting eighteen months to be allowed to go vertical
      would crush their opportunity to put the hospital on the site. They would be open to
      the Certificate of Occupancy being held up, but not the building permit. Mr. Cox
      commented they can work on getting temporary power during construction. By not
      having the ability to move the project forward on the construction makes the project
      not feasible for them.

      Garrett Goff, representative for the Sandberg property, agreed with Mr. Cox. They
      envisioned the building of the substation would run concurrent with the hospital.
      This condition would squash the hospital build.

      Ms. Davidson noted the Energy Services Department suggested the condition as they
      were concerned that there is no power to the property currently.

      Community Development Director John Willis explained staff needs to make sure the
      substation is under construction and to work with the applicant to obtain the
      property. Staff is concerned with a multibillion-dollar project being built without
      having power. Staff feels holding up the building permit until the substation is built
      is the best option.

      Mr. Goff noted they have been working with staff regarding selling the southeastern
      portion of the property.

      Assistant City Manager Deanna Brklacich commented Energy Services Director Laurie
      Mangum indicated that her department could provide temporary power and allow
      them to obtain the building permit as long as the Certificate of Occupancy is not
      issued until the substation is officially energized.

      Government Affairs Director Shawn Guzman explained based on staff’s previous
      experience, it would be very difficult to wait until the Certificate of Occupancy stage.
      He indicated that staff could keep the project moving with the grading done while
      working on acquiring the substation site and easement. He believes the acquisitions
      can be done in a timely manner and still be done in a way to allow the developer to
      get their building permit on time. It would be better for staff to go along with the
      first proposal of holding off on issuing the building permit. He made a commitment
      that if the City is far enough along with the building of the substation, he would be
      flexible with the time frame for issuing a building permit for this project. There is a
      lot of work the developer needs to do on the property prior to going vertical. The
      developer and staff can work on the project as a team without putting the burden on
      staff if the substation is not complete at the time of the Certificate of Occupancy.

      Mr. Cox commented there is no doubt there will be a lot of work on getting the
      substation in place as quickly as possible. He noted they are looking to have the
      building permits in June and only found out about the proposed condition at 3:30
      this afternoon. He is really concerned that they cannot get the building permit until
      the substation is complete.

      Mayor Randall asked if Mr. Cox if he would prefer the item be tabled so he and staff
      could speak with Energy Services Director Laurie Mangun.

      Mr. Cox explained if the Council is going to put the condition staff is suggesting, the
      project will not work. If the Council would table the item and have further
      discussion, it may work. He looks forward to having a more productive conservation.
