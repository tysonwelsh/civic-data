3.     PUBLIC HEARING, CONSIDERATION, AND RECOMMENDATION on
       Proposed Ordinance Text Amendments to Bluffdale City Code Sections 3.80 (Home
       Occupations), 6.20 (Truck Routes), 11.20, and 11.160 (Land Use Ordinance) to
       Update the Standards Related to Truck Routes, Storage, Use, and Parking of Large
       Commercial Vehicles in Certain Residential Zones. (Application 2022-07) - Staff
       Presenter, Grant Crowell.

City Planner/Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell, presented the staff report. He
reported that the document under consideration was the result of work sessions between the City
Council and Staff. The City Council subsequently requested input from the Planning Commission.
The proposed text amendments were the result of concerns over the growing prevalence of large
commercial trucks in some residential zones and the associated impacts of the trucks on the
community.

Bluffdale has typically had many businesses running out of homes. As a result, certain things are
allowed in the Home Occupation Code, contingent upon the size of the home lot. However, with
respect to semis and dump trucks, it has been alleged that there may be trucking operations being
run out of a residential zone. In the case of refrigerated trucks, they generate noise at all hours of
the day and night. Additionally, some streets may not be designed to handle such large and heavy
vehicles. The City Council has received many concerns from residents. Mr. Crowell reported that
he has driven throughout the City and has seen a lot of businesses with large trucks, particularly
along Redwood Road and further west, on 14600 South to the river, south of the 1iver, and on
Camp Williams Road. Many neighborhoods have businesses with large trucks.

The overarching challenge is to distinguish between the different types of trucks and which are
acceptable in residential areas. One exception identified was recreational vehicles. However, once
exceptions are made, people may do things that are not part of the exceptions and be in violation
of the law involving Class C Misdemeanors. Business License and truck route violations are Class
B Misdemeanors. Violations need to be defensible so that every time an exception is established
a defense is also necessitated.

Mr. Crowell next reviewed an infographic of trucks classified by Gross Vehicle Weight Rating
("GVWR"). Bluffdale has two types of Home Occupations consisting of Minor and Major. A
Minor Home Occupation would allow a business vehicle ofup to 14,000 pounds, or Class 4, which
would be comparable to an F-350. A Major Home Occupation would allow a truck of up to 19,500
pounds, which would be Class 5 vehicle. That classification would include farm equipment,
bucket trucks, and cherry pickers. Box trucks with a single axle fall into this category.

Heavier trucks with multiple axles would exceed 26,000 pounds, which is a threshold that is used
in the current Truck Route Ordinance. The Truck Route Ordinance does make provisions for an
individual to take their truck home on the shortest, direct route possible. The challenge is to
determine where the line needs to be drawn for trucks in residential areas . The current thinking is
to set 26,000 pounds as the maximum allowable GVWR for Major Home Occupations. A major
