MAG Public Participation Program Final Approval - Shawn Seager, MAG MAG as the MPO is required through Federal Regulations to have a Public Participation Program that addresses how the MPO shares information and receives public comments as part of the planning process. The approved 2015 document details MAG’s policies and procedures when it comes to Public Participation. In February 2019, MPO Regional Planning Committee approved changes made to the document and authorized MAG to advertise and initiate a 45-day public comment period. The comment period ended on March 26, 2019. There were no comments received. Mayor Reese moved that Regional Planning Committee approve the updated Final MAG Public Participation Program. Commissioner Ivie seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee May 2, 2019 Page 3 Bus Routes, Stops and Service Choices - Trustee Millington and Mary De La Mare-Shaefer, UTA The UTA Service Choices outreach initiative is underway and will continue for the next few months. UTA will contact cities to coordinate a meeting with their city council to get input about priorities for bus service planning that would benefit their communities. To learn more and to take the survey visit www.rideuta.com/About-UTA/Active-Projects/Service- Choices Trustee Millington gave updates on bus route stops, focusing on frequency and coverage. UTA will observe UVX and FrontRunner ridership during the summer as student numbers decline. A heat map was presented showing substantiated ridership in specific areas. Although some areas have lower activity, it is still important and should be addressed to accommodate those needs. UTA will provide detailed information to each of the cities upon request. Workshops are currently taking place with Lehi City, UDOT and UTA to focus on Thanksgiving Point. A committee meets every two weeks to keep ideas flowing. Businesses are joining in as well to come up with options as they prepare a proposal to provide more efficient transportation options in that area. TIP Modification: Spanish Fork Center Street - Shawn Seager, MAG Spanish Fork City was awarded $4,100,000 of MAG funding in 2016 to widen Center Street to five lanes from US6 to 900E. The project has been designed and is ready to bid. Spanish Fork would like to make minor changes to the original scope and is requesting the 10% contingency funds totaling $410,000 and an additional $661,000 of County Transportation Funds from MAG to offset the cost. Revisions to the original project scope increased cost by $2,071,000 totaling $6,171,000. Spanish Fork City will contribute $750,000 and Nebo School District $250,000 to cover the remaining balance. The Technical Advisory Committee recommended that the contingency funds and additional funds be approved for this project on April 22, 2019. Mayor Frost moved that the TIP be modified to add $410,000 contingency funds and an additional $661,000 to the Spanish Fork Center Street widening project. Mayor Hunsaker seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. FY20 Annual Unified Planning Work Program Preview and FY20 MAG Jurisdictional Cash Assessment DRAFT - Shawn Seager, MAG Shawn Seager provided an overview of the Unified Planning Work Program (Work Program). The Work Program is an annual budget that outlines the allocation of planning resources and special studies. The budget proposes to spend $252K on special studies that include Joint Planning Studies with WFRC, UDOT, and UTA; Travel Demand Modeling; Planning Opportunities Contingency; and New Exchange Project Management Consultant Contract. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee May 2, 2019 Page 4 The Exchange Project Management Consultant Contract is a new aspect to the FY20 Work Program that will provide project management in an effort to reduce delays and solve engineering challenges. When MAG started exchanging all their federal funds with UDOT for state funds it increased efficiency and overall savings, however these projects are no longer overseen by a UDOT project manager. Some cities are not as experienced as UDOT in managing these large-scale projects and have been relying on MAG staff to help solve complex situations that they have no experience in. Funding through this program will pay for a professional agency as needed. MAG, UDOT and UTA staff are still developing the detail of the Work Program and will send a draft executive summary to the Regional Planning Committee (RPC) before the MAG Executive Council meeting on May 23rd. The Work Program will be on the June 6th RPC agenda for action/approval. Corridor Preservation Request: Provo 820 North - Jim Price, MAG Provo City applied for funds through the Corridor Preservation Program for the purchase of two residences within the Provo 820 N Corridor, #19 on the Eligible Corridors List. This corridor is a Regional Transportation Plan Phase I project for improving east/west travel and a future connection to I-15. Provo City has been in the process of acquiring properties within the corridor as they become available for sale, and this will be the fourth and fifth purchased with Corridor Preservation Funds. The city has certified that the owners are willing sellers. The property has appraised for $310,900. With anticipated closing costs of up to $7500, the city requests a total of $318,400. The Technical Advisory Committee recommended the approval of this request on April 22, 2019. Mayor Pray moved that this Provo City Corridor Preservation Program application be approved for funding as requested, with approval to sunset after 1 year. Mayor Kaufusi seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. UDOT State Transportation Improvement Program (STIP) Workshop Results - Rob Clayton, UDOT An overview was given of the Statewide Transportation Improvement Program (STIP) process that recently took place with the Transportation Commission. The STIP is UDOT's official work plan for the development of regional projects. The tools within UDOT’s website was used in the decision process as it showed all their projects in detail. Rob Clayton highlighted the UDOT Region Three sections and provided direction on how to navigate through the site. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee May 2, 2019 Page 5 Annually the UDOT Region Three and the Complex conduct a STIP work shop over several months. It concludes in March and April each year. To learn more visit: www.udot.utah.gov and search STIP or TIF. Transit Capital Projects Update - Shawn Seager, MAG FrontRunner South to Payson Coordination FrontRunner to South Utah County is needed and funded in Phase 1 of the draft 2050 Regional Transportation Plan. In preparation of this project, MAG has been asked by Payson to coordinate interests along the route from Provo to Springville and Spanish Fork onto the Payson area. MAG will involve all public agency stakeholders to consider developing better bus service, station location planning and potential land use plans to compliment this future investment. Point-of-the-Mountain A $550K Environmental Alternatives Analysis will be advertised soon to relook at the previous light rail alignment and mode from Draper TRAX station to Lehi. MAG, WFRC and Draper are partners with UTA who is leading this study. MAG is contributing $25K. North State Street Alternative Analysis MAG, UTA, Provo, Orem, Lindon, Pleasant Grove, American Fork, Lehi and UDOT intend to develop and evaluate transit alternatives on a limited number of corridors within Utah County generally along the I-15 and US-89 corridors. It is assumed that the selected Locally Preferred Alternative (LPA) will consist of a north/south transit line between the Provo FrontRunner Station and Lehi costing an estimated $500K. MAG is contributing $300K. Update Reports UDOT Region Three: None. UTA: None. FHWA: None. UDOT Transportation Commission: None.
