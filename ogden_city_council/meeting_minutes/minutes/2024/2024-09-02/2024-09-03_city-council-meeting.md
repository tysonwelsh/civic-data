United Way of Northern Utah. Robert Hunter, Public Policy Advisor, will
provide an overview of the Weber County Housing and Homeless Systems
program
         Mr. Hunter discussed the efforts of the Weber-Morgan Local Homeless Council (WMLHC) to coordinate homeless
service providers in Weber County. United Way of Northern Utah, a 501(c)(3) non-profit organization, is hiring a Housing and
Homeless Systems Coordinator to assist with the work of the WMLHC. United Way serves as the fiscal agent for WMLHC.
WMLHC aims to strengthen the collective efforts of the providers in addressing homelessness with the goal of ensuring that every
individual in the community has access to safe and stable housing. United Way of Northern Utah, is in the process of hiring a new
Housing and Homeless System Coordinator to support the coordinated efforts and streamline services for individuals experiencing
homelessness in Weber County. The coordinator would be responsible for:
               • Updating and implementing the Weber County Homeless Plan based on the Community Assessment and
                   Strategic Plan 2019.
               • Working with local providers to assist with data collection and processing.
               • Researching and facilitating community homeless needs and providing appropriate education.
               • Facilitating the goal setting process and providing recommendations for the homeless and housing systems.
               • Coordinating with other federal, state, and local partners to identify critical funding sources.
              •    Managing the Winter Response and Code Blue task forces.

Proposed Ordinance 2024-26 to rezone property at 4336 South 1650 West
from A-C to FLEX in order to attract more businesses
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed rezone of 4336 South
1650 West came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated MTK Holdings, LLC has submitted a petition to rezone
10.23 acres from the Airport Commercial (A-C) zone to the Flex Industrial (FLEX) zone. The applicant anticipates that the wide
range of industrial and commercial uses of the FLEX zone will attract businesses to the site. The Planning Commission
unanimously recommended approval of the proposal.
          Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide aerial
photographs of the area encompassed within the zoning application, to provide a comparison of permitted uses in the Airport
Commercial Zone and FLEX Industrial Zone, summarize zone change guidelines, and to summarize the Planning Commission’s
recommendation of approval. The Planning Commission felt the requested zone change is consistent with the General Plan,
including the zone change guidelines in Ogden General Plan 8E1. Their recommendation of approval includes a condition that the
applicant completes a subdivision, so the property line matches the zone boundary. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety,
see the information packet for the meeting.
          Vice Chair White referred to the Google Earth image of the subject property and inquired as to what is being built on the
corner of 1700 West and 3400 South. Mr. Brierley stated he is unsure of the scope of that project, but noted the area she was
referring to is located in Roy City. Vice Chair White asked if increased capacity on that road will cause issues for Roy City. Mr.
Brierley stated he does not anticipate problems related to increased capacity because the road has been developed according to
reasonable standards to accommodate truck traffic.
          Council member Hyer asked if Planning staff has analyzed the need for potential future development standards such as
maximum building heights or protection of a runway zone. Mr. Brierley answered yes and noted that there are currently maximum
building height limits for development around the Airport; additionally, permitted uses have been carefully evaluated and uses that
would result in a large gathering or that would otherwise conflict with the airport use have been prohibited.
           Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2024-26, entitled:
                    “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the zoning map of Ogden City, as adopted by Section 15-3-3 of
                    the Ogden Municipal Code, to reclassify as FLEX Industrial Zone (FLEX) property heretofore classified as
                    Airport Commercial Zone (A-C), generally located at 4336 South 1650 West, and providing that this ordinance
                    shall become effective immediately upon posting after final passage.”
          Chair Richey then called for public input regarding the proposal.
          Heath Satow expressed his support for the proposed zone change; the type of product that can be created in the FLEX
zone is very desirable and can be hard to find in other areas of the community.
          Jake Jackson stated he is representing the applicant and he addressed Council member Hyer’s question about
development standards in the FLEX zone; he noted that when pursuing development near an airport, it is a requirement to secure
approval from the Federal Aviation Administration for building construction and crane use.
          There were no further persons appearing to be heard.
          Council member Hyer stated he has appreciated the cooperation of the applicant to this point; he noted the subject
property is directly adjacent to the Airport and it is very important to ensure that the uses the applicant is pursuing are appropriate.
The FLEX zone has the potential to benefit other areas of the community as well and he is excited to see what comes of it.
Council member Choberka agreed.

Proposed Resolution 2024-9 amending the Council Rules and Norms to
add a Council Code of Conduct
          A memo from Council staff regarding proposed amendments to the Council Rules and Norms came before the Council
for consideration. The memo stated in January of 2024 the City Council expressed interest in developing a code of conduct to
provide guidance on council member conduct in various settings. Council staff have met with an ad hoc committee of Council
members to prepare several draft options for a code of conduct to be reviewed and discussed by the Council. During the work
session on August 20, 2024, the Council directed that the conduct language be included in an amended version of the Council’s
Rules and Norms.
          Senior Policy Analyst Burton summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to facilitate a review
of the proposed code of conduct, which is broken into four sections pertaining to a Council member’s role in various settings. He
also discussed the manner in which the proposed code of conduct will be included in the Council Rules and Norms that have
already been adopted. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Council members thanked Mr. Burton for his response to the Council’s request for a code of conduct; they indicated they
feel it provides clear guidance to Council members regarding their role and expectations of them in various settings.
          Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2024-9, entitled:

Public Comments
          Pieder Beeli stated he previously made comments that may have been upsetting to some Council members; he addressed
the Council’s recent action to increase the City’s property tax rate and the Council’s prioritization of City employees. He tried to
express his concern about that prioritization and indicated he feels the City Council’s greatest concern should be for the Ogden
taxpayer. He stated that many people enjoy benefits that can be afforded to them in the free market, but Ogden City uses its
authority to enforce property maintenance codes against City property owners and their threats of enforcement are ‘backed up by
the guns of Ogden’ and the judicial system. This means there is no longer a free enterprise system in Ogden City. Wealth is not
created by the government, but by the private sector and he is very offended that the City of Ogden is in the business of operating
a golf course and imposing regulations on property owners that are contrary to free enterprise. He invited any member of the
Council to meet with him to discuss his concerns and he suggested they read the book entitled “Our Enemy the State”.
          Tiana Tua addressed the Council’s approval of a code of conduct and Rules and Norms; she follows federal legislation on
a regular basis but is fairly new to City legislation. She has read the Rules and Norms document and did not understand what
changes are being made and she asked for clarification or insight into what the language relative to Council member conduct
includes.
          Heath Satow asked for an update on Dinosaur Park.

Council member Comments
        Chair Richey addressed Ms. Tua’s comments and directed her to page five of the Rules and Norms document, titled
"Council member conduct” to find the breakdown of the language that is being added to the Rules and Norms relative to Council
member conduct in various settings. The language was also identified in Mr. Burton’s presentation, which is also available on the
City Council page of the City’s website. Council member Hyer noted that the Council’s Rules and Norms document is
adopted/reaffirmed twice each year. Council member Choberka stated there is a redline version of the document in the August 20
work session packet, which identifies the exact changes being made.

Mayor Comments
          Mayor Nadolski addressed Mr. Satow’s question; the building formerly occupied by the Northern Utah Wildlife
Rehabilitation Center has been demolished, but some utility issues have arisen. Additionally, the Dinosaur Park Executive
Director and Facilities Maintenance Supervisor resigned during the demolition phase of the project and that caused the project to
slow. A new Executive Director has been hired and he is coordinating with the Dinosaur Park Foundation Board to proceed with
the project, which has been broken into two phases and includes an all-abilities playground. Some vandalism has occurred at the
facility over the summer months and that has been very frustrating for all involved in the project, but it is still moving forward.
          Chair Richey stated the Council would like to see the plans for the Dinosaur Park improvements. Mayor Nadolski stated
that the Public Services Department and Dinosaur Park Foundation Board will be invited to a future work session to discuss the
project. Public Services Executive Director Anderson provided additional information regarding the permitting process for the
building improvements and stated he looks forward to providing more details about the scope of the project in the near future.
          Mayor Nadolski then announced a new Airport Director has been hired: Brian Condie. He comes from Rifle, Colorado
where he managed an outstanding facility. He also spent time at the Salt Lake City International Airport during the Utah Winter
Olympics. He sees a great deal of opportunity for Ogden and looks forward to the challenges associated with improving the Ogden
Airport. An open house welcoming Mr. Condie to Ogden will be held on September 26 and he invited the Council to attend. He
also reported on efforts of Ogden City and Weber State University to ensure that Taylor Avenue is safe for residents and students
traveling to school. He participated in a ride-along with a police officer on Taylor Avenue and learned the average speed of
motorists on the road was 21 miles per hour. This changed slightly when school started; traffic increased, and many motorists
were speeding, and he can understand the concerns of residents about the safety of their road. However, some of the perceptions
residents have about excessive speeding are not born out by the facts. He has compared traffic data for pre and post day one of
school and the amount of compliance is impressive. Weber State University is committed to providing a law enforcement presence
in the area, but the City has other areas of the City that are far more dangerous for school aged children walking to and from
school and it is important to focus City law enforcement resources in those areas. He is not fully supportive of completely closing
Taylor Avenue because of the traffic concerns but is fully supportive of ensuring a safe environment. There are opportunities for
encouraging traffic to use the ingress/egress points at the front of the University, rather than the back, and those opportunities will
be pursued. There was brief discussion among the Council and Mayor regarding traffic data pre and post the first day of school,
with Mayor Nadolski reporting that in the last 10 years there has not been a single accident or injury on Taylor Avenue, though
there have been a few on Country Hills Drive. He understands it only takes one accident to cause the death or injury of a motorist
or pedestrian, but based upon data there are far greater needs in other areas of the City.
          Council member Choberka stated that recently she was almost struck by a vehicle while in a crosswalk in 25th Street; she
understands the concerns of residents about speeding and dangerous vehicle activity and wonders about the root cause of the issue.
          Council member Blair thanked Mayor Nadolski for reporting on the traffic issues on Taylor Avenue.
