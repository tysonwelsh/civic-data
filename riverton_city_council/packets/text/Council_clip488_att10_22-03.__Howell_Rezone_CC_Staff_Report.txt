On December 9, 2021, the Planning Commission voted to recommend approval of this rezone
application, following a public hearing. Minutes from that meeting will be provided once
transcribed. The Planning Commission recommended the following motion:

I move the City Council approve Ordinance 22-03, amending the General Plan to Medium-
Density Residential and changing the zoning to R-4 (Single-family residential, 10,000-sf lots)
on 4.07-acres at 2356 West 11800 South, as shown in Exhibit “A”.

Background

This is a public hearing for a General Plan Amendment and Rezone of 4.07-acres at 2356 West
11800 South, the rezone area includes four parcels, 27-21-478-002, -019, -013, and -012. The
properties have frontage on 11800 South and extend north to Midas Creek.

The subject property is currently zoned RR-22, which is a single-family residential zone with
half- acre lots and animal rights. Properties to the east are also Zoned RR-22 and are mostly
developed. To the west is an undeveloped 5-acre parcel which is zoned R-3. R-3 also
extends north across Midas Creek into existing subdivisions. Further west across the canal is
Midas Crossing Subdivision by Ivory, which is zoned R-4. The Canal and Midas Creek are
not bridged and no roads are aligned in anticipation of a crossing. The General Plan
designation for the area is Low Density residential, this designation covers all the area of
Riverton north of 11800 South and east of the canal. Medium Density Residential is equivalent
to the R-3 Zone. The applicant has requested R-4, allowing ¼ acre (10,000 sf) lots.

The R-3 and R-4 zones are both single-family residential zones with many similarities. Set-
backs, lot coverage, minimum house size, and allowed uses are the same for both zones. The
zones differ in lot size, R-3 Zoning requires 14,000-sf lots, with 90-ft lot width, while the R-4
Zone requires only 10,000-sf lots with 80-ft of lot width.

Notices were sent to all property owners within 1,000 feet of this site and posted on the City’s
web-page. The Planning Commission, following their public hearing, voted to recommend
approval of this rezone application, with the zoning at R-4, allowing ¼ acre lots.

       C.     REZONE, A REQUEST FOR A GENERAL PLAN AMENDMENT AND
              REZONE OF APPROXIMATELY 4.07-ACRES LOCATED AT 2356 WEST
              11800 SOUTH. APPLICANT-BEN HOWELL.

Mr. Prestwich described the location of the subject property and the details of the application.
Notice was sent out to the neighbors within 1,000 feet of the proposed rezone. The parcel
is at 2356 West 11800 South. It is 726 feet on the back parcel and 190 feet along Midas
Creek. When the parcel was developed in 2012 through 2015, a canal crossing was
discussed, and it was decided by the Planning Commission and City Council that a
connection should not be made. The Ivory development includes one-quarter acre lots and
north of Midas Creek are one-third acre lots and zoning. The current zoning is one-half and
one-third acre. There are two parcels in the area that allow for animal rights. The zoning is
harmonious but the lot sizes and use varies.

Some neighbors were concerned about the irrigation rights and Staff assured them that it
would be handled with a site plan. The current zoning is RR-22 with animal rights. The
General Plan anticipates change in the area. The current General Plan designation calls for
low-density or R-3 zoning. The applicant is requesting a change to medium-density which
would allow for R-4 zoning. The differences between R-3 and R-4 are lot size. R-3 includes
is 14,000 square-foot lots while R-4 is 10,000 square-foot lots. Lot widths for R-3 are 90
feet and R-4 is 80 feet. Staff had seen multiple layouts for the property without anything
coming to fruition. It is a difficult shape and creates issues for development. Occasionally,
developers will ask for R-4 zoning because they need a narrower frontage, which may or
may not be the case in this situation. This application has received numerous comments
from neighbors.

Chair Cannon opened the public hearing.

The applicant, Ben Howell, explained that several options have been considered and the
R-4 zoning would make the width to depth ratio better without adding a lot of homes. He
had spoken to some of the neighbors and addressed some of their concerns. The maximum
number of lots that would border Midas Creek is two, regardless of the zoning, based on the
shape of the parcel. He mentioned that some of the neighbors with water rights have
concerns and he would make sure they are addressed.

Commissioner Brown asked why the applicant is requesting R-4 instead of R-3 zoning.
Mr. Howell stated that the 90-foot width requirement for R-3 makes for an awkward layout
with extra-large side yards that potentially use excess water. The development lays out
better as R-4 zoning and will ultimately only add an additional two units.

Commissioner Cluff asked what the square footage of the lots would be. Mr. Howell
explained that the minimum would be the required 10,000 square feet, but several of the lots
will be closer to 13,000 to 14,000 square feet. There will be a maximum of 15 homes in the
development.

Ken Higgins stated that he lives at the end of 2260 West. His home is built on one full acre
and he raises animals. All of the surrounding homes initially had animals but over time,
people have sold their water rights and the yards are no longer maintained. His concern
was with the water rights that come from the canal. He wants to make sure that they will still
be able to receive that water so his animals and property can be maintained.

Chair Cannon explained that a developer cannot restrict a homeowner with water rights from
getting water according to Utah Water Law.

Vern Wilson expressed concern with water usage and the overall growth in relation to water.
He had no issue with the R-4 zoning but had concerns with the future of the property to the
west.

Julie Fullman lives directly east of the proposed rezone. She was concerned that the
surrounding low-density zoning will conflict with the proposed one-quarter acre lots. She
would like to see the proposed plan before agreeing to a rezone.

Doug Lightly lives on 2260 West and asked if the City had required additional ingress/egress
to the proposed property other than the 11800 South access. Adding traffic to 2260 West
was of concern for him. He was also concerned with water rights.

Lisa Stocking lives at 2357 West 11625 South. Her home overlooks the proposed property.
She does not agree with the rezone as it may carry through to the property directly behind
her home and that zoning would change from one-third acre lots as well. She felt that the
zoning should remain consistent throughout the area.

Randy Warr was representing the landowner to the west of the proposed property. The
main question they had is regarding the connection and how wide it is. Adequate access to
his client’s parcel was their main concern. He cited the City code regarding access to
adjacent properties. He wanted to ensure that the road would be adequate width with
access to service both properties.

There were no further public comments. Chair Cannon closed the public hearing.

Mr. Prestwich reported that the developer is not proposing to include any property to the
east. The access road is 33 feet wide and would possibly use the provision for a private
lane or establish an agreement with adjoining property owners to increase the access for a
public street. This is a challenging parcel and more rules come into play when looking at a
situation like this. Staff has not looked at the aforementioned lot count of 15 lots to make
sure that it complies with the ordinance.

Chair Cannon asked about the reality of getting 15 homes on a private lane, and if it is
feasible to change the zoning if there is not adequate access to the property. There are
rules for private lanes that may limit the development, but the number of lots is not one of
them. There are other tools the City has to allow for development in areas where public
streets are not possible. Mr. Cannon asked if another zone change would be necessary if
