SLVLESA Update

               Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Copperton Metro Township, stated some of this
would need to be discussed in closed session, but basically, the USDA is willing to have a
discussion to include a bill that is similar to what Copperton proposed in its letter to the County.
This would give the County the option to withdraw from SLVLESA, but it would require assurance
that money will be set aside. This type of bill would affect the provisions in the State Code for first
responder districts. He thought it was unlikely the bill would happen in the upcoming session.
Representative Jordan Teuscher does not feel there is any legislation needed and feels that it
can be worked out within the board. The County is concerned about being voted out of the board.
Representative Tuescher’s staff has met with the attorneys and the County staff. It was a good
meeting. There are risks to approaching the Legislature every time there is a concern. It may be
appropriate to change how SLVLESA runs.

               Mayor Clayton stated he and Council Member Olsen had met with Sheriff Rosie
Rivera and some of her staff the previous Monday. Ralph Chamness, Chief Deputy District
Attorney, delivered a presentation that will be made public tomorrow and SLVLESA’s meeting.
The cost of providing law enforcement services to Copperton would be approximately $265,000
a year, assuming all other metro townships went with the Sheriff’s Office.

Storm Drain & Public Utility Easement Vacation

               Council Member Clayton, seconded by Council Member Severson, moved to open
the public hearing related to the storm drain and public utility easement vacation. The motion
passed unanimously.

                Jeff Miller, Planner, Municipal Services District, stated this was a hearing to
discuss EXP2023000929, which was a proposed storm drain public utility easement vacation
pertaining to the property owned by Jerry and Renata Alder of 8652 W Equinox Circle. There is
an existing 15 foot easement on the property line and the property owner requests the easement
be reduced to 10 feet to allow for a home addition. The home was damaged in August of 2022
from a flash flooding event. Kennecott has since made some changes that should help to reduce
the need for the easement. MSD planning staff has not received any comment in favor of or
objection to this vacation from surrounding residents. The Council has three options: approve the
request as proposed; approve the request with conditions as amended; or deny the request.

               Mayor Clayton asked why the easement should not be vacated entirely.

                  Jeff Miller stated it was also a public utility easement and there might be a need
to run utilities through it in the future.

               Jerry Alder stated the reduction would make a big difference and was a good
compromise.

              Renata Alder stated she would prefer that the easement be vacated, but agreed
the reduction would ultimately allow her and her husband to do the desired project on their
property.

              Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Copperton Metro Township, stated he initially
drafted documents for a vacation. But MSD staff felt it could not recommend that without studies
being performed. The reduction to ten feet, while not ideal, was a sufficient compromise.

               Jeff Miller stated the studies necessary would be costly.

                Mayor Clayton stated, for the record, that the County screwed up and the Alders
are paying for the price. This is the total failure of the government. His preference was to vacate
the entire easement. He felt the Alders were being bullied into compromising with a ten foot
easement. He stated he would talk to the County about paying for the study as the Alders should
not have to.

              Mayor Clayton, seconded by Council Member Olsen, moved to grant the partial
vacation as proposed by the Municipal Services District and to authorize him to work with the
County, Municipal Services District, and Rio Tinto to study whether the remaining easement is
needed and determine how to pay for the study. The Alders will not pay for the study. The motion
passed unanimously.

             Mayor Clayton, seconded by Council Member Severson, moved to close the public
hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

             Mayor Clayton asked Jeremy to set up a meeting for Mayor Clayton, Council
Member Stitzer, Rio Tinto, and Renata Alder.
