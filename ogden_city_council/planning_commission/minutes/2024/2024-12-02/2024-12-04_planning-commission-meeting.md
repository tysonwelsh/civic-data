4.Preliminary group dwelling, Canyon Gate Townhomes, 910 Monroe Blvd

Petitioner Craig North stated it is straight forward on the layout they are trying to bring forth
some quality townhomes for individual ownership.

Assistant Planning Director Joe Simpson addressed this being the first step in a few steps for
this project. It will consist of 9, 3-level buildings with 45 single family units at the top of Monroe near
9" street just under 2 % acres. This proposal is looking at demolishing the site and redeveloping it
anew for a townhome project. (slides shown) With the application of some landscaping corrections
and details they noted, staff feels the landscaping would meet the requirements. They are required to
have an entry feature with some landscaping for a sign naming the project and providing a defined
entrance into the development. With the building design, the same mix of materials on all four sides of
the buildings will need to be included, along with the minimum amount of percentage of patio and
balcony spaces. Additionally required to be added are; electric vehicle charging outlets in the garages,
waste management and access outside of required setbacks to enclosed, screened dumpsters,
garage space for roll out cans, screening for utilities and meters, fencing around the sides and rear,
and corrections implemented relating to utilities, storm water, etc,. So, staff suggests that this project
be considered for final approval.

5.12™ Street overlay project with exception, Forsberg Townhomes, 1027 12" St

Petitioner Geneva Blanchard said the townhome development is simple, and they are just
seeking a small concession on the east property line where their curb and gutter line fall a bit closer at
6.8 feet than the 8-feet requirement.

Planner Alyssa Girardo suggested some considerations for the special exception review.
Slides shown revealed two parcels on the property of about 1.4 acres which staff recommended
combining the two. It is presently vacant as the previous buildings were demolished in 2023. The 18-
unit townhomes will consist of 3 buildings with attached garages, and they are addressing the
requested exception for that driveway as Geneva stated. The corrections they are asking for are some
clarifications on the required earthtone materials, modification to the 38-foot limit the current plans
exceed, meeting the required minimum 10-foot offsets, a complete landscape plan and addressing
some landscape requirements, adding a 6-foot fence around the property to mitigate any on site

6. Mixed-use site plan, Riverwalk Phase 3, 1802 Wall Ave

Petitioner Adam Hughes with Lotus Company recalled prior discussions to exchange with the
City the location of this project to the 1802 Wall Ave as to accommodate the individual areas better,
which they agreed to and was the result of the plan in consideration of 113 units to the north side of
the river and 30 units to the south side. 25% of the units will be designated as For Sale townhomes as
part of the Ogden Bend Master Plan. The project will consist of 5 buildings with amenities such as a
fitness center, club house, play yard, dog walk, water features and trails for connectivity, as well as the
]2-foot river trail on the north side of the river for future connectivity.

Assistant Planning Director Joe Simpson mentioned this review was a fun one because it was
the first development to occur on that last stretch between Wall and Lincoln under the Ogden Bend
Master Plan. With it being a mixed-use zone there are several moving parts like the Master Plan, a
Development Agreement and code regulations. It is basically two locations being developed together
as Lotus River Walk Phase 3 of apartments, townhomes and amenities buildings. (slides shown)
There are many parcels that make up this project and if they are able to get the site plan approved and
move forward, that would include doing a small subdivision to combine the parcels, the parcels on the
south side of the river to be plotted for individual ownership, interaction with public space and
connectivity from the ground units to the trails, main entrance interaction with public sidewalks along
the streets, front-facing features on the buildings, the streetscape development be cohesive with the
existing park strip and sidewalk, restoration of the existing water feature by the river, a minimum 5-
foot setback code from the designated flood area (river) to the buildings which was established by
FEMA, utility and meter screens, with other transportation options in the area, this warrants a
reduction of the parking code and protecting the river from over shadowing by having setbacks equal
to the height of the buildings that same distance away from the river. A detailed amenities plan is
recommended as well for staff to consider. So, staff recommends approval subject to the mentioned
conditions.
