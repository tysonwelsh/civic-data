            AF
elevations. The Preliminary Plat was shared. She explained that this is a Condominium Plat and

3.     Public Hearing – A Petition for Preliminary Plat Approval to Consolidate Six Existing
       Parcels into a Three-Lot Subdivision Plat, for a Multi-Family Dwelling at 2252 South
       300 West.




                                                T
Action Item

Applicant:     Woodbury Corporation
Address:       2252 South 300 West, Times Square Project

Ms. White Halleck reported that the application before the Planning Commission is a petition for
          AF
Preliminary Subdivision Plat approval for the following properties: 320 West Bugatti Avenue,
332-336 West Bugatti Avenue, 358 West Bugatti Avenue (two parcels with the same address), 360
West Bugatti Avenue, and 430 West Bearcat Drive. This was a petition by Woodbury Corporation
on behalf of North 300 West, LLC. She reported that it is a multi-step entitlement path application.

The Planning Commission forwarded a recommendation on an ordinance to the City Council as
part of the petition, which had been finalized. The applicant went through the Design Review
process with South Salt Lake in August 2023. At a future meeting, the Commission would see the
Conditional Use portion. Many of the details of the multi-family use would be discussed during
the Conditional Use portion of the process, but an overview would be shared as part of this petition.
Ms. White Halleck reported that the petition was for a Subdivision Plat to consolidate six existing
parcels and re-subdivide them into three new parcels. Lot 1 is the proposed multi-family that will
be seen during the Conditional Use Permit application. Lot 2 would mostly be overflow parking
R
until it can be developed at a later date. Lot 3 was planned for development in the future.

The existing conditions were reviewed. Ms. White Halleck shared images with the Commission
for reference. With all applications, there is an ALTA survey. There were a number of utilities
that ran through the development area. The layout must correspond to what is happening in the
Public Infrastructure District (“PID”) improvements. It is also important to make sure that the
lines in the area that serve the downtown are located within rights-of-way. The utilities that run
D

through the lots are private storm drains and overhead power. There were a few that South Salt
Lake Engineering will coordinate with the applicant on. Two UTA transformers fell within the
plat area. Those transformers were identified on the map.

The Proposed Site Plan was shared. Ms. White Halleck reiterated that the details about the multi-
family would be shared during the Conditional Use portion of the application. The intention was
for the multi-family to cover Lot 1. There was a hammerhead lane that falls on the western half
of the proposed building. Part of that was to serve the UTA transformer that was there. UTA has
a recorded access easement that covers the area as well as South Salt Lake Fire. Part of the plat
South Salt Lake City Planning Commission Meeting                                                 8
September 21, 2023


application would be to ensure that there is an easement for both parties across Lots 1 and 2. It
had been reviewed by the South Salt Lake Fire Department and was approved.

The multi-family building itself is a 214-unit multi-family building with two levels of parking and
approximately 184 parking stalls. There will be brownstone units that front the rail line and lobby
space that wraps the corner along 300 West and Bugatti Avenue. Something unique to the site that
had to be coordinated through the platting process was the Parley’s Trail. There is a HAWK
crossing light that runs north along the sidewalk on 300 West and wraps down the rail line. There
was coordination with Salt Lake County to ensure that the width was appropriate for the trail.




                                               T
The Site Plan also looked at right-of-way improvements and right-of-way dedications. It was
quickly identified on Bugatti Avenue that the existing cross section is substandard to South Salt
Lake Engineering width requirements. As part of the plat application, there was a proposal to
dedicate nine feet along the north side. It would allow for wider sidewalks, trees, and tree grates,
as well as alignment with the underground utilities. If the parcels to the south are redeveloped, a
similar dedication would need to take place to reach the full cross width. There was no dedication
          AF
requirement on 300 West but improvements to the existing public right-of-way were needed.

The elevations were shared. Ms. White Halleck explained that it is a modern building and there
will be a lot of glass and transparency as well as public and private spaces. This would be a four-
sided building, as there are frontages on the rail line, on 300 West, on Bugatti Avenue, and the
back of the house, which is on the fire lane to the west. The Preliminary Plat was shared. It was
reviewed by South Salt Lake Legal, Engineering, and a third-party engineer. It had also been sent
to Salt Lake County for review. It was currently undergoing technical corrections needed for
recording. Those would be done before the property came back for the Final Plat approval.
General Plan considerations were referenced. There was a lot of language in the General Plan that
supported that type of development in the Downtown District. Ms. White Halleck read some of
the language.
R
As part of the development, it was proposed that the streetscape infrastructure along 300 West and
Bugatti Avenue be upgraded to comply with the Downtown District standards. That means there
will be larger sidewalks, street trees, and other amenities. The development would comply with
all engineering standards for the right-of-way cross sections as well. The property would be near
the TRAX station and that was where high-density development should be supported. Staff
recommended approval of the Preliminary Subdivision Plat with the conditions in the Staff Report.
D

The applicant’s representative, Ted Didas, from McNeil Engineering, was available to answer
questions. Commissioner Carter asked when the other lots would be developed. Mr. Didas did
not have a timeline for that. Chair Pechmann wondered if any sustainability features had been
added to the project, such as stormwater reuse. Mr. Didas was not aware of any other than the fact
that the project would encourage pedestrian accessibility.

Chair Pechmann opened the public hearing. There were no comments. The public hearing was
closed.
