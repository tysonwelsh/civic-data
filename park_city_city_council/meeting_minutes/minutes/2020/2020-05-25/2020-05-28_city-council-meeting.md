2. Consideration to Approve a Level 4 Special Event Permit for Temporary
Activation of City-Owned Property within the Downtown Business Improvement
District, Including Car-Free Use of Main Street on Sundays, to Facilitate a Phased
Re-Opening of Business in Accordance with Utah Leads Guidelines and Social
Distancing Requirements in an Amount Not to Exceed $80,000 per Special Event
Fee Reduction Process and Municipal Code Section 4A-2-9; AND Delegate
Authority to the City Manager to Approve Reasonable Modifications to
Operational and Logistical Plans Associated with the Permit:
Jenny Diersen, Special Events Manager, and Jonathan Weidenhamer, Economic
Development Manager, presented this item with Alison Kuhlow, Historic Park City
Alliance (HPCA) Executive Director. Diersen reviewed that HPCA approached the City
with the request to temporarily activate Main Street for pedestrian only use. Other
requests had come in from Snow Creek and Prospector business districts as well. She
indicated the key to success was to maintain social distancing and this was a good
solution. This would be car-free Main Street on Sundays from June 14 to September 6
and would be called Dine, Shop and Stroll events.

Diersen noted the concern from the Parking Department on the estimated $250,000 in
lost parking revenue for these Sundays, as well as the potential for parking in the
neighboring residential zones. She stated these concerns would be monitored and
adjustments could be made. In addition to approving the special event permit, she
requested that Council approve the City Manager having the authority to approve
modifications that were deemed necessary.

Alison Kuhlow, HPCA Executive Director, explained that HPCA reached out to
proponents and opponents to this proposal and reached a consensus to close Main
Street on Sundays. A survey was sent out and 75% of respondents were in favor of the
proposal.

Council Member Joyce asked if the operating cost would increase if HPCA requested
Saturday and Sunday. Diersen stated the cost could be a little more or less per Sunday,
depending on the weekend, but the estimated cost per day would apply for each
additional day that was scheduled.

Council Member Worel asked about residential traffic management. Diersen stated the
southern section of the Brew Pub lot would be a vehicle turn around so vehicles couldn't
continue up to Hillside and the King Road area. The resort shuttles could come down
via Hillside, but they wouldn't be allowed to go up through Hillside. She noted if a street
was closed, it would need to be specified in the operational plan. Council Member Worel

asked if commercial vehicles would be limited on residential streets. Diersen stated it

Council Member Worel asked if some outcome measures could be put in place so they

Council Member Henney asked how the word would get out about the Main Street
closure. Kuhlow stated they had marketing money they could use for this purpose.

Council Member Gerber didn’t know if comparing sales was the best metric to use in
this scenario because it was not a consistent measurement for all businesses on Main
Street. She was also concerned with safety measures and asked about Condition of
Approval Eight, specifically inquiring if HPCA would coordinate which businesses were
allowed to erect tents or hold sidewalk sales each week, or if individual businesses
would submit their plans to the Special Events Department. Diersen didn’t anticipate a
lot of temporary structures, but stated she would meet with HPCA to discuss the
process. The Building Department would be doing inspections to ensure safety. A list
would be kept of businesses wanting sidewalk sales, etc., so there would not be overlap
with a neighboring restaurant or other business. Council Member Gerber indicated there
were many residents who were in favor of a pedestrian-only Main Street. She asked if
Transit could be increased on Sundays to get people around town. Diersen indicated
that was something that could be explored. There were plans for increased frequency in
some areas, but not all. Mayor Beerman stated many things would be learned as this
was rolled out, and services could be added if there was a need.

Council Member Doilney stated he participated in the HPCA meetings and the main
point of this initiative was to try different things to increase business.

Mayor Beerman closed the public hearing.

Council Member Joyce hoped to see more outdoor dining and shopping due to the
closure of Main Street. He wanted to see something transformative because of
businesses implementing new ideas.

Council Member Doilney thought this program might be a slow process to get going. He
looked forward to trying something new. Mayor Beerman stated in order to make this
successful, the City needed to be nimble, in allowing signage and other needs for this
street. Diersen was confident those measures were in place. She stated the key was
communication between the City departments and businesses.

Weidenhamer suggested amending Condition of Approval 13 in the operational plan to
give authority to the City Manager to authorize changes. Margaret Plane, Special
Counsel, recommended modifying the second sentence to end after “the City Manager
or their designee,” and deleting the remnant of that condition.

Council Member Henney felt the City would know success when they saw it. It would
take being on Main Street to see what was happening and how many people were
there. Kuhlow agreed and stated she would talk with merchants each Monday, but that
