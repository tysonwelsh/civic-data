Millcreek City Council
October 26, 2020
7:00 p.m.
Regular Meeting

The City Council of Millcreek, Utah, met in a regular public meeting on October 26, 2020 at
City Hall, located at 3330 S. 1300 E., Millcreek, UT 84106. The meeting was conducted
electronically per Millcreek State of Local Emergency Directive No. 2 of 2020 and live streamed
via the City’s website with an option for online public comment.

1.3 Public Comment

2. Business Matters
2.1 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution 20-45, Approving Amendment 1 to
an Agreement with Salt Lake County for CARES Act Funding
Mayor Silvestrini said that Salt Lake County gave a disbursement of $1.8 million to
communities in Salt Lake County. And they will be doing an additional disbursement of
$1.8 million based on population size to Millcreek. Resolution 20-45 amends the
agreement to increase the amount that Millcreek may receive in a second disbursement.

Council Member Jackson moved to adopt item 2.1, Resolution 20-45. Council Member
Marchant seconded. The Deputy Recorder called for the vote. All Council Members voted
yes. The motion passed unanimously.

2.2 Discussion and Consideration of Ordinance 20-52, Adopting Millcreek’s 2021
Regular Meetings Schedule

City Recorder, Elyse Sullivan, said that she wanted the City meeting schedule for 2021 as
soon as possible. Mayor Silvestrini explained that regular council meetings will be held
on the 2™ and 4"" Mondays of each month. Council Member Jackson asked how holidays
are dealt with. Ms. Sullivan answered that if a meeting falls on a Monday holiday then
the meeting will be held on the Tuesday after the holiday.

Council Member Marchant moved to adopt item 2.2, Ordinance 20-52. Council Member
Uipi seconded. The Deputy Recorder called for the vote. All Council Members voted yes.
The motion passed unanimously.

Mayor Silvestrini said that he will attend a meeting with Salt Lake County Mayor Wilson

distribution. The Utah League of Cities and Towns has been lobbying for a 3"!

Council will be meeting next week. He also noted that Millcreek Community Council
would like to meet at City Hall.

3.3 Staff Reports

Mike Winder, Economic Development Director, said that demolition will begin this week
Friday for Millcreek Common. Mr. Winder explained, a year ago, EPG gave their
estimate for Millcreek Common, that was their best guess. Now EPG has come back with
a new estimate. Since subcontracting costs more and material prices have gone up the
EPG estimate has gone up. Economic Development and Planning have created three
options that may help guide decision making.

Francis Lilly, Planning Director, said that the original project budget was about $6.5
million. The remaining money was for property acquisition and asbestos removal. The
architect responded with a plan that fit the budget and citizen desire for the public space.
The goal is to have open space amenities for small and large events, classically designed.
CMGC, the construction manager, has come in $2.6 million over budget. Mr. Lilly
explained that option A is to implement the plan as designed. This plan reflects a
community consensus. Plan A would require $2.6 million in additional funds. Option A
included the ice rink and ice ribbon, full improvements around Chambers Ave, a café and
building for administrative support. This plan costs $9.1 million. Option B cuts $2.1
million dollars from the plan, this is closer to the $6.5 million budget. An additional
$434,00 would be needed. An ice ribbon is still in this plan since that was important to
the community, but no ice rink. Mr. Winder and Mr. Lilly explained that the paving
would be done with concrete instead of paving stones.

Option B does not include a water feature, no shade structures, asphalt on Chambers
Avenue, and more austere lighting. Mr. Lilly said that it will get the City a bare bone
open space. Option C cuts $1 million off the potential $9 million budget, this plan still
has an ice ribbon but no rink. Council Member Jackson asked if the rink could be added
later. Mr. Winder said it is more difficult to add a rink later because all the infrastructure
is underground. Mr. Winder explained that they do not wish to spend money the City
does not have, however this is still a once in a century plan. Mr. Lilly explained that the
water feature is less important than shade. Mr. Lilly explained that the community really
wants the ice ribbon. The ice rink was an afterthought. Mr. Lilly believes that parking and
landscaping north of Chambers Avenue is very important. Development will be next to
Millcreek Common right away. Phase two may not happen right away, it will be good to
have the lawn and parking for the next several years. Mr. Winder said this can prevent

3.4 Unified Police Department Report

Chief DeBry commented on a homicide at Tanner Park on the Salt Lake City side of the
park. A suspect has been arrested. Chief DeBry said there is not a threat to the public in
that area. There is no suspect for another robbery and homicide on 6" East do not have a
suspect and Unified Police Department (UPD) is appealing to the public for information.
UPD is working with West Valley Police Department regarding a few robberies. UPD
will be conducting a drunk driving checkpoint on Halloween.

In September there were 3,394 calls for service, 1,185 progressed to cases. 241 citations
occurred and there were 65 booking arrests. Chief DeBry implored that Millcreek citizens
to not idle their cars due to theft. Due to rise in COVID-19 cases all UPD precincts will
be closed to the public for walk-in visits, the precinct will be open by appointment only.
Detectives are looking for families in need during the holiday season. Millcreek C.O.P
Detectives met with students from the Elizabeth Academy and Terrace Elementary. The
Millcreek UPD precinct received two new marked F-250 trucks which replaced
unmarked vehicles for more community visibility. Chief DeBry said Mayor Silvestrini
was supportive of the change. Clean-up at Big Cottonwood Regional Park yielded 1,500
pounds of garbage. Millcreek Detectives conducted an Eliminate Alcohol Sales to Youth
operation in Millcreek. 11 out of 18 businesses failed. The businesses that failed will be
required to come into compliance. Chief DeBry explained that police departments cannot
conduct these operations at the same business consecutively, these checks can occur
quarterly. Council Member Marchant asked if the police could go back to the business
more often after their violation, and what the punishment is for the violations. Chief
DeBry stated that individuals had been cited. Chief DeBry noted there were seven graffiti
removals in Millcreek. Council Member Uipi asked if UPD knew exactly where the
graffiti was. Chief DeBry said he could get the locations to the City Council.

3.5 Unified Fire Authority Report

Chief Duane Woolsey stated that Unified Fire had a busy September. The Unified Fire
Department responded to requests from other states in California and Oregon. This work
was very difficult for the fire fighters, it affected them deeply. Their job was to look
through burnt homes and cars and identify individuals. 55 personnel were deployed out of
the state, but UFA was able to keep full staffing in the local community. Unified Fire is

in the middle of their Captain’s Bootcamp. Unified Fire is also in the middle of a new-
hire process. UFA would like to hire thirty new fire-fighters. Mayor Silvestrini thanked
Unified Fire for their support during press conference with Senator Romney.
