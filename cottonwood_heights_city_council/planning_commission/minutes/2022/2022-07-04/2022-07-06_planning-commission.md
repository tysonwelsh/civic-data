31          1.1       Project ZTA 19-011 – A Public Hearing to Receive Comments and Take Possible
32                    Action on a Proposed Ordinance Update Relating to the Sensitive Lands
33                    Evaluation and Development Standards (“SLEDS”) Ordinance (Section 19.72 of
34                    the Zoning Ordinance).
35
36   Senior City Planner, Andrew Hulka, presented the Sensitive Lands Evaluation and Development
37   Standards (“SLEDS”) Ordinance and stated that the City is conducting an update to the standards.
38   The update is part of the zoning ordinance that establishes the regulations and policies for reviewing
39   new development, building permits, and areas that include a sensitive lands hazard. Public comment
40   was received as well as Commission input while continuing to provide monthly updates. Public
41   comments included concern regarding the allowance of any one staff member having the ability to
42   unilaterally approve items. Approval remains an open process with the Development Review
43   Commission (“DRC”) providing oversight.

 3          •   Definition of ‘City’;

11   Assistant City Planner/Sustainability Analyst, Samantha DeSeelhorst provided updates on the
12   Riparian Protection Area. She stated that the area essentially begins at the waterway top of bank and
13   has been split into four buffer areas. Each is regulated differently from what is allowed for
14   development. Guiding principles were discussed. Based on feedback received from the Planning
15   Commission and the public, she confirmed that the Riparian Protection Area has been extended from
16   100 to 125 feet. Along with the extension, a fourth buffer area was introduced. A visual
17   representation of the area was displayed. Staff recommended a positive recommendation of approval
18   be forwarded to the City Council with the findings set forth in the Staff Report.
19
20   Commissioner Ebbeler asked if the Riparian Zoning Map and GIS overlay will be updated once the
21   Ordinance is passed. Ms. DeSeelhorst confirmed that the overlay will be updated, and staff will
22   reflect that item in the mapping, which will become part of the Ordinance, if adopted.
23
24   Commissioner Mills appreciated the modifications and asked if there would be anything preventing
25   a configuration of property located in Section C from being subdivided or split between Sections C
26   and D. Mr. Johnson explained that multiple buildings will be required since single properties do not
27   generally cross property lines. Ms. DeSeelhorst commented that regardless of where property lines
28   fall, the Riparian Protection Area is measured from the waterway. Regulations were reviewed.
29
30   Commissioner Anderson questioned the deletion of the term “Rezoning” from Section 19.72.17. Mr.
31   Johnson stated that when conducting fault studies, fault hazards are mitigated from habitable
32   structures and a request for a rezone is not required to be accompanied by a development proposal.
33   The property may be screened for fault lines but must mitigate and provide specific engineering once
34   an actual development is proposed. Mitigation efforts were considered.

 3             3.1     Project ZTA-19-001 – A Public Hearing to Receive Comments and Take Possible
 4                     Action on a Proposed Ordinance Update Relating to the Sensitive Lands
 5                     Evaluation and Development Standards (“SLEDS”) Ordinance (Section 19.72 of
 6                     the Zoning Ordinance).
 7
 8   Senior City Planner, Andy Hulka presented the Staff Report and stated that the request involves a
 9   comprehensive update and maps and amendments consisting of the following:
10
11   Development Review Committee (“DRC”) update.
12
13         •   Update the definition of “City” (19.72.020) to state that “for the purposes of this chapter and
14             unless otherwise specified, all decisions, approvals, and recommendations by the city shall be
15             made by the Development Review Committee (DRC).”
16         •   Updated the definition of “DRC” (19.72.020) to state that the DRC consists of members
17             approved by the City Council “by resolution once each calendar year.”
18
19   Non-habitable structures:
20
21         •   Remove the line from the Applicability Section (19.72.040) stating that non-habitable
22             structures are not required to comply with the provisions of the chapter.
23
24   Steep Slope update with detailed exceptions:
25
26         •   Per the recommendation of the City Engineer, the revised the Slope Development Standards
27             (19.72.050.A) were modified to state that “no development, including all grading, retaining
28             walls, and structures, is permitted on slope areas in excess of 30%”. There were exceptions
29             for isolated slopes that are smaller than two acres in size. Retaining structures are permitted
30             in steep slope areas and limited to four feet in height, not to exceed 12 feet total with terracing.
31
32   Table of Minimum Compaction:
33
34         •   Update to the Compaction Table under the Earthwork Section (19.72.050.G) of the
35             Development Standards. Minimum compaction percentages were added for trench backfill
36             and landscape areas.
37
38   Permits on Existing Lots:
39
40         •   Changes to Section 19.72.130 “Building Permits on Lots Recorded Prior to the Effective Date
41             of These Ordinances.”
42         •   A statement was added to clarify that the requirements of this section are in addition to all
43             other applicable requirements detailed in the chapter.
44         •   Removal of portions of the section that are already required elsewhere in the chapter (all
45             reports are already required to include statements regarding suitability for development,
46             factors of safety, and other disclosures).

 1      •   Added the requirement of a Civil Engineer to review all reports, as well as the structural
 2          engineer.
 3
 4   Riparian Protection Area Summary:
 5
 6      •   The updated SLEDS ordinance introduces a Riparian Protection Area, which begins at a
 7          waterway’s top of bank and is split into four buffer areas.
 8      •   Each buffer area is regulated differently, with the buffer area closest to the waterway being
 9          the most restrictive on what is allowed within it.
10      •   The Riparian Protection Area has been designed with five goals in mind:
11
12          1.     Ensure the continuation of natural functions and habitat provided by riparian areas.
13
14          2.     Guide development in a way that enhances the riparian corridor.
15
16          3.     Improve water quality.
17
18          4.     Minimize flood hazards.
19
20          5.     Protect aesthetic and recreation values associated with riparian corridors.
21
22   The Riparian Protection Area was extended from 100 feet from top of bank to 125 feet from top of
23   bank. Along with this extension, an additional buffer area was added for four total buffer areas:
24
25      •   Area A: 0-25 feet;
26      •   Area B: 25-50 feet;
27      •   Area C: 50-100 feet; and
28      •   Area D: 100-125 feet.
29
30   Updates were made to the regulation of structures within the Riparian Protection Area, including:
31
32      •   Accessory structures located in Area B may not be used for human occupancy;
33      •   The total structural footprint is limited to 2,000 square feet in Area C. Additional square
34          footage beyond this 2,000 square foot maximum must be located in Area D; and
35      •   Regulations addressing coarse woody debris were removed and replaced with a general
36          disclaimer that individuals should assess the presence of coarse woody debris on a case-by-
37          case basis after consulting with a riparian expert.
38

44   feels necessary. He expressed concern with the special procedure process to find timely oversight
