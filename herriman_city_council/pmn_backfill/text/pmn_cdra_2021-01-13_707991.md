The following are the minutes of the Community Development and Renewal Agency of Herriman City. The
meeting was held on Wednesday, January 13, 2021 at 6:00 p.m. in the Herriman City Council Chambers, 5355
West Herriman Main Street, Herriman, Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting, as required by law, was posted in
City Hall, on the City’s website, and delivered to members of the Council, media, and interested citizens.

                        January 13, 2021

    2. Discussion Items
            2.1. Discussion pertaining to a Participation Agreement with Herriman 73 Partners, LLC for the
                 designation of the Automall as a project area and the sales tax incentive for the project–
                 Chase Andrizzi, City Attorney
City Attorney Chase Andrizzi offered a history of the proposed participation agreement dating back to December
2019. He reiterated concerns raised by board members of the Tax Increment Fund (TIF) being donated to the
developer to offer prospective tenants. He explained the proposed interlocal agreement anticipated 70-percent of
the point of sales tax would be contributed for the purposes of developing the automall area. Shortly after the draft
participation agreement was presented, concerns were relayed about the cap of sales tax participation. Attorney
Andrizzi explained this topic was revisited during the September 9, 2019 meeting where nothing had been decided
and added this was the reasoning behind the presentation tonight. The board agreed that at a certain point it would
be best for the City to work directly with the end user rather than this negotiation take place between the developer
and tenant. After extensive discussion, the Board determined a small percentage should be given to the developer to
be utilized for development related purposes outlined in the agreement along with the approval of invoices
submitted to the City. Finance Director Alan Rae interjected the point to include the requirement this
reimbursement would be for public improvement and infrastructure. The Board agreed.

Attorney Andrizzi explained historically, participation agreements more commonly pay a dollar cap but the annual
amount is usually a percentage. For comparison, the Anthem TIF is a dollar amount cap. Generally, an annual cap
and lifetime cap would be set. He explained other controls could be set as well to ensure more oversight in relation
to the development. The cap discussed would apply to the whole project. Chair Watts relayed his opposition to
placing an annual limit in the agreement, and leave it as a total participation amount. Attorney Andrizzi agreed with
this approach. The Board clarified these deals are necessary to facilitate growth in the area. Chair Watts indicated he
would like specific plans in place for when construction and opening would take place to avoid the commencement
of the timeline outlined in the agreement. Attorney Andrizzi offered a counterpoint indicating if the timeline was
not triggered the City would maintain the entire portion of sales tax for the area. Chair Watts indicated his comfort
level of the maximum amount of sales tax participation to be set at $15 million. Director Ohrn relayed her position
to have an analysis of the breakdown prior to committing an actual dollar amount of participation. Attorney
Andrizzi confirmed and indicated he would bring the agreement back as requested to the Board for consideration.
