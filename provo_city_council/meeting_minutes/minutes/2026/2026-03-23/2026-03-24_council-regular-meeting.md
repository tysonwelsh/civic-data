 3           A presentation of a $1,000,000 contribution from the Metropolitan Water District of Provo
             to support the new drinking water treatment plant project (26-007) 00:11:26

Ryan York, Water Division Director, introduced Dan Johnson and provided background on the
Metropolitan Water District of Provo. He explained that the District was established in 1938 to assume
the debt obligation for Deer Creek Reservoir and has since played a key role in supporting Provo City’s
water system. Mr. York noted that the District has contributed to water supply efforts through
purchasing canal shares, supporting well development, and helping finance projects such as Big Springs
in South Fork. He stated that the District continues to support Provo City’s water needs and invited Mr.
Johnson to present on the drinking water treatment plant.

Mr. Johnson, Manager of the Metropolitan Water District, presented a letter on behalf of the District
regarding support for the City’s new drinking water treatment plant. He explained that the Board of
Directors has had ongoing discussions with Provo City about assisting with future water needs and
identified the treatment plant as an opportunity to contribute. Mr. Johnson stated that the Board
unanimously approved a $1 million contribution toward costs associated with the new drinking water
treatment plant. He indicated the funds would be transferred electronically and expressed appreciation
for the continued partnership with Provo City.

 4           An ordinance amending Provo City Code to require planning commissioners running for
             public office to take a leave of absence (26-006) 00:15:53

       Motion:       An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2026-10, as currently constituted, has been
                     made by council rule.

Tanner Taguchi, Council Policy Analyst, presented a council-sponsored proposal brought forward by
Chair MacKay and Vice Chair Christensen. He explained that the purpose of the proposed ordinance is to
ensure the Planning Commission remains a non-political advisory body and is not used as a platform for
campaigning during an election year. Mr. Taguchi stated that while past practice has been for
commissioners running for office to voluntarily resign, the proposed change would instead require a
leave of absence during their candidacy. He noted that similar provisions already exist for the
Neighborhood District Executive Board and that the Planning Commission has a higher level of meeting
frequency and public engagement. He clarified that the leave of absence would begin upon filing for
candidacy and would continue until the individual’s campaign concludes. If a candidate is no longer
running, they may resume their role on the commission. Mr. Taguchi concluded by recommending
adoption of the ordinance as presented and offered to answer questions.

Councilor Garrett asked for clarification on whether a Planning Commissioner who loses in a primary
election would be allowed to return to their position and resume service on the commission.

Mr. Taguchi responded that a commissioner would retain their seat during their candidacy and could
return to service if they are no longer running, such as after losing in a primary election. He noted that
there is no provision to temporarily fill the seat during the leave of absence, meaning the position would
remain vacant during that time. He acknowledged concerns regarding maintaining a quorum and stated
that if multiple commissioners were absent and a quorum could not be met, action by the Council or
Mayor could be taken to remove and replace members as needed.

Chair MacKay opened the item for public comment. With none, she closed public comment and invited a
council discussion.

Councilor Whitlock expressed support for the proposal and appreciation for the work put into it. He
shared that, based on his own experience serving on the Planning Commission while running for City
Council, the lack of clear guidance had been challenging. He stated that establishing a structured
process, including the option to return after a campaign, is a positive approach that helps preserve the
Planning Commission’s role as a non-political body.

With no other comments, Chair MacKay called for a vote.

       Vote:         The motion passed 7:0 with Councilors Bogdin, Christensen, Garrett, Hoban, MacKay,
                     Whipple, and Whitlock in favor.

 5           An ordinance enacting anti-idling restrictions for certain commercial trucks. (26-021)
             00:21:23

       Motion:       An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2026-11, as currently constituted, has been
                     made by council rule.

Mr. Taguchi presented a proposed ordinance sponsored by Councilor Bogdin and seconded by Councilor
Whipple. He explained that the purpose of the ordinance is to limit potential negative impacts from
heavy truck idling while not discouraging truck parking within the city. Mr. Taguchi noted that due to
limited designated truck parking along the Wasatch Front, drivers often use commercial lots or public
roadways to take required breaks, which can create impacts for nearby residential areas. He stated that
the ordinance is intended to address concerns such as noise and odors associated with idling,
particularly in locations where commercial and residential uses are in close proximity. He explained that
the proposed ordinance would streamline enforcement compared to the existing noise ordinance by not
requiring decibel measurements and by specifically addressing idling and related impacts. Mr. Taguchi
added that enforcement would be complaint-based, with a warning issued prior to any citation,
consistent with state requirements. Mr. Taguchi concluded by recommending adoption of the ordinance
as written and offered to answer questions.

Chair MacKay opened public comment. With none, she closed public comment and invited a council
discussion.

Councilor Bogdin provided additional context for the proposed ordinance, noting that the City has
received complaints regarding trucks idling in various areas. She emphasized the importance of not
discouraging truck drivers, as they are essential for goods and services and require places to stop,
especially given limited capacity at existing truck stops. She stated that areas such as Springville and East
Bay have begun to experience impacts and that the proposal is intended to proactively address concerns
related to noise and diesel emissions affecting nearby residents. Councilor Bogdin expressed that the
ordinance represents a balanced approach and a good starting point to address these issues.

Councilor Whipple expressed support for the proposal and noted that the concern was initially raised by
Councilor Bogdin. She shared a personal experience with a smaller refrigerated truck idling near her
home, which created noticeable noise, and stated that larger trucks in commercial areas near residential
neighborhoods could have an even greater impact. She emphasized concerns related to noise and
localized air quality and expressed appreciation that the ordinance provides a balanced response while
equipping law enforcement with tools to address resident concerns.

Councilor Garrett asked whether trucks are permitted to leave their engines running at designated truck
stops, particularly in winter conditions when drivers may need to keep warm.

Councilor Bogdin responded that advancements in technology are reducing the need for trucks to idle,
noting that alternatives are becoming more common. She stated that some truck stops, such as Flying J,
are incorporating plug-in options that allow drivers to power their vehicles without running engines. She
explained that while the idea of requiring additional services at truck stops was considered, it was
ultimately determined not to be necessary as part of this proposal.

Councilor Whitlock shared feedback based on discussions with his father, a truck driver, noting that
newer trucks often switch from diesel engines to auxiliary power systems after a short period. He
expressed support for the intent of the ordinance but suggested that a one-minute idling limit may be
too restrictive. He stated that a timeframe of two to three minutes may be more practical for truck
operations, while acknowledging that enforcement is typically complaint-based rather than proactive.

With no other discussion, Chair MacKay called for a vote.

       Vote:         The motion passed 7:0 with Councilors Bogdin, Christensen, Garrett, Hoban, MacKay,
                     Whipple, and Whitlock in favor.

 6           An ordinance requiring payment of civil fines to renew a rental dwelling license. (26-022)
             00:29:41

       Motion:       An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2026-12, as currently constituted, has been
                     made by council rule.

Mr. Taguchi presented a proposed ordinance sponsored by Chair MacKay and Vice Chair Christensen
related to residential dwelling licenses (RDL). He explained that the ordinance would add failure to pay
civil fines, accrued from zoning violations, as grounds for denial, suspension, or revocation of an RDL.
Mr. Taguchi illustrated the limited scope of the amendment through several examples. He stated that a
property owner who resolves a violation promptly and does not incur fines would not be impacted.
Similarly, a property owner who remains out of compliance would see no change, as existing code

property owners who correct a violation but fail to pay any associated civil fines, who would now be
subject to potential denial or revocation of their license. He added that the amendment is intended to
incentivize timely payment of fines and strengthen the City’s position during appeals by clearly
identifying nonpayment as a basis for enforcement.

Councilor Whitlock asked if this ordinance could incentivize faster compliance.

Mr. Taguchi responded that the impact may vary depending on the property owner’s circumstances, but
emphasized that the potential loss of a residential dwelling license serves as a significant incentive to
both come into compliance and pay outstanding fines. He explained that if a license is revoked and the
property owner continues to operate the rental, additional violations and fines could accumulate,
further increasing the financial and legal consequences.

Councilor Whitlock noted for the benefit of the public that, as discussed in a prior work session, the City
may work with property owners who have accrued significant fines by establishing a payment plan. He
asked for confirmation that if a property owner is in compliance with such an agreement, they could still
retain or obtain a residential dwelling license.

Mr. Taguchi confirmed that property owners who are working with the City on an approved payment
plan and making efforts to address outstanding fines would not be subject to denial or revocation of
their residential dwelling license. He clarified that the ordinance is intended to apply in cases where a
property owner refuses to pay outstanding fines despite the City’s efforts to work with them.

Chair MacKay opened public comment. With none, she closed public comment and invited a council
discussion.

Councilor Christensen expressed support for the proposed ordinance, stating that it clarifies existing
expectations rather than creating an additional penalty. He noted that the changes help simplify
enforcement and ensure that individuals who are not following established rules are held accountable.

With no other discussion, Chair MacKay called for a vote.

       Vote:         The motion passed 7:0 with Councilors Bogdin, Christensen, Garrett, Hoban, MacKay,
                     Whipple, and Whitlock in favor.
