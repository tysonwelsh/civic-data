Sam Drown

General plan change from General Commercial and Lowe Density Residential (3 to 4
units/acre) to Medium Density Residential (7 to 12 units/acre) and zone change from C-2
and R-1-12 to RM (3.18 acres)

Sam Drown with Concord Homes has submitted a General Plan/zone change application on 3.18
acres involving 4 parcels located at 3201 S Redwood Road. The two west parcels are currently
zoned C-2 (General Commercial) with a General Plan designation of General Commercial. The
two east parcels are zoned R-1-12 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 12,000
square feet) with a General Plan designation of Low Density Residential (3 to 4 units/acre). The
proposed zone for all of the property except for 0.55 acres surrounding the Beto’s restaurant is
RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling Residential) and the proposed General Plan designation is Medium
Density Residential (7 to 12 units/acre). The zoning on the 0.55 acres surrounding Beto’s would
remain C-2 and the General Plan designation would remain General Commercial.

Surrounding zones include C-2 and RM to the north, R-1-12 and R-1-4 (Single Unit Dwelling
Residential, minimum lot size 4,000 square feet) to the east, R-1-12 and C-2 to the south, and C-
2 to the west. Neighboring uses include a strip mall and 3-story condos to the north; single

and Utah Barricade to the west. The property includes two homes, the Beto’s restaurant, and

The applicant is proposing to demolish all of the existing buildings except the Beto’s restaurant

In reviewing this application, staff suggests that there are two primary questions to answer:

   1. Is this the right location for a townhome development?
   2. Assuming the location is appropriate, does the quality of the project further achieve City
      goals?

These two questions are explored below.

Is this the right location for a townhome development?
According to the City’s zoning ordinance, zone changes to the RM zone can only be considered
by the City if the following four conditions are met:

   1. For properties without existing Structures which are proposed to be developed, the
      property must be a minimum of one acre and the Density must not exceed 12 units per
      acre. For properties with existing Structures proposed to be redeveloped, the property
      must be a minimum of 20,000 square feet and the Density must not exceed 20 units per
      acre. For properties that are west of Redwood Road and within one-half mile of the West
      Valley Central light rail station, the Decker Lake light rail station or the Redwood
      Junction light rail station, the Density may exceed 20 units per acre;

   2. The property shall meet any one of the following four requirements: (i) have access and
      Frontage on a Street with a planned Right-of-way width of at least 80 feet (for properties
      under two acres) or 100 feet (for properties two acres or larger) as indicated on the Major
      Street Plan; (ii) be adjacent to existing multifamily residential development on two sides;
      or (iii) be located west of Redwood Road and within one-half mile of the West Valley
      Central light rail station, the Decker Lake light rail station, or the Redwood Junction light
      rail station; or (iv) if the property is vacant, was vacant as of January 19, 2021, will be an
      exempt housing development under 24 CFR 100.304 or successor regulation with a
      density no greater than seven units per acre, and have access and Frontage on a Street
      with a planned Right-of-way width of at least 80 feet;

   3. A Development Agreement must be proposed with the zone change application that
      addresses Dwelling Unit sizes, exterior materials, architecture, Landscaping, and project
      Amenities; and

   4. A traffic impact study prepared by a registered traffic engineer is required for projects
      with 100 or more Dwelling Units.

Does the quality of the project further City goals?
City Code requires requests for the RM zone to be accompanied by a development agreement
proposal that addresses dwelling unit sizes, exterior materials, architecture, landscaping, and
project amenities. These items, along with other considerations, are addressed below.

Dwelling Unit Sizes
In the zoning ordinance, the minimum unit size for 3-bedroom townhomes is 1,400 square feet.
There are three proposed floor plans with sizes at 1,567, 1,673, and 1,698 square feet.

Exterior Materials
For materials, the City’s multi-unit residential design standards state: “All multi-unit residential
Building exteriors shall be brick, stucco, stone, tile, metal panels or fiber cement siding. With the
exception of brick or stone which may be used on the entire Building exterior, no single material
shall comprise more than 60 percent of a Building’s exterior. Where stucco, tile, metal panels or
fiber cement siding are used, at least 20 percent of the Building’s exterior shall be brick or stone.
Wood and vinyl may be used as trim, soffits and/or accent materials only.”

The applicant is proposing a combination of brick and fiber cement lap siding (horizontal) and
board and batten siding (vertical). According to the applicant, the percentage of brick meets the
20% requirements. While the fiber cement siding exceeds the 60% rule, there is a variety in color
and mixture of horizontal and vertical application and the City has granted an exception in such
cases.

Architecture
Based on a preliminary review of the proposed building architecture by staff, the proposed units
appear to meet many of the architectural requirements in the City’s multi-unit residential design
standards. Areas that fall short of the ordinance requirements are addressed below:
    • The ordinance requires at least 25% of the front façade to be fenestration (i.e. windows).
       Based on rough measurements from staff of the original submittal, the fenestration on the
       front was just under 17%. The applicant has since submitted an updated elevation with
       more windows. Staff is waiting on measurements from the applicant’s architect to verify
       compliance. This standard is easy to meet for townhomes with rear-load garages and
       more difficult to meet for front-load garages. The City has granted exceptions to this
       standard for front-loaded units; however, an exception is typically granted after the
       applicant shows that they have maximized the window space and there really aren’t
       practical ways to meet the standard.
    • The ordinance requires three window treatments with at least two window treatments per
       window on the front of buildings. Based on staff review, two treatments – varying the
       size and/or style of windows and window grids – were identified.
    • The ordinance limits the percentage of the front devoted to garage doors to a maximum
       of 60% of the length of the building. The garage door percentage is at 64%. In this case,
       adding more width to the units would likely result in the loss of units. The applicant is
       requesting an exception here due to the limited width of the property and the relatively
       low proposed density for a townhome development. To deemphasize the garages, the
       applicant is exploring changes to make the entrances more prominent.

Landscaping
The ordinance requires a total of 50% landscaping and recreation space. The applicant’s concept
shows 50.3%.

Project Amenities
For a project of this size, the ordinance requires one amenity. The proposed project includes one
amenity from the ordinance – a tot/lot playground. The applicant is also proposing privacy walls
along both Redwood Road and Lester Street and vinyl fencing along the north and south sides.

Garages/Parking
The ordinance requires all townhome units to include a 2-car garage. The overall parking
requirement for townhomes is 4.75 with 2 in a garage, 2 in a driveway, and an additional 0.75
within the project. All of the units meet the 4.75 stall requirement.

Other Considerations
Back in 2023, the owners of the Beto’s site requested a parking reduction as permitted in Section
7-9-104 of the Zoning Ordinance. The requested parking reduction, which included a concept
plan with proposed site modifications, was approved at 17 stalls (the approval letter is included
with this report). The development of the proposed project would require changes to the Beto’s
site that are possible because of the approved parking reduction. Staff recommends that the
development agreement includes a provision that the Beto’s site be modified substantially like

The original concept plan included 15 parking spaces along the west side of Lester Street that

this amount of parking backing onto Lester Street, which is a collector street. This concern was
