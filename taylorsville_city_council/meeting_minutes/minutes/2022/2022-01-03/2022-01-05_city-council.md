line of “Where Community Connects.” She hoped that the city would continue to foster
relationships with neighboring communities, local volunteers, the business community and the
state legislature.

3.      REPORTS

        3.1      Municipal Justice Court Report – Judge Christopher Bown

     Judge Christopher Bown presented some takeaways from his first seven months on the bench.
He loved his job and believed that those appearing before him felt heard. He invited anyone who
was interested in the judicial process to visit his courtroom. He explained to the council that he
was participating in public outreach at both Taylorsville and Kearns High Schools. There was also
a law student that would soon begin an internship at the justice court. He spoke very highly of the
court staff and bailiffs, indicating that they all made his job much easier. Additionally, he worked
closely with TVPD, the prosecution, private defense attorneys and Stowell & Crayk who provided
defense work for the indigent. He anticipated continuing to use Webex for some virtual
appearances although he mentioned that virtual trials continued to be challenging, particularly
when interpreters were necessary.

Chair Cochran asked if case numbers were increasing or decreasing. Judge Bown responded that
the time-to-file extension which was created due to the pandemic was still in place for small claims
cases and ODR (Online Dispute Resolution) had also been implemented since then, so it was
difficult to compare today’s numbers to pre-Covid numbers. Although traffic and criminal
adjudications were down in December, overall cases were increasing just slightly over what they
were when he was appointed to the bench in May 2021 and since TVPD was created.

Vice-Chair Barbieri asked Judge Bown to explain a little about his plans for outreach to local high
school students. He explained that he would present information about the judicial system, why it
was necessary, etc.

City Attorney Tracy Cowdell explained to the council that he had heard from many members of
the bar and heard nothing but very positive feedback regarding the job that Judge Bown was doing.

5.      PLANNING MATTERS

        5.1      Public Hearing – Consider adopting Resolution No. 22-05, approving the
                 Chantelle Neighborhood Parking Permit Area described as follows:
                 Approximately 2400 West to 2700 West and 4700 South to 4850 South in
                 Taylorsville, Utah – Ben White

       City Engineer Ben White appreciated the opportunity to provide some history and context
about a parking situation that had grown out of the large construction project involving the building
of the temple for the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day-Saints. There was off-site parking
available for construction workers but few of them used it. Instead, they were parking as close to
their work site as possible on the public roadways which presented challenges for nearby residents.
When snow began to fall, the problem was exacerbated because all the vehicles parked on the
street made it difficult for snowplows to move snow in the area. The project was expected to last
another year, so the proposal was to require permits to park in a very specific area only until shortly
after project completion.

Councilmember Harker asked where the employee parking site was. Okland Construction had
made arrangements for their workers to park at a nearby church and be bussed to the worksite.
The employees simply weren’t using the site and instead, were parking on the public roadways
which caused problems for neighbors in the area.

Vice Chair Barbieri opened the public hearing.

Guy Capener pointed out that his home had been heavily affected. He was unable to have guests
visit his home, the staff on garbage trucks had difficulty doing their job as did snowplow drivers.
He indicated he had to put his car in 4-wheel drive to get out of his street for four days recently.
Finally, he mentioned it was particularly challenging when deliveries of large construction
materials were made to the site. He had no problem with permits being issued and wished to
confirm that the permits would not be necessary after construction was complete. He felt that
having the workers park at the Ream’s store would be a good alternative.

Carl Thorup agreed that having construction workers parking at Ream’s was a good idea and
indicated that he might need over ten permits since he had eight children and often had family
gatherings or visitors. He was also concerned he might end up receiving a ticket for parking on
his own street. He was a bit concerned about giving the city power to fine him for parking on his
own street.

DeeAnn Capener wondered if permits would be enforced after hours and on weekends when the
construction workers were not present. Mr. White indicated he would visit with citizens after the
public hearing to address some of their more detailed questions.

Cari Lyn Hall explained that she lived next door to the temple under construction. She expressed
concern not just about the construction workers, but other utility works as well. When attempting
to return home from knee surgery recently, she had been unable to access her driveway and house
and had been asked to walk to her house which obviously was not possible under the
circumstances. Someone had also dug up her yard with no notice or explanation. She seemed in
favor of the permits.

Eric Buck said that Google fiber optics workers were also in the area, and he wondered when
things would return to normal. He stated that his yard and sidewalk were a mess, and the mailman
couldn’t get to his mailbox. He also wondered if there would be signs posted regarding the parking
permits and if parking would continue to be a problem once the temple was completed.

Lenora Buck expressed concern about equipment left overnight and on weekends. She also felt
that the holes that had been dug in front of her house were trip hazards for anyone walking after
dark. She indicated that these problems seemed to come primarily from the installation of Google
fiber, although a fire hydrant had recently been installed by another entity.

Larry Clark loved that the temple was being built because it was bringing new water lines to the
area, fiber optic connectivity, etc. However, he wished the loading and unloading of construction
materials at the temple would be managed better, as he felt that was the biggest problem. He
wished they would keep the loading/unloading on the same side of the street that the temple was
on. He was a contractor himself and stated that Park City had an ordinance in place which would
allow them to tow construction vehicles immediately if they were on the wrong side of the street.

No one else expressed a desire to make additional comments so Vice-Chair Barbieri declared the
public hearing closed.

Vice Chair Barbieri asked Mr. White to return to the podium and address some of the concerns
that had been voiced by the residents.

Mr. White explained that the Ream’s property was privately owned, so the city did not have the
authority to require they make their property available to the construction workers. He also
clarified that there was an end date already built into the resolution. Additionally, the new water
lines and Google fiber were unrelated to the temple. It just happened that the timing for the work
in that particular neighborhood coincided with the temple construction. He indicated it was up to
the Council and the police department to determine if the permit requirement would be enforced
24/7 or only during business hours. Realistically speaking, he understood that enforcement of this
permit requirement would not be at the top of the police department’s priority list. However, he
suspected that some initial enforcement up front would have a definite effect on the problem as
word spread. He did not feel that issuing mere warnings was going to be effective. He also pointed
out that there would likely be some unintended consequences of enacting the parking restriction
and was unsure if restricting the parking in this very specific area might just move the parking
problem outside that boundary. They would not know unless/until the resolution was approved. It
would take a little time but signage would be posted.

City Attorney Tracy Cowdell interjected that if a resident within the permitted area were to receive
a citation for parking without a visible permit, the administration had already asked that he have
the prosecutors dismiss it.

Wayne Harper, the Community/Economic Development Director wished to clarify that the
resolution anticipated the permits to be required through the week following temple dedication.

Chair Cochran felt there still needed to be some details fleshed out regarding some of the concerns
expressed by the public, especially the concern that privately owned tow companies would troll
the area and start enforcing the parking restriction themselves which one resident expressed
concern about.

City Administrator John Taylor explained that tow companies could not tow vehicles from public
roadways of their own volition. Perhaps a homeowner’s association might call a tow company for
their private property but that was completely different from the situation currently being
discussed.

Taylorsville Police Sgt. Scott Lloyd pointed out that the verbiage used on the signs could spell out
what time the ordinance was to be enforced and also indicated that it was only the City of

parking restrictions early on so that violators would understand that they needed to make a change.
Then a few weeks later the police department could do the same thing again. Finally, he suggested
that the city could issue permits for any vehicles registered at the address located within the
restricted area. That would keep people from taking advantage and requesting 10-15 permits that

A member of the public then began to respond directly to Sgt. Lloyd before Mr. Cowdell explained

Vice Chair Barbieri asked if the council needed to decide on the details of the parking restriction

Mr. Taylor pointed out that the current parking situation was very significant for those living in

be addressed.

        6.2      Public Hearing – To receive public comment regarding Resolution 22-04, a
                 resolution of the City of Taylorsville amending the Adopted Budget for the
                 2021-2022 fiscal year to incorporate mid-year budget adjustments.

      For the benefit of the public, Mr. Harrington explained some of the highlights of the budget
adjustments that were being recommended. City administration sought to add $5.2 million to the
budget, including the $2.5 million that the council had just voted to accept from the Governor’s
Office of Economic Opportunity. He anticipated an increase in sales tax revenue to the city based
on the current trend and recommended pulling $1.7 million from the general fund for the property
purchase on Atherton Way, the purchase having been approved at a previous council meeting.
Additional recommended expenditures included $180,000 for the Shop Local program which had
just been completed, $100,000 for crosswalk lighting, an additional $3,000 for the Earth Day event,
another $2,000 for Movies in the Park and food truck events, a wage adjustment for police officers,
$50,000 for the “Flip Your Strip” program to encourage waterwise landscaping and $10,000 for a
small economic development grant.
