The purpose of the Joint Work Session was to review the agenda for the City Council meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00
p.m.; discuss the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2023-2024 budget pertaining to the Community and Economic Development
Department, Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) projects, Fire Department and Medical Fund, and Fire Department and Medical
Fund CIP projects; discuss the proposed Redevelopment Agency (RDA) FY 2023-2024 budget; discuss Council/Board business;
and hear Council/Board comments.
Agenda Review
Chair Choberka and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council meeting
scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.
Proposed Fiscal Year 2023-2024 Budget
Community and Economic Development: Community and Economic Development Director Cooper used the aid of a
PowerPoint presentation to summarize the proposed FY24 budget for his Department. He first noted the purpose of the
Community and Economic Development Department is to contribute to the responsible and sustainable growth of Ogden City by
elevating the quality of life in all neighborhoods through the facilitation of a wide range of housing, employment, and wealth
creation opportunities. The Department essentially enhances prosperity by creating opportunity. The Community and Economic
Development Department staff are place makers, or local leaders who collectively use their influence, authority, creativity, and
abilities to create attractive, prosperous and safe communities; places where people want to live, work, learn, create, and do
business. The Community and Economic Development Department includes the following divisions: Business Development,
Community Development, Airport, Planning, Building Services, Arts, Culture, Events, and Redevelopment. He summarized the
measures that are used to define success of the Department, after which he reported indicators of success of the Department over
the past three years, including an increase in the total jobs in Ogden; annual average wage per worker in Ogden; and increased tax
revenue during a period of decreasing property tax rates. He then provided a graphic illustrating ‘what makes Ogden go ‘round’;
the cycle starts with the establishment of core values and vision that is driven by strong leadership and hiring people who share the
values. These people give great service to citizens, developers, visitors, and other customers. Those people invest and develop,
shop, and serve in Ogden, which generates revenue in the form of fees, sales tax, personal property tax, andreal property tax. This |
allows Ogden to invest in roads, parks, trails, fire, police, water, sewer, and quality of life. Mr. Cooper then reported on highlights
for his Department for FY23, cost cutting efforts, immediate challenges, long term challenges, and significant budget
topics/changes, including the proposed reclassification of a Museum Curator position to a Parking Coordinator position, which
will facilitate between various departments during the implementation of the paid parking program for downtown Ogden.

Fire Department and Medical Fund and Fire Department and Medical Fund Capital Improvement Projects: Fire
Chief Mathieu used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss accomplishments of his Department for FY23, cost savings
efforts, an update on employee retention, long and short-term challenges, and training requirements and demands on his personnel.
He then discussed demands proposed solutions for combatting challenges and demands, including reprogramming existing Fire
General Funding and adding new funding from the Medical Fund to create two additional personnel positions and funding support
costs. He expounded on the proposal, noting he would like to use a portion of Fire Administration funds to fund a step two
Captain with benefits, fund a step four Fire Fighter with a four percent training differential, and provide support costs from the
Medical Fund. The cost of the Fire Captain position is $133,617 and the cost of the Fire Fighter position is $97,843. Support costs
\ for the two positions total $54,925. He then discussed his new hire initiative, which includes a focus on hiring local, training local,
“and keeping local employees. Additionally, a proposed training division mission modification would provide initial fire training to
prepare prospective employees for Fire Fighter certifications and assist in hiring local people already residing in the community to
become Fire Fighters for Ogden Fire Department and he discussed the benefits of changing the new hire initiative. Chief Mathieu
then discussed Fire Department CIP projects for FY24. Fire CIP F1021, the Fire Facility Maintenance Replacement/Fire Medical
Project has two phases with a total projected cost of $3.9 million; the FY24 request is $2.3 million from the Medical Fund and
$300,000 of Business Depot Ogden (BDO) lease revenue. This CIP directly relates to plans to pursue training of local talent;
personnel can be trained at this state-of-the-art facility.
The Council discussed the CIP project and whether the Swift building site is the best location for it; Chief Mathieu
indicated that the location of the property is key for the Fire Department given that it is in the middle of the City and response
times from this area to all other points is the City can be reduced compared to using the training tower on west 12" Street. This led
to discussion among the Council, Chief Mathieu, and Community and Economic Development Director Cooper regarding other
viable uses of the Swift property and the pros and cons of other City owned properties for use as the training facility.
Chief Mathieu then discussed the City’s paramedic contract, noting the annual increase in calendar year 2023 was
$119,000; other notable budget increases in the FY24 budget when compared to FY23 are in the Medicaid Assessment line item
($34,000), Professional and Technical line item ($30,000), and Other Operating Supplies line item ($120,000). He also
summarized major equipment acquisitions, Information Technology (IT) improvements/investments, personnel changes that
occurred in FY23, and improvement in employee retention in the Department. There are currently eight open positions in the
Department, but the applicant pool is strong. He concluded that if the Department were required to reduce its budget by five
percent, he would reduce staffing since 80 percent of the Department’s budget is for personnel. This would result in a loss of 11.5
Fire Fighters, or four per day and would require him to close one Fire Station because it could not be adequately staffed. This
would increase response times by six to 10 minutes, increase employee turnover, and overload Fire Fighters with an
unmanageable workload. He concluded by touching on the Strategic Plan of the City, with a focus on community safety; goals of
the Fire Department for achieving and implementing the Strategic Plan include implementing Station One two-company
operation, replacing the training facility, and developing a local Fire Fighter certification program partnership. He thanked the
Council for their ongoing support of the Fire Department.
