         Ordinance No. 20-31 - Amending the Accessory Structure Ordinance Sections 18-
         225.030.3.ii, 18.225.030.5.i, and 18.225.040.3.a.b.c, Addressing Square Footage
         Requirements

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that the public hearing was being
held for a Zoning Text Change proposed by Riverton City. He said the Zone text change
involved two changes to Chapter 18.225 Accessory Structures. He said the ordinance currently
limited the total allowed square footage for accessory structures to 25% of the rear yard area,
as measured from the closest point of the main structure to the rear property line. He said this
was a difficult measurement for most property owners, and even contractors to make, and had
led to confusion as to the potential buildable square footage for some lots. In addition, it resulted
in a disparity between lots of the same overall size but with different back yard areas. He said
that Staff proposed amending the ordinance to set the maximum allowed accessory structure
square footage at 10% of the total lot area, which is a simpler and more intuitive measurement,
and will make the allowance more consistent across lot configurations. He said that percentage
also resulted in allowed square footages reasonably close to the average allowance under the
current 25% limitation.

Mr. Lethbridge said second change involved the lower end of the square footage requirements.
He said the ordinance currently allowed accessory structures at 120 square feet or less reduced
setback and architectural requirements. He said that number was intended to be consistent with
the threshold in the International Building Code at which a permit was required; however, the
IBC currently does not require a building permit for any accessory structure at or under 200
square foot. He said this had resulted in a situation where a property owner was not required to
get a building permit, and therefore, may not even inquire with the City regarding other
applicable standards, but was still, if over 120 square feet, required to meet exterior materials

consistent with the 200 square foot threshold in the IBC.

         Ordinance No. 20-32 - Text Amendment, Regulating Locating and Operating
         Food Trucks in Riverton City and Creating Section 18.230, Mobile Food Vendors,
         Amendments Proposed By Riverton City

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that Riverton City does not
currently have an ordinance specific to the operation of mobile food vendors, or “food trucks”.
He said the City was seeing an increase in operations of such vendors within the City, and in
response was proposing adoption of language regulating food trucks. He said this issue was
previously presented to the Commission; and, as a result of feedback from the Commission,
the draft was revised for clarity and simplicity. He said the proposed ordinance was modeled
from ordinances of communities similar to Riverton City, which allows the operation of food
trucks within the City, and establishes criteria for location and function. He said that food
trucks would not require special permitting or licensing, but would be required to have a
compliant business license. He said that one of the primary requirements for mobile food
vendors would be limitations on such uses to be temporary, which was addressed in Section
18.230.020 in the proposed text, with a limitation to the total number of calendar days and
hours a mobile food vendor can operate.

Mr. Lethbridge said that on November 12, 2020 the Planning Commission held a public hearing
to review the text amendment and they recommended approval of the ordinance as presented.
He then addressed comments and questions from the Council Members.

Mayor Staggs opened a Public Hearing and called for public comment.

John Scott asked for clarification regarding the 60 day limit and Mr. Lethbridge clarified that the
60 day limit was per truck.

Councilmember Stewart recommended a 90 day limit, which he said would better allow food
truck participation for three day weekends.

There being no further comments, Mayor Staggs declared the Public Hearing closed.

Discussion Item

         Ranked Choice Voting (RCV) Discussion and Proposed Resolution No. 20-80 -
         Requesting Municipal Election Services from Salt Lake County to Participate in
         the Municipal Alternate Voting Methods Pilot Project

Ryan Cater, City Attorney, explained that a letter to Salt Lake County Clerk Sherrie Swenson
regarding Ranked Choice Voting (RCV) had been requested to be placed on the agenda for
discussion. He said he prepared a proposed resolution that included components of the letter;
however, he also included a proposed letter to Ms. Swenson. He said that costs for the RCV
Election needed to be determined and, if chosen, a letter needed to be sent to the Lt. Governor
at the State Elections Office by April 15th. He said the intent of the letter was to express the
City’s interest in continuing to contract with Salt Lake County to conduct the City’s election

Councilmember Buroker spoke regarding RCV and said that it was designed to make more of
the will of the people heard, meaning that they want to make sure that at least 50% of those
voting are voting for the candidate that was going to win. She said that RCV was a pilot project,
something that could be tried and assessed. She said that she and Councilmember McCay
were both up for election in the upcoming 2021 municipal election and they were both willing to
join in the pilot project for that election.

Councilmember McCay also spoke regarding RCV and said she felt it would allow for more civil
campaigns and the cost would be cheaper. Shen then spoke of the April 15th date for a notice to
be delivered to the Lt. Governor.

Councilmember Wells said that he had done some research and education regarding RCV and
he stressed the importance and the cost of voter education. He also said that he wanted it to be
clear in the letter that the Riverton City Council was interested in exploring RCV and not
committed to it. Councilmember McCay clarified that the City Council could pull out of RCV
before April 15th if they decide not to participate.

Lengthy discussion occurred among the Councilmembers regarding RCV.

Councilmember McCay MOVED that the City Council change the resolution by removing,
in the Now therefore be it resolved section, No. 2 and adding onto the end of Point 1 by
sending a notice of intent to participate in Ranked Choice Voting to the office of the
Lieutenant Governor and Salt Lake County Clerk by December 31st. And then I move the
City Council approve Resolution No. 20-80 requesting staff to send notice of intent to
participate in Ranked Choice Voting to the Lieutenant Governor’s Office and Salt Lake
County Clerk’s Office by December 31st. Councilmember Buroker SECONDED the motion.
Mayor Staggs called for discussion on the motion; there being none, he called for a roll call vote
and the vote was as follows: Councilmembers Buroker-Yes, McCay-Yes, McDougal-Yes,
Stewart-Yes, and Wells-Yes. The motion passed unanimously.
