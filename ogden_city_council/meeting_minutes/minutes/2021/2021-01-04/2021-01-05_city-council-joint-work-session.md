        S.B. 6001 - Enacting §17C-1-416, Community Reinvestment Act
        Modifications
                 Council Executive Director Eller-Smith explained Senate Bill (SB) 6001 enacted by the Utah State Legislature authorizes
        the Redevelopment Agency to extend a project area collection period up to two years to compensate for either a delay in the
        implementation of the project area plan or reduced tax increment received from an impacted project area. This extension can be
        done unilaterally if the Agency makes certain fmdings.
                 Community and Economic Development Deputy Director Cooper explained an extension would not change the terms of
        the Area Plan and Budget relating to percentage of tax increment collected, the use of the funds, or the maximum amount that may
        be collected. It also does not automatically extend the payment of tax increment under existing participation agreements. An
        amendment to an existing participation agreement or a new agreement would be required to extend tax increment payments. There
        is currently no pending proposal from the Administration to extend any of the existing project areas. However, the Agency
        Executive Director and staff are evaluating the impacts of COVID-19 on the various project areas and may make a proposal for
        extension of one or more project areas if deemed appropriate and necessary. Ms. Eller-Smith stated that Administration has
        indicated there will likely be one or two areas for which they will make a recommendation at the conclusion of their evaluation; if
        the Council and Redevelopment Agency (RDA) Board are interested in granting that extension, the City must adopt a resolution
        allowing such. She stated she simply wanted to bring that to the Council's attention.

RAMP Project Review
          Council Policy Analyst Mabey explained each year the City Council provides the Administration with recommendations
and its priorities for project applications for Recreation, Arts, Museum, and Parks Projects (RAMP) grant funding. The
Administration takes these requests into consideration as it applies for RAMP funding. Since Fiscal Year (FY) 2013, the
Administration has included the proposed RAMP projects as part of the Capital Improvement Plan (CIP). However, not all
proposed RAMP projects are CIP projects as defmed by City ordinance. Currently proposed CIP ordinance amendments would
adjust the timeline and process for Council consideration of RAMP projects to be included in the Capital Improvement Plan,
which allows for Council approval of these projects prior to City applications for funding. The Administration has provided the
list of projects that it is seeking RAMP funding assistance for during the 2021 grant process.
          Public Services Department Deputy Director Anderson then presented the parks and recreation RAMP grant proposal for
FY21, after which Ogden City Arts, Culture, and Events Division Manager McBride discussed the RAMP grant proposals for the
arts and museum grant category.
          Council member White asked if it is necessary for the scope of various RAMP grant projects to be flexible given that it
may not be possible to proceed with a special event or other arts programming due to COVID-19. Ms. McBride answered yes;
typically, an entire grant amount must be spent in the year in which it was awarded, but RAMP guidelines have been amended to
provide some flexibility responsive to the pandemic. Project scopes have been written in a manner that observes the adjusted
guidelines.
          Discussion among the Council centered on RAMP grant application priorities and opportunities for the Council and
Administration to collaborate and develop a list of RAMP grant application projects that both sides can support.

Capital Improvement Plan Ordinance Amendments
          Council Policy Analyst Mabey explained the Council will discuss ordinance amendments to adjust the Capital
Improvement Plan (CIP) process to more closely align with the annual budget timeline, as well as to modify and update ordinance
language to adjust and clarify various components of the CIP Plan format, contents, and overall approach. This proposal is the
result of a collaborative effort between the Council and Administration, based on Council member requests to evaluate possible
changes to the CIP approach and process.
          Comptroller Stout summarized the proposed changes to the CIP process. Currently, a capital improvement project
includes, among other things, a project that is $30,000 or more and that proposes construction of an addition or major
rehabilitation of the City's physical plant. The $3 0,000 threshold has been in place for a significant period of time. In many cases,
this dollar threshold can be passed for maintenance projects that would not be considered a capital investment. Recent Capital
Improvement Plans have also contained very few projects in this range. For those reasons, it is proposed to change the dollar
threshold to $50,000. Further, Administration would like to defme proposed projects by project type:
               •    One-time: A one-time project is one that describes a discrete action which, once it is complete, can be declared
                    complete. These are the types of projects that may have different priorities in different years or that may be
                    removed from a future year's plan even if it was considered a priority at one time.
               • Perpetual: A perpetual project is one that is expected to require ongoing funding because the work is never
                    really complete. There will likely be a relatively small number of perpetual projects when compared to the
                    overall number of the projects in the plan. These types of projects would likely include things such as general
                    road reconstruction, sidewalk replacement and many of the master plan projects associated with city utilities.
               • RAMP: A RAMP project is a specific type of one-time project. The reason it is identified separately is that
                    these projects need to be presented to the Council at different times than other projects described in the Capital
                    Improvement Plan. For that reason, they are being classified separately from one-time projects.
          Additionally, Administration would like to include a project summary in the Plan document; this would be an
information section that identifies a summary of capital projects that have been funded by the city council as of December 31 of
the preceding fiscal year.
          The timing of CIP review and approval would also be adjusted. Rather than set a deadline for submitting the Capital
Improvement Plan to the City Council, it is proposed to have the Mayor submit the plan to the Planning Commission on or before
March 1st of each year. This would allow the Planning Commission to complete its review in April. After the Planning
Commission review, the projects would be ranked by the Administration and the plan would be transmitted to the Council for
further consideration. This timing will assist the Administration in developing projects and making ranking recommendations
closer to the time when the budget is approved, thereby avoiding challenges associated with changing circumstances that
occasionally arise if the rankings are made months earlier. This change to the submission process also allows the Council to
consider the plan as part of the budget review and adoption process. Also, there is a need to consider the option of submitting
RAMP projects directly to the City Council as development of RAMP projects create unique challenges. First, there is a cost share
component to RAMP grants that needs to be approved as a funding action by the City Council. Second, because of the differences
in fiscal years, RAMP grants are awarded in January which is in the middle of the city's fiscal year. It is unreasonable to expect
RAMP proposals to be included in the Capital Improvement Plan at the time it is adopted. This timing would require RAMP
proposals to be fully prepared almost a full year before the application is submitted. For those reasons, it is proposed that the
administration submit capital projects associated with RAMP projects directly to the City Council prior to the time the projects are

         submitted to the RAMP committee. This means that the Council will be considering RAMP projects in the last few months of the
         calendar year as an amendment to the already approved plan. If the RAMP projects are funded by the RAMP committee, they will
         also be included in the next fiscal year's plan as an existing approved project. RAMP projects will be reviewed by the Planning

                   Although one of the purposes of the Capital Improvement Plan is to serve as a planning tool, it is not uncommon for there
         to be some amendments to the plan. For example, the timing of RAMP applications does not fit in with plan approval process and
         these projects work better if they are approved in a plan amendment. There are two other types of amendments that may be made
         to an approved plan:
                        • Major Amendment- A major plan amendment is one that involves a significant change in scope to a previously
                             approved project or the adoption of an entirely new project. Despite best efforts at future planning, there are
                             times when a project may need to be approved mid-year. One example is when funding becomes available for a
                             project that had been contemplated but not yet included in the Capital Improvement Plan because funding
                             sources were unknown or unanticipated. A major plan amendment follows the same process as the regular CIP
                             with the project being reviewed by the Planning Commission and then going to the City Council.
                        • Minor Amendment- A minor project amendment is one that does not require Planning Commission review to
                             determine compliance with the City's General Plan, but that nevertheless may require an amended project brief
                             to be approved by the City Council. The reason no general plan review is needed is that these types of
                             amendments are made to an already approved project.
                   Many capital projects are closely linked with the growth and development of the City. The City General Plan includes
         information about planned streets, public buildings, and open space. The General Plan also may include implementation
         recommendations for these types offacilities. For that reason, Ogden City ordinance directs that the Capital Improvement Plan be
         submitted to the Plarming Commission to determine whether the projects included in the plan are consistent with the General Plan.
         One of the fundamental roles of the Plarming Commission is to develop and review changes to the General Plan. So, it makes
         sense for the Planning Commission to also review the consistency of capital projects with the General Plan. Occasionally the
         Planning Commission will fmd that a specific project is not consistent with the General Plan. In those cases, a change to that
         project may be required or the Council may be asked to look at amending the General Plan to allow for the project if such changes
         are appropriate for the neighborhood and the growth and changing needs of the City. The current ordinance essentially calls for all
    \    modifications to the Capital Improvement Plan or to a capital project to be reviewed by the Planning Commission. However,
    I)
/        given the purpose for which the Planning Commission conducts is review, this current language unnecessarily involves the
         Planning Commission in issues which are outside the scope of determining compliance with the General Plan. For that reason,
         much of the language dealing with the review and process of making amendments to the plan are being relocated to other sections
         of the ordinance to avoid ambiguities or confusion over the proper role of the Planning Commission.
                   Ms. Stout concluded by discussing the use of Business Depot Ogden (BDO) Lease Revenue and critical contingency
         funds; for a number of years, the Capital Improvement Plan has included a project known as the Critical Contingency project. This
         project is used to fund emergency repairs or unanticipated other critical repairs throughout the City. The nature of the activities
         funded by this project do not meet the defmition of a capital project because they do not necessarily involve extending the life of
         the City's physical plant- but they are more in the nature of repairs. This project has generally been funded in the amount of
         $100,000 per year. While having money available for this purpose is a wise and judicious use of City resources, the project itself
         doesn't really belong in the Capital Improvement Plan. Unlike a one-time project or a perpetual project, this money is not used for
         a specific forecast or as part of a master plan. For that reason, it is proposed that the section of City Code dealing with revenues
         from the operations of Business Depot Ogden be amended to create a direct budget line item that receives guaranteed armual
         funding for this need. By making this change, the Critical Contingency project can be removed from the plan but still receive
         ongoing funding.
                   Assistant City Attorney Stratford facilitated discussion among the Council regarding the implications of the proposed
         ordinance amendments, specifically how the ordinance will adjust the process by which Administration and the Council rank CIP
         and RAMP projects and how the updated process will interface with the annual budget process. There was a focus on the manner
         in which Administration will deal with any cost savings or cost overruns for a specific project included in the CIP; greater detail
         on the process for amending the CIP mid-year; and whether it is possible for the Council to force a project to be included in the
         CIP that may have not been a high priority for Administration.
                   Several Council members thanked Ms. Mabey for her careful review of this proposal from Administration. Ms. Mabey
         noted if there are no objections from the Council, staff will proceed with scheduling a public hearing regarding the proposed
         ordinance amendments for Januaty 19, 2021. No objections were voiced.
