Proposed Ordinance 2025-8 recognizing $1,000,000 in revenues from
Business Depot Ogden fund balance and appropriating those funds to the
Redevelopment Agency Quality Neighborhoods Infill Housing program
          A memo from the Management Services Department regarding a proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 budget
amendment came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the proposed amendment, totaling $1,000,000, will
provide additional funding for the Quality Neighborhoods Initiative (QNI). The Initiative was adopted in November 2015 for a
five-year period with the goals of 1) reducing the concentration of low-income or sub-standard housing in the East Central
neighborhood, 2) increasing the standards of maintenance for residential units, 3) increasing owner-occupancy rates, and 4)
increasing property values. The Initiative was renewed by resolution for an additional five-year period in FY21. This proposed
budget amendment brings forward the FY26 appropriation for the QNI to allow the City to take advantage of development
opportunities that are consistent with the QNI.
          Finance Director Sorensen summarized the memo and presented a spreadsheet detailing the source of funds for the
budget amendment.
          Chair White introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2025-8, entitled:
                   “An ordinance of Ogden City amending the budget for the Fiscal Year July 1, 2024 to June 30, 2025 by
                   increasing the anticipated revenues for a gross increase of $1,000,000 from sources as detailed in the body of
                   this ordinance; and increasing the appropriations for a gross increase of $1,000,000 as detailed in the body of
                   this ordinance; and by providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final
                   passage.”
          Chair White then opened the public hearing.
          Kerry Wayne stated the QNI has been underway for five years and the East Center neighborhood seems to be the focus of
the program; he wondered if the attention of the QNI could be focused on other areas of the City because there are many other
neighborhoods that could use some assistance.
          Heath Satow asked for clarification on the purpose of the funding; he asked if the money will be used to purchase the
Aspen Care Center property from the Weber Housing Authority (WHA).
          There being no further persons appearing to be heard, COUNCIL MEMBER RICHEY MOVED THE PUBLIC
HEARING BE CLOSED. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER HYER, ALL VOTING AYE.
          Council member Hyer addressed Mr. Wayne’s question; the QNI is not specifically anchored to a designated
neighborhood or area of the community. However, it is mostly used in the East Central neighborhood based upon need.
          Chair White addressed Mr. Satow’s question; the money will be located in the QNI fund and will be used to purchase the
Aspen Care Center property from WHA. Council member Richey added that the money was programmed to be included in the
next FY budget for the QNI but is being moved to the current FY for the purpose of acquiring the WHA property. Council
member Choberka clarified that the Mayor has proposed the funding be used to purchase the Aspen Care Center property, but that
action has not been ratified. Chair White stated that is correct; the Council will consider the proposal and take formal action next
Tuesday, March 11, 2025.
          Mayor Nadolski also addressed Mr. Wayne’s comments about the focus on the East Central neighborhood; he agreed
there are needs in other areas of the community and Administration is working on those areas as well.
          ON A MOTION BY COUNCIL MEMBER BLAIR AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER GRAF,
ORDINANCE 2025-8 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – COUNCIL

Public Comment.
          Vice Chair Hyer read the Council’s civility statement pertaining to public input. He indicated he believes Kerry Wayne’s
shirt violates the civility statement.
          Kerry Wayne addressed the proposed Weber Housing Authority (WHA) permanent housing project at the old Aspen
Care Center facility; the public sentiment is that the project is ‘dead’, but he does not believe there has been enough talk about the
heartache of homelessness. He asked the Council to think of their own children and the difficulty in affording a home in Ogden;
Ogden City has a well of resources that can be dedicated to homeless individuals, and it is not appropriate to shift them to another
area of the county. The services that homeless people need are centrally located in Ogden and the WHA could provide housing
and supervision of the people that would be housed at the proposed center. He asked the elected leaders of Ogden City to ‘have a
heart’ and help people get back on their feet in a proper way. The WHA has the funding secured for the project and he encouraged
the City to support them.
          Heath Satow referenced the civility statement and stated that out of mutual respect, the Council and Mayor need to be
perfectly clear with the public about what is happening in the City. He understands now, after asking his question during tonight’s
public hearing, that the $1 million in funding that was part of the budget opening will be used to purchase the Aspen Care Center
property, but he wished that had been mentioned as part of the presentation so that everyone knew what the Council was
considering. On the note of transparency, he did not hear mention during tonight’s work session that the City used leverage to
pursue State legislation that would have impacted the WHA project; that legislation has been cancelled now that the City has
made a deal with WHA. He does not feel the City is getting the transparency it was promised; if the County needed to be
manipulated by the City and the State Legislature in order to get them to participate in negotiations regarding the acquisition of the
property, the public should be informed of that by its leaders.
           Travis Pate stated he wishes to celebrate Women’s History Month, and he invited everyone to participate in the Art Stroll
on Fridays throughout the month of March and view the Women: The Creative Edge display. He then discussed historic
preservation and the role that many women have played in the City’s history; the Nine Rails Creative District was created by a
woman, but the City planning other developments there that deviate from the original plans. He asked the City to refocus.
           Marcus Carr stated he operates a non-profit association, Growing Unified Development, which focuses on education in
the community. He agreed with Mr. Wayne’s comments about the City’s ability to support homeless individuals in the
community. He received an email yesterday from a woman who is sleeping in her truck with her two young children and a dog,
and she needs some assistance; he is going to spend money out of his own pocket to help her. There is a serious problem in Ogden
and other communities, and it is heartbreaking to see children living on the street. He encouraged humanity on the part of the City
and community members; Ogden City needs to take care of its own and use resources appropriately to that end. He stated the
WHA project could house 25 individuals, but there are much more than 25 homeless individuals in the community in need of
support.
           Robert Hunter stated that the legislation that Mr. Satow referred to tonight would not have taken effect before May 7,
2025; it is important to understand that the WHA did not interpret that legislation as a form of leverage from Ogden City and it
could have gone forward with the proposal. He has learned over the course of his life and throughout different relationships that at
times it is important and necessary to gracefully accept a loss in order to have a win. The loss in this case is the inability to house
25 people in a timeline that was hoped for and the loss of $600,000 in American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funding for the project;
those things in are regrettable. He has been involved in government and non-profit organizations for 50 years and one thing that he
has seen that he does not like is battles between two different government entities. He knows the Council has not taken an official
vote on the matter, but after continued negotiations between the City, the County, and WHA, the new goal is to work together
going forward to find alternative locations in a collaborative and expeditious manner. The ‘win’ in this case is the hope that Ogden
City and Weber County will have a stronger relationship in the future. He thanked the Council for the many hours that they have
spent on the matter and wished them nothing but the best in the future. He is hopeful that Ogden City will also help other cities
understand that they have a responsibility to help house homeless individuals in the community.

Mayor Comments
         Mayor Nadolski asked Mr. Carr to visit with him after the meeting about the woman and her children who need
assistance; the City has homeless service advocates that can engage directly with homeless individuals to help find them housing.
He asked Mr. Wayne to consider that service as well as a means to respond to his plea for humanity on the part of the City. He
then thanked Mr. Hunter for his comments and stated that he has appreciated his input and participation over the course of
discussions of the Weber Housing Authority (WHA) project; he believes both parties have felt ‘backed into a corner’ and that has
been challenging for everyone. He has learned many lessons from this situation and looks forward to a positive relationship
between the City and County moving forward. He stated that Ogden City supports many shelters in the community and nearly 85
percent of the subsidized housing units in all of Weber County are located in Ogden City, even though the City represents just 34
percent of the County’s population. He is committed to doing more in terms of providing homeless support and it is incorrect and
untrue to state that Ogden City does not care. He thanked the Council for their patience and consideration of the position
Administration has taken regarding the WHA project.

Council member Comments
          Council member Blair commented on his recent tour of the Marshall White Center project and thanked the employees
that facilitated the tour. He is excited for the Center to open and indicated it will be a wonderful asset to the community.
          Council member Richey thanked Mayor Nadolski for his comments regarding the Weber Housing Authority (WHA)
project; it has been a significant issue for the community over the past few months. Many have made statements that indicate they
feel the City does not support the homeless population, but that is not true. He has thought about all of the things the City does on
its own and with other community partners to address homelessness, but it is still a problem, and the City does have an appetite to
try to help where possible.
          Council member Myers also thanked Mayor Nadolski for his leadership on the WHA project and Mr. Hunter for being a
great statesman. He also thanked Mr. Carr for the work he is doing in the community and commented that the City needs more
men like him.
          Vice Chair Hyer stated that he has always been perplexed by the number of people who stand at the microphone during
public comments and ask the City to do more. The WHA project did not follow the City’s housing policy and guiding principles,
but that does not mean the City intended to do any harm to the disadvantaged members of the community. He asked how many
residents are attending City Council meetings of neighboring cities to ask them to provide homeless support as well; other
communities have similar resources to Ogden City, but not the same demand for resources. It seems it is always Ogden that is
asked to house the homeless population and that is evidenced by the statistic provided by the Mayor regarding the ratio of
subsidized housing in Ogden compared to the City’s population.
         Chair White thanked Mayor Nadolski and other members of City Administration for working with Weber County and the
WHA on the permanent housing project; it is sometimes difficult to have honest conversations about hard subjects, but she was
appreciative of the Mayor’s willingness to provide information to the Council before sharing it with the public to ensure that both
branches of the City’s government were on the same page on the issue. She stated there are seven Council members, all of which
have different ideas and desires, and she commended them for working together to reach a decision. She thanked Mr. Hunter for
his leadership as well and for bringing all parties together for a meaningful conversation.
