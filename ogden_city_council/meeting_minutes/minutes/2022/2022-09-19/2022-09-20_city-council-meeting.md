Proposed Ordinance 2022-41 amending the zoning map to reclassify the
property located at 480 7th Street from R-1-6 to R-1-5

A memo from Community and Economic Development regarding the proposed rezone of property at 480 7" Street came
before the Council for consideration. The memo stated petitioners Mark Koehler, Ken Crockett, and Mike Bastian have filed a
petition to rezone the subject property, which is the site of a church that is no longer being used. The petitioners are seeking to

develop single-family homes on the site. The petitioners have indicated that the R-1-5 zoning, with its smaller setbacks and lot
size requirements, allows for a more efficient layout for the new subdivision that would eliminate remnant parcels or excessively
larger parcels.

Deputy Planning Manager Simpson summarized the memo and stated the subject area is addressed in the
Hillcrest/Bonneville Community Plan, adopted in 2004. Vision Strategy 6.A. discusses the support for and the preservation of the
single-family nature of the area:

“Support and preserve the existing single-family nature of the area through appropriate zoning classifications
(about five units per acre). Multi-family should be limited to existing developed areas within the community.
New multi-family should be directed near to and along 12th Street and Washington Boulevard under the
guidance of a corridor plan.”

In addition to the community plan support, the General Plan’s land use policies (Chapter 8, Section E) lay out best
practices when considering rezoning property. The Planning Commission report provides further detail on how the proposal meets
the applicable policies. However, in summary, the planning staff outlined how this proposal meets three goals, including: creating
a definite edge to a development and buffering between uses, reflecting the prevailing character of the district, and avoiding
having zoning boundaries cut across individual lots or developments. The R-1-5 and R-1-6 zones have similar purposes as smaller
lot single-family residential zoning classifications. The main difference between the two zones is minimum lot size, 5,000 square
feet for the R-1-5 and 6,000 square feet for the R-1-6, and slightly different setback requirements. The Planning Commission
report details the differences and provides an analysis of surrounding lots. The R-1-5 zoning would not be inconsistent with many
of the existing lots in the area. The Planning Commission reviewed the proposal at its July 6, 2022 meeting and forwarded a
recommendation of approval with a vote of 6-0. The Planning Commission’s recommendation was made with the finding that the
proposed rezone will provide a definite edge to development and buffering between types of uses, will reflect the prevailing
character of the overall district, will not cut across lots or developments, and that it is supported in the Hillcrest/Bonneville
Community Plan.

Council member Hyer stated he is supportive of the zone change as he feels that it will provide the developer with the
opportunity to mirror the existing development patterns in the area. He also asked if the developer is willing to consider similar
building/design standards so that the new project will blend in with its surroundings. Mr. Simpson stated he has not seen any
building plans for the project, but the applicants have indicated a willingness to pursue traditional building styles to make the
project harmonious with the surrounding neighborhoods.

Council member Richey stated there are many mature trees on the subject property and he is hopeful they can be
preserved as part of the development. Mr. Simpson stated that if an existing tree can reasonably be preserved, that will be done in
accordance with City Code.

Chair Nadolski invited input from the applicant. Mike Bastian, the applicant, stated he had nothing to add to Mr.
Simpson’s presentation.

Chair Nadolski introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2022-41, entitled:

“An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the zoning map of Ogden City, as adopted by Section 15-3-3 of
the Ogden Municipal Code, to reclassify as single-family residential zone (R- 1-5) property heretofore classified
as single-family residential zone (R-1-6), generally located at 480 7" Street, and providing that this ordinance
shall become effective immediately upon posting after final passage.”

A copy of the proposed ordinance was deposited with the Deputy City Recorder and ordered that the City Recorder have
at least one copy available for public inspection in her office during all business hours.

No persons came forward to be heard regarding this matter.

Proposed Ordinance 2022-42 amending Ogden Municipal Code to allow
metal siding for accessory structures over 400 square feet and on less than
one-half acre

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Director regarding a proposed zone text amendment relating
to metal accessory buildings came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Mike Miller has submitted a petition to
allow non-architectural metal sided accessory buildings over 400 square feet on lots smaller than one-half acre. The current
ordinance allows the case-by-case review and approval of accessory buildings larger than 400 square feet if they are sided with
architectural metal or non-architectural metals on lots larger than one-half acre. The ordinance does not allow an accessory
building over 400 square feet made with non-architectural metals on lots smaller than one-half acre. Mr. Miller applied for a

permit to replace a wooden sided one-car garage with a larger two-car, metal sided garage and was denied based on the current
ordinance. Mr. Miller has filed a petition to amend the ordinance to allow the type of garage he is requesting.

Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and explained the Planning Commission reviewed the proposal at its
June |, 2022 meeting and forwarded a recommendation of approval to the Council. The Commission’s recommendation was

Chair Nadolski invited input from the applicant.

Mike Miller stated that he feels the proposed text amendment will make it easier for Ogden residents living on smaller
lots to build a lasting garage on their property; metal building products have been improved dramatically over the past several
years, but it is still a less expensive product than typical building products.

Chair Nadolski introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2022-42, entitled:

“An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Ogden Municipal Code by amending Section 15-13-33.C to
allow metal siding for accessory structures when over 400 square feet and on less than one-half acre; and by
providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final passage.”

A copy of the proposed ordinance was deposited with the Deputy City Recorder and ordered that the City Recorder have
at least one copy available for public inspection in her office during all business hours.

Chair Nadolski then called for public input regarding the proposed ordinance.

Angel Castillo recalled a previous discussion about this matter during which Council member Hyer voiced concern about
certain metal building products and asked for consistency in the City’s building codes. She stated she agrees it is important that
the City Code contains a very solid description of what will and will not be allowed. It is also appropriate to include language
regarding anything that would trigger an amendment to the regulations. This will help applicants to navigate the approval process
in a faster manner.

There were no additional persons appearing to be heard.

Council member Hyer agreed with Ms. Castillo that it is important for the City’s ordinances to contain clear language so
that the reader can easily understand the types of building materials they can use on an accessory structure. He also believes that if
an ordinance is descriptive enough and an applicant can clearly prove that their application complies with the ordinance, staff
should be able to approve the application rather than submit it to the Planning Commission. Mr. Brierley stated that if the Council
wishes to change the approval authority for this type of application, it would be best for Planning staff to have time to work with
Council staff to make that change to the proposed ordinance. Deputy Executive Director Symes agreed that is one option, but
given that the text amendment is applicant driven, another option before the Council is to approve the current ordinance and
pursue an amendment to the approval authority through a future text amendment. Mr. Brierley stated it would not take more than
two weeks for Planning staff to adjust the proposed ordinance to change the approval authority. Mr. Symes stated that if that is the
route the Council chooses to take, he would recommend extending this agenda item rather than tabling it.

Chair Nadolski asked for additional input from the applicant.

Mr. Miller stated that he hired a contractor for his project eight months ago and he prefers the option that is the speediest
as he would like to move forward with his project as soon as possible.

Council member White stated she prefers to vote upon the most appropriate ordinance the first time, but she
acknowledged Mr. Miller’s desire to move forward with his project. She asked if there is any way to grant Mr. Miller the ability to
proceed with his project prior to taking final action on the proposed ordinance. Mr. Brierley stated there is no real option for
granting Mr. Miller a variance to proceed with his project before adoption of the ordinance. Chief Administrative Officer Johnson
stated that if the Council desires to amend the proposed ordinance to change the approval authority to staff, the process will
actually be quicker for Mr. Miller because as soon as the ordinance is adopted by the Council, Planning staff can act upon the
application. Mr. Symes stated he feels Council staff can work with Planning staff to adjust the ordinance responsive to the
feedback from the Council and be prepared to present it to the Council during their October 11 meeting for final action.

COUNCIL MEMBER HYER MOVED TO TABLE PROPOSED ORDINANCE 2022-42 UNTIL THE

Public Comments

Angel Castillo thanked Council staff for providing the public with the opportunity to participate in open meetings of the
Council via electronic means. She referenced tonight’s work session discussion regarding new market tax credits. There seems to
have been a breakdown in communication regarding the City’s ability to secure new market tax credits; though they are difficult
to get, she believes that the housing development around the Marshall White Center (MWC) would qualify. She was contacted by
the president of the Community Development Finance Alliance (CDFA), Amy Rowland, who indicated that she wants to put the
MWC project in her portfolio; if her portfolio is not big enough to finance 85 percent of the project, she would partner with
another CDFA to finance that amount. She does not know why Ogden City has not reached out to Ms. Rowland, though she has
forwarded her contact information to City Administration. This is a company that is willing to give the city new market tax credits
and she is disappointed that the City has not followed up on that opportunity.
