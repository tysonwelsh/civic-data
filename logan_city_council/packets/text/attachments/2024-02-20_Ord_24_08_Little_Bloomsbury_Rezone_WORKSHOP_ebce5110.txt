PUBLIC HEARING:

PC 24-006 Little Bloomsbury Inn & Gallery [Conditional Use Permit & Zone Change] Little Bloomsbury
Inn & Gallery/Brenda C Sun Tr, authorized agent/owner is requesting a zone change of the .13-acre
parcel located at 181 North 200 East; TIN 06-066-0023, from Traditional Neighborhood Residential (NR-6)
to Town Center Two (TC-2) and a Conditional Use Permit to operate a 3-bedroom hotel and a small art
gallery in the existing structure (Adams Neighborhood).

STAFF: Russ Holley, Planner reviewed the request to rezone a .13-acre parcel from Traditional
Neighborhood Residential (NR-6) to Town Center Two (TC-2) and a Conditional Use Permit for a 3-room
boutique-style hotel located at 100 North 200 East.  The Future Land Use Plan (FLUP) identifies this
property as Town Center (TC) zone.  Most of this block is zoned Town Center Two (TC-2) except the
seven properties along 200 East that are Traditional Neighborhood Residential (NR-6), the block is a mix
of commercial and residential use.

The proposed property has an existing permit and business license to operate a 3-bedroom short-term
rental.  The owner is applying for a new Conditional Use Permit to rent out the three rooms to three
separate parties.  The Short-Term Rental License only allows renting to one party at a time.

The Land Development Code requires hotel parking to have 1 parking stall per room plus 4 additional
parking spaces for employees and guests.  This parking requirement does not account for a 3-room
boutique-style hotel.  The Land Development Code also allows parking requirements to be waived in the
Town Center zone when a commercial building changes use and renovates within the existing walls.

The alternate parking plan submitted showing 5 parking stalls on-site, behind the home is not functional.
As conditioned with 3 required parking stalls, the project complies with the Land Development Code.

Commissioner Peterson asked about the canal setback if a culvert is installed.  R. Holley said that the
setback would most likely be reduced or eliminated if the canal was culverted.

PROPONENT: Sun spoke about the history of the home, the proposed project, and the neighboring
properties.

Commissioner Lewis asked if there would be any exterior additions or modifications done to the home.
Sun said there will not be any exterior additions, only an ADA ramp added for access.


[page 5]

PUBLIC: Matthew Fatuesi spoke about the housing crisis and this project not benefiting the needs of the
community.

Leah Adkins who is associated with the Little Bloomsbury Art Foundation said the proposed project is well
thought out and will benefit the community.

COMMISSION:

Commissioner Guth asked Russ Holley to address the comment on the housing crisis. R. Holley said that
the Town Center Two (TC-2) zone allows for multi-family high-density housing as well as hotels, and
restaurants.  A property owner has the right to build what they want on their property if the zoning allows
for it.

Commissioner Heare asked Russ to go over the parking options.  R. Holley said that the commission can
require the project to meet the full parking requirement in the Land Development Code for hotel use (one
parking stall per room plus four extra), seven parking stalls, waive all parking requirements assuming the
property will be rezoned to Town Center Two (TC-2) (if the property is not rezoned the proposed project is
null and void), or require an alternate parking plan.  Staff felt that the gallery portion of the project is such
a variable, and with the available street parking, designated parking stalls are not needed for the gallery.
Condition number 3 requires three parking stalls to accommodate the three hotel rooms.

Commissioner Peterson said that 3 parking stalls are reasonable for the proposed use.

Commissioner Lewis said he has concerns with rezoning just this property within the existing strip of
Traditional Neighborhood Residential (NR-6) properties along 200 East, it feels like a spot zone.  All
seven properties along 200 East should be rezoned together.  Commissioner Lewis also spoke about the
proposed home being a contributing structure to the Historic District.

Commissioner Guth said this would not be a spot zone because the proposed zone is contiguous with the
TC-2 zone to the west.

MOTION: Commissioner Heare made a motion to forward a recommendation of approval to the
Municipal Council on a zone change and conditionally approve a conditional use permit for PC 24-006
with the conditions of approval and the findings for approval as listed below.  Commissioner Peterson
seconded the motion.

PROJECT
The proponent is requesting to rezone the 0.13-acre property at 181 North 200 East from NR-6
to TC-2. The applicant is planning a small 3-room boutique hotel and sculpture walk for the
property. If the rezone is successful, a Conditional Use Permit will need to be obtained to legally
establish the commercial use. The property is currently a single-family home, built in 1909, with
a detached garage in the rear yard. An open-ditch irrigation canal traverses the property flowing
northward and essentially bisects the parcel with the home on the front section and the garage
on the rear section.

GENERAL PLAN
The Future Land Use Plan (FLUP), adopted in 2008, identifies this property as Town Center
(TC). The General Plan, a nonregulatory visioning plan, describes TC areas as being a hub of
the City and Valley and reflects a vibrant downtown, with a variety of commercial, mixed-use,
and institutionally uses and activities.  The TC designation covers land from 200 South to 500
North and staggers between 100 East and 200 East along the eastern side of Downtown Logan.
The TC designation staggers between 100 and 150 West along the west side of Downtown.

Land Development Code (LDC) - Zoning
The TC-2, which encourages dense, walkable commercial and residential development in the
downtown area with unique and interesting building designs. This zone allows a density of up of
to 30 units per acre along with a mixture of commercial uses. The City went through a deliberate
and robust process in 2018 to redefine the Town Center zoning districts which culminated in the
adoption of Ordinance Number 18-07. This year-long zoning process established TC-2 in the
outer-ring areas of downtown as a way to buffer or transition the residential neighborhoods
further east and west of Downtown. The goal of the zoning strategy adopted in 2018 was to
further implement the visions expressed in the General Plan through a methodical transition of
uses and structure.

Site
The proposed 3-room hotel would be within the current walls of the home and essentially
convert the existing bedrooms to hotel rooms. The conversion will require numerous building
updates to comply with current commercial building codes. The site will need to be redeveloped
to accommodate the required parking stalls associated with the commercial uses and the
required open space and usable outdoor space.

Parking Requirements
The LDC requires 1.0 parking stalls per hotel room plus 4 additional stalls. The additional stalls
are likely for employee's and additional guests. The gallery or outdoor sculpture garden land
use is not specifically addressed in the parking cade and thus would be required to go through
the alternative parking plan process. The applicant has submitted an alternative parking plan

Useable Outdoor Space and Landscaping
The LDC 17.10.070 requires 10% landscaping and an additional 10% useable outdoor space in
the TC-2 zone. The LDC 17.32 generally describes useable outdoor space as outdoor areas
that are designed and used in conjunction with a primary use for the benefit and enjoyment of
the residents and their guests of the development. Useable outdoor space may include public
gathering areas, patios, decks, walkways, plaza's, active recreational areas, and natural open
space areas but do not include parking lots, driveways, and other similar vehicular oriented
areas. Landscaping is defined as planted and natural areas contained trees, shrubs, grasses,
mulches, and other similar materials.

The LDC requires a minimum of 20 trees and a combination of 50 shrubs, flowers and
ornamental grasses per acre of land for multi-family residential projects. The LDC also requires
minimum perimeter and interior parking lot landscaping to reduce the visual and environmental
impacts of asphalt parking lots. At 0.13 acres, 1,132 SF of open space, 2 trees and 6
shrubs/flowers/grasses are required.

STATE OF UTAH CONDITIONAL USE STANDARDS
(a) (i) A land use authority shall approve a conditional use if reasonable conditions are
        proposed, or can be imposed, to mitigate the reasonably anticipated detrimental effects
        of the proposed use in accordance with applicable standards.
    (ii) The requirement described in Subsection (2)(a)(i) to reasonably mitigate anticipated
        detrimental effects of the proposed conditional use does not require elimination of the
        detrimental effects.
(b) If a land use authority proposes reasonable conditions on a proposed conditional use, the
    land use authority shall ensure that the conditions are stated on the record and reasonably
    relate to mitigating the anticipated detrimental effects of the proposed use.
(c) If the reasonably anticipated detrimental effects of a proposed conditional use cannot be
    substantially mitigated by the proposal or the imposition of reasonable conditions to
    achieve compliance with applicable standards, the land use authority may deny the
    conditional use.


SUMMARY
Given the FLUP designation of TC and the current commercial land uses on the block, the
request to up-zone this parcel to TC-2 would be a predictable and expected zoning change.
With the LDS Temple located to the east of the block with extensive setbacks, TC-2
development and land uses on the subject property and the remaining 200 East block face
would be compatible and pose minimal impacts to the residential areas further east. Being a
core development proposal, infrastructure and utilities can be more efficiently utilized as
compared to outer fringe areas. The hotel use will have parking and traffic impacts and possibly
noises during late hours as guest come and go. Being semi-residential in nature, hotel uses
should be quiet once guest are settled into their rooms for nearby residential uses. Parking can
be entirely waive based on TC zoning allowances or the alternative parking plan can be
accepted which uses a combination of on-site and off-site public lots within 600 feet to satisfy
parking demands.
