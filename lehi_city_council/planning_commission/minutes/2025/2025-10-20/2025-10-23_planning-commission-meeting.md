3.3) Public hearing and recommendation of Gardner Company’s request for review of the
Skye Area Plan Amendment, creating some design changes to the General Nonresidential
Design Requirements in the Mixed-Use areas.

Jacob Curtis presented the item. This amendment would add two design exceptions. The first is
for drive aisles, which would allow drive-thrus adjacent to Center Street, Traverse
Terrace, 500 W, and Timpanogos Highway. This would minimize the number of drive-thru
applications that come before the Planning Commission. The second exception is for the glass
location on buildings. They are requesting the flexibility to not provide 20% glass on the façade
facing the public street. They would prefer the glazing to be on the front or side elevation if it
faces the private street. The 20% glass requirement would still be met. Both exceptions are due
to slope issues in the area.

Commissioner Kunze asked where the 20% glass standard came from. Curtis said it was likely
from the city design standards. He explained that staff is looking into where the figure is coming
from.

Mark Murdock was present, representing Gardner Company, along with his colleagues Dave
Denison and Chase. They are trying to push pedestrian traffic off of Center Street. He showed
how backs of buildings are difficult to see due to the grading, thus the desire to move the glazing
to other sides of the building.

Commissioner Kunze asked about the plan for the green space. Murdock explained that they
want to install a path with a lot of connectivity. They will also have bike access and pickleball
courts nearby. The trail will end at a trailhead.

Commissioner Jones clarified that the glass and drive thrus are only shifting due to grading
issues. Murdock confirmed this.

Commissioner Lockhart opened and closed the public comment period.

                Motion: Commissioner Jones moved to recommend approval to the Lehi City
                Council with the findings that the proposed area plan amendment allows
                flexibility with the design requirements and will preserve the intent of the design
                requirements, and that it will provide a quality community in Lehi City. He
                included all DRC comments. Commissioner Kunze seconded.

                Vote: Commissioner Lockhart, yes. Commissioner Kunze, yes. Commissioner
                Jones, yes.

                Motion passed unanimously.

3.4) Public hearing and recommendation of the Downtown Revitalization Plan and State
Street Station Area Plan including parts of Main Street and State Street.

Jacob Curtis presented the item. He said that the engineering department would like to table the
item for a later meeting but would still like to hold public hearing. This plan is a guiding document,
not a regulatory one. It establishes a vision for downtown Lehi. Curtis turned the time over to the
co-applicant.

Sam Taylor, the consultant with Landmark Design, was present online. He gave a brief overview
of the plan. It is an intentionally visionary plan that looks 20+ years into the future. The goal is to
implement it through incremental change so that adjustments can be made as development occurs.
As the plan is an overview, many details and specifics are not present. Taylor shared the eight
guiding principles of the plan which were chosen based on public input. He discussed the different
districts that the plan focused on, the main ones being the Mills, the Downtown Core, and the
Station Area.

The goals for the downtown core include making Main Street more walkable and organizing more
public open spaces to encourage foot traffic. Taylor emphasizes that the city will need to
compromise and cooperate with potential and current business owners in order to encourage
development. The goal for the Mills district is to encourage financial investment and development
through allowing mixed-use zoning. This would also help walkability. Additionally, Taylor
recommends loosening design standards to encourage more development in these already
developed areas.

Commissioner Kunze acknowledged that there will be change in Lehi regardless of how people
feel about it, and she appreciates having a plan to ensure the change is good for the city. She asked
how the Mills district would be connected to the Downtown Core. Taylor explained that the plan

was to make more pedestrian infrastructure down Main Street, but that much of the area between
the districts would likely stay the same in the current iteration of the plan.

The goals for the Station Area district are a little more difficult, as they need to be coordinated
with UDOT and UTA. The city is currently unsure what method of transportation will be
implemented. Taylor said that in the meantime increasing transportation connectivity, expanding
utilities, and creating street safety improvements for pedestrians are goals. Commissioner Lockhart
asked if the plan still hinges on Union Pacific being willing to move their railroad turnaround. That
is still the case. Commissioner Lockhart asked if there was a backup plan for the area. Taylor said
that it would be difficult to get development to happen at all in the area if the turnaround is not
removed, as there will be issues with getting emergency access to the area.

Some changes have occurred since the plan was last presented in a joint work session with the
Lehi City Council. The commercial overlay on 100 E has been removed due to concerns from
Commissioners and Councilors. A massing graphic has been added to the Mills District section.
The possibility of Light Rail Transit (LRT) has been added to the Station Area District section.
Language encouraging homeownership and language encouraging coordination, analysis, and
monitoring of transportation improvements has been added. Additionally, reallocation of
residential unit caps in the Station Area and Mills Districts is now the recommendation rather than
removal.

The draft has been put up for the public to review, and responses are being incorporated. Most
respondents feel that the plan is meeting most of its stated goals. There are concerns over
inadequate reduction of traffic and improvements of parking, as well as concerns over context-
sensitive housing. Respondents are very supportive of open spaces, parks, and community
amenities and gathering places.

Commissioner Kunze asked if public input was still open and how it is being advertised so
residents are aware of it. It is still open. Jacob Curtis explained that fliers have been handed out to
businesses, posts have been made on Facebook and on Engage Lehi. Business owners have also
been emailed. Commissioner Kunze worried about overwhelming respondents with a document
over 100 pages and asked if it would be possible to make a condensed version for public awareness.

The next steps for the plan involve incorporating public feedback and the remaining feedback from
DRC.

Jacob Curtis mentioned that tabling the item tonight will allow for another few weeks to get as
many people involved in the feedback process as possible. Commissioner Jones said that he needs
more feedback before he feels comfortable hearing the proposal.

Commissioner Lockhart opened and closed the public comment period and noted that there would
be an additional public hearing on November 13th.

Commissioner Kunze shared that she thinks that people are worried about massive changes but
explained that the plan is far-reaching and meant to be incrementally implemented.
