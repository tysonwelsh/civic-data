         Farm Animals on Small Lots - Councilmember Stewart

Councilmember Stewart explained he had received a complaint about pygmy goats in his district
and wanted to review the ordinance that Riverton City currently has adopted. He asked Ryan
Carter to give an update.

Ryan Carter, City Attorney, said that officers were sent out to the property in question and found
too many chickens on the property. The property owners also had pygmy goats. He explained
that Riverton City’s ordinance requires at least two pygmy goats and the ordinance does not
give direction as to what lot sizes can have goats. He suggested that the council look at what
qualifications a property should have to allow goats on the property as well as how far from
neighboring property the goats could be kept.

Councilmember Stewart talked about setbacks on different size properties and how this related
to how far the goats would be from another resident’s house.

Councilmember McDougal asked the council if farm animals should be allowed on small lot
sizes.

Councilmember Stewart asked what the requirement is for chickens.

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, said that there is a 10,000 square foot
minimum for chickens.

Councilmember McDougal said that he would support an ordinance that required setback to
have goats and that he personally does not believe we should have pygmy goats on small lots.

Councilmember Stewart also supported a setback to allow distance from the neighboring home.

         Discussion Regarding Riverton Personnel Policies and Procedures Manual - David
         Brickey, City Manager and Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director

David Brickey, City Manager, explained that employees that retire are given 10% of their yearly
salary upon retirement. He asked if the council would want to change this policy.

Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, said that this policy was approved in October
2011. There are two parts of the policy, employees retiring receive 10% of their sick leave and
10% of their yearly salary. He analyzed the costs over the past 10 years and noticed that it is
not a significant amount of money. He asked if the council would like to keep the current
structure or add a years of service requirement in order to get the 10% salary payout.

Councilmember Stewart said there should be a 5 or 10 year requirement for the 10% of the
salary payout. He said he did not have an issue with the sick leave policy.

Councilmember Wells said that he would also like to have a years of service requirement.

Councilmember McDougal said that he would like a years of service requirement or to do away
with the 10% salary payout and increase the sick pay percentage.

Councilmember Buroker said she would like the 10% salary payout to be tied to years of
service.

Public Hearing(s) / Action Items

      Ordinance No. 21-02 - Rezoning 8.32 Acres at 12761 S 3600 W from C-PO & M-1 to
      C-PO/HOV (Professional Office with Elderly Housing Overlay), Ben Oveson
      Representing DEJ Holdings, Applicant (Tabled from CCM 01-05-21)

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director said that Ordinance No. 21-02 was previously
presented to city council in January. The property is 8.32 acres and is north of Stampin’ Up. He
explained that the property is currently zoned professional office and the general plan is
business park and light manufacturing. He said the applicant has proposed zoning this property
professional office with elderly housing overlay. He said that most of the public comments were
concerned with the number of units per acre which is a maximum of 20. He explained that the
city council could lower that figure and that the applicant has proposed 10 units per acre. He
said that the Planning commission recommended denying this rezone. Mr. Lethbridge
explained some possible SD designations that the council could include in a motion to approve
this application.

Ben Oveson, Real Estate Director of Rocky Mountain Care and applicant for the rezone,
displayed a drawing that showed how the property could look if he was granted this rezone. He
explained that Riverton has a shortage of senior housing options especially compared to
surrounding cities.

Lengthy discussion was held among the Councilmembers, Mr. Lethbridge, Mr. Carter and Ben
Oveson, the applicant. Councilmembers expressed their concerns regarding access to and
traffic on Water Road, density and number of units allowed.

Mayor Pro Tempore Buroker opened a Public Hearing and called for public comment.

John Lindley provided public comment. He is opposed to the rezone because he believes the
density is too high for this area and feels like the council should not change the general plan
because they relied on this plan when they purchased their homes.

Stacy Woolley provided public comment. She has lived in Riverton for 25 years and feels
strongly that the density for this area is too high.

David Magelby provided public comment. His primary concern is traffic on Water Street and
does not want an access point to Water Street.

Preston Fueling provided public comment. He said that he is opposed to high density housing
and also traffic on Water Street.

Adam Allred provided public comment. He said that he was opposed to high density housing
and does not want any access to Water Street.

Mayor Pro Tempore Buroker closed the Public Hearing.

February 16, 2021



Ben Oveson said that he had spent a lot of time talking to neighbors and tried to incorporate the
feedback he received into his plans. He requested that the council rezone to include 10 units
per acre.

Councilmember Buroker explained that these decisions are very difficult and encouraged
residents to continue to come and share their comments.

Councilmember McDougal said that the process for rezoning does not include a site plan. He
explained that the applicant cannot bring a final project for approval until the zoning is approved.
He believes the density is the biggest concern and that the site plan should be developed to
have the least impact on traffic in this area.

Councilmember Stewart explained that he wanted to mitigate the impact of this rezone and
thought that an elderly housing development would be a good option because it wouldn’t burden
schools and would bring great residents to the community.

Councilmember Wells believes that we should rely on the general plan because it provides an
assurance to residents when they buy land and believes there should be input when there is a
change proposed.

Councilmember McCay clarified to Councilmember Wells that the units per acre can be
determined by the council. The developer said originally that he was looking at 6 to 8 units per
acre. She believes that housing would be a better option than industrial in this area.

Councilmember McDougal said that it is the council’s job to give this applicant a zoning
designation. He thinks that a housing development would increase the value of the homes in
this area and believes that they could put a restriction on the project that would protect Water
Street.

Councilmember McDougal MOVED that the City Council approve Ordinance No. 21-02, an
ordinance rezoning 8.32 acres at 12761 South 3600 West from C-PO & M-1 to C-
PO/EHOV/SD (Professional Office with Elderly Housing Overlay with specific
development designations as follows:
     1. Density shall be limited to six (6) units per acre.
     2. No more than 4 attached units per building.
     3. Units shall maintain residential character with attached garages and pitched
        roofs, with maximum height of 35 feet.
     4. The project shall be age restricted in compliance with all applicable laws to
        minimum of 80% at 55 or older.
     5. No vehicular access to 12600 South or to the adjacent office/care facility site
        shall be allowed.
     6. Units shall not front onto Water Street, and no individual driveway access onto
        Water Street shall be allowed.
Councilmember Stewart SECONDED the motion. Mayor Pro Tempore Buroker called for
discussion on the motion; there being none, she called for a roll call vote and the vote was as
follows: Councilmembers Buroker-Yes, McCay-No, McDougal-Yes, Stewart-Yes, and Wells-
Yes. The motion passed 4 to 1.
