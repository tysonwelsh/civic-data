Business License Fee

              Council Member Pazell stated he would like to put forth a motion for a resolution
to reduce the business license fee to zero. Copperton is losing money on business licenses.
However, if businesses stay in Copperton, the community will make money on sales tax. He
proposed eliminating the annual renewals and having only a one-time fee. Renewals could be
every three years.

                   Mayor Clayton stated two years ago the state legislature passed SB 91, making
it illegal for cities to collect fees on home-based businesses. He recommended changing the
name from “business license” to “business listing.” Businesses that require inspections will pay
for the inspections themselves. Businesses should be identified as being in Copperton so that
Copperton can collect the sales tax. He will review the information with Brittany Allen, Salt Lake
County Planning & Development Services Division, and create a business website.

                Council Member Pazell, seconded by Council Member Bailey, moved to approve
the resolution including the following amendments: If an inspection is required, the business
owner will pay for the inspection costs. Business owners will notify Copperton Metro Township
that it is doing business in Copperton. The name will be changed from “business license” to
“business listing.” Roll was called, showing the vote to be: Council Member Bailey voting “Aye,”
Council Member Patrick voting “Aye,” Council Member Severson voting “Aye,” Mayor Clayton
voting “Aye,” and Council Member Pazell voting “Aye.” The motion passed unanimously.

               Council Member Pazell, seconded by Council Member Patrick, moved to make the
changes effective as of January 1, 2019. Roll was called, showing the vote to be: Council
Member Bailey voting “Aye,” Council Member Patrick voting “Aye,” Council Member Severson
voting “Aye,” Mayor Clayton voting “Aye,” and Council Member Pazell voting “Aye.” The motion
passed unanimously.

ORDINANCE NO. 18-07-01                                                       Date: July 18, 2018

       APPROVING AMENDMENTS TO THE COPPERTON METRO TOWNSHIP’S
       RULES OF ORDER AND PROCEDURE TO ACCOUNT FOR LEGISLATIVE
       CHANGES AND CHANGES IN PRACTICE

       BE IT KNOWN AND REMEMBERED THAT, the Copperton Metro Township Council held
a regular public meeting on July 18, 2018.

         WHEREAS, the Copperton Metro Township (“Copperton”) is a metro township and
political subdivision of the State of Utah; and

      WHEREAS, the Copperton Metro Township Council (“Council”) approved Rules of Order,
Procedure, and Governance (“Rules”) on June 7, 2017, which it amended on December 20, 2017;
and

        WHEREAS, the Council has adopted and amended the Rules with the understanding that
additional changes will be needed as the Council and the Township, as well as the rules and laws
by which they are governed, become more settled; and

       WHEREAS, the Council desires to amend the rules of order to:

   1. Account for S.B. 175, which the Legislature passed to rename the chairs of all metro
      townships as “mayors” effective May 8, 2018; and

   2. Designate the Council Chair/Mayor as the Council member responsible for preparing the
      agenda for Council meetings and issuing notices for Council meetings; and

   3. Authorize the Council to make routine expenditures up to $2,000.00 without prior Council
      approval, subject to subsequent Council review and approval; and

   4. Consistent with prior Council action on December 20, 2017, increase the gross
      compensation limit for Council members to $20,000.00 per year; and

   5. To make the Council’s independent audit a discretionary rather than mandatory
      requirement; and

   6. To make changes to conform with the decision by Salt Lake County and other relevant
      authorities that metro townships have authority to own and operate their own bank
      accounts; and

   7. To update the ethics and nepotism section of the Rules; and

   8. To make changes to conform to the Council’s current practices.

       WHEREAS, the Council have incorporated the above changes into the attached version

Future of Unified Police Department (UPD)

                Council Member Pazell reported that Riverton City will likely vote to leave UPD.
He reached out to Bart Barker, General Manager, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District
(GSLMSD) to get information about leaving the Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area
(SLVLESA) but not UPD. It can be done, but all tax revenue is aggregated to the GSLMSD. The
problem is that Kearns and Magna spend more than they bring in, so Copperton helps subsidize
those townships. Therefore, the GSLMSD could not afford another contract. There is nothing in
the law preventing Copperton from breaking away from SLVLESA and creating its own taxing
entity. His preference is to stay with UPD.

               Chief Jason Mazuran, UPD, stated he just learned of the Riverton development
and is not sure how that will play out. UPD will continue to take care of business and work with
Copperton. UPD is still working to create an auxiliary precinct in Copperton.

              Council Member Pazell noted the UPD finance committee is looking at creating
new formulas for pooled services.

              Chief Mazuran stated the new administration at UPD is responsive and getting
things done. This week UPD will be interviewing applicants for the Chief Financial Officer job.

             Under Sherriff Jake Peterson stated he was the precinct chief in Riverton up until
19 days ago. He came to tonight’s meeting to hear what people had to say.

              Vern Winkler stated he has lived here over 40 years and never seen the police
presence in Copperton that he has seen in the last few months.

Historical Preservation Plan

            Update on public outreach plan

            Council comments/feedback from the draft ordinance from the Planning
             Commission

               Debora Riddle stated she needed feedback on how the Council would like to
proceed.

                Nathan Bracken stated he is looking at this from a legal perspective. Any policy
should be clear and easy to defend. If the Council wanted to be able to initiate an application for
a Historic Preservation Area or should that only be initiated by residents. If the Council can apply
for historic designation, then the Council would also be the automatic appeal authority. That is
not a good idea. The Council could appoint a hearing officer to handle the appeals.

              Mayor Clayton stated he favored letting the community take the lead on this. He
asked who typically qualified to be a hearing officer.

               Mr. Bracken stated typically the hearing officer is an attorney hired by the Council.

               Mayor Clayton stated he would like to see a high bar for approval, such as 75
percent of area residents in favor the historic designation.

              Council Member Pazell stated this sounds good on paper, but he is concerned
about imposing additional regulations and entrenching things. He is opposed to this.

                  Mr. Bracken stated he will make changes to the draft ordinance and to the
outreach material. He will take out all mention of Council driven historic designations and make
it entirely citizen driven.

              Mayor Clayton stated he would also like a sunset provision of 10 years on the
ordinance. A decision can be made after community feedback during Town Days.

              Council Member Pazell stated the information will be available at Town Days and
then the Council will hold a public hearing.
