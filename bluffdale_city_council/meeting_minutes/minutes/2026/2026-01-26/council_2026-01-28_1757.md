4.2 Presentation of the Police Department Annual Statistical Report. (Presenter,

Chief Andrew Burton).
Police Chief, Andrew Burton, introduced Assistant Police Chief, Bill Robertson, Lieutenant Zach
Robinson, and Sergeant Carl Hamer. He then presented the Annual Report.
Chief Burton reported that the Saratoga Springs/Bluffdale Police Department does an excellent job of
conducting in-service and specialized training. Lieutenant Robinson oversees that training and
ensures that the department meets all State requirements. For the last several years, they held a
weeklong basic SWAT course to train new officers. In 2026, they will be running it alongside the
regular SWAT in-service training.

They were involved in many special events, including City, community, and school events in 2025.
There were also some retirements and resignations, but they were replaced with amazing new officers.
The police department handled 12,875 calls for service in Bluffdale in 2025, only 23 of which required
the use or show of force. Every use of force is documented and reviewed by the Use of Force Review
Board, and none of those cases were found to be excessive or out of policy. No formal citizen
complaints were received, which is a testament to the City’s police officers.
Chief Burton reported that the response time goal of seven minutes or less on priority calls was met
for the first time in 2025, with an average of six minutes, 37 seconds. Emergency call response times
are faster.
Patrol officers’ time is obligated in handling calls, administrative matters, and community support
issues, all of which are easily measured in numbers but not in time spent. For example, a juvenile
problem call might take 30 minutes today or three hours tomorrow, depending on the circumstances.
Officers also need some unobligated time to do things like monitor school zones and neighborhood
speed complaints.
Chief Burton reviewed calls for service and incidents from 2016 to 2025. In some years, there were
more than 1,000 calls for service per officer. That threshold is the point at which there is no
unobligated time. Calls above 900 per officer per year increase officer safety concerns. Calls for
service increased by 20% in 2025. Based on trends, he recommended bringing on at least two more
patrol officers at the supervisor or administrative level. Incidents are calls for service that require a
report. These calls also take longer.
Areas of concern for Bluffdale include:

e Assaults, weapons offenses, and threats have increased by 15%

e Domestic violence has increased by 10%

e Traffic offenses, accidents, and parking have also increased considerably.

e Citations decreased by 40% in 2024 but increased by 63% in 2025.

e Accidents increased by 2%.

e Arrests increased by 30%.

Council Member Lord asked if there were any incidents where officers were in danger. Chief Burton
reported that most use-of-force situations are due to the person resisting arrest or assaulting officers.
Domestic violence calls are hazardous, as are assaults, weapons offenses, and threats. No officers
were injured during an arrest.
“Part One” crime in Bluffdale is very low. These are serious crimes that are reported to the Federal
Bureau of Investigation. In 2025, 2.8% of incidents were Part One crimes, in comparison to 2.2% in
2024.
Calls for service and accident heat maps were reviewed. Most calls are in the higher density areas,
with approximately 65% in apartment complexes. Most traffic citations are issued on main corridors
and in school zones.

| Chief Burton reported that the police department’s staff is its greatest strength. Both its officers and
support staff are top-notch. Emerging strengths include their technical capabilities and finding ways
to improve citizen interaction. They are working to improve their response to mental health issues as
well as internal capabilities to deal with emotional issues officers may experience. They are also
starting to use artificial intelligence and now have a program that transcribes body camera footage.
The Saratoga Springs/Bluffdale Police Department is one of the few accredited agencies in the state
and strives to continue to be a leading agency. The biggest threats to that goal are officer safety and
failing to grow at the correct pace.
Chief Burton reported that in 2025, a patrol officer discovered a suspicious vehicle. Upon
investigation, a deceased individual was found in the rear of the car. It was determined to be a
homicide. Other case highlights were included in the Annual Report.
In response to a question raised by Council Member Austin, Mayor Hall clarified that the report was

Council Member Smith asked if data was available on specific neighborhoods and case type by

neighborhood. Chief Burton stated that he will ask the analyst for that information. The Spillman

system that they use is located on a Utah County server, and the chiefs of police have voted to move

it into the dispatch center. There will then be better access to the data and departmental information

technology specialists will be able to create more detailed maps.

Council Member Smith asked about Chief Burton’s experience with deep fakes and cyberbullying in

schools and what the community’s leaders can do to address that problem. Chief Burton stated that

they are in the process of developing capabilities to address it technologically, but it is important for
schools and families to pay attention to their children’s devices. If they see something, they should

report it so it can be investigated. They are trying to keep up with the rapidly evolving technology

via both in-house and external means.

Council Member Lord stated that over the years, he has heard about Neighborhood Watch and other

ways communities monitor for potential problems. He asked if Chief Burton had any

recommendations on similar things that can be promoted in the community. Chief Burton stated

that Neighborhood Watch is a good concept that is now enhanced by things like Ring doorbells and

other surveillance tools. In response to a follow-up question, he reported that there has not been a

significant uptick in incidents since Smith’s Grocery opened. Sergeant Hamer reported that there

have been traffic-related issues only.

9. Staff Reports, Additional Council Discussion, and Calendaring Items.
City Manager, Bruce Kartchner, reported that the bid for the Parks Department administrative
building expansion had been received. There is enough money in the existing budget to cover one of
the two restrooms. They believe they have identified enough money in the Capital Projects Fund to
complete the second restroom. Ifthe Council is in favor of doing so, he will bring a budget adjustment
to that effect to the next meeting. It will increase the project cost by $250,000 to $300,000.
Mayor Hall stated that when the rodeo arena was renovated, the bathroom cost was so high that the
Council decided to wait and see if it would be more cost-effective to install it with the administrative
building project. Staff has identified available money in the Capital Projects Fund for the second
bathroom. Doing both bathrooms at once will save money because the crew will already be on-site
doing the work. Ifit is not completed in conjunction with the administration building, it will need to
be rebid at a later date and will incur a higher cost.
Council Member Lord asked if a bond would be required for the project. Mr. Kartchner clarified that
Park Impact Fee monies had been set aside for the projects, but that fund does not have enough money
to cover the second restroom. The administration building funding had already been approved. Staff
recommended that the second bathroom be installed at the same time, but the administration building
expansion would move forward regardless.
In response to a question raised by Council Member Smith, Mr. Kartchner reported that the original
bid for one restroom was over $600,000. The current low bid is approximately $320,000 each for
two.
Council Member Austin asked where the funds had previously been allocated. Mr. Kartchner reported
that the City had previously built the rodeo grounds and budgeted that project based on a grant. The
fiscal year 2025 budget included actual costs, and the grant money was not received until the current
