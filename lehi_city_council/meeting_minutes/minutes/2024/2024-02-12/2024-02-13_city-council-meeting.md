5. Citizen Survey Results with Y2 Analytics
    Representatives from Y2Analystics presented the annual survey results. Overall, residents
   expressed concerns about the effects of growth on traffic, housing availability, and overall
   quality of life. While fire and emergency medical services remain highly regarded, ratings for
   snow removal have decreased significantly compared to the previous year. Traffic, housing, and
   water quality are identified as top priorities in managing the city's growth.

   Residents did express positive sentiment towards the location, amenities, and quality of life but
   show decreased trust in city staff and officials, particularly regarding growth management.
   While overall satisfaction with city services remains, there is a decline in perceived value.

   Mayor Johnson reflected on the challenges of managing growth, particularly on the east side of
   Lehi where there is limited land for development. While residents prioritize housing for future
   generations and emphasize the importance of walkability and bike ability, concerns about
   regional growth impacts remain prevalent.

The Visioning Exercise was skipped due to time constraints.

6. Financial Update
   Dean Lundell, Finance Director, provided economic data and budget considerations for the
   council's review. He noted that the state is forecasting a 3.8% growth in sales tax for 2024, with
   low unemployment and a stable economy seen as positive indicators.

   Mr. Lundell also discussed Utah's property tax laws and their impact on residents, explaining
   how property taxes are calculated based on relative value and not just home appreciation. He
   highlighted the challenges of property tax assessments, especially in areas experiencing rapid
   growth, and that property tax laws do not account for inflation.

   He also presented on utility rates and funding, with discussions on the financial health of the
   water and sewer utilities. He highlighted the need for rate increases to fund projects and ensure
   sustainability.

   Additionally, there was discussion of the Public Power Adjustment Charge (PPAC), designed
   to offset market fluctuations in power costs.

7. Alpine School District Update
   Stacy Bateman and Joylin Lincoln, Alpine School District Board members, distributed the
   Annual Financial Report. They noted that the school board examined enrollment trends, staffing
   needs, and ongoing capital projects, emphasizing taxpayer transparency and the breakdown of
   fund revenues.

   They also addressed the potential for a split in the school district. While Lehi has the largest tax
   base, it faces challenges in securing Title One funding due to its student demographics.
   Discussions ensued regarding the process for initiating a split in the district, with various
   pathways outlined for consideration. They stated that the reconfiguration proposal garnered
   some support from those who saw it as a viable alternative following the failure of a previous
   bond.

   School safety measures were also a focal point, with discussions on threat assessments, internet
   safety, and staffing levels to ensure student well-being.

   Moving forward, the district plans to conduct a study to assess the impact of property tax
   increases and determine the best approach for the reconfiguration proposal. Additionally,
   ongoing capital projects, including school rebuilds and new construction, were highlighted as
   part of the district's commitment to addressing infrastructure needs.

    fee schedule for the upcoming budget year.

13. Consideration of Ordinance #08-2024 a Development Code Amendment to Table 37.080,
    Off-Street Parking requirements, removing a proximity requirement for townhomes
    adjacent to on-street parking.
    Mr. Struthers stated that they are trying to be more flexible with the location of the required
    parking stalls. He noted that this will not decrease the number of parking stalls. Concerns were
    raised about guest parking and how this could negatively impact the residents. Proposals
    ranged from increasing the parking distance to allowing exceptions on a case-by-case basis,
    and which legislative body would make that decision. It was also suggested that a parking
    study be required to ensure the best parking locations.

    Motion:     Councilor Condie moved to deny Ordinance #08-2024 and direct staff to propose a
                new recommendation that if an application is going to request an exception to the
                proximity requirements that they can work with staff and do a parking study that
                would show the justification for the exception and that the Council be the final land
                use authority to grant the exception. The Planning Commission may review.
                Councilor Newall seconded the motion.

    Roll Call Vote: Councilor Albrecht, Yes; Councilor Condie, Yes; Councilor Hancock, Yes;
    Councilor Newall, Yes; and Councilor Stallings, Yes. The motion passed unanimously.
