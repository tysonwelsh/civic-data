Chair Sargent called the meeting of the Eastern Summit County Planning Commission 
to order at 6:00 PM.   
 
1.  Public input for items not on the agenda or pending applications.   
 
The Public hearing was opened. No comment was made, and the Public hearing was closed. 
 
2.  Public hearing - Cedar Crest Village Overlay Committee and Staff will provide an 
update and lead a discussion with the Commission on a Draft Land Plan for Cedar 
Crest Village consisting of a mix of residential, commercial, civic, community, 
recreation, and other uses on approximately 1,000 acres in the East Hoytsville area. 
Public input is welcomed and encouraged. Cedar Crest Village Overlay Committee, 
representing applicants. – Kirsten Whetstone, ACIP, County Planner; Peter Barnes, 
Director of Planning & Zoning and Design; Patrick Putt, Community Development 
Director 
 
Director Putt stated that this meeting is for the pubic. Staff is looking for initial public 
input on the concept use plan for Cedar Crest. The Village Overlay Zone (VOZ) is an owner-
initiated, owner-created land plan for potential rezonings, created by landowners via

committee. A decision will not be made tonight about the rezone. There will be more public 
comment opportunities. The Land Plan is not a development plan but is a concept of how 
the village could be created. Any development would have to have the ability to provide 
infrastructure and meet development standards. 
Planner Whetstone shared the background for this application. The purpose of this 
meeting is to hold a public hearing and receive community input, and to schedule regular 
meeting dates for work sessions and public hearings. Staff has provided support to the 
Overlay Committee. There will eventually be a draft Village Plan Document and Planner 
Whetstone outlined what the Village Plan will look like. 
Commissioners Wilde, Sonntag, and Clyde are on the Cedar Crest Village subcommittee.  
Commissioner Wilde stated that the Village Overlay Zone is an interesting concept and 
hopes that the process will help to get better sewer and water in the area. Traffic is a big 
concern. A lot of good could come from this VOZ, but the density is a concern. 
Commissioner Clyde stated that the VOZ changes the way we look at growth on the East 
side of the County. So far, we have been seeing small developments done one at a time that 
are not regional or centrally planned. The smaller developments cannot afford amenities. 
Commissioner Clyde is leery of growth in general, but the population will double whether 
we like it or not. The VOZ is an opportunity to get ahead of the infrastructure problems. 
Commissioner Sonntag stated that current development has problems. The solution is to 
cluster development in appropriate village locations. Proper density needs to be created to 
provide development opportunities and sustainability solutions.

The Public hearing was opened.  
Robert Williams stated that the plan is sweeping in view. How many acres are reserved 
for Open Space? The plan would allow 5,000 units (between 10,000 to 15,000 people), 
which is larger than Coalville City. Very few Hoytsville landowners are included in the 
application. It is very expensive to do a minor subdivision. Are the taxpayers footing the 
bill? This is a huge change that should be delayed until after COVID. 
Todd Hardy stated that he agrees with Robert Williams and Bill Wilde. Density of one acre 
to five units with an average household of four people has the potential for 20,000 
residents in an area that currently has 500 residents. This is exponential growth. The 
suggested density is not a good idea. Why should the proposed density be allowed when 
everyone else can only have one unit per acre? Heber has a population of 15,000. This 
much density would kill the small-town feel. Chair Sargent stated that the density is not a 
final number and will be modified. 
Gaye Stoner and Dick Stoner stated they agree with Commissioner Clyde that it is great to 
plan a major subdivision, but not in addition to the minor subdivisions that are ongoing. 
Density is being created. Development rights could be transferred from surrounding areas. 
The Village Overlay Zone is a very small area. Buildable land gets focused into a high 
concentration, which causes concerns about gray water and emissions. The density will 
completely change the character of the area and is irreversible. Hopefully, the County can 
manage an economic win-win. 
Gale Pace stated that he has heard there will be a freeway interchange at Creamery Lane, 
and he is concerned about fire protection, water, and sewer.

Clark Staley stated that he owns the only home on the north side of Creamery Lane. Most 
of the proposed density would be right behind his house, including all of the affordable 
housing. There is less density proposed on the East side of the VOZ. More roads and more 
traffic will be put in off Creamery Lane. Creamery Lane is not wide enough for more traffic. 
The suggested density would create another Salt Lake City and create an economically 
divided community. How will the economic divide be addressed? 
Mary Chapel stated she supports landowner rights. What is the current infrastructure in 
Hoytsville? What infrastructure would it take to support the proposed density? 
Kent Pace asked for clarification of the shared map. Planner Whetstone stated that a 
concept plan has not been done for the Eastern portion of the VOZ. Areas constrained for 
development are areas that will be recreation or Open Space. The Village Residential area 
will typically have smaller homes. The Village Core will be more mixed use. 
Debbie Lineager stated she agrees with other comments made. Creamery Lane would 
have to be widened. There would be a huge impact on the school system. The proposed 
density would change the look of the community. 
Director Putt stated that the request of the applicants of overall density would equal about 
5,000 units of density. This is not a final number. There are limiting factors, such as 
physical geography and the ability to provide infrastructure. The General Plan and 
Development Code have fairly broad design codes. Discussions include access for an 
interchange at Creamery Lane as a possibility, but no specific commitments have been 
made. The developer or property owners would be required to pay for infrastructure. 
UDOT could pay for an interchange, but a plan is required for the State to prioritize the

interchange. The Village Overlay is created by an owner-initiated process. Other 
landowners cannot be forced into the study area. Landowners may be added into or drop 
out of the study area. Staff is attempting to recognize that decisions being made will impact 
landowners not included in the study area. 
One value of the VOZ is the ability to have a diversity of housing types. The existing area is 
served by private wells and a community system. There is no community wastewater. The 
costs would be borne by the property owners or developers within the planning area. 
The County Council has created the ability to ask for density through the VOZ process, 
which is overall a more comprehensive approach to development to provide more 
amenities with an idealized plan with broader infrastructure. The VOZ is built into the Code 
as a Staff-involved elective process. The advantage to the taxpayers is to limit the back-and-
forth wasteful Staff time and be more proactive with a better design. 
The North Summit School District has been approached by the subcommittee but has not 
shared any input. Staff continues to work through concerns including evaluation of water 
and wastewater. Active participation of the community with valuable input is needed. 
TDR (transfer of development rights) is to move the ability to develop property to another 
location. It would be best to fix the zoning from the outset by visualizing where 
development should occur. TDR might work outside of the planning area and move into the 
planning area, focusing on lands within the boundary. The plan proposes sixty percent 
Open Space. 
Summit County is currently going through an infrastructure study for Eastern Summit 
County to discover what infrastructure is currently available and what the future capacity

Clay Vernon stated there are many residents currently on septic systems. Would those

local knowledge to create a generational plan that is stretched over a long time. The 
infrastructure and school impact will be phased over time.

Chair Sargent stated he was shocked at the proposed density. The VOZ was a good idea as

communication. We have been trained to fight against rezoning and development. The 
difference here is community involvement of landowners and the broader community to 
think of the idealized future community. We wish we could keep things the way they are 
forever, but the community will grow and change. We have the opportunity to plan what
