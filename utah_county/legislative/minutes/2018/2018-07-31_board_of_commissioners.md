14.   ADOPT A RESOLUTION OF THE BOARD OF COMMISSIONERS OF UTAH COUNTY TO
      OPPOSE THE CREATION OF A UTAH COUNTY NEEDLE EXCHANGE PROGRAM
(Track A, 46:55)
UTAH COUNTY BOARD OF COMMISSIONERS
MINUTES – July 31, 2018
Page 9

Commissioner Lee commented on the opioid crisis in Utah County, explaining there has been much community
involvement in how to reduce drug use. He noted a desire to continue the progress by removing a ‘needle exchange’
option from the discussions. Commissioner Lee stated it would be detrimental for the community, and Commissioner
Graves provided statistics for increased drug use in communities which provide a needle exchange program. When
Commissioner Graves cited Fox News as a source for the extensive cost of cleaning up syringes in San Francisco,
Commissioner Ivie pointed out the information directly contradicts findings by the National Institute of Health in
which 87% of needles are properly disposed in the same area. Cities with a needle exchange program also have eight
times less syringe use than those without the option. Commissioner Ivie suggested further research be conducted to
determine how it could help the community, pointing out other states have seen significant reductions in the spread of
Hepatitis C, HIV, and other blood-borne communicable diseases. Commissioner Ivie stated he supports a ‘one-for-
one’ needle exchange so as not to increase syringes in the community while reducing overall harm.

(Track A, 50:35)
Former State Representative Chris Herrod came forward to speak in favor of the resolution opposing a County needle
exchange program. He shared a story of being in Europe and having his 2-year-old son pick up a used syringe in a
park and advised against allowing that possibility here. Mr. Herrod noted the private industry can claim responsibility
for such a program if it proves to be beneficial.

Richard Nance, Director of the Utah County Department of Drug and Alcohol Prevention and Treatment, expressed
it is premature for the Board to take this action at this time. He pointed out suggestions for a County needle exchange
program are not for the government to create a program: there are multiple non-profit organizations in the state already
working with Salt Lake and other counties on this issue. Mentioning an upcoming town hall meeting, Mr. Nance
asked the commissioners to wait for additional public input as a needle exchange program can prevent the elevation
of the opioid crisis. He explained his department would never bring something before the Board that is not rooted in
science and research; many of the fears related to needle exchange programs are simply untrue. Mr. Nance reiterated
the idea is not to use county taxpayer monies for such a program, but to at least allow consideration of all options until
after the town hall meeting. He emphasized “the problem we have now could get much worse” and pointed out drug
use is no longer only a criminal issue but now a public health concern.

The commissioners discussed their individual reasons for supporting or opposing the proposed resolution, and the
following motion was made.

            Commissioner Lee made the motion to adopt a resolution opposing the creation
            of a Utah County Needle Exchange Program as discussed in Regular Agenda Item
            No. 14. The motion was seconded by Commissioner Graves and carried with the
            following vote:

1.    FOURTH QUARTER CENT SALES TAX PRESENTATION BY ANDREW JACKSON AND MATT
      DUGDALE
(Track B, 01:05)
Andrew Jackson, Executive Director of Mountainland Association of Governments and acting (until on or before
November 1, 2018) Utah Transit Authority Board of Trustees member, presented documents (copies of which can be
found attached to these minutes) detailing the purpose and use for a proposed Fourth Quarter Cent Sales Tax. Matt
Dugdale of George K. Baum and Company summarized the bonds, anticipated revenue, and reduced debts.

(Track B, 46:40)
Commissioner Ivie invited public comments and Utah County Public Works Director Richard Nielson was the first to
come forward. He explained the county works with the Mountainland Association of Governments to determine
which transportation projects are pursued; the most recent process included $100,000,000 of requests with only
$40,000,000 available in funds. Mr. Nielson pointed out the county receives approximately $3,000,000 in ‘B’ road
monies annually but would need closer to $9,000,000 for ideal road management. He noted the twenty percent
designation for the county is dependent on the county approval of the tax. If the tax is approved by the cities but not
the county, only the cities would be eligible for those funds.

(Track B, 48:45)
Saratoga Springs resident and President of ‘Americans for Prosperity – Utah’ Heather Williamson commented on tax
increases in general being a bad idea. She noted the decision should be voted on by the community. Provo resident
Pam Jones pointed out each time a taxing entity decides they want more money to spend, they take it from the citizens.
Referring to herself as a self-appointed advocate for people on fixed and low incomes, Ms. Jones stated she is opposed
to any type of tax increase. Provo resident Susan Vivian stated she cannot afford another ten-cent gas increase; this
being the fourth such increase, there is no guarantee the government will stop seeking more money. Commissioner
Graves noted there has already been discussion of a fifth increase.

(Track B, 56:00)
Jenn Ottens of Lindon shared her personal history and stated she already voted against this proposal when it was
previously presented to the taxpayers; the main difference in two years is that the county is being offered a portion of
the proceeds in this draft agreement. Ms. Ottens questioned why the Utah Transit Authority (UTA) would be given
the opportunity to collect even more money from the people when they are continually “in the red,” proving
mismanagement and inability to prioritize budgets. Ms. Ottens expressed UTA is looking for a way to convince local
government to work without the people. Orem resident Claude Richards was the last to comment – he congratulated
Commissioner Lee on recognizing the bribing nature of this presentation. Mr. Richards pointed out the presenter did
not once mention the citizens even though all the money would be coming from the people. He noted every tax that
is passed becomes a drag on the economy and it will eventually stagnate.
