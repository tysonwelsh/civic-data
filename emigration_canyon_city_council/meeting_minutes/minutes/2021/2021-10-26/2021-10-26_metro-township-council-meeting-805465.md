Pursuant to Utah Code 52-4-202 the undersigned, as Mayor of the Emigration
Canyon Metro Township Council, hereby declares that providing an anchor location for the
electronic meetings of the Council will present a substantial risk to the health and safety of those
who may be present at the anchor location for the following reasons:
The Township is located in Salt Lake County and is still under an “Yellow” alert
stage for the COVID 19 pandemic; and
The regular meeting place for the Council (the Fire Station in the Canyon) does
not have sufficient space in the meeting room to provide for the recommended physical distancing
to keep people safe from infection.

Emigration Canyon Community Council Report (ECCC)
Bill Tobey, Chair, stated it has received an invoice from the State Division of
Forestry, Fire and State Lands for the $12,000 contribution that the township agreed to make to
Unified Fire Authority for the fuels crews work in the canyon. It also received a signed agreement
from the Acorn Hills neighborhood for the shared participation in the shaded fuel break work that
was completed in July. Acorn Hills is expecting an invoice from the township for one-half of the
cost of the work completed, which is $5,773.50. There were some hazardous trees along Pine
Crest that were removed last week. The Community Council assisted the Forestry, Fire and State
Lands with two grant applications for future fuel work in the canyon. The U.S. Forest Service
Wildlife Research team would like to meet on November 10, to discuss the canyon’s wildfire
preparedness. Firewise recertification application is due on November 19.

Council Business
Bond for Utility Projects
Mayor Smolka stated the sinkhole on Sunnydale has not been repaired and it has
been a challenge to get someone out to fix it. The ordinance states that they have 72 hours to fix
the problem and that has not happened. There is also language in the ordinance that allows the
township to fix the problem and bill them.
Polly McLean stated the way the ordinance is written, protects the township.
Mayor Smolka stated Salt Lake County Public Works has been sending emails
and making phone calls to Salt Lake City. There response was that they were putting the project
out to bid.

Deputy Mayor Hawkes stated the reason why it might have gone beyond the 72
hours is because they had to put a cold patch on it. However, it has been an extensive amount of
time and the temperature is starting to dip.
Mayor Smolka stated with all the notification that has been sent, the option to
repair the problem and send a bill is still available.
Deputy Mayor Hawkes stated a completion bond was discussed to avoid future

suggested working with Salt Lake County Public Works to get the sinkhole repaired and bill Salt

Ms. McLean stated she does not think the ordinance needs to be amended due
to this one unusual situation. It is at the point where the road needs to be fixed and a bill needs
to be sent to Salt Lake City.
Mayor Smolka stated he will talk to Salt Lake City this week. He does not think
the ordinance needs to be changed to include bonds. There are only two entities where a bond
could be applied.
Deputy Mayor Hawkes stated she does not think it is worth changing the process,
there needs to be an understanding moving forward. If there are reoccurring problems, then the
topic can be discussed at that time.

WHEREAS, Emigration Canyon is governed by a five-member elected Council under Utah

WHEREAS, Emigration Canyon is authorized under the Interlocal Cooperation Act, Utah
Code Ann. § 11-13-101 et seq. (the “Interlocal Cooperation Act”), to enter into an interlocal

prosperity, well-being, peace, and comfort of the community.
NOW THEREFORE IT IS RESOLVED, the Emigration Canyon Metro Township Council
approves, and authorizes the Mayor to sign, the attached Interlocal Agreement (“Agreement”)
with the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District, Copperton Metro Township, Emigration
Canyon Metro Township, Kearns Metro Township, Town of Brighton, White City Metro Township,
and Salt Lake County Respecting the Issuance of Bonds to Pay for Infrastructure (ATTACHMENT
A).

changes to the Agreement prior to signing if needed to respond to comments and suggestions of
other parties to the Agreement, it being understood and agreed that each party to the Agreement
is represented by legal counsel that may have input to the document.

Emigration Canyon Fire Station
Council Member Bowen stated he thought it was important to write a letter to
Chief Dan Peterson, supporting UFA’s decision to have a three-person crew at the Emigration

Canyon Fire Station. Approximately 75 percent of calls are medical with the other 25 percent
being fire issues. The budget cannot support a four-person crew. If it was decided that a fourperson crew was necessary, the Council would need to pay $420,000 to maintain that fourth
person.
Council Member Brems stated sending a letter to UFA supporting a three-person
crew is rationalizing the reason why Emigration Canyon is receiving less services. It is reducing
25 percent of services Emigration Canyon receives. Is there an option to negotiate the level of
service the canyon gets?
Captain Michael Conn stated the ideal number of fire fighters per heavy
apparatus is four. This allows the task at hand be accomplished, a four-handed crew is more
significant than a three-handed crew. Crews from surrounding areas back up UFA when handling
calls. The long-term plan is to build upon the three-person crew.
Council Member Harris stated the Council is not being asked, it is being told how
many crew members it will have. It is hard to support something that does not meet the standard,
she would like to move towards a four-person crew.
Council Member Bowen stated he will withdraw his request to approve a letter of
support and recommend that the letter is not sent.
Council Member Brems stated the Council should consider a resolution to expect
negotiations with UFA on a yearly basis to discuss level of service.

2022 Tentative Budget
Mayor Smolka stated the budget that was reviewed in July or August and was
emailed to the Council is the same budget that needs to be approved tentatively. If the tentative
budget is approved tonight, a public hearing can be held next month and there will not be a need
for an extra meeting in December. A couple of line items received an increase in funding. The
budget can still be amended up until the public hearing and final adoption.
Council Member Harris, seconded by Council Member Brems, moved to approve
the tentative budget with the acknowledgement that certain details still need to be addressed. The
motion passed unanimously.
