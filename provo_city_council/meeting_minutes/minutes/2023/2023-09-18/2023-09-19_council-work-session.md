2. An ordinance amending Provo City Code regarding Performance Development
   Overlay Zones and Open Space Subdivisions (PLOTA20230098). (00:12:37)

   Aaron Ardmore, Planning Supervisor, Presenting. Mr. Ardmore talked about the need to
   change some of the wording in the code (clean up). Councilor George Handley asked if
   there was a report from the Planning Commission about this, was told there was and that
   outside of the wording changes, the report was passed 7-0. Councilor Whipple asked why
   the Planning Commission decided to reduce the number of attached units from eight to
   six. Mr. Ardmore responded that in areas with single family homes eight units seemed to
   be too bulky but six appeared to be a number that worked well.

   Presentation Only, Already set for Council Meeting on 9/19/23.

3. Update from the Mayor’s Office regarding the “Big 6” (2023-020) (00:15:40)

   Nicole Martin, Director of Communications. Ms. Martin introduced herself to the
   Council as it was her first time formally presenting to them. Part of her initiative is to
   create a city brand that incorporates the website and focuses on “storytelling” the many
   stories of Provo City. Councilor Dave Shipley asked what amount of money this project
   was going to cost approximately, Director Martin replied around $80,000. Councilor
   Rachel Whipple asked if search engine optimization (SEO) was being used during this
   process and Information Services Director Josh Ihrig replied that yes it was, and they
   were using some different dates to target goals and to keep the process moving.
   Councilor Hadley talked about the desire for there to be the ability to either have separate
   pages in another language or the ability for the user to translate the page into their native
   language possibly using an overlay feature. Councilor Hoban asked who currently fields
   the live chat on the Provo page and was told that the customer service team does. He
   would be interested in maybe having an AI bot that could answer FAQs and take some of
   that workload off their plate. Chair MacKay expressed a desire to have an app version of
   the final website.

   Presentation Only

4. An update regarding economic development initiatives (2023-055) (00:42:10)

   Keith Morey, Assist. Director of Development Services, Presenting. Mr. Morey shared
   with the group how his team goes about looking to incentivize and develop different
   areas of Provo. Whether it’s an untouched area or an area that has become a bit rundown
   or has vacated lots – what types of business and growth would serve that part of the
   community that surrounds it now. Councilor Hoban asked about bringing a sports bar
   type restaurant to either downtown or close to campus now that BYU is a member of the
   Big XII. Mr. Morey responded that they are very focused on that, but a few hurdles are a
   general lack of alcohol consumption, property owners not wanting alcohol consumption
   on their properties and spending demographics. He doesn’t believe these are deal

5. A presentation regarding Innovation Grant Fund Program funded projects from
   FY 2023 (2023-066) (01:25:11)

   Dixon Holmes, Assistant Chief Administrative Officer, Presenting. Mr. Holmes invited
   two guest speakers to come and share the projects they had been able to implement by
   being awarded a grant from this fund.

   Heather Perkins was first. Ms. Perkins works in the 9-1-1 Command Center and showed
   the council a new system that allowed callers to receive a text on their phones that
   allowed them to take and transmit pictures or video in real-time that could be shared with
   first responders so they can accurately assess the situation and the type of response
   needed. Councilor Whipple asked how long they would be able to use this system with
   the grant money received. Ms. Perkins answered one year, and during that time she was
   going to look for ways to get additional grants or a budget to keep it – they’re very
   impressed with the system so far.

   Kaehan Shour, Engineer, Public Works went next. Mr. Shour’s grant money went
   towards the rental of three speed monitoring devices that can be mobile and help them
   capture speeds in different areas of the city to see if a permanent monitoring device
   should be installed in some areas and/or provide data that can be analyzed as to how fast
   drivers are moving within different types of areas within the city (residential, commercial
   etc.) and perhaps devise solutions that will help keep Provo residents safe.
