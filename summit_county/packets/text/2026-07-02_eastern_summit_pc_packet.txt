Recommendation 
Staff recommends that the Eastern Summit County Planning Commission (ESCPC) review the 
proposed Plat Amendment, conduct a public hearing, and vote to approve the Hidden Lake 
Ranch Subdivision Lots 62 & 64 Amended Plat that proposes to combine three lots and vacate a 
private road, pursuant to the Findings of Fact, Conclusions of Law and Conditions of Approval 
found in this Staff Report. Staff have reviewed the application for compliance with all standards 
in the Eastern Summit County Development Code and have found that it meets the 
requirements for approval.  
 
Project Description 
Project Name:   Hidden Lake Ranch Subdivision, Lots 62& 64 Amended Plat 
Applicant(s):   Langley Kushman 
Property Owner(s):  Kathryn Scherer 
Location:   8163 Aspen Loop 
Zone District:   AG-80 
Parcel Number and Size: HL-62 (0.29 acres), HL-64 (0.24 acres), and HL-Roads-62 (0.01 acres) 
Type of Process:   Administrative 
Final Land Use Authority: Eastern Summit County Planning Commission  
 
Proposal 
The applicant, on behalf of the property owner, is requesting a plat amendment to combine Lot 
HL-62, Lot HL-64 and Parcel HL-Roads-62 to create one lot totaling 0.55 acres. They also petition 
to vacate the portion of the platted private road within parcel HL-Roads-62. These proposed 
changes are depicted in Exhibit A.  
 
  
3
                                             2 
 
Vicinity Map 
 
 
 
Background 
Hidden Lake Ranch Subdivision was recorded May 5, 1962 (Exhibit B). It consisted of 162 lots. In 
2007, a portion of the unbuilt but platted road adjacent to Lot HL-62 was transferred by the 
Hidden Lake Association to the owners of Lot HL-62. The owner of HL-62 now wants to 
combine the unbuilt road parcel known as HL-Roads-62 with Lot HL-62 and Lot HL-64 to 
create one lot. This amendment includes vacating the portion of private subdivision road 
known as parcel HL-Roads-62. The private subdivision road, Aspen Loop, was not built on 
the HL-Roads-62 parcel.  
 
Analysis and Findings 
Section 11-4-5(E.10) of the Eastern Summit County Development Code sets the following 
grounds for vacating or amending a subdivision plat:  
 
1. If the Land Use Authority is satisfied that the public interest will not be materially 
injured by the proposed vacation, alteration or amendment, and there is good cause for 
the vacation, alteration or amendment, the Land Use Authority, may vacate, alter or 
amend a public or private road or any portion of the final subdivision plat.  
  
Analysis: Lot HL-62 and Lot HL-64 and parcel HL-Roads-62 are owned by Kathryn 
Scherer. Because some sections of the platted road were not built, the HOA transferred 
those sections, including HL-Roads-62 to the adjacent lot owners. This plat amendment 
would legally combine the road parcel and the two lots into one lot and vacate the 
private road.  COMPLIES 
 
2. No plat amendment shall be approved which results in an increase in density unless the 
requirements listed in subsection E6 are met. 
 
4
                                             3 
 
 Analysis: Density is defined in the Code as, “The number of dwelling units per acre with 
respect to residential land uses.” The amendment will not result in an additional 
dwelling unit. COMPLIES 
 
Public Notice, Meetings and Comments  
Public notice was published as a public hearing and possible action in The Park Record and 
individual notices were mailed to property owners within 1,000’ from the boundaries of Lot HL-
62, Lot HL-64 and parcel HL-Roads-62. As of the date of this report, no public comment has 
been received. 
 
Findings of Fact: 
1. Lot HL-62, Lot HL-64 and parcel HL-Roads-62 are located within the AG-80 (Agriculture 
80) zoning district in the Hidden Lake Ranch Subdivision. The address for HL-62 is 8163 
Aspen Loop.  
2. On May 7, 1962 the Hidden Lake Ranch Subdivision (Exhibit B) was recorded in Summit 
County, Entry Number 95105.  
3. Lot HL-62 consists of 0.29 acres, Lot HL-64 consists of 0.24 acres, and parcel HL-Roads-
62 is 0.01 acres. 
4. Kathryn Scherer owns Lot HL-62, Lot HL-64 and parcel HL-Roads-62. 
5. On March 4, 2026 the applicant, Langley Kushman, on behalf of Kathryn Scherer, 
submitted a complete plat amendment application to combine the three parcels.  
6. Service Providers, including the County Recorder’s Office and County Engineer’s Office 
reviewed the proposed plat amendment and gave comments. 
7. Prior to recording the plat, the applicant will update the plat to comply with the 
comments of the Service Providers.  
8. Density is defined in the Code as, “The number of dwelling units per acre with respect to 
residential land uses.”  
9. The amendment will not result in an additional residential unit. No new lots, tracts, or 
parcels are created through the plat amendment.  
 
Conclusions of Law:  
1. Public interest will not be materially injured by the proposed amendment and there is 
good cause for the amendment.  
2. The amendment would not result in increased density. 
 
Conditions of Approval: 
1. All service provider requirements shall be satisfied prior to recording the plat 
amendment. 
2. Prior to issuance of a building permit for any structure on the new Lot HL-62, the 
amended plat shall be recorded in the office of the Summit County Recorder.

885 estate lots as part of a base density, 716 incentive density lots, two hotels/300 resort units, 
equestrian facility, up to five golf club houses, and other accessory uses on approximately 6500 
acres with no less than 3900 acres remaining as passive open space. Of the 1901 total units in 
the density pool, 1,535 units have been approved. 
 
The Nicklaus Clubhouse was originally approved in 2014, expanded in 2016, 2020, and 2023 
culminating in a ~42,000 sq. ft. facility. The facilities within the clubhouse include, but are not 
limited to; kitchen with chef’s office, main dining room, private dining room and dining patios, 
conference/reception center, fitness center, locker rooms with restrooms and showers and 
shared attendant station, pro shop, a club repair workstation, full-service spa, restrooms, break 
room for the golf employees, storage, mechanical rooms, and golf cart storage. 
 
The development parameters for this project are specifically set forth in the Development 
Agreement.

RE:  Nicklaus Pool Expansion 
 
Promontory Development is submitting a Final Site Plan application to seek approval for an expansion to 
the Nicklaus Painted Valley Clubhouse facility.  This narrative will seek to outline the purpose, 
location/site history and changes of this application. 
 
Purpose 
 
The Nicklaus Painted Clubhouse originally included a lap pool as a part of the facility that was eventually 
pulled from the plans.  With the expansion of homes now reaching further into the Painted Valley and 
Brown’s Canyon areas, the ability and need to provide a lap pool and spa in this area has grown.  This 
pool will allow greater access to members of the community at a closer range than having to go to other 
pools further away. 
 
Site Location/History 
 
The Nicklaus Painted Valley facility was originally approved and recorded in April 2014.  However, 
since approval, there have been three revisions to the FSP.  This application will be the 5th approval to the 
facility, bringing the facility square footage to 42,115.  A summary of the approvals is listed below. 
 
· Original FSP for the Clubhouse was recorded on April 23rd 2014 
o Golf Clubhouse and Restaurant – 9,564 square feet. 
· Revision 1 to the FSP was recorded on December 1, 2016 
o Revision to add a Fitness Facility – 2,680 square feet. 
· Revision 2 to the FSP was recorded on June 3, 2019 
o Revision to add Banquet Halls and Spa Facilities – 15,949 square feet. 
· Revision 3 to the FSP was recorded on October 13, 2023 
o Revision to enlarge the Spa Facility – 6,922 square feet. 
 
The site is relatively flat with some moderate topo to the north of the proposed expansion.  With the 
building of a pool equipment room in that area, we anticipate that the concrete walls constructed there 
will act as retaining for any areas that will need it.

STAFF REPORT 
 
To:                                     Eastern Summit County Planning Commission                      
From:   Ray Milliner, County Planner 
Date of Meeting: July 2, 2026 
Type of Item: Code Amendment, Parcel Boundary Adjustments 
Process:  Public Hearing, Possible Recommendation – Legislative Review 
   
 
PROPOSAL 
The proposed text amendment would amend the Eastern Summit County Development 
Code to establish a land use authority for parcel boundary adjustments and clarify 
language regarding the eligibility for development when recording a boundary line 
adjustment.  
 
BACKGROUND 
A parcel boundary adjustment allows neighboring property owners outside a subdivision to 
move or modify the shared boundary between their properties without creating a new 
parcel or subdivision. It may be used to: 
 
• transfer a small piece of land from one parcel to another,  
• straighten or clean up irregular property lines,  
• resolve boundary issues, or  
• better align fences, driveways, buildings, or legal descriptions.  
 
Although the process changes the shape or size of existing parcels, it does not create 
additional development rights or increase the number of lots. 
 
This process is available only for parcels that are not within a platted subdivision. Property 
owners within a platted subdivision must instead complete a plat amendment in 
accordance with the Code. 
 
The Eastern Summit County Development Code does not currently designate a land use 
authority for parcel boundary adjustments because, under prior state law, such 
adjustments were treated primarily as recording actions. State law has since been 
amended to establish a process for a “simple boundary adjustment,” which requires: 
 
• Submittal to a land use authority 
• Verification by the authority of compliance and consent 
37
2 
 
 
To bring the County’s process into compliance, staff propose  the following amendment to 
the Code. 
 
2. A parcel boundary adjustment is not subject to the review of the Land Use 
Authority. The Land Use Authority for a parcel boundary adjustment shall be the 
CDD or designated Planning Staff Member.   
 
A land use authority is the person, board, commission, or governing body designated by 
the County to review and decide land use and development applications. The County 
assigns different application types to different land use authorities based on the nature of 
the request. Legislative matters, including code amendments and general plan 
amendments, are reviewed by the County Council. Applications such as conditional use 
permits, subdivisions, and plat amendments are reviewed by the Planning Commission. 
Administrative permits, including low impact permits and building permits, are reviewed by 
the Community Development Director.
 
 
The proposed amendment to Chapter 11 -4-2(C)(2)a clarifies the effect of a parcel 
boundary adjustment involving a nonconforming parcel and a conforming parcel. As 
currently written, the provision could be read to suggest that a parcel may become eligible 
for development merely by recording a boundary line adjustment. That is not the intent of 
the Code. The amendment is intended to clarify that when a nonconforming parcel is 
combined with a conforming parcel through a parcel boundary adjustment, the result is 
one parcel that must independently comply with applicable development standards before 
any development approval may be granted. The proposed language reads:
 
 
Parcel Boundary Adjustment: A Parcel Boundary Adjustment, including the 
combination of that combines a non-conforming parcel with a conforming 
parcel. 
 
ANALYSIS 
 
Section 11-5-3 of the Eastern Summit County Development Code establishes the process 
for text amendments to the Code. When an amendment is initiated, the Planning 
Commission reviews it and forwards a recommendation to the County Council. After a 
public hearing, the County Council may approve, approve with modifications, or deny the 
amendment. 
 
The Eastern Summit County General Plan has specific goals related to future 
amendments.  
 
Goal 2.1. States: 
38
3 
 
 
“Develop land use codes which balance the diversity of desires of Eastern Summit 
County residents, including private property rights.” 
 
Goal 2.1.i States:  
 
“Create appropriate and predictable development procedures in the Development 
Code to ensure that all land use and development is adequately reviewed and 
determined to be consistent with the goals of this Plan before any approvals are 
granted.” 
 
The proposed amendment advances these goals by bringing the parcel boundary 
adjustment provisions into conformance with current state law, which requires review by a 
land use authority and verification that the adjustment satisfies applicable requirements, 
including consent and compliance with land use regulations. Parcel boundary 
adjustments would remain administrative in nature and would not authorize new lots, 
additional development rights, or development on a parcel that does not otherwise comply 
with the Development Code. By designating the appropriate review authority and clarifying 
the effect of combining nonconforming and conforming parcels, the amendment improves 
administrative clarity and reduces the potential for misinterpretation without creating 
additional impacts on neighboring properties.
 
 
RECOMMENDATION 
 
Staff recommends that the Eastern Summit County Planning Commission hold a public 
hearing, receive public comment, and forward a recommendation of approval to the 
County Council based upon the findings of fact and conclusions of law set forth herein. 
 
Findings of Fact 
 
1. The proposed text amendment addresses parcel boundary adjustments for parcels 
located outside of a subdivision. 
2. A parcel boundary adjustment permits adjoining property owners to relocate or 
modify a common boundary line without creating a new parcel or subdivision. 
3. Parcel boundary adjustments do not create additional development rights or 
increase the number of legal lots. 
4. Parcel boundary adjustments are permitted only for parcels that are not located 
within a recorded subdivision. 
5. Applicable state law requires a process for simple boundary adjustments, including 
submittal to a land use authority and verification of compliance and owner consent. 
6. The Eastern Summit County Development Code does not presently designate a 
land use authority for parcel boundary adjustments. 
39
4 
 
7. The proposed text amendment would designate the Community Development 
Director or a designated Planning Staff Member as the land use authority for parcel 
boundary adjustments. 
8. The existing Code language regarding parcel boundary adjustments involving 
nonconforming and conforming parcels could be interpreted as allowing 
development eligibility to be created solely through a boundary line adjustment. 
9. The proposed clarifying language establishes that combining a nonconforming 
parcel with a conforming parcel through a parcel boundary adjustment results in a 
single parcel and does not, by itself, authorize development or create additional 
development rights. 
10. Any development proposed after a parcel boundary adjustment remains subject to 
all applicable requirements of the Eastern Summit County Development Code. 
11. The proposed text amendment would not authorize new land uses, create new 
parcels, or confer additional development rights. 
 
Conclusions of Law 
 
1. The proposed text amendment is consistent with the goals and policies of the 
Eastern Summit County General Plan because it provides a clear and predictable 
administrative process for parcel boundary adjustments.

Community Plan Design Narrative 
 
This Community Plan reflects a deliberate, flexible and context-sensitive approach to growth, 
shaped first and foremost by the site’s existing natural systems, topography, and access 
constraints. Rather than imposing a rigid development pattern, the plan organizes land uses, 
circulation, and open space in direct response to the land, reinforcing environmental stewardship 
while creating a coherent and functional community framework. 
 
The overall layout is structured around the site’s natural drainage corridors, ridgelines, and 
slopes. Hillside preservation areas are strategically designated to protect steeper terrain and 
visually prominent landforms, maintaining the natural character of the landscape and minimizing 
disturbance. These preserved hillsides also function as natural buffers between development 
areas, reinforcing ecological continuity and long-term slope stability. 
 
Open space is not treated as residual land, but as an organizing element of the plan. A continuous 
network of community and neighborhood parks is woven throughout the site, aligned with 
riparian corridors and low-lying areas that already support natural drainage and vegetation. This 
approach enhances habitat value, supports stormwater management, and creates meaningful 
recreational and visual amenities that are accessible from surrounding neighborhoods. The 
central community park will serve as a focal point for gathering and recreation, while a robust 
trail system and smaller neighborhood parks will provide localized open space integrated into 
daily life. 
 
Circulation is carefully designed to respect existing grades and minimize earthwork. Road 
alignments follow natural contours, reducing visual impacts and infrastructure costs while 
improving safety and drivability. Primary access points are limited and strategically located 
along Lost Creek Road, providing efficient connectivity while avoiding unnecessary 
fragmentation of the site. Internal streets are designed to balance mobility with neighborhood 
character, supporting walkability and intuitive wayfinding. 
 
Development areas of the project are clustered in the locations that are best suited for 
construction. These areas are generally on gentler slopes and outside of sensitive environmental 
corridors. This clustering allows for more efficient infrastructure extension, preserves larger 
contiguous open spaces, and reinforces a clear transition between developed and natural areas. 
The result is a pattern of growth that feels embedded in the landscape rather than superimposed 
upon it. 
 
Overall, the Community Plan demonstrates a thoughtful integration of land use, infrastructure, 
and open space that responds directly to existing site conditions. By aligning development with 
natural systems and topography, the plan achieves a balanced framework that supports long-term 
sustainability, visual quality, and community livability, while remaining flexible enough to adapt 
over time as individual phases are implemented.

1) Neighborhood Connectivity 
The proposed Community Plan is designed to establish strong physical and functional

destinations. Connectivity is achieved through a coordinated system of vehicular access, internal

Primary vehicular access to the site is provided from Lost Creek Road at three (3)

New streets are developed as part of an interconnected internal network that follows 
natural contours and drainage patterns to minimize grading and visual impacts. The street 
system provides multiple route options for residents, visitors, and service vehicles,

project exhibits as part of this application. 
c. Pedestrian and Bicycle Routes 
Pedestrian and bicycle connectivity is a core design element of the plan. Sidewalks and 
shared-use paths are integrated along internal streets, while off-street trails are aligned 
with open space corridors and the Lost Creek riparian areas. These facilities provide safe 
and convenient connections between neighborhoods, parks, and key destinations within 
the site, as well as links to existing and planned regional trail systems where feasible.

The proposed development is located within reasonable proximity to existing and planned 
community facilities, including parks, recreation areas, and regional services. The internal trail 
and street network is designed to support walking and bicycling access to on-site neighborhood 
and community parks, reducing reliance on vehicular trips for daily recreation. Vehicular 
connections to nearby retail, employment, and educational facilities are direct and efficient, 
supporting access to broader community services while maintaining a residential character 
internally.

4) Character 
Architectural and site design character objectives emphasize compatibility with the surrounding 
landscape and regional context. Development is envisioned to reflect a mountain- and valley-
responsive design approach, incorporating natural materials, articulated building forms, and 
muted color palettes that complement the terrain. Building orientation and massing respond to 
the street network as well as to climate considerations such as solar access, wind, and snow 
management, while maintaining a cohesive neighborhood identity that supports a sense of place. 
 
5) Site Design 
The proposed development is intentionally designed to work with existing site conditions rather 
than reshape them. Steep slopes, ridgelines, and sensitive hillside areas are preserved through 
designated hillside preservation zones and open space areas. Development is clustered on gentler 
terrain, allowing roadways and building pads to follow existing contours and reducing the need 
for extensive grading. Natural drainage corridors and wildlife movement areas are maintained 
and enhanced through integrated open space and trail systems, supporting ecological function 
and visual continuity. 
 
6) Complete Street Design 
The internal circulation system is designed using complete street principles appropriate to a 
semi-rural and residential context. Streets accommodate vehicles, pedestrians, bicyclists, and 
emergency services while prioritizing safety and ease of navigation. Road widths, curvature, and 
intersection design promote slower travel speeds and intuitive wayfinding. Walkways, sidewalks, 
shared-use paths, and trail connections are incorporated where appropriate to provide safe, 
multimodal travel options throughout the community. 
 
7) Parking Areas 
Parking is provided in a manner that balances functional needs with visual quality and 
environmental sensitivity. On-street parking, where appropriate, supports neighborhood 
character and traffic calming, while off-street parking areas are located to minimize visibility 
from primary roadways and open spaces. Parking areas are designed to follow natural grades, 
reducing cut-and-fill, and are visually softened through landscaping, topographic screening, and 
controlled lighting to limit nighttime impacts. 
 
8) Public and Private Outdoor Spaces 
The development includes a robust system of public and private outdoor spaces that support 
recreation, habitat preservation, and community interaction. The community and neighborhood 
parks are strategically located to be accessible from surrounding residential areas, while larger

Project-related external storage needs, including refuse collection, recycling, and maintenance
