15   Ms. Benson shared the Preliminary Park Analysis with those present. When determining park needs,
16   it is necessary to do a Level of Service (“LOS”) analysis, which is a ratio of the number of acres per

18   and court types to see what the population numbers were per amenity. That work was still underway.

22   For the LOS, the population projections were examined. Currently, the City has just over 17,000

24   residents. The Wasatch Front Regional Council (“WFRC”) population projections were also taken
25   into account. The projections were not significantly different, with the WFRC population estimate
26   by 2050 being 30,146. After discussions with City Staff, the decision was made to use the General
27   Plan projections to account for the possibility of additional population growth.

 6   Some of the key findings were reviewed. Overall, resident satisfaction and utilization of recreational
 7   amenities in Bluffdale were quite high. 77% of residents used Bluffdale parks at least once per month
 8   and 66% indicated that they used City trails at least once a month. It was a very active community in
 9   Bluffdale. The primary driver for which parks and trails residents used most often had to do with the
10   distance from their homes. Residents were looking for conveniently located recreational amenities.
11   Council Member Kallas asked if there were similar statistics in other communities. Ms. Gibb
12   explained that the numbers were quite high, especially when compared to the national average for
13   park usage. Within Utah and along the Wasatch Front, the numbers were normally slightly higher
14   than the average, but not necessarily significantly so.
15
16   Most residents were happy with the number of parks available in the City. There were enough
17   recreational amenities to meet the needs of residents. Residents believed there were increased
18   opportunities for recreation programs, trails, and natural open spaces. Residents wanted to see
19   increased connectivity in the trails throughout the City as well as additional lighting, more trees, shade
20   structures, and overall maintenance/cleanliness.        Most residents were interested in recreation
21   programs in the City for both youths and adults. Residents surveyed were most interest in soccer,
22   basketball, and swimming, while pickleball was the most popular adult recreation program.
23
24   In terms of recreational facilities, residents were very interested in having a Recreation Center within
25   the City and were especially interested in a swimming pool. Two-thirds of residents indicated that
26   they would be willing to pay a monthly usage fee to help fund the developments and improvements
27   that were desired within the City. Most residents who responded to the survey stated that if there was
28   a Recreation Center available, there was interest in a family pass or a monthly pass for admission.
29   Ms. Gibb explained that this was not common among most of the cities that Y2 Analytics surveyed.

       Re: Requirements for operation of the Bluffdale Justice Court

Mayor and Councilmembers:

         Pursuant to the requirements of the State Judicial Council, this letter serves to advise you
of the requirements for the operation of justice courts within the boundaries of the City of Bluffdale
(“City”) and the feasibility of maintaining the existing justice court.

         Utah law requires that certain standards be met in the operation of a justice court. These
statutory requirements include:

       1. All official court business shall be conducted in a courtroom or office located in a
          public facility which is conducive and appropriate to the administration of justice (Utah
          Code Ann. 78A-7-213(1).

       2. Each court shall be open and judicial business shall be transacted every day except as
          provided by law (78A-7-213), although the judge is not required to be present during
          all hours that the court is open.

       3. The hours that the court will be open shall be posted conspicuously at the court and in
          local public buildings (78A-7-213(5)).

       4. The judge and the clerk of the court shall attend the court at regularly scheduled times
          (78A-7- 213(6)).

       5.    The entity creating the Justice Court shall set fixed judicial compensation pursuant to
            statutory requirements (78A-7-206).

       6. The entity operating the Justice Court shall provide sufficient funds to cover the cost
          of travel and training expenses of clerical personnel and judges at training sessions
          mandated by the Judicial Council. (78A-7-103).



                                                                                                 Page 1
       7. The entity creating the Justice Court shall provide a sufficient staff of public
          prosecutors to attend the court and perform the duties of prosecution (78A-7-103).

       8. The entity creating the court shall provide adequate funding for attorneys where
          persons are indigent as provided by law (78A-7-103).

       9. The entity creating the court shall provide sufficient local law enforcement officers to
          attend court when required and provide security for the court (78A-7-103).

       10. The fees, mileage, and other expenses authorized by law for jurors, prosecution
           witnesses, witnesses subpoenaed by indigent defendants, and interpreter costs shall be
           paid by the municipality if the action is prosecuted by the city attorney, and by the
           county if the action is prosecuted by the county attorney or district attorney. 78B-1-
           117, 10-7-76).

       11. Any fine, surcharge, or assessment which is payable to the State shall be forwarded to
           the State as required by law (78A-7-120 and 78A-7-121).

       12. Court shall be held within the jurisdiction of the court, except as provided by law (78A-
           7-212).

       13. The entity creating the court shall provide and keep current for the court a copy of the
           Utah Code, the Utah Court Rules Annotated, the justice court manual published by the
           state court administrator, the county, city, or town ordinances as appropriate, and other
           legal reference materials as determined to be necessary by the judge (78A-7-103).

       14. All required reports and audits shall be filed as required by law or by rule of the Judicial
           Council. (78A-7-215).

       15. All Justice Courts shall use a common case management system and disposition
           reporting system as specified by the Judicial Council (78A-7-213).

       16. An audio recording system shall maintain the verbatim record of all court proceedings
           (78A-7- 103).

        In addition to those requirements which are directly imposed by statute, the Judicial
Council has promulgated additional requirements for the creation and ongoing certification of
Justice Courts, which are:

       1. A clerk shall be available for at least three hours each day that the court is required to
          be open as provided by law, and during court hearings, as required by the judge. These
          hours shall be posted on the court’s website. C.J.A. Rule 9-105.

       2. That the judge be available to attend court and conduct court business as needed,
          performing all duties required and exercising ultimate responsibility for the
          administration of justice as an independent branch of government.



                                                                                                  Page 2
3. All court hearings shall be conducted in designated courtroom, including remote
   transmission, as permitted by the Judicial Council, or in another location authorized by
   the presiding judge.

4. That the minimum furnishings for a courtroom include: a desk and chair for the judge
   (on a riser at least six inches above the well), a desk and chair for the court clerk, chairs
   for witnesses, separate tables and appropriate chairs for plaintiffs and defendants, a
   Utah State flag, a United States flag, a separate area and chairs for at least four jurors,
   a separate area with appropriate seating for the public, an appropriate room for jury
   deliberations and an appropriate area or room for victims and witnesses which is
   separate from the public, as well as a judicial robe, a gavel, current bail schedules, a
   copy of the Code of Judicial Administration, and necessary forms and supplies.

5.    Office space for the judge and clerk shall be appropriate. (Under certain circumstances
     this space may be shared, but if shared, the judge and clerk must have priority to use
     the space whenever needed). The office space shall include a desk for the judge and a
     desk for the clerk, secure filing cabinets for the judge and the clerk, a telephone for the
     judge and a telephone for the clerk, appropriate office supplies to conduct court
     business, a cash register or secured cash box, a computer with word processor software,
     and access to a scanner and copy machine.

6. The court shall provide interpreters as required by Rule 3-306.04 of the Code of Judicial
   Administration.

7. The entity shall have at least one peace officer, which may be contracted.

8. A court security plan must be submitted for approval as required by C.J.A. Rule 3-414.

9. Each court must have at least one computer with access to the internet, and appropriate
   software and security/encryption technology to allow for electronic reporting and
   access to Driver License Division and the Bureau of Criminal Identification, as defined
   by the reporting and retrieval standards promulgated by the Department of Public
   Safety.

10. Each court shall report required case disposition information to the DLD, BCI, and the
    Administrative Office of the Courts electronically, as described above.

11. Clerks’ education hours shall be reported to the Administrative Office of the Courts on
    an annual basis.

12. The appointment of the clerk(s) assigned to serve the court shall be subject to the
    judge’s approval, who may participate in the interview and personnel evaluation
    process for the clerk(s) at his or her discretion.




                                                                                           Page 3
       13. Court staff shall be certified as contemplated by Rule 3-303 of the Code of Judicial
           Administration.

       14. Any interlocal agreement relating to court operations, as amended to date, shall be
           provided to the Justice Court Administrator.

       15. The court shall accept credit and debit cards through a system that integrates with
           CORIS.

       16. The court shall have access to UCJIS.

       17. An audio recording system shall maintain a digital recording of all court proceedings,
           which must be a stand-alone unit that records and audibly plays back the recording,
           index, back-up, and archive the recording and enable the record to be retried; and have
           at least two recording channels.

         Having reviewed the above-referenced requirements, the case load, the financial and
physical requirements of the courts, it is my opinion that it is feasible for the City of Bluffdale to
continue operating its justice court. It is my recommendation that the City of Bluffdale maintain
its existing justice court and apply for recertification to the State Judicial Council. If I can be of
further assistance, please contact me at (801) 438-2000 or jdunkelberger@btjd.com.

2.70.010 Creation; Composition

   1. Creation and Composition: There is hereby created and established a body designated as
      the Audit Committee, which shall be composed of three (3) members.
   2. Appointment: The members shall be appointed by the mayor, with the advice and
      consent of the city council.
   3. Qualifications: All members of the Audit Committee should possess a background in
      finance or accounting. All members should also possess a basic understanding of
      government financial reporting and auditing.
   4. Independence: No person who exercises managerial responsibilities in the City shall
      serve as a member of the Audit Committee.

2.70.020 Purpose; Powers And Duties; Operations

   1. Purpose: The Audit Committee shall provide independent review and oversight of the
      City’s financial reporting processes and internal controls.
   2. Powers And Duties: As allowed by this chapter, the bylaws and other applicable law, and
      consistent with the budget approved by the city council, the Audit Committee shall:
          1. Develop a charter and any other documentation necessary to set forth the Audit
              Committee’s operations, including, but not limited to, the selection of a
              chairperson; time, place and manner of calling of meetings and other procedural
              matters; provided, however, that there shall be at least one meeting each year.
              The Audit Committee shall submit such documents for review and approval by
              the city council. The Audit Committee shall review its charter and other
              documents no less than once every five (5) years to assess their continued
              adequacy.
          2. Establish procedures for the receipt, retention, and treatment of complaints
              regarding the City’s accounting and internal controls. Such procedures should
              specifically provide for the confidential, anonymous submission by City
              employees of any such concerns.
          3. Monitor financial controls performed by City management, including, without
              limitation, financial controls designed to detect and prevent management override
              of other financial controls.
          4. Present annually to the city council a written report of how it has discharged its
              duties and responsibilities. This report shall be made public and be accompanied
              by the Audit Committee’s charter. The report shall include a specific statement
              that the Audit Committee has discussed the financial statements (a) with
              management; (b) with the City’s independent auditors in private; and (c) privately
              between Audit Committee members. The report shall also state that the Audit
              Committee members believe that the financial statements are fairly presented to
              the extent such a determination can be made solely on the bass of such
              conversations.
   3. Other Duties: Members of the Audit Committee shall also serve on the Budget Review
      Committee.
   4. Accounting, Budget Provisions: Prior to March 1 of each year, the Audit Committee shall
      submit a proposed budget to the city manager for the upcoming fiscal year for the audit or
      for other related costs necessary for the committee to fulfill its duties. The Audit
      Committee’s budget shall only include adequate funds to allow it to engage the services
      of financial experts, legal counsel, and other professionals as necessary.

2.70.030 Term; Removal; Vacancies

Members of the Audit Committee shall serve a three (3) year term, except for the first
appointments, which shall be for the terms set forth herein. On the first appointment of members
of the Audit Committee, the mayor may designate one (1) member to serve a term ending in one
year, one (1) member to serve terms ending in two years, and one (1) member to serve a term
ending in three years. All members appointed shall be residents of the city. Members may be
removed by a two-thirds (2/3) vote of the city council or at the recommendation of the mayor and
a simple majority of the city council. Vacancies occurring through expiration of terms of
appointment, death, disability, resignation, moving from the city or removal by the city council,
if applicable, shall be filled by appointment by the mayor, with the advice and consent of the city
council.

2.07.040 Compensation

No member of the Audit Committee shall receive compensation for their service.

2.80.010 Creation; Composition

   1. Creation and Composition: There is hereby created and established a body
      designated as the Budget Review Committee, which shall be composed of not less
      than five (5) members and not more than seven (7) members.
   2. Appointment: The members shall be appointed by the mayor, with the advice and
      consent of the city council.
   3. Qualifications: All members of the Budget Review Committee shall be Bluffdale
      residents, city staff, or city council members. They should possess a background in
      finance or accounting. All members should also possess a basic understanding of
      government financial reporting and auditing.

2.80.020 Purpose; Powers And Duties; Operations

   1. Purpose: The Budget Review Committee shall review the annual budget and make
      recommendations to improve, the content, the transparency and clarity of the budget
      document
   2. Powers And Duties: As allowed by this chapter, the bylaws and other applicable law,
      and consistent with the budget approved by the city council, the Budget Review
      Committee shall:
         1. Develop a charter and any other documentation necessary to set forth the
             Budget Review Committee’s operations, including, but not limited to, the
             selection of a chairperson; time, place and manner of calling of meetings and
             other procedural matters; provided, however, that there shall be at least one
             meeting each year. The Budget Review Committee shall submit such
             documents for review and approval by the city council. The Budget Review
             Committee shall review its charter and other documents no less than once
             every five (5) years to assess their continued adequacy.
         2. Learn and understand the financial needs and priorities of the city council and
             city management.
         3. Review the annual budget prior to management’s submission of the budget to
             the city council and prepare a budget review report summarizing findings and
             recommendations to improve the content, clarity and transparency of the
             budget document.
   3. Accounting, Budget Provisions: This committee is an advisory committee and does not
      create a budget of its own. The initial budget presented to the city council is the city
      manager’s tenative budget. The city council has discretion in all budgetary matters.
       The budget is the city council’s budget from the time they approve the tentative
       budget.

2.80.030 Term; Removal; Vacancies

Members of the Budget Committee shall serve a three (3) year term, except for the first
appointments, which shall be for the terms set forth herein. On the first appointment of
members of the Budget Review Committee, the mayor may designate no less than two (2) but
no more than three (3) members to serve terms ending in one year, no less than two (2) but
no more than three (3) members to serve terms ending in two years, and one (1) member to
serve a term ending in three years. Members may be removed by a two-thirds (2/3) vote of the
city council. Vacancies occurring through expiration of terms of appointment, death,
disability, resignation, moving from the city or removal by the city council, if applicable, shall
be filled by appointment by the mayor, with the advice and consent of the city council.

2.08.040 Compensation

No member of the Budget Review Committee shall receive compensation for their service.

AN ORDINANCE APPROVING THE ADJUSTMENT OF A COMMON MUNICIPAL BOUNDARY WITH
DRAPER CITY

WHEREAS, pursuant to Utah Code Ann. § 10-2-419, the legislative bodies of two or more
municipalities having common boundaries may adjust their common boundaries; and

WHEREAS the City of Bluffdale and Draper City share a common boundary and intend to adjust
the boundary; and

WHEREAS the City of Bluffdale passed Resolution 2023-52 on October 11, 2023, and Draper City
passed Resolution 23-62 on October 17, 2023, stating their intent to adjust a common boundary;
and

WHEREAS the City of Bluffdale has posted appropriate notice according to Utah Code Ann. § 10-
2-419(2); and

WHEREAS the City of Bluffdale has held a public hearing regarding the adjustment of a common
boundary;

WHEREAS Draper City will hold a public hearing to consider passing an Ordinance regarding this
adjustment in January 2024; and

WHEREAS the City of Bluffdale has received no protests that meet the requirements of Utah
Code Ann. § 10-2-419(3);

NOW, THEREFORE, THE BLUFFDALE CITY COUNCIL HEREBY ORDAINS AS FOLLOWS:

        Section 1.     Adoption of the Adjustment of the Common Boundary with Draper
City. The City Council hereby approves the adjustment of a municipal boundary between the
City of Bluffdale and Draper City as represented in the attached Exhibits. Final survey quality
control may make required technical adjustments to the exhibits before presentation to the
Lieutenant Governor as required by Utah State Law.

       Section 3.     Effective Date. This ordinance shall take immediately after passing.

                               RESOLUTION NO. 2023-07

 A RESOLUTION OF THE REDEVELOPMENT AGENCY OF BLUFFDALE CITY RECOMMENDING
  THE APPOINTMENT OF MEMBERS TO THE SALT LAKE COUNTY HOUSING TRUST FUND
                              ADVISORY BOARD

      WHEREAS, the City of Bluffdale Redevelopment Agency entered into an interlocal
agreement with Salt Lake County on October 17, 2023, which provide funds to the Salt Lake
County Housing Trust Fund targeted for the provision of moderate income housing within the
City;

       WHEREAS, Salt Lake County has created the Salt Lake County Housing Trust Fund
Advisory Board and membership criteria in Section 2.91.040 of the Salt Lake County Code,
and the powers and duties enumerated in Salt Lake County Code section 2.91.120 anticipate
membership to include and consult with subject matter experts to assist with advice and
recommendations for the allocation of funds; and

        WHEREAS, the Mayor has recommended to the Redevelopment Agency of Bluffdale
City that the individuals listed below be recommended to be appointed by Salt Lake County
to serve as subject matter experts representing the City of Bluffdale on the Salt Lake County
Housing Trust Fund Advisory Board for the entire term of the interlocal agreement which is
currently approved until June 30, 2028.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE REDEVELOPMENT AGENCY OF BLUFFDALE
CITY:

       Section 1.   The Bluffdale City Redevelopment Agency hereby recommends that
Salt Lake County appoint the following individuals to the Salt Lake County Housing Trust
Fund Advisory Board for the entire period of the initial interlocal agreement:

                                            Traci Crockett
                                             Brent Maruji

       Section 2.    This Resolution shall become effective immediately upon its passage.
