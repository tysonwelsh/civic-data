REED RIDGE COMMERCIAL SUBDIVISION PLAT AMENDMENT APPROVED:

     City Administrator Seth Atkinson updated the council that the Reed Ridge Commercial Subdivision needed a boundary line adjustment between lots 4 and 6 for the purpose of the purchase of lot 4 by the applicants from the owner of the subdivision to build a McDonald’s restaurant.  Joanna Graham representing McDonalds joined the meeting via Zoom. The site plan for McDonalds was reviewed concurrently with the subdivision amendment by the Planning Commission.  
    The amendment  will increase the size of lot 4, and decrease the size of lot 6, as well as extend the northern boundary of the subdivision to include a remnant strip of land inadvertently left  behind during the original subdivision process.  Mr. Atkinson noted that the property is not currently owned by the applicant and the city has not received proof of ownership.  
     Councilor Shari Cowan moved to approve the amended plat of the Reed Ridge Commercial Subdivision which amends Lots 4 & 6, enlarges the outside northern border contingent on proof of ownership of Lot 4 and that both owners of Lots 4 and 6 sign the amended plat.   Councilor Travis Worwood seconded the motion.  The motion passed unanimously.

TEMPEST AWARDED BID FOR THE POWER PLANT NATURAL GAS GATE STATION:

      Mr. Atkinson reported that the City had issued a request for bids to construct the natural gas gate station at the power plant on the south end of the city.  The City received two bids with the lowest bid being Tempest in the amount of $451,379.00.  The City has worked with Tempest in the past when they installed the natural gas gate station at Owens Corning.  Mr. Atkinson said that Tempest has done great work for the City. 
     Councilor Callaway moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the Notice of Award to Tempest for the construction of the power plant natural gas gate station.  Councilor Parady seconded the motion.  The motion passed unanimously. 
      
GLIDER HANGAR AGREEMENTS APPROVED:
   
     Mr. Atkinson explained to the council that several glider owners have constructed glider hangars at the airport.  They are smaller than regular hangars and sized specifically to fit gliders which have a long wingspan and narrow body.  Staff have negotiated with the glider owners on specifics to the template agreement.  Some things that needed to be resolved were the definition of improved space, snow removal, and length of term.  Through several back-and-forth exchanges the terms of the leases have been agreed upon.  They will be 7-year terms, snow removal will not be required in front of these hangars since there is no hard surface, and the improved space issue was agreed upon for both large and small glider hangars.  
      Mr. Atkinson stated that reason for the smaller term of the agreement is that; 1) there are future plans to expand the apron to the north where the glider hangars currently sit; and 2) The master plan process (scheduled for next year) will look for a more permanent home and place for glider operations, most likely on the west side of the runway.   Glider owners have agreed to the negotiated items and are ready to sign the agreements as they are written.  The rest of the agreements conform to the pre-approved template that had been drafted by the City Attorney. 
     Councilor Travis Worwood moved to authorize the Mayor to sign all eight glider hangar agreements.  Councilor Cowan seconded the motion.  The motion carried on a unanimous vote.
