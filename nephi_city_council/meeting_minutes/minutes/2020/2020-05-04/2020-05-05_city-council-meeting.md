Mayor Glade R. Nielson called the meeting to order and welcomed all present and then Councilor Larry
Ostler offered a prayer.

PLEDGE OF ALLEGIANCE:
  The Pledge of Allegiance was led President of the Juab County Daughters of the Utah Pioneers, Peggy
Johnson. Ms. Johnson gave a history of the Daughters of the Utah Pioneers and updated the council on
current projects they are working on including a new fort wall monument at the Pioneer City Park.

CONSENT AGENDA APPROVED:

  Councilor Ostler moved to approve the consent agenda which included the approval of the minutes of
the regular council meeting held on 4-21-20, work session minutes 4-21-20, work session minutes 4-28-
20, the sale of three burial rights to Evan Rodney and Laura Frampton, two burial rights to Kevin and
Nanette Johnson, and a list of claims on the warrant register dated 5-5-20. Councilor Worwood
seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

NO PUBLIC COMMENT

NO PLANNING ITEMS

CEMETERY POLICY AND PROCEDURES, FEES, AND CODE UPDATED AND ADOPTED:

  City Administrator Seth Atkinson reviewed with the council an updated policy and procedures booklet,
cemetery fee changes and code changes affected by the revisions in the policy booklet. Some of the
major changes were adding a clarifying definition of a Nephi City resident and new policy on transferring
burial rights. Councilor Memmott made one motion to adopt Master Fee Resolution 05-05-2020,
Ordinance 05-05-2020 amending section 7-5-7 of the Nephi City code, and the revised Cemeteries
Policies and Procedures booklet. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion passed on a
unanimous vote.

CONTRACT AWARED TO RME FOR SUBSTATION III CONSTRUCTION:

    Mr. Atkinson reported to the council that the city had solicited bids for the construction of Substation
III on the north side of the city. The substation will help to serve existing capacity in neighborhoods, the
industrial park, and the hospital as well as new growth in the area. The city received three bids for the
construction and the low bidder was RME out of Price, Utah in the amount of $1,102,554.23. RME has
been constructing high voltage facilities for 25 years. Councilor Worwood moved to accept the low bid
from RME for the construction of Substation III. Councilor Jones seconded the motion. The motion
passed on a unanimous vote.

TENTATIVE BUDGET FOR FISCAL YEAR 2021 APPROVED:

   Nephi City Budget Officer Seth Atkinson presented to the council a tentative budget for the fiscal year
ending June 30.2021. Mr. Atkinson informed the council that state statute requires the presentation of
a tentative budget on the first meeting in May. He explained that we do not have data to estimate how
the pandemic is going to affect the city future revenues. A state economist has made an analysis of the
revenue projections of each city in Utah and he estimates that Nephi’s revenues could be down from 8%
to 22%. He reminded the council that the tentative budget may be modified as additional information
becomes available. Mr. Atkinson noted that a new Local Building Authority fund had been added in the
past fiscal year. The complete tentative budget document can be found under the Finance Department
tab on the city website. After the budget is nearly finalized, a public hearing will be held to allow
residents of the community to comment and make suggestions before the final budget is adopted.

  Councilor Jones moved to adopt the tentative budget. Councilor Seely seconded the motion. The
motion passed on a unanimous vote.

  Councilor Ostler moved to set the public hearing date for the budget on May 19, 2020 at 7:00 p.m.
Councilor Memmott seconded the motion, which passed on a unanimous vote.

DISCUSSION ABOUT EXTENDING THE STATE OF EMERGENCY:

  Mayor Nielson reported that he had asked the county health department to request from the state a
reduced risk level for Nephi City. The state denied the request. Mayor Nielson said that we can re-
petition for a reduced level. He was disappointed with the decision. The Mayor called for a motion to
adopt an ordinance extending the state of emergency. No motion was made.

CITY ADMINISTRATOR UPDATES:

  Mr. Atkinson reported about the pre-construction meeting held for the redundant gas line project.
Construction should begin in 2 to 3 weeks.
  Mr. Atkinson reported that 4-H volunteers had helped spread wood chips in the new playground at
Town Square Park. Dan Woodland is going to pour the cement pad for the park bench that the FFA has
donated.

CITY COUNCIL UPDATES:

   Councilor Memmott thanked Councilor Jones for all his work, effort and organization of the trail
clean-up project.
   Councilor Jones suggested that the entrances to the trail be blocked of so that ATV’s wouldn’t be able
to enter the trail. He recognized 7-11 for donating pizza to the volunteers. A discussion ensued about
changing the mindset of Nephi residents to help keep public parks and our city clean from litter.
   Councilor Memmott received an email from a resident who would like a list of the businesses in
Nephi. City Recorder Lisa Brough said she would see to it that a list of businesses was posted on the
website.
   Councilor Seely reported that the groundwork had been completed for the scales at the landfill. He
said that a member of the JRDA board had requested more open hours at the landfill, but once the cost
was calculated it was determined to be too costly. The next JRDA meeting will be July 6, 2020.
   Councilor Ostler reported that Mona City had buried their fire chief this past week. The funeral
procession had gone through Nephi and our water department were working along the procession route
and they stopped and removed their hats to honor the chief and he was impressed with their conduct.
   Councilor Worwood reported that the west bench is littered with garbage. He reported the mess to
County Commissioner Clint Painter. Worwood though it might be a good idea to partner with the
county to hep remedy the problem.
   Mayor Nielson said that he had been approached by some residents that were wanting more
information on the local state of health concerning the pandemic. These residents did not get their
news from social media or online and therefore wanted more information in the local paper. A
discussion ensued about putting a “Mayor’s Message” in the local paper. Councilor Seely suggested that
a message could be sent with the utility bill mailing.
