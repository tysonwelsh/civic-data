Wendelin Knobloch said this city-initiated request to amend the Midvale City Municipal
Code provided in Exhibit A harmonizes the local code language with changes that took
place in lhe 2023 General Session of the Utah Legislature.

House Bill 406 modified Utah Code Annotated (UCA) 10-9a-532, which is entitled
"Development Agreements", to read as follows:

(d) A municipality may not require a development agreement as a condition for
    developing land if the municipality's land use regulations establish all applicable
    standards for development on the land.

This change preempts the municipalities in Utah and requires that the following areas in
the Midvale Municipal Code omit development agreements because the land use
regulations establish all applicable standards for development on the land:

After this item was presented to the Planning Commission, the Legal Department
identified a concern with the proposed amendment while working on a related project.
The proposed amendment provided to the Planning Commission, which Legal had
reviewed and edited, may have had the effect of altering the master development
agreements and the vested rights of the current developers in Bingham Junction and
Jordan Bluffs. The Legal Department did not intend for this outcome. The language in
Attachments B, D, and E has been updated to address this concern while meeting the
requirements of state law. The updated language proposed to the City Council in the
attached ordinance should meet the City's contractual obligations under the master
development agreements while also maintaining the developers' vested rights and the
structure of the existing development agreements. ln accordance with state law, the
requirement to enter into a development agreement as a condition to engage in certain
development within these zones will end upon termination or expiration of the respective
master development agreements.

VII. DISCUSSION ITEM
     A. CITY COMMITTEES POLICY DISCUSSION
Matt Dahl reported Chapter 2.24 ol Midvale City Code establishes standing committees,
the rules for creating new committees, and the ability to recognize community
organizations. Midvale City staff is proposing to modify Chapter 2.24 to provide the City
Council with more flexibility to create new committees and to remove the recognition of
community and neighborhood organizations.
Chapter 2.24 indicates that:
"The city council may form committees composed of its own members and/or others for
such purposes as it deems appropriate."

However, Chapter 2.24 also places regulations on the how city committees may be
formed and operated, which creates uniformity, but also limits the options of the City
Council. Staff proposes that Chapter 2.24 be simplified to indicate that the City Council
may form new committees by resolution that will establish the rules by which the
committee will operate. This would allow for committees to be created to meet specific
community needs without conflicting with Midvale City Code. Depending on the purpose
and activities of the proposed committees, state law may require specific policies and
procedures that would need to be included in the resolution creating the committees (e.9.,
noticing requirements, records keeping, etc). Outside of the standing committees
established specifically in City Code (e.9, Audit Committee, etc.), the Harvest Days
Committee is the only currently active city committee. Staff anticipates that the proposed
change will allow the City Council to find creative solutions for forming a new community
council and formalizing the city's relationship with the Community Violence Coalition.

Staff also proposes deleting Article lll. Community/Neighborhood Organization
Recognition, Staff believes that this chapter was originally created to recognize the
Midvale Community Council, regulate its operation, and let it be independent. However,
regulated independence creates liability issues for the City and complicates the
administration of the community organization. Staff believes that it would be better for the
community organizations envisioned by this policy to become formal committees of the
city or remain independent and work with the city by agreement. Given that this chapter
of code has not be utilized in many years, if ever, staff believes that the deletion of this
chapter of code would have no impact on the City.

Staff will provide additional information regarding the proposed changes to Chapler 2.24
during the meeting and will be prepared to discuss options with City Council.
