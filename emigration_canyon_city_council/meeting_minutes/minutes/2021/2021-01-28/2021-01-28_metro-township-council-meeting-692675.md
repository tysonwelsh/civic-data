Mayor Smolka read the following statement regarding Utah Code 52-4-202:
Pursuant to Utah Code 52-4-202 the undersigned, as Mayor of the Emigration
Canyon Metro Township Council, hereby declares that providing an anchor location for the
electronic meetings of the Council will present a substantial risk to the health and safety of those
who may be present at the anchor location for the following reasons:
The Township is located in Salt Lake County and is still under an “Yellow” alert
stage for the COVID 19 pandemic; and
The regular meeting place for the Council (the Fire Station in the Canyon) does
not have sufficient space in the meeting room to provide for the recommended physical distancing
to keep people safe from infection.

PUBLIC MEETING
Community Input
Citizen Public Comment
TJ Winger stated he is concerned about the ongoing battle between bus stops
and residents that want less stops. He has reached out to various entities and read through the
Lochner Study to see if there is something that can be done. The largest bus stop is near his
home and is being eliminated because there is not a crosswalk. He proposed that discussion be
reopen with the PEC, Granite School District, and the Metro Township Council. After talking to
the school district, they say it is one of the most dangerous routes in the district because of the
blind curves and bicyclists. There are some spots that are natural congregation spots. Crosswalks
are a powerful tool; it helps calm traffic and slows traffic speed.
Nick Jacobsen stated he has the same concerns as Mr. Winger. His daughter
came home from school and was informed that she would not be picked up at her normal bus
stop. He called the school district to discuss the situation and the response was that the bus route
was unlikely to resume. As he was preparing a presentation for the school district, they called and
said they would keep the bus stop. A crosswalk at the bus stop would help with safety. How can
a plan be established to determine where bus stops are going to put so the battle does not
continue?

Emigration Canyon Community Council Report (ECCC)
Bill Toby, Chair, stated the Community Council spent its January meeting
developing plans for the coming years. The wildfire preparedness study is moving forward, and
fire season fuel mitigation is underway. The chipping event will be split into smaller events,
depending on what resources are available. An outdoor Firewise open house is being planned for
this spring if the gathering can be held safely. The Community Council is interested in identifying
ways to work more closely with the Metro Township Council to help relieve some work.

Unified Police Department (UPD)
Officer Jake Elsasser reviewed the calls for service for December. There were a
number burglaries in the canyon, most of them were smash and grabs. There were 470 calls for
service in 2019 and 615 in 2020.

Unified Fire Authority (UFA)
Captain Michael Conn stated he wanted to recognize Bill Tobey for the

Council Member Reports
Unified Police Department (UPD) & Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA)
Council Member Brems stated he was appointed as the Chair for the
compensation subcommittee. With the departure of Taylorsville City, UPD is preparing a
reorganization plan. As part of the plan some of the personnel will be eliminated. The Board is
still working on increasing compensation for its officers to bring them into the top three agencies
in Utah. A couple of new members joined SLVLESA. The tax levy is being modified so in the
future there will be enough taxes to fund UPD.

Unified Fire Authority (UFA) & Unified Fire Service Area (UFSA)
