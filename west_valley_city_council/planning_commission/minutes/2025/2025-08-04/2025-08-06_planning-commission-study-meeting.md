PRESENTATIONS:

SMI-6-2025 Table Rock Capital is requesting minor subdivision approval for the Lexington Park Minor
Subdivision. The subdivision will consist of 2 lots on 10.2 acres, is located at 2309 West 3650 South
and is zoned RM. Staff -Steve Lehman at 801-963-3311 or steve.lehman@wvc-ut.gov.

Discussion: Steve Lehman presented a development application regarding a three-acre parcel of land
behind Lexington Park Apartments. The area, previously utilized as a recreational space, has recently
experienced a decline in maintenance resulting in weeds and debris. To address this, the owners are
seeking to increase the density on the property with an anticipated for-sale townhome development. This
opportunity has arisen in conjunction with their current refinancing process to create a second lot.
However, this action would necessitate an amendment to the existing development agreement as the
current terms do not permit a change in density. If the owners proceed, the City would require that 3650
South be terminated correctly in a cul-de-sac by coordinating with Salt Lake County and an individual
owner to secure a little sliver of land at the northwest end. The cul-de-sac would be built at the
developer's expense on condition of approval. The Planning Commission expressed concerns about
potential City Council resistance to higher density townhome development, particularly given the
existing density in the surrounding area.
CA-1-2025 Petition by Intermountain Fuel Delivery Inc. requesting a conditional use amendment for
outside storage. The property is located at 2565 Decker Lake Lane on 2.45 acres (.29 acres for storage
use) and is zoned M, Manufacturing. Staff – Jody Knapp at 801-963-3497 or jody.knapp@wvc-ut.gov.

Discussion: Jody Knapp presented a conditional use amendment application for Intermountain Fuel
Delivery's outside storage facility. The site, which is about 2.5 acres and uses a third for storage, was
previously approved for outside storage in 2007. Prior to that this property was part of the Redwood
Elementary property. The proposal involves storing one tank wagon, a GMC Dually truck, a trailer,
shipping containers for tools, meters, a forklift, and nine fuel rental tanks placed in the back corner on
the existing gravel rather than pavement. Jody highlighted that the site would not be heavily used and
that the business would need to obtain a hazmat permit. No additional landscaping or screening is
required or triggered at this time due to its low level use.

The Planning Commissioners raised concerns about open storage requirements and the potential future
development of the Redwood Elementary site. Jody explained that the current use of outside storage is
simply being amended to allow for about a third of an acre for storing four trucks does not require
screening or landscaping, as it would only have a minor impact. She clarified that any changes to the
neighboring property, such as a school redevelopment, would trigger new requirements for screening.

A few of the Commissioners raised concerns about potential fuel tank leaks onto the gravel and also
road dust. Jody explained that hazardous materials permits are required through the fire department and
storm water permits through Public Works to ensure environmental safety. She noted that tracked
equipment and gravel surfaces are currently acceptable, but hard surfacing could be required if the
Commission so chooses. Jody added that dust control measures are in place, including the use of
compacted gravel, and explained that citations can be issued if dust complaints arise.
