UDOT Funding Briefing: TIF Bond/Transportation Solutions Recommendation - Teri Newell, UDOT A review of the recent UDOT State Transportation Improvement Program (STIP) workshop was given. The STIP selects transportation projects to fund in our area, and the state as a whole. An additional $8-10 million is left over from pavement jobs and the previous choke hold program that will be presented to the Transportation Commission. Items that moved up on the priority list are the I-15 Technology Corridor Project, request for a road expansion on US-189 near Charleston, funding for the SR-73 frontage road, parking issues in Provo Canyon, and various environmental projects. The projects listed are proposed to be included in the STIP and are scheduled to be approved at the next UDOT Commission meeting scheduled on May 19, 2017, 8:30 a.m. in Heber City, Utah. TIP Amendment: Moving Funds from Jordan River Connector Trail to Lehi Rail Trail Phase 3 - Jim Price, MAG Staff Lehi City has two Active Transportation Trails projects: the Murdock Canal Trail that connects with the Jordan River Trail, and the Lehi Historic Rail Trail connecting the Murdock Canal Trail on east side of I-15 north to Draper. It is recommended by MAG staff to transfer $644,000 from Jordan River Connector Trail to the Lehi Rail Trail Phase 3 which will fund under-crossings near Traverse Mountain and the cement plant in Lehi. Commissioner Graves moved to table the requested to transfer funds until the next Regional Planning meeting so the County Commission can have an internal discussion. Mayor Wimmer seconded the motion. Mayor Gygi asked for all who was in favor. The Committee voted yea with the exception of Mayor Wilson who voted nay. Adopt FY18 Annual Unified Planning Work Program Executive Summary - Shawn Seager, MAG Staff Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee May 4, 2017 Page 4 The Unified Planning Work Program is the annual budget for Regional Planning and illustrates where planning resources are allocated; describing the planned work budget, and funding sources that develop a coordinated transportation planning process. The FY2018 budget of $2,620,603 is 3% less when compared to last year’s $2,688,474 budget, with a $67,871 decreases from FY2017. This is due to a decrease in pass-through contracts, rollover and carry forward studies. Revenue for the $2.6M budget consists of approximately 93% federal funds from the United States Department of Transportation, and 7% local funds from UTA, UDOT, and our Local Governments. For questions or concerns, contact: Shawn Seager at sseager@mountainland.org or Steve Johnson at sjohnson@mountainland.org Mayor Hadfield move to adopt the FY2018 Annual Unified Planning Work Program Executive Summary. Mayor Thompson seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. Gardner Policy Institute’s County Population Projections – Aaron Cloward, MAG Staff MAG is collaborating with the Gardner Policy Institute (GPI) in collecting county and state populations. They are also working with county officials to get more accurate population numbers. MAG staff have worked collaboratively with GPI in developing the draft 2017 projection series. GPI projections are out to 2065. The next set of estimates should occur in 2023 after the next decennial census in 2020. The final project will be released in July 2017. UVU Pedestrian Bridge Update - Chad Eccles, MAG Staff The UVU Pedestrian Bridge project is a top concern for UVU. Currently, individuals who commute using Frontrunner, and desiring to get to UVU’s east campus must cross I-15 using the University Parkway Interchange. Due to accidents and fatalities, providing a more convenient and direct walk to campus is essential and may also encourage transit riders to continue to use Frontrunner for their trip to campus. An engineering firm is currently under contract to provide a better cost estimate for the bridge as well as an architectural rendering of what it may look like. Mayor Christopher Pengra raised concerns as to why UTA is funding the UVU Pedestrian Bridge project, when there are fast growing communities on the west side of Utah Lake that lack sufficient transportation. Chad Eccles stated that UTA was required by legislature to fund this one-time project through the bonding bill.
