Open Public Meeting Act Amendment

             Mayor Kelly Bush read the following into the record:

                          Electronic Meeting Written Determination
                                           of the
                                   Kearns Metro Township

      Pursuant to House Bill 5002, “Open and Public Meetings Act Amendments,”
      passed during the 2020 Fifth Special Session of the Utah Legislature and codified
      under Utah Code Ann. § 52-4-207(4), I, Mayor Kelly Bush, hereby make the
      following written determination warranting the Kearns Metro Township Council to
      convene and conduct an electronic meeting without an anchor location:

      Given the ongoing Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic, conducting
      the meeting with an anchor location presents a substantial risk to the health and
      safety of those who may be present at the anchor location.
      This written determination shall expire 30 days from today.

      Dated this 13th day of July, 2020.

      By /s/ KELLY BUSH

Public Hearing – Land Use Authority for Subdivision

               Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, stated on March 3,
2020, the Council adopted a six-month moratorium on certain types of development, in order to
provide time to update the Subdivision Ordinance within the Kearns Metro Township Code of
Ordinances. These updates were needed to make sure the ordinance complies with and
conforms to the Kearns Metro Township form of government. The ordinance sets out a process
for approving subdivisions. It gives the Kearns Metro Township Council the authority to approve
preliminary plans for a subdivision with the recommendation from the Kearns Metro Township
Planning Commission. Once approved, if there are substantive changes such as an increased in
the number of lots, changes to the access lot point, boundary changes, or new plans required for
improvements, then the subdivision plan needs to come back before the Council for another public
hearing.

Another key change is the ordinance requires all materials associated with the proposed
subdivision to be submitted to staff 30-days before a hearing can take place. This will allow time
for staff, the planning commission, and council to review the proposed subdivision.

8th Amendment to Interlocal Agreement with UPD relating to Weighted Voting

               Council Member Peterson stated at the last UPD Board meeting, the board
passed the weighted voting by a vote of 11 to 1. The rights of the minority were heard, now it is
time to do the will of the majority.

             Council Member Peterson moved to approve the 8th amendment to the Interlocal
Agreement to allow for weighted voting on certain budgetary motions.

               Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, stated it is unclear what
would happen if an individual member refuses to ratify a decision by the board. The member
could be kick off the board or if could lose its ability to vote. The interlocal agreement states “that
once the board has approved an amendment, each individual member shall promptly ratify it.” It
is mandatory.

              Council Member Peterson, seconded by Council Member Schaeffer, moved to
approve the 8th amendment to the Interlocal Agreement to allow for weighted voting on certain
budgetary motions. The motion passed unanimously.

First Amendment Discussion

               Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, stated he would like to
get approval from the Council to investigate amendments to the mass gathering ordinance. The
current ordinance lumps athletic, entertainment, or political events together. He felt they should
be separated. The township has the authority to grant special event permits for anything taking
place on its streets, sidewalks or its property, but cannot impede the first amendment – free
speech and the right to assemble. The current ordinance requires a permit with an up to $2
million bond for certain events, which makes it difficult for anyone to comply. He wanted to make
the permit process easier so more people would be willing to work with Kearns, this way Kearns
would have more control over the event.

               Council Member Peterson stated he agreed that political events and parties are
two separate things and should not be lump together. However, he did not want to mess with first
amendment rights.

Council Members unanimously agreed instructing Mr. Bracken to investigate this is more detail.

Electronic Meetings

                Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, stated the Legislature
made changes to the way electronic meetings work. It did away with the requirement to have an
anchor location; however, before any meeting a statement needed to be read indicating the
council feels there is a substantial risk to the health and safety of those who may be present at
an anchor location. The statement needed to be included in any notice regarding the meeting.
