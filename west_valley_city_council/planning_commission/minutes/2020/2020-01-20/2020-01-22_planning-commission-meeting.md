Applicant:    ZT-17-2019
              West Valley City


A zone text change to amend the criteria used to determine whether a property is eligible to
be rezoned to the RM zone, parking lot lighting standards, and the multi-unit residential
design standards

This application was continued by the Planning Commission during their meeting on January 8th.
During this meeting, Randy Moore, a homebuilder, suggested several changes to the ordinance.
These suggested changes are included with this report. The Commission continued this item to
allow time to consider potential changes.

On 9/17/2019, the City Council adopted a 6-month temporary land use regulation or moratorium
that prohibited rezone applications to the RM (multiple unit dwelling residential) zone as well as
ordinance revisions pertaining to multi-family housing. Prior to the moratorium adoption the
City had received several applications to change the criteria used to determine whether a
property is eligible to be rezoned to the RM zone as well as several rezone requests to the RM
zone. The moratorium was passed to allow time for the City to consider where additional multi-
family housing should be allowed as well as the standards under which it should be allowed.

Following the adoption of the moratorium, the Council held two discussions during their study
meetings on 11/12/2019 and 12/17/2019. During the later meeting a draft of several ordinance
revisions was presented for discussion to the Council. The revisions incorporated standards used
in recent development agreements for multi-family housing and changes to the RM zone
eligibility criteria. The Council then directed staff to start the formal ordinance revision process.

The proposed ordinance revisions are summarized as follows:
      Section 7-5-101 (4) a.: The minimum area for a vacant property to be rezoned to the RM
       zone is reduced to match the existing 1 acre minimum for planned unit developments
       (PUDs). A minimum area for a developed property to be rezoned to the RM zone is
       added to match the existing 20,000 sq. ft. minimum lot size for the RM zone. The density
       cap is removed for properties within ½ mile of a light rail station.
      Section 7-5-101 (4) b.: All properties that are within ½ mile of a light rail station and
       meet the minimum area requirements are made eligible for RM zoning.
      Section 7-5-101 (4) d.: A traffic impact study requirement is added for projects with 100
       or more dwelling units.
      Section 7-9-113: Parking lot lighting requirements are updated to reflect standards from
       the Illuminating Engineering Society.
      Section 7-11-404 (1): A prohibition on exterior stairways is added with one exception.
      Section 7-11-404 (7): A requirement for all units to include washer and dryer hook-ups is
       added.
      Section 7-11-404 (9): The garage parking requirements are increased for units that are
       stacked on top of each other and units that are attached to the side of each other.
      Section 7-11-404 (10): A requirement for all units to include a patio or balcony is added.
      Section 7-11-404 (11): The minimum dwelling unit sizes are increased for units that are
       stacked on top of each other.
      Section 7-11-404 (12): The minimum dwelling unit sizes are increased for units that are
       attached to the side of each other.
      Section 7-11-404 (13): A requirement for on-site management is added for all rental
       developments with 50 or more units.
      Section 7-11-404 (14): A requirement for controlled access is included for all rental
       buildings.
      Section 7-11-404 (15): A requirement for security cameras is added for all rental
       buildings.
      Section 7-11-404 (16): A requirement for elevators is included all buildings with four or
       more levels.
      Section 7-11-404 (17): A requirement for an entry feature is included.
      Section 7-11-404 (18): A requirement that all parking is to be made free of charge is
       added.
      Section 7-11-405: The list of allowed building materials is expanded to include tile and
       metal panels.
      Section 7-11-412: The number of amenities required is reduced because certain amenities
       are now required for all projects. The list of amenities is amended to reflect newer
       developments. A separate amenities list is provided for projects with units that are
       stacked on top of each other as well as projects with units that are attached to the side of
       each other.

The changes described above are recommended for primarily two reasons. First, the changes to
Section 7-5-101 will expand the eligibility for RM zoning. While eligibility for RM zoning
would be expanded, the Planning Commission and City Council can still deny zone change
requests to the RM zone even if a property meets the application eligibility standards. Second,
the remaining changes mainly add design standards used in recent development agreements to
the ordinance. These new standards will raise the bar on the quality of new developments.

As a reminder, the multi-unit residential design standards can still be superseded by a
development agreement that is reviewed and approved by the Planning Commission and City
Council.

While the Commission discussed a few potential revisions to the ordinance during the study
session, staff is still recommending the ordinance as written.

Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval. The ordinance should be approved as proposed by staff.

   2. Approval. The ordinance should be approved as proposed by staff with one change. Zone
      change requests to the RM zone should be allowed on properties with access and frontage
      on a street with a planned right-of-way width of at least 80 feet. The density of such
      properties should be limited to 6 units/acre.

   3. Approval. The ordinance should be approved as proposed by staff with the following
      changes:
         a. Zone change requests to the RM zone should be allowed on properties with access
            and frontage on a street with a planned right-of-way width of at least 80 feet. The
            density of such properties should be limited to 6 units/acre.
         b. The three light rail stations that were added to the proposed ordinance should be
            removed.

   4. Continuance.

   5. Denial.

               Enterprise Rent A Car

The applicant, Enterprise Rent A Car, is requesting a conditional use amendment to construct a
detail/car wash bay at the existing car rental facility at 4290 W. 3500 S. This property is zoned
C-2, General Commercial. The West Valley City General Plan designates this area as General
Commercial. The surrounding zone is R-1-8 to the north, RMH to the east and C2 to the west
and south. The surrounding uses include residential to the north, Westcrest Mobile Home Park to
the east, and auto related sales/services to the west and south.

This site was approved as car sales in 1999 (C-27-99). It was later expanded to car rental
(Enterprise) and a shop building was added in 2010 (C-8-10). At this time Enterprise would like
to add an additional building on site for a detail/car wash facility. Per 7-6-301 auto repair is a
conditional use when located adjacent to residential uses.

The new building will be approximately 30’x34’ (1020 square feet). The facility will just be used
for the rental vehicles and would not be open to the general public. It is a bucket wash facility
with one door on the south side and it is not a drive-thru automatic wash. The building will be
used to clean return rental cars that includes vacuuming, light washing and interior cleaning. This
facility would operate Monday – Friday from 7:30 AM to 6:00 PM, Saturday 9:00 AM to 1:00
PM and potentially Sunday 9:00 AM to 12:00 PM.

The building is new development on the site however since the use is accessory to the primary
use on site the building does not have to meet the full commercial design standards. However, it
does need to meet the following in 7-11-312(4):

       Separate Structures (carwash, cashiers booth, canopies over gas pumps, etc.) on the site
       shall have the same architectural detail, design elements and roof design as the primary
       Structure, including a comparable pitch or Parapets for roofs, same Cornice treatment,
       same materials and colors, etc.

The two existing buildings are a combination of block and stucco that are painted cream with a
flat roof with black accents. The exterior of the new building will be stucco painted to match the
existing buildings on site. Initially the proposal was for a pitched roof on the building. Staff
reviewed to Code requirement with the applicant and they are going to modify the submittal and
change the roof design to match the existing buildings on site.

Some of the landscaping on site has been removed or has died and not been replaced over the
years. Therefore, landscaping will need to be added to the site, so it is compliant with the prior
site approvals and the City Code standards. There should be 1 tree every 300 sqft along the north
and east sides of the property adjacent to residential. There should also be 1 tree for every 600
square feet along the street frontages on the west and south. The south frontage along 3500 S. is
also a high image arterial and subject to 7-10 based off the valuation of the new building, which
is $55,000. Staff recommends that an updated landscaping plan be submitted showing the proper
number, type and size of the trees and landscaping for this site.

       N: 4 existing/ 6 required and 50% live plant material
       S: 2 existing / 6 required
       E: 8 existing/ 8 required
       W: 4 existing (Clump)/ 8 required

ISSUES:

     The developer has submitted a preliminary plat to be known as the Hunter Creek
      Subdivision. The subdivision will consist of 2 lots on .61 acres. Although zoned R-1-8,
      both lots exceed the minimum square footage requirement. Lot 1 is approximately 12,550
      square feet while lot 2 is approximately 9,053 square feet in size. Both lots meet the
      frontage requirement of the R-1-8 zone as well.

     The proposed subdivision is a key piece in connecting two different subdivisions. The
      Western Acres Subdivision to the north consists of 2 phases and was platted in 1960 and
      1961. The Bridlevale at Wallace Farms Phase 3 Subdivision was recorded in 1996. The
      property in question was not made part of either subdivision but does have a single-family
      dwelling on what will become lot 2.

     Access to the subdivision will be gained from the extension and connection of Hawkeye
      Street which exists on both the north and south sides of the subdivision. The connection
      of these streets will provide a secondary access into the Western Acres Subdivisions to the
      north. At the present time, the Western Acres Subdivisions consist of 83 lots with a single
      point of access at 4000 West. The Fire Code allows up to 30 single family homes on a
      single point of access. While the subdivisions to the north predate the current Fire Code,
      it will be beneficial to the Western Acres Subdivisions to have a secondary access for
      emergency services.

     As discussed in the study session, part of the reason it has taken so long for this property
      to develop, is that the previous owner sold the east parcel to an individual without
      processing a subdivision plat. It just so happened that within that transaction, property that
      would be needed for the extension of Hawkeye Street was sold as well. The property owner
      did not want to dedicate that portion of his property for the extension of Hawkeye Street.
      Therefore, development of the remaining property could not occur.

     In 2018, the City learned that the east parcel was purchased by a new owner. The City
      approached that owner and purchased the east portion of what will become Hawkeye
      Street. Mr. Hinojo will dedicate the remaining portion of the needed right-of-way to make
      the connection. The City Engineering Division will be designing the public improvements
      and will be building these improvements. Mr. Hinojo will reimburse the City for his half
      width portion.

     An old irrigation ditch is located on the property. Staff understands that this ditch is no
      longer in use. The developer will coordinate this issue with the City Engineering Division.
        A resolution to any concerns expressed by those reviewing this matter such as easements,
        piping and existing irrigation structures will need to be resolved prior to recordation of the
        subdivision.
