Fiscal Items

               Mayor Clayton reviewed the bill for attorney services for the month of September.

               Council Member Stitzer stated she had already read through the documents and
is comfortable moving to approve all three expenditures.

                Council Member Stitzer, seconded by Council Member Bailey, moved to approve
the attorney bill. The motion passed unanimously.

                                  −−−−−−−−−−−−−−

Update on Metro Township Bill

                      Mayor Clayton stated he and Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, attended
the interim hearing the week before and gave input on the metro township bill sponsored by
Representative Jordan Tuescher. He thought the hearing went well, and the vote taken passed
unanimously. He and Mr. Bracken discussed the possibility of meeting with Senator Wayne
Harper and the Municipal Services District to discuss the senator’s questions regarding the
Municipal Services District.

                                  −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
UFSA Proposed Tax Increase Presentation

                       Tony Hill, Chief Financial Officer, Unified Fire Service Area (UFSA),
delivered a PowerPoint presentation to satisfy the statutory reporting requirement for a proposed
tax increase. He reviewed information about UFSA; the purpose for the proposed tax increase;
the tax increase timeline; the fund balance without an increase; the fund balance with the
increase; increased cost factors; general inflation; wage inflation; capital replacement inflation;
upgrading fire stations; building construction inflation; and future needs.

                                  −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
Law Enforcement Community Survey

                         Council Member Stitzer stated Copperton Metro Township has now been
turned down by five different survey companies. The responses she has received so far indicate
that the project is not financially viable. She has two more potential companies to whom she will
reach out. She also plans on reaching out to various universities like the Hinkley Institute or the
Herbert Institute. She noted the total cost falls below the Copperton purchasing policy threshold
but that she will keep the Council informed.

                                  −−−−−−−−−−−−−−

Fee Schedule External Review Presentation

                       Mayor Clayton reported that the Municipal Services District (MSD) has
contracted with CBIZ, an outside contractor, to study fees being charged to ensure the fee covers
the work being done. It should be as revenue neutral as possible. The contractor has come back
with some recommendations, which will be reported on when the Council holds its public hearing
in December to review the fee schedule. Mayor Clayton distributed the Cost of Services Study
Summary of Findings and the Fee Cost Analysis Draft Report to the Council members and asked
that they review it and report back to him with any questions.

                                 −−−−−−−−−−−−−−

Copperton Quarter 3 Financials

                       Mayor Clayton reviewed the financials for the third quarter. Copperton has
a good fund balance, which it is using, in addition to ARPA funds, to upgrade the park. Like other
years, it has not asked for an increased budget.

                     Council Member Stitzer, seconded by Council Member Olsen, moved to
authorize Mayor Clayton to sign any subsequent documents relating to the sidewalk easement.

                                  −−−−−−−−−−−−−−

Amendment to Sidewalk Easement Agreement

                      Steven Kuhlmeier, Project Manager, Salt Lake County Engineering and
Flood Control Division, reviewed the amendment to the easement agreement. The property
owner is asking the County to perform the work of removing the concrete and installing the drain.
The project would cost about $10,400. Mr. Kuhlmeier favors performing the additional work to

alleviate any possible problems.

                       Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, stated 50
percent of the project would be grant funded.

                       Mayor Clayton stated the non-grant funded portion would come from the
MSD’s capital project funding.

                      Council Member Stitzer asked who would be responsible for maintaining
the drain.

                      Mr. Bracken stated the property owner would be responsible for
maintaining the drain and this would require a new agreement that would be recorded.

                      Council Member Bailey, seconded by Council Member Severson, moved
to create a new contract with a notice stating the property owner is responsible for the drain. The
motion passed unanimously.
