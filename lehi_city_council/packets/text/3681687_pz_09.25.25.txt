460   Commissioner Jackson requested a brief history of the evolution of the SHAR for the gathered
461   citizenry. Kim Struthers explained that the state requires all cities to have a moderate income
462   housing plan, and that one of Lehi’s goals was implementing some kind of overlay zone that
463   would help with the creation of affordable housing. Previously it was known as the Critical
464   Housing Overlay Zone (CHOZ). The city was going to identify different areas in which the
465   overlay zone could be applied in order to increase density above what the general plan had. One
466   of the original goals was to maintain owner-occupancy. The intermediate draft, the Affordable
467   Housing Overlay Zone (AHOZ), started discussing practical ways to limit the price of homes by
468   limiting the size of buildings and lots. The current iteration is trying to incorporate as much of
469   the feedback as has been given up to this point.
470
471   Commissioner Lockhart clarified that all previous iterations have been unanimously negatively
472   recommended.
473
474   Commissioner Kunze asked what the time requirement was from the state to meet our moderate
475   income housing goals. Struthers explained that the city reports annually to the state. He thinks
476   that as long as the state can see that the city is making progress and talking about the issue, the
477   city will be okay. He doesn’t think there is a timeline. Struthers clarified that the city is trying to
478   make a bottom-up solution rather than having the state mandate a top-down approach.
      Lehi City Planning Commission                      10                                 September 25, 2025
479
480   Katie Bussell presented the SHAR in its current form. It is a voluntary ordinance that would only
481   be allowed in R-3, R-2, R-1-Flex, R-1-8, R-1-10, and R-1-12 zones. It would grant a 150%
482   density bonus, so long as the lots meet all the requirements of the SHAR: that the lots are less
483   that 6000 sq ft, that the homes are less than 1200 sq ft without a basement or less than 1000 sq ft
484   with a basement, that the homes shall be owner-occupied, and that the homes be sold for
485   $400,000 or less. Applicants will submit concept plans that will be reviewed by city staff and
486   then city council. If the council approves the concept plan, they will direct staff to write a
487   development agreement, the concept plan and development agreement will go through planning
488   commission and city council.
489
490   Commissioner Lockhart asked for clarification on zoning specifics. Anything 12,000 sq ft or less
491   can apply for it.
492
493   Commissioner Kunze asked if there were any undeveloped areas that could be used for this.
494   Bussell explained that there are.
495
496   Commissioner Lockhart clarified that it was quarter acre lots that would be allowed for the
497   benefit of those here for previous items. She asked what the rights were for an overlay as
498   opposed to normal zoning. Struthers explained that applicants would have to go through a
499   development agreement process rather than just using the allowed zoning. Commissioner
500   Lockhart worries that developers will use this as a green light. She asked how the city could
501   prevent developers from using this if needed. Struthers explained that the ordinance requires a
502   development agreement, which the city is under no obligation to sign. He describes it as more of
503   a potential tool than a built in aspect of development.
504
