b. Discussion of ZM-18-014, 4046 South 700 West, Rezone from A-1 to RM, Subject to a
Development Agreement for a Planned Unit Development Consisting of 11
Townhomes by Applicant: Rick Whiting
Robert May said the area of the project proposal is agricultural and the property consists of
over an acre. A multifamily PUD is a conditional use in the RM zone. The General Plan
Future Land Use Map indicates the area as a Neighborhood 2 and 3, which accommodates
a mix of different higher density housing types. That density would allow for a range of 6-
35 units per acre with heights up to 2 and 3 stories. May showed the Council the site plan
but stated that the plan was conceptual at this point. The existing home would be converted
into a clubhouse with the capability to rent out rooms to the units’ guests. May went
through the specs of the project. The 3-story townhomes will be no higher than 28 feet in
height and each unit will comprise of 3,000 square feet, all 3-bedroom units. The covered
parking is included in the design of each unit with unobstructed space that is 20 feet wide
by 22 feet in length. The development meets Millcreek’s PUD and off-street parking
requirements. Adequate fire turnaround is provided where parking will be prohibited within
the approved fire access and turn-around facilities. Unified Fire Authority has found the
conceptual plan to be acceptable. Vehicular access to the development is designed to
provide smooth traffic flow with controlled turning movements and minimum hazards to
vehicular, pedestrian, and bicycle traffic. The maximum lot coverage for the proposal is
approximately 35% and the total open space is approximately 42%.

Council Member Marchant asked for a background of the project through the rezone
process. May said 11 units have been proposed. The first meeting was in August, there
have been 7 meetings total, and minor changes in the landscaping and CC&R’s have been
made during the process. The comments from the neighborhood meeting on September 20,
2018 was that 11 units was too dense for an agricultural area, and there were concerns
about traffic, loss of privacy, and loss of view of the mountains. The Millcreek Community
Council held a meeting on January 8, 2019 and recommended a continuance until another
neighborhood meeting was held to address concerns. The Planning Commission concurred
with the community council’s recommendation and asked the applicant to consider
removing the existing home, adding more guest parking, lowering the building heights, and
putting an age restriction on the project. There was another neighborhood meeting held on
February 1, 2019. Staff commissioned a traffic study and the conclusions stated that
mitigations were not recommended for the additional traffic. The Planning Commission, at
their March 5, 2019 meeting, recommended approval of the rezone with the conditions that
the PUD is a 55 and older community and the maximum height of the buildings is 28 feet
with no more than two stories.

c. Discussion of ZM-19-003, 3999 South 500 East / 534 East Delno Drive, Rezone from
R-2-10 to R-1-3, Subject to a Development Agreement for Three Twin Homes by
Applicant: Byron Goates
Erin O’Kelley said the applicant would like to rezone the property from R-2-10 to R-1-3.
The intent of this rezone is to create 3 duplexes on the property with 6 dwellings total. Each
dwelling will have its own driveway, shall be two stories, and have a 15-foot
backyard/patio. The 3 duplexes will all be accessing off of Delno with a 5-foot setback in
between buildings and no setback along the shared walls of the duplexes. Under section
19.14.050, the R-1-3 zone allows two buildings to share a common wall along a property
line. The applicant is also proposing enhanced energy efficient amenities to the units such
as charging stations and roof mounted solar panels. The proposal will turn into a
subdivision application and be 6 single-family lots. O’Kelley showed a site plan and a
rendering of the buildings to the Council. Staff will add the material use to the development
agreement. She compared the differences between the current zone and the proposed zone,
which allows for: 35 feet in height instead of 30 feet, 3,000 square feet per unit instead of
5,000 square feet, a 5-foot side interior setback instead of 8 feet, and a smaller lot width at
25 feet. The General Plan designates the property for a Neighborhood 3 which allows for
higher density residential, and a mix of private and public uses, commercial and non-
commercial. O’Kelley went over questions that the General Plan asks of rezones and how
the project met those standards.

O’Kelley said the neighborhood meeting was held February 19, 2019 and the concerns
were about the high density, loss of trees, and traffic. There was another neighborhood
meeting after the applicant changed the project on March 14, 2019 where one resident
attended and said they were happy the density had decreased. The project first went to the
Millcreek Community Council on March 5, 2019 with a 10-unit proposal and the council
recommended denial of the project because the density was too high. At their next meeting
on April 2, 2019 with the new proposal, they recommended approval subject to the
conditions in the development agreement as outlined by staff. A resident submitted a letter
and petition to staff about the concern for the high density on March 19, 2019 that
contained 18 resident signatures. O’ Kelley highlighted various altered aerial photographs
demonstrating use, density, and scale compatibility of the project to the existing
neighborhood. The Planning Commission recommended approval of the rezone subject to
the conditions in the development agreement outlined in the staff report by a 6-1 vote on
April 17, 2019.

3.4 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution 19-14, Authorizing the Issuance and
Sale of Not More Than $20,000,000 Aggregate Principal Amount of Sales Tax
Revenue Bonds, Series 2019; and Related Matters

Mayor Silvestrini said this item authorizes the issuance of $20 million in borrowing to
purchase the linear park strip as part of the city center master plan. He said the small area
plan has not been formally adopted for the city center but to get the ball rolling, the
Council needed to approve the resolution to enable the borrowing. The small area plan
has been recommended for approval by all four community councils and the Planning
Commission will review the item on Wednesday. He did not know what bond rating
Millcreek would receive from the rating agencies on the bond. The Council is moving
forward to meet the schedule with the rating agencies and get a bond rating. Millcreek
will submit information about the City’s finances and interview with the agencies in San
Francisco to get a bond rating for the City. It is expected to get a AA- bond rating, which
is good for a municipality. Millcreek will be paying for the bond with incremental tax
revenue from the Millcreek Center Community Reinvestment Area. Staff did not know
interest rates yet. If Millcreek does not like the bond rating, interest rate, or decide not to
do the city center plan, the Council does not have to go forward with obtaining that
financing. Mayor Silvestrini is interested in pursuing the park because the public
engagement on it has indicated that a majority of residents are in favor of it. He said the
area is a magnet for crime because of the vacancies. The City is pursuing redevelopment
of the area and the park will be the kick start to make that happen.

Mike Winder said luck is when opportunity meets preparedness. Staff prepares to keep
options open for the elected officials to use. He said the timeframe is working out
perfectly. The intent of the bond is to fund it with the proceeds of the community
reinvestment area. Community reinvestment areas (CRA) allow cities to capture the tax
increment in an area. Once property in the CRA is redeveloped, property owners will pay
more in property tax, which is the increment that the CRA captures for the next twenty
years. Millcreek wants the lowest interest rate possible, so that is why it is a sales tax
backed bond, but the intent is to fund it with the tax increment. Mayor Silvestrini said
Millcreek has an existing West Millcreek Redevelopment Area which has a fund balance
of about $1 million which can be borrowed to fund the interest cost on this bond, and it
can be paid back.

Jason Burningham, Lewis Young Robertson & Burningham, restated that parameters
resolutions are an enabling document that allows the City to proceed forward but does not
bind the City to issue bonds. It gives notice to constituents of certain parameters under
which the City will issue bonds. The resolution narrowly defines the amount, which is a
maximum aggregated amount of $20 million and could be done in multiple series. The
resolution provides provisions related to the maximum term, which is 21 years, so it can
match with the CRA for the 20-year period. The maximum coupon or interest rate the
bonds can mature at is 5%. He said that is not the borrowing rate, it is the maximum rate.
He anticipated a net borrowing rate of 3.2%. The last parameter is done through a public
offering, an underwriter is retained by the City and purchases the bonds at a rate and sells
those bonds. The maximum discount for the underwriter is at 97%, which is a typical
standard. The resolution sets a public hearing for June 10,2019 which gives more
opportunity for residents and stakeholders to weigh in. The only angst he has was about
the bond rating because Millcreek is a new city.

Mayor Silvestrini asked for public comment. There were no comments.
