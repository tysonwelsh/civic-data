Proposed Ordinance 2025–10 amending the Ogden Municipal Code by
repealing and readopting fees for the Marshall N. White Community Center;
and
Proposed Ordinance 2025–11 amending the budget for the Fiscal Year July
1, 2024 to June 30, 2025 by increasing the anticipated revenues and
appropriations for a gross increase of $161,625
          A memo from the Public Services Department regarding proposed fee and budget amendments for the Marshall N. White
Community Center came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the proposed new fee structure will replace the
previous fee structure for the center, which was last updated in 2015. The new fee structure is based, in part, on the analysis done
by the City’s project consultant as part of the financial operations review. The proposed amendment to the Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-
2025 budget will recognize anticipated revenue from the proposed Marshall N. White Center fee amendments. Based on the
proposed fees, it is anticipated that the new fees will generate $161,625 for the remainder of the current fiscal year. The proposed
budget amendment would recognize this revenue and appropriate the funding in the Public Services Department budget.
          Public Services Executive Director Anderson and Assistant Recreation Director Myers summarized the memo and used
the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the development of the fee schedule, which includes pool rental fees; indoor
facility rental fees; outdoor facility rental fees; daily admission fees; monthly pass fees; annual pass fees; program fees; and
miscellaneous fees. There are fees for residents and non-residents and, for most fees included in the schedule, the non-resident fee
is 30 percent higher than the resident fee. This is consistent with how the fee schedule has been structured in the past. The City
will also provide a fee reduction application process for residents and, if approved, a resident can be granted up to a 50 percent
reduction. There will also be group rates offered to large groups or teams, and the Public Services Executive Director will have the
authority to enter into a contract to establish corporate rates for monthly or annual passes or to provide for insurance
reimbursement of Center fees for wellness programs. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for
the meeting.
          Finance Director Sorensen used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss how the anticipated revenues associated
with the fee adjustments will be programmed in the FY2024-2025 budget. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the
information packet for the meeting.

Proposed Ordinance 2025–9 amending the Ogden Municipal Code to revise
admission fees for the George S. Eccles Dinosaur Park
          A memo from the Public Services Department regarding a proposed fee amendment for the George S. Eccles Dinosaur
Park came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the proposed amendments are being requested on behalf of the
Ogden Dinosaur Park and Museum Foundation. Any fee increases will not increase the city’s revenues but will help the
Foundation keep up with rising costs and to better align with similar providers. Fees for the Dinosaur Park were last updated in
2008 and then again in 2023.
          Dinosaur Park Executive Director Mejeur summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
summarize proposed fee adjustments at the park. The current proposal is to increase regular admission fees from $8.00 to $12.00
per day. Ms. Mejeur’s presentation offered a comparison of the Dinosaur Park fees with fees charged by other similar attractions;
information regarding current building projects at the Center; and marketing and tourism data related to the Park. For a copy of the
presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Council member Choberka asked if there will be a playground located outside of the Dinosaur Park that will be publicly
accessible free of charge. Ms. Mejeur stated there are plans for that type of project in the future; the playground project mentioned
in her presentation is phase one of an all-abilities facility that will be located within the Park and it will replace the old playground
that is nearly 30 years old. Phase two will be located outside of the Park gates and will be free to the public.
           Chair White introduced in writing proposed Ordinance/Resolution 2025-9, entitled:
                   “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Ogden Municipal Code by amending Subsection 4-6-1.B.7
                   to revise admission fees for the Eccles Dinosaur Park; to make technical changes; to appropriately amend the
                   index; and by providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final
                   passage.”

Mayor Benjamin K. Nadolski presented the proposed Fiscal Year 2025–
2026 Budget
          The Mayor’s Proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2025-2026 Budget came before the Council for consideration. A memo from
Council staff explained that, per Utah Code 10-6-111, the Mayor must present a proposed budget for the upcoming fiscal year
during the first Council meeting in May. Following the Mayor’s presentation, the Council accepts the proposed budget for review.
The Council holds a series of work sessions over the course of May and June to review the proposed expenditures for all
departmental accounts and all major and minor funds. A tentative budget is adopted after holding an initial public hearing. At least
one public hearing is held before adoption of the final budget. During the budget hearings, the Council outlines proposed changes
to the tentative and final budgets and accepts public input. Once the final public hearing is closed, the Council makes changes and
adopts the final City budget. By State law, a final budget must be adopted by June 30 unless a property tax increase is under
consideration, then the final budget must be adopted by September 1.
           Mayor Nadolski used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide his proposed FY 2025-2026 budget to the Council;
he stressed that his proposed FY26 budget does not include a proposed tax increase because it is important to recognize the
pressure that Ogden households are under at this time. He then provided a timeline of work that has been done by himself and his
Administration since his inauguration in January of 2024, with the ending goal being to implement “The Ogden Way”. The
mission of the City’s 700 employees is “fueled by a champion’s drive and an unwavering commitment to service, we innovate and
collaborate to overcome challenges, foster opportunities, and create an environment for our people, businesses, and partners to
earn success.” He added the City’s vision of the community and for the people is “A safe, vibrant and thriving community of
opportunity where you and I grow, connect, and succeed.” The seven pillars of the City are safety, lifelong learning, stable
neighborhoods, vibrant economy, strong infrastructure, healthy lifestyles, and meaningful connections. He then discussed recent
local and national economic trends and data that have influenced the FY26 budget proposal, after which he presented a chart
illustrating the changes in General Fund major revenue sources as well as a comparison of the FY25 budget with the FY26
proposed budget. The General Fund budget will increase by approximately $4.8 million, while the total budget including the
Redevelopment Agency, Municipal Building Authority, and Strike Force will increase by $17.2 million. He presented his ideas for
ongoing operational improvements in the City, which are expected to yield increased efficiencies throughout the City. He also
reported the Grant Writer position was created in the FY25 budget and has had much success in securing millions of dollars in
grant funds for the Police, Fire, and Public Services Departments. Relative to employee compensation, the City implemented the
McGrath Compensation Study in August of 2024 and he has worked to continue to improve the compensation packages offered to
employees; this year he proposes a 2.97 percent cost of living increase for general employees and a 1.5 percent merit increase
based on performance. Public Safety sworn employees would also receive a 2.97 percent cost of living increase, and a merit
increase on their anniversary based on the adopted step program for Public Safety employees. Mayor Nadolski’s presentation then
highlighted vital Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) projects included in the budget, including parks and recreation projects,
infrastructure projects, economic development projects, public safety projects, and the Marshall N. White Community Center
(MWCC). He also highlighted the success of the My Hometown Ogden program, which is a joint effort of Ogden City, local
churches, and other organizations, working together to improve lives. It exists to encourage all residents to have the courage to
care for one another by providing platforms in the community that will elevate education, improve property conditions and
promote resources for better job opportunities. This program is expanding into other communities in Utah based upon Ogden’s
model. He concluded by thanking the Council for their support and reported that his proposed budget will be posted on the City’s
website tomorrow morning. For a copy of the PowerPoint presentation in its entirety see the information packet for the meeting.
           Chair White thanked the Mayor for his presentation and recognized the significant amount of time and work that it takes
for City Administration to develop the annual budget. She looks forward to reviewing the budget over the next several weeks
before adoption, which can occur in June.

Mayor Comments
          Mayor Nadolski thanked everyone for the respectful manner in which they engaged with the Council tonight; he
understands the frustration in the community in response to the closure of the trail, but he has spent a significant amount of time
considering this issue and discussing the matter with the City’s internal and external legal counsel and he feels the decision to
close the section of the trail that crosses the golf course was the correct one. The decision was not influenced by the revenue
potential at the golf course, but it is important to balance the needs of all parties and the appropriate use of the golf course and the
trail system. He is willing to consider reasonable alternatives and if there is a way to provide for a safe trail crossing on the golf
course, he is committed to finding it. He apologized to those he had not been able to respond directly to regarding this issue, but
he is grateful for all the input he has received and will commit himself and staff resources to work on a collaborative solution to
this matter. This City was in a vulnerable position due to the trail crossing on the golf course and that section will remain closed
until an alternative solution can be determined.

Council Member Comments
          Council members also thanked the public for their input tonight and expressed support for exploring collaborative
solutions to the matter of the closure of the section of the trail that crosses Mount Ogden Golf Course. They expressed support for
the Mayor as well and indicated that he is charged with making hard decisions for the community.
          Council member Blair stated that he has learned a lot of things over the past few days as this issue has been discussed
throughout the community; he does not necessarily believe the danger of being struck by a golf ball is greater for a trail user than
for a golfer and he is unsure why the liability is greater due to the presence of a trail crossing. He believes that anyone entering the
golf course understands they are at risk if being hit by a golf ball, regardless of whether they are a golfer or a trail user. He
believes this is a great opportunity to engage a passionate and knowledgeable group of residents to work on a solution to a
complex issue. He believes communication between the City and the public can be improved and that may help to prevent this
type of situation from occurring in the future.
          Council member Graf also thanked the public for the respectful manner in which they addressed the Mayor and Council
tonight; he encouraged them to stay engaged and be involved in important matters in the City. He looks forward to collaborative
discussions of the matter of the trail crossing through the golf course.
          Council member Richey echoed the comments that have been made by his fellow Council members; he is not familiar
with the section of the trail that crosses the golf course but is aware of other similar flat trails throughout the community that can
be accessed by the public. He does understand the trail on the golf course has been publicly accessible for over 20 years and it can
be difficult to say goodbye to it, but he hopes everyone will approach the matter with respect and a hope of moving forward in a
positive direction.
          Mayor Nadolski stated he feels there is an opportunity for the entire community to come together and work on this
situation; there may be a way to reduce the risk sufficiently to re-open the trail crossing.
          Chair White stated that she appreciates all the input that has been provided tonight, and she is supportive of exploring
solutions to all issues occurring on the golf course and trail system that are creating risk for the City.
          Vice Chair Hyer also thanked everyone for participating in tonight’s discussion with appropriate decorum and respect.
He agreed that access to outdoor and open spaces is important, but it is important to understand the difference between a child
playing outdoors and risking a scraped knee versus being struck in the head by a golf ball traveling 150 miles per hour. He stated
that everyone must accept that they may not be fully satisfied by the final decision is made, but he supported the idea of a
collaborative effort to find a solution.
