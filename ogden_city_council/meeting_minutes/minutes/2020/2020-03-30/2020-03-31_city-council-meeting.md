communities to determine their response and if there are opp01tunities for combining programs with them when it is possible to

emphasized her concern about allocating funds for the creation of new positions or programming when there is so much
uncertainty about the economy for the next several months; she proposed delaying or phasing implementation of new
programming until more information is available about the City's fmancial position. Council member Nadolski stated those

suggested delaying action on this issue to give the City and the Council sufficient time to take a pragmatic approach to budget
decisions.
          Planning Manager Montgomery then used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the concept of reconfiguring
the public noticing process, for which $5,000 of Strategic Planning funding has been requested. The goal of the reconfiguration is
to improve the manner in which the City provides information. Present notification methods for planning matters include posting a
notice on the subject property, mailing a postcard to property owners and tenants within a defmed area, posting notice of the
proposed action in three public places, advertise public hearings in the newspaper, and publish on the State's Public Notice
Website. Concerns expressed regarding the current process include restricted access to the information and the fact that many
people are not actively looking for information regarding a planning matter so they do not see it. The Planning Division's present
noticing volume is 9,200 mailed notices per year; the City Recorder's office mails an additional 3,000 notices per year. He would
like to make improvements to meet the goals of better infonning the public, provide access to additional infonnation, facilitate
uniformity in notification presentation, and better use staff time. The property posting could be used to encourage viewers to
inquire about the proposed action by calling the City or visiting a dedicated website containing information about it. Additionally,
the size and format of postcards would be adjusted to better use the space available and include clearer information about how to
get details about the proposal that is being advertised. For mailings of more than 200, the postage costs would be decreased from
$.55 to $.29. A website link will be included for the reader to find more information. The dedicated web page will be designed to
provide clear information about the proposal and give the reader the opportunity to provide written feedback. The Planning
Commission rep01t on the proposal will also be included on the page as a web link. He concluded that all noticing methods will be
uniform in their design.
          Discussion regarding the notice redesign project centered on the need to alert residents to the fact that a project has been
proposed on a property located in close proximity to their own property; often residents are not aware ofa proposed project until it
is approved and work has begun. Mr. Montgomery stated that he feels that the use of plain language and uniform design of all
notification methods will draw a person's attention to the importance of the subject matter. Chair Choberka agreed and noted that
she would like to find a way to gauge the success of the redesign. The Council provided positive feedback regarding the redesign

        Water, Sewer, and Storm Sewer Utility Bonds
                   Council Executive Director Eller-Smith explained City Administration is proposing a refunding of the Series 2012 and
        2013 Storm Sewer Revenue Bonds. (Given current market volatility, refunding will occur if it is financially reasonable.) The
        Administration is further recommending that additional bonds be issued in the amounts of approximately $20 million for water
        utility projects, approximately $15 million for sanitary sewer utility projects, and approximately $7.5 million for storm sewer
        utility projects. She noted the City's financial advisors will provide input on the bond proposals.
                   Laura Lewis, Lewis Young Robertson and Burningham (L YRB), used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss
        fluctuations in the bond market due to current economic conditions related to COVID-19; it is still a very good time to issue bonds
        and it is her advice that the City proceed with water, sewer, and stonn sewer utility bond actions. She provided a chart identifying
        projects that could be completed in each utility and summarized the terms of the super parameters resolution that the Council
        would need to adopt to give Administration the authority to proceed with bond issuance.
                   Fred Philpott, L YRB, then used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss financial modeling used to determine rate
        structure proposals for the City's utilities; L YRB has recommended adjustments to the City's rate model responsive to current
        conditions. This includes foregoing rate increases for FY202 l, maintaining inflationary increases, recommending consideration
        for future rate increases for the five-year period, reevaluating the proposed Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) projects dependent
        upon future rate increases, and reducing interest costs based on current market conditions via the $40 million in combined bond
        proceeds. He provided several charts that provided scenario analyses based on a combination of different options available to the
        City; bonding can be used as a tool to avoid immediate rate increases, but consideration of rate policy adjustments or CIP
        adjustments would be necessary. He concluded by noting current outstanding issues include evaluation ofa blended consumer
        price index (CPl)/monetary conditions index (MCI), evaluation of future rate increases or adjustments to the City's CIP, and
        updates to existing ordinances regulating fee collection.

        Proposed amendments to the Small Business Loan Program guidelines
        creating an Emergency Loan Fund: and
        Fiscal Year 2020 Budget Amendment - Small Business Emergency Loan
        Fund Donations:
                 Council Deputy Director Symes explained that as a part of the Annual Action Plan and the City's administration of
        Community Development Block Grant funding, program guidelines are developed for specific programs targeting specific needs.
        The program guidelines serve as general parameters for how the City will use funding for a particular program. These program
        guidelines are referred to in the Annual Action Plan but are entirely developed and adopted by the City. Amending the program
        guidelines is done through a resolution adopted by the City Council. Administration is proposing amendments to the Small
        Business Loan program by adding an Emergency Loan Fund designed to assist businesses impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic.
        As proposed, the Emergency Loan Fund would be funded through donations and would be administered under the Small Business
        Loan program. The Small Business Loan program would still adhere to the national HUD objective of supporting low and
        moderate-income individuals for federal dollars but would add a local objective to retain businesses and prevent job losses for the
        use of non-federal funds. The Emergency Loan Fund could be used in the future if donations are made to the city to fund the
        program. An initial donation of $100,000 has been made to the City to help small businesses affected by the economic impacts of
        the COVID19 pandemic. A companion budget amendment would enable the City to accept an anticipated donation, along with
    !   any further donations in total of up to $1,000,000, to extend suppmt to local small businesses during the Coronavirus Disease
j       (COVID-19) Pandemic.
                 Community and Economic Development Director Christopulos used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on
        the proposed program guidelines for the emergency loan fund. Emergency loan funds will be funded using Community
        Development Block Grant (CDBG) funds made through the Microenterprise Loan Program, the Small Business Loan Program,

           There was a brief discussion among the Council and Mr. Christopulos regarding the qualification criteria for applicants.
Mr. Christopulos emphasized that the funding being provided to businesses is a loan and must be repaid with a 10-year
amortization schedule.
           Council member Hyer asked ifhome-based businesses are able to apply for funding. Mr. Christopulos answered yes; the
loans for those types of businesses would be underwritten in a different manner. The City has not received applications from
home-based businesses.
           Chair Choberka asked why none of the loan programs are available to not-for-profit businesses. Mr. Christopulos stated·
that businesses typically rely upon the public for patronage, whereas not-for-profit entities rely upon contributors. In this type of
health crisis, the public cannot patronize a business while not-for-profit entities can still seek contributions. If the pandemic lasts
too long, however, it may be appropriate to consider a modification to program guidelines to give not-for-profit entities the ability
to apply.
           Comptroller Stout then explained the budget amendment associated with the loan program would be an appropriation of
donation revenue of $1,000,000 and adding an expenditure to the budget for the loans. The City currently does not have $1 million
in commitments and the fund will be monitored very closely to determining that loans are only made once funding has been
donated to the City.
           Council member White asked what will happen if the City does not receive the $1 million in donations. Ms. Stout stated
that at the conclusion of the current FY, the budget will expire and the two accounts will be reconciled. A similar line item can be
included in the tentative budget for the next FY, or once that budget is adopted, it can be amended to include the donations and
expenditure line items.
           Council member Stephens asked if private donors can contribute to the City's budget for this purpose, to which Ms. Stout
answered yes.

Council Business
           Emergency Declaration: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith explained that the Mayor has the authority to declare a
state of emergency, but only for a 30-day period; after the 30 days, a continuation of the state of emergency would require Council
authorization. She noted that Council staff has worked with the City Attorney's Office to develop a draft resolution for
continuation of the state of emergency and it can be included as an action item for the Council during the April 14 meeting.
           Moratorium on Evictions: Chair Choberka reminded the Council of their discussion during the March 24 meeting about
the recommendation from the housing committee associated with the Ogden Community Action Network (Ogden CAN)
requesting that the City Council and Administration consider a joint resolution that would support action by the State of Utah to
enact a 60-day moratorium on evictions. She facilitated a discussion about the information that could be included in the joint
resolution and subsequent letter to the State and whether the Council should focus on residential evictions or both residential and
commercial.
           Council member Blair stated that he has heard from several landlords who are concerned about the City becoming
involved in this issue; they have expressed that it is ah'eady very difficult and expensive for a landlord to pursue eviction. While
they understand the importance of giving everyone a place to live during a mandated quarantine, they are also concerned that
enacting a moratorium would give them an 'excuse' to not pay rent during this health crisis. Council member Stephens agreed and
noted he believes landlords are willing to work with tenants who may be fmancially impacted by a quarantine or shelter in place
order. It is important to remember that landlords have fmancial obligations associated with ownership of the rental property and if
they are not receiving rent, it can be difficult to pay their mortgage.
           Chair Choberka acknowledged the concerns of landlords but indicated that federal government has programs that can
help landlords if they are not receiving rent from their tenants. She stated the greatest concern associated with evictions is that
people that do not have a place to live will be forced to live in a homeless shelter or in another situation where they may be at
greater risk of contracting the coronavirus.
           The Council engaged in discussion regarding the concept of supporting a moratorium; the Council ultimately concluded
not to consider adoption of the draft resolution in its present form given that the City has no authority to impose a moratorium.
However, if the resolution can be adjusted to provide balanced support for both landlords and tenants, the Council may consider
adoption in order to communicate that type of message to the State of Utah.
