Council Business

Magna Coalition

                 Council Member Hull stated Magna United, a coalition of principals, school
coordinators, and community leaders, has merged with the Magna Coalition. It is going to be a
really effective group.

                                  −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
Unified Fire Authority (UFA)/Unified Fire Service Area (UFSA)

               Council Member Hull stated the UFA has had one firefighter test positive for
COVID-19. That person has recovered and is back to work. The UFA is taking precautions to
make sure its staff and firefighters are safe, as well as its new recruits who are still training. Dom
Burchett was promoted to Chief. Station 102 in Magna was significantly damaged from the
earthquake and the strong aftershocks. Firefighters from there have been moved to Station 111,
until the damage can be fix.

Alta is going to join the UFSA, and Brighton has already joined. Riverton is leaving the UFSA,
but not the UFA. The UFSA is working on the budget, hoping to keep the increase to member
cities at a maximum of 4 percent.

                                  −−−−−−−−−−−−−−

Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District (WFWRD)

                 Council Member Peel stated WFWRD has 800 reserve cans on a first come first
serve basis for disaster area cleanup. As of yesterday, 500 reservations had been made for those
cans. WFWRD is doing this as a trial in Magna only. WFWRD’s policies allow for additional cans
for disaster cleanup at discounted prices to municipalities. If the Council wanted to provide
additional help to citizens, he had that pricing information. WRWRD has mailed out a recycling
flyer, which is one of the best it has ever done. It also has an app, which is available at the Apple
store or on its website that tells if an item is recyclable or not.

Unified Police Department (UPD)/Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA)

                Council Member Prokopis stated the UPD Board of Directors recently had a
benefits and compensation meeting and a finance committee meeting. Over the last couple of
years, the board has been trying to address wages and benefits for officers. Many of the officers’
wages were as much as 15 percent out of market. Last year, it gave them an 8.75 percent raise,
and over the next several years, it will try to close the gap. This year, it will try to do a 2 percent
market adjustment and fund an internal step plan. The UPD would like its pay to be in the top
third of the agencies in Salt Lake County.

The Magna Metro Township is a member of SLVLESA, and its cost for police services went up
$440,000 to accommodate the raises. SLVLESA did a tax increase last year, so it should be able
to absorb those costs this year.

Resolution 20-04-04

               Paul Ashton reviewed the resolution identifying the intent to amend to establish
special land use rules, regulations, and management for FEMA-identified flood zones located in
Magna to apply for inclusion in the National Flood Insurance Program. This is based on the
template the Council received from FEMA, which Greg Schulz modified to confirm to Magna.

RESOLUTION NO. 20-04-04                                                          Date: April 28, 2020

       A RESOLUTION OF THE MAGNA METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL
       INDENTIFYING THE INTENT TO AEND SPECIAL LAND USE RULES,
       REGULATIONS, AND MANAGEMENT FOR FEMA-IDENTIFIED FLOOD ZONES
       LOCATED IN MAGNA TO APPLY FOR INCLUSION IN THE NATIONAL FLOOD
       INSURANCE PROGRAM

                                             RECITALS

   A. The Magna Metro Township (“Magna”) is a municipality pursuant to Utah Code
      Annotated (“UCA”) §§ 10-2a-401 et seq.

   B. The Magna Metro Township Council (the “Council”) is the municipal governing body for
      Magna pursuant to UCA § 10-3b-501.

   C. The Federal Emergency Management Agency (“FEMA”) is an agency of the United
      States Government tasked with assisting and supporting State, County, and Local

                                                  3
DATE         TUESDAY                                 APRIL                             28, 2020

      Governments with disaster preparation, training, incident management support, and
      possible emergency recovery efforts.

 D. Pursuant to the protection of people and property, and as part of a statewide flood zone
    identification study FEMA performed, they identified there are locations in Magna that
    are susceptible to a threat of flooding (“Flood Zones”).

 E. The identified flood zones include certain key facilities the public needs for the general
    health and safety of Magna.

 F. Prior to the incorporation of Magna on January 1, 2017, all land use decisions in flood
    zones were made and administered by Salt Lake County as the “municipal government”
    of Magna.

 G. Pursuant to Utah Code § 10-2a-414(3), Magna adopted all county ordinances including
    all ordinances relating to flood control.

 H. Many of the land use ordinances and regulations regarding flood zones that Magna
    inherited from Salt Lake County, along with the flood zone responsibility, may no longer
    conform to FEMA Standards.

 I.   In light of the March 18, 2020 Magna Earthquake, developing and implementing
      emergency management programs that identify and plan for certain disaster events has
      come to the fore.

 J. Identifying, evaluating and pursuing mitigation efforts within possible flood zones, and
    recognizing flood zones in official actions relating to land use as we move forward, and
    addressing issues in the FEMA-identified flood zones in the municipality, it is the intent
    of the Council to bring Magna’s flood zone maps, land use oridnances, regulations and
    hazard management into compliance with FEMA regulations.

 K. Pursuant to Utah Code §§ 10-3c-103, 68-3-12.5, and 10-9a-501 et al, the Council has
    the statutory authority to adopt land use and control measures to reduce future flood
    losses.

                                       RESOLUTION

      THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED, by the Magna Metro Township Council,

 1. The Council assures the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) that it will
    enact or amend as necessary, and maintain in force, in those areas having flood hazards,
    adequate land use and control measures with effective enforcement provisions consistent
    with the criteria set forth in Section 60 of the National Flood Insurance Program
    Regulations; and

 2. Vests the Flood Control Engineer with the responsibility, authority and means to:

               (a)      Assist the Director of Planning and Development Services (the “Director”
or the “Administrator”), at his/her request, in the delineation of the limits of the area having special
flood hazards.

               (b)      Provide such information concerning presents uses and occupancy of the
floodplain, mudslide (i.e., mudflow) or flood-related erosion areas as the Director may request.

                 (c)     Maintain for public inspection and furnish upon request, for the
determination of applicable flood insurance risk premium rates within all areas having special
flood hazards identified on a Flood Hazard Boundary Map (FHBM) or Flood Insurance Rate Map
(FIRM), any certificates of floodproofing, and information on the elevation (in relation to mean seal
level) of the level of the lowest flood (including basement) of all new construction or substantially
improved structures, and include whether or not such structures contain a basement, and if the
structure has been floodproofed, the elevation (in relation to mean seal level) to which the
structure was floodproofed.

               (d)    Cooperate with Federal, State and local agencies and private firms which
undertake to study, survey, map and identify floodplain, mudslide (i.e., mudflow) or flood-related
erosion areas, and cooperate with neighboring communities with respect to management of
adjoining floodplain, mudslide, (i.e., mudflow) and/or flood-related erosion areas in order to
prevent aggravation of existing hazards.

                (e)     Upon occurrence, notify the Director in writing whenever the boundaries of
the community have been modified by annexation or the community has otherwise assumed or
no longer has authority to adopt and enforce floodplain management regulations for a particular
area. In order that all FHBM’s and FIRM’s accurately represent the community’s boundaries,
include within such notification a copy of the map of the community suitable for reproduction,
clearly delineating the new corporate limits or new area for which the community has assumed or
relinquished floodplain management regulatory authority.

                (f)     Submit an annual report to the Administrator concerning the community’s
participation in the program, including, but no limited to the development and implementation of
floodplain management measures.

    3. Appoints the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District with the responsibility,
       authority, and means to implement the commitments as outlined in this Resolution.

   4. Agrees to take such other official action as may be reasonably necessary to carry out the
      objectives of the adopted floodplain management measures.

   APPROVED AND ADOPTED in the Magna Metro Township, Salt Lake County, Utah this 28th
   day of April, 2020.

Appointment to the Inland Port Authority Board

               Paul Ashton reviewed the appointment of Steve Prokopis as Magna’s designee
to the Inland Port Authority Board. The appointee should be a Metro Township mayor or council
member. A letter has been prepared to send to the Inland Port Authority Board. As soon as it
receives the letter, Council Member Prokopis can start attending meetings.

               Council Member Prokopis stated he already met with board members on a
conference call last Friday, at which time he met the Chief Operations Officer (COO) and Chief
Executive Officer (CEO) and was updated. He will have a formalized training this Friday. The
board meetings quarterly, but it did not have a meeting in the first quarter. It will have a couple
meetings in May to get caught up. Some of the legal challenges the board had are over, so it can
start making some progress.

               Council Member Peel asked if Council Member Prokopis could take on the Inland
Port Authority Board and the UPD Board.

               Council Member Prokopis stated he would like to try being on both boards. The
UPD board required a lot of time commitment, but he anticipated the Inland Port Authority Board
would not be that time consuming. He would know better in July whether he could manage both
commitments

             Council Member Hull, seconded by Council Member Peel, moved to approve the
appointment of Steve Prokopis to the Inland Port Authority Board. The motion passed
unanimously.
