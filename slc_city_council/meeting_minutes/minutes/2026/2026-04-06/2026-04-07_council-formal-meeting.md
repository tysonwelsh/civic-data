**Ordinance: Zoning Map Amendment at Approximately 256 East 300 South**
The Council will accept public comment and consider adopting an ordinance that would amend the zoning of the property at approximately 256 East 300 South from R-MU (Residential/Mixed-Use) to D-1 (Central Business District). The proposal would allow the potential development of a hotel within a new 185-foot building. If adopted, the applicant would then need to submit the necessary development applications, and the project would need to comply with all relevant regulations within the City zoning ordinance. The Planning Commission forwarded a negative recommendation. Consideration may be given to rezoning the property to another zoning district with similar characteristics. The project is within Council District 4. Petitioner: Emily Nelson of Fourier Architects, representing the property owner. Petition No.: PLNPCM2024-00423.

**FYI – Project Timeline:** (subject to change per Chair direction or Council discussion)
Briefing - Tuesday, March 10, 2026
Set Public Hearing Date - Tuesday, March 24, 2026
Hold hearing to accept public comment - Tuesday, April 7, 2026 at 7 p.m.
TENTATIVE Council Action - Tuesday, April 21, 2026

  Staff Recommendation - Refer to motion sheet(s).

**Summary:**

Brian Fullmer gave a brief introduction to the item.

**Public Comments:**

**Marilyn Chuch** stated opposition to the project, citing concerns about cityscape views, pedestrian safety, and the building’s incompatibility with the surrounding mixed-use neighborhood.

**Taylor Mott** stated opposition to the proposed rezone, citing concerns with increased building height and scale, incompatibility with the surrounding residential character, and additional traffic and late-night activity associated with hotel use.

**Travis Starley**, representing the Central City Neighborhood Council, stated opposition to the proposed rezone and that mixed-use zoning would better align with the Central Community Master Plan.

**Cindy Cromer** spoke on the responsibility of Council to steer development into designated areas and opposed the proposed rezone, arguing that amenities such as bike stations and hotels did not constitute sufficient community benefits for an upzone of that magnitude.

**Amy Atkinsons**opposed the rezone, citing concerns about unlimited building heights, increased development intensity, and inconsistency with the area's transitional character and planning goals.

**Marisa Mills** stated opposition to the proposed development, citing concerns that increased density would impact traffic and pedestrian safety, exacerbate parking limitations, and conflict with the residential character and existing infrastructure of the neighborhood.

**Ben Engel** representing the neighborhood group “Neighbors Who Care,” stated support for redevelopment of the long-vacant parking lot site but opposed the proposed D-1 upzone, citing concerns with the development team and height incompatibility with the neighborhood.

**Jay Nielsen** stated support for the proposed hotel, describing it as an art-focused development designed to support local artists through gallery space and attract high-end art buyers, potentially leading to economic benefits to local artists and the neighborhood.

**Emily Nelson**, project petitioner, stated support for the proposed development, citing benefits including housing, jobs, cultural-related uses, and its suitability for higher-density mixed-use development downtown.

**Dorothy Hamory** stated opposition to the proposed rezone, citing concerns that the building would reduce sunlight and views for nearby residences and that the transient nature of a hotel was incompatible with the residential character of the neighborhood.

**Annie Isaacson**, HOA President of Sampson Altadena Apartments, opposed the proposed rezone and presented physical building layout comparisons to demonstrate the proposed structure’s height relative to nearby buildings, citing concerns with the scale, massing, and compatibility of the project within the historic residential neighborhood.

 **Motion:
Moved by Council Member Lopez Chavez, seconded by Council Member Wharton to **close the public hearing and defer action to a future Council Meeting.****

**AYE:** Victoria Petro, Alejandro Puy, Chris Wharton, Eva Lopez Chavez, Erika Carlsen, Daniel Dugan, Sarah Young

**Final Result:**7 – 0 Pass

**Ordinance: Expiration of Land Use Approvals Text Amendment**
The Council will consider adopting an ordinance that would amend multiple sections of Title 20 Subdivisions and Title 21A Zoning of the *Salt Lake City Code* pertaining to the expiration of land use approvals. **Ordinance: Sugar House Hotel Master Plan and Zoning Map Amendments at Approximately 2111 South 1300 East**
The Council will consider adopting an ordinance that would amend the zoning of properties at approximately 2111 South 1300 East from MU-3 (Mixed-Use 3 District) to MU-8 (Mixed-Use 8 District). The proposal would also amend the Sugar House Community Master Plan Future Land Use Map from Mixed Use-Low Intensity to Business District Mixed-Use – Town Center Scale. The proposed amendments would allow the developer to build a hotel on the currently vacant site. The property is approximately .80 acres in size and abuts the northwest corner of Sugar House Park. The property has historically been privately owned and is not a part of the Sugar House Park property. Consideration may be given to rezoning the property to another zoning district with similar characteristics. The project is within Council District 7. Petitioner: John Potter with Magnus Commercial Properties, representing the property owner.

**FYI – Project Timeline:** (subject to change per Chair direction or Council discussion)
Briefing - Tuesday, February 17, 2026
Set Public Hearing Date - Tuesday, March 10, 2026
Hold hearing to accept public comment - Tuesday, March 24, 2026 at 7 p.m.
TENTATIVE Council Action - Tuesday, April 7, 2026

  Staff Recommendation - Refer to motion sheet(s).

**Council Remarks:**

Council Member Young emphasized the importance of Sugar House Park as a defining community landmark and cited concerns that approving the rezone would set a precedent for expanding the business district beyond adopted community plan boundaries before the impacts of the existing mixed-use zoning had been fully realized. Council Member Young thanked residents, community organizations, and the applicant for their engagement and professionalism throughout the process.

Council Member Puy thanked Council Member Young for her work on the item and for helping communicate community concerns and park user perspectives to the Council.

**Ordinance: Community Clean Energy Program**
The Council will set the date of Tuesday, April 21, 2026 at 7 p.m. to accept public comment and consider adopting an ordinance that would enact Title 9, Chapter 50 to the *Salt Lake City Code*, establishing participation in the Community Clean Energy program, formally known as Utah Renewable Communities. The proposal would provide a new opportunity for nearly all homes and businesses within the City to choose clean energy through their Rocky Mountain Power (RMP) bill. Customers will be automatically enrolled in the program, with the option to opt out. If customers choose to stay enrolled in the program, a new fee will appear on their RMP bill.
