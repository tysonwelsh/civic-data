April with approval in May. Recruit Camp 54 is currently in training. He said the UFA You-
Tube channel shows the training exercises. They are monitoring the Legislative bills for
health of first responders and several other bills. Captain Millard at Station 126 shared
an incident that happened on February 26'" where Engine 126 was dispatched to Work
Force Services for flooding. Upon arrival of UPD who was called first, determined the
parking lot was filling up with water and called UFA for assistance. They gained access
to the building and figured out that the sprinkler riser pipe had broken just below the floor.

There was no shut off valve to shut the water off. Midvale City Public Works was called.
Duke from Public works was very helpful throughout the incident. Several Midvale City
staff members were on scene. He thanked Midvale City staff and UPD for their assistance.

When these zones were created, they included standards for master planned
developments, streamlining the standards that had been part of the City’s earlier zoning
ordinance. These standards included development requirements, such as architecture,
open space, and trail networks, and development incentives, including a density bonus,
opportunity to build multifamily residential uses instead of single family, and others. These
standards have seen only minor modifications since adoption in 2001.
In recent years, City residents have begun expressing greater concerns with the impacts
master planned developments have on existing neighborhoods, particularly when
constructed as multifamily residential projects. To address the issue, the City Council
adopted a temporary moratorium, prohibiting multifamily residential master planned
developments in the SF-1 and SF-2 zones, in July 2020 (Ordinance No. 2020-O-11, see
attached) and directed Staff to begin working on amendments to the zoning ordinance to
address these concerns.
Staff has spent the last several months researching best practices and interviewing
Councilmembers to develop new standards for master planned developments. The new
language is not yet ready for adoption but the moratorium on multifamily residential
master planned developments expired in January 2021. A permanent fix is needed to
ensure the negative impacts of multifamily projects does not resume. Consequently, Staff

A. AMENDMENT OF SECTION 8.008.080 DISPOSAL OF COMMUNITY
REFUSE, BULKY WASTE, AND RECYCLABLE ITEMS TO ALLOW
RECYCLING SERVICES BY AUTHORIZED QUALIFIED ENTITIES
Garrett Wilcox said On December 8, 2020, the City Council listened to a presentation by
Jason Utgaard, the General Manager of Momentum Recycling, about providing curbside
glass recycling service to Midvale residents and businesses. Staff raised concerns about
the cost of the program and the limitations of Midvale’s current ordinance. The City
Council expressed support for allowing Momentum Recycling to provide its curbside glass
recycling service with the support of the City. The City Council directed staff to make the
necessary adjustments to the ordinance to allow the program.
Midvale Municipal Code Section 8.08.020(B) prohibits any person or entity from moving,
hauling, or transporting waste from the Midvale other than the City, its agent, or authorized
persons. Section 8.08.080(C) allows the City Council to implement recycling programs to
promote waste reduction. However, this section suggests that a Council-created recycling
program would be overseen by the City because it requires the rates to be established
and adjusted by resolution. This approach is not compatible with Momentum’s proposal
and Council’s direction.
In order to allow Momentum’s glass recycling program as envisioned, a new subsection |
has been drafted. The proposed Subsection 8.08.080(D) provides a process for entities
to receive the necessary authorization to move, haul, and transport specified materials
20

Proceedings of Midvale City Council Meeting

for recycling from Midvale. Rather than limiting this authorization to a curbside glass

recycling service alone, staff felt it would be expedient to draft the subsection to give the

City Council the flexibility to authorize other recycling services in the future. This would
remove the need to amend the ordinance if another entity approached the City about
providing another recycling service in the future.
As proposed, Section 8.08.080(D) establishes the requirements for the application
process and Council authorization, the minimum standards for providing a recycling
service, and the City’s ability to review, suspend, or terminate an entity’s authorization. It
has a very similar feel to a franchise agreement. The City will not have a direct relationship
with the entity providing the recycling service but will have legal and contractual authority
to regulate the provision of the recycling service.

Some of the highlights of this proposed recycling collection service subsection include:

° Authorization is limited to specified recyclable material and requires an entity to
receive new approval from the City Council if the entity wishes to expand its
collection service to other materials.

° Written authorization is required from the City’s current solid waste collection entity
and from a destination facility that collects and processes the recyclable material.

° An entity must be reauthorized by the City Council to provide its service at least

L every 5 years.

° Collection bins and collection vehicles must be constructed to prevent leaking of
the collected material.

° An entity must provide its service to any single-family home, twin home, or duplex
adjacent to a public street within the City if the requesting individual is willing to
agree to the entity's standard service contract.

° The City may review, suspend, or revoke an entity’s authorization to provide
services if it fails to meet the application requirements, maintain the minimum
service standards, or violates the law.

° The City’s authorization is not exclusive and does not prohibit the City or its agent
from collecting the same recyclable material.

Public Works staff and Momentum Recycling have provided feedback during the drafting

process. While not every suggestion has been incorporated, the Legal Department

believes that the proposed amendment provides a balanced approach to ensure that
entities providing recycling services are qualified to provide the service and that the City
has sufficient safeguards in place to protect its streets, stormwater system, and safety of
1 its residents and businesses.

Order-2021-5 (Order) that updated the statewide COVID-19 restrictions. According to the

Unit capacity and resources are strained. To prevent and control the causes of COVID-

unique circumstances in different locations in Utah.

approving Mass Gathering Permits again. Therefore, staff recommends revisiting the
discussion to allow community events on City-owned property, and to host 2021 Harvest
Days events with special considerations to ensure the safety of our staff, vendors, guests
and other agencies that participate. While these events are months down the road, event
hosts need to start finalizing their plans over the next month or two.
