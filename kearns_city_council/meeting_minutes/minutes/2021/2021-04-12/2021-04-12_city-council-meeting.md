Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District Area Cleanup

              Pam Roberts Executive Director, Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District,
(WFWRD) stated in 2020 the area cleanup program was modified to using a reservation system
to have dumpsters delivered to individual residents and placed on their driveways. This change
was made because WFWRD was short staffed and not able to do the area cleanup the way it has
previously been done. This new system is the only way WFWRD can continue to provide this
service.

              Mayor Kelly Bush asked what the difference was as far as staffing with dropping
the dumpsters off curve side were everyone could use it versus putting the dumpsters in a
person’s driveway.

              Ms. Roberts stated with the traditional way over 125 dumpsters were dropped off
per day and then it had to hire crews to pick up piles that were alongside the dumpsters. This new
system has been approved by the WFWRD board and is included in the 2021 budget.

                   Council Member Snow stated she thought the program would go back to normal
after the pandemic. The new system makes the person who reserved the dumpster responsible
to dispose of any items that neighborhoods might put in those dumpsters that WFWRD will not
take such as mattresses or tires. There is a charge associated with getting rid of these items and
it is not fair for the homeowner to have to cover the cost of these items which are not theirs.

             Sione Tuione, Manager, Residential Collection, stated WFWRD district did not
charge anyone extra for mattresses that were picked up last year.

                Mayor Bush stated there is a problem with not having dumpsters available to
everyone in Kearns due to code enforcement issues. A lot of residents get ready for the
dumpsters and then they are not able to reserve one and the piles just set. The vouchers, which
are available, do not work because a lot of people do not have a truck or trailer to haul the pile to
the dump. Code Enforcement Officers will cite the resident and it becomes a problem. She
believes the Council would agree with her that this new system does not work for Kearns.

               Ms. Roberts stated if there are code enforcement issues WFWRD can help with
that aside from this service. Sometimes municipalities will sponsor trailers for homes that are in
disrepair and need help.

               Mayor Bush stated Kearns has sponsored dumpsters as well, but it becomes
frustrating when residents are already paying for this service and are not able to use it. This new
system is viewed by Kearns residents as a service they are paying for, but it is being taken away
without a reduction in fees.

               Ms. Roberts stated there is no extra charge associated with this program. Another
option would be to get rid of the service altogether, which she proposed in 2020 and the board
rejected. Because of the new system, WFWRD was able to save $300,000 last year, which will
prolong the time before a fee increase will be needed.



                                                 5
DATE          MONDAY                         APRIL                                    12, 2021

                Mayor Bush stated on the back of the postcard announcing when the dumpsters
will be available showing what is not allowed in the dumpster such as mattresses and electronics.

              Mr. Tuione stated that information is not included on the postcard, it is on a
dumpster rental. Maybe residents are mixing the two services up.

               Council Member Snow stated she remembered seeing the small print on the back
of the postcard indicating WFWRD would not pick up certain items. So if a homeowner reserve a
dumpster and someone else puts a mattress in, then the homeowner would be responsible to
dispose of it and pay the cost associated with that disposal. If WFWRD picks up the mattress,
then the homeowner is charged a fee for disposal. The fee is charged to the homeowner’s bill.

              Ms. Roberts stated with the area cleanup no extra fees are charged.

                Council Member Snow asked what the difference was in staffing between the
traditional way as done in 2019 and the new system done in 2020.

               Ms. Roberts stated in 2019 WFWRD hired 21 drivers and 6 ground crew. In 2020
WFWRD hired 16 drivers and 3 ground crew. WFWRD is not able to find the needed drivers to
continue to run the program as it previously did.

              Council Member Snow asked if the WFWRD bill would be lower because this
service has changed.

              Ms. Roberts stated no, the fee charged is for the weekly services or picking up
trash and recycling items. It does not include this service. Funds are drawn down every year to
cover these extra items.

              Council Member Schaeffer stated he has been against this new system from the
very beginning. The Council needs to get the word out to Kearns residents about this new system
and how it works.

               Ms. Roberts stated the Council could refer any customer to her that had concerns
about this program. She would be glad to handle the concerns and answer any questions.

Other Business

Uses of Available Funding

               Mayor Kelly Bush asked the Council how it would like to use the $1.4 million the
Kearns Metro Township will be receiving from Salt Lake County. This money is from the CARES
Act Fund that the township was unable to use because of the strict requirements attached to it.
The money was returned to Salt Lake County. Kearns will be entering into an agreement with
Salt Lake County wherein the County will be returning the funds using General Fund monies
instead of CARES Act Funds. This will allow Kearns to use the funds anyway it deems necessary.

               Council Member Snow stated she would like to see traffic calming on Impressions
Lane. Some of the money could be used for signage at the Unified Police Department building
that discussed earlier this evening.

               Council Member Schaeffer stated he would like to see some of the money go
towards finishing the lighting project. There are still gaps that need to be filled in.

               Council Member Butterfield stated it could be used to finish the repaving of the
roads.

              Council Member Snow stated it would be nice to put up signs indicating you are
in the Kearns Metro Township.

               Council Member Peterson stated the funds could be used to purchase the
building the UPD is in. The owner has indicated he would be willing to sale it for $1.355 million.
The Township could purchase the building and then charge UPD rent to use part of it. The
remaining portion could be used as a temporary city building. The UPD is currently paying $10,600
a month for rent.

             Council Member Snow stated she thought UPD or the Salt Lake Valley Law
Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA) was going to purchase a building for the Kearns Precinct.

              Council Member Peterson stated SLVLESA does not have funds available to
purchase a building.

               Council Member Bush stated she was concerned with doing this because the
residents of Kearns pay taxes into SLVLESA and UPD and it seems like the Kearns Township is
