3) Dark Skies - An immense diversity and number of birds migrate through or stay to breed at Great 
Salt Lake and its wetlands each year, guided by an internal compass and visual cues that are sensitive 
to high intensity artificial light. A great majority of t hese birds migrate at night. This presentation 
explores a sensitive intersection of human population growth and a globally important stop -over 
along a migratory flyway. Some of the known effects of artificial light at night on humans, birds 
and other wildlife are presented, and city and county level solutions to reducing light pollution along 
this important migratory pathway are discussed. Presenter: Heidi Hoven and Ella Sorensen, 
Audubon Society 
 
Ms. Hoven provided an oversight and presentation on dark ski es, cluster lighting and pollution 
wetlands of the Great Salt Lake on birds. Commissioner Cohen asked how high birds fly when they 
migrate. Heidi said ranges when coming into destination or their stop, usually 400-500 feet up and 
can see brightly lit area from a distance, up to 5000 km away.  Mr. Young said working with the 
Audubon society appropriate to work on the general plan and recommendations for lighting and 
dark skies. Commissioner Cohen asked if the legislature just passed a law to put design standa rds 
into dwellings, but MPD was rejected. Mr. Perry said HB98 and PC zones are applicable. Mr. Shaw 
said the bill included an exception for MPD and was removed and is subject to the bill with limits

applies to square feet and multi-family, but not commercial and not sure if scope of buildings and 
lighting not being called out and focused on materials, land scape standards, architecture, and roof. 
Commissioner Cohen said showed lights out campaign in spring and fall and how are they g etting
