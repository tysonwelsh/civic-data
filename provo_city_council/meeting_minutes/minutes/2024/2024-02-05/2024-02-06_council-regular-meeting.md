1.          A presentation of the Quarterly Report for the First Quarter 2024 (24-018) 0:23:40

John Borget, Director of Administrative Services, delivered a presentation, outlining the purpose of the
quarterly report. He emphasized that the report provides an unaudited summary of revenue and
expenses for the first quarter of fiscal year 2024. Mr. Borget noted that the city is currently trending
approximately two million dollars down and emphasized the need to plan accordingly. He went on to
discuss the influences of sales and rap taxes, as well as fees and revenues from various departments,
providing insights into expenditures.

3.         A resolution approving the appropriation of $119,000 in the General Fund for a Mid-Year
           Market Adjustment for Firefighters (24-006) 0:38:02

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Resolution 2024-6, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Daniel Softley, Human Resources Division Director, presented. He discussed the challenges that the city
is facing regarding recruitment pressure for trained paramedics. He said these funds will be used to
adjust the top of pay grade to be comparable with those across the state and increase the starting pay.

Councilor Bogdin shared her support of training and retaining paramedics. She requested more
information on where the funds will come from to pay for the appropriation.

Mr. Borget responded that this appropriation is a one-time cost and added moving forward, the
department would include the raised personnel costs in their budget. He explained that while going
through the budget process the city must decide which items are most important, try to project the
expected revenues, and pinpoint any variances. He suggested that smart reductions or eliminating
services that are not as critical as others could be required. He said a balanced budget is always the goal
where the city’s revenues match the expenditures.

Councilor Whipple asked how much the city invests in paramedics to see what the cost would be if the
appropriation failed.

Mr. Softley outlined the cost breakdown, encompassing $13,000 for tuition fees and additional expenses
for staffing coverage resulting in a total of approximately $30,000 to $40,000 per EMT. Mr. Softley
emphasized the value of having certified paramedics who can immediately contribute with full services,

accelerated pay roles in the competitor's structure. This competitor had a career progression series like

improving the middle section of the career series to provide newer paramedics with opportunities for
quicker acceleration to enhance competitiveness.

Councilor Bogdin shared where her husband works, if the company pays for tuition, her husband must
pay back a certain number of years in service. She asked if Provo had considered something similar.

Mr. Softley said what Councilor Bogdin explained sounded more like our tuition reimbursement policy.

require a payback. He also stated that he has learned from other agencies that it is not enforceable
unless you decide to spend funds on taking the employee to court.

4.         An ordinance adjusting the common boundary between Provo and Orem along 2000 South
           in Orem (24-019) 0:59:47

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2024-6, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Gordon Haight, Engineering Division Director, presented and reminded the council that this meeting
marks the closing of the public hearing period that was opened for the next 3 items. He said this
particular area includes a detention basin that Orem uses as part of their storm drain facility. He added
that since Provo does not have any homes that front the road, staff recommends this area be owned

                   Hoban, MacKay, and Whipple in favor.

5.         An ordinance adjusting the common boundary between Provo and Orem to accommodate
           a new residential development near the intersection of University Avenue in Provo and
           800 North in Orem (24-019) 1:01:50

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2024-7, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Mr. Haight presented. He discussed the River District Development, emphasizing the challenge of
determining boundaries for a particular parcel of land. He highlighted the logistical issues of having
homes across the street from each other falling under different school districts and voting jurisdictions.
To address this, both Provo and Orem are collaborating to ensure a seamless experience for residents,
eliminating the need for distinct physical boundaries between neighborhoods. He added that Orem has
already had their public hearing, and now they await Provo's ordinance to move forward.

With no public comments or council discussion, Chair MacKay called for a vote.

      Vote:        The motion was approved 7:0 with Councilors Bogdin, Christensen, Garrett, Handley,
                   Hoban, MacKay, and Whipple in favor.

           SDP1 Zone to SC3 Zone - Riverbottoms Neighborhood (PLRZ20230267) 1:08:51

                   made by council rule.

Mr. Ardmore delivered a presentation, clarifying that the developer's intention is to construct a single
office building spanning both parcels. However, the current zoning of the parcels differs. He highlighted
that the common ownership of both parcels facilitates the management of parking and access
easements. Additionally, Mr. Ardmore suggested the possibility of amending the ordinance to
incorporate a development agreement.

Councilor Whipple asked if the developer proffered a development agreement.

Mr. Ardmore responded and said that the submittals received are proffers themselves, but a
development agreement could be added if desired by the council.

Chair MacKay opened public comment. With none, she brought the discussion back to the council.

Councilor Christensen expressed appreciation for the project but conveyed a sense of unease. He
emphasized the importance of having the development agreement in place, citing concerns from
neighbors about a crucial part of the river.

Bill Peperone, Development Services Director, highlighted the significant concern surrounding trail
protection and access, both for the neighbors and the staff, ever since the inception of this project. He
acknowledged the developer's respectful approach to these concerns. He assured that the zoning
change would not progress until the development agreement was signed and attached, ensuring the
safety of the trail.

     Motion:      Chair MacKay made an amended motion approving the ordinance contingent on the
                  signing of a development agreement based on the proffered documents by the
                  applicant. Councilor Garrett seconded.

Councilor Handley asked if a development agreement could be requested by the council.

Mr. Peperone confirmed that when a developer submits a plan, the plan is a proffer that could be
memorialized in a development agreement.

Councilor Whipple concurred with Mr. Peperone's perspective, emphasizing that the council's focus is
not to introduce new conditions that were not originally part of the plan.

Councilor Bogdin asked about the allowed public space or public access.

Mr. Ardmore stated that the is a public access easement along the river for the trail itself but once you
cross over, you would be on private property and allowing public use would be up to the property
owner.

Chair MacKay called for a vote on the amended motion.

     Vote:        The motion was approved 7:0 with Councilors Bogdin, Christensen, Garrett, Handley,
                  Hoban, MacKay, and Whipple in favor.
