Citizen Public Input

             Angie Newson stated she was before the Council because Salt Lake County’s
Code Enforcement would not help her. It is requiring she get a permit for a wheelchair ramp that
her husband built on the property, even though the ramp is temporary and not attached to the
house. She could not afford a permit, as she needed the money to buy medication for her
husband.

              Greg Schulz stated the County has a program called Assist that helps low-income
people and people who have situations like this. It was his understanding, Assist was going to
pay the $175 permit fee for Ms. Newson, after she reached out to it.

              Ms. Newson stated she was told by Assist that her household income was too
much, since her father lived in the home too.

                Mr. Schulz stated he will talk to Code Enforcement and Building Inspection to try
to get this worked out.

Reports from Other Municipal Service Providers

Unified Police Department

                Chief Jason Ackerman, Magna Precinct, stated there were no major incidents
during the holidays, so officers were able to spend some time with their families. Before the
holidays, the UPD did some Sub for Santa work with the community councils here in Magna. It
was a great opportunity. Then, on New Year’s, there was a rave at Saltair, where about 4,000
people attended. Safety measures were taken last August that seem to be working. Lastly, the
UPD is getting ready to start up its Youth Academy. It is a big endeavor for the UPD, and a great
opportunity for youth. He asked the Council to direct any interested youth to the UPD so it can fill
up the academy.

Council Business

Unified Fire Authority (UFA)

                Council Member Ferguson stated he had nothing to report, but the UFA Board
of Directors will meet soon.

              Council Prokopis reported on the following issues:

   ∗   The UFA just promoted 4 captains and is hiring 28 new firefighters. The new firefighters
       begin training at the Magna training facility on February 4th and will have 16 weeks of
       intensive training.

   ∗   Two years ago, the State audited the UFA and noted 120 findings. That precipitated the
       change in UFA leadership and a methodical look into each of the findings. Since then, all
       120 findings have been remedied.

   ∗   The board has decided not to pursue civil action against the previous leadership, and it
       issued a statement on its website owning some responsibility for the issues then. Now,
       there are checks and balances in place to prevent them from happening again.

   ∗   The UFA Board is looking at weighted voting. Some of the larger organizations feel their
       voice is marginalized considering each entity gets the same vote regardless of population.

   ∗   The UFA is watching the Brighton incorporation. The County has been subsidizing police
       and fire services in Brighton, but Brighton may have to bear the burden itself after 2019.
       He hoped the County would continue to help fund services to prevent service levels from
       dropping down considering Brighton does not have a tax base.

   ∗   Magna had one major incident on Christmas Eve, where a bar burned down and had been
       burning for quite some time before being detected. There was little the UFA could do
       aside from putting out the fire.

   ∗   Then, a reminder that it is property owners’ responsibility to keep fire hydrants clean and
       free of snow.

              Greg Schulz asked if the UFA was armed with Narcan in the event of a person
overdosing.
