5. BUSINESS ITEMS:
    5.1       Public Hearing and consideration of amendments to the Vineyard Zoning
        Ordinance to add Medical Cannabis and Pharmacy provisions: Section 15.12.050
        District Use Table to add new zoning uses for Pharmacies, Cannabis Production
        Establishments, & Medical Cannabis Pharmacies; New Section 15.34.210 Cannabis
        Production Establishments and Medical Cannabis Pharmacies to add development
        standards for associated uses; and Section 15.60.020 Definitions for Pharmacies,
        Cannabis Production Establishments, & Medical Cannabis Pharmacies.

                                                                                September 16, 2020

         Community Development Director Mr. Brim introduced the topic by stating that in 2018
         the State provided for medical allowances, through the legislature, for medical cannabis
         production and pharmaceutical uses. He brought up that Life Elevated, a company in
         the industrial district, is in the process of applying for the state permit to allow for
         production of medical cannabis. Life Elevated is also looking into adding a storefront for
         pharmaceutical uses as well.

         Mr. Brim examined the District Use Table to show that medical cannabis production
         establishments were permitted in the FOI and manufacturing districts as long as the
         business meets all licensing requirements as required by Utah State Code. He clarified
         that there needs to be differential code between pharmacies for cannabis and
         traditional pharmacies due to state code. Traditional pharmacies are permitted in retail
         districts and medical cannabis pharmacies are only permitted in the FOI district.

         Chair Jenkins asked Mr. Brim why the City would treat the location of the two (2) types
         of pharmacies different. Mr. Brim explained that the original intent on restricting
         medical cannabis pharmacies to the FOI district was to at least allow them into the City.
         Mr. Brim stated that because of state code, medical cannabis pharmacies have to be a
         certain distance from churches, schools, and parks. The FOI district complies with that
         requirement as well as introduces these pharmacies slowly into the City. If a medical
         cannabis pharmacy wanted to open up elsewhere, the City would have to go through
         an amendment process to allow it.

         Mr. Brim mentioned that in a discussion with medical cannabis companies, they have
         stated that they do not like for their businesses to be located in a standard retail
         environment due to their patients wanting more privacy due to the negative stigma of
         medical cannabis.

         Commissioner Shan Sullivan asked if the Commission was perpetuating the negative
         stigma by hiding these pharmacies in the secret parts of town. Mr. Brim replied that
         because of state code, the current permitted location was the easiest for the City to
         adopt.

         Mr. Blackburn asked to know the requirements to be a medical cannabis pharmacist.
         Mr. Brim explained that there are state laws in place to provide certification. Mr. Brim
         explained that Bev Astin and Justin Arriola at Life Elevated would be great sources to
         explain these requirements.

         Mr. Brim read the development standards from the City code tied to this use.

    1.   Health and Safety:
           1. There shall be no emission of dust, fumes, vapors, odors, or waste into the
               environment from any facility where growing, processing, or testing of cannabis
               occurs, and

                                                                                  September 16, 2020

           2. Cannabis production establishments shall meet the land use requirements for the
               zone in which it is located.
    2.   The following standards apply to all medical cannabis pharmacies:
           1. No cannabis products shall be visible from outside a medical cannabis pharmacy,
           2. The building square footage dedicated to the retail use of the medical cannabis
               pharmacy shall not exceed 25% of the building or development in which it is
               located, and
           3. Medical cannabis pharmacies shall meet the land use requirements for the zone
               in which it is located.

         Mr. Brim stated that due to the limitation of the retail area allowed in the FOI district
         that a traditional pharmacy wouldn’t be permitted. Mr. Brim stated that the code only
         allows for the production of cannabis and a small retail area in the FOI district. Large
         retail is not permitted in the FOI district so as to maintain the health and safety of all
         residents.

6. WORK SESSION
    6.1         Vineyard Tree Manual Updates
          Planning Technician Briam Amaya Perez described the work he has been doing to
          update the tree manual. He listed the changes as followed:
          - The title has changed from Vineyard Tree Manual to Vineyard Tree and Landscape
              Manual. This change is more encompassing and includes groundcover.
          - All of the trees and plants have been approved and shown that they can thrive in
              Vineyards microclimate and soils.
          - The manual now includes a table of contents to be more user friendly.
          - All the plant species have been alphabetized.
          - All the images and sources have been cited.
          - Minor spelling issues have been corrected.
          - Some images of trees were incorrect and the needed changes have been made.
          - The tree classification system was changed to say small, medium, or large trees.
          - The list of prohibited trees has been updated.
          - The local retailer list has been updated.
          Mr. Perez continued to explain that this manual is only required to be followed on
          public lands but is encouraged to be followed on private property.

          Mr. Perez stated that the City has the right to remove dead and diseased trees from
          private property at cost of the property owner. Commissioner Blackburn asked what
          gave the City authority to remove dead trees from private property but prohibited
          them from enforcing which trees get planted on private property. Mr. Brim replied that
          he will have Mr. Perez work closely with Public Works to find better wording to clarify
          the City’s authority.

          Commissioner Rasmussen requested for Mr. Perez to include the scientific names of
          each species. She also warned about male trees dispersing excess pollen.

         Commissioner Blackburn asked if creating a local retailer list could warrant for
