ZONE TEXT CHANGE APPLICATION
ZT-6-2020 Petition by WEST VALLEY CITY requesting a zone text change to Sections 7-1-103
          and 7-6-301 to define and prohibit outdoor waste management facilities. (Staff – Steve
          Pastorik at 801-963-3545) Continued from the August 26,2020 public hearing

            Discussion: Steve Pastorik stated the main question from the August 26, 2020 meeting
            was how the City shop's handle waste collected by the street-sweeping trucks. Steve
            introduced Erik Brondum, Operations Engineer, to explain this process. Erik said that the
            City constructed a new washout facility and storage base at the Public Works shop. He
            stated the City trucks backs into the wash bay to dewater. From there, the water goes
            through a drain containing an oil/water separator directly into the sewer. Any solids are
            disposed of in the sweepings bin and hauled off to the dump.
            Commissioner Winters stated the process seems immediate as opposed to storage. Erik
            explained the process is quicker as they only deal with stormwater and not the sewer
            systems.
Commissioner Fuller asked if there are any oil or grease traps in the system. Erik said
within the drains; there is a pump that separates the oil and grease. Commissioner Fuller
asked where the oil and grease get hauled off to once it is trapped. Erik could not recall
and stated he would follow up. Commissioner Fuller asked Erik if he is familiar with the
proposal. Erik said he was. Commissioner Fuller than stated the biggest concern is this
business causing potential odors. Erik couldn't speak to that. Commissioner Woodruff
asked if the City has a place where the waste is spread out to evaporate. Erik said no, the
bins containing the waste are covered.
Commissioner Lovato asked if there is anything to prevent illegal dumping. Steve stated
he doesn't think so where the dump doesn't take liquid waste.
Commissioner Fuller asked Steve if this was an indoor business would this be an issue.
Steve explained an indoor facility would not be an issue where the companies are taking
their waste to Salt Lake City.
Chair Meaders asked Steve if it would be possible to have an alternative ordinance
change that would allow some outdoor waste management.
Steve said that would be possible and then mentioned two staff concerns. The first
concern is the staff report gets a bit nuanced, and we need to make sure we do not
inadvertently allow something we didn't intend to.
The second concern is business aesthetics. Steve stated there could be a requirement to
have the concrete slab screened off.
Commissioner McEwen asked where the business owner stated speaking with the City
about his conditional use and was given the okay to proceed, and the zone text is being
changed is there any legal issue that could arise? Brandon stated if the complete
application is not filed, there is no vesting, and legally there will not be an issue.
Chair Meaders asked if there is a way to detect how potent smell is.
Brandon said there is a device for that purpose. He stated the issue with using the device
is finding the range that would be acceptable.
Jody Knapp explained the applicant for the carwash waste business had attended a
development meeting. She stated a green light was not given for this proposal due to
many concerns brought up in the meeting.
Commissioner Winters asked Steve what he fore-see's the landscaping to be. Steve stated
in the M zone; there is a 5% landscaping requirement. If we offer alternatives, there can
also be separation requirements added to restrict the use further, but still, allow it.
Commissioner Woodruff asked if this would be a conditional use and the requirements
being part of the condition. Steve stated he would put the restrictions as part of the
standards that the applicant would need to meet. Commissioner Woodruff was
wondering if this came before the Commission would they be able to say no. Steve
             explained they couldn't say no; however, some standards could be put in to mitigate the
             impact to the City.
             Jody Knapp stated the need to add something in the conditional use to prevent illegal
             dumping. Commissioner Lovato was concerned that the City would be responsible for
             enforcing the unlawful dumping. Jody explained the responsibility would fall back on
             the applicant through the conditional use. She explained the applicant would need to
             obtain permits through the state that would require annual inspections, so there might
             need to be communication with the state.
CONDITIONAL USE APPLICATION
C-11-2020 Petition by PM DESIGN GROUP INC., requesting a conditional use for a fast food
          restaurant with drive-up. The property is located at 3346 South 5600 West on 1.02
          acres and is zoned C-2 (General Commercial). (Staff – Jody Knapp at 801-963-3497)

Discussion: Jody presented her application for a new Raising Cain’s restaurant. Jody explained that
            the applicant would be using a pad site that the current owner will lease to the applicant.
            She said there was a lot of discussion about the placement of the restaurant on the subject
            location. Most of the meeting was about access to the building and pedestrian traffic.
             Jody stated the developer would limit access on the northeast corner of the site to a right-
             in and right-out only and there would be no direct access to 5600 West. The primary
             pedestrian access will be on the south side of the site connecting to the adjacent shopping
             center.
            Jody explained the proposed building design has a lot of building articulation and
             different materials and complies with the commercial design standards. She said this
             parcel already has landscaping along the frontage; however, the applicant will have to
             update the landscaping along 5600 W.
            The Planning Commission and staff spoke about this being the first Raising Cane’s
            located in Utah and how excited they are to have this addition.


DISCUSSION ITEM
COVID-19 Land Use Effects and Underdeveloped Properties


Discussion: Steve Pastorik addressed the discussion item. He discussed the housing market trends
            from 2000 through 2020. From 2000 through the first half of 2020, there was a 150%
            increase in housing prices. Rentals also increased, however, not as much as homes. The
            prediction, towards the end of the year, is that home prices will increase by 5%, and
            rentals will be flat or potentially go down. Steve explained the percentage of online
            purchases with the introduction of COVID-19 has risen from 12% to 16%. He also
            pointed out the increase in unemployment is a factor. The upside is Utah has the lowest
            unemployment rate in the country.
              Next, Steve presented the potential impact on West Valley City. He explained that
              developers could redevelop vacant or underutilized parcels along major streets if the
              office, retail and restaurant business decline. With the unemployment rate increasing, we
              could see more illegal conversions of homes to duplexes. With businesses and
              homeowners struggling, we could see a decline in property maintenance, causing a need
              for increased enforcement. Steve said there are parcels within the City where the land is
              worth more than the structures on it, and those properties would be more likely to
              redevelop.
              Commissioner McEwen hopes that we could have the townhomes and apartments
              attached to commercial property. He feels this could help drive up business. Steve
              explained this discussion is more for understanding why there have been more requests
              for apartments and townhomes before the Commission. He explained the challenge is to
              keep balance in the City with the market changes.
              Commissioner Lovato asked if the City has a strategy for the “new normal.” Steve said
              he feels this has already been addressed in our General Plan. The focus for higher
              intensity units is along major streets, keeping the neighborhoods intact. Steve explained
              an update to the General Plan is done every five years. He stated an update is due, which
              would be a great issue to address at that time.
              Commissioner Winters feels it is hard to judge if our actions are positive or negative to
              the City when the outcome of today’s vote will not be seen for 15-20 years down the
              road.
