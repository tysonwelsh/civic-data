         3. 1   Unified Fire Authority Report— Captain Richard Rich


 IR       UFA Captain Richard Rich explained that although the agenda packet only
contained a report for Q1 of 2024, he had been able to prepare a report for Q2 and would
present that information as well.


He explained that the numbers of fire and medical calls during those quarters were very
similar to what they had been during previous quarters. There was also little change
regarding the types of calls they had responded to in Taylorsville. He displayed heat and
plot maps depicting the various locations of calls that had taken place in the City.

Regarding fire activity on Independence Day, UFA crews responded to five different grass
fires caused by fireworks. Although they had not caused any house fires in Taylorsville,
there was one such fire in Herriman caused by fireworks remnants that were disposed of
in a garbage can immediately adjacent to the home. He reminded those in attendance
that if they disposed of remnants in such a manner, it was important to at least take the
time to move the can away from structures and vehicles. He pointed out that close to half
of all the July 4th fires in UFA's service area were trash- can related.



Captain Rich pointed out that both Council Members Barbieri and Harker attended Fire
School recently. They learned a great deal and were able to glimpse a bit of what it was
like to be a firefighter.


Fortunately, there had been a recent uptick in the number of people interested in
becoming fire fighters. It was unclear how many would end up being fully tested and hired,
but further information           would   be   forthcoming.    Fourteen   lateral hires ( from other fire

departments) were on the streets and Captain Rich anticipated they would be on their
own by August 1.

A special hiring process had been created for those already working for UFA as part-
timers but who were interested in full- time employment with them. It was anticipated that
approximately thirteen such individuals would go through recruit school in October.

Wild fire       activities were   very    active   in the U. S.,
                                                       particularly in the southwest portion.
Although Utah was technically no longer in a drought, there had still been an increase in
calls for service pertaining to wildfires. Utah currently had teams deployed in Alaska, New
Mexico, and here at home.


The UFA had recently received three new engines, with one of them being housed in
Taylorsville. They also received five new ambulances as well as two refurbished rigs.
Replacement of aging apparatuses was an ongoing expense for UFA.

         6. 1   Resolution     No.   24- 17   A   Resolution     of   the    City of Taylorsville
                Approving an Interlocal Cooperation Agreement Between the City of
                Taylorsville and South Salt Lake Regarding Shared Building Inspector
                Services —Ben White


 e City Engineer Ben White briefly explained why this agreement would benefit both
cities. In short, because state legislators had recently passed a bill that required cities to
complete building inspections within a specific time frame, it became appropriate for

different municipalities to back one another up if inspectors became ill, took vacations,
etc. The goal was to ensure that such occurrences did not have an adverse effect on
builders. This agreement would allow Taylorsville and South Salt Lake to share reciprocal
services on an on- call basis. Other municipalities would soon be added.

         7. 2   Parking Discussion —Ben White

Mr. White presented a summary of the number and type of parking citations that had been
issued in Taylorsville during the previous twelve months. He also provided information
regarding the location of the tickets. They totaled 295 citations.

He then addressed the ways that several surrounding communities were addressing
parking issues. Some of those were:
         Restricting parking over 24- 72 hours
         Restricting parking during and immediately following a snow event
         RV and trailer parking restrictions
         Restricting parking on one side of streets according to street width
         Restricting parking within 30' of an intersection

Council Member Harker inquired if the pink tags ( warnings) program was effective. He
asked her if she noticed fewer cars on the roadway during snow events. She responded
that there had been a resident who complained on Facebook after receiving a parking
ticket and most who commented seemed to appreciate that the parking restriction was
enforced.



Council Member Barbieri mentioned the previous citizen comment regarding too many
cars parked at a single residence. She had also seen the same problem in District No. 3.
She was concerned that a fire truck would not be able to get where it needed to in the
event of an emergency. She was also aware that Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling
had reported that at times they were unable to pick up cans that were surrounded by
parked vehicles. But on the other hand, she understood that due to the current housing
crisis, many individuals had to live with family members, which added to the number of
cars at many homes. She asked for input from her fellow Council Members.

Council Member Burgess said there were many issues in his district with people parking
too close to stop signs. He agreed that the large number of people living in single-family
homes contributed to widespread parking problems. Like Council Member Barbieri, he
expressed concern of fire trucks being able to get where they needed to go when so many
vehicles were parked on both sides of the road.


Regarding parking near stop signs, Council Member Harker pointed out that there was
already a law in place about not parking too close to one. Mr. White pointed out that many
residential streets did not have stop signs. He explained the ordinance that Draper passed
regarding a prohibition on parking within 30 feet of an intersection.

          7. 1      Proposed Updated Taylorsville Policy Manual— Jay Ziolkowski


 e       Jay Ziolkowski explained that the Personnel Policy Manual had recently been
updated and he wished to provide a brief overview for the City Council.

The policy manual was last updated in March 2012. It was recommended to be reviewed
and updated at least every five years. This update had been in progress for the past two
years.



He asked that the Council Members review the document between now and the next City
Council meeting but reminded them that it was administrative in nature and under the
Mayor' s purview. However, one update that required legislative approval was the change
to the employees' appeals process which now involved a hearing before the
Administrative Law Judge as opposed to an Employee Appeals Board.


The plan was to update the document in the future on an as- needed basis so that it
remained fresh and applicable.
