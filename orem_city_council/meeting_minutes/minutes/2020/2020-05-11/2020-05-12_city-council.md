      City Council Reports (Boards and Commissions, New Business, Etc.)

Chief Giles indicated there would be a press conference tomorrow at noon that will include a video
of the incident regarding the recent officer involved shooting. All the officers are doing well and
they were fortunate to only have one officer injuried.

Mr. Davidson stated they have been concerned about the economic climate in the city specifically
regarding sales tax. The city received their first report and as they look specifically at the general
sale tax they had a 5.5% decline which they felt was encouraging and not as bad as first thought.
He indicated the 5.5% decline represents only the general sale tax there are other several taxes that
are not included. They are preparing themselves for the report from April that will show the full
shut down results.
He continued to state that they are proceeding with the interviews for Fire Chief position and they
are now at a point to bring in and provide face to face interviews with 3 specific candidates.
He updated the council regarding the CARES Act and the Covid-19 pandemic. The State of Utah
received approximately $1.2 billion dollars in funds to go to specifically Utah Covid Response.
Jurisdictions with more than 500 thousand residents received direct allocation but in Utah, the
State, Salt Lake County and Utah County received allocations for Covid-19 response. The intent
of those funds is for it to be distributed so that all jurisdictions within the state can use those funds
to cover expenses due to Covid-19. The challenge in recent weeks is the State of Utah has been
willing to distribute all of the money on a population basis throughout the state. However, Salt
Lake County and Utah County have both expressed reservation about doing that and at this point
has not committed to that process. This is continuing to be discussed and they hope that they will
come to a consensus soon. He continued to discuss the proposed process as to how the funds are
to be distributed and how Utah County is wanting to keep the funds and approach the
reimbursement in a different direction. He feels it is just a matter of leaning on the County and
help them understand the way to allocate the funds should be consistent with the States
recommendations.

Mayor Brunst stated the County Commission has indicated they are willing to commit to
reimbursement to expenses used during the Covid time period. They just have to wait to see what
the federal government will do based on new legislation introduced just last week.

Discussion ensued regarding the current situation of the federal funding.

Mrs. Lauret briefly updated the Council on her committee assignments that include Utopia,
Summerfest and Arts Council. She stated that Utopia has 4,259 Orem subscribers which is about
a 36.4% take rate with an additional 1,133 commercial accounts giving them an overall take rate

buildout in the third quarter of 2022. Utopia has the Purple Air Project and the Edwin Projects
currently underway. Arts Council has been working on a major project with the utility boxes in
wrapping them with artwork. They have done a prototype at the public works and will continue
to work on that project in conjunction with Utopia boxes. Summerfest will be called “Summerfest
Light” this year and they are looking at August 13-15, with holding the fireworks on the 15th. They
are looking a pool party and a car show in conjunction with the Geezer Fest.

RESOLUTION - Smrtchar Ventures, LC requests the City approve a fence modification at
Sunrise Square located at 240 North State Street in the C2 zone.

Mr. Bench stated that as part of the Sunrise Square site plan that was approved by the Planning
Commission in February 2018, the applicant was required to install a seven foot high masonry
wall to replace the existing wood fence adjacent to the residential property on the north property
line. City ordinances require a seven foot masonry fence for any commercial development that is
located next to a residential zone.

The applicant has now completed the project, but the masonry wall was not built. The adjoining
residential property owner states in a letter (attached) that they are opposed to replacing their
current wood fence with a masonry wall. They like the aesthetics of the existing wood fence that
encloses their back yard and feel it would detract from the beauty of their yard to have a masonry
wall replace the wood fence. They are happy with the buffer between their residential property
and the commercial property of Sunrise Square.

The applicant requests that the masonry fence requirement be waived entirely as the residential
property to the north prefers to leave the wood fence in place.

Pursuant to Section 22-14-19(F) of the City Code, the City Council may modify the fence
requirement if it finds that:

1. The proposed fence provides an adequate buffer for the adjoining residential zone.
2. The appearance of the fence will not detract from uses in the residential zone.
3. The proposed fence will shield the residential use from noise, storage, traffic or any other
   characteristic of commercial or professional office uses that are incompatible with residential
   uses.

Staff has reviewed the proposed fence modification and believes the request meets all of the
requirements listed above.

Mr. Lambson inquired as to what would happen in the future when the wood fence would need to
be replaced or repair. Mr. Bench responded that in the future it would have to be a negotiation
between the two parties. By granting this exception it is not a requirement the applicant be fully
responsible for any upkeep in the future and to his knowledge the resident is aware of that
stipulation.

Mayor Brunst moved by Resolution approving Smrtchar Ventures, LC requests for a fence
modification at Sunrise Square located at 240 North State Street in the C2 zone. Mr. Sumner
seconded the motion. Those voting aye: Richard F. Brunst, Jeff Lambson, Debby Lauret, Tom
Macdonald, David Spencer, and Brent Sumner. The motion passed.


          PUBLIC HEARING - RESOLUTION - FY 2020-2021 Tentative Budget Adoption

Mr. Nelson stated prior to being presented the FY 2020-2021 Tentative Budget, the City Council
and staff have met in a series of public meetings to discuss elements of this budget, reviewed the
General Fund and Enterprise Funds, and some of the recommended adjustments to the City's fees
and charges. This budget does not contain any request to increase the property tax rate. Mr.
Nelson reviewed the budget calendar and the budget information that was discussed at the
beginning of the year and some of the highlights of the current tentative budget which include:
    Modified expenditures and projected revenues due to Covid-19 pandemic;
    Health insurance increase for all employees;
    New internals service fund for Facilities Maintenance, which is a newly created
        fund;
    Fleet Replacement and
    Capital Projects.

Mr. Nelson continued to review the various Revenue Funds, Sale Tax graph, Property Tax graph,
and a Franchise Tax graph.

General discussion ensued regarding the possible reason for franchise fees being more level and
what causes the changes.

Mr. Nelson continued to review Building and Business Services Charges, various Expenditure
Funds and General Fund additions for 2021. He continued to review various funds which
included the following:
