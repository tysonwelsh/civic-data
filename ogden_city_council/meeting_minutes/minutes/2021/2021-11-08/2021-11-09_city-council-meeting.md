Update- Marshall White Center Upgrades
         Council Executive Director Eller-Smith repmted a structural evaluation of the Marshall White Center (MWC) was
completed in early 2021 the Marshall White Advisory Cmmnittee (MWA C) has provided initial recommendations to update and
expand the MWC as the City's recreation center. The recmrunendations focused on use of the MWC as a recreation center and
provided a list of amenities that would enhance the current recreational offerings. The recommendation also included adding
additional space for community events and charity functions, and provided conceptual drawings created by VCBO. In September
2021, the City Council sent a letter to the Administration and the Cmmnittee requesting that additional options for reuse of the
MWC be explored and forwarded to the Council for consideration. There was a brief discussion among the Council, Council staff,
and Administration regarding the results of the community survey regarding the MWC project; the intention of the language and
questions in the survey, and the role the consultant and MWAC in preparing the survey.

some of the direction from the City Council that was a result of their visits to the facility. This is an opportunity to create a
wonderful community center, not just a recreation center, that could provide services and programs for the entire community.
Council member Choberka agreed and stated that there may be a problem with the process that was observed on conducting a
survey regarding this project.
         Public Services Director Lowder indicated that there are at least three tiers of data resulting from the survey and staff
needs to drill down through that data to develop a comprehensive plan or proposal that addresses that input. This led to discussion

BRT Station Art Project
          Ogden City's Arts, Culture and Events (ACE) division is working on a project to provide art enhancements to the future
Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) platforms in Ogden. Typically, a project like this would have been proposed during the annual budget
process, but this project was not available to consider at that time. The Administration is now requesting the approval of$190,459
of public art funds to be disbursed to fund the program. Per City Code 4-1 C-5, the Planning Commission has reviewed the
proposed art project and unanimously recommends approval.

imagery. Finally, she presented the OGC BRT public mt project budget. This is a collaborative project, and many community

Zone Text Amendment- Allowing Painted Brick in East Central R-1-5,
R-2EC, and R-3EC Zones
        Planning Manager Montgomery reported the City's zoning code restricts brick homes in the East Central Community
from being painted or covered with another material. This regulation is based off the community plan's emphasis on maintaining

              Minutes of Work Session of Council of Ogden City, Utah, November 9, 2021 Page 361


     historic elements in the area. An applicant is requesting to remove this restriction so that brick homes can be painted in the East
     Central Community. The East Central Community is part of the Central Bench National Historic District, which was established
     in 2005. Certain zoning regulations, such as this, have been placed in this area to help preserve the historic nature of the
     community. The Planning Commission reviewed the request in July 2021, followed by a work session in August, and a fmal vote
     took place on October 5, 2021. The Planning Commission ultimately decided that the painting restriction should remain, but with
     some minor amendments that would allow homes to be painted in exceptional circumstances where paint or a clear seal coat
     would be necessary to preserve deteriorating brick.
                The Council asked Mr. Montgomery several questions trying to determine the root of the regulation and the background
     of a Code Enforcement case with the applicant and other residents in the East Central Community relating to painted brick. Mr.
     Montgomery reported the applicant, Brett Rodgers, bought a brick home in the East Central Community earlier this year. He was
     unaware that the zoning code did not allow brick to be painted, so he proceeded to paint his home white. A citizen called the city
     to file a complaint, and Mr. Rodgers received a violation notice. Mr. Rodgers has explained that the cost of removing paint and
     potential damage to the brick make it difficult for him to bring his home into compliance. He further reported the restriction on
     painting or covering brick is intended to help fulfill historic preservation ofhomes in the area. Enforcement of the ordinance is a
     challenge. Once somebody has painted their home the only way to fix the issue is to remove the paint. One quote provided by
     Abstract Masonry Restoration states that paint removal could cost up to $25 per square foot, which could mean a total cost of over
     $10,000. Some residents may fmd it more feasible to pay the city's violation fees instead of paying to fix the issue. There may
     also be an issue with removing paint from eroded brick, which could be further damaged by certain removal techniques.
                The Council indicated they would like to continue this discussion during an additional work session meeting before
     placing an action item on a council meeting to respond to the petition.
