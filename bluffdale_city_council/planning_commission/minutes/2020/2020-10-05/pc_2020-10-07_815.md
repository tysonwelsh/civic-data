       Commercial (HC) Zone. City of Bluffdale, Applicant. (Application 2020-49). Staff
       Presenter, Grant Crowell.

C ity Planner/Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell, presented the staff report and
identified the changes to the numbering system in the City Code. He displayed the Zoning Map and
pointed out the locations of the Heavy Commercial (HC) zone. He recounted the back story
associated with the proposed text amendment. He explained that there are parcels in the HC zone
that are difficult to develop because of the incompatibility of the current setback requirements with
the size and configuration of the lots, pai1icularly when public streets are constructed on the lots.
The challenge was shared with the City Council to seek guidance on how to remedy the situation.
The City Council was amenable to adjusting the setbacks to facilitate prospective development. It
was noted that the current setback is 30 feet with a proposed setback of 15 feet. The City Engineer
and Fire Chief did not see any problems with the proposed change. Mr. Crowell reviewed the
proposed text amendments provided in the staff repo11. The proposed setbacks would still allow for
landscaping to be provided on the lots.

In response to Commissioner Walston ' s question regarding whether the other setbacks would also
need to be adjusted to make everything more equitable, Mr. Crowell said it cou ld be done at a later
meeting if deemed necessary. He explained that the goal is to impl ement more design flexibility ,
without deviating from safety concerns. His intent in the staff report was to provide comparisons of
the various setback requirements. Commissioner Walston was willing to forward a positive
recommendation to the City Council but wanted consideration to be given to the other setback
requirements to ensure equitability .

In response to Chair Cragun's request for clarification on what the proposed change would achieve,
Mr. Crowell stated that the map would not be amended and only the text would be impacted. The
intent was to show where the proposed text amendment would be applicable in Bluffdale. When
Chair Cragun sought to confirm that the proposed text amendment would not impact any existing
businesses, Mr. Crowell replied in the affirmative. The goal was to encourage redevelopment of
property that: has not been touched in some time. Current trends gave reason to believe that the
change could be benefic ia l to the City's economic deve lopment and infrastructure i.mprovements.
Mr. Crowell stated that the City Council is very open to adjusting the setback requirements, as
proposed.

5.     PUBLIC HEARING, CONSIDERATION, AND RECOMMENDATION on
       Engineering Standards Regarding Street Sign Requirements. Staff Presenter, Dan
       Tracer.

Assistant City Engineer, Dan Tracer, presented the staff repo11 and expl ained that staff has been
working with the City Council and Planning Commission to update the City ' s branding throughout
the committee, including updates to the signage requirements. One element includes updating the
City 's street signs, specifically the coloring of the street signs to match the City's adopted style
guide. Public streets will have a blue background using Pantone color 299C, and private street
signs will have a white background. Both types of signs will have the Bluffdale "B" logo on the left
side. The change will apply to all new street signs and the signs will be installed by the developer.
Staff is developing a plan to replace all of the existing signs to the new standard.

In response to Chair Cragun's question regarding other signs that exist on major roads in Bluffdale,
Mr. Tracer stated that some of the signs come under the purview of the Utah Department of
Transp011ation ("UDOT") so the City will have to work with them with regard to those signs. He
did not anticipate a problem from UDOT if Bluffdale is willing to pay for the sign replacements to
meet the new City standards. The major street signs will be at the top of the priority list since they
are the most prominent and visible.

Commissioner Walston expressed support for the proposed change. Chair Cragun agreed and stated
that she likes the co lor. Mr. T racer indicated that there is a prototype in front of the Planning
Department offices.

Mr. Tracer indicated that the old signs will likely be recycled. Commissioner Luker expressed
interest in having the street sign from his street. Mr. Crowell stated that it was a good idea to see
how the City wi ll surplus the signs because they should not be thrown away. Mr. Tracer stated that
they could potential ly be raffl e prizes at Old West Days. Chair Cragun suggested there be a contest.

6.     City Council Reports.

Mr. Crowell reported that at a Work Session on September 23. there was discussion regarding the
revitalization project on 855 West. There was also discussion regarding a Special Di strict (SD)
zone, which is mixed-use on Porter Rockwell Boulevard and Camp Williams Road. It was reported
that Porter Rockwell Bridge has been funded and it is expected to be done in May 2022. As a
result, there will be more attention and traffic in that part of the City.

Mr. Crowell noted that the City Council has been hearing a lot about the impact fees. The
assessment of impact fees is a delicate balancing act between fostering economic development and
protecting the entities and citizens being assessed. For the home builders, the Water Fee is of
greatest concern and for commercial developers, it is primarily the Transportation Fee.

Mr. Crowell stated that City Attorney, Todd Sheeran, has done excellent work with updates on
home occupations and business licensing. At some point, there will be changes to the approach to
home occupations. Some of it will involve how they are addressed in the Zoning Ordinance. It
may be removed from the Zoning Ordinance and placed in the Business Licensing Code.

Mr. Sheeran expounded on the home occupation matter by stating it is a difficult topic because it
seeks to balance what people can do in their homes without negatively impacting the neighbors .
Staff has met to discuss the different types of home occupations people are running. An accountant
likely does not generate much traffic or disruption to the neighborhood. Problems occur when the
business grows and generates more people and traffic. The challenge is to know where and how to
draw the line. Daycares are a specific business that requires more analysis. Variables include the
number of trips versus the number of people.

In response to Chair Cragun's question regarding what would trigger the necessity for a home
occupation to move to a different location, Mr. Sheeran stated that it would typically be handled
tlu·ough the business licensing process. The City would be ale1ted to problems through neighbor
complaints lodged with the City. He cited a specific example of a business that began small and
had grown so substantially had generated numerous neighbor complaints. Mr. Crowell added that
the City needs to somehow develop a more objective and measurable standard. He acknowledged
that it is difficult to know w here the line is on the business owner.
