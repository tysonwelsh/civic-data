15            12600 South CRA Discussion
16
17   Mayor Staggs reminded the Council that during the January strategic planning meeting, they
18   discussed the skate park’s deteriorating condition. A contractor had inspected the skate park
19   and advised that it could not be repaired and would require a full tear-out and replacement, as it
20   was at or near the end of its useful life. He noted that a community survey had been conducted,
21   and asked Josh Lee to report.
22
23   Josh Lee, Communications Director, presented results from two surveys conducted by the
24   Communications Department—one online and one in person at the skate park. The first
25   question addressed skate park usage, showing that a significant majority of residents or their
26   family members either never or rarely used it in the past year. The second question addressed
27   the $1–1.5 million estimated cost to replace and relocate the skate park, with most respondents
28   not supporting the replacement or relocation. About 17% of respondents selected “other,”
29   largely expressing opposition to relocation and suggesting alternative improvements. Among
30   the 28% of respondents who supported replacing and relocating the skate park, 54% preferred
31   Centennial Park or the new regional park east of Mountain View Village, while 8% preferred
32   keeping it at the current location or suggested other alternatives.. When asked how they would
33   prioritize spending the $1 million estimated cost, a majority favored other amenities, like bike
34   paths or pickleball courts, over a new skate park.
35
36   Mayor Staggs explained that in 2022, the Council had designated the half-acre skate park
37   parcel, along with surrounding property along 12600 South, as part of a community
38   reinvestment area. At that time, the decision allowed for potential redevelopment if the skate
39   park needed replacement. Additionally, a May boundary realignment addressed a state law
40   preventing restaurants serving alcohol from being within 300 feet of a community space,
41   ensuring future restaurant development could proceed legally. He emphasized that this
42   boundary adjustment did not automatically open the skate park for development and clarified
43   that misinformation had circulated about that.
44
45   Councilmember McDougal, drawing on personal experience speaking with youth, he noted that
46   the skate park’s appeal is short-lived, which raises questions about cost-effectiveness. He
47   suggested that the funds proposed for the skate park might reach more youth if allocated to
48   other programs with high current demand, such as softball, where modest investments like
49   additional lighting could accommodate more participants and provide longer-term benefits.
50
51   Councilmember McCay expressed concern about prioritizing the skate park over other
     Page | 3 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1   community needs, noting that residents generally support maintaining the current city park and
 2   softball facilities rather than replacing part of the park with a new skate park. She also
 3   highlighted safety concerns, particularly after a recent tragic accident, and noted that alternative
 4   recreational options like pump tracks or motocross/e-bike facilities are growing in popularity.
 5   She recommended considering a dedicated six-acre area for motocross or similar activities

36            Ordinance No. 25-21 – RM-6 Amendment, Proposed Updates to the RM-6 Zone to
37            include provisions for using private roads in specific circumstances and clarify
38            Development Standards
39
40   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, reviewed an ordinance regarding the RM-6
41   zoning district, which is primarily multi-family but has been adjusted over time to function more
42   like single-family detached housing. The ordinance mainly addresses private lane use, but a
43   key issue was the rear yard setback. Currently, the standard is 15 feet, or 25 feet if the project
44   is adjacent to single-family zoning. The Planning Commission recommended standardizing it
45   at 15 feet for all projects, but the Council expressed concern.
46
47   The revised ordinance (Exhibit A) proposes keeping the 25-foot setback when adjacent to
48   single-family zoning, with the option to modify it through an SD designation during rezone
49   approval. Option B would standardize it at 15 feet but still allow changes via SD designation.
50   The staff recommendation is to adopt Option A (25 feet), reasoning that it is generally easier to
51   reduce a setback later than to increase it.
