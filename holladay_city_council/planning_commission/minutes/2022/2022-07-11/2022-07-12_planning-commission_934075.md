WORK SESSION
Chair Howard Layton called the Work Session to order at approximately 5:38 p.m.

The agenda items were reviewed and discussed. Community Development Director, Jonathan
Teerlink, reported that the first item on the agenda was an administrative action item. It related to
the Bella Casa Subdivision located at 4555 South Highland Drive. Mr. Teerlink explained that it
is a two-lot single-family detached subdivision in the R-2-10 Zone. The R-2-10 Zone allows
duplexes on 10,000 square-foot lots. However, there was a provision in the Ordinance to allow
for single-family homes on smaller lots. For the Bella Casa Subdivision, there was an 8,000
square-foot lot in the front and a 10,000 square-foot lot in the back. The lot in the front was not
large enough to have a twin home but was large enough for a single-family home. The applicant
put forth a concept and preliminary level application for a lot split.

Mr. Teerlink noted that a Water Utility Letter had not yet been received from Salt Lake City Public
Utilities. That was still forthcoming. He explained that this is not unusual as Salt Lake City Public
Utilities was often behind in sending out those letters. The City needs verification that the lot can
be serviced by gas, power, water, and sewer. The Technical Review Committee (“TRC”) reviewed
the application for emergency access, drainage, utility connections, and irrigation protections. The
Big Cottonwood Canal Little Irrigation Water Facility runs in front of the property. The company
draws water from Big Cottonwood Creek at Arbor Lane and services quite a few properties in
Millcreek. There was no proposal to alter the irrigation lateral in any way, however, an Irrigation
Protection Agreement would be required before the plat is recorded.

Commissioner Prince wondered what an Irrigation Protection Agreement involved. Mr. Teerlink
explained that whenever there is a waterway, irrigation, or canal that bisects or is within a property,
an Irrigation Protection Agreement is required. It states that either the existing facility will be
protected or, if changes are proposed, that the irrigation company is aware of the proposed changes

noted that the existing conditions are too narrow. The bond will ensure that the 20-foot width is
paved to the second lot. There was further discussion regarding fire access.

Commissioner Banks noted that the minutes from the Neighborhood Meeting, which were
included in the packet, state that there was discussion related to rezoning and subdivision.
Mr. Teerlink clarified that when the applicant approached City Staff, he was originally looking for
rezone to Neighborhood Commercial (“NC”). It was uncertain whether that would be acceptable
from a Planning Commission and City Council point of view, so the applicant was directed to
conduct a neighborhood meeting. The proposed use was professional massage therapy and the
neighbors were not supportive of a commercial use there. Ultimately, the subdivision was
preferred.

PUBLIC HEARING – ACTION
1.      “Bella Casa” Subdivision – Concept / Preliminary – 4555 South Highland (R-2-10)
        Review and Consideration of an Application by Property Owner, David George to
        Subdivide a Total of 0.418 Acres of Land (18,198 square feet) to Accommodate a Two-
        Lot, Residential Subdivision, as Per Holladay Ord. 13.10. File #22-1-05.
Community Development Director, Jonathan Teerlink reported that the above item is a subdivision
application in the R-2-10 Zone. The property is located at 4555 South Highland Drive and includes
0.418 acres of land or 18,198 square feet. The applicant requested that the property be split into
two. No rezone was needed or required for the application. Mr. Teerlink explained that the
application was reviewed in a three-step process consisting of concept, preliminary, and final level
review. Each level includes more significant and detailed items and requirements. The first level,
which is the concept, is part of the public hearing process. This review was focused on compliance
within the R-2-10 Zone and looks at whether the lots meet the minimum lot size requirements.

Mr. Teerlink noted that the R-2-10 Zone is a duplex zone where a twin home could be built on a
10,000-square-foot lot. However, there was also a provision that a single-family detached home
could be built on a lot that was 6,250 square feet. The current proposal was to divide the lot into
two with one lot being approximately 8,000 square feet and the other lot 10,000 square feet. Both
lots would exceed the minimum requirements for single-family development. Certain service
letters were required as well. He explained that service letters were from utility providers and
indicated a willingness to serve. It would not be possible to develop a residential lot without the
necessary services. The applicant had pursued Service Agreement Letters.

The second level of review was preliminary. This was where most of the development details are
provided to the Planning Commission. For instance, the public utilities are located where the
utility connections are and in this case, where the existing irrigation lateral is. Information would
also be provided describing where the driveway access will be and how the access will be
compliant with the Fire Code. Mr. Teerlink explained that the actual building plans and review
will be done through the Building Department. The current review was related to the two-lot split
that will allow the homes to be built and ensure that there are utility connections and access for
emergency vehicles.

building heights are not related to the zone that the construction project is in. The heights are

elements of height regulation. Those essentially force the mass of the building to the center of the
property. There was discussion regarding appropriate language for a motion.

DISCUSSION ITEM
3.      Text Amendment – Chapter 13 Land Use Development Code; Off-Street Parking
        Requirements, 13.80. Continued Discussion: Review and Recommendation to City
        Council on Proposed Amendments to Title 13 of the Holladay City Code, Land Use
        and Development Regulations. Updating Minimum Parking Ratio Requirements by
        Use and Consideration of Allowing Flex Ratio Provisions as Adjustments to Required
        Minimums. File #01-04-23-3.
Mr. Teerlink reported that the commercial zones in the City, such as C-1 and C-2, include parking
ratio standards that may be considered dated by some. The Holladay Village and Holladay
Crossroads Zones have more modern parking ratio standards. Those standards were significantly
less than the more traditional parking ratios. The Land Use Table includes a list of land uses with
minimum requirements for off-street parking. Over the years, there has been a desire to shift the
parking ratios to avoid massive and unused parking lots. One example of that was in the Holladay
Village Zone with Harmons. When the Planning Commission reviewed the Site Plan for that
development, parking seemed tight for a grocery store, especially when the lot would be shared


                                         City of Holladay Planning Commission Meeting –07/12/2022

                                                7
with other neighboring uses. However, there was a turnover rate, with visitors coming and going
quickly, so it was fairly easy to find a parking spot most of the time.
The City was looking to create a change in the C-1 and C-2 Zone as it related to parking.
Mr. Teerlink explained that the Council could discuss some of the different parking ratio theories.
He noted that in terms of parking ratio theories, there is a large spectrum. On one end, there is a
more traditional parking ratio theory. On the other, a developer could determine what the parking
ratios would be based on their expected needs. The latter worked well for specific developments,
but over time, the business use may shift as businesses went in and out. That parking ratio could
limit the type of businesses that were able to utilize the space in the future.

Mr. Teerlink stated that a middle ground solution was a shared parking lot design. This meant
there were parking lots shared among different property owners. For instance, there could be
residential, office, commercial, and retail users utilizing the same parking lot. He noted that this
solution would involve specifically assigned stalls for residential uses or even retail spaces.

It was proposed that continued discussions take place with the Planning Commission to determine
whether parking ratios should be dropped to a more modern level. This would reduce the size of
future parking lots and limit the number of expansive heat islands in the City. Mr. Teerlink
explained that on the heels of the Parking Ratio Change Study, there would be a request from the
City Council and the Mayor to look at the commercial zones in terms of mixed uses. If the parking
ratios were too high, it was unlikely that there would be quality mixed-use developments.

In an effort to create more flexible and fluid redevelopment opportunities for property owners, the
parking ratios were being reexamined. Chair Howard Layton noted that this was an interesting
issue. It was much easier to determine parking when there is mass transit nearby. A lot of areas
that have access to mass transit have lower parking requirements. Mr. Teerlink reported that on
Page 3 of the Draft Amendments document, there is a table of menu items. Those items could be
used to explain why a parking reduction may be appropriate at a specific property. For instance,
if there was a certain proximity to transit, shared parking, or on-site bicycle facilities. He clarified
that a minimum amount of parking would need to remain.

Commissioner Banks wondered if the suggested parking reductions on the table had been
borrowed from another municipality. Mr. Teerlink denied this. The information came from a
study that had previously been shared with the Commission. Commissioner Banks wondered if
the Xs shown on the table reflected strategies that would reduce traffic. Mr. Teerlink confirmed
this and stated that those menu items could potentially reduce traffic.

Commissioner Roach noted that the City of Holladay is trying to create more walkable
neighborhoods. He wondered if that had been considered with the parking ratio amendments.
Mr. Teerlink stated that walkability nodes are extremely important when looking at traffic and
parking reductions. He reported that there is a Reciprocal Parking Agreement with a clinic in the
Holladay Village Zone. Outside of the clinic hours, the entire parking lot is open for public use.
That was part of a plan that was still being developed for the Holladay Village Zone.
Commissioner Prince wondered if the property owners were still liable for injuries after hours or
if there was shared liability. Mr. Godfrey explained that those situations had been considered



                                           City of Holladay Planning Commission Meeting –07/12/2022

                                                   8
during negotiations with the Olympus Family Medicine Clinic. Those occurrences were extremely
rare and the property owners agreed to the after-hours use.

Commissioner Chris Layton believed the numbers included on the chart in the Draft Amendments
document were rational as far as dwelling units and bedrooms. However, he noted that residential
projects need to have enough parking for daytime and nighttime uses. Mr. Teerlink agreed. There
was a hierarchy of uses that should not be tampered with. When there were multi-family
developments, the primary concern typically had to do with guest parking. Certain parking ratio
elements would need to be maintained. He noted that there was an opportunity to be more flexible
when it came to commercial, retail, and restaurant uses. Commissioner Prince echoed comments
shared by Commissioner Chris Layton. Residents would park their vehicles at their homes each
night, so it made sense to have stronger guidelines for residential dwellings.

Mr. Teerlink shared the proposed amendment for multiple dwelling units. The definition of
multiple dwelling units was three or more units proposed. He shared the ratio currently enforced
in the Holladay Village and Holladay Hills Zones. Rather than two spaces for each unit, similar
to the single-family ratio, it would depend on the number of bedrooms within the multi-family
unit. Chair Howard Layton wondered what City Staff was asking the Planning Commission to
consider. Mr. Teerlink explained that the intention was for there to be a recommendation to the
City Council in the future. However, he currently wanted to determine whether the Commission
was comfortable maintaining the peak parking ratios, with the exception of multi-family
developments, and the provision that would allow applicants to come to the Planning Commission
during the Site Plan review process to ask for an allowed reduction based on items in the table.

Chair Howard Layton wondered how the parking ratio amendments would be implemented.
Mr. Teerlink explained that the amendments would be implemented and codified immediately
after approval. If an existing parking lot is redone, the developer would need to consider the new
tree and planting requirements per stall. If they want to reduce the size of the parking lot, the menu
items would need to be considered during the Site Plan process.

Commissioner Chris Layton noted that some of the base numbers seemed high in certain areas.
For example, banks, businesses, or professional offices. He pointed out that it started with two
spaces and then one space for every 200 square feet of floor area. He felt that needed to be looked
at, especially for professional office uses, since there was a lot more remote work being done. He
believed remote work would continue to take place. Additionally, the numbers for doctors and
dental clinics seemed high, since the parking needs were spread out throughout the day. With
those uses, there was no peak period to accommodate. He liked the progressive nature of the
proposed amendments but stated that there were still other opportunities. Mr. Teerlink asked that
Commissioners share written comments so additional amendments could be made to the draft.

Mr. Godfrey noted that the standards presented were minimums. Most apartment buildings would
have more parking than the minimum amount required. This would likely be true for other
residential uses as well. Commissioner Prince liked the idea of seeing another draft of the
document. Mr. Teerlink noted that amendments would be made based on Commissioner feedback
and the item would return to the Planning Commission for additional discussion and consideration.
