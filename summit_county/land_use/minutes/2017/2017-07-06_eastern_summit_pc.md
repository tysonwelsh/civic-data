1. Review the approval of Chapters 2 and 4. – Dave Thomas, Deputy County Attorney 
Attorney Dave Thomas stated the County Council enacted amendments to Chapter 4 and 
Chapter 2, as well as the definitions. There is no longer a Lot of Record. Now there are 
conforming parcels and non-conforming parcels. In Chapter 4 there is a definition of what a 
Conforming parcel is: a subdivision lot, a parcel that has been legally created and conforms 
with zoning, a statutory 100 acres that can carve off one acre under the state statute, 
grandfathered parcels (those established before May 6, 1996), and Agricultural parcels 
(must be continually used for an agricultural activity).  Non-conforming parcels can 
become Conforming parcels through a conversion process through a parcel boundary 
adjustment, going through the subdivision process, a plat amendment or a special 
exception. There are provisions now in the Code that say when we talk about development, 
this is not about people deeding off parts of their property, which they can do and does not 
affect if they are Conforming or Non-conforming. If someone passes away and wants to 
deed off the land, that is legal. Commissioner Wharton asked if the lots are six acres in a 
six-acre zone, would that still be Non-conforming parcels. Attorney Thomas answered if 
they were deeded off because of an estate sale, but still conform to Zoning, then they were 
probably legally created. If the purpose for dividing off was an agricultural parcel 
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission

(agricultural activity), then it must remain in use for agriculture. It cannot be used to create 
a Subdivision. This allows a lot more flexibility than the Lot of Record, and allows 
individuals who have a piece of property divided in the past, but after 1996 to become a 
Conforming parcel, if they conform to zoning. If a parcel is a Conforming parcel it does not 
need to go through the process and would be eligible for development. Development is a 
more defined term that is the same on the East side and in Snyderville Basin. 
This should catch everything, and if it does not, there is still the Special Exception. The 
County Council’s position is this is a new thing and there will be bumps in the road. If 
problems arise, there will be changes made and Council recognizes that this is not a 
completely finished document. Director Putt stated that the language has not changed for 
forty years as Lot of Record and that language went away with the adoption of these 
changes.

According to Chapter 4, the permits that are now available were shown. There is a dividing 
line. Where there used to be Major and Minor Subdivisions, it is now three Lots or less and

Director believes there may be some controversy or things the Planning Commission 
should know about. The Subdivisions that are four lots or more are now County Council 
approval. The Planning Commission will see those and have a Public Hearing to give a 
recommendation to the County Council. This will be teamed up with Master Plan 
Developments (MPD) because some of those Subdivisions may have to go through the MPD 
process. The Planning Commission is still the approving body for the Conditional Use 
Permits. Low impact permits have the Community Development Director as the approving 
body, and will provide the Planning Commission with a periodic list of the approvals. 
Temporary Use Permits are Director approval. Special Exceptions still go to the County 
Council. Master Plan Development is required when there are rezones, a legislative act, ten

remnant versus remainder parcels. An example of a remnant parcel is a twenty-five-acre

Chapter 2 had several changes made. Geologic hazards precluded development, but the
