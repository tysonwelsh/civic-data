          Proposed Scope of Work for the Butterfield Creek Master Plan
Storm water runoff generated in the area generally bounded by 13200 South on the north, Bangerter Hwy
on the South, Jordan River on the east, and the Utah and Salt Lake Canal on the west in Riverton (Study
Area) discharges to the historic south Butterfield Creek channel. A master plan for that area was developed
in 2006. Since that time, development patterns in the area have changed significantly. Riverton City has
requested that Bowen Collins and Associates (BC&A) complete a new master plan for the Project Area.
Because the area is mostly developed, the master plan will focus on recommending improvements to
Butterfield Creek to safely convey runoff from the design storm to the Jordan River. The purpose of this
project is to develop a hydrologic and hydraulic model of the Study Area and a prepare list of capital
improvement projects based on the model results that will resolve capacity deficiencies along Butterfield
Creek.

                               PROPOSED SCOPE OF SERVICES

Phase 1 – Design Phase Services

Task 1: Progress and Coordination Meetings. BC&A staff will coordinate with Riverton City staff
during the project and will prepare for and attend up to four progress/coordination meetings: the first
meeting will be a project kickoff meeting to review data previously provided by the City and discuss project
objectives, the second meeting will be to review the hydrologic and hydraulic model results, the third
meeting will be to review the draft list of capital improvement projects and discuss project prioritization
criteria, and the last meeting will be review the draft version of the master plan report. We will also identify
the design storm criteria that will be utilized as the basis of this study.

Task 2: Data Collection and Review. BC&A personnel will collect and review existing data to provide
information needed to complete the analyses. The data will include the 2006 Butterfield Creek Master
Plans and relevant studies, the Riverton GIS storm drain database, survey data of the Butterfield Creek
channel, design drawings and discharge rates for developments in the Study Area, topography and aerial
photos of the area from the Utah Geospatial Resource Center (UGRC) website. We have assumed that
Riverton City will provide us with the following data in the Study Area: GIS database with pipe size and
pipe material data, manhole rim and invert data; storm drain outfall locations; culvert sizes on Butterfield
Creek; locations, capacities, and design parameters for all known storm water detention facilities; existing
survey data, digital zoning and land use maps of the study area; and design record drawings of storm drain
facilities in the area.

Task 3: Site Visit. BC&A will complete a field visit to observe general conditions of the Butterfield Creek
channel. City personnel may participate in the site visit. We have assumed that City staff will contact
private property owners to get permission to access culvert locations (if needed).

Task 4: Survey. We will collect survey data at the major structures (culverts and bridges) that cross
Butterfield Creek. The surveyor will also set-up control points for a drone survey of the channel. We have
assumed that the City will perform a drone survey of the channel and develop topographic data for the
channel. We will utilize LiDAR data from UGRC collected in 2013-2014 for the area outside the channel.

Task 5: Hydrologic Modeling. BC&A will use information collected in Tasks 2, 3 and 4 to develop a
hydrologic model of study area. A model will be developed to simulate runoff from both existing and
projected future land use conditions. The model will be used to simulate the rainfall/runoff process from
the Study Area using the storm event identified in Task 1 to estimate flow rates in South Butterfield Creek
that can be used in the hydraulic model. The Autodesk Storm and Sanitary Analysis (SSA) modeling
software will be used to simulate urban runoff. We will use the storm drain GIS database for pipe sizes and
slopes. Where pipe invert data is not included in the database, we will assume the pipe slope match the
ground surface slope.

Task 6: Hydraulic Modeling
A hydraulic model of the South Butterfield Creek will be developed using the HEC-RAS computer
software. Channel cross sections will be spaced at approximately 500 foot intervals and will be based on
data collected in Task 4. The hydraulic model will be used to estimate channel capacity and identify where
capacity deficiencies exist. The HEC-RAS model will be developed using similar methodology as the 2006
Butterfield Creek Master Plan.

Task 7: Identify Deficiencies and Develop a Capital Improvement List. We will identify portions of
the existing South Butterfield Creek channel that have capacity deficiencies based on hydraulic model
results. We will meet with City operations personnel to discuss deficiencies and identify potential
improvements to the South Butterfield Creek channel and culvert crossings. Potential improvements that
will be analyzed include detention basins, culvert improvement, and channel improvements. After
modeling the potential improvements, a draft list of capital improvements projects will be developed and
reviewed with City representatives.

Task 8: Master Plan Report. After BC&A has received comments from the City on the draft capital
improvements projects, we will prepare a master plan report. The draft report will document the analyses
and recommended capital improvement projects. After submitting the draft report to the City for review,
we will meet to discuss comments the draft report. After addressing the City’s comments, the final report,
with technical appendices, will be prepared and submitted to the City. We have assumed that the City will
only need to provide one set of comments on the draft report before the report is finalized. The draft and
final reports will be submitted digitally to the City in PDF files.
Optional Modeling Approach An optional approach that would replace Task 5 and 6 and provide more
accurate results would be to develop a combined 2-dimensional hydrologic and hydraulic model in the
FLO-2D software. FLO-2D is a volume conservation, two-dimensional (2-D), flood routing software. FLO-
2D routes flow (rainfall runoff) over a grid comprised of square elements based on topography (grid element
elevations) and watershed roughness (Manning’s n-value assigned to each grid element). This 2-D
modeling approach is well suited for simulating shallow, unconfined flow typical of highly urbanized areas
(e.g. flow within streets and around buildings). Additional information regarding the FLO-2D software can
be found at https://www.flo-2d.com/. The FLO-2D model will also incorporate hydraulically significant
features within the study area, such as: culverts, regional channels conveying one-dimensional flow, and
storm drain networks. The FLO-2D model will be developed using the known existing and future land-use
condition at the time of the study, 2013-14 LiDAR topographic data and survey, provided GIS storm drain
database and other readily available data. Where pipe rim elevations and inverts are not available or do not
match the topographic data, we will assume the pipe slope matches the ground surface slope. We will also
assume a pipe depth based on the pipe size. It is anticipated that a 10 to 15 ft grid cell size will be used for
this study area.
