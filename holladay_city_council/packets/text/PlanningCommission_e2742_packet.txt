SUMMARY
Applicant, Jackson Haslam, representing property owner Ivory Homes, is proposing the creation of a
new lot by subdividing the existing Lot 1 within the Charleston Place Subdivision, requiring an
amendment to the existing subdivision.
The Property is .55 acres (23,958 sq. ft) and is within the R-1-10 zone, a residential single-family zone
with a minimum lot size of 10,000 sq. ft, minimum lot width of 80 feet, and minimum lot frontage of 60
feet at the right of way line.

A home previously existing on Lot 1 and was torn down with a new home under construction. The new
home was started with the intent to subdivide and is thus pushed toward Highland Dr. All setbacks meet

This subdivision amendment creates a net increase of one additional lot, approved for a single-family
dwelling unit.

BACKGROUND
The existing parcel and others within the Charleston Place Subdivision, an 11-lot subdivision that was
created in 1954. The lots were all larger, averaging around half an acre, with some smaller and some
larger.
The subdivision proposed to be amended was originally created with Covenants Conditions and
Restrictions (CC&Rs), in place, which are managed and enforced by an association of homeowners. The
Charleston Place subdivision CC&Rs elect to place certain legal restrictions on owners of properties
within the subdivision. This particular CC&R had restrictions regarding property sizes that prevented
subdivisions. The property owner has been working with the HOA and will follow a non-municipal legal
process to change the CC&Rs that would allow the recordation of the subdivision.

NOTE: CC&Rs are private legal agreements, governing properties within the subdivision but do not
exempt compliance with municipal ordinances. Cities do not enforce them, nor ensure/compel property
owners meet the legal obligations within them. The Planning Commission’s, responsibility as the Land
Use Authority, in subdivision application review is to ensure that statutory and local requirements are
met.

This new home that is currently under construction (foundation poured recently) was designed to meet
both the setbacks for the current lot configuration and those setbacks required for the future
subdivision of the property. The subdivision as proposed will not create any non-compliance for the
proposed Lot 1A.

Offsite improvement requirements are determined at the time of a building permit application and are
reviewed by the City Engineer. As a single lot, gutter and sidewalks would be required on the full
Charleston lane frontage of the property, in order to connect existing improvements made at the corner
of Highland Dr. and Charleston. The sidewalk requirement would be reassessed for Lot 1B upon applying
for a building permit if the subdivision amendment is approved. Sidewalk on Charleston Lane and corner
improvements would still apply to Lot 1A.

The TRC recommends that the commission hold the required public hearing and consider comments
presented. The CED Director has found that all required preliminary and final elements of a residential
subdivision proposal have been reviewed and accepted by the TRC and have been determined to be
substantially complete as per the City’s submission requirements. Future development plans for a
single-family structure on each lot has been communicated and plans have been approved for a new
home on the existing parcel. The new structure has been designed in a way to comply with setback
requirements for Lot 1A should the subdivision amendment to create a new lot be approved. The TRC
can thus recommend that The Commission approve the PRELIMINARY SUBDIVISION AMENDMENT
PLAN.

 2   1.      Text Amendment - 13.04: DEFINITIONS and 13.100.010: TABLE OF ALLOWED
 3           USES; (A STATE REQUIRED AMENDMENT) Review and Recommendation to the
 4           City Council on Proposed Updates, Consolidations and/or Clarifying Amendments to
 5           Title 13, of the Holladay City Code, Land Use and Development Regulations as they
 6           Relate to Statute Obligations, Set Forth by State of Utah Legislation, Modifying
 7           Provisions Related to Home-Based Micro-Schools and Micro-Education Entities.
 8           Item Reviewed as a Legislative Action, According to Procedures Set Forth in
 9           Holladay Ordinance §13.07. File #25-4-03.
10   City Planner, Carrie Marsh, presented the Staff Report and explained that the item is a Text
11   Amendment for 13:04 – Definitions and 13.100.010 – Table of Allowed Uses. The Text
12   Amendment is related to State requirements for micro-schools that are home-based and micro-
13   education entities. The changes are in line with Senate Bill (“S.B.”) 13, which was passed during
14   the 2024 Legislative Session. S.B. 13 provides home-based micro-schools and micro-education
15   entities with similar requirements, waivers, and rights as private and charter schools. Ms. Marsh
16   explained that a home-based microschool is a K-12 education service for up to 16 students from a
17   residential dwelling or property. A micro-education entity provides K-12 education services for
18   up to 100 students. The State regulates a lot of the educational requirements, so most of the
19   requirements are based on those. The definitions in Title 13 match the State definitions for a home-
20   based micro-school and a micro-education entity. Those are included in the Meeting Materials
21   Packet and the draft text.
22
23   All of the regulations for home-based micro-schools and micro-education entities are regulated in
24   Title 5 under Business Licensing. Cities can put various regulations on home-based micro-schools
25   and micro-education entities to address parking, traffic, hours of operation, and site development.
26   In addition, cities can regulate the location in order to avoid risks to health and safety. There are
27   size requirements for the floor space needed per student in each application. For home-based
28   micro-schools, it is 35 square feet per student. For micro-education entities, it is 20 net square feet
29   per student. Microschools have to file with the State and the municipality as a business. The
30   standards for parking and the size requirements will naturally filter the use out of residential areas,
31   with the exception of the home-based micro-schools. There needs to be one parking space for
32   each non-resident employee as well as one additional space. Outdoor play areas need to be secured
33   and rooms have to have carbon monoxide and smoke detectors. The State requires them to be
34   allowed in all zones, so that has been reflected in the Land Use Table. A recommendation will be
35   made to the Council.
36
37   Chair Roach asked if a micro-education entity is considered separate from a boarding school. He
38   wanted to know if there is anything for a boarding school in the City Code. Ms. Marsh does not
39   know if there is anything related to a boarding school in the code. The closest would be a treatment
40   center. With home-based micro-schools and micro-education entities, it is possible to restrict
41   hours of operation.

26   PUBLIC HEARING
27   1.      Zone Map Amendment – Rezone from R-1-87 to R-1-43 – 2232 East Pheasant Way.
28           Review and Make a Recommendation to the City Council on a Proposal by Applicant,
29           Randall Paul, to Amend the Holladay Zone Map at this Location from the Current R-1-
30           87 Zone (2-acre Minimum Lots) to the R-1-43 Zone (1-acre Minimum Lots) for
31           Approximately 2.06 Acres of Property. Item Reviewed as a Legislative Action,
32           According to Procedures Set Forth in Holladay Ordinance §13.07. File #25-4-04.
33   Ms. Marsh presented the Staff Report and explained that the item is a Zone Map Amendment request.
34   The property is located at 2232 East Pheasant Way and the request is to rezone from the R-1-87 Zone
35   to the R-1-43 Zone. She noted that the R-1-87 Zone has a 2-acre minimum lot size and the R-1-43
36   Zone has a 1-acre minimum lot size. The intention of the rezone is to facilitate a property line shift
37   between two neighboring properties. In the General Plan for Holladay, this property is identified as
38   Country Estates – Protected. The area is largely serviced by private lanes, has a dense and mature
39   tree canopy, and has large parcels. When the City incorporated in 1999, there was an intentional
40   zoning pattern, where areas were designated with a zone that had a minimum lot size that was larger
41   than some of the existing lots. As a result, some non-conforming lots were created in the area.
42
43   In 2007, a Preliminary Subdivision and Planned Unit Development (“PUD”) for five units on 15.75
44   acres of land was approved by the Planning Commission, but never recorded. It involved 2232 East
45   and 2242 East as well as several other properties. In 2014, the property owner wanted to split one of
46   the lots, but instead of going through a new PUD approval process, there was a two-lot subdivision

 2   land.
 3
 4   Within the General Plan, the Country Estates – Protected designation is intended to preserve existing
 5   large lot development patterns. It calls out the mature tree canopy and the ambiance created by estate-
 6   type development on private lanes as being protected features within that area. New development
 7   can be appropriately accommodated through utilizing unclaimed density by subdivision and not by
 8   rezone. Ms. Marsh explained that the intent of the two-acre standard was to preserve open space.
 9   During the Work Session, there were discussions about lot coverage and how much of the structure
10   would cover the subject parcel. The analysis of the rezone is included in the Staff Report with the
11   rezone approval standards. The role of the Planning Commission is to make a recommendation to
12   the City Council on the rezone. The applicant was invited to share comments with the Commission.
13
14   The applicant, Randall Paul, introduced himself to the Planning Commission. He understands what
15   the City has tried to do with the standards that have been set. Mr. Paul shared an image with the
16   Commission and pointed out the location of the property line. There is a desire to move the property
17   line so there is a 3-acre lot and a 1-acre lot. Mr. Paul noted that this rezone will not change any of
18   the structures, but the intention is to move the property line. There will be no new development on
19   the sites. In the center portion, there will be a no-build area, as there is no desire for someone to build
20   an additional structure on the neighboring property. One of the Conditions of Approval could be for
21   a no-build easement. He does not believe what is proposed will harm the City in any way.
22
23   The applicant stated that the intention is to have the open space remain. Chair Roach wanted to
24   understand the reason for the property line adjustment. Mr. Paul explained that there was a divorce
25   last year and the house next door will be sold. The house would be easier to sell with a 3-acre parcel.
26   He pointed out that it would be difficult to sell the house with the property line in the current location,
27   as it is about 25 feet off of the house and in the middle of the driveway. Commissioner Prince asked
28   what year Mr. Paul and his daughter purchased the properties. Neighboring property owner, Jane
29   Snow explained that when the land was purchased, all four acres were purchased at once. The person
30   who sold the land always knew that the plan was to have her parents on one acre with her family on
31   the three acres. The Covenants, Conditions, and Restrictions (“CC&Rs”) that are in place state that
32   the land can be divided, but there will need to be a City process. That is the reason for the application.
33
34   Given the CC&Rs, Ms. Snow explained that there was always an assumption this process would
35   happen, but that it would be straightforward due to the 1-acre zoning on most sides. Commissioner
36   Fonte asked if the applicants have explored ways to address this issue without a rezone. Ms. Snow
37   confirmed that a lot line adjustment was looked into. Ms. Marsh reported that a lot line adjustment
38   could not be approved, because it would create a non-conforming lot in the zone. It sounds like the
39   previous property owner who was facilitating the PUD gave the impression that these two lots could
40   follow that same PUD process. It might be the better route for the applicant to work with the
41   neighboring property owner for a PUD in the area. That is the only way to make a non-conforming
42   property within the R-1-87 Zone. She reported that a PUD does not need to adhere strictly to the
43   minimum requirements for a zone. There is flexibility with setbacks and property size in exchange
44   for meeting the goals of a PUD. The goals include open space and the preservation of natural features.
45

 2   to be six acres to have a PUD. The two properties are just over four acres in total and would not
 3   qualify for the minimum area. In order to pursue a PUD, the applicant would need to bring in a
 4   neighboring property owner. When it comes to the two properties, there was a standard subdivision
 5   rather than a PUD process, because the two lots met the minimum lot size requirements of the zone.

 7   Chair Roach noted that Staff has proposed an alternative to the rezone request. The Staff Report
 8   states that the rezone request does not meet the zone requirements. The applicant has made a case for
 9   the reason he believes the zone should be amended in spite of the Staff argument. Before the process
10   moves forward, he asked the applicant if there was interest in temporarily withdrawing the request in
11   order to explore the alternative. Alternatively, the applicant can move forward with this request and
12   potentially have a negative recommendation forwarded to the City Council. Discussions were had
13   about the current zone. Chair Roach noted that there is a precedent of other applicants who have been
14   denied a rezone request in the zone. Mr. Paul pointed out that those previous applications have
15   included development. In this case, there is no new development proposed on the properties.

17   Chair Roach opened the public hearing.
18
19   Leanne Stillman gave her address as 2149 East Pheasant Way and stated that the Paul family have
20   been wonderful neighbors. She asked the Commission to think about what would happen if this was
21   approved. There would be a three-acre parcel on the east and a 1-acre parcel on the west. The 3-acre
22   parcel is what is proposed to be sold. The buyer could come to the Planning Commission and argue
23   that only two acres are needed for their property and there could be a separate 1-acre parcel. Based
24   on that, an argument could be made to develop that additional one acre. Her concern has nothing to
25   do with the Paul family, but with a potential new buyer, as that will create a lot of unknowns.
26
27   There were no further comments. The public hearing was closed.
28
29   The applicant was invited to address the comments made during the public hearing. Mr. Paul reported
30   that there will be a permanent Homeowners Agreement between the three-acre and one-acre lot. This
31   will ensure that there is a permanent no-build easement in the deed of the property. When the three-
32   acre parcel is purchased, it will state that there is a permanent no-build section. He pointed out the
33   location.
34
35   A question was asked about whether or not a future owner could change the rules of the agreement.
36   Mr. Christopherson confirmed that it would be possible if there was a Homeowners Association
37   (“HOA”). There would need to be unity of ownership. If the two homes were owned by the same
38   entity or individual, the boundary could be eliminated as well as the easement and HOA. It is possible
39   to place a permanent no-build area on the property, but there are ways someone could get around that.
40
41   Mr. Christopherson explained that if this rezone is approved by the City Council, a one-acre lot would
42   be created. The General Plan would need to be amended with a carve-out of that section. That would
43   likely necessitate changing some other zones around it to avoid creating a spot zone situation. This
44   would create a precedent in the Country Estates – Protected area to reduce the size down to one acre.

 1   Commissioner Berndt suggested that City Staff share the General Plan map. Mr. Christopherson
 2   clarified that the nearby 1-acre lots are legal non-conforming lots. Ms. Marsh reiterated that in the
 3   R-1-87 Zone, there are a lot of legal non-conforming parcels that were there when the R-1-87 Zone
 4   was established. The R-1-87 Zone clearly communicates the desire to retain two-acre lots in the area,
 5   even with the non-conforming parcels. Commissioner Berndt appreciates what the applicant wants
 6   to do, but worries this will set a precedent that will have future consequences. He does not want to
 7   encroach into the two-acre zoning with additional 1-acre lots. His concern is that there might be
 8   unintended consequences associated with this application. Commissioner Vilchinsky pointed out that
 9   the property owners purchased the parcels knowing that there were two acres each. The homes and
10   driveway were built with the intention of changing the property line after the building took place.
11
12   Mr. Paul reported that there were discussions with James Dreyfous when he sold the land to them
13   about this. Mr. Dreyfous stated that it is possible to have a 1-acre parcel and a three-acre parcel. It
14   is in the CC&Rs that it is possible to make this split. Ms. Marsh wondered whether there are specific
15   details in the CC&Rs about that process. Ms. Snow explained that the four acres were purchased
16   together with the intention of having a smaller lot for her parents. Commissioner Berndt pointed out
17   that there could be a problem created if the rezone application for this property was approved.
18
19   Commissioner Prince moved to forward a recommendation to the City Council to DENY an
20   application by C. Randall Paul to amend the Holladay Zoning Map for 2.06 acres of land, located
21   at 2232 East Pheasant Way, from R-1-87 to R-1-43, based upon the following findings:
22
23          1.      A change to the R-1-42 Zone is not supported by the General Plan.
24
25          2.      Reduction in lot size has the future potential to reduce the valuable tree resources
26                  that have been identified as a high-value commodity for preservation.
27
28          3.      Retaining the “semi-rural” feel of the community in the part of Holladay is critical
29                  to the long-term quality of life and identity of the City.
30
31          4.      Changing the zoning in this case would make it difficult for the City to maintain the
32                  integrity of the large lot zoning in this area in the future.
33
34          5.      No change in the R-1-87 zone should occur given the General Plan’s emphasis on
35                  retention of the land use character of the area.
36
37   Commissioner Berndt seconded the motion. Vote on Motion: Commissioner Cunningham-Aye;
38   Commissioner Vilchinsky-Aye; Commissioner Fonte-Aye; Commissioner Prince-Aye;
39   Commissioner Berndt-Aye; Chair Roach-Aye. The motion passed unanimously.
