ZT-8-2020
West Valley City
A zone text change to Section 7-2-114 to add specific fence regulations to properties along
4100 South between Redwood Road and 8400 West. 0.34 acre

The City’s Public Works Department is requesting a zone text change to Sections 7-2-114, which
is the Section of the Zoning Ordinance that addresses fence regulations. The proposed change
would add specific fence regulations to properties along 4100 South between Redwood Road and
8400 West. The following language from the Public Works Department is proposed:

       “This paragraph applies to all properties fronting on 4100 South Street. All new fences
       constructed on the front property line and side yards in front of structures shall be black
        ornamental Iron or aluminum metal fence; it shall have a three rail panel, with three or
        four inch air gaps; 2.5" posts; 0.75" square pickets.”

The following justification for this change was provided by the Public Works Department:

        “The City has invested millions of dollars to the reconstruction of the 4100 South
        roadway. Inside the project, it is planned to replace current fencing with a wrought iron
        (steel/aluminum) fence. As a main artery through the City, it would be valuable to have
        uniform fencing along the entire corridor to continue the aesthetic of the roadway. Within
        the 4100 south Reconstruction Project, all fences removed will be replaced with the new
        style of fence. The City is offering to replace all existing fences not included within the
        project scope with the new style of fence. The new ordinance will require all NEW fences
        to be in the same style/specification.”

By way of background, the 4100 South Reconstruction Project extends from Bangerter Highway
to 5460 West and is nearing completion. Fencing is currently being installed.


Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval.

   2. Continuance.

   3. Denial.

ZT-9-2020
Randy Moore
An ordinance amendment to define qualified 55 or older development and to allow
properties with access and frontage on a street with a planned right-of-way width of at
least 80 feet where a qualified 55 or older development is proposed to apply for the RM
zone

Randy Moore has requested an ordinance amendment to define qualified 55 or older
development and to allow properties with access and frontage on a street with a planned right-of-
way width of at least 80 feet where a qualified 55 or older development is proposed to apply for
the RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling Residential) zone.

Section 7-5-101 of the zoning ordinance includes conditions to determine whether or not a
property is eligible to be rezoned to the RM zone. Subsection of 4 of Section 7-5-101 states:

       “Property within City limits may only be amended or rezoned to the RM zone if all of the
       following conditions are met:

               a. For properties without existing Structures which are proposed to be developed,
               the property must be a minimum of one acre and the Density must not exceed 12
               units per acre. For properties with existing Structures proposed to be redeveloped,
               the property must be a minimum of 20,000 square feet and the Density must not
               exceed 20 units per acre. For properties that are west of Redwood Road and
               within one-half mile of the West Valley Central light rail station, the Decker Lake
               light rail station or the Redwood Junction light rail station, the Density may
               exceed 20 units per acre;

               b. The property shall meet any one of the following three requirements: (i) have
               access and Frontage on a Street with a planned Right-of-way width of at least 80
               feet (for properties under two acres) or 100 feet (for properties two acres or
               larger) as indicated on the Major Street Plan; (ii) be adjacent to existing
               multifamily residential development on two sides; or (iii) be located west of
               Redwood Road and within one-half mile of the West Valley Central light rail
               station, the Decker Lake light rail station, or the Redwood Junction light rail
               station;

               c. A Development Agreement must be proposed with the zone change application
               that addresses Dwelling Unit sizes, exterior materials, architecture, Landscaping,
               and project Amenities; and
               d. A traffic impact study prepared by a registered traffic engineer is required for
               projects with 100 or more Dwelling Units.”

The applicant’s proposal would amend (changes are underlined) condition b above to:

       “b. The property shall meet any one of the following three requirements: (i) have access
       and Frontage on a Street with a planned Right-of-way width of at least 80 feet (for
       properties under two acres or a qualified 55 or older development in which the density
       must not exceed 8 units per acre) or 100 feet (for properties two acres or larger) as
       indicated on the Major Street Plan; (ii) be adjacent to existing multifamily residential
       development on two sides; or (iii) be located west of Redwood Road and within one-half
       mile of the West Valley Central light rail station, the Decker Lake light rail station, or the
       Redwood Junction light rail station;”

The applicant is also proposing the following definition from the U.S. Department of Housing
and Urban Development (HUD) for qualified 55 or older development:

       “Qualified 55 or older development” means a development where at least 80 percent of
       the units must have at least one occupant who is 55 years of age or older; and The facility
       or community must publish and adhere to policies and procedures that demonstrate the
       intent to operate as "55 or older" housing; and The facility or community must comply
       with HUD's regulatory requirements for age verification of residents.”

Included with this report is map that shows remaining vacant land within the City as well as
roads designated on the Major Street Plan as an 80’ right-of-way and roads designated as 100’
right-of-way or more excluding nonaccess highways and freeways like I-215 and Bangerter
Highway. The map also highlights the one-half mile areas within eligible light rail stations. The
purpose of this map is to illustrate where rezone requests to the RM zone are currently allowed
and where they would be allowed if this application is approved.

Also included in this report is a graph showing the population of the City by age. As of 2018,
17% of the City’s population was aged 55 or older. Over the next 10 years, just over 15,000
more residents will fall into the 55 or older cohort.

For reference, the table below lists four developments within the City that were developed as
qualified 55 or older developments:

All of the projects listed in the table above were developed as planned unit developments (PUDs)
in the R-1-8 or R-1-10 zones. Section 7-5-101 limits rezones to the R-1-8 and R-1-10 zones to
properties under 2 acres. So, if this application is not approved, any future qualified 55 or older
development as defined by the applicant would need to be:
     rezoned to RM,
     less than 2 acres and rezoned to R-1-8 or R-1-10 or
     on property already zoned R-1-8 or R-1-10.

Since the four existing qualified 55 or older developments don’t exceed 7 units/acre in density,
staff recommends that the density for qualified 55 or older developments be capped at 7
units/acre.

As a reminder, if this application is approved, zone changes will still remain a legislative
decision. This means that the Planning Commission or Council does not have to approve rezone
requests to the RM zone that meet the conditions outlined in the ordinance.

CA-4-20
Wasatch Fleet Service Expansion
2290 S. 5600 W.
LI Zone (.37 acres)

The applicant, Scott Haslam representing Wasatch Fleet Service, is requesting conditional
approval for expansion of a commercial vehicle upfit facility at 2290 S. 5600 W. This property is
zoned LI, Light Industrial. It is located within the 5600 West Overlay Zone and the West Valley
City General Plan designates this area as Light Manufacturing. The surrounding zone is LI to the
north, south and west. The zoning is M, manufacturing to the east across 5600 West. The
surrounding uses include salvage yard type businesses to the west, warehousing to the south,
UDOT property to the north and the Warner Truck Center to the east.

Wasatch Fleet received conditional use approval for their main facility at
2265 S. 5650 W. in 2019 (C-12-2019). The business sells and installs truck and van racks, truck
flatbeds, snow plows, custom van interiors, service truck bodies and lift gates. Office uses and
vehicle sales are also conducted from the site.

During construction Mr. Haslam acquired additional property to the south and has been working
with UDOT to get an access approved on 5600 West. The original plan was to only have access
from the west off 5650 West. There is a Delay Agreement in place for those street improvements
and the 5600 West would access would solve some Fire access issues and feasibility issues with
actually constructing 5650 West so they are working diligently to get that alternative access
approved. However, one of the issues with the 5600 West access is UDOT will most likely
require a median and turn/decel lane. That would impact the previously approved design for the
5600 West Streetscape Improvements which includes a 15’ landscaped area, a 6’ sidewalk and a
9’ landscaped area with decorative street lighting. Those improvements have not been
constructed at this time so that provides some flexibility with the location of the sidewalk and
landscaping in that setback area. Staff is working with the applicant and UDOT and will continue
to review plans as the location of the roadway improvements get determined to ensure that there
is appropriate sidewalk connectivity to the parcels to the north and south for when they
(re)develop in the future.

The final site design has not been completed but they will need an exception for those setbacks
based off the location of the newly constructed on site improvements (parking lot and building)
that were part of the original construction and now the new right-of-way requirements. The
streetscape design will most likely just be reduced in the 9’ landscaped area behind the sidewalk
on the north side of the site but again that depends on the final location of the curb and gutter and
how the sidewalk lines up with the adjacent properties.

Other site considerations that are being reviewed include an existing building on this expanded
site. It will be used for storage and the exterior will be updated and maintained. The landscape
plan will also be updated so overall there is a minimum of 10% landscaping throughout the entire
site and all areas on the new parcel will be improved and maintained accordingly. There is an old
septic system and well that is in the process of being abandoned. The parcels all need to be
consolidated and the sidewalk and lighting easements will be updated. We are also coordinating
a new address off 5600 West (2272 South 5600 West). The Fire Department has indicated that is
for some reason the access along 5600 West does not get approved or installed that the original
24’ fire lane requirements for 5650 West would need to be installed within 30 days of written
notice or occupancy will be revoked. Lastly, we are also working with Mr. Haslam to ensure a
proper fire access is maintained around the building, that storage is not placed out in the right-of-
way of 5650 West and that the required parking spaces are not used for vehicle display or
merchandise.

Staff Alternatives:

Approval, subject to the resolution of any concerns raised at the public hearing as well as the
following:

           1. The overall site and landscaping must be installed per the approved plan and in
              accordance with all the applicable standards set forth in Title 7.

           2. An exception is granted for the 5600 West Streetscape Improvements and the
              requirements may be modified according to the UDOT right-of-way requirements
              and that setback area shall be designed to ensure connectivity to the adjacent
              parcels to the north and south.

           3. The exterior of the existing building on site must be maintained and repaired.

           4. No outside storage or merchandise display is permitted in required parking areas,
              within the right-of-way or along the fire lane around the perimeter of the building.
              All merchandise shall be stored in the building or within the designated paved
              area that is secured with decorative fencing (wrought iron).

           5. Must meet the requirements of all affected departments and agencies including
              but not limited to the Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT) and the Salt
              Lake Valley Health Department.

           6. Subject to review upon valid complaint.


Continuance, to allow for the resolutions of any issues raised at the public hearing.

Project Overview:

       The Alpizar Planned Unit Development will be a residential community consisting of 50
       units on just over 3 acres. This equates to an overall density of 16 units per acre. Proposed
       townhomes will be 3 stories with two different building types. Proposed unit sizes are
       1,750 square feet for the west units and 1,950 square feet for the east units. Each unit will
       have 3 bedrooms and a bonus room that could be used for an office or similar.

       As with similar proposals from Mr. Platt, the architecture is very urban. All units will have
       a roof top patio which has been used in the Cobalt Landing PUD on Redwood Road. This
       has been a nice selling feature in Cobalt Landing, and they are perpetuating that here with
       all units having that amenity. Proposed building materials are stucco, fiber cement siding
       and tile.

       The developer is suggesting that two color schemes be used for this project. The colored
       variations will need to be substantial enough so that one color does not dominate the
       project. Final colors for this project will be reviewed by staff during the final plat stage of
       this project.

       All townhome units will have a two-car garage for parking. Overall, there are 174 parking
       spaces shown on the subdivision plat, of which 100 of these spaces will be in garages. 40
       spaces will be in driveways on the east units, and 34 guest spaces will be provided along
       the west side of the private street. The resulting parking ratio is 3.5 stalls per unit. This
       number is consistent with other recent projects such as Cobalt Landing and the Villages on
       27th. These projects were parked at 3.9 and 3.7 spaces respectively. Erin Hills on 5600
       West and 3500 South was parked at 3.2 spaces per unit.

Other matters related to this project:

Access:

       Access to the subdivision will be gained from 3500 South. Since this project contains less
       than 100 units, a single access is allowed under the Fire Code. The preliminary plat
       indicates that the street width for the main access road will be 34 feet which does provide
       for 26 feet of asphalt. A sidewalk will be located on the east side of the private street.

       Per the development agreement, the developer is dedicating an additional 17.5 feet along
       3500 South to accommodate a future right-of-way width of 115 feet. Public improvements
       will be installed in accordance with plan and profiles approved by UDOT. It very well
       may be that the existing improvements will remain until a later date when full right-of-way
       improvements are installed. The developer will coordinate all matters related to access
       with UDOT.

       All internal drive access will be private. Drive courts will be a minimum of 20 feet in
       width. Depths of the drive courts are shallow enough that turnarounds will not be needed.
       The drive court and main access road itself will serve as a hammerhead configuration for
       emergency services and garbage collection.

Building Setbacks:

       Building setbacks for this project are illustrated on the site plan. Along 3500 South, a 25-
       foot setback is proposed. Setbacks along the remaining sides will be 10 feet or greater.
       Since this project is a planned unit development, there is flexibility in site design standards.
       As the project moves through the review process, changes may occur to the site plan.
       However, a substantial change would warrant review by the Planning Commission.

Landscaping/Fencing:

       While landscaping is generally addressed at the final plat stage, staff would like to mention
       a few things at this point in the review process. The overall open space will comprise 50%
       of the project site. The developer will be required to submit a full landscape and irrigation
      plan. This plan will need to address landscaping along 3500 South, the perimeter of the
      entire property and of each building.

      The project has a large central open space on the west side of the development. Staff wants
      to make sure that if storm water is planned to be retained/detained in this area, that the
      developer ensure that this space does not have a depressed area that would reduce the
      amount of useable open space. The developer may need to look at other storm drain storage
      options to ensure that this requirement is met. Staff will work with the applicant and the
      City’s Engineering Division to ensure that the large common space is able to be used for
      the purposes it is intended.

      Upon completion of all of the on-site improvements, the developer shall provide a letter to
      the City from an independent engineering firm which certifies that all of the on-site
      improvements are completed as shown on the plans approved by the City. For the purposes
      of this provision, on-site improvements shall include landscaping, irrigation, private
      streets, sidewalks, parking, lighting, entrance features, fencing, playground equipment,
      picnic tables and any other outdoor recreational amenity.

      The developer is required to install a 6-foot masonry wall along the south boundary of the
      development. In addition, all areas adjacent to the EZ Auto Sales will be fenced with the
      same masonry wall. According to the developer, the masonry wall will be a post and panel
      wall. A pattern and color have yet to be selected. Staff will coordinate this item during
      final plat review.

North Jordan Canal:

      A right-of-way or easement for the North Jordan Canal is located along the southern
      boundary of the subdivision. According to a recent site visit by the City Engineer, it does
      not appear that this site takes any storm water to the canal easement. In fact, there is not
      much of a canal operation at all. I spoke with the Canal Company and they are going to
      research whether the canal can be abandoned or not. If it can, the developer can fence right
      to the property line.

      If the canal cannot be abandoned, the developer would need to coordinate with the Canal
      Company as to their preference. They could still fence at property line and pipe the canal,
      landscaping the area inside of the masonry wall, or install the required masonry wall to the
      easement line and leave the property as has been done on both the east and west sides of
      this property – vacant with weeds to be maintained by the canal company.

      The Canal Company will review the canal operation and decide as to what can and cannot
      be done at this location. The applicant will need to coordinate the results with the Canal
      company and respond accordingly.

Entry Feature:
      The development agreement specifies that an entry feature will be installed. The developer
      will provide staff more information as the project moves forward. The frontage for this
      development is only 125 feet in width. However, the east side of the development will
      have sufficient area for the entry feature. Staff will coordinate the design and location of
      the feature as further reviews take place.

Lighting:

      The project site will require internal streetlights. In addition to the streetlights, there will
      need to be lighting within and around the open space areas. Staff will recommend that a
      lighting plan be submitted at final plat. In previous planned unit applications, developer’s
      have simply used the City’s standard acorn light. They are however, built to a more
      residential scale.

Amenities:

      The City’s multi-family residential design standards require projects with 50 to 75 units to
      include at least two amenities from a list included in the ordinance. The amenity package
      as submitted will meet or exceed this number. During the rezone, the applicant agreed to
      install courtyards, rooftop patios for all units, a tot lot/playground, a walking path and BBQ
      and picnic areas. The walking path will allow residents to walk around the entire project
      with a connection at 3500 South. As the project moves forward, staff will coordinate the
      amenity package with the applicant.

Soils Report:

      The applicant is not planning basements for these units. However, whether baements are
      planned or not, a soils report will need to be submitted. The developer will need to
      coordinate findings of the soils report with the Building Division and Engineering Division
      regarding the construction of housing and street improvements.

Garbage Collection:

      The proposed project will need to coordinate with a private garbage collection company to
      serve this project. Dumpsters are not required here as each resident will be allowed to have
      their own garbage can. In previous Russ Platt designs, the garages have been large enough
      to store the can inside. Staff will work with the applicant to try and accomplish the same
      thing here.

BACKGROUND

Tyler Bodrero, representing the property owner, is requesting preliminary subdivision approval
for the Wallace Stegner Academy Subdivision. The subject property is bordered on the north and
west by agriculturally zoned property, the east by Rocky Mountain Power and the Mountain View
Corridor and Parkway Boulevard to the south.


ISSUES:

The proposed subdivision has been submitted to divide an existing 42 acre site into one 6 acre lot
and a remainder parcel of approximately 36 acres. The 6 acre lot or lot 1 as noted on the plat, will
be used for a new Charter School. The remainder parcel will be developed at a later date.
As a requirement of the subdivision, the property owner will be dedicating right-of-way along
Parkway Boulevard. Improvements will be installed along this right-of-way consisting of curb,
gutter, sidewalk and asphalt. The right-of-way improvements along this section of Parkway
Boulevard will be constructed by the City as a City project.

To provide access to the school itself, a new public road will be installed along the west boundary
of the subdivision running north from Parkway Boulevard. This road will be a 54-foot right-of-
way and will also be constructed with curb, gutter, and sidewalk. This road will be built by the
applicant. The new road will stub to the north providing a connection to the remainder parcel for
when it develops. Access to the school will be via the new road with two points of ingress/egress.
Staff has included a preliminary site plan for the School and will include that in the power point
presentation.

The preliminary plat identifies two parcels along the west boundary of the plat. The alignment of
the new road is taking into consideration improvements from the adjacent land owner that encroach
the proposed subdivision. Anticipating a resolution with the property owner to the west, the road
has been designed to accommodate those improvements along with a new drive access to the single
family dwelling. These issues are being negotiated at the present time. Staff will provide an
update during the regular meeting. If a resolution to these issues cannot be worked out, it is
expected that the School would maintain the parcels as part of their overall project maintenance.

A permitted use application will be reviewed for the school itself. Site considerations regarding
parking, landscaping, storm water etc. will be handled with that application. Therefore, staff and
agency comments will be better addressed through that process which is already on-going. The
subdivision will create the new lot and will dedicate Parkway Boulevard and the new right-of-way
for future access to the remainder parcel.
