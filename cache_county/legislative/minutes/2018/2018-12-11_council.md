INITIAL PROPOSAL FOR CONSIDERATION OF ACTION

¢ Ordinance No. 2018-15 ~ Amending Sections 10.20.040(A) and 10.20.040(D)

relating to the Vehicle Emissions and Maintenance Program — Josh Greer, Bear
River Health Department, reminded the Council this amendment is because Cache

County will discontinue tailpipe testing for emissions. OBD testing only will be
conducted. The number of vehicles requiring tailpipe testing is incredibly low — 3% of
vehicles — and the change will not impact Cache County’s air quality. The
Environmental Protection Agency will no longer give credit for tailpipe testing. Attorney
Swink added that the tailpipe testing equipment is very expensive and is not capturing
very many vehicles. The offending vehicles will soon be gone from the roads.

According to the monitoring data the EPA has determined Cache County has met the
standard for air quality. Council member Zilles feels the program has been a waste of
taxpayers’ money. He recalled that when the EPA required the emissions program, it
was projected that if nothing was done, Cache County would be in compliance by 2019
because of older vehicles being gone and asked Greer his opinion. Greer said he
doesn’t know whether that would have been the case.

(Attachment 2)

ACTION: Motion by Council member Merrill to waive the rules and approve Ordinance
No. 2018-15 - Amending Sections 10.20.040(A) and 10.20.040(D) relating to the Vehicle
Emissions and Maintenance Program. White seconded the motion. The vote was
unanimous, 7-0.

e Resolution No. 2018-31 ~ Adopting Cache County’s Request to the State of Utah
for the Utah Roadless Rule Petition to the U.S. Forest Service — Director Runhaar

explained it was decided to use the State’s terminology so he converted the federal
terminology to the state’s; therefore, the seven areas discussed at the December 4,
2018 Council meeting go into Management Area 2 -- Forest Restoration Area and all
the other areas stay in Management Area 1 — Primitive Area.

Council member Worthen checked into how Idaho addressed the issue and found that
the Forest Service contracts with those coming in to harvest timber, etc. The contract
addresses restoration after the temporary road is no longer needed, so there is no
need for the enforcement language she asked for at the December 4, 2019 Council
meeting. Runhaar agreed.

(Attachment 5)
ACTION: Motion by Council member White to waive the rules and approve Resolution
No. 2018-31 — Adopting Cache County’s Request to the State of Utah for the Utah

Roadless Rule Petition to the U.S. Forest Service. Tidwell seconded the motion. The
vote was unanimous, 7-0.

Cache County Council
12-11-2018

Jon White thanked Greg Merrill for his help with the budget process. White believes it has
been an improvement.

Gina Worthen reported the committee charged with deciding on what pictures to put in the
Event Center is recommending a contest be held during the 2019 Fair for photographs of the
valley with the winners being hung in the South Function Room. The North Function Room
would have a map and historic pictures and old fair ads. They would like to find a local artist to
create a painting of the valley to put above the main entry as you come into the building.
Council member Merrill suggested a mural rather than a separate painting. Ward remarked
Cache Valley Bank has a beautiful painting of the valley by Jeff Wallis. Merrill also mentioned
the possibility of an audio-visual display for the Event Center that can be operated by patrons.

Worthen thanked Merrill for his service.

Gordon Zilles said he is excited over what the Council has been able to accomplish, in
particular, the Water District which will be beneficial to the future of water in the county for years
to come. He also thanked Merrill for his service as well as all the Council members and county
employees.

Karl Ward thanked Merrill and said Merrill has been a mentor to him and he appreciated
Merrill’s analytical capabilities. He also told the Council it is a pleasure to work with them and
can’t conceive of a finer group of people to be associated with.

Dave Erickson said he considers Merrill and his wife as good, good friends, cherishes his
friendship and noted Merrill is a friend to Cache County. Merrill has always looked at the county
as a whole and has never been a one-item person. Merrill's roots go deep throughout the valley.
He, too, appreciated working with Merrill and all the Council members.

Greg Merrill thanked Erickson for serving as Chair commenting it is a little different role than
Council member. Erickson has a love for the valley. Merrill thanked Council members,
Executive Buttars, Janeen Allen, clerks, etc. The past five years have been an eye-opener to
him. He has been on many boards where differing opinions create lasting problems. This has
not been the case with his time on the Council. All can express their differing opinions, still
respect one another and remain friends.

RESOLUTION 2018 - 30

A RESOLUTION ADOPTING A COUNTY BUDGET FOR THE CALENDAR YEAR 2019

The County Council of Cache County, Utah, in a regular meeting, lawful notice of which has been
given, finds that a public hearing was held on November 27, 2018, upon lawful notice and that it
is necessary and statutorily required that a budget be adopted for Cache County for the Calendar
Year 2019.

THEREFORE, the Cache County Council hereby adopts the following resolution:

BE IT RESOLVED that the 2019 Cache County budget in the total amount of sixty million, nine
hundred forty-nine thousand, seven hundred dollars ($60,949,700), the original of which is on
file in the Finance Department of the Office of the Cache County Executive and a copy of which
is attached to this resolution, is hereby adopted as and for the Cache County budget for the
Calendar Year 2019 beginning on January 1, 2019 and ending on December 31, 2019 with a
property tax increase of 3.31% above last year’s property tax budgeted revenue excluding new
growth.

This resolution was duly adopted by the Cache County Council on the 11‘ day of December, 2018.

FEDERAL ROADLESS RULE DESIGNATION: Nov. 27™, 2018

The State of Utah is developing a petition regarding the Federal Roadless Rule (FRR) and has asked that all
Utah counties review this policy. Enacted in 2001, this policy restricted the development of additional
toads through 49% of the US Forest Service (USFS) areas within Utah. Specifically, the County has been
asked to:

1) Review the current inventoried roadless areas within the County

2) Coordinate public meetings with PLCPO to receive feedback

3) Assist PLPCO with designing management regimes to better manage forests, protect property,

and ensure healthy and sustainable watersheds and wildlife habitat.

The Federal Roadless Rule specifically categories USFS lands into Inventoried Roadless Areas (RAs) with
three classifications:
1B: IRAs where road construction and reconstruction is prohibited
1B-1: IRAs that are recommended for wilderness designation in the forest plan and where road
construction and reconstruction is prohibited,
1C: IRAs where road construction and reconstruction is not prohibited.

While Federal map data is currently incorrect, a verbal confirmation has affirmed that all of the USFS areas
in Cache County affected by the FRR are class 1B. The mapping provided on www.cachecounty.org/gis has
been updated from the Federal maps to reflect this verbal confirmation, Note: Wilderness areas are not
designated as roadless in Cache County, but fall under different regulations. They are not being included by
the State in this petition.

The State has asked that the County provide comment on the FRR classifications by indicating a preferred
management type as follows:
1) Primitive Areas ~ little to no evidence of human use
2) Forest Restoration Areas — managed areas that still retain their roadless character
3) Active Management Areas — Display increased levels of human use (roads, facilities, vegetation
manipulation)
4) Areas Recommended for Re-inventory or Boundary Adjustments — areas that should be changed
from a roadless status.

Staff has completed an analysis of the issues, communicated repeatedly with the USES, and understands
that the primary result of changing the FRR classification will provide the USFS with additional tools to
help with forest management, specifically the building of temporary roads. In looking at the available data,
staff is recommending that a few areas within the County have their FRR classification changed from 1B to
1C, allowing for additional temporary road building to assist with vegetation, watershed, and wildlife
management. These areas are locations that have slopes that will accommodate road building, tree stands
that are sizeable enough to manage, and already have existing built roads through the area including IRAs;
