2.  Presentation of Alta Canyon Sports Center History and Progress - Dan Medina, Parks and 
Recreation Director
                       Dan Medina provided a history of the Alta Canyon Recreation Center. In 1981 the Alta 
                       Canyon Recreation Service District was formed and bonded to raise funding to build Alta 
                       Canyon Recreation Center and four parks in the service district area. In 2002, Sandy City 
                       took over the maintenance and operations of the Alta Canyon Service District. The only 
                       tax increase to the Special Service District occurred in 2007. In 2008, Sandy City 
                       planned an expansion and update of the recreation center. Due to the recession, that 
                       planned expansion and remodel was put on hold. In 2021 AECom Consulting was hired to 
                       conduct a comprehensive feasibility study and recommended options for the future of 
                       ACRC. Based on that study, the Council chose to move forward with Option D of the 
                       feasibility study - the rebuild of Alta Canyon Recreation Center. If the City moves forward 
                       with rebuilding ACRC, the Council voted to cap the costs of the rebuild at $40,000,000.

3.  Overview of City decisions and discussions - Council Member Zach Robinson

                       Council Member Zach Robinson reviewed the discussions and decisions considered and 
                       acted upon by the Sandy City Council over the years.

                       In 2021, the Council hired AEComm Consultant to conduct a comprehensive feasibility 
                       study and recommend options for the future of the ACRC. Based on that study, the 
                       Council voted in 2023 to rebuild the ACRC and chose Option D of the feasibility study. 
                       The Council also voted to cap the building costs at $40,000,000. The Council currently 
                       acts as the governing body of the ACRC and has direct oversight over the recreation 
                       center. The Council formed a citizen advisory committee which includes residents from 
                       across the City. Recently, Council Members Sharkey, Robinson, and Earl proposed to 
                       the Council to incorporate private corporate sponsors to help fund the ACRC. Naming 
                       rights and corporate sponsorships are emerging tools used by many municipalities to 
                       fund capital projects. Council continues to gather information and feedback on the ACRC.



4.  Discussion on the future of Alta Canyon Sports Center - Council Members Zach Robinson and 
Scott Earl, and Mayor Monica Zoltanski
                       Council Member Scott Earl is the former Director of Sandy Parks and Recreation and is 
                       hugely supportive of the ACRC. He spoke of the community benefit of bringing a new 
                       center to Sandy City. Additionally, the entire Parks Administrative staff will office at the 
                       new center and he spoke about the need to provide new office space for the P&R staff. 

                       Mayor Zoltanski invited Shane Pace, CAO, to discuss the financial impact to the 
                       residents and reviewed the tax increase for residents in the special service district versus 
                       the impact city-wide. The goal of the new recreation center is to offer amenities that 
                       attracts residents city-wide. Mayor Zoltanski spoke about the importance of city-wide 
                       support of the new recreation center and also the need to invest in public safety 
                       infrastructure.

                       Council Member Zach Robinson spoke out the complexities of funding the center. The 
                       Council is supportive of the ACRC. Council hears the resident feedback from both 
                       supporters and non-supporters of the new center and appreciates the perspectives 
                       offered. He assured residents that there was no plan to build apartments at the site. He 
                       encouraged residents to reach out to the Council Members. This is an important decision 
                       and the Council wants to hear from you.

                       Council moved into Questions and Answers item on the Agenda.

5.  Question and Answer Period

                      Council Member Zach Robinson invited the public to ask questions and provide feedback. 
                      Council and Administration responded to residents' questions.

                      Mr. Mike Finnerty asked about whether a Plan B was in place if the City does not move 
                      forward with building a new recreation center. He stressed the importance of maintaining 
                      the open space in the area. He also asked about the City's ability to secure $20,000,000 
                      in private funding. 

                      Ms. Janet Johnson and her family has used the center for a long time and inquired about 
                      offering a flat membership fee that is the same for all residents. She also spoke about 
                      center amenities. Council Member Mecham commented that the Alta Canyon Recreation 
                      Center currently receives a subsidy from the City's general fund budget, so in effect, all 
                      Sandy residents are contributing to the funding of the recreation center through the city 
                      general fund budget.

                      Mr. Bruce Blanchard asked the Council to consider the funding and the potential subsidy 
                      that may be needed to fund the recreation center.

                      Resident spoke about the building costs and the financial impact to just the residents of 
                      the special services district versus residents city-wide. She also spoke about the need to 
                      build office space for the Parks & Recreation staff which offer services to all residents 
                      city-wide. $10 million is needed to build office space for the P&R administrative staff.

                      Resident asked about the impact of continued funding of the center from the special 
                      service district taxes.

                      Resident expressed concerns about parking and asked if a parking study had been 
                      included in the feasibility study. He spoke about the difficulties getting out of his 
                      neighborhood due to parked cars and traffic.

                      Resident suggested moving the center to a new location. He also asked for a breakdown 
                      of revenue from pool admissions and center memberships. He is supportive of a review of 
                      new locations for the center.

                      Resident asked about proposed amenities for the new center.

                      Resident does not use the center and is not supportive of a building a new recreation 
                      center and is not supportive of increased taxes. 

                      Ms. Pat Jones thanked the Council and Administration for holding the Town Hall and 
                      acknowledged the great turn out. She is not supportive of building a new recreation center 
                      and would like for a survey to go out city-wide to gather resident feedback. She 
                      expressed support for building a new fire station. She spoke about the rising costs due to 
                      inflation and the impact of the cost to build a new center. She expressed support for 
                      holding more town hall events.

                      Resident spoke in support of building a new recreation center. With a new center, more 
                      residents will join.

                      Mr. Tom Christenson expressed concerns about higher taxes and is not supportive of 
                      mixing private-public funds to build a new center. He is supportive of looking at new 
                      locations and mentioned that parking is a problem. More work needs to be done.
