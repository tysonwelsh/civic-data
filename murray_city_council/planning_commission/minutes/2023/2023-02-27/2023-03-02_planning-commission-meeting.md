MURRAY CITY HALL SUBDIVISION - 10 East 4800 South- Project #23-028:

Mr. Smallwood presented the application for Murray City to request preliminary and final
subdivision approval to consolidate multiple (thirty-one) parcels into 3 lots and a road dedication
for the new City Hall site. The subject properties are located between 4800 South and 4th
Avenue and between the old Poplar Street on the east to Box Elder on the West. The proposed
subdivision is part of the on-going efforts to modernize and revitalize Murray’s downtown area.
The intent is to create three (3) new lots. Lot 1 will be the location of City Hall and the new
Police Headquarters. Lots 2 & 3 are included as parking areas to serve the City Hall and
neighboring properties as public parking. The MCCD Zone does not have a minimum square
footage for lots.

On November 7th, 2019 the Planning Commission approved the Certificate of Appropriateness
for the proposed City Hall. This included reviewing the proposed improvements to the larger
area. At the time of this report most of the public improvements have been installed at the three
proposed lots and along Hanauer Street. Based on the analysis and review of the proposed
preliminary and final subdivision and a survey of the subject property, staff concludes the
following:
1. The subdivision of land is allowed by Utah State Code Section 10-9a-608, and with
     conditions this preliminary and final subdivision plat meets the requirements therein.
2. The proposed subdivision complies with the MCCD Zoning regulations.
3. The proposed subdivision complies with the regulations of Title 16, Subdivision Ordinance
     and with the applicable standards of Chapter 17.170, of the Murray Land Use Ordinance.

Based on the information presented in this report, application materials submitted and a site
review, staff recommends that the Planning Commission grant preliminary and final subdivision
approval for the Murray City Hall Subdivision on the properties located at 15,17,21 W Fifth Ave;
47 E Fifth Ave; 40,48 E 4800 S; 40 E Murray Holladay Rd; 6,12,16,20 E 4800 S; 3 W 4800 S;
4872 S Poplar; 33,35 E Fifth Ave; and 18,24 W Fifth Ave, subject to conditions.

A total of 104 notices were mailed to property owners within a 500’ radius of the subject
properties. As of the writing of this staff report, there has been one comment from Mr. DeLynn
Barney expressing concern with existing property lines. The proposed project does not involve
the property near Mr. Barney. This project has been designed and stamped by a civil engineer.

Mr. Hacker asked a question regarding the parcels that cross between Poplar and Hanauer
Street. He wanted to know if they get added to the Poplar Street piece. Mr. Smallwood
indicated they will be cut and become two new modified parcels. They will exist as they, minus

THE RIDGES AT MURRAY - 5322 South Allendale Drive & 662 West 5300 South – Project #23-
027 and #23-026:

Ms. Nixon presented the applications for conditional use permit and preliminary subdivision
review together, as much of the information is identical for both applications. The applicant, Mr.
Brad Reynolds, is requesting approval to develop the subject property into sixty-six (66)
townhomes. The application before the Planning Commission is for a Conditional Use Permit for
Planned Unit Development and Preliminary Subdivision approval. If the Planning Commission
approves the preliminary subdivision, the applicant will work with the city staff to prepare a Final
Plat to be reviewed and approved by the Planning Commission. After Final Subdivision approval
is granted, the applicant will prepare a Final Subdivision plat. When the Final Subdivision plat
has been approved and accepted by the City Engineer, it will be circulated for signatures of
approval which will allow the recordation of the plat at the Salt Lake County Recorder’s Office.

The proposal is a planned unit development townhome subdivision with a total of sixty-six (66)
new units along Allendale Drive and 5300 South Street. The applicant is requesting a Conditional
Use Permit to allow for a Planned Unit Development and for Preliminary Subdivision approval.
The applicants are requesting the Planned Unit Development approval to allow a variation of
setbacks.

The purpose of Planned Unit Developments is to allow the clustering of dwellings in such a way
that allows for the consolidation of open space, to provide a meaningful and usable open space
and to make more efficient use of utilities. It is not intended to be universally applied, only when
circumstances make it appropriate and of special benefit to the residents of the development and
surrounding area. The proposed development has been carefully considered with the
neighborhood to the east the adjacent rehab centers to the north and west and impacts from
5300 South Street. Through many iterations, a site plan has been submitted that separates the
potential development from the surrounding uses while maintaining the allowed density. Placing
the homes oriented towards the single-family residential area to the east provides a low-density
residential feel. The project also serves as a “buffer” between the single-family residential
neighborhood to the east from the businesses to the west on Murray Boulevard.

A Traffic Impact Study (TIS) was conducted in January of 2022 in conjunction with the zone
change. The TIS determined that the intersections of 700 West & Murray Boulevard / 5300
South and Allendale Drive / 5300 South are currently operating at poor levels of service during
the evening peak hour. These results serve as a baseline condition for the impact analysis of the
proposed development during existing (2022) conditions. The intersection of Allendale Drive /
5300 South is too close to existing signals to be signalized. The southbound queuing at this
intersection may hinder movements at the south access at times during the peak hour but the
intersection’s LOS is still acceptable. The total trip generation for the development is 468 daily
trips; Morning Peak Hour Trips, 30; and Evening Peak Hour Trips, 38. The City Engineer is not
in favor of having speed bumps or speed tables, bulb-outs or pedestrian islands. The City
Engineer is planning to install an electronic feedback speed sign on Allendale Drive. No other
mitigation measures are recommended.

The 2017 General Plan includes five key initiatives intended to guide implementation. The third
of these initiatives, Livable + Vibrant Neighborhoods, states support for creating neighborhood
nodes designed for people and scaled to complement the surrounding area, providing life-cycle
housing to allow residents to age in place, and increasing access to parks and open space. One
of the goals to accomplish this is to encourage a variety of housing options. Providing a for-sale
townhome product helps to create a more vibrant neighborhood. This location is also preferable
as it provides opportunities to access local retail such as the Smiths to the south.

Based on the analysis of the Conditional Use Permit for a Planned Unit Development and
Preliminary Subdivision review, staff finds the following:

1. With conditions, the proposed subdivision complies with the standards of the Murray City
   Subdivision Ordinance.
2. The proposed lots comply with the development standards for properties found in Chapters
   17.120 and 17.60 of the Murray City Land Use Ordinance.
3. The proposed subdivision is in harmony with the purposes of the R-M-15 Zone, representing
   appropriate residential development.

Mr. Pehrson confirmed that the residents have enough room to park outside the garage. Ms.
Nixon that each unit has a two-car garage and residents have a 20-foot driveway, with the
exception of units #153-164, from the garage door to the sidewalk, to accommodate standard
sized cars. There are 17 onsite visitor stalls provided for units #153-164, including one ADA stall

Ms. Patterson indicated that the school district sent a letter to the planning commission stating

complex. She was pleased to receive such a letter.

Ms. Milkavich expressed concerns about traffic. She points out that in the TIS, the engineer
states that the new complex will contribute to the current traffic problems. She stated that she’d
love for Murray to address the existing traffic issues in the area now that it has been identified as
a problem. Ms. Nixon stated that it is on the city’s master plan to improve the connection at 700
West 5300 South and College Drive. Allendale cannot be modified; they are not allowed to have
an additional signal (that is stipulated by UDOT). The timeframe is slated to be improved over
the next seven to ten years.

Ms. Patterson asked about the traffic study. She felt that the impact study didn’t indicate any
specific ways to improve it. Trae Stokes, city engineer, stated that they wouldn’t employ speed
bumps, as that won’t work on this street. He suggested an electronic feedback sign on 5300
South. Murray City doesn’t have any control over 5300 South – that is UDOT.

Ryan Hales, the traffic engineer who conducted the impact study, spoke about the situation.
He talked about how difficult it can be to make a left-hand turn out of a driveway on a busy street,
such as 5300 South. He stated what most drivers do is route divert, which is when a driver will
make a right-hand turn and flip around to turn at a signal, during peak times. This proposed
project would not make the criteria with UDOT to install a new signal. An electronic driver
feedback speed sign is about the only option to encourage drivers to slow down, and they are
effective, based on research. Side friction (i.e., parked cars and garbage cans) will help to slow
traffic as well. At some point, UDOT could add a raised medium to keep drivers from making a
left turn to prevent accidents.
