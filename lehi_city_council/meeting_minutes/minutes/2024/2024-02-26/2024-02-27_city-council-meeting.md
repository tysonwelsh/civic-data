29   2.1 Olympus Insurance Training
30   Brian Child, with Olympus Insurance, presented to the Council. He emphasized the importance
31   of a risk-aware culture in identifying and mitigating potential risks throughout the city. He noted
32   that the city faces various risks, including cyber threats, accidents on trails, and legal liability,
33   and has implemented measures to address these risks and maintain a culture of risk awareness.
34
35   2.2 Discussion of the 2100 N / Holy Cross Hospital Station Area Plan
36   Jordan Swain and Jonathan Young, Consultants with Arcadis, presented an update on the Station
37   Area Plan. Discussions centered on sustainability and transportation options for new light rail
38   stations in Utah. Mr. Swain outlined the project process, emphasizing community engagement
39   and iterative discovery. He noted that through community engagement, they found that the
40   public prioritized parks, plazas, and affordable housing near transit stations. Design elements and
41   placemaking were suggested to address the area's lack of identity. A refined concept was
42   presented, focusing on grid connectivity and pedestrian-friendly design. Mixed-use development,
43   including creative space and essential retail, was envisioned. Mayor Johnson expressed
44   frustration with UTA's transit planning. Suggestions also included a phased development
45   process. Councilor Stallings raised concerns about property acquisition and existing traffic
46   issues.

 96   2.2 Utah Southern Railroad Depot Museum Presentation- Darlene Stice van Workhoven, Don
 97   West, and Karl Farnsworth.
 98   The presenters addressed the Council and shared their family history and ties to the Railroad Depot.
 99   They thanked the efforts of Lehi City officials and preservationists for the depot being
100   meticulously restored and relocated to its current location. The Lehi Depot Museum, housed within
101   the depot, continues to serve as a repository of the city's heritage.
102
103   The presenters stated that efforts to revitalize the depot and museum are ongoing, with plans for
104   establishing a volunteer-led museum and promoting educational field trips and group tours. They
105   emphasized the importance of community involvement and collaboration in preserving the depot's
106   cultural heritage.
107
108   The presenters also underscored the depot's benefits, including its role in educational field trips
109   and its adjacency to a scenic bike trail. Suggestions were made for investing in signage and historic
110   preservation to showcase Utah's history and enhance surrounding development. They proposed the
111   creation of a nonprofit organization, the Utah Southern Railroad Foundation, to spearhead
112   preservation efforts and attract volunteers.
113
114   Representatives presented how they would like to reopen the Railroad Depot and move museum
115   artifacts back to the Depot.
116
117   John Rockwell reflected on years of volunteerism and his personal connection to historical
118   preservation.
