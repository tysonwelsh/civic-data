came forward to read a letter related to an award present to Utah County. Mr. Fotheringham joked that the county has

economic development consulting firm. Mr. Fotheringham read a letter from Fourth Economy Chief Executive Officer
Rich Overmoyer congratulating Utah County on being the third leading mega-county (500,000 or more residents) in the
entire United States (a copy of which can be found attached to these minutes). He explained how this recognition serves
as a high-level benchmark for community stakeholders to gauge their capacity to attract and retain sustainable investment.
Mr. Fotheringham presented the certificate to the Board, and Commissioner Ellertson commented on the team effort by
all to make Utah County such a wonderful place.

           PUBLIC HEARING FOR THE PROPOSED KENYON FARLEY AREA 1
           AGRICULTURE PROTECTION AREA APPLICATION, SEC 30, T9S, R2E, SLB&M,
           72.01 ACRES; MODIFICATIONS TO ADD TO THE PROPOSED AGRICULTURE
           PROTECTION AREA: BNH, 27.48 ACRES; KULTANI BRIARPATCH PLATINUM
           LIMITED PARTNERSHIP, 5.26 ACRES; AND HELINA CARTER-THOMAS, 9.028
           ACRES; MODIFICATION TO EXCLUDE: PAYSON CITY MUNICIPAL
           CORPORATION AND PAYSON CITY POWER AND LIGHT, PROPOSED 40 FOOT
           WIDE RIGHT-OF-WAY EASEMENT APPROXIMATELY 40 FT. NORTH OF 12400
           SOUTH ROAD (Public Hearing set March 10, 2015)

Peggy Kelsey of the Utah County Community Development Office explained that two public hearings would be held
today to discuss proposed agriculture protection areas submitted by Kenyon Farley. She presented a slide-show detailing
the location of the Area 1 application in relation to Santaquin City, Payson City, and other already established agriculture
protection areas. When Ms. Kelsey noted that state code does not require a buffer zone between the areas, Commissioner
Lee pointed out that poorly managed fruit trees can adversely affect other crops. Bryce Armstrong of the Utah County
Community Development Office clarified that the Land Use Ordinance has built-in requirements for raw ground
development in order to protect agriculture from urban infringement. He confirmed for Commissioner Ellertson that the
proposed area is surrounded by RA-5 Zone.

Peggy Kelsey went on to note a request from Payson City Corporation to exclude a 40-foot section along 12400 South
from the proposed agriculture protection area for the future installment of a light and electric power line. The Utah
County Public Works Department has also requested exclusions for a 66-foot right-of-way for a designated collector road
along 12000 South, and a 96-foot right-of-way for an arterial road along 12400 South. Both roads currently have twenty-
four feet of asphalt. Ms. Kelsey mentioned that a variance would be needed for the northern area if the excluded sections
are granted, since the remaining agriculture protection area would be less than the required 40-acre minimum. She and
Commissioner Ellertson discussed the definition of contiguous areas and deeded right-of-ways.

Peggy Kelsey noted the Utah County Planning Commission majority vote to approve the proposed agriculture protection
area with the exclusion of the requested right-of-ways by Payson City and Utah County. The Utah County Agriculture
Protection Advisory Board voted unanimously to approve the application, but not to approve the requested government
exclusions. Commissioner Lee questioned why the Planning Commission would prefer the carved out areas while the
Agriculture Board would vote to keep the area whole, despite the requested exclusions. Ms. Kelsey responded that the
Agriculture Board found it necessary to not carve out the sections in order to protect the land, and also to not create a
hardship for the applicant.

The applicant, Kenyon Farley, came forward. Mr. Farley summarized the Agriculture Protection Advisory Board had
voted to approve the application as submitted, and stated his preference for that approval rather than the recommendation
of the Planning Commission (to approve the agricultural protection area but exclude the requested right-of-ways from
the protected area). Mr. Farley reviewed qualifications for Agricultural Protection: currently being used for agricultural
production, zoned for agriculture use, is viable for agricultural use, and is available to agricultural improvement. Mr.
Farley encouraged the Board to follow statute in considering whether his application is eligible for an agricultural
protection area. He detailed the roads in the neighborhood, acknowledging government concern to plan for the future
but also clarifying that those are hypothetical situations.

Kenyon Farley went on to note his applications for agriculture protection were submitted to the county before Payson
City held any meetings regarding proposed power lines in the area. He has also been working with the Strawberry
Electric Service District. Future roads can be created once it becomes necessary, but Mr. Farley currently owns the land
and pays taxes upon it; he should be permitted to farm it. Mr. Farley commented on neighborhood group efforts for fruit
tree pest management.

Commissioner Ellertson clarified that should the Board grant the requested rights-of-way, Kenyon Farley would still own
the land and could use it as desired. Mr. Farley partially agreed, stating it would be his until someone changed their
mind. Commissioner Lee asked whether approving the easements would alter Mr. Farley’s plans for the land, and Mr.
Farley stated yes; it would be too much of an investment to farm land which may eventually be condemned.

Sterling Brown, Vice President of Public Policy for the Utah Farm Bureau, approached the bench. He explained
agriculture protection laws were implemented by state legislature approximately twenty years ago to minimize or
eradicate nuisance complaints against farms. While there are 16,000 farms in Utah, farmers and ranchers represent less
than one percent of Utah County yet the county leads the state in agriculture production. Mr. Brown talked about how
agriculture spurs on a viable sustainable industry and the balance between development and agriculture. He reminded
the Board that protection areas are a mighty tool for a community that will be healthy for everyone.

Helina Carter-Thomas owns some of the property proposed to be included within the agricultural protection area. She
commented on the county subdivision between her nine acres of land and the five acres belonging to her parents,
clarifying that both properties meet the minimum acreage requirements for protection in Santaquin City. Ms. Carter-
Thomas expressed her belief in private property rights, reiterating that Kenyon Farley meets statutory requirements for
agricultural protection; another entity should not have the right to impose on his property. She pointed out confusion
about the proposed Payson City road to be created on 12000 South versus 12400 South.

Helina Carter-Thomas addressed the detrimental impact the requested exemptions would have on irrigation for Kenyon
Farley’s property, specifically mentioning the interrupted use of land and the waste to satisfy a “maybe” situation.
Commissioner Graves noted power lines already near the property, and Helina Carter-Thomas explained the difference
between the current layout and the proposed changes to the land. Commissioner Graves pointed out a government entity
could attempt to condemn the land whether or not an easement is in place, and Ms. Carter-Thomas countered that it
would be more difficult for the city to do so if it is included within an agriculture protection area. She encouraged the
Board to give Mr. Farley the advantage.

Calvin Crandall of the Utah Farm Bureau is a farmer and rancher in Springville. He requested the agriculture protection
area be approved without the requested exemptions, stating the proposed road and power line are not yet definite. Mr.
Crandall asked the Board not to tie the hands of Kenyon Farley, stating development can occur when needed later.

Bill Ferguson is a local fruit grower with experience serving on the Utah County Planning Commission. He recalled the
time before agriculture protection areas existed, and spoke of approved areas between Santaquin City and West Mountain
- there had been no discussion of easements and rights-of-way. Mr. Ferguson explained why fruit trees cannot be planted
around power poles. Since the protection area is designed to minimize nuisance complaints, it does not make sense to
remove certain sections of the submitted application; it would be too difficult to determine whether noise is coming from
within or just outside of the protected region. Mr. Ferguson suggested the application be returned to the Planning
Commission for further review, speaking of other proposed roads, overpasses, and freeway off-ramps. When Mr.
Ferguson commented on the importance of sour cherries and pipelines, Commissioner Ellertson clarified that he is
friendly towards agriculture. Mr. Ferguson stated he does not want to see urban sprawl abuse agricultural rights.

Peggy Kelsey reminded the commissioners that it would not be possible to return this application to the Utah County
Planning Commission, as there is an April 17, 2015 deadline for a decision. She also noted the Board is not obligated
to follow the Planning Commission’s recommendation.

Robert McMullin farms property west of Kenyon Farley’s proposed agriculture protection area, and is in favor of the
zone being approved without the requested exclusions. Mr. McMullin explained his stance based on giving farmers one
more tool to negotiate with government and other entities that would have an interest in the land. Agricultural protection
areas encourage government to protect farming in the southern portion of Utah County.

Dennis Marker came forward as a representative of Santaquin City on behalf of Mayor Kirk Hunsaker. Santaquin is in
support of Kenyon Farley’s application, as they want to protect local agriculture as a viable industry. Mr. Marker noted
the protection would be for the farmers and also to stop government from interfering. He mentioned Pioneer Crossing
in northern Utah County, noting that it was successfully re-routed in part because a farmer did not agree to sell their
protected land. Mr. Marker repeated that agriculture is important, and while there are plans for future interchanges in
the next thirty to forty years, that should not preclude protection now; those issues can be addressed as needed when the
time comes.

Commissioner Ellertson asked whether the proposed agriculture protection area is within an annexation declaration, and
Peggy Kelsey stated yes, outlining the locations on the map. Dennis Marker mentioned pending annexations for both
Santaquin City and Payson City, and discussed with the commissioners potential complications. Commissioner Ellertson
acknowledged Santaquin has been recognized by the state for including agriculture in their general plans.

Mark Sorenson came forward to represent the power and light department of Payson City. He expressed concerns about
an impending 46kv transmission line, identifying the path it would take in relation to the proposed agriculture protection
area. Mr. Sorenson provided copies of the 2040 Metropolitan Transportation Plan adopted by the Mountainland
Association of Governments to each of the commissioners (a copy of which can be found attached to these minutes),
providing a timeline of when the public would have had access to that same information. He emphasized that plans have
been underway for the area since 2011, even if it does seem much further down the road. Payson has also been working
with the Strawberry Electric Service District to address power regulations. Mr. Sorenson and Commissioner Ellertson
discussed electricity distribution and impact on the roads compared to agriculture protection areas. Mr. Sorenson
expressed a desire to minimize impact, noting that the requested exemptions may be able to overlap. He encouraged the
Board to consider the importance of community planning.

Commissioner Graves asked whether the Mountainland Association of Governments plans would alter Payson City’s
plans if the impending power line was moved to 12000 South. Mark Sorenson replied consultants are actively reviewing
all options. Property owners need to be identified before negotiations begins; the city would have to consider the
additional factors.

Commissioner Lee summarized both sides of issue, asking whether Kenyon Farley would be willing to work with the
city directly. Mark Sorenson stated negotiations would be much more difficult if the requested exemptions were to be
included in the proposed agriculture protection area; rather than knocking on the door of the property owners, each step
of the process would require involvement from boards and commissions. Commissioner Lee asked about alternate routes
for the proposed power line, and Mr. Sorenson responded he does not know if there are other options - the alternatives
may other have pre-existing circumstances.

Utah County resident Eldon A. Neves came forward in favor of the agriculture protection area being approved as
submitted, without the requested exemptions, in order to strengthen the agriculture community. Peggy Kelsey confirmed
the Utah County Planning Commission vote was 4-2 in favor of approving the application with the requested exclusions.
The two ‘nay’ votes were on account of the members agreeing with the opinions expressed today: the agriculture
protection area should be continuous over the entire property, and not interrupted.

9.     APPROVE AND AUTHORIZE THE SIGNATURE OF THE COMMISSION CHAIR ON THE
       SETTLEMENT AGREEMENT RELATED TO CIVIL CASE NO. 2:11CV00652
Commissioner Ellertson questioned the settlement dollar amount being due “within 14 days after the end of any appeals,”

1.     DISCUSSION OF IDEAS ON HOW TO BRING GREATER TRANSPARENCY TO THE MEETING
       AGENDAS OF THE BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS
Commissioner Lee brought up a discussion which had taken place during an administrative meeting regarding public
access to paperwork attached to the weekly Commission agendas. Commissioner Lee listed the document types he
deemed appropriate to have available online for review - resolutions, ordinances, procurement rules and regulations,
policy changes, board appointments, interlocal agreements, grant applications, and surety bond release letters - and asked
the opinions of his fellow Board members. Commissioner Graves stated he is against the idea because once something
UTAH COUNTY BOARD OF COMMISSIONERS
MINUTES - March 24, 2015
Page 11

is on the Internet, there is no control mechanism for distribution. The Board is constantly updating agenda items such
as the minutes before they are approved during meetings, and resolutions which approve volunteers could be considered
private personnel records. Commissioner Graves also questioned county liability if confidential items are published.
All of these concerns would also effect the timeliness of posting the agenda.

Commissioner Ellertson noted the Board is considering options, and Commissioner Lee suggested following the state
legislative website model. Commissioner Lee explained that the public has access to documents even in their draft forms
which are continually amended and updated. Ordinances would be considered obvious public documents, and
Commissioner Lee does not anticipate privacy issues for board appointments. Commissioner Ellertson countered that
not all action taken by the Board of Commissioners is legislative, as in the example of the state website. He
acknowledged that the process may be improved by inviting input before the Board votes.

Commissioner Lee also mentioned that when items are moved from the Regular Agenda to Consent Agenda without
discussion during open meetings, the public may be confused. Commissioner Graves responded that the information is
available online within a few days after approval, either on the county website, by contacting the Board directly, and by
submitting GRAMA (Governmental Records Access and Management Act) requests. Commissioner Lee reiterated that
the public cannot accurately be involved if the information is not available until after the voting takes place.
Commissioner Graves encouraged that finances and ordinances should be available, but repeated his concerns regarding
other documents. Commissioner Ellertson added that these meetings occur to complete the business of the county; the
Board has allowed a public comment process, but it is by no means required to do so.
