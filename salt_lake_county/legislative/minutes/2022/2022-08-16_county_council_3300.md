Pacific Islander Heritage Month Proclamation (002)
Presenter: Chair Stringham
Council Member Stringham  stated a lot of Pacific Islanders are facing 
difficulties, including losing youth, gangs, drugs, and challenges due to being 
here illegally or struggling with paperwork.  One group, Alofa Fa ’Asamoa, 
focuses on preserving heritage and protecting its community, and it is 
sponsoring activities outside the Council Chambers after the meeting.  She 
read the following proclamation declaring August 2022, as Pacific Islander 
Heritage Month in Salt Lake County:
A PROCLAMATION OF THE SALT LAKE COUNTY COUNCIL 
DECLARING AUGUST 2022, AS PACIFIC ISLANDER HERITAGE 
MONTH IN SALT LAKE COUNTY, UTAH
WHEREAS, in August of 1889, forty -six Pacific Islanders arrived in Utah 
and built the Iosepa settlement in Tooele’s Skull Valley; and 
WHEREAS, this first group of pioneers was the vanguard of many more 
Pacific Islanders who would follow, with nearly 50,000 Pacific Islanders 
living in Utah in the present day; and 
WHEREAS, over 20,000 Pacific Islanders are residents of Salt Lake County; 
and 
WHEREAS, August was first declared as Utah ’s Pacific Islander Heritage 
Month by former Governor, Gary Herbert in 2012, and has subsequently 
been declared as Utah ’s Pacific Islander Heritage Month every year since, 
including by Governor Spencer Cox in 2022; and
WHEREAS, there are many groups of Pacific Islanders in Salt Lake County 
who strive to lift up their people and improve their lives, working in 
conjunction with the efforts of state and local governments; and
WHEREAS, Iosepa, a ghost town located seventy -five miles southwest of 
Salt Lake City in Tooele County, was once home to over two hundred 
Polynesian members of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter -Day Saints,

and now draws an annual gathering of Pacific Islanders and others from all 
over the United States on Memorial Day each year; and 
WHEREAS, Tongans make up the largest group of Pacific Islanders living in 
Utah, followed by Samoans, Native Hawaiians, Maoris from New Zealand, 
Tahitians from the Society Islands, as well as Pacific Islanders from the 
Cook Islands, Fiji, Nuie, Raro Tuman and other small islands; and
WHEREAS, the Polynesian migration to Utah accelerated after the Second 
World War, with Pacific Islanders bringing with their customs, folkways, and 
traditions to Utah, and contributing to our state ’s growing and diverse 
communities; and
WHEREAS, a large majority of Salt Lake County Pacific Islanders live on 
the west side of the county, and are involved in various community groups 
that foster self -identity and pride in Pacific Island heritage by sponsoring 
sporting and performance competitions, reunions, and anniversary 
celebrations - none of which would be proper without traditional foods and 
often imported island fare; and
WHEREAS, many of our Utah sporting teams benefit from the incredible 
sporting talent and the rich heritage in athletics fostered by this group of 
Utahns; and
WHEREAS, Pacific Islander youth in Salt Lake County face many 
challenges, and community and government leaders should consider these 
challenges and engage the community in order to find solutions for them; and
WHEREAS, Salt Lake County recognizes he achievements of Pacific 
Islanders in the arts, cuisine, government, business, sports, religion, and 
other cultural elements that enrich, strengthen, and define our county ’s 
history, culture, and politics; and
WHEREAS, the histories and accomplishments of our Pacific Islander 
residents deserve to be told and retold to recognize the incredible 
contributions given to the many communities they helped to build in Utah and 
in Salt Lake County;
Now Therefore, Be It Proclaimed:

The Salt Lake County Council does hereby declare August 2022, as Pacific 
Islander Day in Salt Lake County.  The Council further encourages all 
residents of our community to join us in honoring our Pacific Islander 
neighbors and celebrating their place in Salt Lake County.  August 2022 shall 
be a month of remembrance for the Pacific Island pioneers, their families, 
their descendants, and those who continue to come and call Utah and Salt 
Lake County home.
Dated this 16th day of August, 2022.
