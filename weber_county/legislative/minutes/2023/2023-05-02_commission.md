G. ACTION ITEMS:

    1. APPROVAL OF DECLARATION OF LOCAL EMERGENCY DUE TO IMMINENT FLOODING IN
       WEBER COUNTY.
         Chair Froerer: Our first action item this morning is a Proclamation declaring a local emergency and invoking
         emergency powers in Weber County due to imminent county-wide flooding [Proclamation was read as
         follows]:

                     PROCLAMATION DECLARING A LOCAL EMERGENCY AND INVOKING
                    EMERGENCY POWERS IN WEBER COUNTY DUE TO IMMINENT COUNTY-
                                         WIDE FLOODING

                        WHEREAS, pursuant to Utah Code Ann. §§ 53-2A-205 and 208, the Board of
                  County Commissioners of Weber County has authority to declare a local emergency for
                  Weber County; and

                          WHEREAS, the nature of this emergency is imminent and ongoing flooding
                  resulting from excessive storm water run-off that is anticipated to increase as temperatures
                  rise and the snowpack melts; and

                          WHEREAS, on March 18, 2023, Utah Governor Cox issued an executive order
                  declaring a state of emergency as a result of imminent and ongoing flooding from the
                  record-breaking snowpack that exceeds 200% in some areas; and

                         WHEREAS, as the snowpack melts, regional reservoirs located within the Weber
                  Basin are anticipated to reach capacity and the water discharged from the reservoirs in the
                  Weber Basin directly impacts the Ogden River, Weber River, and other watercourses within
                  the County both natural and man-made;

                          WHEREAS, the County Roads Department, Emergency Management, and
                  Engineering have been working to try to prevent or mitigate the risk of flooding by
                  implementing flood control measures to protect the general public, businesses, and property
                  within the County;

                          WHEREAS, despite those efforts, the banks along the rivers and watercourses
                  within the County have been or are anticipated to be breached by high-water flow;

                          WHEREAS, an undetermined amount of precipitation may yet fall, further addition
                  to the problem, and soils in many parts of the County have reached saturation and cannot
                  absorb additional storm water;

                         WHEREAS, it is anticipated that extensive damage will occur on private and public
                  property, trails, roads, bridges, utilities, and other public and private infrastructure;

                         WHEREAS, it is anticipated that the circumstances of the imminent flooding will
                  exceed the capacity of the services, personnel, equipment, supplies, and facilities of Weber
                  County; and

                         WHEREAS, these conditions meet the definition of a local emergency described in
                  the Disaster Response and Recovery Act, specifically in Utah Code § 53-2a-208(1);

                          WHEREAS, declaring a state of emergency will activate the response and recovery
                  aspects of local disaster emergency plans, facilitate the protection of persons and property
                  from the imminent flooding, and permit the County to request and receive mutual aid
                  assistance from the state, federal government, and other political subdivisions through
                  mutual aid agreements;

                  NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT DECLARED BY THE BOARD OF COUNTY
                  COMMISSIONERS OF WEBER COUNTY:

                  Section 1.    Local Emergency. A local emergency exists in Weber County, Utah, due to
                  imminent flooding.

                  Section 2.      Assistance. This local emergency exceeds the resources of the community.
                  Assistance is requested from both the State of Utah and the Federal Government to provide
                  all necessary resources to protect against and restore damage done to Weber County
                  residents and businesses.

                  Section 3.      Emergency Powers. All necessary emergency powers and operations
                  authorized under Utah Code Ann. § 53-2a-205 and 208 are hereby invoked, including the
                  utilization of available resources to address the imminent flooding. All applicable local
                  disaster and emergency plans are hereby activated.

                  Section 4.       Mutual Aid. Weber County authorizes the furnishing of aid and assistance
                  in relation to this Declaration through mutual aid agreements. Weber County also requests
                  all assistance available pursuant to the laws of the United States of America, the laws of the
                  State of Utah, and local ordinances and regulations.

                  Section 5.      Government Immunity. Pursuant to Utah Code Ann. § 63G-7-201(4)(p)
                  and (q), the County, its officers, and its employees are immune from suit for injuries that
                  may arise from the management of flood waters or natural disasters and from the
                  construction, repair, or operation of flood or storm systems.

                  Section 6.     Duration. This Declaration shall take effect immediately and be declared
                  ongoing and continuing for no more than 30 days until terminated or extended pursuant to
                  governing law.

                  Section 7.   Publication. This Declaration shall be on file for public inspection at the
                  Weber County Clerk/Auditor’s Office.

                  Section 8.    Notice to Governor. A copy of this Declaration shall be sent to the Office of
                  the Governor of the State of Utah.

         Kudos to our county Roads (department) that started out plowing roads to snow slides to 18 foot high
         avalanches then transitioning to removing debris from our streams and rivers; I am very proud of what they
         have done.. I think the timeliness of this is important.
         Chris Crockett (Attorney): Our office met this morning to go over the provisions of this document. We
         determined it was important enough to read out loud because, thankfully, these types of declarations are rare
         but they’re incredibly important and do specific things under the law. It recognizes that we are facing a serious
         public safety issue. We have plans and there are statutes preparing us for moments like this. Essentially, this
         Proclamation let’s the state of Utah and the federal government know we are facing a disaster with a financial
         cost to that. It also indicates time is of the essence, speeding up the process. I have been involved in watching
         our commission, engineering department, our roads department, our emergency management team, meet
         regularly and have in-depth discussions and they are continually following this situation because it changes
         every day. This Proclamation allows us to utilize more resources and give us more flexibility to respond even
         more quickly as we expect those increased flood waters to come. We are anticipating damage with this
         Proclamation helping us to mitigate that damage. The law gives protections and legal authority to do that. We
         anticipate that several cities will declare a local emergency as well. Proclamations are valid for 30 days and
         can be extended.

         Lisa Swartz (Director, Emergency Management Services): Started over two months ago to talk about
         preparations for this with the Engineering and Roads departments. That conversation expanded one month
         ago to include the Commission and DWR, with Weber Basin Water Conservancy District and UDOT also in
         attendance. Mitigation has been ongoing for several years with efforts the past two months focusing on checks
         of all tributaries and rivers including using Sheriff’s drones to check areas hard to access. I feel where we have
         done all of that preparation to the best of our ability. We are at that time of needing to respond. That is why
         we need the Declaration.
         Gary Myers (Director, Engineering): The Weber in beginning of April was running at 2000cfs with a peak of
         4300cfs; currently running 3900cfs with a projection of 4500cfs Wednesday night through Friday. Huge shout
         out to the agencies involved in helping; they have been tremendously helpful. Historically, in 2011 we lost a
         river bank at 3300cfs, leading to the building of the Little Weber Channel. We are in a much better spot than
         in 2011.
         Eli Johnson (Sheriff): A big shout out to Weber Basin Water, National Weather Service, State hydrologists
         (Glenn), DWR staff at Ogden Bay keeping everything west of their gates clear. We are doing a lot better than
         in 2011 thanks to all those folks. All drainages are being flown once or twice a week to keep drainages clear.
         Utaba Reservoir has begun spilling over its top, adding to the water flow. 65,000 sand bags distributed since
         December. Anticipate new records for spring flows this year leading us to unchartered territory for stream
         flows and the amount of water through the drainages.
