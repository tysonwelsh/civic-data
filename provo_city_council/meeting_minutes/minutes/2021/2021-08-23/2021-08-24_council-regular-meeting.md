 Presentations, Proclamations, and Awards

 1.        A presentation from the Mayor and City Council to Karl Snow and Glee Zumbrennen in
           appreciation for their work and service in changing Provo’s form of government to what it is
           today and for presenting a written history of those events. (21-088) (0:05:07)

Chair Sewell introduced the item and explained Mr. Snow and Mr. Zumbrennen were critical in changing
the form of government in Provo from a commission to the council-mayor form of government.

Mayor Kaufusi said Provo’s history and form of government was unique. The council-mayor form was
effective and worked well in Provo. Mr. Snow and Mr. Zumbrennen were responsible for the work that
went into making this change in 1981.

Mr. Snow spoke to the Council about his involvement in the change of government in Provo. He
explained that in 1911, legislation was passed that mandated a commission form of government in every
major city in Utah. In his studies at BYU, he learned that the commission form was meant for smaller
cities. Provo City operated under that form of government until new legislation was passed that
permitted other forms of government. He sought out Glee Zumbrennen who was a member of the
Provo Chamber and one of Mr. Snow’s neighbors to help research what option would work best for
Provo.

Mr. Zumbrennen explained to Council that a group of about 12 individuals begin meeting to learn about
the other optional forms of government that were available. They identified issues with the commission
form of government. The commission form of government left it up to the voters to select a city
manager, and the committee determined this was not always the best choice. They ultimately
determined that the council-mayor form of government, with an appointed CAO, worked best. The
committee was pleased with the outcome.

Mayor Kaufusi presented the gentleman with plaques to commemorate their service to the City.

 2.        Ordinance 2021-31 amending Titles 14 and 15 of the Provo City Code to remove gendered
           references and to make stylistic and grammatical corrections. (21-073) (0:21:30)

       Motion:            An implied motion to adopt Ordinance 2021-31 as currently constituted, has been made
                          by council rule.

Melissa McNalley, Management Analyst, presented. This text amendment was intended to correct
inconsistencies and grammatical errors in the code. She reviewed each of the items that would be
amended. References to animal control were referred to Chapter 8, where animal control was
addressed. Religious activities would be permitted in all zones, rather than as a condition use.
Amendments were also made to the definition of accessory buildings to be clearer.

Chair Sewell opened public comment.

David Horne, Provo, had two questions about the changes. First, he wondered why it was necessary to
make the language gender neutral. Second, he thought the changes to the accessory building definition
was problematic because it seemed to prohibit sheds and other buildings that do not conform to the
main building, he thought this was an overreach.

Chair Sewell asked if there had been any substantive changes regarding accessory buildings. Robert
Mills, Planning Supervisor, said there were no substantive changes. It was a common practice in code to
require the accessory buildings be similar in detail to the primary dwelling. This was already a design
standard that was encouraged.



Provo City Council Meeting Minutes - August 24, 2021                                                  Page 2 of 5
Councilor Harding considered this to be a substantive change that did not previously exist. Mr. Mills said
this was already existent in the design standards, it was a recommendation to have the accessory
building match the style of the main building. Mr. Mills thought these were not extreme changes.

In response to Mr. Horne’s comments, Councilor Harding explained that in the Spanish language, gender
typically defaulted to the masculine form when gender was unknown or included all genders. Changing
words like “he” to “the applicant” was a good change that removed potentially controversial language.

Chair Sewell asked to review the changes that were proposed. Staff displayed the changes and Brian
Jones, City Attorney, explained the background of these changes. Utah State Legislature previously
made similar changes to State Code. And several years ago, by ballot initiative, the voters approved
removing gendered language from the Utah State Constitution. As a result of these changes, city staff
proposed similar changed to Provo City Code to remove references to only one gender. This effort was
in accordance with the published guidelines of the State Legislature’s style guide. Because changes to
Titles 14 and 15 are required to be reviewed by the Planning Commission, those sections were not
amended at the same time as the rest of the Code. Those were the changes being presented to the
Council today.

Chair Sewell wanted to evaluate the changes that were proposed for accessory buildings to ensure they
changes were simply stylistic and not adding a new requirement that the buildings be similar in design.
Mr. Mills showed where the existing code already included language about the accessory buildings being
architecturally similar in design. Mr. Parker also pointed to several other places where this language
already existed in code, he estimated there were at least 15 instances of this language throughout.

There were no other comments from the public, Chair Sewell closed public comment. He called for a
vote on the implied motion.

       Vote:              The motion was approved 5:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Harding, Sewell, and
                          Shipley in favor. Councilors Hoban and Handley were excused.

 3.        Ordinance 2021-32 amending the Zone Map class. of approx. 1.07 acres of real property
           located at 50 E 3900 N from Residential (R1.10) to Low Density Residential (LDR) and Two
           Family Residential (R2) and authorizing a related Development Agreement. Riverbottoms
           Neighborhood PLRZ20190265 (0:40:40)

       Motion:            An implied motion to adopt Ordinance 2021-32 as currently constituted, has been made
                          by council rule.

Chair Sewell asked Vice Chair Shipley to chair this item since he had personal connections with those
involved.

Aaron Ardmore, Planning Supervisor, presented. He explained the history of the item, stating this dated
back to 2020 and had been through the Planning Commission several times. When the Council first
heard this item in November, they took issue with the request and saw an altered request in February.
At that time, the applicant was encouraged to work on resolving the neighborhood’s concerns.

This request was for two different zones, with a concept plan for nine units on the portion being zoned
as LDR. The applicant had proffered a development agreement to ensure no more than nine units were
built. Mr. Ardmore presented the elevation renderings to Council.

David Rosen, Riverbottoms Neighborhood Chair, spoke to Council. He explained the neighborhood
respected Mr. LaFontaine’s right to request this zone change and develop his property, but the
neighborhood was not supportive. Even after the revised plan with fewer units was presented, they still
opposed the plan. Mr. Rosen said there were already 10 multi-family structures in the neighborhood for
a total of 941 individual units.

The applicant, Dan LaFontaine, spoke to the Council. He acknowledged there were some multifamily
dwellings in their neighborhood, but not as many as there were in southeast Provo. He said people were
struggling to afford housing, and this was needed. He thought the proximity to University Avenue was a
good location for this type of housing.

Vice Chair Shipley opened public comment.

Wayne Tanner, Provo, said the council had previously demanded R1.10 for this area. He thought there
was already enough multifamily housing. He said there would be 70 neighbors impacted by this and they
were opposed. He argued this was not affordable housing, he said the units would likely start at
$500,000. He asked the Council to leave this neighborhood R1.10.

David Horne, Provo, said Mr. LaFontaine did not meet with the neighborhood as the Council had
requested. He could see Mr. LaFontaine’s attempt to satisfy some neighbors, but they still preferred
single-family housing.

Mark Steed, Provo, had been watching this development process with interest. He was exasperated by
the process. The Council had advocated for VLDR, and the corresponding proposal met the letter of the
law, but not the essence of the zone. He agreed this was not affordable housing. They loved their
neighborhood the way it was and thought this development would ruin the tenor of the neighborhood.

Kierra Christensen, Provo, read from an article written by Robert Vernon regarding affordable housing. It
described a disconnect between the generation enacting housing legislation and the people who were
buying the housing and who were impacted by the shortage. Ms. Christensen was concerned about the
housing shortage and felt millennials, like herself, would be unable to find housing in Provo. She thought
there should be more units.

There were no other comments from the public and Vice Chair Shipley closed public comment.

Chair Sewell explained that he knew and loved everyone who spoke tonight, they were all good people.
One of the things that encouraged Chair Sewell to run for council was that people were passionate
about what was happening near them. Over time, he came to understand the housing needs of the City.
When a property is near an arterial road, like this, it made sense to have more density. His preference
would have been VLDR, but he was supportive of this proposal. He was thankful that he was given the
opportunity to work with Mr. LaFontaine and Mr. Peperone to find a reasonable solution. He thought
the neighborhood would gain a lot from this proposal. The density was not situated on the east side
where the property bordered the existing neighborhood, instead, it was on the west side closer to
University Avenue. He thought this proposal created a good buffer and transition into the R1.10
neighborhood.

Councilor Ellsworth expressed sympathy to the neighborhood, she understood change could be
uncomfortable. The thriving Utah economy would continue to attract jobs and consumers and Provo
would see increased traffic and homes. She hoped to find ways to collaborate with the changes.

In response to a previous comment, Councilor Harding explained this proposal was for nine units on 1.8
acres and was less dense than what had been approved in the Maeser neighborhood in July. He was
comfortable with the proposal. Mr. Harding responded to Ms. Christensen’s comment, and said he
hoped to avoid generational contention. He wanted to avoid ideas that would pit groups against each
other. He called for people to work together and keep Provo the place that it was, even as it continued
to grow.

Councilor Fillmore was torn. He was pleased the density had decreased and the density was located
closer to University Avenue. However, he understood the concerns the neighborhood shared.

Vice Chair Shipley thanked Chair Sewell for meeting with the developer, Mr. Peperone, and Dave Rosen
to arrive at a reasonable solution. At the last meeting, many neighbors worried about the layout,
number of units, parking, and increased traffic. This was a good middle ground and if it was acceptable
to Mr. Sewell and the others, Mr. Shipley was satisfied. He was aware change to a neighborhood could
be difficult and was sympathetic to those concerns.

Vice Chair Shipley called for a vote on the implied motion.

       Vote:              The motion was approved 5:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Harding, Sewell, and
                          Shipley in favor. Councilors Handley and Hoban were excused.
