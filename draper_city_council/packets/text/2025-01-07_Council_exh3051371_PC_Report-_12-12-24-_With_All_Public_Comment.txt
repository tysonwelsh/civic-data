BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY
This application is a request for approval of a Zoning Map Amendment for approximately
1.28 acres located at approximately 2021 East Village Green Cir. (Exhibit C & D). The
property is currently zoned C-2. The applicant is requesting that a Zoning Map Amendment
be approved to the CG zone to allow for a reception center to operate in the existing office
building.

This property is part of the Suncrest development area and is tied to the 1999 Draper City
Municipal Code (1999 DCMC). The proposed zone change includes three parcels. The main
parcel was originally developed with an office building and was used as the sales center for
Suncrest. The other two parcels were originally developed as a market and parking lot and
that building is now utilized as the Royal on Ridge restaurant.

Public comments can be found in Exhibit K of this report.

According to 1999 DCMC Section 9-5-030 the purpose of the C-2 zone is to “provide areas,
in appropriate locations where convenience buying outlets may be established to serve
surrounding residential neighborhoods. The regulations of this district are designed to
promote a combination of retail and service facilities which, in character and scale, are
necessary to meet day-to-day needs of area residents.”

The Suncrest Guidelines envisioned the Village Center area of Suncrest to be developed
with retail shops, a market, community meeting place, and offices (Exhibit J). The Suncrest
Guidelines state that allowed uses in the C-2 zone under the 1999 DCMC are allowed in
addition to those uses specifically listed in the Suncrest Guidelines. A reception Center is
not an allowed use in the C-2 zone. The list of allowed uses in the 1999 DCMC can be found
as Exhibit I of this staff report. Changes to the General Plan Land Use Map are not
proposed with this application.

The property owner for the office building has been operating a reception center business
in the office building called the Suncrest Reception Center without land use, fire, building,
or business licensing approvals. The property owner has suspended business to try and
come into compliance.




Suncrest Reception Center                                            App. # 2024-0371-MA
Zoning Map Amendment Request                  3
The business and property owner has been working with the Draper City Fire Marshal and
Chief Building Official since August to determine if an assembly occupancy could be
allowed in the building and what the allowed occupant load would be for the assembly use.
The Draper City Fire Marshal and Chief Building Official have determined an allowed
occupant load of 81 persons, including staff and event attendees.

If the proposed zoned change is not approved the property owner will not be permitted to
use the property for a reception center or any other unapproved or unallowed uses.

Zoning Map Amendment. The applicant is requesting to rezone the property to the CG
zone. The applicant’s questionnaire can be found in Exhibit G of this report. The list of
allowed uses in the CG zone can be found in Exhibit H of this report. Reception Center is a
permitted use within the CG zone, as is Restaurant.

According to Draper City Municipal Code (DCMC) Section 9-8-020 the purpose of the CG
zone is to “provide areas to accommodate commercial uses which are not within shopping
centers of integrated design. This zone is intended to apply to existing "strip commercial"
developments. The zone should not be used for new commercial development unless
integrated shopping center development is not practical or desirable because of difficult
size, shape, topography, or similar problems related to land otherwise deemed to be
appropriate for commercial use. Typical uses in this zone include offices, retail stores,
personal services, heavy commercial, and institutional uses.”

If approved, the Zoning Map Amendment allow a reception center as a permitted use and
would bring the properties under the current DCMC standards. The properties would still
be required to comply with the Suncrest Guidelines as well as any private requirements
from the Suncrest HOA. The proposed change in zoning does not impact the existing
restaurant. If approved, staff will verify compliance with requirements for parking, building,
and fire prior to any issuance of a business license for a reception center.

        1. Map Amendments:
            a. Whether the proposed amendment is consistent with goals, objectives and

Planning Division Review.

       1.     The property has 41 parking stalls. Any parking calculation would have to
              account for the reception center and the office use located in the basement
              of the building. The uses would meet parking requirements under the
              Current DCMC with a standard of 18 stalls required. There are three possible
              parking calculations that the 1999 DCMC has that could be used for the uses,
              requiring either 36 stalls, 61 stalls, or 83 stalls. A thorough analysis has not
              been done to determine which standard would apply since reception center
              isn’t a listed use in the 1999 DCMC.

Fire Division Review.

       1.     The applicant has been operating in this location as a real estate office then
              converted to a reception center and has never had a business license
              inspection because they have never applied for one until recently. It is a
              change of use to go from an office to a reception center and if that is what
              they wanted to do they would have to comply with current code. The owner
              asked for an occupancy load of over 99 which could not be allowed. They
              have since done some work as asked by myself and the Building Official so
              that we could determine a reasonable safe occupancy load which is 81
              occupant’s total.

              comply with City and State codes. Suncrest is a fireworks restricted area

              more than five (5) feet above the floor.

      December 11, 2024

      Draper City Planning Commission
      c/o Jennifer Jastremsky
      Community Development Director
      By email: Jennifer.jastremsky@draperutah.gov

      Re: Suncrest Reception Center Zoning Map Amendment (Application 2024-0371-MA)

      Dear Members of the Planning Commission:

      As a member of the Suncrest Owner’s Association, I write to express my opposition to the
      zoning change requested in the above application. The Draper City Municipal Code (DCMC), in
      Title 9, Chapter 5-060, asks the Planning Commission to consider three relevant criteria when
      evaluating proposed amendments. I submit that the application fails all three.

      First, the proposed amendment is not consistent with the goals and objectives of the city’s
      General Plan. It violates several provisions of these goals, including:
          • “Concentrate future development in growth areas and other centers of activity” (LUC-4).
               Re-zoning would create a higher-intensity commercial use well outside a center of
               activity. Draper residents in general—not just Suncrest residents—oppose this: the Plan
               shows that 87% prefer commercial development to occur in non-residential areas,
               predominantly surrounding Interstate 15.
          • “Allow for a diversity of residential uses and supporting services that provide for the
               needs of the community” (LUC-1). Suncrest was originally envisioned with a Village
               Green zoned as Neighborhood Commercial, and re-zoning would require proving that
               the new zone is necessary to meet a community need, or that the new zone inherently
               better serves this goal. Neither is true. There is no evidence of need for another
               reception center, and the presence of the adjacent HOA clubhouse and the larger
               Siempre only 5 minutes away argues against it. Moreover, the Plan characterizes
               General Commercial zones as “intended to be traveler-or commuter-oriented” and as
               such, they do not necessarily directly serve the surrounding community. The current
               Neighborhood Commercial zone better aligns with this goal, since it “in character and
               scale, meet[s] the day to day needs of nearby residents” (DCMC 9-8-020).

      The applicant argues that re-zoning brings economic benefit to gas stations, restaurants, and
      other businesses. These are indirect benefits, whereas the proposal lacks any direct benefit for
      the community and creates direct harms. When pursing the City Mission of “provid[ing]
      protection and services for its community” it should be recognized that direct benefits and
      harms weigh more heavily in the quality-of-life calculus than indirect and uncertain benefits.
Second, the proposed amendment is not harmonious with the overall character of existing
development in the vicinity. The Plan’s Goals state that redevelopment should be done
“respecting the character of adjacent neighborhoods” (LUC-6) and should “encourage
transitions between different land uses and intensities through the use of gradual land use
changes”(LUC-5). The Plan characterizes General Commercial zones as “placed along high-
traffic corridors,” and elsewhere in Draper, these zones are located in close proximity to
interstates and other major roads. Nowhere are they embedded centrally in such a large
residential zone, because it creates incongruity, abrupt transitions, and fails to respect
neighborhood characteristics. Residents seek a secluded mountain community in Suncrest, and
both a reception center and General Commercial zone are antithetical to this.

The applicant argues that the socializing of reception guests enhances the community feel and
that they add to the “hustle and bustle” of the area. Yet you have heard from Suncrest
residents that the “hustle and bustle” is precisely the problem with receptions. This also
neglects that reception guests are not there to socialize with residents, and they may at times
antagonize residents. Furthermore, while the existing restaurant, pool, and fire station directly
benefit surrounding residents, the reception center—inharmoniously—does not.

Third, the proposed amendment is likely to adversely affect adjacent property. Other
residents have separately complained about the noise and traffic caused by the reception
center. Noise is commonly accepted to be a risk factor for depressing property values, and re-
zoning would permanently entrench this risk for nearby homeowners. The applicant offers no
strategy to mitigate these risks, and there is no rationale or evidence suggesting that re-zoning
would benefit adjacent properties. In contrast, uses permitted under the existing Neighborhood
Commercial zone, such as a coffee shop, boutique, or convenience store, would be more likely
to be perceived as amenities. They would increase the area’s walkability, potentially improving
property values.

Although the DCMC explicitly forbids use as a reception center in a Neighborhood Commercial
zone, the “Suncrest Reception Center” has advertised itself online since 2018 or earlier, so we
have a preview of the proposal’s effects. The noise, traffic, trash, and fireworks have created a
nuisance as well as a real, physical threat to residents of the Suncrest community. The
amendment serves no community need and benefits only the business owner, at the expense
of Suncrest residents. Both the business itself and the nature of the proposed zone fail to meet
the principal criteria that the DCMC asks the Planning Commission to consider. Therefore, I ask
you to deny the application.

Sincerely,


Neil Rosenberg
