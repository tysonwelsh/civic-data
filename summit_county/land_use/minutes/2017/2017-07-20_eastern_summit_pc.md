Planner Caus introduced the Work Session. This project was first introduced to the 
Planning Commission for Public Comment on June 1, 2017. At that meeting, it was 
explained what a SPA was and what a SPA is meant to do, the history of the Promontory 
Specially Planned Area (SPA), the South Point Development Agreement amendment, the 
proposal, and received public input. The general consensus of the Planning Commission 
was community benefit did not outweigh the proposed increase in use and densities and 
directed the applicant to come back with revisions for the proposal. The vicinity map of the 
proposal was shown. 
The current proposed changes are: 735 residential units and 190,000 square feet of 
commercial density, with a change in the breakdown of the residential units and the 
commercial density. The recreational facility (waterpark) was removed and replaced with 
office density. The Children’s Justice Center parcel increased from one acre to four acres. 
The hotel and the helipad have been removed. After the Staff Report went out, the applicant 
let Staff know that the additional lakeside properties by the reservoir are no longer being 
asked for, and are being reorganized with the original ten lots in the subdivision. 
Staff’s recommendation is to have the Planning Commission give input and direction to 
Staff and the applicant. Representatives from Wasatch County have been asked to give 
input to give a better picture of what is being proposed in Wasatch County. Kip Bigelow 
from South Summit School District is also in attendance to give input and answer additional 
questions concerning the school site. 
Doug Smith, the Wasatch County Planning Director shared a presentation. Wasatch County 
has a lot of growth planned in the area near the South Point planned development, near the

1.   Public hearing and possible action regarding a Plat Amendment to combine Lots 17 
& 18 of the Aspen Camp Subdivision; 7560 N Promontory Ranch Rd; AC-17 & AC-18; Scott 
Jaffa, applicant. – Amir Caus, County Planner  
 
Planner Caus shared the background for the application. This is an application asking for 
two lots to be combined. Typically, this is an Administrative process with the Community 
Development Director. Because the utility easements were identified on the edges of the 
lots, they need to be removed as part of this plat. There was a recommendation from the 
utility providers. Staff is recommending approval as outlined in the Staff Report. One unit of 
density is extinguished, which lessens demand on the infrastructure. The developer does 
not get credit for an additional unit in this case. Staff sees that as a good cause. This was 
appealed to the Council in 2009 and the Council found that no new density can be created 
when two lots are combined. That is included as a Condition of Approval for this 
application.
