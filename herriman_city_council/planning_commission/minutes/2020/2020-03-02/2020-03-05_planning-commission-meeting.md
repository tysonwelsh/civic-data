The following are the minutes of the Planning Commission Meeting of the Herriman Planning Commission. The
meeting was held on Thursday, March 5, 2020 at 6:00 p.m. in the Herriman City Hall Council Chambers, 5355
West Herriman Main Street, Herriman, Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting, as required by law, was posted in the
City Hall, on the City’s website, and delivered to members of the Council, media, and interested citizens.

Bradford, Heather Garcia, and Joy Kaseke,

Assistant City Planner MacDonald stated the applicant requested an amendment to the site plan for a portion of the
Belle Vea development. Public hearings and notices were not necessary for conditional use applications. The property
March 5, 2020 – Planning Commission Minutes                                              Page 3 of 12
was originally approved in 2015 for 501 units and 30,000 square feet of commercial, which was still the same. The
original site plan and elevations were compared to the amended site plan and elevations and changes were identified.
Commercial locations were pointed out.

Grade changes were used to lower the height of several buildings. Commissioners offered that the original site plan
differed from the one displayed today. Staff offered to confirm the plan as Commissioners discussed the development
with the applicant.

Applicant Tim Soffe with Think Architecture offered to answer questions and highlight the changes. He displayed
the site plan and identified the stacked flat and courtyard areas. Density was higher in areas near SLCC. Commercial
areas were along 4000 West, to be near SLCC. Changes to phase two and three included turning the stacked flats due
to the steep grade. In some areas the slope was retained for tuck-under parking. Applicant Soffe offered that it
contained 35% open space, much higher than the 15% required. The applicant tried to maintain open courtyards
across from the townhomes. Garage locations were identified. Buildings were more broken up to accommodate the
grade. They met the City parking requirement per bedroom plus 90 guest spots.

The correct plan was distributed to Commissioners and displayed.

Commissioner Jacobson offered that the front part of phase three looked awesome and the courtyards had a
neighborhood feel. He added the opinion that the revised courtyard area looked more like apartments.

The applicant offered that phases two and three were blended together. Many of the stacked flats were removed and
added to the courtyard area because the buildings were very tall. Parking distribution was much better, in his opinion.
And tall buildings were not preferable.

Commissioner Jacobson preferred the phase two transition with parking inside the homes. He opined there was no
longer a smooth transition between phases. Commissioner Ferguson added that original courtyards were accessed at
grade. The recent changes made it a different product type and better transitions were needed in the middle area.

Commissioners offered the opinion that tall buildings should be along the SLCC side and that hidden, tucked in
garages were preferable. The other areas felt like their own little neighborhood.

Applicant Soffe offered he had built a lot of town home communities and he thought this transition was better than
others.

The Commissioners offered that high buildings would be okay back near SLCC and they hoped to get away from
huge parking lots. They also stated the apartment building looked too much like apartments and they preferred
individual courtyards. They felt the changes looked like they had less open space. The Commissioners offered specific
recommendations regarding parking, larger courtyards, preferable commercial locations, pedestrian crossings and
areas for additional green space. They requested that unique elements and other items from the first plan be
reincorporated.

be between the commercial development and adjacent residential.

Staff offered that the application was just for the phase one condominium plats and it does not change the density,
amenities, or building elevations that were previously approved. The applicant requested preliminary plat approval for
a Condominium plat for 60 units known as Parkside Phase 1. The subject property is located at 13049 S Cannavale
Drive and is 1.95 acres in size. The property is zoned MU-2 (Mixed Use). Staff mailed notices to all property owners
within 300 feet of the subject property on February 24, 2020. As of this meeting, staff had received zero comments
regarding the application.

The Parkside development was given final master plan approval by the Planning Commission on October 17, 2019.
The project consisted of 259 units, 120 of which were condominiums. The condo units required a separate condo
plat be recorded prior to the sale of units. The applicant had also submitted the CC&Rs and Bylaws for the condos
and common space.

Applicant Brandon Watson with Edge Homes approached the podium and offered to answer questions. He informed
the Commission that the site plan had been approved two months ago and the first two were being constructed. The
particulars for the live/work corner units were addressed in the CC&Rs.

Commissioner Bradford inquired if language was included requiring garages to be used for parking only, not for
storage. The applicant replied in the affirmative.

Chair Berbert opened the public hearing.

Tom May stated he understood this property would be developed with townhomes. He wondered why the core of
the City was surrounded with condominiums. He stated there should be something better on the property.

Skylee Knapp has lived in Herriman Towne Center since 2011 and loves J. Lynn Crane Park. She worries about being
able to access the park, and stated pathways to connect the neighborhood behind the condos were promised. She was
concerned that the high density housing would restrict access and it would no longer be a good place to go. She
March 5, 2020 – Planning Commission Minutes                                                     Page 7 of 12

access to the park without going through scary parts of town.

Darryl Larson was concerned with children safely accessing the park, and stated children needed safe crossing
locations during construction to prevent children getting injured crossing construction areas.

Chair Berbert closed the public hearing.

Commissioners added that safe walking locations had been addressed and approved. Trails and connection areas were

Applicant Jaren Nichols offered the main levels of the commercial units have separate entrances to Master bedrooms
that could be converted to offices. The opportunity is there but was not required. Canopies would be installed initially

living somewhere else, if they chose.

Commissioners wanted commercial required in the live/work units and discussed enforcement options, possibly

Staff informed that the applicant requested a plat amendment to move the location of a trail in Fort Herriman Cove
Phase three. The property was located at 14486 S Herriman View Cove and is zoned A-.25. Staff mailed notices to all
property owners within 300 feet of the subject property. Notices were mailed to 20 property owners on February 24,
2020. As of this meeting, staff had received no comments on the request. Plat amendments are administrative
decisions The Commission would make a decision based on compliance with the applicable ordinances. If the
applicant received preliminary plat approval, the next step would be obtaining final plat approval from the Engineering
Department. All of the lots would still meet the minimum size and width as required by the zone.

There is a trail access between lots 310 and 311 that is owned by the City. The applicant proposed to move this trail
location to lot 308. The applicant worked with the Engineering department on the new trail location.

Jared Ford with Ensign Engineering approached the podium on behalf of the applicant and stated the new trail was
located in a more natural location.

Community Development Director Blake Thomas address the grade of the trail. It was laid out to maintain access to
open space, makes the lots more usable, and has a better slope.

Chair Berbert opened the public hearing.

Jeff White owns a neighboring property and was curious about long term trail access and land use to the southwest
of the trail.

Eric Platt stated his land bordered the trail. He has accessed the original trail, which was very steep. He offered a
potential problem with the new location that the grade at the beginning was better, but the trail quickly becomes
vertical. He requested information regarding connectivity to the main trail and long term trail plans. He was concerned
that if it’s too steep people would bypass the trail and walk through backyards.

Susie Babcock lives in the cul-de-sac and was concerned with the new house being built on the other side of the new
trail location. She asked if that landowner had been notified.

Chair Berbert closed the public hearing.
