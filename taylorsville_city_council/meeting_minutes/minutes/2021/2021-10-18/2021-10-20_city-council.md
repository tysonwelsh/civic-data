        1.2      Mayor’s Report

     Mayor Kristie Overson reported she had recently attended a ribbon cutting at Trolley Wing
Company. She stated the food was delicious and the business owners had gone out of their way
to express how fast, efficient, and friendly city staff had been as they prepared to open their
business. There were several Trolley Wing Co. locations and nowhere else had the process been
handled as quickly as it had been handled in Taylorsville.

The other ribbon cutting the mayor had attended was at Centennial Plaza, located on the south side
of city hall. Ninety-nine percent of the project was complete, and she stated it was a delightful
evening. The city thoroughly enjoyed introducing the community to this special space, including
the art that had been placed within it. More art would be added in the coming years, and some
would be replaced on a rotating basis.

Mayor Overson wished to thank the Taylorsville Police Department and the Fraternal Order of
Police for the Trunk or Treat event that had been held the previous Saturday at city hall. She
enjoyed seeing families meet members of the new police department.

It wasn’t just the police officers that were valued in the police department. There was an incredible
civilian staff of 7 people who were also very deserving of recognition. They were a fun, dynamic
group with a combined total of 105 years of experience related to law enforcement.

Google Fiber was quickly moving through the city and some homes had already started receiving
service. She was delighted that Taylorsville was the first city to bring Google Fiber to the west
side of the Salt Lake Valley.

When passing through the area it would be clear to passersby that construction at the I-215 Trail
was well underway. The result would be increased convenience for those that used alternate
transportation or wished to walk and enjoy the new Centennial Plaza. The improvements were
part of Taylorsville’s Trails Master Plan.

Mayor Overson stated that the Shop Local program had really taken off and there were many more
participants than there had been the previous spring. During the previous week alone, $19,000 in
Shop Local bucks had been redeemed at businesses throughout Taylorsville. Of the funds allocated
for the program, 64% had been spent thus far.

That evening, the Taylorsville Salt Lake Community College symphony would be performing next
door at the Mid-Valley Performing Arts Center. The mayor was excited they could perform in the
new facility but sorry that the performance conflicted with the council meeting so that she and the
council were unable to attend.

Mayor Overson explained that as was the case each year, leaf bags were available at city hall to
help residents clear their yards of fall leaves. Once the bags were full, they could be dropped off
at Swensen Valley Regional Park or at Vista Park.

The mayor ended her report by mentioning a group of residents who had been volunteering their
time to clean up trash along I-215 around Redwood Road and 4700 South. The group had gone to
the site four different times and had filled a total of 96 large bags with litter.

Chair Cochran stated that he too had attended the ribbon cutting for Trolley Wing Company and
had been very pleased to hear how smoothly the process had gone with city staff.

        1.3      Citizen Comments

      The chair reviewed the Citizen Comment procedures for the audience before calling for any
citizens who wished to address the council.

Chair Cochran read an email into the record which had been submitted by Margaret Harper shortly
before the meeting began. The crux of the email referenced a piece of property being developed
near Taylor’s Ridge Cove. She believed that the driveway access of the property was not in
compliance with city code. Ms. Harper was present at the meeting and Chair Cochran let her know
that he had also noticed activity on the site and had questions. He would ensure that her email was
given to the appropriate individual and that she would be receive a response.

There were no other citizen comments, and Chair Cochran closed the comment period.

3.       REPORTS

         3.1     Taylorsville Police Department Report – Chief Brady Cottam

      Taylorsville Police Chief Brady Cottam made a brief announcement that due to the recent
on-duty death of University of Utah Sgt. Kory Newbold, part of southbound Redwood Road would
be blocked the following day from about 5800 South to Memorial Redwood Cemetery at 6500
South. The procession would begin around 1:00 p.m. to as late as 3:00 p.m.

Chief Cottam wished to introduce the new department’s first two employees of the month. He
invited his two deputy chiefs Todd Grey and Brett Miller to step forward and help him present the
awards for the July and August Employees of the Month.

The July Employee of the month was Officer Matt Landolfi who had been nominated by Sgt.
Kresdon Bennett. Sgt. Bennett described Officer Landolfi as his “go to” officer and pointed out
his “steadfast work ethic” and was “willingness to handle any problem that presented itself.”

Chief Cottam then asked Merari Lopez to step forward and receive the award for the August
Employee of the Month. Merari was the police department’s victim advocate and truly cared about
all the victims she encountered. Her supervisor indicated that she had done an amazing job putting
the new Victim Advocate Program together and was a huge asset to the department.

Chief Cottam then presented statistical data pertaining police activity from July through September
2021, including general offenses in each council district. His report also included:

        The number and type of each citation issued (both traffic and criminal)
        Citations issued vs. warnings issued
        The number of cases screened for both District Court and Juvenile Court
        A discussion of the fact that Unified Police had turned over 2,000 pieces of evidence
         collected by the original Taylorsville Police Department (in existence from 2005 to 2012)
         to the new Taylorsville Police Department.
        The amount of new evidence collected
        The number of records requests, expungements and victim advocate activities that had
         taken place

        The number of CAR team call outs (Collision Analysis & Reconstruction)
        Call response times

Councilmember Harker inquired about morale among the officers now that the department was
three months in. Chief Cottam responded that the officers were enjoying being so appreciated by
the city and the community.

Because Jay Ziolkowski could not be present that evening, Chief Cottam then presented Mr.
Ziolkowski’s report summarizing the large-scale transition from contracting with Unified Police
Department for law enforcement services to creating a new police department. He pointed out what
a great decision the city had made in picking Jay Ziolkowski to lead the transition. Chief Cottam
stated that cities from throughout the country had reached out to him about the transition and Mr.
Ziolkowski had created an amazing road map that could help other municipalities who were going
through the same process. It included not just the things that worked well but also lessons learned
and what they would do differently if they were to begin again.

7.             OTHER MATTERS

        7.2 Ordinance No. 21-16 An Ordinance of the City of Taylorsville amending
        Taylorsville Municipal Code Section 9.61.080: Hours of Use of Parks- Tracy Cowdell

Mr. Cowdell pointed out this was a council-driven policy decision. A previous city council had
determined the hours of city parks, but the current council had expressed interest in standardizing
and spelling out specific hours of operation to agree with those of Salt Lake County operated parks
within Taylorsville. Those hours were 6:00 a.m. to 10 p.m.
