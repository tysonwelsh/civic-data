Chair Cragun next asked for the histmy of limiting home occupations to just one, thereby requiring
a Conditional Use Pennit for an additional home occupation business on the same property. City
Planner/Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell believed the requirement had been around
for a while. This section of the Home Occupation Code has not been revised in several years. He

requirements for obtaining a business license because people can potentially operate more than one
business under the same business name. He added that the Business License Code dates back to
around 1979. Chair Cragun did not have an issue with approving the application but she wanted to
determine if there was an intentional reason the City did not want more than one home occupation
operating out of the same location.

Commissioner Flynn noted that if further similar applications are submitted, this situation would set
a precedent and a threshold would need to be set. Mr. Crowell stated that there is a point at which a
section of the City should be rezoned to Commercial of some sort. He noted that Bluffdale is quite
permissive in its allowance for home occupations.

s.     PUBLIC HEARING, CONSIDERATION, AND VOTE on Proposed Amendments to
       the City Engineering Standards and Specifications. City of Bluffdale, Applicant.
       Trevor Andra, Staff Presenter.

Assistant City Engineer, Trevor Andra, presented the staff report and explained that he would be
presenting new standards and changes to the existing standards. He first discussed the changes to
the drinking water meter, which the members of the Public Works Department can read while
driving by the meter. The result is a change of the lid to a specific DNL number. The goal is to
make the lid as flush with the ground level as possible to avoid accidental breakage. The change to
the meter service resulted in a change to the meter vault and the manhole. Mr. Andra explained that
a bypass option was incorporated in the event a meter breaks. The water service would not be
interrupted, and the water use could still be read. A meter would be put in place if the need arose.

Mr. Andra presented a new standard regarding a curb inlet for storrnwater. Currently, the City has
standards for concrete boxes but there are circular PVC types of boxes, which have a circular grate.
Consequently, new standards are necessary. This new type of design is used on a project near City

Hall. There is more flexibility in the design for stonnwater drainage. The grate and frame would
have the same loading requirements as the metal ones.

Mr. Andra addressed an existing standard regarding Low-Impact Development ("LID") structures
in Section D-Stonnwater Storage Facilities. The water is stored on-site for infiltration. The
standard was changed to make it more flexible in tenns of what developers use and to provide a
clean-out manhole in the front. The previous standard did not include that requirement, so it was
difficult to maintain the system and clean it out. A chamber system will be used to facilitate the
infiltration of the water into the ground. This was intended to be an improvement to an existing
standard.

Mr. Andra next addressed a new standard because of changes made by the State with regard to
stonnwater. The changes, which went into effect on March 1, 2020, require all development to use
some type of LID element within the stonnwater. With the addition of the new standards, the City
will have five LID standards because the State requires five options. The standards need to be
based on pollutant removal from the stonnwater.

The first option is for the use of "swells." It goes over the specifics of how they are to be built with
the different types and levels of soils. Bluffdale City adapted infonnation that was obtained from
the state of Minnesota, which has been progressive in its approach to LID. Mr. Andra referenced
the retention areas near the Simple Products property along 14600 South. He explained that those
retention areas were the premise behind the proposed standards and design guidelines.

Mr. Andra addressed a new standard that falls in line with the LID stonnwater. The new example
acts as a sump, the pipe has perforations and is wrapped in a stone matrix with fabric on the outside.
That design would be used in a new development where there is not a stormwater system close by.
There would be options for using the design along parking areas, and not along a curb and gutter.
They would also have a bag that helps catch the large debris to help filter out some of the pollutants.

In response to Commissioner Luker's question regarding who would replace the drainage
infrastructure, Mr. Andra stated that the City would if it was a City facility. On private streets, the
HOA would have to budget for the replacements.

Mr. Andra reviewed another new standard, which was also a LID that would use a rain garden. He
pointed out that they are effective at removing pollutants, but they are complex to build. There are
companies that make such systems "in a box" that can be dropped into place. There are also
companies that sell each of the individual components so that a contractor can build the system.

Mr. Andra noted that a forthcoming requirement is that any site would have to keep an 80th
percentile rain event on site, which would equate to approximately the first half-inch of water. The
rain gardens would allow that water to run into them so the water can infiltrate and remain on-site.

Mr. Andra reviewed another new standard, which was the subject of much internal discussion by
staff. The City has many detention/retention basins that are associated with specific cul-de-sacs that
serve five to 20 lots. Part of the discussion centered on what would be best for maintenance and

Mr. Andra next discussed the conduit that comes up through the foundation to the light pole.
Previously, the metal conduit was allowed, however, industry practices and standards determined
that PVC, 1igid metal, and fiberglass are all comparable for those installations. Consequently, the
three different materials will be pennitted for conduits.

Existing roads, and their cross-section detail, were Mr. Andra's next topic. The changes will
specify the type of slun-y fill that will be required. It will need to consist of a larger aggregate. The
high-density mineral bond surface treatment will be another option.

Mr. Andra next addressed a new addition to the standards: asphalt types that will be allowed.
Currently, all asphalt in Bluffdale follows PG64-28, which is a type of binder. Some options were
added to allow a deviation from that standard for the entire City. The new standard on Trails and
Path Pavement would allow the PG58-28 because they do not have the loading as a roadway.

The trail standard would also apply to parking lots. Mr. Andra sought feedback on how to define a
parking lot. Mr. Crowell stated that a distinction would be the fact that large parking lots have drive
aisles, which are not roadways. Roads that include on-street parking would still need to meet the
road standards and not the parking standards. Mr. Andra explained that roadways have their own
definition. He then read the proposed definition of a parking lot. Discussion ensued on how to
make distinctions between roadways and adjacent parking lots. He stated that there may need to be
a definition of how a roadway and adjacent parking lot are connected. The intent was to not lose
the flexibility being proposed for parking lots and roadways. The City Code contains a definition of
parking space and parking lot. There was additional discussion on this complex subject. Mr. Andra
indicated that the definitions would be adjusted.

Mr. Andra noted that all of the cross-sections have varying widths, whether it be the park strip,
travel lane, and bike and shoulder lanes. The cross-sections were updated to show that though
cross-sections vary, they do have a minimum width. The goal was to simplify the requirements and
make them more understandable and consistent. Mr. Crowell observed that when Special
Development Districts are proposed, developers like to propose different road cross-sections so that
