## F3(i) — pypdf text corruption, 2015–2018 → FIXED

pypdf inserted stray mid-word spaces on this county's born-digital PDFs
(`"carried with the f ollowing vote"`, `"the mot ion was seconde d by"`), breaking the
extractor's literal anchor. **2016-08-30 held 26 `AYE:` roll blocks; the markdown carried 5
usable anchors and the db recorded 5 motions.**

Measured on a random sample of the 228 born-digital files, poppler's `pdftotext -layout`
drops the split-word rate from **~13 per 1,000 tokens to ~0 — better on 10 of 10**. New
`db/reextract_borndigital.py` re-derives the body of every born-digital markdown from its
retained raw PDF, preserving the provenance front-matter and restamping `extraction:`.
Scanned/OCR files are untouched. Idempotent.

Two traps handled while building it:
- **Multi-part meetings.** 30 meetings have 2–7 raw parts joined with `----PART BREAK----`.
  A first-match-only version silently truncated them — 2015-03-31 fell from 41,332 to 7,336
  chars and lost 9 motions. Parts are now ordered by the front-matter's own `source_url`
  list, which preserves the original sequence.
- **A shrink guard.** The script refuses to replace a body with poppler text shorter than
  half of it, and prints the skip. Post-run check: exactly **1 of 495** files shrank >15%
  (2023-05-10, 0.82×) and its token count is unchanged (2,306 → 2,305) — pypdf had split
  "COMMISSION" into "COMMIS"+"SION"; the loss is whitespace, not content.

Recovery: 2016 243→404 motions, 2017 194→354, 2018 249→409; named-roll motions 822→1,203.

## F3(ii) — the 2019+ ALL-CAPS roll → FIXED (2019), PARTIAL (2020–2024)

`NAME_LINE_RE` matched Title-Case only and `ok_name()` rejected every ALL-CAPS line as a
label, so the caps era yielded **zero** named votes. Worse, `VOTE: 3-0` was not an anchor at
all — **2019-01-29 published a full named roll and produced 0 motions.**

- `VOTE: n-n` and bare `PASSED:/FAILED: n-m` added as result anchors.
- `COMMISSIONER LEE` resolves through **this meeting's attendance block**
  (`PRESENT: COMMISSIONER BILL LEE, CHAIR`) — the repo's proven per-file pattern — with a
  corpus-wide surname map as fallback for multi-part documents that begin mid-meeting.
- Blank lines no longer terminate a vote block (the caps era prints `AYE:` / blank / names).
- The 2020–2024 parenthetical form is parsed, **including the direction trap**: the clerk
  writes both `AYE: ALL IN FAVOR (…)` and `AYE: THOSE OPPOSED (COMMISSIONER LEE)`, so the
  direction is read from the phrase, never from the `AYE:` prefix.
- Multi-voter category lines (`NAY: COMMISSIONER GRAVES AND COMMISSIONER IVIE`) split.
- The vote block is now searched **backward** from the anchor too, because when the verdict
  line itself anchors the roll sits above it.

2019: 0 → **264** named motions. 2020–2024: **21 of 63** parenthetical blocks captured;
the residual are in OCR text too fragmented to pair reliably — honest, not a ceiling.

**A phantom-motion regression caught and fixed mid-pass:** adding the bare `PASSED:/FAILED:`
anchor inflated the corpus by ~1,670 motions, because `relineate()` separates `AYE:`, the
names and the verdict onto their own lines and the 2-line merge window treated the verdict
as a new motion. Adjacency is now measured in **non-blank** lines and tolerates an
intervening vote block.

**Recovered failure:** 2018-08-28 printed `AYE: (COMMISSIONER LEE)` / `NAY: COMMISSIONER
GRAVES AND COMMISSIONER IVIE` / `FAILED: 2-1` (nays-first) and was **absent from the db
entirely**. It now carries `outcome=Fail` with Lee=Aye, Graves=Nay, Ivie=Nay.

## F3(iii) — 20 meetings the county's API lists → 15 FETCHED, 3 confirmed dead

**Not a code defect.** The current fetcher parses every missing filename correctly; the
minutes were simply posted after the 2026-07-20 build. Re-running it recovered **15
meetings** (11 in Sept–Nov 2025, 2 in June 2026, plus 2019-07-16). The 3 remaining
(2021-06-02, 2022-08-15, 2024-01-31) return **404 today** and stay documented gaps.

Two defects the re-fetch itself exposed, both now guarded in `fetch_legislative.py`:
- **A meeting filed in 2029.** The county describes `07.16.2019CommissionMeetingMinutes.pdf`
  as "July 16, **2029**". `parse_date` trusted the description, creating a meeting ten years
  in the future. When the filename carries a year and the two disagree, the **filename now
  wins**. Gate: `meeting_date > date('now')` returns **0**.
- **A placeholder ingested as minutes.** `pendingminutes.pdf` reads *"Meeting minutes file
  for this date/item is pending creation."* It is now logged `unrecovered` with that reason
  instead of becoming an empty meeting.

## Audit D4 — person fragmentation → FIXED

One commissioner was split across three person rows and OCR garbles added more. New
`db/person_aliases.csv` (7 rows, read by `build_db.py`) consolidates **15 name variants into
8 real commissioners**. Every entry carries its evidence, including a **non-co-occurrence
test** — no variant pair ever appears on the same motion, which is what rules out two
different people sharing a surname (the alta Bourke pathology).

`Nathan Lee` is the instructive one: the **source itself** misprints it on 2018-07-17 line
504 while every other roll in that file says `Nathan Ivie`, and the row co-occurs with
William C. Lee, so it cannot be Lee. The flat layer keeps the misprint verbatim; only the
person layer resolves it.
