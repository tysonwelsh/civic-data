GPZ-1-2020
Fetzers’ Inc.
General plan change from mixed use to light industrial and zone change from C-2 (General
Commercial) to M (Manufacturing).

Joe Wixom, CEO of Fetzers’ Inc., has submitted a General Plan/zone change application for a
7.73-acre parcel located at 4850 West 3500 South. The property is currently zoned C-2 (general
commercial) with a General Plan designation of mixed use (office and high density residential).
The proposed zone is M (manufacturing) and the proposed General Plan designation is light
industrial.

Surrounding zones include R-1-8 to the north, east and a portion of the west side and C-2 to the
south and the remaining portion of the west side. Surrounding land uses include homes to the
north, east and a portion of the west side, a charter school and vacant property for the remaining
portion of the west side and commercial uses to the south. The subject property includes a vacant

                                                                                       1|Page
building of approximately 95,000 square feet that was formerly used as Shopko. Shopko vacated
the building last year.

When Shopko was built in the late 80s a 20’ space for landscaping was reserved north of the
masonry wall along the north side because, at the time, a road was planned along the north
property line. However, the road was not constructed adjacent to the north property line. Instead,
a subdivision was constructed with the rear yards of lots adjoining the north property line. The
decision was made by the City to allow the 20’ area once planned for landscaping to be absorbed
into the adjoining residential lots.

The applicant is proposing to utilize the existing building as a warehouse. Since the C-2 zone
only allows up to 50% of a building to be utilized as a warehouse, the M zone is needed to
facilitate the applicant’s proposal. Included with this report is a letter from the applicant
describing the proposed use as well as reasons to support this application. His letter also outlines
uses that he is willing to prohibit in the development agreement. After reviewing the proposed
list of prohibited uses and the applicant’s description of the proposed use, staff is recommending
that the only uses allowed beyond what is allowed in the C-2 zone should be warehousing and
light industrial. In the zoning ordinance, light industrial “means the indoor manufacturing,
predominantly from previously prepared materials, of finished products or parts, including
processing, fabrication, assembly, treatment, and packaging of such products, and incidental
storage, sales and distribution of such products, which does not produce noise, odors, vibration,
hazardous waste materials, or particulate that will disturb or endanger neighboring properties.”

In the applicant’s letter he mentions that the current owner, Mike Kehoe, has tried unsuccessfully
to secure retail tenants to fill the building. Included with this report are two emails from the
owner that provide a list of tenants that were contacted.

The applicant also mentioned that traffic and parking for the proposed warehouse would be less
than retail. The parking requirement for warehouse is 1 space/2,000 sq. ft. and 1 space /250 sq.
ft. According to the Institute of Transportation Engineers, warehouse uses generate 3.56
trips/1,000 sq. ft. and retail generates 42.94 trips/1,000 sq. ft.

During the Planning Commission study session, the Commission expressed four
concerns/questions. These four concerns are listed below along with a response from the
applicant:

Planning Commission concern: Given the age and plainness of the building, is the applicant
willing to make improvements beyond paint to enhance the appearance of the building?

Applicant response: We will be performing significant repairs to the building HVAC System,
Roof, Fire Sprinkler and Electrical Systems that have been vandalized while the building is
vacant. Due to the significant costs of repair our exterior improvements have been limited to
removal of existing signage and a two-tone paint scheme with future signage based on partner
occupants in the warehousing space. We are also planning on restoring all the existing landscape
spaces on the parcel and maintain them as we do our other properties. We are exploring upgrades



                                                                                          2|Page
to the exterior lighting on the building and parking lot for security purposes as well.

Planning Commission concern: Light industrial uses could create a conflict with residential
neighbors. If the Planning Commission recommended to the Council that the only use, they
would be comfortable allowing beyond those already allowed in the C-2 zone is warehousing,
would you still pursue the rezone?

Applicant response: We are still inclined to pursue the rezone, however there are some uses that
we have outlined in our discussions that we would want to make sure are covered. We discussed
the potential for breaking down store fixtures for our large clients recycling effort. This would
be isolated to the interior of the building and would be done with hand tools. As our logistics hub
there will also be instances requiring us to build crate or pallet for shipments arriving loose at the
warehouse that need to be crated for specific needs at the job sites. We often accept finished
products in large quantities and those crates/pallets are broken down into smaller quantities to be
delivered to projects in an organized manner. There are also some occasions where we would
preassemble some of the components like door hinges to doors or broken-down cabinet parts
assembled with hand tools because of storage and layout space constraints on the job sites. I am
not sure if this type of light assembly can be performed under warehousing without being in
breach of our zoning or if keeping "Light Industrial" as the only other use beyond C-2 would
allow for that. We are willing to concede on the commissary use, it was a thought we had of a
community value like others done in Holladay and Sandy, but if that represents a concern for the
commission, we are willing to go away from that.

Planning Commission concern: Given the proximity of residential uses, hours of operation are a
concern. In the applicant’s letter he mentions that the business will operate with “traditional
business hours Monday – Friday”. Is the applicant willing to limit the hours of operation in the
development agreement?

Applicant response: We have researched several large retail establishments to determine
their exterior operations timing (trucks coming and going) to make sure we can fit within that
and even provide a reprieve for the neighbors who experienced the previous tenants. We
have determined from those conversations the deliveries are often arriving all hours throughout a
24-hour period and the businesses often operate close to 24 hours when stocking of materials is
included. We are willing to limit exterior use to 6 am to 8 pm Monday through Saturday. Our
traditional operation hours will be 6 am to 3:30 Monday through Thursday and 6 am through 11
am on Friday. We occasionally have large rollout deliveries that require a Saturday presence, but
this is typically limited to just a few Saturdays a quarter. We have a company policy that forbids
work on Sunday.

Planning Commission concern: It doesn’t seem necessary to rezone the entire parcel to M. Is the
applicant willing to limit the area of the rezone to just the area needed for the warehouse?

Applicant response: We are willing to limit the rezone to just the building. We are willing to
start the process of carving just the building and its surrounding access to M Zone request if that
is valuable in getting the zoning approved.

If the Commission believes that this rezone is appropriate if the area of the rezone is reduced and
the right development agreement restrictions are imposed, staff recommends that this application
be continued to allow more time to work out the details.

Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval. This application should be approved subject to the following:

           a. The area of the General Plan/zone change shall be limited to the area needed for
              the warehouse and associated parking. The remaining area shall remain C-2.
           b. A development agreement should be required that includes the following
              standards:
                   i. In addition to the uses allowed within the C-2 zone, the only other uses
                      allowed are warehousing and light industrial. Light industrial uses shall be
                      limited to the assembly and disassembly of products from pre-
                      manufactured components. No sanding, cutting, painting or any other use
                      that would require venting to the outside shall be allowed.
                  ii. The entire exterior of the building shall be painted with two tone paint.
                 iii. All existing landscaped areas shall be repaired or replanted to comply with
                      City ordinances and prior approvals.
                 iv. All loading and unloading shall occur between 6 AM and 8 PM, Monday
                      through Saturday.

   2. Continuance. This application should be continued for reasons determined during the
      public hearing.

   3. Denial. Manufacturing zoning should not be placed adjacent to residential uses.



Discussion: Steve Pastorik presented the application. The applicant, Joe Wixom, stated that
Fetzers’ Inc. does lease other buildings and they would like to use the location in question to
consolidate storage. Joe explained this would allow for more capacity at their central plant. Joe
explained that Fetzers’ will be looking to parcel out the parking lot that is not needed for their
location. Joe also explained this location would add approximately 50 new job opportunities for
West Valley City. Commissioner Wood asked Joe where the employees would park. Joe
explained most of the parking would be in the garden area. Commissioner Wood asked if
Fetzers’ is looking to have any customer’s coming to this location. Joe explained this would not
happen immediately; however, there has been talk about the addition of a showroom in the
future.

Chairperson Meaders opened public comment. Eulogiu Alejande, who works at the Elementary
School adjacent to the property in question, had a few concerns. Eulogiu wanted confirmation
that if Fetzers’ Inc. ended up selling the property the new owners would need to follow the
development agreement. Eulogiu then added 13 additional standards he would like to see as part
of the development agreement. Eulogiu does not feel a manufacturing zone would fit well in this


                                                                                         4|Page
location. Steve explained any future owner needs to abide by the development agreement
standards.

Jake Swapp, property owner of a home to the east, then addressed the Planning Commission.
Jake stated that the addition of a manufacturing zone across the street from his home would drive
down the property value.

Jake explained that surrounding the property in question is a residential area with a school,
dentist office, and restaurant. Jake feels that customers will not want to eat at a restaurant with
large trucks coming and going. Jake also stated he does not want to live near a warehouse. Jake
asked the Planning Commission to think about this warehouse being next to their homes and how
they would feel. Commissioner Woodruff asked Joe if he lived there when Shopko was at this
location. Jake said he did live there, and he liked Shopko in this location for ease of shopping.
Steve explained that the current property owner has tried to backfill this location with retail
stores i.e. grocery stores. Steve stated the challenge is the building is too big to fill with a retail
business. Commissioner Fuller commented that he thinks the deliveries to Shopko would be
more than to Fetzers’. Steve explained when looking at traffic for existing retail stores and
warehouses, retail businesses overall traffic is significantly higher than a warehouse.
Commissioner Lovato stated that he does not expect a higher impact with Fetzers’ Inc. in this
location as opposed to a retail store.

CA-1-2020
Kenworth Pole Sign
2125 S Constitution Blvd
M Zone (27.8 Acres)

The applicant, Dustin Latimer with IG Sign, is requesting a conditional use amendment for a
pole sign at Kenworth Sales. The zoning for the property and the adjacent parcels is M,
Manufacturing. The General Plan for the area is Light Manufacturing.
Kenworth Sales received conditional use approval for truck sales and service in July of 2005 (C-
26-2005). Two freeway-oriented pole signs were approved for the property at that time. One
serves the I-215 frontage and the other is just off 2700 West near the northern boundary of the
parcel along SR-201. Recently, a two-story building was constructed on an adjacent parcel. The
building obstructs the visibility of the 2700 West sign when traveling northbound on that road.
The applicant is requesting to raise the existing pole sign higher and move it a few feet to the
east.
The existing sign is 27.5 feet tall. The proposed sign will be 40 feet tall and the existing cabinet
will be reused, which has a sign area of 68 square feet. The pole sign will have a pole cover that
matches the building. A 35’ minimum setback from the property line is required.


                                                                                          7|Page
Title 11 requires that the sign be incorporated in a landscaped area that is at least equal to twice
the area of the sign, which would be 136 square feet, with 50% live plant material. Some
additional vegetation shall be added around the sign so that there is 50% live plant material
within the 136 square foot area.

Staff Alternatives:

Approval, subject to the resolution of any concerns raised at the public hearing, as well as the
following conditions:

   1. The sign shall be constructed per the approved plan.
   2. All requirements set forth in Title 11 of the West Valley City Municipal Code shall be
      met.
   3. Additional vegetation shall be added around the base of the sign so that there’s at least
      50% live plant material.
   4. A building permit shall be issued for the sign.

Continuance, for reasons determined at the hearing.


Discussion: Brock Anderson presented the application. The applicant, Dustin Latimer, then
added that IG Sign is looking to keep as much of the original sign as possible. Dustin also stated
that they would add the landscaping around the sign.

C-1-2020
Decker Lake Business Park (Outside Storage)
2340 S. Redwood Rd.
M Zone (3.15 acres)


                                                                                           8|Page
The applicant, Andrew Reyna representing the Decker Lake Business Park, is requesting
conditional use approval for outside storage at 2340 S. Redwood Rd. This property is zoned M,
manufacturing and the West Valley City General Plan designates this area as light
manufacturing. The surrounding zone is M, manufacturing on all sides except for a portion that
is C-2, general commercial, to the north. The surrounding uses include light industrial, office and
retail.

As part of the City’s cleanup efforts a thorough site inspection was conducted of this property
during a business license review. A courtesy notice was issued for violations on this property,
including outside storage. There are two businesses on site that would like to utilize the back
western portion of the property for outside storage. Therefore, they have submitted a conditional
use application to get that violation resolved.

The outside storage area would be used for a granite company and a camper restoration business.
All work including repair/restoration of the campers or cutting of granite would be conducted
within the existing buildings on site and the storage area would only be for storage of granite
materials or campers until they are repaired. The site shall not be used for junk, salvage or as a
vehicle recycling facility. The outside storage area shall be limited to the west side of the site and
screened from public view. The entire storage area must be surfaced with asphalt or concrete and
the appropriate access shall be maintained throughout the site.

The remaining site maintenance issues are being resolved as part of the enforcement process.

Staff Alternatives:

Approval, subject to the resolution of any concerns raised at the public hearing as well as the
following:

           1. The approved use for this application is for outside storage. The site shall not be
              used for junk, auto salvage or a vehicle recycling facility.

           2. The outside storage area must be paved with asphalt or concrete within 6 months
              of this approval and the appropriate access shall be maintained throughout the
              site.

           3. Outside storage is only permitted on the west side of the site as outlined in the
              approved site plan and must be screened from public view.

           4. Must meet the requirements of all affected departments and agencies.

           5. Subject to review upon valid complaint.

Continuance, to allow for the resolutions of any issues raised at the public hearing.
