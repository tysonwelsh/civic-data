Flag Day is scheduled for June 14th this year. It is a day when we have a chance to not

including honor, sacrifice, duty, independence, and liberty. We all know the symbology of
our flag with 13 red and white stripes representing the original 13 colonies, a field of
blue with a white star for each state. I would at this time like to remind us of the
symbology of the colors of our flag:

RED
Hardiness: Ability to endure difficult conditions
Valor: Great Courage in the face of danger

WHITE
Purity: The condition or quality of being pure, freedom of that debases, contaminates,
or pollutes. INNOCENCE: Lack of guile or corruption

BLUE
Vigilance: Keeping careful watch for possible dangers or difficulties
Perseverance: Continuing in a course of action even in the face of difficulty
Justice: Just behavior or treatment

In this past year, there have been many opportunities as individuals, families, and
communities to implement and practice some if not all of the symbology represented by
our flag and its colors. May we continue to emulate all that our flag symbolizes and
proudly display it on June 14th.

CODE AMENDMENTS – Consideration of proposed amendments to the Land
Development Code Tale 17.11.030 in Chapter 17.11 “District and Corridor Zone
Uses” to Permit Financial Institutions, General Sales & Services, and Sales &
Services in Buildings with a footprint exceeding 100,000 square feet as a Conditional
Use in the Industrial Zone – Ordinance 21-12 – Russ Holley, Planner

Planner Russ Holley addressed the Council regarding the proposed code amendments.

RECOMMENDATION
Staff recommends that the Planning Commission recommend approval to the Municipal
Council of these minor amendments to the Land Development Code.

REQUEST
This is a proposal for a minor amendment to the Land Development Code (LDC)
modifying the Commercial & Industrial Use Table (17.11.030) in Chapter 17.11 by
expanding the range of uses conditionally permitted in the Industrial zone. The proposed
amendment to LDC Chapter 17.11 is attached and summarized below:

Allowed Uses in District and Corridor Zones - 17.11.030: Amend the Commercial &
Industrial Use Table by changing “Banks, Savings & Loans, Credit Unions”, “General
Sales/Service” and “Sales and Service, Footprint Greater than 100,000 sq. ft. (Big Box)”
from not permitted to conditionally permitted in the Industrial zone.

The purpose of these changes is to encourage the transition of “big box” retailers from
Main Street locations to the westside of Logan where large, undeveloped tracts are
available. The proposed amendment would expand the range of uses permitted in the
Industrial zone by including financial institutions as well as other general sales &
services similar to the range of uses permitted in the Commercial Services zone. The
purpose for considering these as conditional uses (C) rather than permitted uses (P) is to
utilize the conditional use review and permitting criteria to adequately evaluate the
compatibility of land uses while also mitigating for any potential impacts to the site,
surrounding properties, uses, or adjacent roadways.

The nature of big-box types of construction and uses are very similar to industrial
warehouses in their sizing, scale, uses, and functionality, while also being very land
consumptive with large parking areas that sit vacant for a significant portion of the
day/night. The one primary difference will be volumes of traffic. These types of uses
have a need for clear and accessible semi-truck loading/unloading areas, wide drive
aisles, outdoor storage, and significant amounts of shipping (both in/out). They are not
conducive to pedestrian or bike traffic and create very large "void" areas that are difficult
and/or uncomfortable to move through unless in a vehicle. Other than the amount of retail
traffic, the typical big box establishment is a large warehouse. In addition, the
transportation system of Logan's west side has seen significant improvements in the road

network, and by pushing new big-box types of activities further west, it will be possible
to shift and disperse some of the traffic demand off Main Street.

STAFF RECOMMENDATION AND SUMMARY
The proposed changes to the Land Development Code would expand the range of uses in
the Industrial zone to include financial institutions, general sales/service, and big-box
types of sales & service. The nature of these amendments provides additional flexibility
in locating additional land uses on the west side of Logan in order to move large,
warehouse types of projects off Main Street along with the traffic generated by it. Staff
would recommend that the Planning Commission forwarded a recommendation of
approval to the Council for their consideration.

GENERAL PLAN
The Land Development Code was prepared and adopted to implement the vision
expressed in the General Plan. The proposed amendments are minor in nature and are
consistent with the General Plan.

Councilmember A. Anderson asked what some of the points of discussion were.

Planner Holley replied that Croshaw and Goodlander voted in favor of the proposal,
while Dickinson and Lewis voted against it. The concern is about banks, savings, and
loans, or credit unions being built in this location. There was a bit of a concern that if an

Mr. Holley responded that banks are often located within a grocery store. If the bank is
not affiliated with the grocery store, the bank institution must acquire a separate business
license, and go through the land use table of authorization.

Consideration of a proposed resolution approving a Telecommunications Franchise
Agreement between the City of Logan and First Digital Telecom, LLC – Resolution
21-29 – Bill Young, City Engineer

City Engineer Bill Young addressed the Council regarding the proposed resolution. The
franchise agreement is between the City and First Digital Telecom, LLC. First Digital is a
fiber-based carrier in the Western United States providing a full suite of business services
including local voice, long-distance internet, DOIP, and CO-locations. They began work
in the City, before realizing that they did not possess a franchise agreement with the City.
The franchise agreement was drafted thanks to the efforts of the City Attorneys. Assistant
City Attorney Craig Carlston and Mr. Young congregated prior to the Council and will
make one final edit to article 9.2 dealing with indemnification. As previously mentioned
First Digital commenced work without a franchise agreement and the edit will cover the
previous work which has already begun.

Mayor Daines inquired if Mr. Young inspects that the areas are suitably repaired by First
Digital as they have compromised portions of the newly installed sidewalks.

Mr. Young replied that the inspectors will inspect these area's and most of the time
contractors do not need to be reminded of due diligence.

Mayor Daines asked if First Digital needed to post a bond.

Mr. Young responded that with a right-of-way permit in place, they can only charge a
$500 deposit on every right-of-way permit. It was done in such a manner so that the
permit is accessible to residents, businesses, and other entities.

Mayor Daines stated that a telecommunication company boring through the entire City is
different. She asked if the Council was interested in creating an ordinance to address such
an issue for the future. The Council voiced their agreement in favor of such a proposed
ordinance.

Consideration of a proposed resolution approving Elected Official’s Wages FY
2021-2022 – Resolution 21-31 – Ambrie Darley

Human Resource Director Ambrie Darley addressed the Council regarding the proposed
resolution which is to increase elected official wages for the Mayor and Councilmembers
by 3%.

Councilmember A. Anderson recalled that on average across the State, the average salary
of a council member is $17,000 or more. Logan City is still far under what the State
average is at this time.

Miss. Darley responded that for approximately ten years, the City Councilmember’s and
Mayor did not accept any increase in wages. The proposal now is to increase these
wages to the State average. She is aware that most of the time the Mayor and
Councilmember's are serving the community and not for compensation. However, there is
the possibility that in the future, someone will seek out the position as a job rather than a
service. It would be appropriate to compensate them for their time.

Mr. Anderson interjected that the idea may be uncomfortable to the Council and Mayor
to approve their own wage increase and henceforth that is why they are doing so in their
stead. However, it is important to increase wages incrementally each year rather than
having to wait until they are $30,000 per councilmen behind the State average. There is
also the concern that no community member may wish to dedicate their time to public
service without compensation. Furthermore, the same is applicable to the Mayor's role
which requires that good people are willing to run and serve in office. It may be
uncomfortable, but it allows for the next Councilmember and Mayor that will take office
the ability to be properly compensated.

Councilmember A. Anderson asked for a public clarification on car allowances.

Miss. Darley explained that the Council and Mayor travel for training and meetings
including meeting with citizens, so they receive compensation for their vehicle

Councilmember A. Anderson agreed that for the Council it is about service, but for
lower-income members of the community, it may not be a possibility without additional
compensation despite their desire and interest to serve.
