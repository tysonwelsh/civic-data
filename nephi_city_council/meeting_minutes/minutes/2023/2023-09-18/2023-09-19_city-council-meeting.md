PUBLIC COMMENT:

 Trinity Evans addressed the council and said that she has been a lifetime resident of Nephi except for a
short time when she moved to Moroni. She sold her home in Moroni and moved back to Nephi because
they realized Nephi was home and she has a great love for the community. She purchased the home
where she was raised and has torn down the home and is building a new home. She asked the Nephi
City Council to change the code and allow a homeowner who is building a home to live in an RV on the
site during construction of the new home. Her understanding was the code only allowed one dwelling
unit is allowed per lot. Her reasons for requesting the code change is that she can’t sign a long-time
rental agreement that all landlords want. She stated that she is currently living in a RV Park, and it is not
a desirable place to live with children. She stated that she could save money if she was allowed to live in
her RV on her own lot where the new home is being built and could help with the project and keep an
eye on tools and construction materials.

  Councilor Memmott thanked Andrea Holm for the condition of the gun range. He stated it is clean
and well-kept and he had noticed new targets.

REZONED REQUEST FROM MR. PEAK DEVELOPMENT FOR NORTH RIDGE PLAT E TABLED:

   City Administrator Seth Atkinson updated the council that Mr. Terry Messersmith owner of Mr. Peak
Development had approached the planning staff with a proposal to rezone his property located at
approximately 1475 N and Main Street from HC1 (Highway Commercial 1) zone to a R2-8 (Residential 2-
8) zone for the purpose of creating eight (8) units in four (4) residential twin homes.
  Mr. Atkinson stated that he had spoken to Mr. Messersmith earlier in the day and Mr. Messersmith
requested that the Council table this request for 30 days. He said he would like a chance to speak with
staff more about the issue and to try to market this piece of land more for commercial use.
  Councilor Memmott moved to table the rezone request. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The
motion passed on a unanimous vote.

CR CIRCLE 3 FINAL PLAT APPROVED:

   Mr. Atkinson updated the council that the CR Circle 3 plat is simply a boundary line adjustment
between two subdivisions that affected four total lots, including Lot 60 and 61 of the Winn Ridge
Subdivision, and Lots 1 & 2 of the CR Circle 2 subdivision. No new lots are being created, some property
that is currently in Lot 2 of the CR Circle 2 Subdivision is being added to Lots 60 and 61 of Winn Ridge
Subdivision, increasing the size of those two lots and decreasing the single Lot 2.
   Councilor Memmott moved to approve the final plat of CR Circle 3 with the boundary line
adjustments in Winn Ridge. Councilor Pardy seconded the motion. The motion carried unanimously.

DESERT STREAM REZONE REQUEST APPROVED:

   Mr. Atkinson updated the council that a request had been received by the Planning staff to rezone the
parcel behind the meat pack plant that is being constructed, from HC-1 (Highway Commercial) to ID-3 or
Light Industrial. This area was originally zoned HC-1 because it was unclear what types of businesses
would locate in the area but commercial businesses seemed to be a good fit with access to Highway 132.
   Desert Stream’s business could fit into the light industry and warehouse, storage use categories of the
Highway Commercial zone, staff felt that the ID-3 zone fit best since there will be a manufacturing
component to the business. The parcel is located south of the meat processing plant and is not directly
connected to Highway 132. Planning Commission had reviewed the rezone request at their last meeting
and recommended approval.
   Resident Jan Reed asked if the parcel had been annexed into the City. Mr. Atkinson answered yes that
it had been annexed as part of the Bar W annexation.
    Councilor Parady moved to rezone the Desert Stream property from HC-1 to ID-3. Councilor
Callaway seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

RESOLUTION 09-09-2023 MASTER FEE RESOLUTION-WATER CONSTRUCTION FEE ADDED APPROVED:

   Mr. Atkinson reported that in the last City Council meeting staff presented a recommendation for a
fee on construction water. Currently many builders rig the water lines to access water during the
construction of a home or commercial building. The city is not being compensated for this water being
used. In addition, when the water department is called to install the meter during the build, they often
need to remove the rigged water lines to install the meter. The City Council opted to table the item to
ensure that the pricing is commensurate with the water usage.

 Public Works Director Kyle Marchant stated that the staff took a deeper look at this fee, it was based
on the base water rate, on temporary rates. The temporary base water rate is $49/month and home
                                                                                              Page 2 of 4
builds can take between 4-8 months typically. The construction water fee was also meant to pay for
staff time to remove the rigged water lines to install the meter. Based on these costs, staff feels that the
construction water fee is justified at $200/permit. On a commercial build, even if a builder is metering
water on a hydrant, staff will still need to manually read the meter for as long as the build takes. In this
case the construction water fee would pay for the staff time to do these manual reads.
  Mr. Atkinson stated that If the Council still feels that the proposed fee is too high as a fee on the
permit, it could be reduced to what the Council feel would be acceptable. If the fee is reduced, the
council should bear in mind that any water usage or staff time that is not covered by the construction
water fee would be subsidized by existing water users.
  Terry Messersmith commented that $200 was very excessive and said that at some point the
homebuilders and developers are assessed fees to death and that developers can’t afford to develop.
Mr. Atkinson said that the Council should ask themselves who they want to pay for development-
existing rate payers of the developers.
   Councilor Worwood motioned to approve Master Fee Resolution 09-19-2023. Councilor Ostler
seconded the motion. The motion passed on the following vote:
