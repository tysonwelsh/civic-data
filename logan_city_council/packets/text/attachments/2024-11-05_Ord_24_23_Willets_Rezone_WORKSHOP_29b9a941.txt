Meeting of October 24, 2024


Logan City Council Chambers (290 N 100 W Logan, UT 84321) www.loganutah.org

Minutes of the meeting for the Logan City Planning Commission convened in regular session on
Thursday, October 24, 2024. Commissioner Guth called the meeting to order at 5:30 p.m.

Commissioners Present: Jordy Guth, David Lewis, Eldon Peterson, Sarah McNamara

Commissioners Excused: Sara Doutre, Ken Heare, Jessica Lucero

Staff Present: Russ Holley, Aimee Egbert, Mike DeSimone, Mohamed Abdullahi, Amanda Pearce,
Jeannie Simmonds (City Council)

Approval of the Agenda: Project PC 24-043 was removed from the consent agenda and placed uder the
public hearing section of the agenda for further discussion.

PC 24-041 Willets Rezone [Zone Change] Jed Willets is requesting a zone change of a .24-acre
property located in the southwest corner of the Royal Point Subdivision at 1354 North 220 West from
Mixed Residential Low (MR-12) to Mixed Residential Medium (MR-20) to add a four-plex unit; TIN 05-
041-0056 (Bridger Neighborhood).

Staff: Russ Holley, Planner reviewed the request to rezone lot six of the Royal Pointe Subdivision from
MR-12 to MR-20. Russ explained the history of the zoning in the area.

Commission: Peterson asked if this zone change was approved, if the entire subdivision would be
upzoned. R. Holley said, yes the entire subdivision gets rezoned with a zone change.
Guth asked if as it is zoned now they could put a duplex on it. Russ explained as of now in the MR-12
zone this subdivision is already over density. The property was upzoned twice to MR-20 for the applicant
to then build the duplex with the understanding that the City would down zone the subdivision back to MR-
12.

Proponent: Jed Willets reviewed the time frames with the first and second zone change approvals.

Public: Jeannie Simmonds, City Council said she would like to see a timeline showing when permits were
issued and when they expired.

Leann Wilkins, who owns a home on 200 West near the proposed project, complained about the weeds
that grow on the property and explained her frustration with the City and the proponent and is opposed to
the rezone request.

Staff: Darren Farar, City Engineer said the proponent has had ample time to complete the conditions for a
duplex.

Motion: Commissioner Peterson made a motion to forward a recommendation of denial to the
Municipal Council on a zone change for PC 24-041 Willets Rezone with the findings for denial as listed
below. Commissioner McNamara seconded the motion.

[page 5]

FINDINGS FOR DENIAL
        1.  The Logan City FLUP identifies the area as MR. The surrounding areas are zoned MR-12 and
            this property should also be zoned MR-12 for consistency.
        2.  The Bridger Neighborhood was specifically downzoned to reduce the number new multi-family
            developments in order to better balance housing stock in this neighborhood as compared to
            other Logan City neighborhoods.
        3.  The MR-12 zone would prevent future multi-family development as this 1.80 acre lot is already
            above the maximum density of 12 units per acre.

RECOMMENDATION
Staff recommends that the Planning Commission recommend denial to the Municipal Council
for a rezone of approximately 1.80 acres of property located at approximately 1360 North 200
West (TIN# 05-041-0056) from Mixed Residential Low (MR-12) to Mixed Residential Medium
(MR-20).

Background
In 1992, the Logan City Planning Commission approved the six (6) lot Royal Point Subdivision
located south of 1400 North between 200 and 400 West. Each lot was approximately 1.30 to
1.80 acres in size. Lot #6 is 1.80 acres and the location of this request. In 1992, the zoning was
R4, which allowed multi-family at a rate of one unit per every 6,000 square feet (SF) and an
additional 1,000 SF for each subsequent unit (4-plexs would require 9,000 SF). Over the course
of two (2) years, seven 4-plexs were built on Lot #6. In 1995, after the seventh fourplex was
built, the owners illegally subdivided lot #6 into eight tax identification numbers (TIN) at the
county and never received city permits or approvals for this action. The remainder eighth area
(0.24-ac.) was subsequently sold off and is now the subject property for this request. In 2000
zoning changed to MFH-14 and the project became "legally existing nonconforming" because
lot#6 (the city recognized 1.80-acre building lot) sits at 15.5 units-per-acre with the existing
seven permitted four-plex's. In 2013 zoning changed to MR-12, and the project remains
nonconforming. Since 2000, zoning has been based on a units-per-acre density rather than the
6,000 SF plus 1,000 SF formula.

The current applicant (Jed Willets) purchased this remainder property (illegal building lot)
thinking he could build a duplex because it was zoned MR-12, and it was a 0.24-acre property
size. Logan City decided to rezone the 1.80 acre building lot to MR-20 in 2016 to allow for a
duplex to be built. This same applicant received a Design Review Permit for a duplex but never
pulled a building permit and allowed this entitlement to expire. Again in 2023, the City upzoned
the 1.80 acres to MR-20 and issued a new Design Review Permit for the duplex. Again, this
same applicant let this entitlement expire in March of 2024 without obtaining a building permit.

This request is from the same applicant; however, this request is an upzone to build a four-plex
rather than the duplex. If up-zoned to MR-20, the 1.80 acre building lot could support 36
dwelling units max. With seven existing four-plex's (28 units existing), that could allow for a new
12-plex.

GENERAL PLAN
The Future Land Use Plan (FLUP) identifies this area as Mixed Residential. It does not
distinguish between medium and high. As part of the revised Land Development Code (LDC)
and Zoning Map update in February of 2011, the area was zoned Mixed Residential Medium
(MRM) rather than Mixed Residential High (MRH) because this area was over saturated with
multi-family structures exceeding the balance of housing options outlined in the General Plan.

Numerous Bridger Neighborhood meetings and public comments from the Logan City School
District has indicated a desire to down-zone developable areas to get more low density
detached single family homes for better neighborhood balance and longer-term occupancy in a
neighborhood that struggles with transiency. There have been several down zones in the area
and new housing developments in the neighborhood over the last several years have been
predominately single-family homes (Quayle Meadows, Lloyd Estates, Cottonwood Estates).

LAND DEVELOPMENT CODE
The Land Development Code (LDC) allows for up to 20 residential units per acre of land in the
MR-20 zone. The MR-20 zoning district permits a range of multi-family residential structures.
The current zoning designation of MR-12 allows for similar structures but at a lower density.

SUMMARY
Jed Willets originally purchased the property thinking he could build a duplex. After discovering
the illegal subdivision, the City of Logan rezoned the property twice. Once in 2016 and again in
2023, for the applicant to build a duplex. Both times, the applicant failed to build a duplex. The
most recent entitlement was issued in March of 2023. The applicant submitted a duplex building
permit application just days prior to the one-year (March 2024) deadline of that entitlement.
Over the last six months, since March of 2024, the applicant did not complete the building
permit plans as instructed and pay the fee for final building permit issuance. The Logan City
Building Department cancelled the building permit application after six months and the failure of
the applicant's part to finalize and pay the fee, in September of 2024. A typical duplex building
permit application is finalized and issued with fees paid in 2-4 weeks.

This application is requesting an upzone for a different reason now, the applicant is seeking to
build a four-plex, not a duplex. A four-plex was never discussed or contemplated on this
property and in this situation. Staff is recommending denial of this rezone because the Bridger
Neighborhood has deliberately made numerous and consistent efforts to downzone remaining
developable areas into single family zones, to better balance out the housing stock that
currently has a much higher concentration of multi-family as compared to other Logan City
neighborhoods. Going from a duplex to a fourplex is contrary to this neighborhood goal.
