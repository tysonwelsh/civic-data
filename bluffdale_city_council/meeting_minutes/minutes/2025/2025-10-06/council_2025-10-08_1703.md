and then accrue additional Paid Time Off (“PTO”). The PTO buyback was also increased.

Mayor Hall asked how it was determined that Bluffdale was not competitive with other fire
departments. Mr. Swingle stated that he and the Bluffdale Fire Department conducted additional
research. Mr. Kartchner added that some candidates mentioned that Bluffdale’s offer was different
from nearby fire departments. The City’s research discovered that the 48-hour work shift creates
hardships, and accruals were not in line with schedules. Firefighters have a 29/12 hour year,
whereas other City employees have a 28/80 hour year, and the accruals did not properly reflect
that difference. The amendment corrected that issue and made the City competitive with the rest
of Salt Lake County, although Bluffdale would still be on the low end of competitive.
Council Member Lord asked if the changes would make the City competitive enough.
Mr. Kartchner reported that the Fire Department was very pleased with the final amendment.

Bluffdale City Employee Policies & Procedures Manual Related to Fire Fighter Leave.

Council Member Lord-Yes. The motion passed unanimously.

7.2 Ordinance _2025-25_— An Ordinance Amending Encroachment Permit

Requirements. (Staff Presenter, Fred Donaldson & Michael Fazio)
City Attorney, Fred Donaldson, reported that an Encroachment Permit is required for work in a
City right-of-way and the applicant must have insurance coverage and submit a bond. The
proposed changes would remove the requirement for contractual liability coverage. That provision
had created some confusion because standard commercial general liability policies typically
include contractual coverage. Bluffdale was the only city Mr. Donaldson was aware of that
included this provision, and his recommendation was to remove it.
City Engineer, Michael Fazio, reported that the ordinance was drafted to address large construction
companies performing utility work in the public right-of-way. However, in reality, many
Encroachment Permits are issued to homeowners who are repairing their driveways or doing other
small projects in the right-of-way. Staff believed that the provision requiring a large amount of

insurance posed an undue burden on these homeowners and proposed that the liability requirement
for minor residential projects be decreased to $100,000. Mr. Donaldson added that most
homeowners’ insurance policies carry a personal liability rider of $100,000 to $500,000 which
would cover this type of project. The current requirement is $2 million.
Council Member Lord stated that his background is in insurance and he believed $100,000 was

other minor items, and a $300,000 limit could still be excessive. However, the minimum could be

City Planner, Caitlyn Tubbs, presented the Staff Report and indicated that the subject property is
3.38 acres at the intersection of 2950 West and 13800 South in the GC-1 Zone. There was an
existing Development Agreement for the Tri-City Center development on the property, which
entitled construction of a multistory professional office building. That project was subsequently
abandoned and the property was listed for sale. The Applicant, CDO Properties, was under
contract on the property and proposed a new Development Agreement to facilitate the construction
of a micro-flex development.
The Concept Plan and renderings were reviewed, indicating three buildings with a total of 46
microflex units ranging in size from 1,100 to 1,500 square feet, for a total project size of
approximately 65,000 square feet. The development was intended for third-party sale. The
building at the intersection of 2950 West and 13800 South would have a retail focus with pedestrian
amenities and additional architectural treatments, including awnings, planters, and benches.
Development Agreements allow applicants to propose specific site development standards. The
Applicant proposed the following deviations from existing GC-1 Zone requirements:

e Corner lot setbacks: 10 feet (GC-1 Zone requires 20 feet)

This deviation was requested to better fit the retail use and be more pedestrian-friendly.

e Rear setbacks: 0 feet (GC-1 Zone requires 20 feet)

The Building Official indicated that there were no life safety concerns with this deviation.

However, all building code requirements must be met.

In response to a question raised by Council Member Lord, Ms. Tubbs stated that no construction
was allowed within the Utah Department of Transportation ("UDOT") easement at the rear of the
