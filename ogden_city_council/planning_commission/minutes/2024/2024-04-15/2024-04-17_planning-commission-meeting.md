      Mr. Rypien recapped the progress in reviewing the “Zone Ogden” process in developing a
      unified development code, indicating the assessment portion has been completed and the
      next few steps are to engage the public with an open house scheduled for April 24th and
      then a kick-off meeting scheduled for June 26th,.

       He reviewed current processes and indicated flow charts would be created for each of the
       specific approval types to better guide development. He reviewed the current code and
       practices of noticing items which are to be discussed at public meetings and identified
       noticing requirements for particular agenda items, indicating that when required, postcards
       are mailed to property owners within a 300’ radius of the project to be discussed and signs
       are placed on the property. He asked the Commission if they felt these practices are
       adequate. Commission discussed the use of signs, stating while they often become
       damaged or lost, they serve a purpose in identifying a particular property. They felt while
       maps on the postcards are helpful, it is often difficult to determine which property is on the
       agenda and a sign better identifies the particular location. Mr. Brierley indicated Staff is
       pursuing the option of lawn signs which are much cheaper and would typically be a single-
       use sign.

       He then reviewed some existing and potential ordinances and asked for input from the
       Commission.
       Building Height in Residential Zones
       Should height be measured by the mid-point of a pitched roof as current standards indicate,
       or to the top of a roof, whether flat or pitched, or by the number of stories.

Multi-Household Dwellings:
These are distinguished by lot size which limits the use:
Lots between 10,000 square feet and two acres are limited to twin homes, duplexes,
fourplexes and attached townhomes with density determined by the underlying zone. Lots
over 2 acres allow those same uses as well as stacked flats in buildings not exceeding 200’
in length. Lots greater than two acres in the MU zone are required to include a commercial
component with the development. Common issues identified with multi-household
buildings are height, density, transition standards, building orientation and building design.
     • Commission felt latitude in regulations would serve to fill in the missing middle
        housing. Commissioner Southwick asked if there is a way to emphasize properties
        be platted so they can have individual ownership. It was felt the building design is
        often more important than the density. Commissioner Safsten questioned the need
        for commercial components in large developments, stating increased density is
        important to encourage commercial uses. Mr. Rypien indicated the amount of
        commercial vs. residential space would be further defined in the General Plan,
        stating changes in traffic patterns alters the need for commercial space. The Plan
        will consider what areas could be supported by housing, transportation and other
        components of the Plan.
     • Commission felt a graduated height or some transition standards between multi-
        family and single-family uses is important but also indicated buffering between
        zones is almost as important as setbacks.
     • It was felt each project should provide its own recreation or open space and not just
        a number of residential units. Gathering spaces in neighborhoods are important

Building Design
What standards create more attractive buildings. Staff suggests standards identifying
building materials, projections and/or recesses and architectural details, including a
covered entry, front-facing door and/or porch, and location of garages.
    • Commissioner Blaisdell felt rear-loaded garages are preferable so long as access
        can be provided, but it was noted this often reduces rear yard play areas. Chair
        Sandau stated garages can face the street if they fit with the architecture of the
        home. Commissioner Southwick stated Ogden has much variety in architectural
        style which adds interest, and felt building material such as brick makes buildings
        more compatible with other uses. He voiced concern about new buildings being
        consistent with existing architecture, suggesting it be changed to compatible rather
        than consistent which would add more variety. He felt many of the older brick
        apartment buildings still exude charm, and felt the newer materials such as stucco
        and metal won’t maintain this type of charm as they age.

Open Space and Recreation
Mr. Rypien indicated some zones require up to 40% open space for multi-family uses and
suggested this could be reduced to 15-20% so long as the open space is functional and
usable for all tenants. He felt better designs for open space would make it more attractive
and used by tenants.
    • Commissioners discussed items which also could count as open space such as
        plazas, exercise rooms, a clubhouse, balconies or patios. Mr. Rypien noted projects
        often define both private open space for individual tenants as well as common open
        space which is usable by all tenants. While a private fenced back yard is useful for
        the tenant, the overall gathering spaces within a project should not be eliminated.
        Chair Sandau suggested a percentage of common open space should be identified.
        Commissioner Southwick noted there is a challenge in creating usable open space
        for individual tenants, particularly if designed as rear-loaded garages. Because of
        the need for access and backing space, the rear yards are asphalt with no play area.
        Mr. Rypien stated the recently revised group dwelling standards allow a front
        fenced-in patio to count towards the percentage of usable open space. He also
        identified grassed side yards as an example of open space which is not functional,
        noting however if widened with a walking path giving access throughout the
        development, it might be counted as more functional open space. Mr. Brierley
        added that indoor recreation space such as a gym, pool or clubhouse would also
        count towards usable open space.
Mr. Rypien there is a need to protect existing trees and these should be designed around if
possible. This would pertain to larger healthy trees and not to trash trees.
    • Commission noted while there have been ordinance standards to protect existing
        trees for some time, it has been difficult to enforce, stating fines are inadequate to
        give incentive for developers to make any effort to preserve trees.

Use of Flag Lots to address infill areas: This concept would allow up to two residential lots
with a single access, with lot area equal to the zone requirement but may reduce setbacks.
Depending on the length of the access road, a turn-around for fire code may be required.
Could these also be allowed on smaller lots? These would be limited to smaller vacant
properties with minimal frontage. Commissioner Southwick preferred this concept over
cluster subdivisions. Commissioner Blaisdell stated there is a similar project in her
neighborhood, and it works well, except for the narrow access which limits the view of
oncoming traffic. Mr. Rypien indicated standards for a minimum access width could be
adopted. He also stated the required rear setback of any existing home must be respected
in identifying the location of common property lines. Chair Sandau noted this is an
instance where the new homes should be compatible with existing homes, stating if the
front home is single-story, those in the flag lot should not be allowed to be 35’ in height.
She felt the new homes should match the height of the surrounding neighborhood.
Discussion continued relating to whether a flag lot might allow more than two homes if
adequate space is available. There also is concern about care and maintenance of the
access road and trash removal as it would be private property. Commission then
discussed the options of subdivisions on a private lane or private right-of-way. This would
be an extension of the flag lot concept but would allow more lots on a deep parcel. Owners
would be responsible for maintenance of the road or right-of-way. Mr. Rypien indicated he
had contacted the fire marshal who indicated lots with more than a 150 access may be
allowed without a turn-around if they were to have fire sprinklers. Commissioner Southwick
favored the concept but felt there should be a written agreement between the interior
property owners to address care of the access road.

Cottage lots were introduced in the R-9 area which allows lots on 1,500 square feet of
property to be created from an existing lot if it has frontage on a public street or alley and
can meet setback standards. Could these also be allowed in other areas?

Established Neighborhoods
Mr. Rypien indicated last year’s legislature adopted a law which prohibits cities from
implementing design standards for new single-family and duplexes unless they are in what
they term as “established neighborhoods”. These are then defined as neighborhoods which
were developed prior to 1950. He identified existing neighborhoods in Ogden which fit this
criteria and stated new construction should be designed to fit with the existing scale, mass
and building materials as the surrounding community. Items such as front porches,
entrances addressing the street serve to make these communities more walkable. Other
features such as eaves, a covered porch and garage at the rear of the property create a
sense of community. He showed slides of existing homes in the East Central community
which are not identical, but are compatible. Commissioner Shale felt the architectural style
also should be compatible with the surrounding community. Mr. Rypien stated the intent is
to identify established neighborhoods and adopt specific building standards for each of
these areas. Mr. Brierley indicated where there are areas just under 50% established prior
to 1950, Staff intends to seek comments from residents relating to whether specific
standard should be established.
