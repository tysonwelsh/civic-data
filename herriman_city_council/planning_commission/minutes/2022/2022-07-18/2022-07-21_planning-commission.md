21, 2022 at 6:00 p.m. in the Herriman City Council Chambers, 5355 West Herriman Main Street,

the City’s website, and delivered to members of the Council, media, and interested citizens.

Economic Development Coordinator Sandra Llewellyn shared that Herriman City currently had 195
commercial businesses, 120 home occupations with customers and 680 home occupations without
customers. Current projects included two commercial businesses, a bike shop and a salon, for Herriman
Town Center. A restaurant may be interested in building in the town center, dependent upon local
alcohol restrictions. The Auto Mall currently had a boat dealer, a new car dealer and two used car
dealers under contract and a hotel has expressed interest in the area. The 13400 and Mountain View
Corridor development received interest from a fitness center, a large major retailer, and a sporting
goods store. A hospital expressed interest in moving to the City and has toured possible locations. Over
thirty construction businesses were either under construction or working with a developer. Including
Slackwater Pizzeria, La Fountain, Einstein Bagel, Crepe Cookie, Panda Express, and Game Haven, among
others. In the Auto Mall commercial center along 12600 South a Crust Club, Quench it, and Seven
Brothers Burgers were under construction. At Mountain Point retail near Redemption a Cowboy Donut,
Beans and Brews and Arctic Circle were being constructed. Construction was also pending in the Teton
Commercial for a car wash/gas station. Staff regularly networks with developers regarding proposed
developments and community needs. She also encouraged Commissioners to reach out to staff with any
referrals, suggestions, or recommendations. Commissioner Jacobson suggested Trader Joes.
Commissioners expressed appreciation for the update.

1.4. Discussion of a proposed Master Development Agreement (MDA) for Teton Commercial at
11840 S and 11918 S Mustang Trail Way in the C-2 (Commercial) Zone — Clint Spencer, Planning
Manager

Planning Manager Clint Spencer provided a brief update of modifications to the proposed Teton
Commercial development. He identified pedestrian connections, reviewed parking standards, and
presented the updated elevations. The proposed color board and street design elements including
benches and bike racks were viewed.

The proposed parking ratios included the following:

* General Retail: 4 stalls per 1,000 square feet of retail floor area

* Restaurant — Fast Food with drive-thru: 7 stalls per 1,000 square feet of dining area

* Restaurant — General: 10 stalls per 1,000 square feet of dining area

Developers John and Cory Gust explained more detail behind the parking lot adjustment. They felt with
the cross-access parking agreement in place the restaurant would have adequate parking. lf the
proposed change was rejected the City standard would apply. They explained a similar development
allowed a parking standard of five per thousand across the board. Chair Berbert identified two areas in
other parts of the City, that were constructed with adequate parking and sought assurance these
standards would be adequate. Planning Manager Spencer responded the current policy was based on
national standards, but added staff was more likely to allow a parking reduction in shared parking
scenarios. Developer Cory Gust replied they were basing if off their history and existing standards.
Commissioner Jacobson confirmed that the cross-access parking agreement had no location restrictions.
Developer John Gust emphasized they were very comfortable with the proposed parking standards, and
offered to complete a parking study to verify the recommendation. Commissioners debated ideas for
overflow parking. They confirmed two access points from 11800 South were right in, right out. Applicant
Gust stated the elevations were updated after packet distribution. The latest renditions were not
included in the presentation, but they had been updated based on Commission feedback and were
distributed to the Commission. The developer was reluctant to include glass on the rear elevations but

1.5. Discussion of draft Master Development Agreements proposed by Herriman 73 Partners

LLC for the development of an auto mall and surrounding commercial properties — Michael
Maloy, City Planner

Bruce Baird, with the Auto Mall explained the four different development agreements. The main area
would be known as the Auto Mall Special District. Secondly, Auto Mall North for the fourteen-acre
section which additionally contained flex space. Thirdly, they were no longer pursuing residential in the
crescent section, and would instead allow limited C-2. Fourthly, the Garden Plots area would remain C-
2 with the addition of hotel use. Portions of each MDA would be similar with an added exhibit B with
customized requirements. He further explained that used car franchise dealerships have a more
polished look and would not resemble the dingy used car dealerships of the past. The drafts have been
forwarded to the Commission. City Planner Maloy requested all Commissioners forward feedback and
recommendations to him.

Planning Manager Spencer reviewed the request for a Home Occupation Daycare. Notices were sent

two spaces in front of the garage, as well as a recreational vehicle (RV) pad, for employee parking and

Main Street in the MU-2 (Mixed-Use) zone
Applicant: David George

File Number: C2022-064
Planning Manager Spencer reviewed the request for a new preschool and daycare, which was an
expansion of the applicant’s local business. The site plan included one 7,950 square foot building for the
preschool and the space to add another commercial site. There were twenty-four parking stalls, which
exceeded city requirements. The building was eighteen feet tall and to be finished mostly with brick,
stone, and glass storefronts and entrances. This site had one vehicular access off Main Street. The
location was adjacent to residential homes on the north and east sides of the building and the recreation
center to the west. The building proposal, finishing materials and landscaping plan were reviewed. lf no
waiver was acquired the dumpster would be moved to a different location, as described in the conditions
of approval. The outdoor play area was to be fully fenced.
