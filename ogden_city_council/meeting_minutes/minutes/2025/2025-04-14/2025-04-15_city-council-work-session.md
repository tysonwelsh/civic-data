         The purpose of the work session was to review the agenda for the City Council meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.;
discuss a proposed rezone at 2850 Washington Boulevard; discuss proposed fee amendments for Ogden’s George S. Eccles
Dinosaur Park and the Marshall N. White Community Center; discuss a proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 budget amendment
for the Marshall N. White Community Center; discuss Council business; and hear Council comments.

Agenda Review
       Acting Chair Hyer reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00
p.m.

Proposed Rezone – 2850 Washington Boulevard
          Planning Director Brierley stated Heritage Land Development has submitted a petition to rezone 0.61 acres from CP-3
(Regional Commercial) to R-3 (Multiple-family Residential). The petitioner would like to establish zoning on the property to
allow for condominiums or townhomes that will be subdivided for individual sale. He used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
provide the Council with information about the subject property and surrounding properties and; the Planning Commission
considered the proposal and recommended approval of the rezone to R-3, with a Conditional Overlay (CO) and development
agreement to require the residential units to be platted to allow individual ownership. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety,
see the information packet for the meeting.
          Council member Graf stated that he was contacted by the owner of the adjacent property, who is opening a juice bar that
fronts Washington Boulevard; he bought his property with this development in mind, and he had sufficient space for seven
parking stalls, and he is now concerned about the elimination of those parking spaces due to the zone change and development of
the subject property. Mr. Brierley stated that parking for the subject property and the property in front of it will be carefully
considered when formal development plans are submitted to the City for approval.
          Council member Richey noted that two Planning Commissioners who voted in opposition based their vote on the belief
that the subject property should be combined with property to the north; he asked Mr. Brierley for more information about that.
Mr. Brierley stated that the lot to the north recently came on the market for sale and there was some thought that it would be
beneficial to combine that property with the subject property for a more meaningful development.
          The Council and staff engaged in discussion about whether it is appropriate to change zoning of a property from
commercial to residential, especially for properties located in downtown Ogden; Chair White stated that she is anxious for the
update of the General Plan to be completed and wondered if there would be any harm done by waiting for that process to be
completed. She noted that Planning staff and the Planning Commission have expressed hesitation about using conditional overlay
zoning to address concerns regarding residential development, but the Council has received multiple recommendations of zone
changes with conditional overlays in the past several months. Mr. Brierley stated the applicant is very eager to move forward and
has done some infrastructure work on the property in anticipation of this zone change. Chair White stated she understands the
applicant’s position, but she is very concerned about continuing to approve zone changes with conditional overlay zoning and a
development agreement. Mr. Brierley stated the property owners have been discussing the concept of a residential development on
the property for nearly four years; the Utah Department of Transportation was doing construction work adjacent to the property
and the property owner had to complete their infrastructure improvements while the roadway was open. That is why infrastructure
work has been done in advance of the application being submitted. He stated the Council has no obligation to approve the
application; he noted the Planning Commission was not unanimous in their support of the recommendation that has been offered.
Some felt that this is not an appropriate location for multi-family development. This is a legislative decision for the Council to
make. This led to Council discussion of the input provided by the Planning Commission.
         Acting Chair Hyer stated that the Council has adopted an ordinance limiting residential development on commercial
properties and he is inclined to deny the application until after the General Plan is completed. Council member Myers stated that
the property owner has explored other options for the property, and he feels the current proposal is appropriate; he has been
working on a development concept for nearly four years, and the City only adopted the ordinance regarding residential
development on commercial properties last year. There are not many other viable uses for the property at this point and he feels
that is why the Planning Commission offered a recommendation of approval. Council member Choberka agreed.

Proposed Fee Amendments – Ogden’s George S. Eccles Dinosaur Park
          Dinosaur Park Executive Director Mejeur used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize proposed fee
adjustments at the park; any fee increases will not increase the ity’s revenues but will help the Foundation keep up with rising
costs and to better align with similar providers. Fees for the Dinosaur Park were last updated in 2008 and then again in 2023. The
current proposal is to increase regular admission fees from $8.00 to $12.00 per day. Ms. Mejeur’s presentation offered a
comparison of the Dinosaur Park fees with fees charged by other similar attractions; information regarding current building
projects at the Center; and marketing and tourism data related to the Park. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the
information packet for the meeting.
          Discussion among the Council and Ms. Mejeur centered on the anticipated use of the increased revenue associated with
the fee increase; and the number of daily pass visitors that come to the Park versus season pass visitors. Public Services Executive
Director Anderson noted that the daily pass entry fee has not been increased since 2008 and the increase is commensurate with
cost-of-living increases for the area.

Proposed Fee Amendments – Marshall N. White Community Center
          Public Services Executive Director Anderson used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the proposed fee
structure for the newly rebuilt Marshall N. White Community Center. The fee structure will replace the previous fee structure for
the center, which was last updated in 2015. The new fee structure is based, in part, on the analysis done by the City’s project
consultant as part of the financial operations review. For a copy of the presentation and proposed fee schedule in its entirety, see
the information packet for the meeting.
          Discussion among Mr. Anderson and the Council centered on topics such as the size of group that could rent the full pool
or half of the pool at the Center; allowed uses of the kitchen at the Center; activities allowed on the turf field areas; opportunities
for visitors to apply for reduced fees for entry or use of the facility; and the appropriate timing of review and consideration of
additional adjustments to the fees in the future.
          Mr. Anderson then presented the site plan for the project to orient the Council to the location of the amenities located
throughout the Center and the surrounding property. The Council discussed fencing and public access to the property surrounding
the building and the amenities that have been removed from the property and not replaced as part of the new project.

Proposed Fiscal Year 2024-2025 Budget Amendment – Marshall N. White
Community Center
          Finance Director Sorensen explained the proposed amendment to the Fiscal Year (FY) 2024- 2025 budget recognizes
anticipated revenue from the proposed Marshall N. White Center fee amendments. Based on the proposed fees, it is anticipated
that the new fees will generate $161,625 for the remainder of the current fiscal year. The proposed budget amendment would
recognize this revenue and appropriate the funding in the Public Services Department budget.

Council Business
         Chair White offered additional comments regarding the first item on tonight’s work session agenda, the proposed rezone
of 2850 Washington Boulevard. She does not feel the roadway is wide enough to provide appropriate access to the subject
property, especially if it is developed for residential use. She also wanted to know if each unit will be individually platted. She
concluded she would like to consider a moratorium, or other mechanism for delaying action on any future residential zone
changes, until after the General Plan update project is completed. She would like answers to these questions before taking a formal
vote on the application.


         The meeting adjourned at 5:37 p.m.
