25. ADOPT A RESOLUTION AUTHORIZING THE SEGREGATION, USE AND EXPENDITURE OF
       FORFEITED ASSETS RECEIVED FOR THE LEGAL COST OF CONDUCTING FORFEITURE
       PROCEEDINGS
(Track A, 1:04:30)
Utah County Attorney Jeffrey Buhman mentioned forfeiture of criminal proceeds has become a big legislative
debate in recent years. Most of the debate and opposition surrounds civil forfeitures, which Utah County does not
perform. The Utah County Attorney’s Office uses the criminal justice process when seeking to forfeit property, so
the owner has a ‘proof beyond reasonable doubt’ standard applied and all associated protections the criminal justice
system provides. Mr. Buhman summarized it is a good time to consider and define how the money is used.
Currently, the county receives twenty percent of the cash value of forfeited items towards the General Fund. The
national debate is over whether forfeiture funds should be used towards operations of government, or segregated for
different purposes. Mr. Buhman explained how the State Asset Forfeiture Grant Program allocates the remaining
eighty percent through legislature, and this proposed resolution would mimic how the state utilizes those funds. He
provided background on the previous uses of these monies and alleged abuse, none of which occurred in Utah
County. Commissioners Lee and Graves noted the ability to use forfeited funds for salary payments could be
negatively perceived, and the commissioners discussed with Mr. Buhman how to appropriately determine expenses.
UTAH COUNTY BOARD OF COMMISSIONERS

Utah County Clerk/Auditor Bryan E. Thompson asked about designated forfeiture funds, restrictions, and judicial
authority of the courts. Commissioner Graves asked about the necessity of this resolution, and Jeffrey Buhman
reminded the Board the County Attorney’s Office is already in full compliance with the law; the proposed resolution
is only a request regarding county policy. Utah County Sheriff James O. Tracy also commented on state use of
monies, summarizing the pro-active stance of removing the funds from the general account and only releasing them
upon mutual approval. Commissioner Graves suggested potential beneficial uses, and Mr. Buhman reiterated this
resolution is a way to say, “Utah County does not use forfeiture monies to fund day-to-day operations.”

5.     ADOPT (OR DENY) AN ORDINANCE AMENDING THE UTAH COUNTY GENERAL PLAN, 2014,
       TO ADOPT A RESOURCE MANAGEMENT PLAN FOR PUBLIC LANDS IN UTAH COUNTY
(Track A, 1:28:25)
Commissioner Lee commended Bryce Armstrong and additional staff of the Utah County Community Development
Office for taking on this project with the United States Bureau of Land Management (BLM) and Forest Service. Mr.
Armstrong commented on the process and cooperation with federal partners in identifying objectives, and
Commissioner Graves echoed the gratitude expressed by Commissioner Lee. Shannon Ellsworth of Rural
Community Consultants came forward with Mike Hansen to address how the plan will be incorporated by the BLM,
and Commissioner Lee talked about growth in the county. The group discussed agriculture, water, the National
Environmental Protection Agency, and the need for continuous updates. Commissioner Ivie commented on
environmental impacts and thanked all involved for their data collection and ongoing hard work.

           Commissioner Graves made the motion to adopt the ordinance amending the
           Utah County General Plan, 2014, to adopt a Resource Management Plan for
           Public Lands in Utah County as outlined in Regular Agenda Item No. 5. The
           motion was seconded by Commissioner Ivie and carried with the following vote:
                                                                         AYE: William C. Lee
