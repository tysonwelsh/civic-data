Municipal Redistricting

Council Policy Analyst Watkins summarized the background information regarding the municipal redistricting process.
Every 10 years, after each census, the City Council is responsible for adopting new municipal district boundaries. New municipal
district boundaries must follow the new precinct boundaries—which were established by the County in December 202 1—and
various minor changes will need to be made accordingly. This must be done within six months of the state establishing legislative
boundaries. The latest date to adopt municipal boundaries is March 15, 2022. There are three basic requirements that must be
followed when redistricting:

1. Each district must be substantially equal in population.
2. Each district must be geographically compact (constituents should live as near to one another as possible).
3. Areas within a district must be contiguous (physically adjacent).

Mr. Watkins indicated that three different map options have been created; he presented each map and facilitated review
and discussion of the boundaries on each. Discussion among the Council centered on the similarities of the three maps and the
importance of ensuring fair and adequate representation of all areas of the City. The Council concluded to select maps one and
three as the two preferred options, but that they would like to accept public input regarding this subject before taking final action
on an updated district map. Council Executive Director Eller-Smith stated Council staff can either schedule a fact-finding work
session regarding the topic or allow a question-and-answer public input period during a meeting. She indicated she will work with
Council leadership to determine the most appropriate method for accepting public input.

Proposed Rezone — 25th Street/Porter Avenue

Planning Manager Montgomery explained this rezone request is the first of a two-part process, the second part being an
amendment to the Mixed-Use (MU) zone. Additionally, developments within the MU zone must have development agreements,
so a development agreement will be considered with this rezone petition. This rezone petition was originally considered by the
Planning Commission in 2019, with the Commission recommending approval of the rezone and development agreement. Several
Commission meetings were held, and the original petition changed throughout the process. The original petitioner, Mike Vowles,
proposed building a mixed-use project with townhomes, apartments, and a mixed-use building. In order to do so, it would be

Nine neighbors spoke during the March 6, 2019 Planning Commission meeting in opposition to the rezone.

Discussion among the Council centered on the concerns of the surrounding community and additional project

Council Business

Council Rules and Norms: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
summarize the proposed amendments to the Council’s Rules and Norms as follows:

1. Modify the Regular Meetings section to update meeting schedule.

2. Modify the Regular Meetings section to add Acknowledgements to the Opening Ceremony.

3. Modify the Civility and Decorum section to clarify the reason and purpose for limiting outward expressions of
support or disagreement with a given position or statement.

4. Adding a Signage section to provide guidance for meeting attendees.

She briefly expounded on each of the proposed amendments as follows:

e Regular Meetings - Meeting Schedule Change. In July 2021, the Council reduced the number of regular City
Council meetings from three per month to two per month on a trial basis. There have been no negative impacts
to workflow or other unintended consequences. The proposed amendment to the Regular Meetings section will
formalize these changes for the meeting schedule going forward, acknowledging that the budget review process
may require additional meetings.

e Acknowledgments. The Council office receives many requests to formally recognize individuals, organizations,
events, and other observances. Meeting time constraints and limited staff time make it difficult to grant all such
requests. This additional section would provide space on the agenda to allow Council members to informally
recognize special guests, events, observances or other items of interest to the community.

e Decorum and Civility — Outward Expressions of Support or Disagreement. The proposed language clarifies the
purpose for limiting clapping, booing, and other outward expressions of agreement or disagreement with any
position. The Council wants to ensure all in attendance feel safe and free to express a differing opinion.

e Signage. A proposed new section on Signage outlines the limitations and expectations for those attending
meetings who want to communicate through signage.

Ms. Eller-Smith then facilitated discussion among the Council regarding the proposed revisions; the Council concluded
they are supportive of the amendments and are comfortable proceeding to adoption of the document in a future meeting.
