8. PUBLIC HEARING, CONSIDERATION, AND RECOMMENDATION _on_a

Proposed Text Amendment to Increase the Allowed Lot Coverage in the R-1-43 Zone

from 30% to 35% (Application 2025-31). Justin Anderson, Applicant, Represented

by Casey Jones, Esq. Staff Presenter, Caitlyn Tubbs.
Ms. Tubbs presented the Staff Report and displayed the Zoning Map indicating areas of the City
that are zoned R-1-43 and would be affected by the amendment. The R-1-43 Zone requires a one-
acre minimum lot size, and the current lot coverage maximum is 30%. The Applicant proposed
increasing the maximum lot coverage to 35%. Lot coverage includes the sum total of all buildings
and structures on any parcel but does not include hardscape or flatwork. Ms. Tubbs explained that
a homeowner could choose to pave the majority of their lot and still meet the standard. An increase
in the allowed lot coverage would not affect the City’s stormwater calculations but would allow
5% more living area on properties in the zone. Under existing requirements, the maximum lot
coverage is 13,068 square feet. With 35% lot coverage, that would increase by 2,178 square feet
to 15,246. Staff recommended that the Planning Commission consider the request and forward a
recommendation to the City Council.
In response to a question from Chair Cragun, Ms. Tubbs reported that the Andersons would be
constructing a family home in the Kenna Lane subdivision, and the increase would allow them to
accommodate personal medical uses on-site.
Chair Cragun asked about historical increases. Ms. Tubbs reported that the maximum lot coverage
was increased from 20% to 30% approximately five years previously.

In response to a question raised by Commissioner Flynn, it was clarified that Accessory Dwelling
Units (“ADU”) would be included in the lot coverage calculations. If approved, the Text
Amendment would allow for a larger ADU.
Commissioner Luker asked about maximum coverages in other zones. Ms. Tubbs reported that
cluster zones within the R-1-43 Zone, like Parry Farms and Sage Estates, allow a maximum lot
coverage of 40%. The R-1-10 Zone also allows 40% coverage.
Casey Jones of the Strong and Hanni Law Firm spoke on behalf of the Applicant. The change
would maintain the character of affected neighborhoods, and no changes to setbacks, lot widths,
building heights, etc. were proposed. It would, however, allow more flexibility. The Andersons
have a child with an illness and would like to build a wellness center on the property for their child.
The amendment would also allow for larger ADUs, which would be in alignment with the City’s
Moderate-Income Housing Plan and General Plan. The amendment would also bring Bluffdale
more in line with neighboring cities. Draper and Herriman do not impose lot coverage limits on
one-acre or larger lots, and Riverton and South Jordan restrict coverage to a maximum of 40%.
Chair Cragun opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public hearing
was closed.

Chair Cragun thanked Commissioner Luker for his question regarding maximum coverage in other
zones. More coverage is allowed in other areas, and the proposal was only for 5%. She had no
concerns about allowing the increase. Commissioner Flynn agreed and noted that a larger structure
with less irrigated landscaping was a good thing.

Commissioner Flynn moved to forward a POSITIVE recommendation to the City Council
for the Proposed Text of Section 11.80.010.110 Permissible Lot Coverage to increase the
allowed Lot Coverage in the R-1-43 Zone from 30% to 35%, based on the findings presented
in the Staff Report dated July 12, 2024. The motion was seconded by Commissioner Luker.
Vote on motion: Ulises Flynn-Yes, Kori Luker-Yes, Chair Cragun-Yes. The motion passed
unanimously.

indicated that no improvements were needed.

e The current level of service (“LOS”) is 4.8 acres of park per 1,000 residents. This
was determined by dividing the 92 acres of existing park acreage (excluding the

service level in relation to other communities.

o To maintain that LOS in 2035, 96 acres of parks would be needed, or four

Bluffdale and one community park in southern Bluffdale to help meet LOS and
distribution needs.

additional baseball or softball fields would be needed.

e Park standards were reviewed to determine the types of amenities that would
typically be found in each park category and which City parks meet that standard.

e There are 96.7 acres of Bluffdale-owned open space and 228.9 acres of county-

In response to a question from Chair Cragun, Mr. Crowell indicated that the Geneva property was
not considered in the 2050 projections, but it will likely be part of future development in the City.
Plans are updated every five to 10 years.
Chair Cragun indicated that $82 million is a large figure, but the projection was based on a 25-
year period. She asked if the City could set aside funds each year to achieve that goal or if projects
would be considered on a yearly basis. Mr. Crowell explained that the City does not have a specific
savings account for the parks plan. Capital projects are funded yearly. Master plans are intended
as a guide for the budget, but fire stations and water tanks usually take priority over recreation
items pickleball courts. The plan will make it easier to find grants and identify opportunities for
joint projects with other departments and developers. Ms. Benson noted that Herriman has relied
on developers to build out their paved trail network while the city focuses on unpaved trails.
Commissioner Flynn stated that the City would need to save $3.28 million per year to fully fund
the plan, which is a lot for Bluffdale.
Mr. Crowell stated that the survey was conducted to help understand which projects the community
would prioritize. Funding opportunities can be identified for priority projects. For example, the
City could bond for a swimming pool, but once the actual cost is determined, it could be decided
that it is no longer a priority. Chair Cragun stated that some things, like a recreation center, could
be all or partially self-funding once open. Mr. Crowell indicated that pools are very expensive to
run, but there would be offsetting revenues.
Chair Cragun opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public hearing
was closed.

forwarding a positive recommendation to the City Council. Chair Cragun and Commissioner
Luker agreed. Commissioner Luker indicated that he served on the committee when the plan was
last updated, and it was interesting to see how much things had changed since 2014.
