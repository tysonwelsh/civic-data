   2. PUBLIC HEARING TO CONSIDER & TAKE ACTION ON A REQUEST TO AMEND THE SIDE YARD SETBACKS
      IN THE OGDEN VALLEY DESTINATION AND RECREATION RESORT (DRR-1) ZONE (§104-29) IN THE
      COUNTY UNIFORM LAND USE CODE

         Ronda Kippen, with County Planning, stated that the applicant is requesting to amend the development
         standards from an 8-ft. minimum side yard setback, with a minimum overall side yard setback of 18 ft., to
         a 5-ft. setback with no minimum combination. The Ogden Valley Planning Commission and County
         Planning staff recommended approval. The proposed revisions to the side yard setbacks will bring
         multiple lots into compliance (this area was developed under a PRUD) and the county is working toward
         clustering the developments as much as possible to provide open space. Chair Ebert noted that there was
         mention of the sidewalls being engineered so that they serve as retaining walls, and he asked if this will
         be a requirement when plans are submitted. Ms. Kippen responded that was correct, that the engineering
         firm will be reviewing them to ensure those sidewalls are engineered adequately to withstand the snow
         loads.

H. PUBLIC COMMENTS:

   Sylvia Salsbury, of Ogden, noted that at the last Commission meeting it had been pointed out that there is no
   comma after “nation” in the Pledge of Allegiance and she stated that there is also no comma after “flag.” She
   would like the Pledge to be amended to say something such as, “I pledge allegiance to the constitution of the
   United States of America, and I honor the flag and republic for which it stands, one nation under God,
   indivisible, with liberty and justice for all."

   Rodney Egan, of Uintah Highland, stated that Uintah Highlands is having a disaster—that snowplows are not
   keeping up with the snow on their hillside area. Last night vehicles could not drive up some of their streets.
   People were abandoning their vehicles and walking home. He left message with County Roads at 4 p.m. and
   snowplows did not get to their area until 7:45 p.m. Their roads are down to one lane and there are 5-6 feet of
   snow piled between curb/gutter and property lines. However, the plows are spending time on the flat lands.
   Their area needs help and he asked what could be done; the residents do not know where to turn. He has
   complained before but nothing has happened. The commissioners understand. The plows are working about
   24 hours/day, there has been an inordinate amount of snow, and crews are also dealing with cleaning up
   avalanches, etc. Commissioner Harvey, who also lives in the unincorporated county, noted that it was about
   10:30 p.m. last night when plows came to his area. Commissioner Gibson said that they are hearing from a
   lot of communities and reiterated that the crews are working really hard. Chair Ebert expressed thanks for the
   comments and said that phone calls will be made to express these challenges and hopes to open up better lines
   of communication.
