6. Public Hearing regarding potential amendments to Section 10-2 Zoning Districts 
and Development of the Snyderville Basin Development Code to create 
Neighborhood Mixed Use (NMU-1) Zoning District; Marketplace at Silver Creek, 
applicant – Ray Milliner, Principal Planner  
 
Planner Milliner said the applicant is the Marketplace at Silver Creek Junction.  This is 
located by Home Depot.  They are looking at possible amendments to the Development 
Code.  For the benefit of the public, he gave the background of this application.  In 2016, 
the County Council approved an amendment to the Snyderville Basin General Plan.  At 
that time, future land use maps were created.  Some areas were designated to possibly 
have some mixed use zones.  A discussion took place about creating a mixed use zone.  
In the spring of 2017, a draft copy of the zone was presented to the Planning 
Commission.  No formal action was taken.  This item remained relatively inactive until 
February 2019.   
 
In Feb. 2019, a private citizen applied for a Code amendment to create the NMU zone.  
This application is being processed like any other application.  If it is approved, a new 
zone will be created.  If approved, an applicant would need to apply to rezone their 
property to the NMU zone. There are no properties being proposed to be rezoned at this 
meeting, but if the NMU zone is approved by the County Council, someone could apply.  
Planner Milliner said one of the requirements to apply for the NMU zone is that the 
Snyderville Basin Planning Commission 
August 27, 2019 
Page 16 of 32 
 
property must lie within one of the eight areas designated as mixed use.  Slides were 
shown of the different areas that could be considered for a NMU zone.  The NMU zone 
will be designed to be compact.   
 
One purpose of the NMU zone is to create development that is more focused on 
pedestrian movement and less on the automobile.  Currently, the only option for a 
mixed use zone is the CC zone.  The CC zone is the most liberal of all zones.  Planner 
Milliner said an apartment building could be permitted as a conditional use in the CC 
zone.  Only setbacks and height would be capped.   
 
Planner Milliner said that a rezone would be processed as a master planned 
development.  These applications would be thoroughly reviewed by Staff, the Planning 
Commission, the public, and the County Council.  There would be a requirement for 
25% open space.  The type of open space would be determined as it proceeds through 
the Master Plan Development process.  
 
It also creates incentives to allow increased density if additional affordable housing or 
restricted parking is part of the project.  A 60’ height limit is being proposed.  The 
performance standards would include things like open space, connectivity, and the 
other usual standards.  Parking requirements would be 5 spaces per 1,000 square feet.  
Architecture and sustainability would be considered.

1. Discussion regarding amendments to the Snyderville Basin Development Code for 
a possible creation of a Master Plan development Process- Ray Milliner, Principal 
Planner  
 
Planner Milliner said the Master Planned Development process is intended to replace 
the SPA process.  The idea is to apply this process to any of five outlined scenarios: 
1. A rezone 
2. An application for a density bonus, including an application for the NMU zone 
3. A residential project with “x” amount of lots.  (The number is yet to be 
determined.)   
4. A hotel lodging project with “x” amount of residential units (This number is yet 
to be determined.) 
5. A commercial project with 10,000 square feet  
Director Putt said this is a new process.  It could be thought of as a replacement for the 
specially planned area (SPA), but it will be more predictable.  The first key difference is 
Snyderville Basin Planning Commission 
August 27, 2019 
Page 29 of 32 
 
how this process starts.  There would be a pre-application meeting with the property 
owner and their development team.  Staff will explain how the process is going to work 
and then listen to what the project consists of.  It is information sharing upfront.   
 
Director Putt said a public pre-application meeting would then be held in a public 
setting.  This could be thought of as a pre-work session.  At that meeting, the developer 
presents a concept plan.  It will be a first look for both the public and the Planning 
Commission.  At this meeting, the basic nature of their proposal will be presented.  The 
only action taken by the Planning Commission is a determination if the application is 
generally consistent with the General Plan.  If it is not, what needs to change to allow 
the application to move forward?   
 
Director Putt said one of the Commission’s tools will be the future land use map.  Is the 
proposed use consistent with its purpose?  Is it consistent with the community design, 
open space, transportation, and housing?  If there is a need to change the plan, it is best 
for Staff and the developer to know that early on in the process.   
 
Director Putt noted that this process was built into the Park City development process 
over 20 years ago.  It has worked well.  One reason is because the public understands 
early on what the nature of the project is.   
 
Commissioner Fine asked how many questions will be gone over with the applicant 
before it comes to the public meeting.  Director Putt said there are 14 conclusions of law 
associated with the Master Planned Development.  They are the targets the applicant 
has to hit.  He reviewed the 14 conclusions of law.  Most likely, these will be altered 
before this process is completed.    
 
Director Putt said a unique requirement will be a design narrative.  This is a written 
statement with a presentation to the Planning Commission.  The applicant will explain 
why the project is designed the way it is.   This explanation will consist of things like the
