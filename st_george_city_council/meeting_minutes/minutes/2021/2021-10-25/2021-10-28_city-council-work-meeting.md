     invocation was offered by Laura Fairchild with the Baha’I Community and The Pledge

      The Council moved into the RDA meeting; after the meeting, they moved back into
      the City Council work meeting.

DISCUSSION REGARDING THE PROPOSED PERFORMING ARTS CENTER:
     Members of the committee for the proposed performing arts center introduced
     themselves.

      Alyce Stevens Gardner with the Southwest Symphony provided a PowerPoint
      presentation covering the following topics: Background information; Case for
      support; Economic Impacts; Performing Arts Center of Southern Utah; Mid-Valley
      Performing Arts Center; Adjacencies; Capital investment; Programming plan; Site
      analysis; Renderings; Governance models; and Summary: Keys to Success. She
      read a statement regarding the arts from Andy Cohen. The committee hopes they
      can work with the City to make the performing arts center a reality.

      Mayor Randall mentioned she and City Manager Adam Lenhard met with the
      committee recently. She has always been concerned with spending money for a
      performing arts center that may only be used by a couple of the arts organizations.
      Tuacahn is really excited about this proposal because they can bring in shows to the
      performing arts center throughout the year to help keep year-round use.

      After being asked by Councilmember Curtis, Ms. Gardner explained the $45 million
      included land acquisition; however, they did not factor in the cost of inflation.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard mentioned the proposed cost does not include parking.
      It will need to be addressed as parking will be approximately $23,000 per stall.

      Ms. Gardner noted the center can utilize the parking areas already in place. She is
      not worried too worried about parking.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard advised the parking around the proposed site is private;
      therefore, parking will need to be built and there will be an expense. He noted that
      Isaac Barlow with Tech Ridge was present. Mr. Barlow has the contract to takedown
      the property at Tech Ridge.

      Lowry Snow noted there is a possibility that the City could be involved with a
      donation of property at Tech Ridge.

      Ms. Gardner commented the area was recommended during the feasibility study.

      Mr. Snow explained this proposal promotes what is being done at Tech Ridge also.
      Some of these improvements could become part of what Mr. Barlow is trying to
      accomplish.

      Councilmember McArthur asked if there has there been a model for operation.

      Ms. Gardner stated a full pro forma has been completed by Webb Development. She
      commented that the center is estimated to break even at year one and that the
      model is very sustainable.

      Councilmember Larkin commented that art enhances lives. She believes this facility
      would be amazing at Tech Ridge; however, looking at Abravanel Hall, it is not shared
      by many organizations. At the end of the day, there are so many hours and so much
      space for everyone who would like to use the facility. She asked who will be able to
      use the facility as one of her fears is that another center would be needed to
      accommodate everyone.

      Lucas Darger with the Southwest Symphony explained Mr. Webb spoke to those in
      the arts community while doing the study. Scheduling will be challenging; however,
      there are only a number of organizations that would use that many seats. This
      model allows for a smaller section on the main level with tiers above. Turning the
      lights off on the tiers makes it seem as though there is a full house when only 500
      seats are sold. This would allow opening the facility to the smaller organizations.
      One of the studies outlines the groups that Mr. Webb spoke with.

      Ms. Gardner stated that touring arts groups can also utilize the center. The pro
      forma takes into account national averages all of the different groups that could
      perform at the center. They could have a large variety of uses, keeping the center
      busy.

      Mr. Snow noted Councilmember Larkin’s question underscores what the need is. He
      hopes there is a discussion that makes the center expandible.

      Councilmember Larkin explained she asked the questions because there is such a
      demand and who gets to choose their dates first.

      Mr. Snow commented that it will also be determined by who the partners are. He
      wants to make certain that those that make the investment are at the table to help
      make these decisions.

      Ms. Gardner commented that the Mid-Valley facility has art pieces hanging in the
      lobby; therefore, visual arts can have a home also.

      Community Arts Manager Emily Reed noted the Electric Theater is full for the entire
      year; the demand is there. The Theater supports approximately six organizations;
      however, they are still able to accommodate several organizations outside of the
      City.

      Cheryl Snow, Chairman of the Performing Arts Center of Southern Utah, strengthen
      you can bring in from the outside depends on how many seats are in the auditorium.

      Jeri Rhodes, President of the Heritage Choir spoke about costs. Her hope is that
      community organizations would benefit by having a facility that would be affordable
      for them.

      Mr. Snow stated that they have met with County. A statement by Commissioner
      Iverson indicated that he feels there is a need to do this and to figure things out. He
      believes the County is willing to work with the City to accomplish this proposal. He
      then spoke about how to get the funding needed and noted he has been in touch
      with Jason Burningham and are ready to retain him as their financial advisor. At this
      time, the committee is not looking at any commitment, other than that the Council is
      prepared to engage with all parties involved to find a model that is workable. He
      noted the 2019 survey done with citizens showed they want this here.

      Mayor Randall stated she is happy to sit at the table and speak to the County.

      Councilmember McArthur noted behind the times with where the city should be at
      with performing arts.

      Mayor Randall asked Councilmembers McArthur and Larkin if they would be City
      liaisons on this project. Both agreed to do so. She and the Council thanked the
      committee for their time.
