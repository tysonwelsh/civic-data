MOTION: Motion by Commissioner Almquist to Open the Public Hearing. Motion
seconded by Commissioner Iverson and carried by unanimous vote, with
Commissioners Snow, Almquist and Iverson voting aye.

Commissioner Snow explained the purpose of the bond is to renovate and update the interior of
the Boulevard Building.

Commissioner Iverson specified the amount of debt that Washington County currently retains,
including general obligation bonds on the libraries scheduled to be paid off in 2026 and bonds on
the Senior Centers that will be paid entirely in 2024, He also mentioned the bond on the
Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV) building that is due to be paid off in 2036 with the lease on
the DMV building paying the entirety of the bond.

Alex Barton questioned if demolishing the Boulevard Building would be a more cost effective way
to house the courts. Commissioner Almquist explained that the Boulevard Building could be
retrofitted for less than what it could be rebuilt for, He further clarified that leasing and remodeling
the Boulevard Building is the most fiscally responsible action for both the County and the Federal
Courts.

Quinn Denning asked what square footage would be leased to the Federal Courts versus how much
is being kept for the Justice Court. County Administrator Nicholle Felshaw answered the building
is Just over 45,000 square feet with the Federal Courts occupying approximately 26,000 square
fect and the Justice Court occupying the remainder.

Larry Meyers thanked the Commissioners for being fiscally responsible, He asked if this is the
best time to bond or if it would be better in the future and questioned how the current
Administration building would be used. Commissioner Almquist stated that the current
Administration building's future is uncertain, however, the requirement for Washington County to
have a Justice Court will always there and the need to have a safe building is of foremost concern.

Larry Mulcock questioned if the current Administration Building would be able to be retrofitted
for the courts. Commissioner Iverson stated this building is not large enough to hold the courts.
Commissioner Snow clarified that the current Administration Building is too small by two thirds
and would be much more expensive to remodel.

Dan Cooper challenged the idea of taking on new debt and stated Washington County should get
rid of any existing debt before taking on anything new.

April Pinkston stated that more debt should not be taken on with an uncertain future and the
reasoning behind remodeling a building simply due to creaking floors. Commissioner Almquist
replied that safety is a large concern for the courts and the rooms are small and cramped and not
ideal for court dealings. He stated that Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) concerns are
another concern that remodeling will address.

Commissioner Iverson related the story of Sheriff Pulsipher being stuck in an clevator with an
inmate in the Boulevard Building. He stated if there were no tenants, County funds would be used
to upgrade the building, however, with tenants the cost is mitigated.

Commissioner Snow clarified that the Boulevard Building is structurally unsound and unsafe and
will need many upgrades regardless.

Patricia Kent questioned using the Children’s Museum as a Courthouse, Commissioner Iverson
clarified it was not the Children’s Museum, but the old Courthouse and stated that St. George City
owns that building. Ms. Kent further questioned buying or trading buildings with St. George City.
Commissioner Snow stated that St. George City has other plans for that building and all those
options have been discussed with the city.

Bruce McWelch queried why the building would be renovated for a tenant. Commissioner
Almquist specified that has already been worked into the lease agreement.

Dana McCabe questioned why the amount of money allocated is $20,000,000 if the remodel will
not cost that much. Commissioner Iverson replied that $20,000,000 is a cap where there is an
unknown amount of money needed for renovations. Commissioner Almquist clarified that the only
money that would be needed for renovation would be borrowed.

Quinn Denning questioned if any preliminary design and costing have been done. Commissioner
Almquist answered that has been done. County Administrator Nicholle Felshaw responded that no
schematics would be until agreements have been reached,

Jake Burningham queried why all the building is happening which is why he feels there is
resistance in the community.

Betty Hackett questioned why bond issues are not on a ballot for citizens to vote on. Commissioner
Iverson clarified that general obligation bonds are generally on the ballot while revenue bonds
such as this are not,

Steve Hirschi asked why the County feels the need to compete with the public sector with rent. He
further questioned if once the courts outgrow the space if a new tenant search will need to happen.
Mr. Hirschi asked how much has been spent on this project already, Ms. Felshaw stated the only
thing that has money spent on it is the seismic study, which is necessary regardless for a total of
$28,500. Mr. Hirschi questioned why not sell the building as is. Commissioner Iverson clarified
that Washington County currently rents buildings only to federal or state agencies. Commissioner
Iverson also stated that the County's current position is to retain the land for future use.
Commissioner Snow reminded the public that the County is required to hold a Justice Court and
would have to have a building regardless.

Bruce MeWelch related a story of large building projects that then crushed the town and relayed
the hope that this is not what will happen in Washington County,

Erica Hodges questioned if the Commission should get a twenty percent vote in order to get a
bond, Deputy County Attorney Devin Snow stated that is the current process per state code and
that a vote will not happen tonight,

Laura Bean questioned if Ms. Hodges’s suggestion is against state code, Mr. Snow clarified that
the time lines are so tight that an election would not be practical or feasible,

Dana McCabe asked what the largest bond Washington County has taken on previous to this.
Commissioner Iverson stated that the Quail Creek Bond of $40-$50 million bond was the largest
bond based on the size of the County.
April Pinkston stated that she had trouble understanding the public hearing announcement.
