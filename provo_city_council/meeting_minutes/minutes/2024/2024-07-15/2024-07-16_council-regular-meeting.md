2.         A presentation recognizing Provo City for receiving the GFOA Distinguished Budget
           Presentation Award for FY2024 (24-008) 0:10:45

Mr. Borget announced that a panel of independent reviewers has awarded Provo City the Distinguished
Budget Presentation Award from the Government Finance Officers Association (GFOA) for the city's
annual budget document for the period ending July 2023. This award is the highest form of recognition
in governmental budgeting, marking a significant achievement. He credited Kelsey Zarbock and the
budget and finance teams for their major role in securing this honor. Provo has submitted its budget
report for several years and consistently received this award. Mr. Borget noted that the feedback from
the review is both positive and constructive, helping the city continually improve its budget
presentation. He emphasized that this recognition provides valuable information to both residents and
the council, enhancing their understanding of the budget process. Mr. Borget also shared the city’s
intention to earn the award again in 2024, despite the lengthy review process that takes about a year to
complete.

3.         An Acknowledgement of Provo City being honored with the "Best Partnership Award" at
           the 2024 Utah IT Excellence Awards (24-008) 0:12:30

Mr. Borget presented details about the Strengthening Utah's IT Backbone project, which began in 2023
through a partnership between Salt Lake City CIO Aaron Bentley and Provo's IT Director Josh Ihrig. This
collaboration led to the formation of the Utah Local Government IT Leaders Group, now consisting of
over 40 governmental entities across the state. The group holds quarterly meetings to discuss important
topics like cybersecurity, AI policy, IT mutual aid agreements, and changes in law, such as new privacy
regulations. He said one of the group's key initiatives is the IT mutual aid agreement, allowing member
cities to support each other during crises, such as cyberattacks or IT infrastructure failures, like how
power agencies helped Provo during a windstorm years ago. The initiative has improved cross-
jurisdictional coordination, streamlined IT policy implementation, and enhanced service delivery by
sharing knowledge and resources. The group's use of digital collaboration tools has helped reduce
redundancy and speed up project timelines. Mr. Borget highlighted the significance of the award
received by Provo and Salt Lake City, recognizing the group's success in uniting stakeholders from
various levels of government, including city, county, state, and federal agencies like CISA, the FBI,
Homeland Security, and Utah State. He praised Mr. Ihrig for playing a crucial role in the group's efforts
and congratulated him and his team for this significant achievement.

5.         A presentation regarding the Quarterly Report for the 3rd Quarter of Fiscal 2024 (24-018)
           0:37:34

Mr. Borget provided an overview of Provo City's financial performance for the third quarter ending
March 31, 2024. The quarterly report offers an unaudited summary of the city's revenues and expenses,
comparing them to the previous year and including a five-year comparison for each fund. He noted that
although revenues are slightly ahead, the city is projected to be about $2 million short due primarily to a
decrease in sales tax revenue. Despite this, savings from unfilled positions and operating cuts have set
aside funds to cover the shortfall, ensuring there will not be a decrease in the fund balance. Borget
shared that while sales tax revenue is 1% above last year, it has not increased as anticipated. He also
discussed other revenue streams, indicating that building permit revenues are tracking slightly below
budget, and parks and recreation fees are up from last year but also slightly below budget. Franchise
fees are on target, and justice court fees are performing well. He provided an update on expenditures,
mentioning transfers to the airport and a market adjustment for firefighters. The library currently shows
a net income of $502,000 for the quarter but is expected to end the year around $580,000 in the
negative due to the timing of property tax receipts. He also reported that while the golf course is
showing a slight loss, it is expected to be positive by year-end. The water fund is below budget, due to
weather variability, while the wastewater fund is on track. He noted strong growth in the airport's
revenues, with $3.8 million year-to-date, nearing its annual budget target. Sanitation and storm drain
revenues are performing well, like prior years.

9.         An ordinance amending Provo City Code to make corrections and updates related to Cross
           Connection Control and Backflow Prevention (24-066) 1:16:48

     Motion:       An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2024-36, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Emilly Guerrero, Cross Connection Control Coordinator, presented. She said the code was established
years ago following a state mandate requiring water purveyors to maintain a functioning cross-
connection control program. A key element of this program is an authority statement, typically an
ordinance or code, which enables the water purveyor to eliminate or require protection for all cross-
connections. She mentioned that the program has been running for several years, and now they aim to
update the code to better meet state standards and align with program needs. She said the department
utilized a template provided by Rural Water, which has been adopted by several cities in Utah, and
customized it for Provo’s specific requirements. Ms. Guerrero provided the council with a copy of the
proposed program and highlighted some key updates to the code.

Chair MacKay opened the item for public comment. With no comments or council discussion, she called
for a vote.
