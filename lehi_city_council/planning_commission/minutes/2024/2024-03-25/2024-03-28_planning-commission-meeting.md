2.2) Public hearing and consideration of Ryan Wilson’s request for conditional use
approval for an 8-foot fence at 1455 North Sierra Way.
Commissioner Beau Jones joined the meeting.

Jacob Curtis presented information on the item.

The applicant, Ryan Wilson, was present. He stated this would help cut down on the wind.

Commissioner Vice Chair Everett opened the public hearing.

Joe Caudell left an online comment which was submitted into the record.
“I am submitting this comment to oppose the requested conditional use permit. My home is
located at 1448 N Sierra Way, on the eastern side of Sierra Way, across the street from the home
in question and slightly to the southeast.

I would first note that I am in agreement with the DRC redline comments from Kim and Gary. I
can attest to the fact that the grading is very slight, nothing unusual at all. I can also attest to the
fact that the wind in this neighborhood is significantly less than anything I experienced during
the five years that I owned and lived in a home in the Canyon Hills neighborhood of northeast
Lehi - in that neighborhood I was unaware of any 8 foot privacy fencing, and certainly none on
any corner lots.

As support for opposition, I do believe that pursuant to Chapter 9, Section 09.050.A.2 of the Lehi
City Development Code, the proposed conditional use would be detrimental to the safety of
persons and properties within the vicinity of the proposed conditional use. The home in question
is on a corner lot right at the intersection of 4100 W, the main entrance to the community, a main
N-S arterial street 1470 N, as well as Sierra Way. As you can see in the first picture of the
application (Page 20), if an 8 foot fence were placed, it would significantly obstruct the lines of
sight for vehicular and pedestrian traffic flowing to or away from 4100 W, 1470 N, and in
particular Sierra Way.

I would also note that having lived in two other fairly large planned unit developments in North
Salt Lake and Lehi, I don't believe it is common at all to permit 8 foot high privacy fences in
PUDs per standard CC&Rs and ancillary rules and regulations issued pursuant to CC&Rs - as
Edge Homes is still the sole declarant governing the CC&Rs, and the development is in its early
stages, no uniform and consistent fencing standards have been published by Edge Homes. It is
very likely that at some future date restrictions will be placed on fence height, and so allowing
this would create an inconsistent design standard in the PUD as the fence would likely be
grandfathered by the HOA.

Lastly, if this conditional use permit were issued, it is not unlikely that other neighbors tying in
to the 8 foot fence will want to maintain a consistent look - the result would be 8 foot fencing
through the homes bounded by the west side of Sierra Way, the south side of 1470 N between
Linen Way and Sierra Way, the east side of Linen Way, and the north side of a to-be-built street
between Linen Way and Sierra Way. If such a precedent were to be set, similar vehicular and
pedestrian obstruction previously mentioned would follow on each subsequent corner.

Taking into account that the rationales proffered by the applicant for a conditional use permit are
not justified, the detriment to safety previously described, the unusual nature of an 8 foot fence in
a PUD, and the concerning precedent this would create, compounding the expressed safety
concerns, I believe these factors make clear that the conditional use permit should be denied.

Thank you for your time and consideration. Joe Caudell”

Commission Vice Chair Everett closed the public hearing.

The item was deliberated by the Commissioners. Craig Chambers emphasized the necessity for
the commissioners to provide valid reasons why it wouldn't be allowed. Brad Kenison pointed
out that the site triangle is neither visible nor obstructed.

        Motion: Commissioner Eyre moved to grant approval Ryan Wilson’s request for
        conditional use approval for an 8-foot fence at 1455 North Sierra Way; including all
        DRC comments and findings stated in the report, note the request is because of privacy
        and note that the site triangle is not obstructed and that this would be an 8-foot fence for
        the entire perimeter. Commissioner Jones seconded.

        Vote: Commissioner Everett, no; Commissioner Eyre, yes; Commissioner Lockhart, no;
        Commissioner Kunze, yes; Commissioner Jones, yes. The motion passed 3 – 2.

        Craig Chambers stated he has spoken to the Planners and the City is looking to switch the
        8 foot fence from a conditional use to an exception.

2.3) Public hearing and recommendation of BCP Developments request for review of the
BCP Property General Plan Amendment on 14.67 acres of property located at
approximately 1200 West 1450 South, changing the land use designation from VLDRA

(very low density residential) with the Pioneer Village Mixed Use Overlay to LDR (low
density residential).
Jacob Curtis presented information on the item.

The applicant, Tony Trane, was present. He stated the lots and R1 flex allow for flexibility and
trail connection through that zone. The recommendation at the time was to come up with balance
in the area, and this was the solution at the time. Mr. Trane wanted to create a ribbon road
through the park. There will be a variety of lot sized on the south end of this zone.

Commission Vice Chair Everett opened public hearing,

Larry Hadfield, who owns adjacent property, wishes to commend Tony for easing the overlay
situation. However, he expresses apprehension regarding the traffic congestion on 1100 West,
particularly with the presence of schools and churches making it difficult to exit his driveway.
He also voices concerns about future traffic on the road due to the planned park. He believes that
the village overlay does not harmonize with the neighborhood and suggests exploring the
possibility of relocating the design to Pioneer Crossing.

Kenneth Glade thanked the developer. The neighborhood opposed the overlay. There were no
rules or restrictions in place. He noted that no one had taken the time or consideration to review
the build-out. The definitions surrounding it were unclear. Glade believed the village overlay
was excessive, a sentiment echoed by Condie and Albrecht. Another concern was Pioneer
Crossing, which was under study for an expressway, potentially turning all neighborhoods into
feeder roads and impacting schools and churches. Glade predicted that 1100 West would bear the
brunt of the congestion, particularly due to the constant traffic from STRATA Networks,
Maverick, and a storage facility. With numerous unresolved issues, he urged the removal and
resolution of the overlay. Glade questioned the effectiveness of transitioning to an LDR (Low
Density Residential) and worried about the lack of protection it might offer against future
changes. He requested a firm, current decision to eliminate the overlay and recommended a
negative recommendation be given.

Paul Benz stated that from 2300 West to Millpond, the situation remained consistent. However,
he expressed concern about the subsequent item involving the movement of heavy commercial
activity into neighborhoods. He observed that each GPA or overlay was being approved without
consideration for the opinions of residential neighborhoods, leading to inconsistency. Mr. Benz
described it as a slippery slope and expressed his concerns regarding it.

Eve Brown mentioned that she had relocated from Montana. She inquired whether Lehi aimed to
attract mature families or promote high-density housing. She observed that the available space
was gradually diminishing.

Jessica Telford expressed concern about the traffic on 1100 West. She failed to see how more
homes could be constructed, and she voiced her opposition.

Randy Mellor is opposed to the overlay and stated the traffic is horrendous.

Elizabeth Ott wanted to state changing the overlay is irresponsible until any divisions with Utah
Lake and Pioneer Crossing have been taken care of.
Brent Huffer opposed the change and echoed concerns about traffic. He agreed that there could
be more density but emphasized the need for solutions to address the traffic issues.

?? Brown, who lived close to 1100 West, expressed that the traffic was terrible and emphasized
the need to address the existing traffic issues before considering any additions to it.

Jonathan Maelin expressed that it didn't feel like a General Plan. He advocated for taking a step
back to reevaluate the overlay and make more sense of it before making any further decisions.
He also mentioned that when STRATA was being established, there was discussion about the
need for a traffic study to be conducted.

Jacob Curtis also addressed the online comments which are submitted into the record.

Tori Savage
“We are strongly against the heavy commercial zoning as it poses a threat to the safety of the
nearby school and the amount of traffic that is already over capacitated for that area.”

Michelle Christensen
“This area is already too congested and there’s an elementary school right there making it much
more dangerous for little kids. This area needs to be maintained a very low density zoning. Stop
cramming everything into one area. It’s creating a huge danger.”

Brittney McHenry
“I am opposed to changing the zoning here. The traffic is already very dangerous for our kids to
get to Dry Creek. Dry creek is also overcrowded and there is zero parking at the school.”

Commission Vice Chair Everett closed the hearing.

Tony Trane asserted that the process involved in altering the general plan was underway and
reassured that solutions would be sought. He confirmed that a traffic study would be part of the
process, incorporating numerous ideas. He expressed the belief that implementing the ribbon
road would enhance safety. Simplification was now a priority. He clarified that there was no
immediate intention to develop; rather, the aim was solely to modify the overlay. Tony
mentioned the intention to discuss rerouting buses with the school district (ASD). Additionally,
he suggested that the ribbon road could potentially traverse the park near 1700 West.

Commissioner Jones inquired about the completion of a traffic study. Mike West confirmed that
one had not been conducted. Tony mentioned the necessity of creating a stub road and informed
that work on the Pony Express was in progress. He added that a traffic study would address
traffic concerns when the full development occurred. Commissioner Everett remarked that the
absence of a concept plan made it difficult to consider the proposal.

Mike West emphasized the importance of having amenities near residents and accessible by
walking and biking. He acknowledged the numerous uncertainties regarding connectivity. Brad
Kenison commented on their awareness of the traffic issues. Maverick informed that they were
implementing a no left-hand turn policy and were in the process of installing a median. Pioneer
Crossing and UDOT adjusted signal timings to prioritize East and West traffic, inadvertently
affecting timings on 1100 West. An application through MAG for Pony Express Parkway had
been approved and would soon be available. Additionally, the Parkside plan has received
approval.

        Motion: Commissioner Kunze moved to send a positive recommendation of BCP
        Developments request for review of the BCP Property General Plan Amendment on
        14.67 acres of property located at approximately 1200 West 1450 South, changing
        the land use designation from VLDRA (very low density residential) with the
        Pioneer Village Mixed Use Overlay to LDR (low density residential); including all
        DRC comments and findings stated in the report. Commissioner Eyre seconded.

        Discussion of the motion: Commission Vice Chair Everett disagreed and does not
        believe it fits the land use. Commissioner Jones agreed with the Vice Chair.

        Vote: Commissioner Everett, no; Commissioner Eyre, yes; Commissioner Lockhart, yes;
        Commissioner Kunze, yes; Commissioner Jones, no. The motion passed 3 - 2.

2.9) Public hearing and recommendation of Lehi City’s request for review of a Development
Code Amendment to Chapter 23- Signs, to include property address signs as being exempt
from the sign regulations.
Jacob Curtis presented information on the item.

Commission Vice Chair Everett opened the public hearing.

Cameron Spencer of Flagship Homes expressed his appreciation for the discussion that evening.
He emphasized the importance of limiting notations to letters, not just numbers, to ensure
legibility as a builder. Cameron expressed his desire to comment on the GPA and highlighted the
need for builders to have a space to collaborate. He pointed out that creativity isn't always
factored into market shifts, advocating for building homes that match the lot size and
emphasizing that LDRs sometimes turn into dreams that are unattainable for people. Cameron
expressed a desire to see a transition towards balance and thanked the commission for their
cohesive efforts.

Commission Vice Chair Everett closed the public hearing.

        Motion: Commissioner Lockhart moved to table Lehi City’s request for review of a
        Development Code Amendment to Chapter 23- Signs, to include property address
        signs as being exempt from the sign regulations; Until further clarifications can be
        made, consider simplifying regulations for wall size, wayfinding, and location
        requirements. Focus on maximum sizes rather than open-ended specifications. Including
        all DRC comments and findings stated in the report. Commissioner Kunze seconded.

        Vote: Commissioner Everett, yes; Commissioner Eyre, yes; Commissioner Lockhart,
        yes; Commissioner Kunze, yes. The motion passed unanimously.
