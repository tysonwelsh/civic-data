PUBLIC COMMENT PERIOD
Donna Martin expressed concern regarding the recent 62% property tax increase. She
explained that she is 78 years old, works full-time to cover living expenses, and the increase
presents a significant financial hardship for her and others in her condo community. Ms.
Martin noted that her property was assessed at $309,200, while multiple real estate sites
(Zillow, Trulia, Realtor.com) listed values between $294,000–$303,000, which she
believes indicates overvaluation. She also referenced a nearby, larger, updated property
that recently sold for under $300,000. She emphasized that the tax increase not only
impacts residents now but will continue to burden them in the future. On behalf of herself
and her neighbors, she requested that the Council reconsider the extent of the property tax
increase.

Jim Vesock raised concerns about selective enforcement of city code, stating he has
evidence that enforcement is inconsistent. He requested the adoption of a clear standard
operating procedure to ensure uniform enforcement, noting that he and others have been
requesting this for over five years with many issues still unresolved. Mr. Vesock also
announced the upcoming Northeast Granger “My Hometown” Fall Festival, an event
typically attended by over 2,000 people. He invited Council members to attend, noting
the event will feature food, including international dishes, and highlighted a video created
by the youth council promoting the event.


PUBLIC HEARINGS
A.   ACCEPT PUBLIC INPUT REGARDING THE BUDGET OF WEST
     VALLEY CITY FOR THE FISCAL YEAR 2025-2026
     Mayor Lang informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Special Council
     Meeting scheduled August 19, 2025 in order for the City Council to hear and
     consider public comments regarding Input regarding the Budget of West Valley City
     for the Fiscal Year 2025-2026.

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

               West Valley City will adopt a final budget that will be made available for
               public inspection during Special office hours in the City’s Recorder’s office
               and gave notice of a hearing to receive public comment before the final
               adoption of this tentative budget for FY 2025-2026.

       Jim Welch explained that the special meeting date was set by the county to avoid
       conflicts with other entities holding property tax hearings. He provided an overview
       of the City’s $170 million budget, with most operations funded through the general
       fund (police, fire, public works, administrative services).

 Key points included:
    • Revenue Sources: 69% from sales and property taxes; 31% from utility
       taxes, permits, court fines, interest, and reserves.
    • Fiscal Responsibility: Budget must be balanced, meet state requirements for
       reserves, and follow Government Finance Officers Association standards
       for transparency.
    • Property Valuations: Determined by the county, not the City. Residents may
       protest valuations through a formal process if they believe assessments are
       too high.
    • 2026 Budget Impact: Operations change of $2.9 million (7.7% increase in
       City’s portion; 1.7% of the overall property tax bill).
    • Judgment Levy: About $50,000 in one-time refunds to businesses from
       prior appeals.
    • Use of Increase: Required disclosure (per Senate Bill 29) designates that
       additional funds will go toward public safety services.
    • Public Information Tools: The City website allows residents to input home
       values to estimate tax impact and see distribution of their tax bill (about
       22% goes to the City).

 Mr. Welch concluded by noting that the county’s consolidated notice outlines all
 entities with tax increases this fiscal year.

 Mayor Lang opened the Public Hearing.

 Julie Jacobs, a resident of a gated condo community, expressed concern regarding
 the diverse population growth and the perceived increase of undocumented
 immigrants in the city. She questioned whether property tax revenues are being
 used to support or shield undocumented residents and asked if West Valley City is
 considered a sanctuary city.

 Ifo Pili, City Manager, emphasized that the proposed property tax increase is
 primarily designated for public safety services and to address the general rise in
 operational costs of running the city. He responded to concerns raised during the
 public comment period by assuring residents that West Valley City does not fund
 or operate any programs intended to shield undocumented immigrants. He also
 addressed the confusion regarding a referenced 62 percent property tax increase,
 explaining that the figure does not accurately reflect the City’s portion of the
 adjustment. He asked Finance Director Jim Welch to provide clarification to help
 avoid misunderstandings.

 Jim Welch acknowledged that while the property tax increase feels significant,

 much of the change is due to higher property valuations set by the county. He
 explained that residents who believe their property has been overvalued can utilize
 the county’s appeals process, which he has successfully done himself by submitting
 documentation of comparable sales. He noted that increases in property taxes are
 not solely from the City but also from other taxing entities such as school districts
 and special service areas. For example, Granite School District’s increase this year
 averages about $100 per household, while Canyons School District and Midvale
 City will see increases of approximately $70 and $67 respectively. He emphasized
 that although West Valley residents are not affected by all entities, these
 adjustments accumulate over time, making it important for property owners to
 review their tax notices closely. Jim reiterated that property valuations are the
 largest factor in the increase and encouraged residents to file protests with solid
 documentation if they disagree with their assessments. He concluded by
 highlighting that only about 22 percent of a property tax bill goes to West Valley
 City and explained how those funds are allocated, with a significant portion
 dedicated to police and fire services. He noted that this breakdown is available on
 the City’s website for public review.

 Ifo Pili addressed a question regarding whether West Valley City is a sanctuary
 city. He clarified that the Council has never taken any action or passed an ordinance
 or resolution designating the City as such. He further assured that, as City Manager,
 no policies have been implemented to limit cooperation with federal agencies.
 While he has directed police officers that they are not to act as ICE, he emphasized
 that West Valley City is not a sanctuary city and has no policy, resolution, or
 directive restricting federal cooperation.

 Ms. Martin expressed that the proposed tax increase would place significant
 financial strain on her household. She explained that she already faces rising costs
 from utilities, groceries, and HOA fees, and noted that her property is in poor
 condition, making its assessed value unrealistic. She emphasized that these added
 expenses could force her to seek additional employment or risk facing
 homelessness again, a situation she fought to overcome in the past. She urged the
 Council to consider the hardship residents like her face when making decisions.

 Jim Welch responded to Ms. Martin’s concerns by noting that resources are
 available to assist residents with financial hardships. He mentioned the county’s
 circuit breaker program for low-income households and encouraged citizens to
 explore other available assistance programs. Jim also clarified that if a resident
 missed the deadline for protesting their property valuation, it may still be possible
 to address the issue by contacting the county. He emphasized that the City has no
 role in determining property values or assessments, as all calculations are handled
 by the county.

 Jim Vesock acknowledged that while the increase is unwelcome, most of the
 property tax bill is attributable to external entities, such as school districts, rather
 than West Valley City. He explained that the City’s portion of the property tax is
 relatively small and emphasized the challenges of managing West Valley’s 35
 square miles with available resources. He noted that past deferred maintenance and
 unpaid obligations have created unavoidable costs, and the City must address them
 to maintain services and facilities. While he expressed dissatisfaction with the
 necessity of raising property taxes, he stated that the increases are currently
 essential to fund the City’s ongoing operations.

 Mayor Lang closed the Public Hearing.
