HB 374 “UPD Bill”

                Mayor Clayton stated this has been a hot topic and he wanted to keep the Council
up to speed on what has transpired so far. He wrote a letter to Representative Jordan Teuscher,
the bill’s sponsor, expressing his opposition to the bill. The bill has subsequently been pulled and

                                                 2
DATE            WEDNESDAY                                 FEBRUARY                         15, 2023

substituted with a bill that would dissolve UPD in two years. It passed in the House Judiciary
Committee and will be going onto the floor. Representatives from UPD, the Sheriff’s Association,
and the labor union spoke. Mayor Kelly Bush, Kearns, spoke in opposition arguing this would
affect Kearns’ bid for the Olympics. Mayor Clayton argued this would be bad for morale and many
officers will probably resign and work elsewhere now. It is hard in the current climate to get people
to work in law enforcement and officers need support. Though there was much opposition, he
felt Sheriff Rivera’s testimony and reluctant acquiescence to the bill carried the day.

                Council Member Stitzer stated her recommendation was to explore other options
now in anticipation of this bill passing. There will be officer staffing issues. She opposed parts of
the bill but could not say she opposed it completely.

              Mayor Clayton stated he has spoken with representatives from Riverton and
South Jordan and Copperton’s rates would likely double if it withdrew from SLVESLA. He asked
Nathan Bracken if the Township could levy its own taxes if it were to withdraw from SLVLESA.

               Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, stated the Township did not have the authority
to levy taxes outside SLVLESA. If HB 374 were to pass, the easiest thing to do, from a legal
standpoint, would be to have SLVLESA remain as-is and execute a contract with the County for
services. There are considerations he would like to go through in closed session as to not
undermine negotiations.

            Mayor Clayton stated he would like to create an infographic that speaks to the
money. He disagreed with the double taxation narrative and would like to see some data and
draw some comparisons.

               Later in the meeting, after reopening from closed session, Mayor Clayton read a
proposed official statement into the record:

                  Copperton is concerned about HB 374 as written because it would dissolve UPD
before a replacement law enforcement model is created. Copperton, however, could support the
bill if it were amended to remove the deadline on UPD’s dissolution and to provide adequate time
for Copperton and other interested parties to develop a mutually acceptable law enforcement
model.

               Council Member Stitzer, seconded by Council Member Bailey, moved to officially
adopt the statement. The motion passed unanimously.

              Council Member Stitzer, seconded by Council Member Olsen, motioned to direct
Council Member Olsen to cast a vote of “neutral” if given the opportunity to vote to remove Sheriff
Rosie Rivera as the CEO of UPD.

               -Park

               Council Member Stitzer stated Phase 1 of the park restoration was complete. An
appropriation of $20,000 has been secured for the park.

               Mayor Clayton stated the money was in the Capital Improvement fund.

              Council Member Stitzer stated there had been discussion on energy-efficient
ways to manage the park and she felt it would be good to move on that. The park is a big asset
to Copperton. She would also like to see ownership of the park finally transferred from the County
to Copperton.

              Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, stated his negotiations with the District Attorney
have indicated the reversionary clause is non-negotiable for both parties, which has led to an
impasse. He did not understand why this was a deal breaker for the County.

               Council Member Bailey and Council Member Stitzer agreed to work on the park
issue.

               -Fiber Internet

              Council Member Stitzer stated she would like to continue to investigate fiber
internet. She would like any approval of fiber optic internet to be non-exclusive.

              Mayor Clayton stated Copperton is close to being able to get access to Google
Fiber. He agreed to look into it along with Council Member Severson.

               - Storm Drain Project

               Council Member Stitzer stated she would like to move forward with this. Rio Tinto
would like to consult regarding surrounding areas.

               Council Member Olsen agreed to take the lead on this.

               -Annexation Continuation

               Council Member Stitzer agreed to stay involved, along with Mayor Clayton.

               -Public Engagement / Website

               Council Member Stitzer stated she would like to manage the website.

             Mayor Clayton stated he took the website down because he was being
bombarded with complaints it was not ADA accessible.

              Kayla Mauldin, Planner, Municipal Services District, stated Maridene Alexander
would be able to help Copperton with the accessibility issues.
