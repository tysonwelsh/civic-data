E. AWARD PRESENTATION TO SWANSON FOUNDATION.
   Chuck Swanson, Tami Swanson and Cindy Swanson Purcell were present. County Sheriff Terry Thompson
   emphasized that the Swanson Family Foundation has been a tremendous friend in this community for many years and
   in many levels, including assisting the Sheriff’s Office/law enforcement family for many years. At considerable
   expense they constructed, planned and dedicated a unique fire arms training complex, the Swanson Tactical Training
   Facility, now known as the Weber County Sports Shooting Complex, that provides an environment previously
   unavailable to law enforcement in northern Utah and includes a unique, successful village façade for law enforcement
   training that is used by state and federal agencies, which is an economic boom to Weber County. This could not have
   happened without the Foundation. At considerable sacrifice, they graciously offered the benefit of this range for public
   use through a minimal monetary purchase agreement with Weber County. He recognized the Foundation with an
   Honorary Sheriff Award that spoke to the Foundation being a major contributor to community projects over many
   years and recognized them for their significant contribution to public and law enforcement. Mr. Swanson, on behalf
   of the board of directors, expressed thanks for the honor and Ms. Purcell said it was a fun experience. The
   commissioners expressed thanks to the Swansons’ indefatigable assistance to the community.

 11. CONTRACT WITH LSI BUSINESS DEVELOPMENT, INC., TO ASSIST IN INTERGENERATIONAL POVERTY (IGP) EFFORTS.
      Commissioner Ebert noted that about six years ago the State passed legislation on how to address poverty locally
      but with a State-wide view. The State had collected data for four years but nothing happened at a county level until
      Weber County took the lead and the responsibility to move it forward. There is no model; Weber County is creating
      it. Our county started holding meetings, created an intergenerational welfare reform commission, an advisory
      committee, and a comprehensive plan to address the issue holistically and longitudinally. Other counties started
      seeing this opportunity. He said that the initiative goes beyond our State, it is a national movement. He feels that
      working with LSI, a large company with capable professionals/expertise, will bring drastic and tremendous
      change—poverty affects every facet of our lives. Commissioner Gibson outlined his concerns with the contract,
      which will be amended as follows: regarding measurables/deliverables, this language will added to the first bullet
      point, “including the creation and implementation of a pilot program by January 1, 2019 and submitting the
      documentation for the program by the State’s deadline;” the contract term will be corrected so that it may renew
      up to two times, one year each; and regarding keeping to the $100/hour rate presented in the procurement process,
      a requirement will be added for LSI to track its hours and to provide that to the county at the quarterly reports.
      Commissioner Ebert stated that there may have been an RFP per hour figure but, for this specific agreement, it is
      lined out not in hours to be worked but in returns. The county only had $60,000 for this contract, which is well
      below the market for the highly specific work that the county is requiring. The county backed away from an hourly
      rate because it could price itself out of the market. The pilot program and plan that the county submitted to the State
      has the items that Commissioner Gibson is asking for. Commissioner Ebert is very comfortable with this first
      contract phase. The State has 5 and 10-year goals and the county mirrored those goals in its plan. Chair Harvey
      noted that the per hour amount was not in this contract revision.
      Public comments: Jim Johnston asked if the contract could be put off to another meeting so that he could meet with
      the pertinent group. He volunteered more time in this subject area in various boards than worked in his entire career.
      Commissioner Ebert noted that the changes to the contract are not substantial and just create more clarity.
      Commissioner Gibson moved to approve the contract with LSI Business Development, Inc., to assist in
      intergenerational poverty efforts with the following changes: adding to the first bullet point, “including the creation
      and implementation of a pilot program by January 1, 2019 and submitting the documentation to the State for the
      program by the State’s deadline;” that the term may be renewed up to two times for one year each; and that LSI will
      track the hours spent for work done under this agreement and will provide that information to the county during its
      quarterly reports; Commissioner Ebert seconded.
      Commissioner Ebert – aye; Commissioner Gibson – aye; Chair Harvey – aye

  12. RATIFY A CONTRACT WITH AMPEDARTS FOR A CURATOR TO BIMONTHLY FIND ART EXHIBITS FOR THE
      WEBER CENTER MAIN ENTRY HALL.
       Commissioner Ebert stated that over the last several years the county has worked on a systemic approach to
       strengthen our community. He noted that one current area of major focus is downtown Ogden urbanization and he
       outlined some projects by the City, the creation of more RDAs for downtown investment incentives, etc. He said
       that in an urban core there is a strong need to have cultural opportunity/experience and that it plays a major role in
       revitalization of an area. Ogden City has invested tremendously in this in the past decade, including the art stroll
       and recently the creation of a downtown arts district. A couple of years ago the county desired to be a part of this
       and to partner with the City. As part of this effort, the county has created in the foyer of the Weber Center one of
       the stops for the art stroll, and Scott Patria, of AmpedARTS, was initially working with artists and bringing art to
       the Weber Center for free but realized that there are some associated costs. The contract formalizes this action.
       Chair Harvey appreciates that the county participates in various roles to help perpetuate growth and to bring all
       facets of our community forward.
       Commissioner Gibson noted that the contract summary sheet showed the cost of $450/month but the contract body
       showed $475/month and there was discussion to change the contract amount. However, subsequent to this meeting
       it was discovered that the invoices have been paid for $475/month, as stated in the contract. Commissioner Gibson
       supports this great idea but felt strongly that this could be done on a voluntary basis and mentioned a city that found
       a local volunteer to coordinate local art for its building. He expressed concerns noting that this item is a ratification,
       which meant there had been some discussion and the fact that there has been payment on this already, with two
       commissioners signing off on it, but that he had to scramble to get information on this before the agenda because
       he did not know that the county was paying someone to display the art, the amount of the contract, etc., and felt this
       was inappropriate. Commissioner Ebert apologized for not passing the information along more quickly stating that
       the level of funding had been kept to a minimum to see if there was a value additive.
       Commissioner Ebert moved to ratify the contract with AmpedARTS for a curator to bimonthly find art exhibits for
       the Weber Center main entry hall; Chair Harvey seconded.
       Commissioner Ebert – aye; Commissioner Gibson – nay; Chair Harvey – aye
