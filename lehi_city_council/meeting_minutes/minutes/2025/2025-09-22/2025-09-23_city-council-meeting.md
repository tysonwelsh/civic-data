2.1) Discussion of Parking at Lehi High School.

The Council discussed the ongoing parking challenges at Lehi High School, which recently lost
178 parking stalls when the local church stopped allowing student parking. City officials
explored several potential solutions, including utilizing a grassy UDOT-owned area north of the
school for additional parking. Still, there were concerns with feasibility, and the school district
would be responsible for the improvements. Mayor Johnson expressed frustration, noting the city
has often been asked to help solve the school's parking problems. The Council agreed that while
they could explore temporary solutions like utilizing the UDOT property and allowing the school
district to improve it, the ultimate resolution would likely require the school district to purchase
additional land for a permanent parking solution. The city was cautious about immediately
changing municipal parking codes, preferring to first exhaust alternative parking options.

2.2) Discussion of participating in the UAMPS Fremont Solar and Battery Storage Project.

The Council discussed the UAMPS solar and battery storage project. The proposed project
involves a solar facility with 49.5 megawatts of power and 198 megawatt-hours of battery
storage, capable of providing additional hours of energy generation. There was discussion about
how this project might impact existing natural gas plant contracts and energy utilization. Joel
stated that the project's energy costs align closely with their current blended wholesale rates. The
power purchase agreement provides significant protections, ensuring the city only pays for
energy actually delivered and mitigating risks associated with low solar production. A key
attraction is the project's timing, allowing the city to take advantage of current investment tax
incentives before they expire in July 2026. This would also represent the city's first venture into
battery storage, offering energy diversification. Joel highlighted that by 2031, with projected city
growth, all their energy projects would be fully utilized. The Council agreed to further review the
detailed agreement, with plans to consider approval of the project at their October meeting.

14) Consideration of Ordinance #58-2025 Water’s Edge General Plan Amendment on
     approximately 13.02 acres located at approximately 1300 East 175 South, changing
     the land use designation from Transit Oriented Development to Mixed-Use.

Representing the applicant was Bob Perdue, Mr. Mossiono- owner, and Abe Neilsen- architect.
The applicants sought to change the land use designation from transit-oriented development
(TOD) to mixed use, allowing for a combination of residential and commercial uses, potentially
including apartments, townhomes, and a hotel. The proposal included plans for preserving
wetlands, constructing for-sale housing, and creating community amenities. They noted that the
residential unit max would be 330 units according to the city’s infrastructure density map.

There was a discussion on alternative commercial uses and the possibility of adding retail. The
applicant stated that a hotel would be the most feasible commercial option, due to the lack of
frontage, exposure, and appeal.

The suitability for this project was questioned due to the homeless camps and dumping. The
applicants stated that they believe this project would help address homelessness and nuisance
issues on the site and argued that once people begin living in and owning homes there, residents
would take more pride and watch over the area, making it less attractive for homeless
encampments and illegal activity. Staff and Council also acknowledged that increasing legitimate
use and oversight of the property could help resolve these ongoing problems.

The Council raised concerns about infrastructure demand and the impact on existing
infrastructure. Lorin Powll noted that the hotel would take up some of the ERUs, which would
then limit the number of residential units. Further study would be needed to analyze the impact
on utilities and traffic.
