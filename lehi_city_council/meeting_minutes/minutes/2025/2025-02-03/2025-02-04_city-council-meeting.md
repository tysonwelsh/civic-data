City Council Budget Retreat, 8:30 a.m.

1.   Welcome, Opening Comment, and Breakfast
     Mayor Johnson welcomed everyone and noted that all Councilmembers were present.
     Councilor Newall gave the opening comment.

Morning Session Presentation and Discussion Items, 9:00 a.m.

1.   Financial Update and Policies- Dean Lundell, Finance Director covered the city's financial
     updates, including the status of various utility funds. The culinary water fund is in a strong
     position, allowing the city to avoid a rate increase. However, the pressurized irrigation (PI),
     sewer and storm drain funds are facing pressure, prompting a possible rate increase. Regarding
     financial policies, the city is considering establishing a $21.6 million "rainy day" fund to cover
     recession and disaster risks, as well as reserves for physical and liability needs. The general
     fund balance is currently around 24-25 million, with a 30% target.

2.   Fiber Network - Shaye Ruitenbeek, Fiber Network Manager, stated that the project is near
     completion but an additional bonding of approximately $24-25 million will be needed to cover
      remaining costs and future connections. The impact of proposed state legislation on the city's
      bonding abilities for the fiber project was also discussed.

3.    Elections Update - Teisha Wilson, City Recorder, presented a proposal to increase filing fees
      for candidates, ranging from the current $35 to fees as high as $150. The council emphasized
      not making the fees so high as to deter potential candidates. Comparisons were made to state
      legislative filing fees. Ultimately, the council directed further research on the actual
      administrative costs, aiming to find a balance between covering expenses and not creating
      barriers to participation in the democratic process.

4.    Power Purchase Adjustment Clause- Joel Eves, Power Director- Joel Eves, Power Director,
      led a discussion of the city’s power purchase adjustment clause (PPAC) which allows the city
      to adjust electricity rates based on fluctuations in wholesale power costs. He explained that the
      city is on track to fully recover a previous shortfall by July 2025 through the PPAC and other
      cost-saving measures. Joel proposed lowering the PPAC, which will result in rate decreases for
      residential customers. The city's efforts to manage wholesale power costs through hedging,
      resource acquisition, and project delays were highlighted. Joel also introduced the concept of a
      "rate stabilization fund" to help smooth future rate increases as new, more expensive power
      generation projects come online. Overall, the discussion demonstrated the city's proactive
      approach to maintaining affordable and stable electricity rates for residents.


5.    Legislative Update- Sierra Pierson, Senior Management Analyst, reported that the League of
      Cities and Towns is tracking approximately 180 bills that could impact municipalities. Several
      key bills were highlighted, including House Bill 90, which would require cities to allow by-
      right development of small lots, and House Bill 256, which would allow cities to work with
      short-term rental platforms to enforce licensing requirements. The update also covered election-
      related bills, such as proposals to require ID at ballot drop boxes and make mail-in voting opt-
      in, as well as Senate Bill 165, which could limit the city's flexibility in financing its fiber optic
      project. The council was encouraged to provide feedback on any other bills they would like the
      city to monitor during the legislative session.

6.    Lunch

Afternoon Session- Presentation and Discussion Items, 1:00 p.m.

1.    AI and Government- Michael Atkinson, Guest Speaker. Mr. Atkinson presented on the
      potential of AI, describing it as a powerful tool that can quickly analyze data and enhance
     productivity. He explained generative AI, specifically ChatGPT, as an advanced autocomplete
     system that uses natural language processing. There was discussion regarding the various roles
     AI can play, including as a tool to assist with tasks, a tutor to enhance learning, and a thought
     partner to improve cognitive processes and creativity. Mr. Atkinson encouraged the participants
     to use AI for upskilling, learning, and leveraging it as a collaborative partner.

2.   Citizen Survey Results- Y2 Analytics Tatiana Flaxman presented the results to the Council.
     The survey results indicate that overall, residents of Lehi report high levels of satisfaction with
     the city, with 75% saying they would recommend it as a great place to live and an average
     quality of life score of 75 out of 100. However, there are consistent concerns about growth,
     traffic, infrastructure, and housing prices impacting the city. Residents are interested in more
     frequent communication from the city on issues like development plans, events, and road
     closures. The survey found that 85% of residents feel the city is growing too quickly, with the
     west and lakeside areas being most concerned. Residents are generally satisfied with city
     services, with fire, emergency medical, and garbage collection receiving the highest ratings.
     The survey also looked at residents' attitudes towards the city's goals, communication, and
     transportation issues.

3.   Council Priorities- Jason Walker, City Administrator. The council and mayor discussed
     proposed changes to the community vision. They were then asked to participate in an activity
     and list out their key priorities and goals. The purpose was to capture not just the projects they
     want to undertake, but also operational improvements they'd like to focus on, such as adding
     more park staff to better maintain the parks. Additionally, the council was encouraged to
     consider any policy changes they feel are important.

The Council then took a break to see a demonstration of Flash, an electronic smelling dog. The
Officer explained that E-dogs are trained to detect a chemical on storage devices, which helps locate
hidden evidence in special victim cases.

There was a break from 3:35- 3:55.

4.   Goals and Visioning- Jason Walker, City Administrator, and Lehi City Mayor & Council.
     The Council’s priorities and goals were then discussed. Highlights from the council include
     completing the full build-out of Family Park, including adding an ice rink, to make it a
     year-round attraction. There was also a focus on increasing the library budget to expand
     offerings, especially with the new civic center coming. The possibility of exploring a
     theater performance space to complement the existing children's theater was also
     mentioned. Maintaining competitive wages and benefits to retain the best police, fire, first
     responder. Improving the city's web presence and communication, including a high-tech
     website with an AI bot for better navigation, was another priority. The discussion also
     covered focusing on transportation and transit solutions, such as the freeway connector,
     and coordinating with neighboring cities. The discussion also included the need for more
     sidewalks, and preparing for future financial challenges.

5.   Council Assignment- Paige Albrecht, Mayor Pro Tempore, led the discussion which focused
     on aligning Council members’ assignments with their interests, availability, and expertise to
     best serve the city’s needs. Changes in assignments were made. Most assignments will
     continue. One key change included adding Dave Norman, Pubic Works Director to the TSSD
     Board.
6.   Miscellaneous Updates including Business Licensing, Downtown Revitalization, Station
     Area Plans, Affordable Housing, Parental Leave, and PARC Tax.
     Regarding business licensing, the council discussed the challenges of monitoring home
     occupations and ensuring compliance with city code. The staff noted that they currently rely
     on an "honor system" for home occupation licenses, but lack the resources to proactively
     enforce the regulations. The council explored options to either increase staffing for
     monitoring and enforcement, or potentially review and modify the code requirements to
     better reflect the changing nature of businesses. The council also discussed pursuing federal
     funding for an environmental impact study on the proposed North Shore Freeway alignment.
     This was seen as an important step to determine the feasibility of this transportation solution,
     which has been a long-standing priority for the city. On the affordable housing front, the
     council reviewed the city's current goals and timeline, and discussed the need to update these
     to reflect evolving priorities and challenges. Reducing regulations on internal or detached
     ADUs, as well as considering more multi-family housing options, were identified as potential
     focus areas. Finally, the HR department presented challenges around retaining and attracting
     quality employees, particularly in areas like public safety. The council explored ideas like
     flexible leave policies, collaboration on recruitment and training programs, and better
     marketing of the benefits of public sector employment.

Mayor Johnson was excused from the meeting at approximately 5:25 p.m.
