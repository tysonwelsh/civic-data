      a.     COMMERCIAL SITE PLAN: “PREMIER MEDICAL” TO BE LOCATED AT
             3433 WEST WATER STREET.

Chair Rushton opened the public hearing.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich, presented the staff report and stated that the above item is a
request for a Commercial Site Plan for Premier Medical to be located at 3433 West Water
Street. It includes a Master Site Plan, Final Site Plan, Commercial Subdivision, and
Conditional Use Permit. Mr. Prestwich explained that it is in an M-1 (Light Manufacturing)

that the application includes a Commercial Subdivision for Lots 2A, 2B, and 2C, a Master
Site Plan for Lots 2B and 2C, and a Final Site Plan and Conditional Use Permit for Lot 2B.
A Conditional Use Permit was required because according to the City ordinance, all uses
within 200 feet of a residential zone in M-1 (Light Manufacturing) become conditional.

Mr. Prestwich reported that there are two entrances on Water Street and two entrances onto

entrance onto the private road. Measures were taken to prohibit truck traffic from going in
the direction of the nearby roundabout. Mr. Prestwich shared example images and signage.

       b.     CONDITIONAL USE: RICHARD’S FENCE WITHIN A FENCE TO BE
              LOCATED AT 2310 WEST 13145 SOUTH.

Chair Rushton opened the public hearing.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the above item is for a conditional
use. The Richards Family has an existing home at 2310 West 13145 South and are
requesting permission to place a fence next to the existing fence. Mr. Prestwich explained
that there is already a chain-link fence in place. The family had since put up a privacy fence
on the east and north line. They wanted to construct a privacy fence along the west line as
well but the family had been unable to agree with the neighbor about replacing the shared
chain-link fence. However, the City ordinance allows a Conditional Use Permit to be granted
to allow a fence within a fence.

A fence within a fence could lead to issues such as weeds since maintenance cannot occur.
As a result, a Conditional Use Permit is required prior to installation. Staff believed the
applicant had met all criteria in the City Code. Mr. Prestwich reported that the property was
surveyed and the intention was to install a concrete mow strip underneath for weed
mitigation. A map of the area was displayed. A Conditional Use Permit was needed for
approximately 60 feet. The additional fence would be entirely on the applicant’s property
and the applicant would bear the costs.

Mr. Prestwich read from City Code Section 18.155.110: Fence within a Fence. He noted
that the applicants met all listed requirements as the second fence will be on the applicant’s
property, the land has been surveyed, the fence would not utilize the existing fence for
support, concrete will be laid down, and future owners will be responsible for maintenance.
Staff recommended approval with the conditions listed in the staff report. Mr. Prestwich
informed the Planning Commission that a few comments had been received in favor of the
application and were included in the packet.

Commissioner Lefgren asked if there were limitations on an eight-foot fence between
residential units. Mr. Prestwich explained that anything beyond the first 20 feet of the
property can have an eight-foot fence.

Jenny Richards clarified that the Richards family had not spoken to the neighbor about
removing the existing fence. However, she felt that they had followed all of the required
guidelines to apply for the Conditional Use Permit.

There were no further public comments. The public hearing was closed.

Commissioner Lefgren wondered if the ordinance needed to be revised. He felt that an
application like this could likely be handled at staff level in the future. It was noted that the

             APPROXIMATELY 3.09 ACRES OF LAND LOCATED AT 13040 SOUTH
             REDWOOD ROAD.

Commissioner Russell was excused from the remainder of the meeting.

Chair Rushton opened the public hearing.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the above item involves a rezone
application for 3.09 acres of land located at 13040 South Redwood Road. He shared a map
of the property with the Planning Commission. He explained that the property was seen
previously as a General Plan application. At that time, the idea was to present the General
Plan to the Planning Commission and the City Council. Staff would then come back and
present the rezone to the Planning Commission before moving forward with the rezone
presentation to the City Council. However, that process changed and the General Plan and
rezone presentations would be combined for the City Council. Both the General Plan and
rezone would be heard at the May 4, 2021, City Council Meeting.

The current zone is RR-22 and the rezone application is for R-3. Mr. Prestwich discussed
the difference between the two zones. While they are both single-family residential zones,
there is a difference in the lot size. The RR-22 zone is 21,780 square feet or one-half acre.

The R-3 zone is 14,000 square feet, which is close to three units per acre. The frontage
requirements are also different since the RR-22 zone requires an additional 10 feet. The
RR-22 zone has animal rights, while the R-3 zone does not. However, setbacks and lot
coverage were the same. Both zones also have the ability to develop with a Conditional
Use Permit for a private lane and both permit accessory structures.

The Planning Commission previously recommended denial of the General Plan
Amendment. As a result, staff included a sample motion for denial of the rezone.
Mr. Prestwich clarified that either approval or a continuance could be passed. He added
that there has been a lot of input from neighbors regarding the rezone. The overwhelming
opinion was for denial. The applicant contacted staff via email prior to the meeting to indicate
that he would be unable to attend.

Stewart Grow reported that between the last meeting on this property and tonight, the
applicant had not reached out to the neighborhood. Mr. Grow tried to contact the applicant
multiple times and only received a one-sentence text message response. He did not believe
the applicant was willing to work with the neighbors. He asked that the Planning
Commission recommend denial of the rezone application.

Sherri Withers indicated that she lives in the Riverton Ranch subdivision. Ms. Withers
believed that people were looking to own one-half acre lots and hoped that the Planning
Commission would deny the request.

Bob Ford reported that he was one of the original founders of the Riverton Heritage Group,
which represents citizens from Redwood Road to the river bank. He reported that the
Riverton Ranch Subdivision was a subset of the Riverton Heritage Group. The group had
been very engaged to ensure that part of Riverton has one-half acre lots at a minimum. He
did not feel it was accurate to assume that because there were fewer members of the public
at this hearing that there was any less opposition. The citizens feel strongly about
maintaining what was reflected in the Master Plan. Mr. Ford added that the Riverton
Heritage Group wanted to be more involved but no interaction had been requested.

City Attorney, Craig Bott noted that a witness in a hearing can only testify to their own
personal knowledge. They cannot testify on the position of other individuals, especially in a

that the testimony from Mr. Ford should not be considered.

Garey Exeter reported that he lives on Christan Way and was represented by Mr. Ford as

Mr. Bott reminded the public speakers that they can testify to their own observations but
cannot speak for anyone other than themselves.

Michael Shane Portner reported that he owns a construction company and supported the
idea of developing homes. However, he purchased his home because he wanted a one-
half acre lot and he pays taxes on one-half acre.

Lee Brown asked that the Planning Commission deny the rezone application.

There were no further public comments. The public hearing was closed.

Commissioner Lefgren asked Mr. Bott for clarification of his earlier statements. For instance,
he wondered if someone representing the Homeowners Association (“HOA”) could
represent the group if they are a member. He also asked if signatures from residents could
be presented to the Planning Commission to represent a neighborhood. Mr. Bott explained
that for an individual to allege that they are a spokesperson for a group, they need to be able
to show that the group exists and show the membership of that organization. If the bylaws
of the organization state that once you belong to that organization you agree with the position
of the governing board, someone could then state the position of the group.

Mr. Bott addressed Commissioner Lefgren’s question related to signatures. He stated that
someone can gather signatures and submit a petition. However, it is important to remember
that the courts have stated that public clamor is not a basis for making a decision. The basis
is law and the application of facts to the law. Mr. Bott noted that public statements can be
taken into consideration by the Planning Commission but the Commissioners needed to
ensure that they are making decisions based on their own perceptions of law rather than
simply responding to public clamor.

Commissioner Breinholt asked about a dedicated spokesperson speaking on behalf of a
group of neighbors. Mr. Bott explained that if a group with similar opinions elect someone
to speak for them, that would be acceptable because they all shared their intent. It becomes
more complicated when there is a large group or organization involved. Commissioner
Cannon appreciated the fact that Mr. Bott had brought this issue to the attention of the
Planning Commission. He also thanked the public for their comments.

Commissioner Lefgren moved to recommend DENIAL of Application PLZ-21-4002
Rezoning 3.09 acres of land located at 13040 South Redwood Road, thus keeping the
zoning RR-22. Commissioner Cannon seconded the motion. The motion passed with
the unanimous consent of the Commission.

       e.     REZONE: IVORY DEVELOPMENT, LLC IS REQUESTING A REZONE FOR
              APPROXIMATELY 8.9 ACRES OF LAND LOCATED AT 11850 SOUTH 3600

Chair Rushton opened the public hearing.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the above item is for a rezone for
the Osterloh Property by Ivory Development, located at 11850 South 3600 West. The
applicant was asking for single-family detached lots and single-family attached twin home
lots with all rambler homes. The property was discussed previously at the March 25, 2021,
Planning Commission Meeting. At that time, the Planning Commission recommended a
slight amendment to the General Plan with the following modifications:

   •   Medium-high density residential with a maximum of six units per acre or RM-6, which
       is in line with Elderly Housing Overlay.

The property is currently in the C-N (Commercial Neighborhood) zone and the requested
zone was RM-6. Mr. Prestwich noted that the application did not specifically include the
Elderly Housing Overlay, so two sample motions were provided to the Commissioners. One
included the Elderly Housing Overlay and one did not. Mr. Prestwich explained that the
matter will go to the City Council as a General Plan and rezone application. It would then
come back to the Planning Commission as a site plan.

Mr. Lethbridge discussed the Elderly Housing Overlay. He reported that Ivory Development
did include the Elderly Housing Overlay in the original application because of the previous
ordinance. Mr. Lethbridge stated that the version the City Council approved was essentially
what the Planning Commission forwarded. There was a requirement for a certain number
of age or mobility-friendly features in the homes to ensure that they remain targeted toward
elderly housing. The version the Planning Commission reviewed requires a minimum of
three features. The City Council felt there needed to be more of a sliding scale that is based
on density. The approved version requires one for each unit of density. For example, in the
RM-6 zone, the units would need to include at least six of those age or mobility-friendly
features.

Mr. Lethbridge stated that by approving a rezone that includes the Elderly Housing Overlay,
the Planning Commission and the City Council would have the ability to make limitations or
requirements related to unit type or height. If those limitations are approved as part of the
rezone, they would apply regardless of whether Ivory Development chooses to develop on
the property. Discussions were had regarding the density bonus.

Commissioner Breinholt asked about regulating elderly housing. Mr. Lethbridge felt that it
would be appropriate to include some direction in the motion. Commissioner Breinholt also
asked about potential lawsuits as a result of age discrimination. Mr. Bott explained that age
discrimination protects individuals over a certain age, not individuals under a certain age.

Commissioner Cluff asked about the zone change. She wondered if it was possible to keep
the zone in R-3 with the Elderly Housing Overlay. Mr. Lethbridge stated that in order to allow

The applicant, Bryon Prince from Ivory Development reported that the application process

support. Mr. Prince stated that the proposal was a rezone to an RM-6 with the Elderly
Housing Overlay restrictions. When it came to elderly housing, Ivory Development had
handled several of those types of projects in the past.

Mr. Prince noted that with this type of housing, it was typically either age-targeted or deed-
restricted. Age-targeted means that the units are marketed toward a specific demographic.

of a deed-restricted subdivision. The Federal Fair Housing Act specifies that only 80% can
be regulated. This means that 20% of units would need to be available to people under a
certain age. Mr. Prince noted that for accessibility reasons, the rambler-style home may be
desirable to people of various ages. He reported that a deed-restricted subdivision is strictly
regulated and needs to be recertified annually. This was something that Ivory Development
has done in the past and has experience with. Mr. Prince added that they were supportive
of a condition that would include a buffer of detached single-family homes along the western
boundary of the property.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the above item is for a home
occupation for “Sol Journey Mindful Massage Self-Care,” to be located at 12061 South 2740
West. The applicant will provide professional therapeutic massage out of her home. There
will be no employees and there are currently no other home occupations at the property.
Mr. Prestwich reported that there will be up to four customers per day. Those customers
would be scheduled, arrive one at a time and stay for approximately 90 minutes. Four
vehicles will arrive per day and park on the street or in front of the home. The applicant
wanted to have two customers in the morning and two in the afternoon.

The applicant wanted permission to operate from 6:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m. The home
occupation request came to the Planning Commission due to the requested hours of
operation. The ordinance states that home occupations can operate between 7:00 a.m. and
7:00 p.m. However, the Planning Commission can adjust the qualifications.

Mr. Prestwich reported that several comments had been received on the item. Some
neighbors pointed out that the applicant rents the home. However, the homeowner was
present at the Planning Commission Meeting and granted permission for the use. Other
concerns from neighbors related to potential code enforcement issues at the property.
Mr. Prestwich explained that there was an ongoing investigation and may not have an impact
on the Conditional Use Permit. Several public comments also related to parking. While the
applicant is proposing four customers per day, the qualifications allow up to eight. Staff
included language to allow for five customers per day in the sample motion.

Commissioner Cannon was uncertain about the proposed hours of operation. Discussions
were had about the property being a rental. Mr. Bott explained that home-based businesses
can be conducted from an owned home, a rented home, or even a room rented from a
landlord as long as the process clearly denotes the relationship. Chair Rushton asked if off-
street parking is required for home-based businesses. Mr. Prestwich explained that if the
home-based business has an employee, the employee would be required to use off-street
parking. Off-street parking must also be provided for customers, clients, or business-related
visitor vehicles. That parking does not necessarily have to be used but it needs to be
provided. Mr. Bott added that anyone visiting the neighborhood has the right to park on the
street if they choose to.

Commissioner Cluff pointed out that many of the concerns from residents relate to parking.
She asked if there were conditions that may help address worries about crowded streets
and blocked mailboxes. Mr. Bott stated that conditions would only be appropriate if the
business creates a situation that is substantially different from the current conditions of the

5.     DISCUSSION ITEMS

       a.     DISCUSSION   OF   AMENDING      ORDINANCE                         REGULATING
              DEVELOPMENT OF LOTS ON PRIVATE LANES.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that there had been discussions with the Planning Commission and
the City Council regarding proposed amendments to the Private Lane Ordinance. He
wanted to review the proposed draft with the Planning Commission to see if there were any
additional corrections or changes the Commission would like to see reflected in the draft
before the next meeting.

The current ordinance for private lanes includes a maximum of four lots on a private lane
and a maximum length of 450 feet. However, the ordinance lacked clarity on how setbacks
and other lot size issues were addressed. Mr. Lethbridge overviewed some of the proposed
changes and discussed the width standards. For one to two lots, there would be a 20-foot
minimum. Anything above three lots would have a 25-foot minimum. The ordinance would
allow for additional width to be required by the City or due to the Fire Code.

In situations where there is an existing private lane, additional lots could not be added if the
lane does not meet the minimum width standard. The private lane would either need to be
expanded or further development would not be possible. There was no maximum number
set for the number of lots and the length. That would be established in a Conditional Use
Permit, which would give the Planning Commission discretion to determine the appropriate
number of lots. Mr. Lethbridge felt that would provide additional flexibility.

The proposed amendment included a requirement that the lane be paved with asphalt or
concrete and that there be street signage. Setbacks would be measured to the edge of the
required lane area. The proposed amendment would also allow the lane to be counted
toward the total lot area; however, it could not be counted toward the buildable area. It also
would not count toward the accessory structure allowance.
