3.     Public Hearing: Siempre at South Mountain Site Plan (Administrative Action)
       On the request of Randall Deschamps, representing Siempre LLC, for a Site Plan on the
       South Mountain Golf Course property that contains the Golf Course Club House for the
       development of an event center. The property is located at 1247 E Mike Weir Dr.
       Application SPR-994-2020. Staff contact is Travis Van Ekelenburg, 801-576-6522,
       travis.vanekelenburg@draper.ut.us.

       Mr. Van Ekelenburg stated that the property was 8.99 acres, and 2.13 acres would be
       used for proposed center. In 1990’s the golf course was developed, and Salt Lake County
       partnered with Siempre, LLC to develop an event center next to the course. Salt Lake
       County approved a 45-year ground lease for the event center. The land use is
       neighborhood commercial. The designated area was C2 and RM split Zoning.

       The property is located in the South Mountain jurisdiction and is not under the current
       Draper City Municipal Code but is reviewed under the 1990 Draper City Municipal
       Code. Parking will be on-site only with no parking allowed on Mike Weir Drive. The
       Landscape Plan shows they will add trees around the gazebo to help mitigate noise and
       light from events in the daytime and the evening. With 15% landscaping required, is the
       Landscape Plan shows 8.8% manicured landscaping and 60.8% in native landscape. The
       height for the building is at about 30 feet similar to the golf course clubhouse. The East
       gazebo is 18 feet wide and about 23 feet tall. The gazebo/patio area on the west side has a
       14-foot structure. The lighting will be mostly downward facing. There will have foot
       lighting, wall pack lighting, 43-inch shielded pole lights, and landscaping illumination.
       Lights will be off when the facility is closed. The center will operate 7 AM to 11PM.

       There was a video shown which showed the new event center from different renderings.
       He asked staff if there were any questions at the end of the video.

       Ms. Squire asked about deviation requests.

       Mr. Van Ekelenburg noted there were not the same deviations as in the current plan due
       to the 1990 requirements.

       Ms. Squire asked clarification that the event center is totally compliant with the 1990’s
       code.

4.     Public Hearing: City Initiated Chickens for Familial Gain Text Amendment
       (Legislative Action)
       On the request of Draper City for a Text Amendment to amend Sections 9-3-040, 9-9-
       080, 9-10-090 and 9-27-055 of Draper City Municipal Code. This application is known
       as the City Initiated Chickens for Familial Gain Text Amendment. Application:
       TEXTMAP-1125-2020.        Staff    contact:  Todd    Draper,    (801)     576-6335,
       todd.draper@draper.ut.us.

       Todd Draper stated this comes by concerns of residents regarding the current ordinance
       and the maximum number of six chickens. The proposed ordinances are intended to
       respect the historical ties to the City and the raising and keeping of chickens. Staff had
       reviewed 16 nearby city ordinances and that of Salt Lake County. There is a wide range
       between the jurisdictions, from 2-250 chickens allowed. He showed charts with the
       number of chickens allowed by some surrounding cities. Some cities show a range, with
       increases based on lot size.
Draper City Planning Commission Meeting
January 28, 2021
Page 6
       The current ordinance 9-27-055 allows a maximum of six chickens, permitted in all
       residential zones, for familial gain only, no sale or income from keeping chickens, only in
       conjunction with a detached single-family dwelling, no roosters are permitted and
       includes setbacks and sanitary standards for pens, coops and run areas.

       They are proposing a reduction to 5 chickens for smaller lots, allowing an increase of one
       chicken for each additional 2000 square feet in properties above 10,000 square feet.
       Proposal is for a maximum limit of twenty chickens. A new regulation allowing chicks
       and juvenile chickens equal to 40% of the allotment of chickens is proposed. He listed the
       specific zones where keeping chickens would be allowed (RA1, RA2, RH, R3, R4, R5,
       A5 and A2). He showed a slide that analyzed how many chickens could be allowed on
       the minimum lot size for each zone and the approximated number of eggs can be
       produced on different lot sizes. He showed a map of where the RM1 and RM2 zoning
       was located.

       The proposed text amendments were:

       9-3-040 Definitions-

       Animal Specialties: Add the word “commercial” to the definition

       Add, “Keeping of chickens for familial gain: The keeping of chickens on a lot for the
       exclusive use by persons residing thereon”.

       He proposed the update for land use tables as a permitted use and the residential zone.

       Corrections were also applied to “animals and fowl for recreation and family food
       production” in the land use tables for residential and commercial zones. The proposed
       text strikes the conditional use aspects with the sentence starting “Any property of
       residential that cannot maintain any or all of the standards…regarding the keeping of
       animals on property as constituted in this code”. The proposed text also strikes “all
       residential” and “agricultural” and add specific zones “RA1, RA2, RH, R3, R4, R5, A5
       and A2”. The larger change was the reduction to 5 chickens and the addition of the 1
       chicken per 2,000 square feet on lots of 10,000 square feet and larger, and regulations for
       chicks or juvenile chickens. There was an addition on number 14 to clarify that dead
       birds and discarded or rotting eggs needed to be removed “from all enclosures, pens,
       coops and run areas”, within 24 hours. There were no changes in section D.

       He noted that Commissioner Hawker had questions about how the number of 20 was
       arrived at.

       Todd Draper noted that this number applied to free range chickens. With considerations
       to the numbers including setbacks and property size. They felt it was appropriate to set
       the number at 20.
