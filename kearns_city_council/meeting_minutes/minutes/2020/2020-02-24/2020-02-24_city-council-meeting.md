Public Hearing

            THIS BEING THE TIME heretofore set for a public hearing to receive public
comment regarding the following request:

                Nathan Bracken reviewed the Ordinance adopting a temporary moratorium
prohibiting the acceptance or approval of an application for subdivision approval, and other new
developments or redevelopment in the Kearns Metro Township.

               Council Member Snow, seconded by Council Member Schaeffer, moved to open
the public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

                 Fred Cox stated he is an architect and is concerned with how restrictive the
moratorium is. If the moratorium is tied down so tight, Kearns may not get a new subdivision or
office building.

               Council Member Snow, seconded by Council Member Schaeffer, moved to close
the public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

               Council Member Peterson, seconded by Council Member Snow, moved to adopt
the following Ordinance:

ORDINANCE NO. 20-02-02                                                  Date: February 24, 2020

       AN ORDINANCE OF THE TOWNSHIP COUNCIL OF KEARNS METRO
       TOWNSHIP, UTAH, ADOPTING A TEMPORARY MORATORIUM PROHIBITING
       THE ACCEPTANCE OR APPROVAL OF AN APPLICATION FOR SUBDIVISION
       APPROVAL, AND OTHER NEW DEVELOPMENT OR REDEVELOPMENT IN
       THE KEARNS METRO TOWNSHIP

       BE IT KNOWN AND REMEMBERED THAT, the Kearns Metro Township Council (the
“Council”) held a public hearing on February 24, 2020, pursuant to Utah Code Ann. § 10-9a-205
to consider this ordinance and take public comment on the same.

                                           WITNESS

   A. WHEREAS, the Kearns Metro Township (“Kearns”) was established to give the residents
      of Kearns greater control over the affairs of their community, including but not limited to
      planning and zoning decisions; and

   B. WHEREAS, pursuant to Utah Code Ann. § 10-2a-414(3), until such times as the Council
      enacts Kearns-specific planning and zoning ordinances, the planning and zoning
      ordinances of Salt Lake County that were in effect as of the date of Kearns’ incorporation
      regulate development within Kearns; and

   C. WHEREAS, Salt Lake County adopted the current “Kearns Township General Plan” on
      September 11, 2012, prior to the incorporation of the Kearns Metro Township in 2017; and

   D. WHEREAS, Utah Code Ann. § 10-9a-401 charges Kearns with developing its own general
      plan to guide land use decisions; and

   E. WHEREAS, on November 11, 2019, the Council amended the current general plan to
      include a moderate-income housing element (“Housing Element”) pursuant to Utah Code
      Ann. § 10-9a-404(5)(c), which requires various amendments to the Kearns Municipal
      Code to implement; and

   F. WHEREAS, notwithstanding the Housing Element, the Council finds that the current
      general plan, which Kearns did not create or adopt, no longer reflects the best interests of
      Kearns and its residents and needs to be updated and replaced to guide future
      development in Kearns and to assist in revising and updating Kearns’ planning and zoning
      ordinances; and

   G. WHEREAS, Kearns has established a General Plan Steering Committee that is working
      to develop a Kearns-specific general plan, which the Council expects to adopt within the
      next six months; and

   H. WHEREAS, Kearns requires time to develop its new general plan and to develop Kearns-
      specific planning and zoning ordinances to implement the Housing Element; and


                                               2
DATE          MONDAY                             FEBRUARY                                  24, 2020


  I.   WHEREAS, Utah Code Ann. § 10-9a-504 allows a municipal legislative body to enact an
       ordinance establishing a temporary land use regulation, or moratorium, for any part or all
       of the area within a metro township to “prohibit or regulate the erection, construction,
       reconstruction, or alteration of any building or structure or any subdivision approval” if the
       Council finds that a “compelling, countervailing public interest” justifies the moratorium;
       and

  J. WHEREAS, Utah Code Ann § 10-9a-504(1)(a) authorizes the Council to adopt a
     temporary moratorium “without prior consideration of or recommendation from the
     planning commission;” and

  K. WHEREAS, the Council finds that there is a compelling countervailing public interest that
     justifies the adoption of a six-month temporary moratorium prohibiting the acceptance or
     approval of new applications for subdivision approval or other land use applications in
     Kearns that involve the construction, reconstruction, or alteration of any building or
     structure in order to develop and adopt the new general plan and to amend its planning
     and zoning ordinances to implement the Housing Element.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED BY THE TOWN COUNCIL OF THE KEARNS METRO
TOWNSHIP AS FOLLOWS:

       1. For the purposes of this moratorium, the following definitions will apply:

          a. The term “government entity” means the federal government and any of its
             agencies, the State of Utah, a county, municipality, local district, special service
             district, or any other political subdivision or administrative unit of the State of Utah

          b. The term “land use application” shall have the definition set forth in Utah Code
             Ann. § 10-9a-103(27).

          c. The term “utility” means any private or public utility, that provides electricity, gas,
             water, sewer, telecommunications, Internet service, or any combination of these
             services for sale to consumers.

       2. Pursuant to Utah Code Ann. § 10-9a-504, the Council expressly finds that the adverse
          effects of accepting or approving an application for subdivision approval or other land
          use application that involves the erection, construction, reconstruction, or alteration of
          any building or structure before the Council adopts a Kearns-specific general plan and
          amends its planning and zoning ordinance to implement the Housing Element are not
          in the best interests of Kearns or its residents, and constitute a compelling,
          countervailing public interest sufficient to justify a temporary six-month moratorium on
          accepting or approving such applications.

       3. This moratorium remains in effect for six (6) months following the effective date of this
          ordinance.



                                                 3
DATE         MONDAY                            FEBRUARY                                 24, 2020

      4. During the moratorium, Kearns will not accept or approve: (a) applications for
         subdivision approval; or (b) other land use applications that seek approval for
         conditional uses, and planned unit developments within Kearns that involve the
         erection, construction, reconstruction, or alteration of any building or structure.

      5. The Council may grant an exception to this moratorium for a utility or government entity
         if the Council determines that granting such an exception is necessary for public
         health, safety, or welfare.

      6. Nothing in this moratorium will impact any permits that were validly issued prior to the
         effective date of this moratorium.

      7. Nothing in this moratorium shall impact any entitlements properly vested prior to this
         ordinance taking effect pursuant to Utah Code Ann. § 10-9a-509, meaning that Kearns
         will continue to process complete land use applications that were submitted prior to
         the effective date of this ordinance.

      8. This ordinance shall take effect immediately upon its publication pursuant to Utah
         Code Ann. § 10-3-711(1) and Utah Code Ann. § 10-3-712.

19.05.010 Appointment – Term

                                          WITNESS

       A. WHEREAS, the Kearns Metro Township (“Kearns”) incorporated in 2017 as
          municipality to give the residents of Kearns greater control over the affairs of their
          community; and

       B. WHEREAS, Section 10-1-203 of the Utah Code authorizes Kearns to “fix the amount,
          terms, and manner of issuing licenses;” and

       C. WHEREAS, Title 5 of the Kearns Code governs business licenses and regulations:
          and

       D. WHEREAS, Section 5.02.010 of the Kearns Code states that “it is unlawful for any
          person to engage in or operate any business in the metro, or to use any property for
          such business, without first obtaining the required license from the license official”
          (emphasis added); and

       E. WHEREAS, the Council finds that many businesses are operating in Kearns without
          first obtaining a business license and, in some cases, these businesses are operating


                                               6
DATE          MONDAY                          FEBRUARY                               24, 2020

           in ways that violate applicable laws, regulations, or ordinances governing zoning,
           public safety, and other such matters; and

      F. WHEREAS, notwithstanding Section 5.02.010’s clear requirement that “any person”
         engaging in business within Kearns requires a business license, the Council has
         discovered that businesses are installing and operating amusement devices and
         vending machines without complying with Kearns’ business license requirements,
         including but not limited to filing for and obtaining a business license, paying the
         applicable business license fees, and obtaining the necessary inspections to ensure
         compliance with applicable building and zoning requirements; and

      G. WHEREAS, the Council finds that some business license applicants are
         pressuring Kearns staff to process business license applications before the
         applicants have provided all the necessary documentation needed for the
         staff to process their applications; and

      H. WHEREAS, the Council desires to create a culture of compliance within
         Kearns to ensure that business owners comply with Kearns' business
         license requirements; and

      I.   WHEREAS, the Council desires to amend Title 5 to: (1) provide more detail
           on the information needed for Kearns staff to process a business license
           application; (2) require denial for business license applications that do not
           comply with the factors of Section 5.02.140 of the Kearns Code; (3) further
           clarify that business license applications shall not be deemed complete and
           entitled to processing until the applicant has provided all required information
           and documentation; and (4) augment Section 5.02.0l0's requirement that
           amusement devices and vending machines must comply with Kearns'
           business license requirements; and

      J. WHEREAS, the Council has reviewed the proposed amendments to Title 5
         of the Kearns Code outlined herein and desires to adopt the proposed
         amendments.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED BY THE TOWN COUNCIL OF THE KEARNS
METRO TOWNSIDP AS FOLLOWS:

SECTION 1: The Council instructs and directs its business license staff and code
enforcement officers to use any and all legal means to ensure that business owners
comply with Kearns' business license requirements, including but not limited to denial of
business license applications for those businesses that begin operations before securing
a business license.

5.02.120 – Compliance with Building, and Zoning, and Other Requirements.

SECTION 6: Chapter 5.23, entitled “Amusement Devices and Vending Machines,” is added to
the Kearns code as follows:

Chapter 5.23 Amusement Devices and Vending Machines

Section 5.23.010 Applicability

       This Chapter 5.23 shall apply to all applications filed after March 1, 2020.

Section 5.23.020 License Requirements

       A. Except as otherwise provided in this Chapter 5.23, the owner or operator of an
          amusement device or vending machine is subject to the requirements of Title 5.

       B. A business license is required for any amusement device or vending machine and
          must be obtained before an amusement device or vending machine may be operated.
          If an amusement device or vending machine is to be located within another business,
          the business license for the amusement device or vending machine shall be in addition
          to the business license issued for the underlying business, provided:

               a. Only one application and business license shall be required for amusement
                  devices and vending machines operated in the same location that are
                  substantially similar in nature and operation; and
               b. Only one application and business license shall be required for an amusement
                  facility.

       C. All owners or operators of an amusement device or vending machine must either (1)
          obtain and maintain a sales tax identification number for each amusement device or


                                                12
DATE          MONDAY                              FEBRUARY                                   24, 2020

           vending machine from the Utah State Tax Commission and provide the sales tax
           identification number to the license official; or (2) provide the license official a
           document in writing from the Utah State Tax Commission that states that the
           amusement device or vending machine is exempt from sales tax. Only one sales tax
           identification number shall be required for multiple amusement devices or vending
           machines operated in the same location if the amusement devices or vending
           machines are substantially similar in nature and in operation.

       D. The license official may authorize the installation of an amusement device or vending
          machine in writing prior to the issuance of a business license to allow for inspections
          to ensure that the amusement device or vending machine complies with applicable
          laws, regulations, and ordinances. No amusement device or vending machine shall be
          installed without the prior written authorization of the license official. Authorization from
          the license official to install an amusement device or vending machine for inspection
          purposes does not constitute a business license and the machine may only be
          operated temporarily for the purposes of any required inspections. If the license official
          denies an application after authorizing the installation of an amusement device or
          vending machine, the applicant shall remove the amusement device or vending
          machine at their sole cost and expense as soon as is reasonably practicable following
          the denial.

(Revised and Restated Interlocal Agreement to Create and Govern Unified Fire Authority)

      A RESOLUTION OF KEARNS METRO TOWNSHIP APPROVING ENTRY INTO
      A REVISED AND RESTATED INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT TO CREATE AND
      GOVERN THE UNIFIED FIRE AUTHORITY

      A.     The Interlocal Cooperation Act, Utah Code Ann. §11-13-101 et seq. (the “Interlocal
             Cooperation Act”), provides that any two or more public agencies may enter into
             agreements with one another for joint or cooperative action following the adoption
             of an appropriate resolution by the governing body of each participating public
             agency.

      B.     The multiple parties to the proposed Revised and Restated Interlocal Agreement
             to Create and Govern the Unified Fire Authority (“Revised Interlocal Agreement”)
             are all public agencies for purposes of the Interlocal Cooperation Act.

      C.     The Unified Fire Authority (“Authority”) was initially created via Interlocal
             Cooperation Act in 2004 to provide fire protection and emergency medical services
             to public agencies for a fee. As described in the Revised Interlocal Agreement,
             some members of the Authority directly pay this fee to the Authority while others
             do so as members of the Unified Fire Service Area, a special district for fire
             protection and emergency medical services.

      D.     To better reflect the current status and governance of the Authority, the members
             of the Authority, including those members of the Unified Fire Service Area, wish to
             revise and restate the original 2004 interlocal agreement by entering into the
             Revised Interlocal Agreement in the form attached hereto as Exhibit “A,” so that
             the Authority may continue to provide fire suppression, rescue services,
             emergency medical services, emergency protection, and related services to the
             parties.

      E.     Each legislative body of the Authority and its members has met or will meet in
             regular open session in compliance with the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act
             to approve the respective entity’s entry into the Revised Interlocal Agreement.

      F.     The Chief Legal Officer of the Authority has reviewed and approved the form of the
             Revised Interlocal Agreement as required by Utah Code Ann. §11-13-202.5(3).
             Each member entity’s respective legal counsel must similarly approve the Revised
             Interlocal Agreement.

      G.     After careful consideration, the Council of the Kearns Metro Township has
             reviewed the form of the Revised Interlocal Agreement and determined that it is in
             the best interests of the Kearns Metro Township to approve the entry into the
             Revised Interlocal Agreement as proposed.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the Council of the Kearns Metro Township:

      SECTION 1. PURPOSE. The purpose of this Resolution is to approve the entry of the
Kearns Metro Township into an Interlocal Agreement.

       SECTION 2. APPROVAL OF INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT. The Council hereby
authorizes and directs the execution and delivery of the Revised Interlocal Agreement, in the form
attached as Exhibit “A” hereto, on behalf of the Kearns Metro Township.

        SECTION 3. RECORDS. The Kearns Metro Township’s keeper of records is authorized
and instructed to keep and maintain an executed copy of the Revised Interlocal Agreement
available for inspection.

        SECTION 4. EFFECTIVE DATE. This Resolution will take effect upon approval. The
Revised Interlocal Agreement will be effective when the following Interlocal Cooperation Act
requirements have been satisfied: (i) the Revised Interlocal Agreement has been approved by all
parties as required by Section 11-13-202(2); (ii) each party to the Revised Interlocal Agreement
has submitted the Revised Interlocal Agreement to an attorney authorized to represent the said
party for review as to proper form and compliance with applicable law as required by Section 11-
13-202.5(3); and (iii) the approved, signed Revised Interlocal Agreement has been filed with the
keeper of records of each of the parties as required by Section 11-13-209.
