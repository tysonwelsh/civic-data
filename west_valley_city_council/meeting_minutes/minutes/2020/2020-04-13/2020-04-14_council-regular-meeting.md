RESOLUTION 20-35: EXTEND THE LOCAL EMERGENCY DECLARATION
CONCERNING THE COVID-19 PANDEMIC
Mayor Bigelow discussed proposed Resolution 20-35 that would extend the Local
Emergency Declaration concerning the Covid-19 Pandemic.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       On March 15, the City Manager declared a local emergency in response to the
       COVID-19 pandemic. The Utah Emergency Management Act provides that such
       a declaration cannot be effective for more than thirty days without the approval of
       the City Council. It is apparent that the COVID-19 pandemic will continue to be a
       major concern for federal, state, and local governments for some time and that
    MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING – APRIL 14, 2020
                           -3-

       emergency conditions will continue for more than thirty days. Accordingly, this
       resolution extends the declaration of a local emergency to May 15, 2020.

       If emergency conditions no longer exist, the local emergency declaration can be
       rescinded by resolution prior to May 15. If the emergency continues beyond that
       time, the emergency declaration can be extended in the same manner.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Bigelow there were no further questions from members of the City
Council, and he called for a motion.

       Councilmember Buhler moved to approve Resolution 20-35.

       Councilmember Lang seconded the motion.

RESOLUTION 20-36: APPROVE A COOPERATIVE AGREEMENT BETWEEN
THE CITY AND THE UTAH DEPARTMENT OF TRANSPORTATION FOR THE
INSTALLATION OF BLYNCSY SENSORS
Mayor Bigelow discussed proposed Resolution 20-36 that would approve a Cooperative
Agreement between the City and the Utah Department of Transportation for the installation
of Blyncsy Sensors.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       UDOT is purchasing Blyncsy sensors to assist with traffic reporting and modualtion
       along 3500 South and along the Redwood Road corridor. UDOT has requested to
       piggy back on the West Valley City contract with Blyncsy and will be purchasing
       9 sensors to be installed along the State road (Redwood Road) for real time
       reporting of traffic counts. UDOT will provide WVC all of the data from the UDOT
       sensors to both reciprocate from the reporting.
    MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING – APRIL 14, 2020

       West Valley City has identified the corridor along 3500 south and Redwood road
       to study traffic patterns in order to plan for future growth. This information will
       also help economic development as we will be able to provide real time traffic
       reports to retail clients.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Bigelow there were no further questions from members of the City
Council, and he called for a motion.

RESOLUTION 20-37: APPROVE THE PURCHASE OF TWO FORD F-550
TRUCKS FROM YOUNG FORD
Mayor Bigelow discussed proposed Resolution 20-37 that would approve the purchase of
two Ford F-550 trucks from Young Ford.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       The public works department operations division would like to purchase a Ford F-
       550 cab & chassis. This truck will be upfitted with a dump bed. The truck will be
       used as a city clean up truck. Young Ford has the State contract (AV2529) to sell
       Ford vehicles. The prices indicated below are competitive and the vehicles meet the
       City’s needs.

        Number of Vehicles           Type of Vehicle                            Cost Per Vehicle
        1                            Ford F-550 cab & chassis                   $42,509.00
        1                            Ford F-550 cab & chassis                   $42,134.00

Councilmember Lang stated that she doesn’t recall approving two vehicles and four new
employees. She stated that she would prefer to approve the purchase of one vehicle and
two employees to see how the program goes and determine the need. Mayor Bigelow asked
if the employees for the proposed program have already been approved. Wayne replied no
but clarified that only one truck is new for the cleanup crew while the other is a
replacement. He stated that after talking to staff about the goal of the Council to clean up
the City, the recommendation from staff was 8 hires. He indicated that the funds for these
hires are in place and coming from the sanitation fund. Councilmember Buhler stated that
he is pleased with the program despite the number of proposed employees. He indicated
that the City should be a clean, inviting place and this purchase is about the trucks, not the
hiring of employees.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Bigelow there were no further questions from members of the City
Council, and he called for a motion.

       Councilmember Buhler moved to approve Resolution 20-37.

       Councilmember Christensen seconded the motion.

Councilmember Lang stated that she is fine with purchasing the trucks but would like to
discuss the hiring of the employees further.
