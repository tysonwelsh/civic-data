    1. ACTION ON THE 2016 WEBER COUNTY LOCAL TRANSPORTATION FUND PROJECT PRIORITIES
         Douglas Larsen, Weber County Economic Development Partnership Director, noted that since 2008 the
         county with the Weber Area Council of Governments (WACOG) have managed a local transportation
         fund that is funded by the third quarter cent sales tax and the $10 corridor preservation fee. He stated that
         WACOG unanimously approved this item. The total amount from the sales tax is $8,103,758 and
         $2,019,110 from corridor preservation. The requests for the latter were funded at 100%) and the sales tax
         funds were more limited this year and project approval will be based on the evaluation system. Chair Bell
         has run into roadblocks with UTA, they want some of the local transportation funds from the sales tax
         that passed in Weber County, however, UTA receives a lot of funding/has a great funding stream. Some
         of that transportation funding could be used for the BRT line but the UTA has not been amenable to it.
         Mr. Larsen addressed Commissioner Gibson’s questions regarding the BRT line stating that the project
         has been through 3-4 studies since 2005 and is currently going through a federal phase of the process; it
         will be supported federally at some level. For the 2016 cycle the project requested $5M for final
         design/engineering. The decision from WACOG’s transportation subcommittee and WACOG is that the
         county’s funds will support up to 50% of that request. Commissioner Gibson also expressed frustration
         with UTA. He heard at a recent meeting that UTA is absorbing most/all of the cost for Utah County’s
         BRT line. Weber County continues to be treated differently.
       Commissioner Gibson noted that these funds are so limited and valuable to our county. He wants the
       UTA to hear the message loud and clear that he is tired of it. WACOG and the county need to say no to
       some of these things until UTA comes to the table and treats our county appropriately in these
       negotiations. As long as the county continues to approve lists like this that take valuable money out of
       our very limited funds for road projects the county will continue to be left in the dust. The city and
       county, however, understand the need to move this project forward. UTA got 40% of the sales tax on the
       quarter cent passed with Prop 1 and they are making decisions unilaterally. They are hearing from Salt
       (which may have different mass transit priorities) and there needs to be a local mechanism for local
       communities to be able to direct where that spending will occur. Chair Bell stated that UTA dictates
       everything for Weber County and he does not want them to lease more buses, that our buses are all
       empty.
       Commissioner Ebert moved to approve the 2016 Weber County Local Transportation Fund project
       priorities; Commissioner Gibson seconded.
       Commissioner Gibson – aye; Commissioner Ebert – aye; Chair Bell – aye

       Commissioner Ebert addressed items F.7 & F.8. Last week the county approved two contracts for
       companies to assist the county to create the structure and the logistics of rolling out that structure for an
       initiative that is brand new—the intergenerational poverty initiative. Currently, there is no initiative
       within the county moving forward on addressing poverty. There is no staff within the county to move the
       projects forward or to help with sustainability and these contracts are basically staff contracts. Instead of
       hiring staff, which can be a 20-year commitment, the county is hiring contract employees to serve as the
       staff. The first contract is for Michael Lindenmayer whose tasks include finding funding sources.
       Presently, the county has no money to move this project forward, which he said could be a multi-million
       dollar project, and is relying on State funds as well as looking for other funding sources. The second
       contract is with Ms. Watkins, who has worked with the intergenerational poverty program from day one
       and will be the manager, working on development and continuing to strengthen partnerships.
       Commissioner Gibson is supportive of the intergenerational poverty initiative; however, he is deeply
       concerned with having our taxpayers foot the bill for pushing a change in the welfare system, uphill on
       our own. Commissioner Ebert had mentioned that there is State funding coming in but there has only
       been the contract from last week that paid partially by a $15,000 grant, and he is not aware of any other
       grants. He is concerned with large sums of money of new spending being the responsibility of our county
       as we try to move this forward. He was comfortable with an administrative assistant putting time/effort
       into this program. There were two contracts last week and two this week making it five individuals on
       contract and he does not know who they answer to, what measurable benchmarks there are, etc. Mr.
       Lindenmayer was hired nine months ago at $60,000 and Commissioner Gibson has been asking him for
       months to show what funding sources or measurables he has brought to the table but he has seen none.
       He suggested tying Mr. Lindenmayer’s compensation to some funding that is brought in. It is not right to
       pay an individual that kind of money on a promise and in nine months there have been no results.
       Additionally, with today’s contract, the price had gone up to $90,000. The county needs to be more
       careful and be able to show that there is a benefit to our taxpayers. He expressed strong objections to
       both contracts stating that this is not a wise use of taxpayer money, especially where the Commission just
       asked people to sacrifice dramatically to pay for a property tax increase.
      Commissioner Ebert disagrees that the county should not be a leader in welfare reform and said that it is
      our responsibility to show vision. He said that taxpayers of this county have spent over $496 million on
      areas that are directly affected by poverty in the last 10 years. The best place to spend the money right
      now is in poverty because it costs the most money and the county cannot afford not to do it anymore or
      we will continue to have the lowest economic development, property values will continue to be depressed
      and continue to struggle. By addressing poverty the county can grow its economy, create a workforce,
      and the culture of community. Poverty drives the quality of life down. He is committed to a systems

    2. PUBLIC HEARING TO PROVIDE OPPORTUNITY FOR REPRESENTATIVES FROM NON-PROFIT ORGANIZATIONS
       AS WELL AS OTHERS TO DISCUSS POTENTIAL PROJECTS, (INCLUDING COUNTY PROJECTS) – CDBG

       Chad Meyerhoffer, with County Engineering, said that this hearing is to consider potential projects for
       which funding may be applied under the 2017 Community Development Block Grant Program. The
       national objective is for grant money to be spent primarily to benefit low to moderate income persons but
       other national objectives include using funds to aid in the prevention/elimination of slums/blight and may
       be used to meet urgent needs because existing conditions pose a serious/immediate threat to health and
       welfare. The Wasatch Front Regional Council, of which Weber County is a member, is expecting to
       receive approximately $1,000,000 for 2017. All eligible activities that can be accomplished under this
       program are identified in the CDBG Application Policies and Procedures Manual and is available for
       review. Eligible activities include construction of public works and facilities, acquisition of real property,
       and provision of public services. Also eligible are loan programs for private businesses, which would
       then hire low income persons and homeowners. Unincorporated Weber County does not currently have
       any active grant projects. In the past, the county has sponsored various projects, including the Weber
       Housing Authority for a first time home buyers program, received grants for restrooms modification for
       the County Library to bring them more in compliance with ADA standards, an emergency housing
       rehabilitation project administered through the Weber Housing Authority, developed ADA compliant
       campsites, installed storm drains in low to moderate income communities to reduce flooding, sponsored
       non-profit agencies such as Your Community Connection, Ogden Weber Community Action Agency,
       Catholic Community Services and others. Mr. Meyerhoffer was not contacted by any non-profit agencies
       at the CDBG How to Apply Workshop that would like the county to sponsor them and wanted to submit a
       pre-application for a grant. The County has handed out its capital investment plan as part of the regional
       "Consolidated Plan" that shows which projects the county has identified as being needed in the
       community.
       Andi Beadles, with the Weber Housing Authority, stated that they are interested in applying for two
       programs: $30,000 for an Emergency Rental and Deposit Assistance Program to assist 60 households in
       the county to avoid homelessness and also to help homeless households get into a unit; $50,000 for a
       Down Payment Assistance Program to assist 10 first time homebuyers purchase their first home in Weber
       County. The funding will allow the Weber Housing Authority to assist 60 households with assistance.
       Ms. Beadles addressed Commissioner Ebert’s question explaining the Down Payment Assistance
       Program and stating that it is a payback program; there is no new funding. He said that getting people
       housed first helps them to get more stabilized and they are then better able to participate in other
       programs and get on their feet. Chair Bell invited public comments and none were offered.
