Mr. Godfrey spoke at length about the three areas that the district is focusing: health and

Tami Pyfer, Community Engagement Specialist, said that she spent eight years on the Logan

community well. She asked people to contact her if they have questions or concerns.

district on numerous events and issues.

Councilmember McCay said that she appreciates Jordan School District and the great job they
are doing.

Councilmember McDougal explained that he has one issue in his area that has been stalled for
years. The south boundary of Midas Creek Elementary has been tied up in litigation for many
years and this property is a blight on the neighborhood and possibly a safety issue.
Superintendent Godfrey will follow up on this issue.

Councilmember Buroker explained that many residents complain that the schools can’t handle
any more growth during rezone applications. She asked if the school district is prepared for this
growth. Superintendent Godfrey said there are multiple ways the district manages growth.

expand and retract.

he appreciates the City for allowing them to come and present.

      EntryPoint Fiber Network Presentation

Mayor Staggs explained that Phase 2 of the broadband project is to put out a request for a
proposal. Entry Point has finished Phase 1 and will present the design.

Bruce Patterson, Solutions Services Director for EntryPoint, and Tyler Riblett explained and
displayed the design for broadband connections throughout the City. The design is included
with the packet.

Councilmember McDougal asked Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, what was
currently budgeted for Phase 2 of the broadband project. Mr. Hicks replied that $1.2 million is
currently budgeted. Councilmember McDougal said he would like to order all of the supplies
before the project begins because he is concerned about the project stalling due to supply
issues.

Mr. Riblett said that supply issues exist and the City could purchase supplies before the project
begins.

Councilmember Buroker asked about the upgrade on Bangerter and 13400 South and whether
this would affect the broadband project. Mr. Patterson said they will coordinate with UDOT and
doesn’t believe this upgrade will affect the broadband project.

Councilmember Wells asked if there is UDOT conduit on Redwood Road and whether the City
would be using this conduit for broadband. Mr. Patterson explained that there is UDOT conduit
on Redwood Road, but this conduit does not have microduct. The City will need to place
microduct in three new ducts which would then be placed in the large duct owned by UDOT.

Councilmember Stewart asked whether fire stations would be connected in the broadband
project. Mayor Staggs said that fire stations were not included in this project and Mr. Patterson
confirmed that the fire stations were in the original scope of work but were removed.

already occurred. Most of Deer Creek comes from the Duchesne River which is at 96% and this

         2022 Legislative Update

Mayor Staggs reported that Rob Jolley, lobbyist, was very impressed with legislators at this
session especially since they were able to finance the 13400 South and Bangerter project by
moving funds into the TIFF fund. In 2011 to 2013, there was a 21-acre parcel that Riverton City
gave to UDOT to build out Mountain View Corridor and the exchange was to be a continuous
flow interchange at 13400 South and Bangerter which was received. However, there was a
question of whether the City received the full value for this land. Mr. Jolley is planning in April to
have a conversation with Senator Harper about the final cost for 13400 South to go fully
submerged. House Bill 242 was approved and the City will receive a 70% match for secondary
water meters. The City should be one of the first ones to receive the money that has been
appropriated.

Councilmember Stewart clarified that the State is acknowledging the 21 acres as a donation
from Riverton City. David Brickey, City Manager, said that Mr. Jolley did say that the State
acknowledged this donation and he is willing to come and answer questions.

Mayor Staggs thanked representatives that serve Riverton City and said he appreciated them
pushing items along that were important to the City.

         Discussion on Lovers Lane

Councilmember Wells explained that Lovers Lane from 13600 South to the S curve has been a
rural dirt road but will eventually be paved. He is proposing that going south the road would be
a one-way road until the S curve. However, drivers heading south would be able turn around at
the springs. He believes this will keep the rural feel of this area.

Mr. Brickey said the Council is the roadway authority for the City. He said that the Council could
invite the public to make comments or the Council could make this tough decision and move
forward.

Councilmember Buroker commented that developing this narrow road into anything other than a
one-way road would be very expensive. Mr. Brickey agreed and said that he would maintain
this as a Class D road because the residents like the rural feel of this road.

Councilmembers, Mayor and staff had further discussion. After the discussion, all
Councilmembers were supportive of making this road a one-way road.
