Presentation from James R. Taggart, President of Ogden-Weber Technical
College
          Ogden-Weber Technical College (OWTC) President James Taggart used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss
the highlights of the College's FY2019 Annual Report. OWTC's Mission is to "build a prosperous community by creating a
technically-skilled workforce one student at a time." The guiding principles for the college are:
              •    We transform lives;
              •    Student success is our focus;
              •    Employers drive success;
              • Employees are key; and
              •    Integrity is essential.
          The College has experienced tremendous growth; emollment at the college is usually cyclical depending upon the
economy and when unemployment is under four percent, emollment decreases. However, when unemployment rates are high,
people come to the OWTC to get the skills they need to get a job. The increase in emollment is due to the emphasis on creating a
technically skilled work force in the community; many employers have high paying jobs that are unfilled because of a lack of
technically skilled workers. Secondary students in the community have emolled for365,834 hours over the past year; by emolling
while in high school, these students saved $731,668 in tuition. He provided a 'snapshot' of the Fiscal Year (FY) 2020 OWTC
student population; 4,214 students have emolled through the end ofNovember, with 31 percent being eai·ly college high school
students. Thirty-four percent are students of color and 28 percent are Hispanic/Latino. One in four students is an apprentice. He
discussed the OWTC return on investment relative to building a prosperous community; last year students generated over $60
million in wage earnings and these wages generated $3 million in State income taxes; this increased tax revenue repays the cost of
the education that students receive at OWTC in approximately eight years. Most training at OWTC takes less than a year and
students are able to go to work with no student debt. OWTC students typically remain in the Ogden community to work, which
means their wage and tax dollars stay in Utah. Entry level wages of students will increase over the next several years and this

Proposed Ordinance 2020-1 designating the Stone/Brown/James Home,
located at 159 West 2nd Street, as a local historic resource
         A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed historical resource
designation came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated petitioners Richard and Tammy Creeger are proposing
the home located at 159 West 2 nd Street, known as the Stone/Brown/James home, be listed as a local historic resource on the
Ogden City Register of Historic Places. The designation would require any significant changes proposed for the building,
including demolition, to be approved by the Landmarks Commission through the approval of a Certificate of Historic
Appropriateness. The designation would help ensure the preservation of the building and would help to ensure that any changes
proposed would maintain the character and historicity of the building. The City's Landmarks Commission has reviewed the
proposed designation and has forwarded a recommendation of approval finding that the building meets both the historical and
architectural criteria listed in the City's code for local designation.
         Chair Nadolski then introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2020-1, entitled:
                   "An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, designating the Stone/Brown/James home. Located at 159 West 2nd Street,
                   as a historic resource; and providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after
                   final passage."
         A copy of the proposed ordinance was deposited with the Chief Deputy City Recorder and ordered that the City Recorder
have at least one copy available for public inspection in her office during all business hours.

Proposed Ordinance 2019-55 amending the budget for Fiscal Year July 1,
2019 to June 30, 2020 by increasing the anticipated revenues and
appropriations for a gross increase of $812,975
         A memo from the City Comptroller regarding a proposed budget amendment pertaining to the Swift Building demolition
came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated this proposal includes a transfer of$812,975 from Business Depot
Ogden (BDO) lease revenue to the Redevelopment Agency (RDA) for additional project funds needed for Swift Building
demolition costs and a 13% project contingency, with some of this funding intended to suppmi asbestos abatement.
         Comptroller Stout summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to orient the Council to the
budget appropriations for the Swift Building demolition project. The original budget appropriation for the entire project was
$2,202,000; expenses made to date relate to the property purchase ($362,670), asbestos abatement ($536,183), and site work
dating back to 2018 ($259,704). The remaining appropriation available is $1,043,443. City Administration has received cost
estimates for performing the demolition and additional site work; demolition costs are estimated at $1,428,000. Additional costs
include bank stabilization at $117,000, asbestos survey work at $25,000, and removal of an underground tank on the property at
$40,000. With a request for a 13 percent contingency line item, the total cost estimate for demolition is $1,812,975. City
Administration is anticipating $495,000 in future project costs. The $495,000 was a reference for future anticipated costs related to
the project. The funding source for this appropriation is Business Depot Ogden (BDO) lease revenue, which had an available
balance of$2,889,775 on December 31, 2019.

Proposed Ordinance 2020-2 to amend Title 15-25-4 to create building roof
line options and establish building lengths for multiple family buildings in the
12th Street Overlay Zone
         A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed amendments to the 12 th
Street Overlay Zone came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the Planning Division is proposing several
changes to the 12th Street Overlay Zone specific to rooflines, wall plane lengths, and maximum building lengths. The Planning
Commission recently reviewed a proposed project using the 12 th Street Overlay Zone standards and questions came up about the
potential for buildings that are out of scale with the area and direction was given to have the Planning Division review the
standards. The proposed amendments provide standards for maximum wall plane lengths to break up long walls, requirements for
roofline offsets to break up long roofridgelines, and maximum building lengths for multi-family buildings.
         Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
proposed amendments to the 12 th Street Overlay Zone. He noted the purpose of the Overlay Zone was to provide development
options following the widening of 12th Street. The Overlay Zone provides standards that are more exacting than the underlying
zone of the property. The Overlay Zone also includes incentives with no density requirement; the process for getting project
approval in the Zone has been streamlined. Interest in developing properties along the Corridor has recently been renewed and it is
important to consider the design standards for both the overlay zone and the underlying zone. There is consistency between
commercial and residential standards to provide compatibility with the natural rustic surroundings in certain areas along the
corridor. This includes modulation of building facades and roof features that will break up massing. Building length is a
consideration to allow for maintenance of the corridor scale and reflection of the scale of single family uses in the area. The
amendments to the Overlay Zone that have been proposed by the Planning Division are based upon desired outcomes for
continued development of land along the corridor. Currently there is no clear standard for larger scale development to stay in
scale. Total building length is important for compatibility and he presented a map providing a comparison of existing single
family uses along the corridor with potential longer-length building projects that could occur on undeveloped properties in the
area. He presented photos intended to provide an illustration of the importance of a maximum building length requirement coupled
with roofline requirements intended to break up mass of a building. He concluded the Planning Commission voted unanimously to
recommend approval of the proposed amendments to establish maximum building length and required offsets and ridgeline
variations in the 12th Street Overlay Zone.
