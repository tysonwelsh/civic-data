2. Chair Report [00:06:36]
a. ACTION: Endorse appointments to WFRC Council and Committees
Mayor Ramsey, as the WFRC Chair, presented the appointments of WFRC Members to the Regional
Growth Committee (RGC), Transportation Coordinating Committee (Trans Com), WFRC Budget
Committee, Active Transportation Committee (ATC), Wasatch Front Economic Development District
(WFEDD) and Joint Policy Advisory Committee (JPAC). Appointments to WFRC and its Committees

Commissioner Jim Harvey seconded the motion and the vote was unanimous in the affirmative.
b. Legislative session report and endorse positions on legislation [00:11:20]
Mayor Ramsey introduced Miranda Jones Cox, WFRC, who explained the legislation that is pertinent
to the region. WFRC members can keep themselves apprised of notable legislation by subscribing to
WFRC’s Government Affairs emails which are frequently distributed during the session. Members
may also reference WFRC’s bill tracker, found on WFRC’s website, and/or provide input on these
pieces of legislation by attending our weekly policy meetings on Thursday mornings at 8 AM at the
Utah State Capitol building. ACTION: [00:39:44] Mayor Ramsey asked for a motion to endorse the
recommended positions as presented. Commissioner Bob Stevenson made a motion and Mayor
Mark Shepherd seconded the motion. The vote was unanimous in the affirmative.

4. Transportation Coordinating Committee (Trans Com) [00:41:31]
Mayor Mark Shepherd, Trans Com Chair, introduced Ben Wuthrich, WFRC, who discussed
modifications requested for the TIP.
a. Board Modifications to the 2024-2029 Transportation Improvement Program (TIP)
Mr. Wuthrich stated that since the last meeting of the Wasatch Front Regional Council (WFRC),
WFRC received requests to modify the current 2024-2029 TIP. The modification required action from
Trans Com (since WFRC was not meeting) and the Utah Transportation Commission but did not

attached to the approved resolution.

6. Reports [01:26:43]
Mayor Ramsey invited Bryce Bird, with the Utah Division of Air Quality, to review current ozone level
findings.
a. Potential Impacts of Federal Ozone Air Quality Standards
Utah is currently not able to meet requirements of the Federal Clean Air Act related to ozone levels.
These requirements are extremely difficult to meet because of high levels of “background” ozone,
which comes from outside Utah or from natural sources. This situation poses potential risks for
significant highway and transit capacity-increasing projects to proceed. WFRC staff, in collaboration
with Mr. Bird, briefed the Council on this issue and discussed ongoing collaborative efforts to address
the situation. Materials that can help leaders inform their communities will be sent as follow up to this
discussion.
