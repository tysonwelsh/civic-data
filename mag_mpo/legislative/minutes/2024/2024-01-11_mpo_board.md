Study and Project Updates Loretta Markham, AECOM (7:44) FrontRunner South Update The Utah Transit Authority (UTA) is studying an extension of the FrontRunner commuter rail service from Provo to Payson, primarily along UTA-owned property with proposed Springville, Spanish Fork, and Payson stations. These efforts are building upon the South Valley Transit Study completed in 2022, which identified the FrontRunner extension as the Locally Preferred Alternative. The project's current phase includes an environmental review under the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) and a preliminary design for the proposed commuter rail extension. There is ongoing coordination with the cities, UDOT, and the Station Area Planning teams at the following proposed FrontRunner Stations: 1. Springville Station 2. Spanish Fork Station 3. Through continued conversations with the county’s legislators as well as legislative leadership, MAG heard a strong and clear message that our process must be unified and data driven to receive legislative support. The MPO Board has worked to develop a list of coordinated priority transportation projects when seeking one-time legislative appropriation monies during the 2024 legislative session. MPO Board January 11, 2024 Page 4 MAG staff worked with the MPO Board members to create a process and vetting criteria that could be used as a decision making tool for developing a list of data-driven priority projects. In November, the MPO Board approved the vetting criteria that ranked projects along with a set of voting procedures. In December, each MPO Board member voted for their top five priority projects in order to create a countywide list of top ten priority projects. When staff combined the Nov. Rank list and the Dec. Rank list, only three projects met both priorities (projects that scored higher (80 or more) using the vetting criteria and projects voted into the top 10 list in December). MAG staff believe that the process used over the last four months to create the list of projects provided an opportunity for the entire county to better understand countywide needs and priorities and was a good exercise. Due to the lack of legislative support for a list that is not 100% data driven MAG staff recommends that we do not promulgate a list of projects for the 2024 legislative session. Unified Transportation Plan Update LaNiece Davenport, MPO Director (1:31:59) The MAG MPO, WFRC, Cache MPO, Dixie MPO, UDOT, and UTA have finalized Utah's Unified Transportation Plan (UTP) 2023-2050. The Unified Plan incorporates projects, revenues, and costs of the recently adopted MPO regional transportation plan, and UDOT rural transportation plan. This nationally recognized process comprehensively includes all MPO Board January 11, 2024 Page 6 modes of transportation (roadway, transit, and active transportation). It collectively identifies new capacity needs to accommodate for growth in travel demand and the maintenance, preservation, and operation of the transportation system, in addition to a financial plan. The UTP identifies $153 billion (2023 dollars) of total transportation needs statewide between now and 2050, and is fiscally constrained to $113 billion of these needs, based on the revenues that are projected to be available during that period. The UTP is a shared vision for how transportation can support key quality of life outcomes through better mobility, good health, connected communities, and a strong economy. An updated interactive map is available on the plan website to review capacity projects. A user can view projects by planning area, mode of transportation, phase of time, UDOT region, Senate district, and House district.
