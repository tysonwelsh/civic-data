4.       CONSIDERATION AND RECOMMENDATION on Proposed Ordinance Text
         Amendments to Bluffdale City Code Sections 3.80 (Home Occupations), 6.20 (Truck
         Routes), 11.20 and 11.160 (Land Use Ordinance) to Update the Standards Related to
         Truck Routes, Storage, Use, and Parking of Large Commercial Vehicles in Certain
         Residential Zones. (Application 2022-07) - Staff Presenter, Grant Crowell. (Continued
         from April 6, 2022 Meeting.)

City Pla1U1er/Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell, presented the staff repo11 and state
that a public hearing took place at the April 6 meeting. His understanding was that the Planning
Commission wanted the proposed ordinance text amendments to be less restrictive. Mr. Crowell
enumerated the changes as follows:

     •   The Truck Routes Map was reconciled with the ordinance text to remove Honda Park Drive,
         Concord Park Drive, 790 West, and 800 West. These streets do not need to be included in the
         routes map because they are directly accessed from 14600 South and Heritage Crest Way,
         which are designated routes. He provided an updated map.

     • Renamed proposed 11.160.300 to Commercial Vehicles in Residential Zones .

     •   Added "personal vehicles" and " trailers for hauling livestock" to the definition of Recreational
         Vehicle (which are technically not considered as conm1ercial vehicles). There was discussion
         as to whether a vehicle with a sleeping facility is considered a Recreational Vehicle. The issue
         ofrecreational vehicles was resolved by revi sing the definition.

     •   Added an allowance to use of restricted vehicles during agricu ltural harvest times .

     •   Added an allowance to be able to idle for up to 15 minutes between 10:00 p.m. and 7:00 a.m.
         (for the allowed take-home truck). Previously, idling a truck was prohibited. The City
         Attorney crafted this sentence. A noisy vehicle idling for more than 15 minutes currently
         constitutes a noise violation. This amendment would allow for an exception so that drivers
         can get their brakes running so the truck is ready to be driven.

     •   Removed requirement for the allowed vehicle to be screened or placed behind the front plane
         of the dwelling on the lot. This meant removal of the fencing requirement.

     • Removed requirement to be located on a truck route to address the public comment
         from last meeting.

     •   Changed minimum lot size to 0.95 acre to address public comment for those living in lots in
         Country Living Estates on 15000 S which are 0.98 acres. This would include A-5 , R-1-43 ,
         and R-1-87 zones.

        Applicants (Application 2022-10) - Staff Presenter, Jennifer Robison.

Senior City Planner, Jennifer Robison, presented the staff repo1t and noted that D AI (aka Banner Hill,
LLC, submitted a request to include in some of their multi -fam ily projects a sales and leasing type of

Development Agreement created in 20 12.

Mrs. Robison next reviewed the General Definitions in the Zoning Code and noted that it included an
item called "Office, General" 11.20.020. Within that definition reference is made to " property
management firms" If the entire definition were modified, it cou ld be done by add ing the phrase
" including sales/leasing." That was the current proposal.

office." This type of office facility is typically very small. Often, the sales/leasing office is located
in an end unit. Since the subject area will have more rental unit types, the applicant was suggesting
a permanent sales office that would not occupy one of the rental units.

lf the proposal were approved, it would be reflected in the Mixed Use Table of Allowed Uses. ln the
Residential Uses column, it would be labeled as "Property Management Sales/ Leasing Office,". In
the Approval Required column it would be deemed as an Accessory Use to the residential uses.

Mrs. Robison commented that the request was made specifically for the apartment complex project
of rental units. Typically, developments have a leasing/sales office in a clubhouse or sales/leasing
unit. In townhome units, for example, a townhome is used as the sales office until the area sells out
and the sales office is then sold as a residence. Since the project being developed is just for rental
units, the applicant seeks to have a completely separate facility to serve as the sales/leasing office. If
the proposal is approved, the developer would be able to apply for the building using the normal site
plan approval processes. Mrs. Robison noted that in the Independence Development Agreement, site
plan review and approval must be obtained for anything that is non-residential or for multi-family
residential portions of the project. The Planning Commission is the Land Use Authority that approves
site plans. The sales/leasing office approval process would be the same and the Pl aiming Commission
would have to approve the site plan.

Vice Chair Luker asked about the scope of the property management office. If the office is
responsible for properties outside of the development he was concerned that it might create inequities
between what the developers are allowed to do and what other professionals are not. He referred to
the "exclusions" identified in 11.20.020. Mrs . Robison explained that it would not be necessary to
make the proposed change to the General Definitions in 11 .20.020 if the definition in 11.11.070.030
Mixed Use Zone is approved. The motion could include a recommendation to not include the
amended definition in 11.20.020.

In response to Conm1issioner Griffis' question regarding the proposed size of the property
management office, Mrs. Robison stated that the one being proposed is quite small. She reiterated
that the applicant would still have to go tlu·ough the site plan process so the Planning Conm1i ssion
would have some authority to specify what an acceptable size would be. The building would also be
subject to all Building Permit requirements and would have to include a Business License.

to maintain a residential look.

Commissioner Flynn asked what would happen to the office, as an accessory unit, if at some point in
the future the developer decides to sell off all of the townhomes, Mrs. Robison stated that there were
no provisions included for that scenario. Commissioner Flynn was reluctant to make changes if they

Commissioner Griffis asked why there was a distinction between the two sets of definitions included
in the packet. Mrs. Robison explained that City Code includes "General Definitions" that could be
applied to all the different zoning districts in Bluffdale. Having definitions specific to a zone place
limitations on how those definitions can be applied. The changes were included in both definitions
because Staff did not know what direction the Pla1ming Commission or City Council would want to
go with regard to the definitions. If the definition were applied just to the Mixed Used Code, that
would fill the needs of the applicant. Commissioner Flynn liked the idea of making sure the allowance
for a property management office is narrowed down to a specific place and purpose. By making the
facility an accessory to the property it serves, it could no longer function as a property management
office or any other type of office.
