6. CONSIDERATION AND VOTE on a proposed Condominium Plat for Nipko Medical

Office Building, located at 1595 West 14000 South, Zoned Gateway Redwood (6W-R),

to Create Seven Commercial Condo Units, LDHB Properties, LLC, Applicant (2025-12)

— Staff Presenter, Ellen Qakman.
Associate City Planner, Ellen Oakman, presented the Staff Report and stated that Lot 21 of the Ranch
was being considered tonight. The Site Plan for Lot 21 of the Nipko Medical Office Building was
approved previously. The current request is for a condominium plat to create seven separate suites
in the existing building. The Ranch subdivision was approved by the City Council and recorded in
October of 2019. The Ranch Amended Subdivision was approved and recorded in March of 2023.
There was an additional Amended Subdivision by Smith’s Fuel Center, and the Nipko Medical Office
Site Plan was approved administratively in March of 2024. The Site Plans for The Ranch are
administrative because they were previously reviewed by the City Council. The current approval is
to create seven separate legal condominium units, with the Site Plan being part of the proposed
condominium plat. Photos of the site were presented of the different condominium units on the first
and second floors.
Commissioner Flynn referenced the packet that addresses the legal descriptions and the fact that the
internal units have changed, while the easements and setbacks were not impacted by the adjustment.
Ms. Oakman explained that no external changes were made, and the modifications were to create
legal ownership of the interior units.
The applicant, Logan Johnson from Wright Development, was present and gave the business address
as 1178 West Legacy Crossing in Centerville. What is proposed is a final step, as the building is now
complete. They have a few interested tenants and are prepared to close on the first few condominium
units. They need the plat to be recorded to do so. The only change was to the building plans, where
the walls may have shifted slightly, and to solidify the size of the units. The tenants include an
Orthopedic Surgeon. Mr. Johnson explained that there is a restriction on dental, so there will be no

7. CONSIDERATION _AND VOTE on _a Proposed Site Plan Amendment for the
Independence Rock Apartments to Modify Architectural Materials and Design and to
Reduce the Unit Mix and Unit Count from 218 to 217 (Modification to Application 2022-
18). Dakota Pacific, Represented by Ray Bryson, Applicant. Staff Presenter, Caitlyn
Tubbs.

Planning Manager, Caitlyn Tubbs, presented the Staff Report and stated that the request is an

amendment to a Site Plan that was reviewed and approved by the Planning Commission on March 1,

2023. It has since undergone an ownership change, with the new owner being Dakota Pacific, who

has conducted more up-to-date market studies and is proposing revisions to the project to respond to

the new market findings. They are looking to add amenity spaces and update the unit mix within the

buildings. With that, they would like to update some of the elevations, accommodate different

materials and colors, and change the roof line, as it will have a significant effect on the cost of

construction. They are also working with the Redevelopment Agency (“RDA”) to reach an agreement

for Tax Increment Financing (“TIF”) for the project. They are looking to dial in the construction
costs while bringing a high-quality product to the City.
Ms. Tubbs reported that this project was part of the original Independence Master Plan as part of
Area P. This is the only remaining ground to be developed in Independence. The applicant has
provided an exhibit showing the vicinity of the project and how it will fit into the overall
Independence area. The market study conducted was referenced. As approved, the apartment
buildings included studio through two-bedroom units; however, the current property owner has found
that there is less demand for studios in the area and more demand for larger units to accommodate
living areas for young families or those who are aging out of larger homes.
As a result, they are proposing to remove some of the studio units and accommodate more three-
bedroom units. The original Site Plan entitled the applicants to 218 units. As proposed, the total unit
count would be 217. A portion of the change pertains to the number of amenities they would like to
provide to their tenants. An image was presented showing a rendering of the amenities space and the
swimming pool deck from the original approval. There is a small cube that is about half the height
of the overall structure, which was the amenity space from the original approval. The current
applicant has decided to add some spaces for children and work from home office availability, which
has increased in volume from half the height of the building to the full height of the building.
Floor plans of the amenities were presented and included a pool deck, exercise facility, community
room, parcel pickup area, and a game lounge. The applicants were also looking to update some of
the materials. They are still proposing a brick and Hardy board siding. The only change was to the
paint shade, which will remain a warm neutral color.
Ms. Tubbs reported that the applicants are looking to update the architectural style of the building,
which will be similar to what was originally proposed. On the roof structure, there are more steps up
and down than in the original approval. When the applicant looked through the plans with their
General Contractor, they found that it would involve a separate roof structure, and they determined
that simplifying it while maintaining a similar style would benefit the project in the long run and still
maintain an attractive-looking facade. They also enlarged the windows in the new proposal. Staff

looked through the proposal, the original Development Agreement, and approval packets and
recommended approval to the Planning Commission.
The applicant, Ray Bryson from Dakota Pacific, was present to answer questions.
Commissioner Kraupp asked about Dakota Pacific’s work with the RDA and the type of tax benefits
they are seeking. Ms. Tubbs stated that they are looking to deed restrict 30% of the units to an 80%
Area Median Income (“AMI”) for a term of 20 years. They continue to work through the actual
agreement documents, but they will be looking to accommodate lower-income families that make
80% of the AMI or less in the area. They would provide the same high-quality product in the deed-
restricted units as the market rate units.
In response to a question raised by Commissioner Flynn regarding the extent of the proposed changes,
Ms. Tubbs explained that the unit mix has changed, and they are proposing more amenities. As a
result, the internal layout of the building has changed. The architectural style, however, will remain
substantially the same, as will the materials. There is additional volume required for the additional
amenity spaces; however, the footprint, parking, and landscaping will not be impacted.
Mr. Bryson reported that the windows are 40% larger than in the previous plans, which will have a
huge impact on livability and the perception of the space from the outside. The amenity spaces in the
originally approved proposal included 4,500 square feet of amenity space. They have increased that
to 12,000 square feet. He noted that amenities in the market today are a way to maintain the tenant
base. There is less turnover when they provide spaces that tenants enjoy. One of the features they
think will be important for the space is a daycare play area for children within the gym space. That
is not often seen in urban-type settings, but they are trying to meet the needs of the market and the
user base, which is why they changed the unit mix. They are decreasing the project by one unit, but
also decreasing it by 12 bedrooms, which has a significant impact on the parking requirement. They
would make more money with the extra unit or by having more bedrooms, but they believe it will be
better to lease and for those who will live in the space.
Mr. Bryson corrected the material changes and stated that they originally proposed a metal panel,
which is extremely expensive. They are proposing a change to a board and batten Hardy plank. The

Another change Mr. Bryson considered to be significant was to the retaining walls on the property.
The originally approved project included all concrete retaining walls. He noted that there is a
significant amount of fall from the south to the north. On the north side is a five-story building, and
on the south is a four-story building. The fall is at thé maximum allowed grade to park and adheres
to the Americans with Disabilities Act (“ADA”). In addition to that site change, there are four to five
feet on the south side of the property to the road. The retaining walls range in height from six to 12
feet. When excavating for the footings, the Geotechnical Report requires them to lay the walls back
at nearly a 45° angle. Geneva owns the property to the south, and utility easements run along the
property line. If they were to excavate the walls, on the east side, they would be required to be nearly
50 feet into the neighboring property for the footing wall. There are challenges in doing that, and the
cost would be significant. They found that using an H-Pile System for retaining walls, they do not
have to excavate the site to the neighboring property or impede any of those easements. There is also
a significant cost savings in excavation and backfilling costs, but also construction of the retaining
wall. They will have the added benefit of growing vegetation and being able to paint it. That was
determined to be a need of the property along the entire south boundary.
Mr. Bryson included the sections that were originally proposed as concrete to be part of an H-Pile
system. They are driven into the ground before excavation takes place. Drainage issues were
discussed. Mr. Bryson stated that they will need a French drain system to avoid pressure behind the
wall. The wood panel system will connect the drains behind the system and allow it to drain to the
lower surface. In response to a question raised by Chair Cragun, Mr. Bryson stated that the H-Piles
will have to be buried into the ground to withstand the pressure. The depth will depend on the soil
condition. An Engineer will work with their Geotechnical Engineer to design the system. He
explained that tie-backs have been drilled into the soil to help carry the load. Chair Cragun
commented that the proposed wall looks better than a cement wall would.

Commissioner Flynn asked about the durability of the timber to be placed between the sections.
Mr. Bryson explained that it is a 3” x 12” plank. Some are pressure-treated. Redwood products do
not have as much resistance load as pine, which is pressure-treated to withstand corrosion over time.
Typically, buildings are designed to have a 50-year life span. They would want the wall to withstand
water and sun for at least that amount of time. Potential materials were discussed. He noted that
there will be a five to 10-year Maintenance Plan.
Mr. Bryson stated that they hope to break ground in September, but recognized that there are various
things, such as the RDA Agreement, that will define the parameters. They are currently in discussions
with lenders who are asking for construction documents and Contractor Agreements. Their goal is to
submit construction documents in early August.
Commissioner Kraupp asked Mr. Bryson if they do not receive the desired tax benefits if they will
proceed with the project. Mr. Bryson explained that there are two parts to the RDA request. One is
the deed restriction portion, while the other is an incentive for them to get started right away. If they
do not receive the incentive, it would be very difficult for them to begin work today based on loan
interest rates and construction costs. He recognized that they could be forced to go six to 18 months
without the incentive. They were pleased that the RDA Board was willing to support the request and
the need to provide housing. He believed his company’s interests align well with the community in
terms of providing housing and affordable housing options. Providing housing this way allows
tenants to get more amenity spaces. He noted that bonded housing projects typically limit the amenity
spaces and features that exist in market-rate housing. Mr. Bryson and his team believe it is a net
benefit to the community and the project to integrate those into the community. He noted that without
the tax benefits, they would still move forward with the project. They were pleased, however, that
the goals of the RDA seem to align with theirs.
Community and Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell, noted that staff has been trying to
figure out how to spend the affordable housing set aside in the community. Some has been given to
Salt Lake County, and there are other options that distribute it further. The thought was that in a few
years, the City could be inundated with thousands of units. With regard to affordable housing, the
