7.3         Resolution No. 20-30 – A Resolution of the City of Taylorsville to ratify the
Mayor’s letter to the Unified Police Department – Mayor Kristie Overson

       Chair Harker explained that the City of Taylorsville was at a crossroads regarding police
services. She noted that the City had contracted with Unified Police since 2012 but was now
considering leaving UPD and starting its own police department once again. She indicated that
John Taylor and Tracy Cowdell would provide some background information and then the Council
would open the floor to any citizen comments on the matter.

Mr. Taylor began his comments by stressing the great value of the UPD officers and the work they
did for the Taylorsville community. He also stated that the City administration would not
recommend moving in the proposed direction without very careful consideration of a variety of
factors. He explained that the Unified Police of 2012 had changed drastically in the last eight
years. The organization had continuously decreased in size, reducing the opportunities for
economy of scale. He touched on several outside influences that influenced the organization of
UPD. For example,
     Three original member cities had left UPD to form their own police departments.
    • Taylorsville needed to consider what the cost for services would be if it remained with
        UPD, but other member cities chose to leave
    • Midvale (who had also recently discussed the possibility of leaving UPD) estimated much
        higher potential startup costs than did Taylorsville because they were assuming the “net
        pension liability” would need to be paid to UPD up front. It did not appear that Herriman
        or Riverton made such cash payments when they left UPD, nor did Taylorsville anticipate
        doing so. Additionally, Midvale would need to construct a building to house a police
        department whereas Taylorsville would not (but would have some costs to re-configure the
        current precinct in city hall.)
    • It was important to not just look at the current situation but to anticipate what was likely to
        take place 2-4 years into the future
    • Taylorsville’s portion of liabilities would be roughly $1.5 million, and assets would be
        about $2 million
    • Taylorsville had pushed very hard for increased officer compensation for quite some time.
        UPD had failed to keep up with compensation increases and had also chosen to postpone
        vehicle replacement.

    •   The costs of membership in Unified Police would only increase over the next couple of
        years, possibly substantially so.
    •   UPD was dipping into the fund balance (savings) each year—most recently to the tune of
        about $3.2 million. This was not a sustainable way to operate.
    •   UPD had acknowledged that if Midvale were to withdraw as a member city and
        Taylorsville were to remain, Taylorsville’s cost would increase by about $1.14 million

Councilmember Cochran pointed out that if Midvale were to leave UPD but Taylorsville remained,
a property tax increase would be necessary. Vice Chair Christopherson ran a quick calculation
which estimated that the increase would need to be approximately 32%.

Vice-Chair Christopherson explained that the UPD Board had directed UPD administration to
reduce the shared services budget and shift those costs to individual precincts which did nothing
to reduce the overall UPD budget. And Copperton, with only 900 residents, had as much influence
over Taylorsville’s costs and police policy as did Taylorsville itself. That was not the case when
Taylorsville joined the UPD in 2012. The Vice Chair also wished to point out that costs for UPD
services increased this year for not only Taylorsville, but Midvale, Millcreek and Holladay as well.
Yet somehow Salt Lake County’s cost went down. Other than withdrawing from UPD,
Taylorsville’s only options were to either reduce police services or pass the increased costs to the
citizens in the form of a property tax increase. The four cities that provided 60% of UPD’s budget
had no ability to influence policy decisions or make much needed changes.

Mr. Taylor explained that the City had made repeated efforts to work with UPD administration
over the last 2 ½ years but that UPD had eventually made it clear that the changes the City needed
to see were not going to happen. Both he and Vice Chair Christopherson confirmed that the actual
police services provided by UPD were excellent. But economies of scale had been lost and UPD
had not been willing to address the issues that had been brought forth by the City. Costs were
going to increase even more the following year and officer compensation had still not been
addressed. The Board had promised to address it but had failed to do so.

      City Attorney Tracy Cowdell explained that he had been with the City when it had its own
police department and now as it contracted with Unified Police. He agreed that the women and
men with Unified Police and the services they provided were outstanding. However, there needed
to be improvement for the organization to be sustainable. UPD needed to make significant
changes in order to continue to operate long term. While he could not speak to the financial issues,
he was there to weigh in on legal and policy matters.
    • There needed to be material changes to the interlocal agreement for police services. Where
        those changes were once aspirational, they were now imperative.
    • During the last several years, Taylorsville had worked internally to make policy and
        governance changes, specifically to provide for weighted voting so that, for example, if
        Taylorsville was responsible for 68% of the total membership and budget, it was not left
        with only 33% of the vote when it came to policy making.

Mr. Cowdell continued to explain some of the steps the City had taken to influence policy changes
that would make the UPD model sustainable and acceptable for Taylorsville.
    • Taylorsville had sought to collaborate with other UPD board members
    • Mayor Overson had repeatedly raised the issues in board meetings
    • The City sought to send issues to an interlocal re-write committee
    • City administration had participated in the audit with the State Auditor
    • Taylorsville had attempted to get a bill passed which would have made structural changes
       to the form of governance

Councilmember Cochran returned at 7:52 pm.

The audit findings in 2018 indicated that:

     •   The Interlocal Agreement with UPD “established a weak governance structure.”
     •   “The UPD Board does not control the selection of the CEO and the CEO is not directly
         accountable to the Board.”
     •   “The CEO can unilaterally veto (UPD) Board policy”
     •    “Voters served by UPD are out-numbered by voters outside of UPD service area.”
     •   The independent State Auditor ultimately recommended that the UPD Board amend the
         interlocal agreement with members to ensure that the Board can hold management
         accountable through means such as appointment, removal and setting compensation for
         police officers.

Nothing had been done to adjust so that the UPD was more congruent with the findings of the State
Auditor.

Mr. Cowdell stated that his advice to the City Council as its legal counsel, was to ensure that the
City had more say in policy decisions, budgeting, and compensation. To do that, Taylorsville
needed to have control of its police services. UPD was a great fit when the model was created but
times had changed, and he believed it was time for Taylorsville to return to providing police
services itself.

     Councilmember Armstrong felt that Salt Lake County and the unincorporated areas had
gotten a sweet deal at Taylorsville’s expense. Unified Police had no incentive to give Taylorsville
a proportional vote and it was time for the City to make a change.

      Mayor Kristie Overson wished to clarify that the voting structure at UPD was tweaked 2 ½
years ago, in a baby step for moving toward weighted voting. She compromised and said yes to
that baby step 2/1/2 years ago, but it hadn’t been enough.

Chair Harker said it was clear how much time and effort had been expended toward resolving
issues so that the City could continue to remain with Unified Police.

Vice Chair Christopherson spoke in favor of ratifying the letter of intent to withdraw from UPD
and said he had already been asked if he would reconsider that position. However, if UPD made
