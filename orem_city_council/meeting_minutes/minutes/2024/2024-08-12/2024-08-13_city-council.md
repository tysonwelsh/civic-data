Alpine Aqueduct Project Update Presenter: Cort Lambson, Project Manager Central
Utah Water Conservancy District

Mr. Tschirki introduced Mr. Lambson who is the project manager for the Alpine
Aqueduct project. The Alpine Aqueduct is the main water line that comes through the upper part
of the Cascade Golf Course area. This area has experienced landslides and seismic activity over
the years. Approximately 60% of Orem’s drinking water comes through this pipe.

Mr. Lambson explained that the Alpine Aqueduct is a key component of Utah's Central
Utah Project, which aims to efficiently use the state's Colorado River water allocations.

Specifically, the Alpine Aqueduct is part of the Bonneville Unit, designed to transport water
from the Provo River Basin to the Bonneville Basin.

The aqueduct system includes the municipal and industrial (M&I) system, which conveys
water from various mountain lakes and dams to the Jordanelle Reservoir and then via the Provo
River to Deer Creek Reservoir where the water is discharged into the Lower Provo River and
then diverted to CUWCD’s Don A. Christiansen Regional Water Treatment Plant at the Olmsted
Diversion. From there, water travels through aqueducts to north Utah and Salt Lake Counties,
with deliveries reaching up to 21st South in Salt Lake.

Alpine Aqueduct Reach 1, which was originally constructed in 1980 and since repaired in
2001, is critical for water distribution because it supplies approximately 60% of Orem's drinking
water and serves over 1.6 million people along the Wasatch Front as supplemental water.

Recent geological studies revealed the aqueduct crosses an ancient landslide (Paleo slide)
and several fault lines, necessitating a redesign to avoid the slide and address fault movement. A
proposal includes rerouting the aqueduct to bypass the slide and crossing the fault at a right angle
to handle displacement. With a significant earthquake risk in the region (43% chance of a 6.75
magnitude quake in the next 50 years), ensuring the aqueduct's resilience is crucial.

The new design involves a tunnel and vertical shaft, with construction expected to start in
the fall. The project will use specialized pipe manufactured in Japan, which can handle seismic
displacement. This update will take two construction seasons, with tunneling beginning on the
east end while pipe fabrication continues.

The Council and staff asked questions about how this will affect the residents in that area
and how it will be coordinated with those residents.

The city facilities project involves significant updates, including the rebuilding of sewer,
water, and storm drain systems, with either full or partial reconstruction based on the location.
Residential streets used as haul routes for heavy trucks will be damaged but will be repaired with
milling and overlaying once construction is complete. Emergency access will be maintained by
coordinating with the contractor and Orem’s Fire Department and Police Department. CUWCD
will be requesting a non-exclusive easement on 1060 North and 1360 East where the pipeline
will be constructed. Currently, the project is in the final stages of acquiring the necessary
easements and pre-qualifying contractors. The design is 90% complete and the target completion
date is October 14.

The City Council had questions about the current pipeline and how it works, as well as
how the new pipeline will work. They also discussed who residents will contact if they have
questions. Central Utah Water will have a hotline for questions and will let the City Council
know when the neighborhood meetings are held.

Station Area Plan Presentation Presenter: Grant Allen, Senior Planner

Mr. Allen presented the Orem Station Area Plan. The overall objectives are to maximize
development potential in appropriate areas and have cites (and citizens) determine how best to
meet shared objectives. The shared objectives are to increase the availability and affordability of
housing, promote sustainable environmental conditions, and increase transportation choices and
connections. The public visioning and station area plan process is first, a public workshop for
brainstorming. Second, hold an open house to consider preferences. Third, have a vision concept
celebration to show vision scenarios. Fourth, Implementation strategy and adoption of the station
area plan. The 2023-2024 public engagement include | planning commission meeting, 3 city
council meetings, 7 stakeholder meetings, brainstorming workshops, brainstorming real time and
online surveys, scenarios evaluation workshop, scenarios evaluation real time and online surrey,
vision celebration public meeting and outreach postcard, newsletter, and social media.

The Orem Station Area plan scenario chosen will be presented to Mountainland
Associated Governments (MAG). MAG is the approval body selected by the state. The plan
scenario will be presented tonight to the City Council. Mr. Allen displayed images of the chosen
plan scenario. The station area plan certification process includes applying 45 days prior to
scheduled SAP Policy Committee meetings, delivering needed documents to MAG for review,
and MAG will circulate the application to UTA for a two-week review and comment period
After the review MAG will deliver a list of deficiencies, the applicant responds to MAG and
UTA comments, and then Orem will present the Orem Frontrunner SAP to SAP policy
Committee Meeting on September 18. Following the SAP Committee Meeting, MAG SAP
Policy Committee will issue a conditional certification, Orem SAP will return for final adoption
in the General Plan and any applicable zoning updates, and MAG will issue the final certification
following receipt of applicable resolutions. The deadline for all plans is December 31, 2025.

Ms. Millett is concerned about some parts of the station area plan and would like to table
or postpone the proposed resolution in today’s meeting. She would like to see the plan more
targeted and less general. The City Council questioned and discussed the station area plan
proposal. The Council plans to table this station area plan for today’s City Council meeting.

The Riries will be the directors of the Orem Geneva Heights myHometown community.

Citizen’s Budget Presentation Presenter: Jennica Jones, Strategy and Innovation Division

Manager and Trevor Bell, Budget Division Manager

Mr. Bell explained that this citizen’s budget is an approach to make complex financial
information accessible and understandable to the citizens. It is meant to simplify the 297 page
fiscal City budget that represents 9 months of planning and forecasting, 6 public meetings, and
countless hours of staff work. Going forward the citizen budget will be presented to the City
Council at the same time as the full budget.

Ms. Jones explained that this citizen’s budget will be available digitally on the City
website on the financial page and transparency portal. Printed copies will be available at the 311
help center and the Orem library. The City Council were grateful for the staff involved in
creating this budget.

Resolution - Temporary Judge for Justice Court Presenter: D. Jacob Summers, Deputy City
