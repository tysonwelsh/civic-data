Order of the Salt Lake County Council Calling a Special meeting of the Salt Lake County 
Council on January 13, 2022 at 4:00 P.M.
Consistent with Utah Code Ann. § 17-53-205, Section 2.09 of the Optional Plan for Salt 
Lake County Government, and Section 2.04.090 of Salt Lake County Ordinance, a special 
meeting of the Salt Lake County Council has been ordered by a majority of the Council . 
The meeting has been ordered by to be held on January 13, 2022, at -4:00 P.M. in Room 
N1-100 of the Salt Lake County Government Center.
The purpose of the proposed meeting will be for the Salt Lake County Council to 
consider exercising its authority under Utah Code Ann. §26A-1-114(7)(d) to terminate an 
order of constraint issued by the Salt Lake County Health Department on or about 
January 7, 2022. A copy of the proposed agenda for this special meeting is attached to 
this order.     
Council staff shall provide notice of this special meeting to the Salt Lake County Council 
and Salt Lake County Mayor, and shall also provide notice to the public and media as 
required by the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act, Utah Code Ann. § 52-4-101 et seq ., 
and Salt Lake County Ordinance. A copy of this order shall be included in the minutes of 
the special meeting.

2. DISCUSSION ITEMS
2.1 A Resolution of the Salt Lake County Council Terminating 
an Order of Constraint Issued by the Salt Lake County 
Health Department On or About January 7, 2022 in 
Response to a Declared Public Health Emergency
21-1496
Attachments: Resolution Terminating an Order of Constraint January 2022 
(AATF)
Public Health Order of Constraint
A motion was made by Council Member Alvord, seconded by Council 
Member Theodore, that this agenda item be approved.  
A substitute motion was made by Council Member Theodore that should the 
Council vote to repeal the order, it adopt a resolution strongly encouraging 
the Health Department to convert the order and publish it as a health 
advisory. 
Council Member Stringham  stated this should be bifurcated so the 
Council can vote on one and then the other.  
Mr. Mitchell Park , Legal Counsel, Council Office, cautioned the Council 
that under Senate Bill 195, County legislative bodies do not have the ability to 
modify or amend public health orders; they can only be overturned.  
A motion was made by Council Member Alvord, seconded by Council Member 
Theodore, that this agenda item be approved. The motion failed by the 
following vote:
Council Member Snelgrove, Council Member Alvord, Council 
Member DeBry, and Council Member Theodore
Aye:
Chair Stringham, Council Member Bradley, Council Member 
Bradshaw, Council Member Winder Newton, and Council 
Member Granato
Nay:
Council Member Stringham  asked if there were any comments Council Members

wished to make regarding their vote. 
Council Member Alvord  asked that everyone show respect to the elected officials 
regardless of their vote.  His constituency is well informed and has made their wishes clear 
to him.   
Council Member Newton  stated she appreciates all the feedback she has received.  She 
understands masks are heavily politicized and nobody likes to wear them.  People often 
bristle when government tells them what to do, but our community is in an unprecedented 
crisis and everybody needs to work together.  The virus has resulted in supply issues and 
schools are threatening to close and many businesses are experiencing staffing shortages, 
including the hospitals and emergency responders.  Health care workers are exhausted and 
feel abandoned.  Even if masks do just a little to stop the spread, it will be worth it.  Data 
from other states shows that the virus hits hard for a few weeks and then subsides.  She 
feels there are better times ahead and encouraged Utahns to work together and help each 
other.   
Council Member Granato  stated her position is based on research that has been very 
extensive as well as overwhelming input from her district.    
Council Member DeBry stated he has already commented and those comments stand.  
Council Member Theodore  stated some of the input from the public has been very 
aggressive, and she asked the public for kindness and respect;.  Her position has been the 
same all along. Most of her constituents do not want a mask mandate.  She believes in 
science, but feels the science on this issue has been skewed - people are still getting sick 
and hospitalized and this mandate will not stop that.  
Council Member Stringham stated she serves all of Salt Lake County, which is very 
divided on the issue of masks.  Some areas are very red, and others very blue.  She 
worries about schools being able to stay open and businesses and restaurants who cannot 
operate due to staffing issues. First responders and hospitals are having staffing issues.  A 
mandate is not the best option, but her choices are limited. Salt Lake County has ample 
masks sitting in warehouses and as Chair, she cannot order that those masks be 
distributed to those who want them without a mandate.  She plans to meet with State 
leaders on Monday to devise a plan that will allow for choice.  If they can come up with a 
plan, she will call another meeting and overturn the mandate.
