ORDINANCE 11-05-2024 GOVERNING SHORT-TERM RENTALS ADOPTED:

     Mr. Atkinson presented Ordinance 11-05-2024 to the council for adoption.  He explained that the Planning Commission had recommended this ordinance governing short-term rentals.  He stated that it is currently illegal to have a short-term rental in Nephi City but they do exist.  The ordinance will allow for a short-term rental and set the terms for how one may operate.
     A short-term rental is a residential unit that is rented for less than 30 consecutive days to the same person or entity.  The ordinance does not allow them in internal accessory dwelling units, accessory structures, or temporary living structures.  The rental unit must be licensed and pay the city and county transient room tax.  The previous draft required a fire inspection, but the Fire District does not offer that service.  Initial building inspections will be inspected for fire safety.  The previous draft was also under discussion for the off-street parking requirements.  After that discussion, the current ordinance requires an off-street parking space per guest bedroom with a 1 guest bedroom unit requiring 2 spaces.  
     The ordinance does not set a maximum for the number of short-term rentals units allowed..  Staff is recommending that the city wait to see how many are licensed and determine whether or not the housing supply is affected for residential homeowners.  If there is an impact, a maximum limit could then be set.  
     Councilor Skip Worwood moved to adopt Ordinance 11-05-2024-Short Term Rentals.  Councilor Jeramie Callaway seconded the motion.  The motion passed on a roll call vote.

RESOLUTION 11-05-2045 -THIRD PARTY EMPLOYEE REIMBURSEMENT POLICY ADDED TO THE NEPHI CITY PERSONNEL POLICIES AND PROCEDURES ADOPTED:

   Mr. Atkinson reviewed with the council that from time to time, there are third party companies and individuals who break or damage city infrastructure.  This has recently happened several times with our electric department.  In most of these instances, the insurance policies of these companies or individuals reimburse the city for the damages.  In the case of these instances happening after business hours, the city is reimbursed at an employee’s overtime rate, or 1.5 hours per hour worked.  
       The city would bill the responsible party and, in most cases, collects for the costs of the repairs.  Currently the city has a policy that requires overtime hours to be compensated back to the employee in the form of compensatory time.  This gives the employee time in their leave bank that they can use during the year.  The city also has a policy that requires each employee to have their compensatory time down to a certain level by April 1st of each year.  
     Mr. Atkinson presented Resolution 11-05-2024 that would make revisions to the Nephi City Personnel Policy and Procedures Article VI to allow these reimbursements for afterhours overtime to be paid out once per year to employees who have eligible hours.  This would allow the employees to be compensated for these hours at no cost to the city and to reduce their compensatory time bank.  In this way, the employees could be more available during the time leading up to the April 1st deadline.  Eligible hours would only include hours for which the city has received a reimbursement.  Department Heads would be responsible for tracking those hours and verifying with the Finance Department that the city has received a reimbursement.  Receiving the overtime compensation versus the compensatory time would be at the employee's discretion. 

     Councilor Travis Worwood moved to adopt Resolution 11-05-2024.  Councilor Shari Cowan seconded the motion.  The motion passed unanimously.

NATURAL GAS RATE STABILIZATION FUND DISCUSSED:
	•	Mr. Atkinson explained to the council that for the last couple of years the city has hedged their gas rates at a higher than historical average.  This was due to some volatility in the market at the time that the city needed to lock into a new hedged cost rate with Summit Energy.  The hedging proved extremely beneficial when the price of natural gas skyrocketed to 10 times its normal rate just a few months after we locked in a rate.  Unfortunately, the price of natural gas has now gone below historic averages, and the city is still locked in to a higher hedge rate.  Locking in a firm price is the blessing and curse of hedging a cost.
UMPA also deals with similar market forces when dealing with electricity.  UMPA members benefit from their policy of having a rate stabilization fund.  When prices are high, UMPA can pull funds from the rate stabilization fund and help members to weather a large price fluctuation.  This, in turn, allows Nephi City to offer stable prices to their customers.
Mr. Atkinson said that he would like to recommend establishing something similar in the city’s natural gas rates.  Specifically, adjusting the rates to include a small portion that will be allocated to a rate stabilization fund.  Once this fund has sufficient resources, it will provide the city with flexibility when deciding on a hedge time frame or rate and will provide customers with stable gas rates. 
He stated that no specific rates are being recommended at this time.  He suggested maybe hiring a consultant to help with establishing the rates.  The council agreed with the concept and directed staff to continue studying the idea.
