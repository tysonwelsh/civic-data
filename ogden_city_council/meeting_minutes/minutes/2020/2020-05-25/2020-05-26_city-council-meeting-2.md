Proposed Resolution 2020-12 adopting the 2020 Ogden-Hinckley Airport
Master Plan
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department came before the Council to consider a resolution
adopting the 2020 Ogden-Hinckley Airport Master Plan. The memo stated the City contracted with Airport Design Group in
August 2018 to develop a comprehensive master plan for the Ogden-Hinckley Airport. The master plan will serve as a guide for
the continued development of the airport as well as meeting requirements from the Federal Aviation Administration to receive
federal funding. The proposed master plan covers six basic areas including inventory, forecasts, facility requirements,
development alternatives, implementation, and an airport layout plan. This is the first comprehensive master plan developed for
the Airport since 1994. The proposed master plan will be accompanied by updates to the Transportation Element of the City's
General Plan. Those amendments will be addressed in a separate item for consideration.
           Ogden Airport Manager Garrett summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the
proposed Master Plan, the purposes of which include the following:
                • Define Big Picture/Vision for 20+ Years;
                •   Respond to industry, air transportation system and community trends;
                •   Justify to the Federal Aviation Authority (FAA) the need for development projects; and
                •   To be eligible for FAA funding up to 90.63 percent, a project must be shown on the approved Airport Layout
                    Plan (ALP).
           The community outreach program utilized to facilitate development of the Master Plan entailed a user/pilot survey, two
public information workshops/open houses that were publicly adve11ised, five Planning Advisory Committee (PAC) work session
meetings over an 18-month period; and communication via newsletters, email, and a dedicated webpage. He reviewed the
membership roster for the PAC; there is representation from many different sectors or stakeholders that have an opinion about
what should be occurring at the Airport. He then discussed the components of the Master Plan, which include an Airports
Geographic Information System (AGIS) survey; inventory of existing on and off Airport conditions; forecasts of aviation demand;
facility requirements; development alternatives; plan implementation proposals; and ALP drawings. He provided an overview of
forecasting infonnation, which requires FAA approval; forecasts cover activities regarding passenger totals, based aircraft, and
aircraft operations for the base year, short term (2023), intermediate tenn (2028), and long term (203 8). Relative to facility
requirements, the Master Plan addresses parking for additional aircraft needs, the total additional apron area required, hangar
inventory and condition, additional hangar needs, tenninal space, and terminal parking for the same short term, intermediate term,
and long-term ranges. Long-term development considerations include:
                •   Capacity in terminal facilities and movement areas;
                • Development opportunities on west side;
                • Redevelopment needs where hangars are in poor condition;
                •   Supporting utility and roadway infrastructure needs;
                • Access roadway improvements;
                • Property needs; and
                •   Off-airport compatible land use development
           Mr. Ga1Tett then presented charts from the proposed Master Plan identifying development alternatives, a comparison of
feature highlights, and preferred alternatives. He also provided an accompanying map to illustrate the current Airport
configuration and the areas for which new development or improvements have been proposed in the Plan document.
Implementation of the proposed Master Plan recommendations would be handled through the City's Capital Improvement Plan
(CIP) and analysis of funding availability; the total cost of short-term CIP projects is $42,305,000, with the City's match of these
costs being just $ 1. 7 million. The City's overall total matching requirement for Master Plan projects through 203 8 is $14,802,213.
He concluded that ALP drawings in the Master Plan, which require FAA approval, will be used to determine project funding
eligibility for the FAA and other funding sources.
           Council member Hyer asked if general aviation activities have been impacted in a similar manner to commercial aviation
with regard to COVID-19. Mr. Ga1Tett answered no; while general aviation activities were somewhat reduced, they were not as
negatively impacted by COVID-19 as commercial aviation.
           Discussion among the Council and Mr. Garrett then centered briefly on the competitive nature of access to FAA and
other airport funding opportunities.
           Council member White then asked if there are height restrictions for development on land outside of the airport, namely
Roy City. Mr. Garrett stated that he and other members of City Administration are working with leadership of neighboring
jurisdictions to prevent the construction of tall buildings surrounding the Airport; existing development with taller building
heights has encroached on the Airp011 and it is important to try to prevent similar activities from occurring in the future.

important step in preserving it. He then thanked the City's public safety employees for the great service they provide to the
community and he was grateful to receive information from them tonight during the work session meeting; it has caused him to
