    2.1. Discussion of revisions to Mountain View Corridor Office and Self-Storage Master
         Development Agreement located at approximately 5101 West 11800 South - Michael Maloy,
         City Planner
City Planner Michael Maloy presented the proposed zoning map amendment for the Mountain View Corridor
Office and Self-Storage development. He notated the developer had accepted recommended changes offered
by the Planning Commission and staff.

Planner Maloy referenced the Master Development Agreement featured a “survivor” clause which would
require the development of the office space be constructed prior to the self-storage could be developed. Prior
to storage being permitted on the property, access much be granted and constructed from Mountain View
Corridor. All fees must be paid and the building permit for a 10,000 square-foot office building must be
secured. Planner Maloy referenced the Planning Commission proposed zoning condition to limit the land use
specifically to “Professional Office” which would exclude any medical services. The Planning Commission also
recommended a zoning condition in the M-1 zone to limit the building height to 45-feet; however, the applicant
proposed a height limit of 15-feet.

    2.2. Blackridge Reservoir Update and Discussion - Justun Edwards, Public Works Director and
         Anthony Teuscher, Deputy Director of Parks, Recreation and Events
Public Works Director Justun Edwards offered a history of the reservoir in which the reservoir would supply
secondary irrigation water to the Herriman and Riverton communities. The recreational amenities were added
as an additional benefit to the community and patrons.

Director Edwards explained operationally the City expended between $12,000 -$16,500 annually for water
quality treatments for the 62 acre-foot storage reservoir. The operational season for secondary water runs each
year from April 15 through October 15; however, the season may be adjusted based on weather.

Director Edwards informed the Council the department utilizes six aerators to circulate the water in the
reservoir to prevent stagnation and stratification of the water. Typically, during the first week of June, herbicide
has been applied to help mitigate weed growth. Also, an algaecide has been injected to mitigate the growth of
algae and to improve the general water quality within the reservoir.

Deputy Director of Parks, Recreation and Events Anthony Teuscher offered a synopsis of the 2019 staffing
costs to maintain the park including Herriman Police Department staffing, landscape maintenance, utilities and
restroom supplies. He noted the gates for Blackridge Reservoir open at 7:30 a.m. with four Herriman Events
Staff being present at the park at all times. A member of the team speaks to every car as they enter the parking
area to offer education to patrons and offer instruction on where to park their vehicle. When the parking lot
is full, patrons are directed to drive through the parking lot, exit, and return at a later time. The reservoir and
beach area close at 7:00 p.m. and gates are closed at 9:00 p.m.

    2.3. 2020 NWSL Challenge Cup Joint Marketing Opportunity with REAL Salt Lake – Wendy
         Thomas, Assistant City Manager and Tami Moody, Assistant City Manager
Assistant City Managers Wendy Thomas and Tami Moody presented the opportunity to participate in
marketing and outreach for two upcoming nationally broadcasted, professional sporting events to be held in
Herriman City with REAL Salt Lake. It was noted City staff, Councilmember Ohrn and Councilmember
Shields met with REAL Salt Lake on June 2, 2020 to discuss the opportunity.

The 2020 National Women’s Soccer League (NWSL) Challenge Cup consists of a 25 game tournament,
scheduled to begin June 27, 2020 and will end July 26, 2020. The NWSL Challenge Cup would be broadcast
on CBS, CBS All Access, and Twitch. The majority of games would be played at the Zions Bank Real Academy
in Herriman.

Also, the 2020 Professional Lacrosse League (PLL) Tournament consisting of a 14 game tournament has been
scheduled at the same venue and has been scheduled to commence July 21, 2020 running through August 9,
2020. Both of the events have been approved by the Salt Lake County Health Department. The PLL
Tournament will broadcast on NBC.

The request for the joint effort requests an investment of $100,000.00 from Herriman City which would include
participation in joint marketing and outreach, among other things.

Councilmember Smith asked if in-kind donations would be considered. Assistant City Manager Thomas
explained that option had not been explored. Councilmember Smith relayed his concern of donating to a
private event; however, could see the value of advertising. He requested staff explore other options or at least,
a combination thereof. Councilmember Shields highlighted this would be the first major sports league betting
back into business, and would take place in Herriman. This marketing opportunity would allow the City to
have significant input on how outreach would be conducted. This would present an Economic Development
opportunity to highlight the assets of Herriman City. Councilmember Shields acknowledged the realization the
return of investment would not be immediate. Councilmember Henderson expressed his support of the
lucrative opportunity. The consensus of the Council expressed support of the request.

    2.6. Unified Fire Service Area Discussion Regarding potential Bond and Options - Alan Rae,
         Finance Director and Chase Andrizzi, City Attorney
Finance Director Alan Rae reported he reviewed the financial reports for the Unified Fire Service Area and
explained numbers had to be generated and extracted by the service area, and he was not able to just “pull” the
numbers. He offered an overview of their budget with total revenue was expected to be $5.7 Million with
Herriman City’s share being $4.4 Million. The assigned portion of the debt and expenses were expected to be
$5.2 Million. Over the last four years, there has been an annual excess; however, for the next year the service
area would go from a positive to a negative situation as expenditure would increase $3.1 Million to be able to
construct one station and rebuild a second. It was confirmed the service area could not afford operating costs
and bonding without changes.

Finance Director Rae highlighted the bond a tax calendar. Councilmember Shields asked about the urgency
of having a bond in place. Director Rae explained the bonding market was favorable, and noted some of the
funds would be placed in an account for a future project. Councilmember Henderson opined the bonding
process would commence this fall and thought there would be more time to review the proposal. He explained
the service area board determined to step forward earlier looking for lower bonding rates. Councilmember
June 10, 2020 – City Council Meeting Minutes                                           Page 5 of 10
Henderson suggested the process was done hastily in the current climate. He notated Riverton City announced
their desire to leave the taxing district, but not the service district.

Councilmember Shields clarified the service area was looking to go into debt for infrastructure that was not
currently needed. He noted the question was if it is right to have self-determination regarding these type of
decisions. In response to a question posed by the Council, Attorney Chase Andrizzi reported there was a 12-
month notice period to the service district, and the City would still be obligated during that timeframe.
Councilmember Henderson expressed concern of the timing of the service district going to bond for the
buildings.

Finance Director Rae noted the bonds could not be paid off early. Councilmember Henderson agreed entities
should be in control of the funding aspect to conduct business in the best interest of the City. He reiterated
his concern of the accelerated bonding schedule. The Council agreed and noted an analysis should be
conducted, and directed staff to move forward with a notice of intent to leave the district if the board decided
to move forward with the bond.
