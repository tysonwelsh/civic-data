      Licensed Family Child Care Amendments
                Jenny Rogers Miller has submitted a petition to amend the City's zoning ordinance to allow home-based licensed family
      childcare facilities in the City's R-2 (Two-family Residential) Zone. A licensed family childcare facility is a child daycare that
      allows up to 16 children to be cared for in a home setting. Cwrently, residential zones allow for home occupation child daycares
      which limit the number of children to eight. Non-home-based child daycare facilities which care for more than eight children may
      exist in the R-1 and R-2 Zones if they are part of an educational institution and may be approved as a conditional use in the R-3
      and R-3EC Zones and as a pennitted use in the R-4 and R-5 Zones. The licensed family childcare facility would allow for the care
      of more children than are permitted with a home occupation but would prohibit non-home-based daycare facilities in the R-2
      Zone.
                Planning Manager Montgomery made a presentation to review the implications of the petition. The present regulations
      for a daycare as a home occupation are that they have eight or less children, no outside employees, State and City licensure, and
      compliance with Building and Fire Codes. A daycare is allowed in any residential zone of the City and can be strictly for childcare
      purposes or offer a preschool as well. There are two types of daycares: occupied residence or other facilities. The petitioner's
 ,)   request is to 'fill the gap' between a home occupation day care facility and commercial day care facilities by creating the licensed
,     family care facility classification. This use would act as a home occupation in the sense that it takes place in the home but allows
      up to 16 children and outside persmmel who have passed sufficient backgrow1d checks or licensure requirements. The facility
      must have sufficient outdoor play area and satisfy increased inspection requirements. He indicated the following increased
      requirements would be placed on a licensed care facility:

           The petitioner cited several reasons she feels the amendments would benefit the community. She indicated Ogden needs
more quality preschools/daycares as most good schools currently have long wait lists. Parents are looking for small in-home
facilities rather than large commercial centers. This is especially true now with the risks of COVID-19. Preschools are critical to
many children's success in public school later in life. Ogden parents are having to keep their young children home because there
are not enough quality preschools to emoll in. More Ogden preschools would get State licensed if they could run at the State
allowed capacity. That would also ensure increased accountability and any code violations would be part of the public records.
There are also benefits to allowing and encouraging the use of non-resident personnel in a licensed care facility as parents want
lower ratios of children to adults to increase safety and interaction. They also want qualified professionals working with their
children rather than unqualified provider family members. Early childhood experts agree that lower child to adult ratios are safer
and healthier for young children regardless of group size. Allowing non-resident personnel would provide jobs for many qualified
early childhood professionals graduating from Weber State University (WSU) every year. These types of providers are leaving
Ogden City to start licensed care facilities in other cities. The petitioner also indicated that quality licensed care facilities will
improve the neighborhoods they are in because the properties will be nicely upkept and create a positive daytime presence that
will deter criminal activity. Any concerns about increased traffic can be addressed in drop-off and pick-up procedures for the
facility. Mr. Montgomery provided photographs of the petitioner's property, the interior layout, and the exterior conditions to
illustrate the improvements that have been made to cater to young child development. The Planning Commission considered
whether the desired use is consistent with the City's General Plan, which encourages neighborhood communities to have a variety
ofland uses, diverse housing types and densities, and the opportunity for residents to benefit from being close to public uses, such
as daycare centers. They considered whether it is appropriate to add a new definition for the term 'licensed family childcare' as an
allowed land use permitted in the City's R-2 Zone. They also debated parking requirements for such a facility and any need to
create additional requirements that exceed State requirements for such a facility. They voted unanimously (nine to zero) to
forward a positive recommendation to the City Council regarding the petition, including:
                •   Creating a definition of licensed family childcare;
                •   Revising parking to only require parking for home and staff;
                •   Set hours of operation from 7:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m.;
                •   Provide drop off in staggered times;
                • Limit the use to the City's R-2 Zone; and
                •   Require compliance with State and local codes for use.
           Mr. Montgomery noted that this amendment would change the facility license type from Type One to Type Two, which
entails different building and fire code requirements. He invited Fire Marshal Brown to provide information about the differences
in fire code regulations when comparing Type One and Type Two facilities. Mr. Brown stated Utah State Administrative Rules
cover Type One and Type Two facilities and they rely upon the local fire department and health department to license and inspect
the facilities. The Ogden City Fire Department inspects for safety issues and the two main differences between Type One and
Type Two facilities relates to the number of entrances and exits into a space. He provided a general overview of the exit
requirements based upon occupancy and the number oflevels in a building. Having inspected many home daycares in Ogden over
the past several years, there are many that would not comply with the exit requirements for Type Two facilities. He noted he has
met with the petitioner at her home and it would not comply with the code requirements to be licensed as a Type Two facility
because there is no direct exit from the basement to the exterior of the home. This would still limit the occupancy of that facility to
eight children and this would be communicated to the State Health Department as well. He reported on research he has conducted
regarding the types of daycare licenses offered in surrounding cities; Roy does not allow a Type Two facility with more than eight
children while North Ogden and Brigham City both allow Type Two facilities but require an exit from the basement directly to the
exterior of the home.
           Council member Stephens said he spoke with the petitioner and she indicated that the daycare facility is separate from
her home. Mr.' Brown stated that when he visited the property, he found the daycare center is attached to the main structure. State
Codes require the Fire Department to take into consideration that an entire home could be used for the daycare even if the owner
communicates that they will not use a basement or other level of the home for that purpose.
           Council member Lopez said that his brother owns two daycare facilities in Ogden, and he asked if it is necessary for him
to recuse himself from discussing or voting on this topic. Council Executive Director Eller-Smith answered no and indicated there
is no conflict unless Council member Lopez has a financial interest in the business.

Ogden CARES Act Nonprofits Grant Program
          Council member Hyer declared that he is a small business owner participating in the City's CARES Act small business
program and recused himself from discussing or voting on this item.
          The U.S. Government established the CARES Act (Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security) with $150 billion in
funding to address needs related to COVID-19. According to the U.S. Department of the Treasury, this funding is designed to
provide assistance to various governmental entities and requires that funds be used to cover expenses that are necessary
expenditures incurred due to the public health emergency with respect to COVID-19; were not accounted for in the budget most
recently approved as of March 27, 2020 for the government entity; and were incurred between March 1, 2020 and December 30,
2020. The Council has approved two budget amendments and appropriated CARES Act funding for business and nonprofit grants.
Currently, Administration is requesting consideration of a resolution to establish the Ogden CARES Nonprofits Grant Program,
similar to the current program for small business grants. To date, nonprofits have been awarded 35% of the total amount of
approved Ogden CARES grants. As impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic continue to increase, many nonprofits have identified
emerging critical funding needs. Current budget proposals include an additional CARES Act allocation of $4.3 million dedicated
to small business and nonprofit grant programs. This includes a commitment of up to $500,000 specifically for nonprofits, as
outlined in a proposed addendum to the City's interlocal agreement with Weber County.
          Business Development Division Manager Meess made a presentation to provide background information regarding the
guidelines for the grants that Ogden City has awarded to nonprofits under the existing Ogden CARES Grant Program and stated
$3.37 million has been approved to date in grants for nonprofits. A total of 44 nonprofits have received funding, 36 of them
located in Ogden and the other eight in Weber County. The average grant award is $76,657. Nonprofit grants are funded through a
partnership with Weber County and the needs of nonprofits have not been fully met as they have emerging critical needs due to
increases in COVID-19 cases. Administration is proposing an additional program guideline specific to nonprofit grants. The
purpose of the guideline is to provide additional funding to nonprofits previously determined ineligible by Ogden City or
determined ineligible by Weber County, that still have unmet financial needs. The maximum grant amount will be $500,000,
including prior awards; however, most awards will be much lower. The evaluation criteria for grant applications will consider
service type, the financial impact the pandemic has had on the nonprofit, any public revenue generation and/or cost reductions due
to services provided, and the number of employees and average wages of the nonprofit. Grant awards must be given by the end of
December 2020 for them to be funded with CARES Act monies.
          Council member White asked if the nonprofit entity will be required to spend the money by the end of December 2020.
Ms. Meess answered yes; however, the funds can be spent retroactively to reimburse expenses already incurred.

Utah Community Renewable Energy Act - House Bill 411
          Council Policy Analyst Mabey indicated that during the past several months, Ogden City has been involved in the
Community Renewable Energy Act process with other partnering communities. In addition to the regularly scheduled meetings,
the City has also participated in community-specific discussions with Salt Lake City, Park City, West Jordan and West Valley. An
interlocal agreement (Governance Agreement) is being developed to formalize the approach of collaborating communities who
desire to move forward with a utility rate study and cooperative agreement. The proposed Governance Agreement for
consideration includes cost modeling that detennines individual city contributions based on population and total electricity usage.
The total costs to complete the study are estimated around $1,000,000, and if Ogden chooses to participate, the City is anticipated
to be one of the higher contributors to the program. Ms. Mabey presented charts illustrating the proposed funding model, which
provides a distribution of cost sharing based upon each entity's population and total electricity usage. Ogden's population and
electricity usage is 11 percent and 10.49 percent, respectively, of the same metrics for all 23 participating entities. Ogden City
residents will likely experience utility adjustments based on the costs associated with procuring new renewable energy resources.
The Council discussed the decisions that have been made thus far by the cities that have joined together to form the Community
Renewable Study Group. The Council and members of Council staff expressed frustration about the lack of buy-in from all
partnering entities, and, more specifically, buy-in from the governing bodies of each entity.
          Ms. Mabey reviewed the characteristics of the program to be created by the Governance Agreement. The program is
intended to:
               • Procure new renewable energy resources;
               • Provide long-term environmental benefits and reduce Ogden's overall carbon footprint;
