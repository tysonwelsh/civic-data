         Long Range Plan Review

Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, explained that the Long Range Plan Review is a
follow-up from the Strategic Planning Meeting. Staff has taken the comments and feedback
from the meeting and have put together priorities for the Long Range Plan. The staff will
emphasize year two of the Long Range Plan so that budget requests can be completed for next
year.

Mr. Hicks said his main objective for the Administrative Services department is the new software
package.

Chief Hutson, Riverton Police Department, explained that the funds projected in his department
for the next five years are 80% salaries and personnel. The only other major budget item will be
the replacement of bulletproof vests which will take place in 3 years because there is a five-year
warranty on the current bulletproof vests. He has included the purchase of a four-seat utility
vehicle in his budget and would ask for an additional officer if crime increases in the future. His
main objective is to stay competitive with salary and compensation packages.

Mayor Staggs said that Chief Hutson had eliminated some of the items that were discussed at
the Strategic Planning Meeting.

Councilmember Wells asked how the increasing costs in the police department will be paid. In
response, Mr. Hicks explained that at some point the tax will need to be raised or suggested
that sales tax could be used to cover these expenses. He is working with Chief Hutson on a
metric for this amount. He asked the Council to consider if they would like smaller increases or
to hold the rate.

Councilmember Buroker said she would push back on the expense for the four-seater if the only
use were on the Jordan River trail. Chief Hutson responded that the two-seater they currently
own would need to be replaced and that there would be multiple uses for the four-seater
besides the Jordan River trail.

Councilmember Buroker asked if the typical metric for police officers is one police officer per
1,000 residents. Chief Hutson explained that the metric is dependent on more than just
population. For example, South Salt Lake has 70 officers and about 30,000 residents but police
case numbers in this area justify this.

Councilmember Stewart agreed with Chief Hutson that the City should budget to maintain a
competitive salary and compensation to keep police officers at Riverton.

Councilmember McCay asked how much wages were being increased and Mr. Hicks responded
that it was 4%.

Councilmember McDougal asked if the budget includes an inflation kicker on costs. In
response, Mr. Hicks said numbers are determined by their best educational estimate. Chief
Hutson said it is important to keep up with inflation because the RLESA rate will stay flat.

Public Hearings

         Ordinance No. 22-09 – Proposed Amendment to Sections 18.225.040(a) &
         18.225.080(8)(a), Addressing Accessory Building Height and Size, Amendments
         Proposed by Riverton City

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained this is a public hearing for a
Zoning Text Change proposed by Riverton City. The Zone text change involves Section 18.225
Accessory Structures. The amendment addresses two issues. The first is related to the allowed
height of accessory structures of any kind. The current ordinance allows a maximum height of
20 or 25 feet for accessory structures, depending on property size and distance from the
property line. However, the ordinance includes the limitation that an accessory building “may not
exceed the height of the main dwelling, measured at the highest pitch of the roof”. This has
proven to be a difficult standard to maintain, in part because that measurement is not always
intuitive or possible for a homeowner to make. Even Riverton City staff have difficulties
establishing the finished height of a home that has been in existence long enough that the City
no longer has plans on file, which typically are only kept for a few years. Staff is recommending
that the requirement for accessory structures not to exceed the height of the home be
eliminated.

The second is a requirement in the section dealing with accessory dwelling units (ADU). That
section includes a limitation that the accessory building containing an ADU cannot exceed 50%
of the footprint of the main dwelling. This limitation does not apply to accessory buildings in
general, only where an ADU is included. This has made the process of adding an ADU to an
existing building difficult for some property owners, where an accessory building was permitted
but exceeded the size requirement. Staff is proposing this limitation on the size of the
accessory structure containing an ADU be removed, allowing ADU’s in any accessory structure
which meets the requirements of the ordinance. Alternatively, the limitation may be amended to
limit the size of the ADU within the building. Should the Council so desire, staff can provide
assistance in crafting that language into the motion.
