2. Presentations and Reports
2.1 Fiber Network Update
Shaye Ruitenbeek gave an update to the Council on the Fiber Network Construction. She stated
that they connected our first customer yesterday. By today they already connected 13 customers,
resulting in approximately 59 service orders. She stated that they expect more customers to come
in gradually over time. In terms of pre-registration, they have almost 7,200 total pre-
registrations. As for the build-out process, they are currently working on cabinet five.

During the discussion, it was clarified that construction follows the DRC process, with some
areas having already gone through the approval stage. However, there are more approved areas
than those ready for construction due to factors such as capacity and the availability of
subcontractors. The criteria for determining the next area is not yet finalized and pre-registration
is still a factor. The proximity to the backbone and the demand for service play a role in the
selection process.

Mrs. Ruitenbeek reported that the backbone from area one to two is nearly completed. The
number of cabinet areas in the city is around 41, each capable of serving approximately 400 to
500 homes, with provisions for future growth.

2.2 State Auditor's Office Fraud Risk Questionnaire Report
Dean Lundell, City Finance Director presented on the State Auditor's Office fraud risk
questionnaire report. Mr. Lundell explained that it is an annual requirement to complete a self-
assessment survey to ensure financial resources are secure. The questionnaire examines factors
such as separation of duties and financial policies. He noted that a fraud hotline was
implemented to allow anonymous reporting.

The city received a score of 375 out of 395, indicating strong adherence to controls. While the
city strives for improvement, they consider their score to be excellent. The report will be posted
on the State Auditor's website. Dean Lundell mentioned the collaboration among Finance
Directors in the area to exchange ideas and best practices.


2.3 PARC Fund Recommendations and Discussion
Nicole Kunze, Chair of the PARC Committee, presented the Council with the Committee’s
recommendations. She said the committee had been meeting for the past few months and had
reviewed a total of 22 applications for arts and culture grants, a significant increase compared to
the previous year. The requested grant amount was $849,409, but the available funding was
$475,000. The committee recommended funding $444,740 for certain organizations and carrying
over $30,260 for the following year. She highlighted that Thanksgiving Point was partnering
with the community by providing free venues for some events.

The Council expressed their appreciation for Thanksgiving Point's involvement in the
community and their contribution to other nonprofits. The Council commended the committee
for their thoroughness in summarizing the grant applications and expressed support for their
recommendations.

There was discussion of how to provide funding to refurbish the Lehi Silver Bandwagon as they
did not qualify to receive funds. Thanksgiving Point volunteered to provide funding for the
refurbishment.

13. Consideration of Ordinance #39-2023 the Griffith Estates Zone Change from A-5
   (agriculture) to R-1-22 (residential/agriculture) on 3.43-acres located at 300 East 1630
   South.
   Items 13 and 14 were heard together.

    Steve Potter was representing the applicant. He explained that they are submitting a concept
    with a PUD that would then allow them to cluster the townhomes in the center of the property.
    The concept plan proposed a density bonus in exchange for a trail, road widening, and a
    substantial piece of land dedicated to open space. Concerns were raised about the donated
    parcel being unusable for other purposes, but it was seen as valuable for preserving natural
    areas. The Councilmembers debated the density increase and the importance of preserving
    agricultural uses. Councilor Koivisto expressed concerns about granting density bonuses in
    low-density zones and emphasized the need to be cautious about increasing density. The
    applicant provided details about the proposed development, including plans to reroute a ditch
    that runs through the property.

    Mayor Johnson asked for public input.

    Brent Cullen stated that his property neighbors this proposed development. He mentioned a
    nearby ditch and expressed concerns about its potential diversion, as it is currently a water
    source for their cows during winter.

    Teresa Mott stated that she is part of the Cullen family. She questioned whether townhomes
    were permitted on the property. She further expressed concerns about the proposed
    townhomes, their impact on the agricultural setting, and the potential issues with flooding. She
    criticized increasing density and argued for preserving the agricultural nature of the area. She
    also expressed doubt about the compatibility of three-story townhomes with the agricultural
    environment. She also highlighted discrepancies in property usage and expressed concerns
    about the burden of maintaining the additional land if it were given to the city. She referenced
    reports of the nearby disk golf course's current condition and raised doubts about the potential
