     OPENING CEREMONY- COUNCILMEMBER WILLIAM WHETSTONE
     Councilmember Whetstone stated that National Code Talker Day was celebrated last week
     and honors the contributions of Native Americans and their efforts during WWII.
     Councilmember Whetstone provided a brief history and introduced Harry James and Stan
     Watts who were in attendance. Stan Watts introduced himself as a sculptor. He displayed
     a Navajo Code Talker draft sculpture that he intends to construct as a bronze statue to be
     located at the Veteran’s Memorial site located in West Valley City. Mr. Watts detailed his
     proposal and discussed the importance Navajo Code Talkers had in the war. Harry James
     introduced himself in both Navajo and English. He described how Navajo Code Talkers

devised a special language to translate vital information to troops serving in WWII that
significantly helped the United States win the war. Mr. James detailed requirements for
being a Code Talker and expressed appreciation for Mr. Watts and his sculpture.

COMMENT PERIOD
A.  PUBLIC COMMENTS
    Tom McClellan stated he lives in the Bingham Court community and represents the
    HOA. He expressed concern about a park south of the community and the pathway
    that runs through the community. Mr. McClellan indicated that trees have
    overgrown within the boundaries of the park that have begun to impact the
    neighboring homes and has caused damage to the sidewalk. He also stated that the
    public pathway does not have a barrier that separates it from the community (which
    is 55+) and there have been issues with vehicles using the pathway as a shortcut,
    pedestrians using the restroom in the bushes, and skateboarders setting up ramps
    and jumps. Mr. McClellan provided a letter to be distributed to the Council with
    suggested solutions.

       Dennis Tenney indicated that he also lives in Bingham Court. He stated that public
       safety is a significant concern within this private community. He noted that
       jaywalking seems to be encouraged based on the lack of a crosswalk. Mr. Tenney
       stated that the community has conducted studies and are prepared with simple, zero
       cost solutions to solve these issues. He noted that the residents love this community
       and are looking forward to discussing solutions.

       Kelly Bertoch stated that he appreciated the Code Talker presentation. He
       expressed concern regarding parking on streets and high density housing
       developments being constructed. Mr. Bertoch stated that his heritage is Hunter and
       he sometimes gets overly passionate about his care for his community but stated
       that it is because he loves it.

       Matt MacPherson stated that the City Council will be seeing application GPZ-5-
       2023 come before them in the near future. He indicated that many of his neighbors
       spoke in opposition to the proposal during the Planning Commission discussion and

       are still opposed to the proposed development. Mr. MacPherson stated that the
       neighborhood was promised that this property would remain low density. He noted
       that developers have tried over the years to get higher density proposals approved
       but have been unsuccessful. Mr. MacPherson suggested proactively rezoning this
       parcel to low density.

       Jim Vesock stated that he would like to discuss Form of Government. He also
       expressed concern about Code Enforcement and encouraged that reporting methods
       be shared with the public more openly.

       Mike Markham expressed concern about a home in his neighborhood that has
       weeds in the front yard. He also noted that a truck was parked in front of a fire
       hydrant and expressed concern that there are 8 adults living in one household.

       Michael Dye stated he is also present to express concern regarding the upcoming
       Council discussion regarding application GPZ-5-2023. He indicated that he chose
       the Highbury Community because it is a safe, quiet place to live. He noted that he
       uses his home for many activities and the proposed development would
       compromise the peaceful and productive work environment that residents enjoy.
       Mr. Dye expressed concern about an increase in parking and noise issues as well as
       the impact to children and the units being rentals.

       Richard Nowak stated that the slurry cover in his neighborhood looks great. He
       encouraged the Council to hold meetings for residents and not just developers.

       Dawn Garrett stated that she lives down the street from the Redwood Rec Center
       and thinks it would be an ideal place for a library. She indicated it would make
       more sense to be located there than at Valley Fair Mall.
