2. Discussion Items

   2.1. Discussion of Herriman City Community Services Unit – HPD Operations
        Commander Brent Adamson

       Adamson introduced the members of the Community Services Unit, Sgt Greg Shaver,
       CSO Jenni Harward, Officer Chuck Malm and CSO Aaron Carney. He oriented the
       group to the organizational structure of the unit. For the past 3 months they have
       held multi-disciplinary team meetings including legal for better communication within
       the city.

       Adamson gave a brief list of case types code enforcement handles: parking
       enforcement, encroachments, short team rentals, crash team and events.

       Adamson recently visited other cities to review their workflow for ways to improve
       ours. The team is looking into newer law enforcement software solutions for better
       tracking.

       Mayor asked how council can help them do their job better. Adamson emphasized
       that the newer software would expand their administration capabilities, saving
       employees time. He disclosed adding people in the field and in office work will be
       needed soon.

       Commissioner Rypien questioned enforcement’s point of view on lots that are storing
       old cars, RV’s, Boats, and trailers. Adamson provided it depends on zoning and if it’s
       being used for business or just family/friends’ situations.

       Commissioner Berbert questioned short term rentals enforcement. Council debated
       the issue settling to watch for state legislative decisions for now.

   2.2. Herriman Parks and Trails Update – Wendy Thomas, Assistant City Manager
   Wendy-lot of projects going on in the city. Open spaces 822 2015 to 2700 acre
   Parks 217 to 240, urban trails 24.96 to 34.38, primitive trails 3.81 to 16.2 miles

   Wendy presented updates on recently completed project Juniper canyon recreation area,
   east trailhead featuring “the pit”, a pump track, bicycle playground, restroom, water
   fountain, and pavilion.

   Another recently completed project is the Main Street Park strips which includes .5 mile
   asphalt trail parallel to Main St. The strip features a waterwise and low maintenance
   landscape. We received grants from JVWCD and Utah Division of Forestry, Fire and State
   Land. The strip uses 50% less water, only needs to be mowed 1 or 2 a year.

   Upcoming projects include Detention Basin landscaping, Herriman History Park,
   Cemetery Restroom, Midas Creek Trail Expansion, Wide Hollow Trailhead and Challenge
   Staircase, Rose Creek Trail Connector, Bonneville Shoreline Connector, Olympia Park,
   Butterfield Trailhead, Mountain Ridge Parks, Jackson and Teton Parks, Herriman Athletic
   Complex, and Maintenance Projects.

   Commissioner Rypien commented that detention basin- from a development standpoint-
   cause the builders to lose a lot or two, instead they could build a swell on the park strip.
   Community Development Director Blake Thomas pointed out the new law requires 80%
   retention. It is hard to get that amount in a swell. City Planner Michael Maloy recounted
   the city updated our standards to allow swells, but the developers aren’t choosing that
   option.

   Councilmember Shields expressed his appreciation of the work the project team does
   with planning these projects even though resources are frequently insufficient. We want
   to make the public aware we are making them a priority, but the budget takes a while to
   fill in.

   Mayor conveyed the new Friends of Herriman Non-Profit group is hoping to get these
   projects accelerated. The community can come together to get some of these projects
   moving with donations.

   Commissioner Berbert sought adding discussion about ordinances to be added to meeting
   agendas going forward.
