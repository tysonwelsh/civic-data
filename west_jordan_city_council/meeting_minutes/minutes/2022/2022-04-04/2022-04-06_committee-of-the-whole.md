3.    STUDY ITEMS
        a. Review Legislative Session Affecting the City
Government Affairs Director Tauni Barker reported that staff tracked over 150 bills during the 2022
Legislative Session. Staff prioritized the bills into three different categories: top tier bills were bills
staff believed would require some type of significant policy, process, or legislative change in the
City; second tier bills may impact the City, but would not affect day-to-day operations; and third tier
bills may have some long-term effect on the City, but would involve a choice regarding
implementation. Ms. Barker provided an update on the following bills considered by staff to be of
top tier importance to West Jordan City.

       •   House Bill 22 Open and Public Meetings – establish how a quorum is created for
           electronic meetings. Vice Chair Green asked that Council Office staff take on
           responsibility of implementing requirements in HB 22.
       •   House Bill 23 First Responder Mental Health Services Agreements – mental health
           resources for employees, spouses, children and retirees. Ms. Barker said staff were
           working to include related expenses in the budget.
       •   House Bill 96 Government Records Fee Amendments – first quarter-hour of records
           research and management is free unless within 10 days of a prior request.

        •   House Bill 121 Water Conservation Modifications – prohibit municipalities from having
            lawn or turf requirements. Ms. Barker said staff believed West Jordan was already in
            compliance.
        •   House Bill 303 Downsizing Notice Amendments – heavily modified over the session. Ms.
            Barker said HB 303 would require review by the Legal Department, but would most likely
            not have a financial impact on the City.
        •   House Bill 399 Garrity Statements – protects Garrity statements for public employees.
        •   House Bill 440 Homeless Services Amendments – homeless overflow plans.
        •   House Bill 439 Open Public Meetings Act – (staff priority level 2) Vice Chair Green stated
            necessary changes could be made by the Council Office.
        •   Senate Bill 46 Medical Cannabis Patient Protection – protects public employees holding
            a medical cannabis card. Ms. Barker said SB 46 may affect the City’s ability to receive
            federal grants. The City supported amendments made in Senate Bill 190.
        •   Senate Bill 89 Water Amendments – requires municipalities to file a conservation plan
            with a goal to meet regional goals. Ms. Barker said the Public Works Department would
            draft an update to the City’s current Water Conservation Plan.
        •   Senate Bill 110 Water – requires municipalities to adopt a Water Use and Preservation
            Plan as a part of the General Plan by 2025.
        •   Senate Bill 126 Officer Intervention Reporting Amendments – requires minor changes,
            but will not impact the West Jordan Police Department in a major way.
        •   Senate Bill 190 Cannabis Update
        •   Senate Bill 140 Housing and Transit Reinvestment Zones – no changes to process or
            policy, but could certainly benefit West Jordan.
        •   House Bill 462 Affordable Housing Bill – largest fiscal and policy impact on West Jordan
            of all bills passed in 2022. The West Jordan Moderate Income Housing Plan would need
            to be updated, with additional reporting required. Ms. Barker commented that the City
            could create zoning to encourage affordable housing, but could not “create” affordable
            housing. Vice Chair Green said he agreed density did not necessarily equal affordability,
            and pointed out the Bill did not include a specific target goal. Ms. Barker said HB 462
            would require amendments to the City’s ADU ordinance, and would require allocation of
            funds for Station Area Plans.

        c. Discuss City Implementation of Passed Legislation
Assistant City Attorney David Quealy said the prioritization levels assigned to bills may change as
staff continued to work through the information available. He said he currently had 18 bills listed
as top priority. Mr. Quealy explained implementation needed for the following bills:

        •   House Bill 22 – City Council could pass a resolution or an ordinance
        •   House Bill 23 – Implementation by Police/Fire Departments and Administrative Services

           •   House Bill 96 – Training issue for City Recorder and records custodians
           •   House Bill 121 – Bulk of bill relates to State facilities, nothing new for City to implement
           •   House Bill 169 (defines all local government employees as local disaster response
               personnel) – Staff will update existing Emergency Operations Plan
           •   House Bill 282 – May require amendments to existing landscaping requirements in City
               Code
           •   House Bill 303 – Staff will review and evaluate for possible policy, practice, and City Code

   Community Development Director Scott Langford said implementing Station Area Plans (HB 462)
   would have a potential financial impact. He said the intended end goal for the Station Area Plans
   was still unclear. If Station Area Plans could be created in line with current zoning, the financial
   impact may be less.
