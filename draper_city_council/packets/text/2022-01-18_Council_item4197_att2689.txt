BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY
This application is a request for approval of a Zoning Map Amendment, Land Use Map
Amendment, and Development Agreement for approximately 1.5 acres located on the west
side of 300 East, at approximately 13666 South 300 East (Exhibit C). The property is
currently zoned RA1. The applicant is requesting a Zoning Map Amendment from RA1 to
the RM1 zone and a Land Use Map Amendment for the corresponding Residential
Medium/High Density designation. A Development Agreement is also proposed to approve
lot size, architectural standards, specific setbacks and overall subdivision layout.

ANALYSIS

General Plan and Zoning. The Land Use Map of the General Plan calls for the Residential
Low/Medium Density land use designation for the subject property (Exhibit D). This
category is characterized as follows:




The property has been assigned the RA1 zoning classification, supporting approximately
one dwelling unit per acre (Exhibit E). According to Draper City Municipal Code (DCMC)
Section 9-8-020 the purpose of the RA1 zone is to “foster low density development with
little impact on its surroundings and municipal services; to generally preserve the character
of the City’s semi-rural areas; and to promote and preserve conditions favorable to large-

lot family life, including the keeping of limited numbers of animals and fowl. This zone is
intended to promote a combination of retail and service facilities which, in character and
scale, meet day to day needs of nearby residents.” The RA1 zoning abuts the subject
property on the west, east, and south sides. The RA2 (Residential Agricultural, 20,000
square foot lot minimum zone) abuts on the east side. To the north is the RM1 zone.

The applicant is proposing to change the land use designation to the Residential
Medium/High Density designation. This category is characterized as follows:




The applicant is proposing to change the zoning designation to the RM1 zone. This
designation supports up to eight dwelling units per acre. Per the DCMC Section 9-8-020 the
purpose of the RM1 zone is to “permit well designed apartments, townhomes, twin homes, and
condominiums at relatively high densities that are appropriately buffered from and compatible
with surrounding land uses.” The RM1 zone allows for single-family homes on lots with at
least 6,000 square feet in size.

Development Agreement. The applicant has submitted a development agreement (Exhibit F).
The applicant is proposing a 9 lot single-family subdivision which equals 6 dwelling units
per acre, well within the range allowed in the RM1 zone. The City Council approved a text
amendment on August 3, 2021 which sets the minimum setbacks for single-family homes
in the RM1 and RM2 zones. This application pre-dates that recent text amendment. The

Zoning Map Amendment
A Text and Map Amendment is a matter committed to the legislative discretion of the City
Council and is not controlled by any one standard. However, in making a recommendation
to the City Council, the Planning Commission should consider the following factors in
Section 9-5-060(E) of the DCMC:

        1. Map Amendments:
            a. Whether the proposed amendment is consistent with goals, objectives and policies
               of the city's general plan;
            b. Whether the proposed amendment is harmonious with the overall character of
               existing development in the vicinity of the subject property;
            c. Whether the proposed amendment is consistent with the standards of any
               applicable overlay zone;
            d. The extent to which the proposed amendment may adversely affect adjacent
               property; and
            e. The adequacy of facilities and services intended to serve the subject property,
               including, but not limited to, roadways, parks and recreation facilities, police and
               fire protection, schools, stormwater drainage systems, water supplies, and
               wastewater and refuse collection.

Development Agreement
DCMC does not contain criteria for review and potential approval of a Development
Agreement. It does, however; contain a list of items that are required as part of an
Agreement and limitations to a Development Agreement, as found in DCMC Section 9-5-
200(C) and (D). This section depicts the required materials for such requests as:

        C.     Procedure: All development agreements, upon proper execution, shall be recorded
               with the respective County Recorder's Office, shall run with the land, and shall be
               binding on all successors in the ownership of the affected property. A development
               agreement shall contain, at a minimum, the following:
                1. A legal description of the land subject to the development agreement.
                2. The restrictions or conditions to be attached to the property including
                    development standards and the provision of public facilities.
                3. The configuration of the project as shown on a project master plan.
                4. A statement of the benefits and value the development agreement will have

                    standards, dedication and improvement of open space, parks, trails,
                    amenities, or infrastructure such as public rights-of-way or utilities.

This Development Agreement ("Agreement") is made and entered into as of this          day
of August 2021, by and between Concord Lane, LLC (hereinafter collectively referred to
as "Developer"), and Draper City, a municipal corporation of the State of Utah (hereinafter
referred to as the "City").
                                        RECITALS

A.      Developer owns approximately 1.5 acres of real property located near 13666 and
13696 South 300 East within City's boundaries (the "Property''). The legal description of the
Property is set forth on "Exhibit A" attached hereto.

B.     The Property currently is zoned RAl (Residential Agricultural, 40,000 square foot lot
minimum), a residential zoning designation identified in Title 9, Chapter 10 of the Draper
City Municipal Code (the "Code").

C.       Developer has made application to City for a general plan map amendment and
rezone or map amendment necessary to assign RM1 (Multiple-family residential, up to 8
units per acre) zoning designation to the Property in anticipation of developing the Property
as a single-family residential subdivision (the "Project").

D.      The intent of this Agreement is to facilitate development of the Project in
accordance with the Code and the site-specific.land uses and development standards
specified in this Agreement.

E.      City, acting pursuant to its authority under UTAH CODE ANN.§ 10-9a-101, et
seq. ("LUDMA") has made certain determinations with respect to the Property and the
proposed Project, and, in the exercise of its legislative discretion, has elected to process
and approve this Agreement after all necessary public hearings and procedures have been
conducted.

F.      The Parties recognize the RM1 zone allows a multi-family product to be built as
permitted. Developer agrees by the acceptance and execution of this agreement to build only
single-family detached units and agrees to comply with the development standards listed
herein that are not generally applicable to single-family detached units.

G.      H.B. 1003 was recently signed into law and enacts Utah Code Ann. 10-9a-534 which
bars the City from imposing a requirement for a “building design element”, as that term is
defined therein, on one and two family dwellings. Utah Code Ann. 10-9a-534(3)(d) permits
the Parties to agree to building design elements in a development agreement. The Parties
agree to be bound by this Agreement insofar as it implements requirements for building
design elements.

4.      Project-Specific Development Standards.

        4.1.     Site Standards. By this Agreement, the Parties intend to define the uses,
        development standards and other regulations otherwise applicable to the Property under
        the RMl zoning designation as described in Title 9, Chapter 10 of the Code in order to
        promote the efficient and creative development of the Property. The specific allowed
        land uses, development standards and regulations for the Project that are to be used in
        substitution for the uses, development standards and other regulations contained in
        Title 9 of the Code are described below. Development standards that are not addressed
        in this Agreement shall be those development standards set forth in Title 9 of the Code
        in place at time of complete subdivision application submission. Developer shall
        develop the Project in accordance with the Plan (subject to Section 4.3) and in full
        compliance with the Site Standards and the Code.

        4.2.    Minimum Lot Size. The minimum lot size shall be 6,000 square feet.

        4.3.    Unit Variety. To avoid an undesirable aesthetic impression that may be created
        by building neighboring uniform models, Developer shall provide for a variety of
        models with different building elevations and finishes for the units in the Project which
        may consist of color change and roofline change. Unit variety shall be as depicted in
        the elevations and renderings attached as Exhibit C.

        4.4.     Unit Exterior Building Materials. The architecture and design will consist of
        25% brick and 75% painted fiber cement siding or stucco, or 25% cultured stone and
        75% siding or stucco. The front elevations will have different facades with different
        ratios of exterior materials after minimum requirements are met. To maintain an
        overall aesthetic theme while simultaneously encouraging variety, some buildings
        may have a larger percentage of brick or stone than other buildings and each building
        will have its own unique architectural features accented by brick or stone. The
        dwellings will include the following 7 architectural features:
              A. Porch with support columns (6 foot minimum depth).
              B. Portico entrance at front doorway.
              C. Moldings around windows (4 inch minimum width).
              C. Decorative brackets under eaves.
              D. Exterior crown molding with eave returns.
              E. Roof gable with staggered shingle.
              F. Roof dormer.
              G. Board and batten look achieved through vertical fiber cement siding.
              H. Corner trim extending from the base to roofline.

      4.5.    Unit Size. The single-family structures will range in size from 2,200 Square
      Feet to 3,800 Square Feet with optional basement included.

      4.6.    Unit Garages. Each unit will include an attached and/or detached garage
      with a minimum of two (2) stalls. Each driveway will be a minimum of twenty feet
      (20’) deep.

      4.7.    Unit Height. Height of all units will be a maximum of thirty-five feet (35').

      4.8.     Unit Set-backs. Front set-back fifteen feet except for a front facing garage that has a
      minimum setback of 20 feet (20’), side setbacks of 5’except for a minimum setback of
      15’from on east side of lots 1 and 9 adjacent to 300 E., and rear set-backs to be twenty feet
      on interior lots and five feet (5’) on corner lots.

      4.9.     Private Roads. As depicted in EXHIBIT B, the road into the Project will be a
      private lane with a cross-section of 31 feet. The hammerhead portion will be 20 feet from
      back of sidewalk on the west to back of curb on the East. Developer or HOA is required to
      maintain private road and private utilities.

      4.10. CC&Rs and HOA. Developer shall establish a homeowners association ("HOA")
      andadopt conditions, covenants and restrictions ("CC&Rs") and will ensure that reserves
      are put in place to maintain the private lanes and storm drainage facilities.
