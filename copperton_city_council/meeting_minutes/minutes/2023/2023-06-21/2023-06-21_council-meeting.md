Citizen Comments

              Dennis Carrigan wanted to know who he could speak with about having the
restrooms unlocked during special events held in the park and garbage pickup afterward. He

the Council so someone should always be available. He stated Council Member Stitzer, who
sits on the Board of the Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District (WFWRD) could arrange
for extra garbage pickup.

               Council Member Stitzer stated Council Member Bailey could arrange for the
sprinklers to be turned off. She added there is a “How Do I” section on the coppertonutah.org

Unified Fire Authority (UFA)

               Ed Walden, UFA, stated there are still three fire stations under construction
which are scheduled to be complete mid or late summer. Thirty-one new recruits recently
graduated and there have also been lateral hires from other departments. He recently
contacted Rio Tinto because the grass behind the fire station is waist high. He distributed some
handouts on residential burning. UFA encourages people to attend fireworks shows over the
holidays instead of lighting their own fireworks. UFA has no enforcement authority regarding
fireworks usage; only Unified Police Department (UPD) does.

                                  −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
Unified Police Department (UPD)

              Detective Harry Holt, UPD, stated there were 44 calls for service resulting in 9
cases for the month of May. The motorcycle unit has asked that someone go through the
parade route and ensure that all debris is removed due to safety concerns. There have been a
few complaints about dirt biking behind the Copperton apartments at the beginning of town.
That property is owned by Kennecott. He would like the Council to request that Rio Tinto
secure the property.

                Council Member Stitzer stated Rio Tinto is aware of the issue and is working on
it. She will see that the Community Council makes an extra effort to make sure the parade
route is clean of debris.

Fiscal Items

                Mayor Clayton reviewed the attorney bill for $4,827.50 for legal services for the
month of May.

               Council Member Olsen, seconded by Council Member Severson, moved to
approve the bill. The motion passed unanimously.

               Mayor Clayton reviewed the annual liability insurance bill for $8,775.00 covering
the park and the cemetery. The amount was the same as last year.

               Council Member Bailey, seconded by Council Member Severson, moved to
approve the bill. The motion passed unanimously.

                  Council Member Bailey reviewed the first quote, which was for the removal of
17 dead trees remaining in the park. She reviewed the second quote in the amount of $9,000.,
which was for planting 30 trees. She reviewed the third quote in the amount of $13,161. for the
maintenance of the 30 trees, should they be planted. Her preference was not to move on the
first bid, but to approve the other two.

               Council Member Stitzer, seconded by Council Member Bailey, moved to approve
the estimates for planting and maintaining 30 trees and to table the estimate to remove the 17
dead trees. The motion passed unanimously.

              Council Member Stitzer reviewed an estimate for refrigerator magnets to be
mailed to Copperton residents. The magnets would contain a few pictures and some most
requested phone numbers. The magnets would cost $1.15 apiece and her recommendation
was to purchase between 350 and 400 magnets.

               Mayor Clayton requested to include a QR code on the magnets. He also asked
that he and the other Council members be given a chance to look over the final proof before the
order is placed.

             Mayor Clayton, seconded by Council Member Severson, moved to approve the
purchase of the magnets which would be a little over $400.00. The motion passed
unanimously.

Law Enforcement Update

               Mayor Clayton stated he recently met again with Mayor Jennifer Wilson and
Sheriff Rosie Rivera regarding what Copperton wants for law enforcement. He reiterated that
Copperton wants transparency in pricing and a contract model. It would like to see a menu for
services and to have input. He recommended an advisory committee with a contact for
Copperton to reach out to. He is concerned that Mayor Wilson has no intent to stay with
SLVLESA. They discussed the possibility of a substation that would be owned by the County.

                Nathan Bracken stated the County has questioned whether a feasibility study

                                                3
DATE           WEDNESDAY                               JUNE                            21, 2023

would be required if the metro townships become cities/towns through legislation. A study
should not be necessary because there is already over six years of data on the subject. The
metro townships’ revenue is increasing, and the County’s revenue is decreasing. This is due, in
part, to how the pandemic changed the way people shop. His recommendation is to consider
doing a redevelopment agency. The financial viability of the metro townships is improving
drastically.

             Council Member Olsen stated he learned recently that SLVLESA, with a vote
from the Board, can support multiple contracts. Copperton is not bound to UPD exclusively
through SLVLESA. He asked everyone to keep this in mind.

                                −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
HB 374 – Interim Legislative Update

               Mayor Clayton stated there was an interim session a week ago at the
Legislature. A committee met and the issue of making metro townships cities/towns through
legislation was on the agenda. Representative Jordan Teuscher, who ran the bill to dissolve
UPD, supports the metro townships in their efforts. They were successful in opening a
committee bill file and Nathan Bracken and the other metro township attorneys will continue to
work on legislation to that effect. The Committee gave an informative synopsis of the metro
areas dating back to the 1970s. It will meet again in August.

                                −−−−−−−−−−−−−−

Street and Park Sign Update

              Council Member Stitzer stated she and Council Member Olsen have been
working on this issue. Months ago, they discussed letting the public give input on the sign
design. There were five submissions, which she will circulate to the Council so it can narrow
them down. There are still a few street signs that do not have stickers with the Copperton Metro
Township logo, so those will need to be made. She and Council Member Olsen will soon get to
working on removing the signs that no longer meet the Copperton Code.

             Mayor Clayton suggested having citizens vote for the new sign design at
Copperton Town Days in August.

              Council Member Stitzer stated that sounded like a good idea to her.

Curb and Gutter Projects

because although Salt Lake County recently contracted with Copperton Metro Township on a
curb and gutter project in 2018 or 2019, this resident’s curb and gutter was never completed.
When the resident contacted the County, they were told there was no work order in place. She

               Mayor Clayton suggested Council Member Stitzer follow up with the Salt Lake

The Copperton Metro Township (“Copperton”) Council resolves as follows:

WHEREAS, the County participates as an “urban county,” as defined by federal regulation, in
the Community Development Block Grant (“CDBG”), Emergency Solutions Grant (“ESG”), and
the HOME Investment Partnership through a consortium that includes the urban county
(“HOME”) programs administered by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development
(“HUD”); and

WHEREAS, an Interlocal Cooperation Agreement (“Agreement”) has been prepared for
approval and execution by and between Copperton, Salt Lake County, and participating
municipalities, a copy of which is attached hereto as Exhibit 1, which states the purposes
thereof, and the extent of the required participation of the parties and the rights, duties,
responsibilities, and obligations of the parties in the conduct and administration of the CDBG,
ESG, and HOME programs as specified therein; and

WHEREAS, under the Utah Interlocal Cooperation Act, Utah Code Annotated, 11-13-101 et
seq. (2020) any two or more public agencies may enter into agreements with one another for
joint or cooperative action and may also contract with each other to perform any governmental
service activity or taking which each public agency entering into the contract is authorized by
law to perform.

NOW, THEREFORE, IT IS HEREBY RESOLVED by the Copperton Metro Township Council
that the attached Interlocal Cooperation Agreement between Copperton, Salt Lake County, and
the Town of Alta, Town of Brighton, Bluffdale City, Cottonwood Heights City, Draper City,
Emigration Canyon Metro Township, Holladay City, Herriman City, Kearns Metro Township,
Magna Metro Township, Midvale City Corp., City of Millcreek, Murray City, Riverton City, City of
South Salt Lake, and White City Metro Township relating to the conduct of the CDBG, ESG, and
HOME Programs is hereby approved by the Council and the Mayor is hereby authorized to
execute the same on behalf of Copperton.

       Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD)
       Council of Governments (COG)
