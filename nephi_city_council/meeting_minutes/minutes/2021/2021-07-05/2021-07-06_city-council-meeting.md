PUBLIC HEARING CANYON MINES ANNEXATION:

  Mayor Glade R. Nielson called the hearing to order and stated the purpose of the hearing was to
accept public comment on the Canyon Mines Annexation. The annexation area is located at the mouth
of canyon, east of the golf course on both sides of Hwy. 132 and consists of approximately 75.23 acres.
The requested zoning of the annexation area was Highway Commercial (HC). The property is owned by
Nephi City.

 Raymond Dinsdale asked why the city would annex city owned property.
 Donald Ball asked what the nature of the proposed development was.
 Mayor Nielson answered, “commercial”.

Seeing no further comment, Mayor Nielson closed the hearing at 7:05 p.m.


Mayor Glade R. Nielson called the regular meeting to order and welcomed all present and then via zoom
Rajan Zed, President of the Universal Society of Hinduism offered a prayer.

WELL-BEING SURVEY RESULTS REPORTED BY COURTNEY FLINT:

  Courtney Flint, USU Faculty/Community Research Specialist, presented the results of the Well-Being
Survey that 144 city residents participated in. Ms. Flint stated that the project started in 2019 and Nephi
City began participating in 2020. There are over 30 cities and towns across the state who participated.
Overall, the respondents think Nephi is a great place to live. The top concerns of the respondents were
opportunities for youth, water supply, and affordable housing. Councilor Memmott stated that the data
gathered from the survey will help with the city’s general plan update.

BARBARA FINLINSON NAMED NEPHI CITY JUSTICE COURT JUDGE:

   Barbara Finlinson was announced by Mayor Nielson as the recommendation from the Juab County
Commissioners and himself as the new Juab Justice Court and Nephi City Justice Court Judge to replace
Judge Sharla Williams who passed away in November 2020. Mayor Nielson said that the Judicial
Nominating Committee had narrowed the 18 applicants down to four and Finlinson was chosen to fill
the vacancy. Councilor Ostler moved to accept the recommendation and to name Barbara Finlinson as
the new Nephi City Justice Court Judge. Councilor Memmott seconded the motion. The motion passed
on a unanimous vote.
  Ms. Finlinson stood and stated that she was truly grateful for this opportunity. She gave a history of
her work experience as a prosecutor in the Utah County Attorney’s Office for the past three years. She
has a desire to help both victims and defendants. She said that there had been several judges that have
mentored her, and she hopes to bring what she has learned from them to the bench. Council members
expressed their congratulations.

REVISED STREET CURB AND GUTTER STANDARD ADOPTED:

  Mr. Atkinson reported that In the last state legislative session, House Bill 409 was introduced and
passed very late in the session. It was a bill that did not receive much attention due to the late time
frame in which it was considered and approved by the legislature. One of the provisions of the bill
requires that certain subdivisions, deemed “low impact developments”, be granted the ability to
construct only 32’ of asphalt. While none of the subdivisions in Nephi are called “low impact
developments”, the definition in this bill fits the requirements that Nephi City imposes on new
subdivisions. The definition for low impact is basically a subdivision that requires a stormwater facility.
The standard street width in the city is 43’ to accommodate snowplowing and solid waste collection
services. It also provides additional travel space since all residential areas have parked vehicles on the
side of the road. The road width has also been a measure the Council has used to define the “rural”
nature of the city and something to be preserved as outlined in the General Plan.
   To maintain the city’s street width standard of 43’, a different standard would need to be adopted.
The state law does not count curb and gutter as part of the road width. The recommended revision to
the city’s street standard requires a larger width for the gutter to achieve the road width of 43’. The
developer would have the option to simply apply asphalt of 43’ and a traditional curb and gutter.
   Mr. Atkinson stated that It is hopeful that developers will opt for the alternative and city streets can
continue to be constructed according to the historical standard. This may not be the best long-term
solution for the community, but it would allow the city to maintain the current required road widths.
Councilor Memmott moved to adopt the revised street curb and gutter standard and ask the planning
commission to provide recommendation to modify the land use code to meet he new street standard.
Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

CITY ADMINISTRATOR AND CITY COUNCIL UPDATES:

   Mr. Atkinson reported on the recent power outage. He stated that we receive power through
transmission lines owned by Rocky Mountain Power (RMP). If there is a problem with a transmission
line we are at the mercy of RMP to fix the problem. The city’s power department does not have the
authority to assist RMP in the fix. A micro-burst of wind knocked over three power lines and the power
was out for approximately 16 hours.
   Mr. Atkinson reported that the billboard on the south end of the city has been updated.
   Councilor Seely reported on the JRDA meeting and said that the board had voted to charge a $10.00
fee for all loads starting the first week in August. Uncovered loads will cost double. The council worried
that this new fee would put added pressure on the city’s green waste site and would create more illegal
dumping on the west bench.
   Councilor Memmott asked about an individual that was requesting to make a music video on city
owned property. He wanted to know what steps were being taken that the city would be represented in
a positive light. Mayor Nielson said that due diligence would be ensured.
