LDC Chapter 17.62: Definitions
The Definitions chapter of the LDC has been updated to consolidate all alcohol-related definitions into one area and add definitions for a Bar; Small Brewery, Distillery, or Winery; Brewery; Distillery; and Winery.

Planning Commission recommended changing the staff proposed definition for "Alcohol, Small Brewery" to "Alcohol, Small Brewery, Distillery, or Winery", and to remove the alcohol volume limitation and instead use only the square footage limitation to allow for small manufacturing of alcoholic beverages other than beer.

Staff proposed definition for Small Brewery:
"Alcohol, Small Brewery" means a brewery that produces less than fifteen thousand (15,000) barrels of beer, heavy beer, or malt liquor annually and occupies less than ten thousand (10,000) square feet in gross floor area.

The revised definition for Small Brewery, Distillery, Winery following Planning Commission recommendations:
"Alcohol, Small Brewery, Distillery, or Winery" means an alcoholic beverage manufacturing use that occupies less than ten thousand (10,000) square feet in gross floor area, and produces beverages as specified in the definitions for Alcohol, Brewery; Alcohol, Distillery; and Alcohol, Winery.

RECOMMENDATION
Staff recommends that the Planning Commission recommend approval to the Municipal Council of the proposed amendments to Chapters 17.11 District and Corridor Zone Uses, and 17.62 Definitions of the Land Development Code (LDC).

REQUEST
This code amendment makes changes to the Logan City Land Development Code Chapters 17.11 District and Corridor Zone Uses, and 17.62 Definitions. This amendment also includes changes to the Logan City Municipal Code (LMC) Section 5.10 Alcoholic Beverages, but the Planning Commissions purview is to make recommendations on the LDC portion of the amendment only. There is a considerable amount of connections between the amendment to the LDC and the LMC, and changes to one may affect the other. To place the amendment to the LDC within the context of the whole amendment, a summary of the changes and review of the LMC amendment are includes in this report. The proposed amendments change the administrative procedures for alcohol licensing to better conform with state and local laws, policies, and procedures; and makes changes to the local restrictions regarding alcohol uses, including bars, taverns, brew restaurants, package agencies, off-premise beer, manufacturing, and distribution.

Municipal Code 5.10
The proposed amendment includes extensive revisions to Section 5.10 within the business licensing chapter of the municipal code specifically dealing with regulations regarding alcohol licensing. Staff has reviewed the code section and identified extensive redundancies, areas that do not align with State code, and areas that do not correspond to current policies and procedures. This has resulted in a code section that is challenging to administer and is not user friendly for staff or the public. One of the intents of the amendment is to alleviate the administrative issues with the alcohol licensing code by consolidating and clarifying the administrative and application process for alcohol licensing. These changes are contained in the revised Article I. Administration, which includes the definitions, license requirements, application and renewal process, review process, enforcement, and appeal process. The reworked article consolidates several sections of the previous code into a single administrative section.

Article II Local License Requirements articulates the local restrictions that go beyond State code. This contains limits on total licenses and codes that pertain to unique alcohol uses, such as the Brew Restaurant and Taproom. Previously, the code had extensive redundant sections and articles for various license types that had no specific local restrictions. Those sections have been removed or consolidated into the streamlined Article II to make locating local restrictions easier and more intuitive.

The amendment includes several policy changes, including permitting additional taverns, expanding manufacturing opportunities, and revising the permitted locations of bars, taverns, and brew restaurants. The changes include the addition of a couple of new use types, including

a taproom in the TC-1 zone, and a small brewery in the CS and IP zones. The proposal to expand the number of tavern licenses is intended to allow for innovative products to develop in downtown. The opportunity to include manufacturing in conjunction with a tavern license through the new taproom use will incentivize local business and entrepreneurship. An item for the Commission to consider is whether to generally expand tavern licenses or instead specify those licenses for taproom uses only. This could be done by creating a unique population limit for taprooms (such as 1 license: 25,000 population), instead of increasing the total number of tavern licenses.

Changes to the Brew Restaurant include allowing it in areas where restaurants are permitted and updating the definition of a Brew Restaurant to permit sales of beer for off-premise sales. The change to permit off-premise sales would still require the majority of beer manufactured to be consumed on-site. This change would allow more flexibility for Brew Restaurants to market and distribute their products.

The proposed Taproom use aligns closely with the Brew Restaurant model and would permit manufacturing in conjunction with a bar or tavern. As proposed, the Taproom would be subject to the population limits of either a bar or tavern, depending on the type of license they acquire from the State. The use would also be limited to no more than 5,000 sf gross area to limit the impacts and scope of alcohol manufacturing in downtown.

Another policy change is to remove several general prohibitions from Section 5.10 and instead address those uses through zoning regulations. This is particularly applicable to the current tight restrictions on manufacturing, which only allow for manufacturing as part of a Brew Restaurant. The proposal is to expand manufacturing uses and refer to zoning to identify the permitted location and scale of the manufacturing.

There is also a general prohibition in the current code regarding Package Agency. A Package Agency license is a license granted by the State to allow the sale of heavy beer and liquors that are typically required to go through the State Liquor Store system. These licenses are closely regulated by the State and are only available in a few instances, the most applicable being at a brewery or winery. Acquiring a Package Agency license would allow a brewery in Logan to bottle and distribute their heavy beer products without going through a state liquor store. The State has a proximity restriction for a Package Agency (600' pedestrian travel, 200' straight line from community locations).  With the clear enumeration from the State regarding who can acquire a Package Agency license and where they can be located, Staff supports the removal of the local prohibition.

Staff also proposes the removal of proximity restrictions for off-premise beer sales (grocery or convenience stores). The current restriction is 600' pedestrian travel or 200' straight line from a community location such as a park or school. The State no longer restricts the proximity of off-premise beer sales and staff's recommendation is to follow suit with the State and remove the local restriction. Eliminating the restriction will make it easier for neighborhood-serving markets and convenience stores to find suitable locations to develop.

There is a brief reference to homebrew in the current code addressing alcohol content. Homebrew has an extensive State regulation that is completely adequate to regulate home brewing. Staff recommends removing the local restriction and yielding to the State regulations for homebrew. State regulations include the total volume that can be brewed (100 gallons per individual), sets whom and where the alcohol can be consumed, and regulates the sale of the alcohol.

Land Development Code 17.11 & 17.62
Proposed changes to the Land Development Code address alcohol use as it pertains to zoning. 17.62 Definitions have been updated to consolidate all alcohol-related definitions into one area and add definitions for a Bar, Small Brewery, Brewery, Distillery, and Winery.

Chapter 17.11 District and Corridor Zone Uses is updated to include both the new uses of Taproom, Small Brewery, and Brewery, Distillery, or Winery, as well as address permitted zones for the various alcohol uses. The retail use changes include limiting the locations of bars and taverns. Bars would no longer be permitted in the TC-2, MU, COM, CS, and IP. Removal from the TC-2 and MU zones is to reduce conflict between bars and zones that are primarily residential. Removal from the COM, CS, and IP is to remove bars from zones that are primarily car oriented. An exception in the use chart has been carved out to allow bars in conjunction with a manufacturing location in the CS or IP zones, or within a hotel. These changes are similar for a Tavern, other than no exception for a hotel. It is also proposed to change taverns and bars from permitted to conditionally permitted in the TC-1 zone.

The Brew Restaurant Use is currently limited to only the TC-1 zone. This was a deliberate decision made at the adoption of the use in 2008 to help attract a Brew Restaurant to downtown. Staff's position is that expanding the Brew Restaurant zoning to include other areas where restaurants are located may incentivize additional development of innovative restaurant options. The proposal is to allow a Brew Restaurant in the COM and MU zones.

Regarding new uses, the Taproom would be conditionally permitted in the TC-1 zone, which aligns with proposed change to bar and tavern uses. The Small Brewery use would be permitted in the IP zone and conditionally permitted in the CS zone. The small brewery would be limited to 15,000 barrels per year and no more than 10,000 sf. Staff found that the 15,000 barrels a year is a standard dividing scale between large and small breweries within the industry. Staff had included a land use for a Brewery, Distillery, or Winery, but is proposing that the city start small with manufacturing and not permit larger scale manufacturing in any zone at this time. Staff recommends keeping the Brewery, Distillery, or Winery use listed in the use table even if it is not permitted to clarify that larger breweries would not fall under other manufacturing uses in the land use table.

Chapter 17.11: District and Corridor Zone Uses

§17.11.010.   Purpose
              This Chapter sets forth the types of land uses permitted and conditionally permitted within Logan's Districts and Corridors.

§17.11.020.   Districts & Corridors Land Use: Classification
              A. Primary Uses.  Land use shall conform to the designations in Table 17.11.030 (Use Table).  Land uses are designated as follows:
                 1. Permitted Uses. A "P" indicates that a use is allowed in the respective zoning district.  Permitted uses must conform to the applicable requirements of the Land Development Code. Permitted uses requiring a public hearing are subject to the Procedures for Processing Applications contained in Chapter 17.48.
                 2. Conditional Uses. A "C" indicates that a use is allowed as a Conditional Use in the respective zoning district. Conditional uses are subject to review and approval under the provisions of Chapter 17.49, Conditional Use Permits, and following the Procedures for Processing Applications contained in Chapter 17.48. Conditional uses must conform to the applicable requirements of Chapter 17.42.
                 3. Uses Not Allowed. An "N" indicates that a use is not allowed in the respective zoning district, except where State or Federal law otherwise preempts local land use regulation.
              B. Accessory Uses and Structures. An accessory use or structure may be permitted in compliance with the applicable provisions of the zone in which it is located. An accessory use shall not commence, and no accessory structure shall be constructed, without a primary use first being lawfully established on the subject site.
              C. Temporary Uses. Temporary uses may be permitted for a period not to exceed twelve (12) calendar months in compliance with the provisions of the zone in which it is located and the provisions of Chapter 17.60.  The procedures for review and approval of a temporary use are the same as for a permanent use; however, the reviewing authority may waive certain Land Development Code standards, and impose conditions of approval on the temporary use, as it deems necessary, to promote compatibility between the proposed use and adjacent permitted uses.
              D. Non-Conforming Uses. Non-conforming uses and development are subject to the provisions of Chapter 17.52.

                                                          17.62: Definitions

Chapter 17.62: Definitions

The definitions of words in this chapter are established as specific meanings for this Title only. Words with specific meaning in the Land Development Code are as follows:


"Alcohol, Bar" means a retail business establishment that serves alcoholic beverages in accordance with definitions and requirements of the Utah Alcoholic Beverage Control Act

"Alcohol, Brewery" means a business establishment that manufactures more than fifteen thousand (15,000) barrels of beer, heavy beer, or malt liquor as otherwise defined and regulated in title 32B, Utah code, as amended.

"Alcohol, Brew Restaurant" See Logan Municipal Code Chapter 5.10.

"Alcohol, Taproom" See Logan Municipal Code Chapter 5.10.

"Alcohol, Distillery" means a business establishment that manufactures distilled, spirituous beverages, not to include those alcoholic beverages produced in a brewery or winery.

"Alcohol Liquor Store (Package)" means a store that sells alcoholic beverages for off-site consumption.

"Alcohol, Small Brewery, Distillery, or Winery" means an alcoholic beverage manufacturing use that occupies less than ten thousand (10,000) square feet in gross floor area, and produces beverages as specified in the definitions for Alcohol, Brewery; Alcohol, Distillery; and Alcohol, Winery.

"Alcohol, Tavern" means an establishment in which alcoholic beverages are served, primarily by the drink, and where food or packaged liquors may also be served or sold.

"Alcohol, Winery" means a business establishment that manufactures alcoholic beverages from the fermented juice of grapes, fruits, or other liquid bearing plants for off premises consumption, not to include those alcoholic beverages produced in a brewery or distillery.

"Brew Restaurant" See Logan Municipal Code Chapter 5.10. [struck through]

"Liquor Store (Package)" means a store that sells alcoholic beverages for off site consumption. [struck through]

"Tavern" means an establishment in which alcoholic beverages are served, primarily by the drink, and where food or packaged liquors may also be served or sold. [struck through]
