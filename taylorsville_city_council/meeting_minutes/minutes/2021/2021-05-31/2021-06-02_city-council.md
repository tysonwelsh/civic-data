     Mayor Kristie Overson expressed her appreciation to Salt Lake County as well as the City
Council for the new Mid-Valley Performing Arts Center located just east of city hall. The building
was spectacular and both test events and open houses were currently being held.

Judge Christopher Bown had been sworn in the previous week by his father, Judge Gregory Bown
and would be the new Taylorsville Justice Court judge.

The mayor wished to give a “shout out” to Unified Fire Authority for the 42 new recruits who had
recently graduated from a 16-week recruitment camp. When not fighting fires, the firefighters of
Station #119 spent some time with the students at Calvin Smith Elementary during their end-of-
year “field day.” She thanked UFA for their continued involvement with the community.

Mayor Overson had recently had the opportunity to visit with fourth grade students about city
government and the importance of being good citizens. Her hat was off to both students and
educators for the job they had done navigating what had been a very challenging year.

The public could now take a virtual tour of the Taylorsville Bennion Heritage Center via the city’s
website. Ten short videos had been created by Communications Director Kim Horiuchi and the
Historic Preservation Committee.

The mayor pointed out how vital good relationships were. She was grateful for the partnerships
Taylorsville enjoyed with both West Jordan and West Valley City. Together, the three cities
represented about 300,000 citizens. She and her counterparts at those cities had recently attended
a virtual meeting with Governor Cox which was a great opportunity to make him aware of
Taylorsville’s needs. She appreciated the opportunity to share ideas and continue great
relationships with surrounding communities.

       Mayor Overson presented some statistics regarding some of the work being conducted by
city staff. In May, there were 146 business license applications submitted with 131 issued and 17
renewed. The Code Enforcement division opened 111 cases, closed 94 and had a total of 351
active cases. Code Enforcement Manager Kathleen Richins spoke to the Youth Council earlier in
the day and explained that division’s role in keeping Taylorsville a community to be proud of. The
Planning Department had 11 plan applications, three of which had been completed. The Building
Department had 126 building applications submitted in May with 49 active permits. As always,
the entire Taylorsville staff was very productive.

The most frequently asked question recently involved the flags, wood stakes and spray-painted
markers found in various locations throughout the city. The answer was, “It’s Google Fiber
coming to Taylorsville!”

   o Cracked concrete created increased risk

        5.1      Ordinance No. 21-11 – An ordinance of the City of Taylorsville approving a
                 General Plan Map Amendment from Regional Commercial to High-Density
                 Mixed-Use for 3.83 acres of property located at 1758 West 5400 South – Jim
                 Spung

      Jim Spung displayed a vicinity map and aerial image of the parcel in question and explained
the current Zoning Map designation (Community Commercial) and current General Plan
designation (Regional Commercial). The applicant was asking to amend the General Plan map to
a high-density, mixed-use designation to create 182 apartment units and approximately 4700 sq
feet of commercial retail space.

He then displayed a concept Development Plan but pointed out it was a concept only and it was
not currently up for approval. What the applicant currently sought was an amendment to the
General Plan Map. They had also submitted a Zoning Map amendment to an SSD (Site Specific
Development) Zone but were not yet ready for that to be presented to the city council.

Mr. Spung showed the council potential parking plans, concept elevations and various project
details. He also asked that they consider numerous principles in the General Plan map when
making a decision regarding the proposed project. Considering those principles, he indicated that
staff could not recommend approval. He indicated that the Planning Commission had tied 3-3 on
the proposal, with one commissioner absent from the meeting. City Attorney Tracy Cowdell
clarified that if a motion did not pass, it was deemed to have failed.

Chair Cochran indicated the proposed project appeared to be spot zoning. Mr. Spung stated that
spot zoning typically involved changing the land use designation for one parcel in the center of
something that was very different and that was not the case here.

Chair Cochran invited the applicant to step forward. Chris Zarek from Cowboy Partners stated
that the project in question was called Liberty Beacon and it was the product of a collaboration
between Cowboy Partners and Legacy Real Estate Partners. He invited landowner David Werts
of Legacy Real Estate to join him at the podium. Mr. Wertz explained that his company had
created 45,000 square feet of retail space when it acquired the land, which had previously been
home to Furniture Warehouse. The current proposal was the last piece of the project which would
result in it being a true mixed-use development. Retail had changed since the property was
purchased six years ago and it was important that the city and real estate developers keep that in
mind. They had built a brand new 13,000 square foot retail space on Redwood Road four years
prior, 6,000 sq feet of which had still never been occupied. In addition to sales tax coming from
brick-and-mortar retail, it was also generated by on-line sales where people lived. If people were
brought to the city, tax revenue would continue to be realized. There was 30,000 sq feet of vacant
shop space across the street, in addition to an additional 80,000 sq feet of empty “big box” space.
Taylorsville City Council Minutes

Clearly, more commercial space was not needed but the current housing crisis was a matter of
record. Residential space would bring people to the city which would in turn, help support
commercial activity.

Councilmember Armstrong asked if the residential units would be single owner rentals and Mr.
Werts explained that was correct and that Cowboy Partners would continue to manage the
property. They owned Bridgeside Landing in Taylorsville, Liberty Landing and The Ridge, both
of which were in West Jordan and Liberty Bend in Sandy City. Mr. Zarek pointed out that similar
residential mixed-use developments were being considered for Jordan Landing, Sugar House and
other locations throughout the Salt Lake Valley.

Councilmember Armstrong inquired if there had been any traffic studies done in the area. Mr.
Zarek responded that they had, and that they’d love to share that information with the council.

Councilmember Harker pointed out that the council was there to consider a General Plan Map
amendment—not talk about traffic patterns.

Troy Hardy, a commercial real estate broker since 2003, explained that many commercial projects
were trying to pivot and be more successful considering the changes taking place in the retail
industry. The city had a unique opportunity in that there was an empty field sitting in what could
be a very vibrant retail center if a multi-family residential component could be added. It was
nonsense to say that we are only talking about a 4,700 piece of property. They should be talking
about a 200,000 sq foot retail project with a multi-family component at the center. Retail centers
were currently being torn down to make way for multi-family housing. Multi-family was the
future of retail. Without the density of multi-family homes, brick and mortar retail would continue
to die off. We need to accept that things have changed—to be bold and create something for the
future.

Mr. Zarek expressed appreciation to Mr. Spung and Wayne Harper, both of whom had been very
straightforward in their dealings with his business partners and with him. He indicated there were
some nuances to what was presented in the staff report. The idea of updating the center master
plan was something that could be taken care of through the Site-Specific Development process.
They wished to continue to work with staff on things like development standards, a zoning
ordinance, and a development agreement so that they could find the correct balance for that specific
property. They would be back in front of the planning commission as well as the city council
when those details had been worked out. Mr. Zarek wished to know if the council could agree that
the site was appropriate for a mixed-use, multi-family project. If so, they could then move forward
with city staff to determine what the right conditions, plan and vision were for the project.

Chair Cochran asked Mr. Zarek how much of the property he and his business partners owned. He
responded that they had sold the land where Francesco’s stood, as well as Arby’s and three
Taylorsville City Council Minutes
June 2, 2021
Page 9



buildings on the corner. Otherwise, they owned everything along the north side—from Teriyaki
Grill to Redwood Road.

Councilmember Barbieri indicated that because the property had been parceled out, she did not
feel it was ideal for mixed-use. She was also not sure there was proper ingress and egress. So, at
this point, she did not feel this was the best place for the type of project he was proposing, even
though it looked beautiful.

Mr. Zarek reiterated that there was a lot of commercial property across the street which was sitting
empty. He felt his proposal was the next step to invigorating the area and he hoped the council
would support the idea.

Chair Cochran asked Mr. Zarek if he had built Bridgeside Apartments. He responded in the
affirmative.

After the public open house where they learned that most of the publics’ concerns were traffic
related, he and his partners submitted an SSD application. City staff then indicated that the general
plan map amendment should be addressed separately from the SSD application. There was still
time to work with the planning department to create a better traffic pattern, ingress and egress, etc.
He felt strongly that mixed-use residential was appropriate and desirable for the site, although he
recognized that there was still a lot of work to be done.

Councilmember Harker stated that if we truly want a mixed-use development, the entire area
should be mixed-use instead of just a single building.

Mr. Zarek pointed out that Kmart at 5400 South Bangerter Highway had been left vacant for ten
years and asked that the Council not allow that to happen to the property in question. He felt the
city needed to act now in order to avoid having the same problem. Incremental change was the
correct way to reverse the decline of brick-and-mortar retail—particularly here, where the Planet
Fitness building, for example, still had an eleven-year lease.

City Attorney Tracy Cowdell interjected that it was important for the city council not to delve into
the weeds of the issue. The applicant was simply seeking a yes or no vote of having a mixed-use
project on the site. It was important not to let the applicant spend hundreds of thousands of dollars,
only to end up having the entire project rejected. If the council could accept a mixed-use concept,
the applicant would then negotiate with staff to do a site-specific development plan. If the answer
was going to be ‘no’, sooner was better than later.

Councilmember Harker stated there had been plenty of time to study the issue. Mr. Zarek indicated
he still wished to explain his reasoning behind the assertion that the site in question was appropriate
for mixed-use. He felt it promoted economic sustainability and resilience, and it would provide a
Taylorsville City Council Minutes
June 2, 2021

built-in customer base for nearby businesses. Introducing the project now was a way to strengthen
the center and redevelop the surrounding buildings as they became available. Young families
could not currently purchase a home in today’s market. Rental units such as these provided them
a place to start out in life. He hoped to come back to the city council in the future with a site-
specific application for their consideration.

The primary concern appeared to be traffic. He and his partners had a traffic engineer conduct a
trip-generation study based on their concept plan and determined that converting the property to
mixed-use would reduce traffic rather than increase it. In short, he felt the project would:
    • Be economically sustainable
    • Be an efficient use of land and public infrastructure
    • Protect environmentally sensitive land
    • Support alternative modes of transportation

Councilmember Barbieri stated she would never approve residential use with small pockets of
drive-throughs. The word “incremental” scared her as she felt it meant something may or may not
end up happening. She loved the development but would probably say ‘no.’

Chair Cochran wished to clarify some information about Ordinance 21-11 with Mr. Cowdell. It
was the Chair’s understanding that the ordinance only applied to the 3.83 acres in question—not
to the entire center. Additionally, the council was not yet being asked to approve a specific plan.

Mr. Cowdell responded that if approved, the council would not be changing the zoning—that
would require a separate application. Instead, it would be changing the general plan. However,
implicit in changing the general plan was the understanding that it would be congruent with the
zoning map. The city could be exposed to legal liability by sending a message that it would
consider a different type of zoning than what is there today. By approving the ordinance as
presented, the council would be giving the applicant a nod to continue with an SSD application.

Councilmember Harker stated she agreed with everything Councilmember Barbieri had said. She
believed the city had a general plan for a reason and should stick with it.

MOTION:          Councilmember Harker moved to deny Ordinance No. 21-11 as presented.
