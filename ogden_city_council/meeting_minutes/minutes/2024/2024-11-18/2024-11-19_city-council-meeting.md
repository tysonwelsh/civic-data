Proposed Conveyance of Significant Parcels from Ogden City to Ogden
Redevelopment Agency – 18th Street and Wall Avenue and Lincoln Avenue
south of the Ogden River; and
Proposed Conveyance of Significant Parcels from Ogden City to Ogden
Redevelopment Agency – 1820 Washington Boulevard.
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed conveyance of significant
parcels of property from Ogden City to the Redevelopment Agency came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated
the Mayor has provided notice of a proposed property conveyance of six parcels of Ogden City property totaling approximately
3.5 acres to the Ogden City Redevelopment Agency. The conveyance of properties located at 18th and Wall Avenue and south of
the river on Lincoln Avenue will facilitate development of additional housing in the Ogden River Redevelopment Agency project
area. Additionally, a multi-family residential and commercial development located at approximately 18th Street and Washington
Boulevard is being developed which is anticipated to include up to 110 units in two 4-story residential buildings and a small
commercial building where the Pioneer Heritage building (Piccadilly’s building) was located. The overall project approvals have
included a rezone to C-3/CO, a street vacation of River Drive east of Washington Boulevard, both of which were approved by the
Council on September 26, 2023, property sale and transfer from the City, Redevelopment Agency, and Utah Department of
Transportation to the Parkway Group, and a participation and incentive agreement which were approved by the City and
Redevelopment Agency on November 23, 2023. Per Ogden Municipal Code §4-3A-5 the Council is required to hold a public
hearing to allow residents to comment on the proposed conveyance. Per Utah Code Ann. §17C-1-207 a 15-day notice prior to the
conveyance is required.
          Community and Economic Development Executive Director Johnson addressed the transfer of property at 18th Street and
Wall Avenue and Lincoln Avenue south of the Ogden River. He explained Ogden City currently owns two parcels located north
of the river on the corner of 18th Street and Wall (12-127-0014, 12-127-0019), three additional parcels on 18th Street between Wall
and Lincoln Avenue (12-12-0043, 03-031-0005, 03-031-0001), and an additional property on the south side of the river on the
west side of Lincoln Avenue (03-034-0005). Together these properties total approximately 3.5 acres. Upon conveyance from the
City to the Redevelopment Agency, it is proposed that the Redevelopment Agency transfer the subject property to Lotus 1900
Lincoln LLC (Lotus) via a Land Exchange Agreement to facilitate development of Riverwalk Phase 3. As consideration for the
property, Lotus will convey approximately 2.45 acres of property located east of Lincoln Avenue and south of the Ogden River to
the RDA. The property transfer and subsequent conveyance will allow development to proceed according to the newly amended
Master Plan.
          Economic Development Director Meess then discussed the transfer of property at 1820 Washington Boulevard. This
specific action is for the Council to hold a public hearing on the transfer of four parcels from Ogden City ownership to the Ogden
City Redevelopment Agency to facilitate the larger transfer of property to the Parkway Group, LLC. Three small remnant parcels
and a portion of a larger parcel must be conveyed to the Redevelopment Agency to fully execute the Land Transfer and
Development Agreement.
          Chair Richey then opened the public hearing regarding the proposal.
          Heath Satow stated that several concerns have been raised about the property transfers and he asked if those concerns
will be addressed before the transactions are finalized. He asked if the property that presently contains storage units will be
involved in the transaction. He does appreciate the fact that an updated appraisal for the property was completed, which means the
taxpayers will have just $400,000 outstanding responsibility on the Riverwalk project.
          Teresa Bramwell stated she continues to be concerned about any transaction that contemplates a transfer of land owned
by taxpayers to a private developer; these projects should include a requirement for the developer to build for-sale units rather
than developing projects that are entirely for-rent. This practice has influenced the demographics of the City and there are more
and more residents who are not investing in their own future in Ogden City because they only have access to rental units.
          There being no further persons appearing to be heard, COUNCIL MEMBER HYER MOVED THE PUBLIC
HEARING BE CLOSED. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER BLAIR, ALL VOTING AYE. Council
members Choberka and Myers were not present when this vote was taken.
          Chair Richey invited Administrative staff to address the questions raised during the public hearing. Mr. Johnson stated
the Beehive Storage property is not included in the land trade transaction; the current use of that property will be allowed to
continue, and the owner of the property has not indicated any interest in selling their property to be included in the development
project. If and when the owner chooses to sell or redevelop the property, the Master Plan and zoning regulations that are in place
at that time will regulate the future uses of the property. Relative to the appraisal of the property, he had requested the appraisal
some time ago, but received the results just this afternoon and did not have time to include them in the staff report regarding the
proposal. He stated the appraisals were performed by Western States Multi-Family Appraising and Consulting from Salt Lake
City. Chair Richey then asked Mr. Johnson if the City can require the recipients of these properties to include for-sale units in
their projects. Mr. Johnson stated that Administration is exploring the option of creating a First-time Homebuyer Investment Zone
(FHIZ) in a few project areas to ensure home ownership opportunities are included. This type of zone will also be considered for
future development and redevelopment projects throughout the City. Chair Richey inquired as to the timeline of the negotiations
surrounding the transfer of properties in the area of 18th Street and Wall Avenue; concerns have been expressed that this proposal
has been rushed and there has not been sufficient public outreach. Mr. Johnson stated the proposal dates back to 2022; the
property owner Lotus currently owns on Lincoln Avenue was not sold to them by the City, but instead they acquired it from a
private owner. Lotus made a proposal to build an apartment complex on the property and staff engaged in discussion with them
about the City’s vision for the area. Lotus has been working with the City to create a project concept that is in line with the Master
Plan for the area, but the City engaged a consultant to assist with modification of that Plan responsive to the changes that have
occurred in the market since the Plan was first created in 2002. Lotus has secured different types of approval they need for the
project, including a Low-Income Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC), for the project and they must proceed in order to avoid losing
those approvals.
          Council member White asked if the City has discussed with Lotus the option of building market rate apartments on the
project, which would mean they would forego the LIHTC funding for the project. Mr. Johnson stated Lotus has indicated doing
that would not be feasible for the multi-family aspect of the project. However, they have been receptive to the City’s intent to
consider a FHIZ for other properties they own in the City to require for-sale units. Council member White asked why building
market rate apartments in this area is not feasible. Mr. Johnson stated he would defer to Lotus, but noted it is his understanding
that they have certain funding sources and LIHTC monies that can be used on the project; however, that funding is not available to
build market-rate apartments. Council member White asked if any other government entity has been successful in creating a FHIZ
since the creation of the development tool by the State Legislature. Mr. Johnson answered no.
          Mayor Nadolski discussed the importance of home ownership opportunities in Ogden City; this is an opportunity for the
City to ‘leverage the past in order to get what is needed in the future’ and he thanked Community and Economic Development for
working on this project. He recognizes the public feels this project may seem rushed, but that is not the case, and he is aware
Community and Economic Development staff, including Mr. Johnson, have worked on this project for the past two years.
          Chair Richey invited public input from a Lotus representative. Adam Hughes approached and stated that Lotus wants to
be aligned with City Administration and the City Council in terms of what types of development they want to see in the
community. He has been working with the City for the past 2.5 years with two different Administrations to try to find a path
forward for this project. Lotus originally acquired the property with the intent to build a multi-family development, but later
learned the City desired single-family ownership opportunities on the site. That is why Lotus has explored a land transfer with the
City; this transaction will ensure that another developer can build for-sale units on the land that Lotus is transferring to the City
and Lotus will have the ability to proceed with the type of project they desire. The Master Plan requires 25 percent of the units
south of the Ogden River to be individually platted and available as for sale; there will be 16 homes that will be for-sale as part of
the Lotus development. Over the course of the past 2.5 years, as Lotus has worked with the City to amend the Master Plan, there
has been a great deal of public support to relocate the area identified for a future park from 18th Street and Wall Avenue to a
property more central to the project area and something that is surrounded by residential neighborhoods. The current development
proposal conforms with the City’s Master Plan. Lotus has been patient in its efforts to ‘get this project to the finish line’ and they
are anxious to receive approval of the land transfer this evening in order to move forward. He addressed Council member White’s
question regarding the feasibility of a market-rate apartment project in the Master Plan area; when a developer makes an
investment in a property, there is a development thesis that drives the investment. For market-rate apartments, there are specific
land use economics that must be supported for the market rate to work. The property has always been an expensive piece of
property, and the market supports the development model that Lotus has pursued. He is anxious to work with the City on other
for-sale projects, but he asked that the Council consider the current proposal as presented.

Public Comments
          Andrea Baltazar expressed frustration with the 20th Street construction project between Brinker Avenue and Harrison
Boulevard, particularly regarding a general lack of communication from the City to residents. She came home recently to find the
curb in front of her home painted red, including the driveway approach to her home. All homes on half of the block she lives on
experienced the same. This has created a lack of trust, and she is concerned about the lack of transparency on the City’s part.
Residents were told one thing, but something entirely different happened and it is now too late for residents to intervene. She
invited the Mayor and Council to drive by her home and consider if they would have approved the project if they had known this
would be the outcome. She and her neighbors have been told that the project is now done, and nothing can be done to change it.
The project managers refused to meet with her and her neighbors, and she is deeply disappointed. This situation impacts her
property value, and she does not have sufficient parking space for herself and her guests. She asked that the Council do something
to resolve the problem that has been created.
          Marie Hogge echoed Ms. Baltazar’s comments; she also lives on 20th Street and is very negatively impacted by the
situation Ms. Baltazar addressed.
          Brody Smith stated he also lives on 20th Street, and he echoed Ms. Baltazar’s comments; he implored the Mayor and
Council to visit his home to understand the true impact this project is having on the daily lives of the citizens living on that block
of 20th Street.
          James Edwards stated he owns property in Ogden and over the past three to five years he has found difficulty in getting
aid from law enforcement or the Mayor. He has reached out to the Mayor’s assistant and asked to schedule a meeting with him,
but that request has been refused. The Mayor has sent officers as a way to communicate with him; Officer Jared Nelson visited his
home two years ago when he was renovating the home that was formerly used as a drug house. He has invested over $150,000
into the community and planned to invest even more, but he was treated very disrespectfully by Officer Nelson while he was
responding to a wildfire situation near his property. The lack of response and professionalism from the City has left him with no
other option than to take matters into his own hands multiple times, but he has been called a criminal as a result for protecting his
neighborhood and himself. He is not a violent person, but acts of violence have been committed against him and threats made as
well. However, the City’s police officers do not agree and have not supported him.
          Heath Satow thanked Council member Graf for responding to his email regarding the ongoing discussion of campaign
donation limits. He thought tonight’s discussion about the matter was great, but he still believes the campaign donation maximums
should be reduced as Ogden’s limits are much higher than surrounding cities in Utah and in other states. Higher limits allow for
more influence from donors. He would also suggest a cap on anonymous contributions as this would help to avoid large sums
being hidden as untraceable donations listed as $50 anonymous donations. He added he also appreciated hearing a discussion
about the status of the Brown Ice Cream building, but he wished to remind everyone that the building sold at such a low price
without going through a public bidding process in order to remedy the blight quickly. The original deadline for occupancy was
November of 2023, but after a one-year extension the deadline has passed. He hopes this expedience will be used to strengthen
future contracts similar to this one and to protect Ogden taxpayers.
          Teresa Bramwell referenced the transfer of land on 18th Street and Wall Avenue involving the Lotus company; she raised
concerns about the land adjacent to this property last week. The land she is concerned about is on Grant Avenue between the
Ogden River and 20th Street and it was sold to another developer by Ogden City. Ther are many lots on the property, which was
formerly home to single family homes. She has researched the property ownership records and found that the taxes for these
properties have not been paid for three years and the LLC that owns them is not active with the State of Utah. This is a dire
situation, and the properties could be sold at a tax sale in the near future. It is her understanding that the City wants this area to
become a park, and her suggestion would be to use eminent domain to take other land from Lotus to use as the City sees fit,
especially if Lotus is not willing to develop as the City desires. The City is doing a huge favor for Lotus in the form of land trades
and financial incentives, and these are benefits that no other property owner in the City receives. She then concluded that she
opposes the homeless housing project planned for Madison Avenue and 23rd Street; Ogden City does not need more homeless
people in the neighborhood, especially since Lester Park is being improved so that it is safer for children.

Mayor Comments
          Mayor Nadolski addressed the comments about the 20th Steet construction project; there was a great deal of excitement
around the benefits of the project, but he is saddened to hear the sentiments that have been expressed tonight. One priority for the
project was to improve safety on the street for residents, motorists, and pedestrians; this means that some curbs had to be painted,
but he understands that is not ideal for everyone. However, it is his job to look out for the life and safety of the entire City and he
will not compromise that to create convenience for one homeowner. He will look into the issue to determine the amount of
communication that took place between the City and residents. He then addressed Ms. Bramwell’s comments about the Lotus
property; the current Administration and Council has inherited projects that were approved several years ago, and the current
Administration has the responsibility to proceed with those projects. The developer has been sitting on the property for years and
that has come at a cost; he does not believe anyone is getting rich due to any action taken by the City in an effort to complete a
project. He also addressed Mr. Edwards and stated that he is willing to discuss his concerns, but he and his family have also been
threatened and he has had to be very careful about who he chooses to meet with and when. He then noted he does not have a
strong opinion about the campaign donation issue; many cities are simply following State law, which does not provide a
maximum contribution amount. He also addressed the Brown Ice Cream building, noting this is another issue that he inherited
from the previous Administration; he is pushing to get the project completed and he is committed to continuing to operate above
board on these types of projects and contracts.
