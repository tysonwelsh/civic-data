Public Hearing of 2022 Budget approval

             THIS BEING THE TIME heretofore set for a public hearing to consider the 2022
Copperton Metro Township Budget.

               Council Member Stitzer, seconded by Council Member Bailey, motioned to open
the public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

               Mike Heagren asked several questions about how the metro township model
works. He inquired about sales tax collection, what percentage Copperton receives, the
population of Copperton, taxes paid by Copperton businesses, road taxes, etc.

               Mayor Clayton stated the Utah State Tax Commission collects taxes for the State
of Utah and sales tax is governed by a population-driven formula from the census. He answered
all of Mr. Heagren’s additional questions one by one.

             Mayor Clayton gave an overview of the budget and the anticipated funding
revenues. There were no questions from the Council.

               Mayor Clayton stated they need to move $7000 to employee benefits section.

               Council Member Bailey, seconded by Council Member Olsen, motioned to close
the public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

RESOLUTION NO. 2021-12-01                                             Date: December 15, 2021

       A RESOLUTION ADOPTING THE COPPERTON METRO TOWNSHIP’S FINAL
       2022 BUDGET

                                          RECITALS

       WHEREAS, Copperton Metro Township (“Copperton”) is a municipality and political
subdivision of the state of Utah; and

        WHEREAS, on November 16, 2021, Copperton adopted a tentative budget for the 2022
fiscal year; and

      WHEREAS, Copperton provided notice pursuant to Utah Code Ann. § 10-6-113
concerning the tentative budget and of the date, time, and place of a public hearing on the same;
and

       WHEREAS, Copperton made said tentative budget available for public inspection for a
period of at least ten days prior to the public hearing in accordance with Utah Code Ann. § 10-6-
112; and

        WHEREAS, on December 15, 2021 at 6:30 p.m., Copperton held said public hearing on
said tentative budget in accordance with Utah Code Ann. § 10-6-114.

       NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED as follows:

       1. That the tentative budget adopted on November 16, 2021, as amended (if amended)
          and attached hereto, be and hereby is adopted as the Final Budget of Copperton for
          the 2021 fiscal year.
       2. The Copperton budget officer is directed and authorized to file the final budget with
          the Utah State Auditor within 30 days of the date of this resolution pursuant to Utah
          Code § 10-6-118(2).
       3. This resolution shall take effect upon its adoption.

       APPROVED AND ADOPTED this 15th day of December, 2021.

Public Hearing of 2021 Budget Adjustments

             THIS BEING THE TIME heretofore set for a public hearing to consider the 2021
Copperton Metro Township Budget Adjustment.

               Mayor Clayton reviewed the 2021 budget adjustments. The first adjustment is due
to the CARES Act funding Copperton received that needs to be accounted for. Copperton also
received additional funding from the ARPA Act, as well as COVID expenses. They will also need
an adjustment to deal with the grave openings. This final adjustment will need to be done every
year because it is not possible to know how many people will die.

               Council Member Bailey, seconded by Council Member Olsen, motioned to open
the public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

                Mike Heagren asked if Copperton received the funding that the Jordan School
District had.

                 Council Member Stitzer stated Copperton did receive the funding. The money
that is used is from renting the equipment. She added that they have added $20,000 to the fund.

              Nathan Bracken stated the money Copperton received is not sufficient to run the
cemetery and that Jordan School District didn’t need much money because of the equipment and
people they have on staff.

               Council Member Bailey, seconded by Council Member Olsen, motioned to close
the public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

              Council Member Bailey, seconded by Council Member Severson, motioned to
approve the following Resolution adopting the proposed amendments to the 2021 budget. The
motion passed unanimously.

2022 Fee Schedule

             THIS BEING THE TIME heretofore set for a public hearing to consider the 2022
Copperton Metro Township Consolidated Fee Schedule.

                Mayor Clayton stated the fee schedule is the same as last year. The town does
not charge a business license fee but will need to charge a business license fee for ADUs. The
philosophy with the MSD is the fees are intended to be revenue neutral. If a person wants to pay
a fee with a credit card, they will have to pay the processing fee for the credit card as well.

               Council Member Bailey, seconded by Council Member Severson, motioned to
open the public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

              There was no public comment.

      WHEREAS, the Copperton Metro Township (“Copperton”) contracts with the Greater Salt
Lake Municipal Services District (“MSD”) to provide planning and development services; and

services to the metro townships of Kearns, Emigration Canyon, Magna, and White City as well as

the…council, the fee schedule shall be placed on file with the…clerk;” and

       Unified Police Department (UPD)

                Council Member Olsen stated the increase discussed previously passed at the
fourth position. Cottonwood Heights and Salt Lake City may be pushing for an additional increase
that could affect UPD’s position. Mayor Hale did not get voted in and will be replaced as a board
member by Marcus Stevenson. UPD will be looking at a new insurance broker. They have
interviewed three candidates and will be scoring the new broker.

       Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA)

                Council Member Olsen stated SLVLESA wants to change the tax cap rate. There
was a lot of resistance to this. Frank Pignanelli, SLVLESA’s lobbyist, circulated a memo saying it
would not be wise to wait until September. The reasoning for this is unclear.

               Nathan Bracken stated he does not support increasing the tax cap.

              Council Member Stitzer stated she wanted it stated strongly on the minutes that
she does not support increasing the tax cap.

               Mayor Clayton stated he also does not support increasing the tax cap.

            Mayor Clayton, seconded by Council Member Stitzer, moved to instruct Council
Member Olsen to vote against raising the tax cap. The motion passed unanimously.

               Council Member Stitzer asked how much Frank Pignanelli is being paid by
SLVLESA.

               Mayor Clayton stated in April of 2020, the board approved $20,000 for this issue.

               Council Member Olsen stated Frank Pignanelli’s reasoning is based on the fact
that 1) No emergency exists, 2) There is a reluctance to create more taxes given recent increases,
3) Rio Tinto provides a subsidy and receives few benefits; 4) metros obtained MET taxing
authority in 2021 and that funding has not been fully understood and legislators may not want to
pass another tax bill, particularly when they are looking for tax cuts; and 5) legislators will be
unlikely to support an increase in the cap when the are up for reelection.
