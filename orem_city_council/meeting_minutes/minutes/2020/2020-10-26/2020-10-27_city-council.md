     DISCUSION – Commercial Day Care Services Ordinance

Mr. Bench presented possible updates to the Commercial Day Care ordinance for commercial day
care facilities located in a residential zone, including parking requirements and other
modifications. He shared the conditions for commercial day care centers in residential zones,
including the parking ratio of 0.24 parking stalls per child, plus one parking stall per full time
caregiver/teacher. Mr. Bench said the condition also required that parking be located on site and
on-street parking may not be counted toward the parking requirements.

The Council discussed the Smiling Faces Day Care Center and current enrollment and parking;
Mr. Bench said they were meeting current standards. Mr. Bench said part of the discussion had
been centered on eliminating the conditional use permit and making Smiling Faces a permitted
use. In response to Mr. Spencer’s question, Mr. Earl said they liked to eliminate general use permits
if they could determine the use to be approved. He said it helped with the perception that the

        DISCUSSION – Utility Capital Improvement Project Funding

Mr. Tschirki introduced the discussion on funding for utility capital improvement projects, then
turned the time over to Laura Lewis with Lewis Young Robertson & Burningham, Inc. (LYRB) to
give the presentation. Ms. Lewis said the City currently had four outstanding utility system revenue
bonds; two would pay off in 2026, one would pay off in 2029, and the 2010 Issue was a state loan
at 0% maturing in 2032. She said the City’s utility systems had capital improvement plans in place
that had not been fully funded, but the City had saved significant balances for many of the projects.
Paying for multi-million-dollar pay-as-you-go multi-generational projects required years of saving
and could be perceived as unfair, so they evaluated funding options other than pay-as-you-go.

Ms. Lewis shared information on construction buildings costs like inflation indices and the
Municipal Bond Index. The current estimated True Interest Cost (TIC) for an Orem City utility
system revenue bond was approximately 2.98% for a 25-year maturity. She provided the estimated
aggregate water, sewer, and storm water project costs, and said when municipal bonds were issues
the construction proceeds were required to be spent within three years. Ms. Lewis said they had
planned on two bond issues due to anticipated construction timelines; one issues in 2021 and the
other in 2024. The City would utilize approximately $31 million that had been saved in the utility
funds to help pay for these projects. She shared a table with bond statistics for the two bond
issuances as well as debt service coverage considerations. In response to a question from Mr.
Macdonald, Ms. Lewis said the City had significant capacity to issue additional bonds and it
appeared at the moment it would be without the need to increase utility rates.

Ms. Lewis shared assumptions and summary analysis information, and utility fees and tax rate
comparisons to selected Utah cities. She said if the City aimed to continue with pay-as-you-go
plans and tried to pay for all of the needed improvements, they would spend more than currently
spending and would need to save more than currently saving. Mr. Tschirki said the projects they
were targeting had 50, 75, even 100 year lifetimes. Ms. Lewis said she understood the desire and
tendency to avoid debt, but for long-life projects it was more prudent to issue bonds with current
construction inflation and interest rates. The analysis of bonding with current rates showed a
savings of approximately $55 million to the City and residents.

Discussion ensued. Mr. Macdonald said he was not necessarily concerned over rates but rather
evenly spreading the costs of construction for the life of the project so the rates were not overly
burdensome for residents. Mrs. Lauret felt the numbers in the analysis made sense and said it
seemed prudent to move forward with bonding. Mayor Brunst said he was also in favor of bonding.
Mr. Davidson clarified that the current discussion was an update regarding capital facility plans
and impact fees, not a request for a decision from the Council.

     DISCUSION – HUB District Zone Amendments

Mr. Bench presented amendments to the HUB District zone pertaining to the creation of an Edge
zone adjacent to University Parkway. He said the purpose of the HUB District was the center of
commercial activity in Orem, filled with vibrant shopping, restaurants, and entertainment
destinations. Existing structures in the zone were legal, non-conforming. Mr. Bench said the
current design standards for the HUB District zone required that all buildings have a minimum of
three stories. The proposal for the Edge Zone removed the height requirement; Mr. Bench said the
existing shopping center buildings were one story and redevelopment in the future would mean
each lease would need to be renegotiated. He reviewed the setback requirements and the proposed
permitted retail uses for the Edge Zone within the HUB District.

Mr. Peterson said there were similar issues with existing businesses along State Street and asked
if this kind of modification could be considered along that corridor. Mr. Bench said they could
consider that, and they would use this proposed change to see how the modification worked going
forward. He said they planned to bring the proposed ordinance change before the Council at the
next scheduled meeting.


     DISCUSION – City Retention Schedule

Ms. Lambert said this was the next section of the ongoing approval for the overall retention
schedule. This would cover the Recorder’s Office division from the City Manager’s Department,
many of which had lengthy retention periods or were to be kept permanently.

Mayor Brunst asked about the archive rooms within the City. Ms. Lambert said there were four
archive rooms within the campus: two in the City Center building, and two in the Justice Court
building. As more and more city departments moved toward paperless files, the goal was to
eliminate the need for all four archive rooms and consolidate the physical location of city records.


     UPDATE – Range Fire in Orem

Chief Sanderson said the fire that began at the City’s public safety range originated at 11:56 a.m.
on October 17, with all four Orem stations responding, along with several other agencies including
Provo, Pleasant Grove, American Fork, Utah County, Forestry Service, and the Division of Natural
Resources (DNR). Acting Battalion Chief Captain Dunn served as the incident commander, and
Chiefs Earl, Hirst, and Myers were all on site that first day. Chief Sanderson expressed his gratitude
to the leadership team for their hard work and dedication, not only day to day but with this incident
specifically. Eventually the State stepped in but they made it clear that it was an Orem fire and the
State would follow the direction of the incident Liaison Chief Shaun Hirst.

Chief Hirst provided information about the day by day growth of and response to the fire. He
shared photos of the fire, and showed the area the fire had spread to which triggered the possibility
of an evacuation of Sundance. He said approximately 150 fire personnel had worked tirelessly to
contain the incident, which was currently at 93% containment. In response to a question from Mrs.

Lauret, Chief Hirst said one personal vehicle had been in the damage zone, but no structures had
been lost. Mr. Davidson added that there was some damage to the City’s water infrastructure but
the extent of the damage was yet to be determined. Chief Hirst said the Water Section of Public
Works had been incredibly responsive and helped provide map layers to avoid further damage to
the water infrastructure as they fought the fire.

Mr. Lambson asked about the need to evacuate the foothills and mountain areas where deer hunters
might be. Chief Hirst said they called Utah County Search and Rescue, who rode the area looking
for people. A helicopter was dispatched to pick up two hunters that were stuck above the fire line.

for mitigation strategies. Mr. Stevenson said he and other experts would monitor the area through
the next three years or so. Mr. Davidson added that they anticipated the incident would result in a
broad discussion about the shooting range and safety protocols to implement going forward. The
range was an important city asset for law enforcement personnel, so they would be evaluating and
determining what improvements they would include at the range to avoid any future incidents.

Mr. Peterson and Mr. Macdonald both expressed their pride in how Orem’s Fire Department team
had represented the city on the news and social media. Chief Sanderson echoed his appreciation
for capable colleagues that were able to step up in a big way with this incident.


       DISCUSION – Purchase of Hillcrest Elementary Property

Mr. Davidson shared an update and property details about the 9.33 acres the City had the
opportunity to purchase at approximately 650 East 1400 South. He said the property transaction
included the assignment of two cell phone tower lease agreements. The Alpine School District
Board of Education had unanimously approved a resolution of sale to the City of Orem on October
13, 2020, at a purchase price of $5,605,000. Mr. Davidson said the plan to fund the property
purchase was the use of Redevelopment Agency (RDA) dollars, park impact fees, and
reimbursements from the RDA to the General Fund for past completed projects. He reviewed the
following:
     Outstanding issues to resolve
     Park considerations
     2017 Level of Service (LOS) Analysis
     Park development costs & details (for 9 acres)
     Steps going forward

Mr. Davidson reviewed the inventory of Orem parks, and identified three specific types of parks
owned by the City: 1) regional/community, 2) neighborhood, and 3) mini parks. He said there were
unique amenities at the various parks in Orem, like sport facilities including futsal courts, pickle
ball courts, and baseball fields, splash pad, skate park, dog park, and more. Those unique amenities
created regional draw to those parks, even if they were smaller in terms of acreage.

     REPORT – Recreation Advisory Committee

Skip McWhorter, Chair of the Recreation Advisory Commission (RAC), listed the members of the
RAC, including Mr. Spencer as the City Council liaison. Mr. McWhorter said the differing
backgrounds and perspectives of the RAC members was a great mix in seeking how to best serve
Orem residents. The biggest project the RAC had been working on for some time was the new
Orem Family Fitness Center. He said the construction was on track and shared a video with time
lapse footage of the facility’s constructions. He also shared a photo taken from the second story of
the Fitness Center with a beautiful, scenic view of the mountains. Mr. McWhorter said he was
impressed with Method Studio, the firm which designed the building, and the construction crew
for their attention to detail and desire to create an attractive and functional center the whole
community could be proud of.

Mr. McWhorter said people loved the recreational offerings in Orem, including parks and open
green space, playgrounds, sport courts and amenities, and the splash pad. He talked about the early
success of the Fitness Founders Grassroots Fundraising efforts, which had already raised more
than $16,000. Mr. McWhorter personally expressed gratitude that Orem had been able to have
youth sports programs during recent months. He felt it had been a blessing in people’s lives during
the COVID-19 pandemic where so many had been quarantined and unable to recreate.

Mayor Brunst thanked Mr. McWhorter and the RAC for their efforts with the new Fitness Center
and recreation opportunities in Orem.

     RESOLUTION – The City will receive Public Comment on the Community Development
     Block Grant (CDBG) Projected Use of Funds for CARES ACT CDBG-CV funding and
     consider approval of a substantial amendment to the 2019-2020 Annual Action Plan

Ken Ransom, Chair of the CDBG Advisory Commission, stated the City of Orem initially received
a grant for $411,983. At present the amount remaining for allocation was $197,532.66. The city
would receive additional dollars but not until the end of the year, at which time the CDBG
Commission would make further recommendations. The funds must be used to prevent, prepare
for, and respond to the COVID-19 pandemic and related issues and the City had four priorities
which were:
     Projects serving Orem residents
     First Responder support
     Mental Health
     Domestic Violence

Mr. Ransom said they were recommending $12,000 be donated to the Alpine School District for
touchless drinking fountains for eight Title 1 schools in Orem. The City had an additional
allocation of $287,940 for CDBG-CV projects. He said they would continue to bring
recommendations as projects were identified for the community.

Mayor Brunst opened the public hearing at 6:31 p.m. Hearing no public comments, the Mayor
closed the public hearing and brought the item back to the City Council for further discussion.

Annual Action Plan of the Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) Projected Use of Funds
for CARES ACT CDBG-CV funding. Mr. Macdonald seconded the motion. Those voting aye:
Richard F. Brunst, Jeff Lambson, Debby Lauret, David Spencer, and Brent Sumner. The motion
