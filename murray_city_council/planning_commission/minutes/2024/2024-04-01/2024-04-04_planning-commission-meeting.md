GENERAL PLAN / ZONE MAP AMENDMENT

Murray North Station Area Plan - West Fireclay - General Plan Amendment in the TOD Zone - Project #24-
039

Mr. Smallwood introduced this agenda item by stating that the Murray North Station Area Plan is
mandated by House Bill 462. He showed a map of the properties that are required to be included in the
area plan for the commissioners to keep in mind during the presentation. Mr. Smallwood introduced
Erika Chmielewski from GSBS Architects.

Ms. Chmielewski provided a comprehensive overview of the Murray North Station Area Plan process and
the preferred scenario that emerged as a result. She began by explaining the state statute requirements
for station area plans, which mandates the identification of opportunities and constraints within a half-
mile radius of the station platform, with a particular emphasis on affordable housing and the
development of a community-shared vision. To facilitate the planning process, the station area was
divided into five distinct categories, with a focus on the area that showed the most potential for a five-
year implementation plan while also considering a longer-term, 20-year perspective.

Ms. Chmielewski described the three-part planning process, which commenced in January/February of
the previous year. The first part involved a thorough assessment of existing conditions and economic
opportunities, accompanied by a round of public engagement to identify the area's strengths,
weaknesses, opportunities, and threats. The second part delved into an in-depth public engagement
process, exploring alternatives to achieve the desired vision, and identifying a preferred approach. The
final part, conducted in the fall, focused on determining how to implement the vision, considering factors
such as land use, design considerations, and funding opportunities.

The analysis of existing conditions revealed several key findings. The area has a significant amount of
affordable and moderate-income housing, with nearly all of Millcreek's moderate-income housing
located in close proximity. Additionally, there were ample spaces available for retail and commercial use,
with a mixed-use transit-oriented zone requiring ground-floor commercial and retail beneath apartment
buildings. However, connectivity issues were identified, particularly on the west side of the station,
where early transit-oriented developments like Brickgate and Avida apartments had been built with
assumptions about car ownership that proved to be inaccurate. This has led to parking issues in the area,
which Murray addressed by updating zoning codes, resulting in newer developments with more
adequate parking. Safety concerns and a lack of parks and trees were also noted.

Community engagement efforts, which included an online website, tabling events, open houses, and
stakeholder interviews, revealed a desire for services, a positive perception of Fireclay, improved
connectivity for bicycles and pedestrians, increased safety and visibility, more shade, and additional
community gathering spaces. The overarching takeaway was the need to create a transit-oriented
community rather than simply focusing on transit-oriented development.

The preferred scenario that emerged aimed to establish Fireclay (also known as the Murray North Station
Area) as a distinct neighborhood, encompassing areas in both Murray and Millcreek. The plan framework

focused on quality of life, safety, land use, connectivity, livability, and urban design. Key elements
included redistributing the underutilized UTA parking lot on the east side of the tracks into shared
parking structures within new developments, adding a three-quarter acre station plaza to increase green
space and community interaction, enhancing the Main Street corridor to improve safety and connectivity,
and introducing a small-scale grocery store to address the area's food desert status, potentially located
underneath housing on a Salt Lake County-owned parcel at Main and 4500 South.

Commissioner Pehrson asked Ms. Chmielewski if Murray City would need to petition Salt Lake County to
build it. She indicated that the county is already looking at developing that site for housing and have been
talking to GSBS about changing their commercial to allow for a grocery store.

Ms. Chmielewski continued by discussing the proposed additions to the Murray North Station Area Plan,
focusing on the implementation of a system of parks and greenways to address the area's lack of green
spaces and low tree canopy coverage. She emphasized the potential to increase the tree canopy from the
current 8% to a level closer to Murray's overall coverage of 18%, with a goal of adding 24.9 acres of green
space over a 20-year period, including trails along Big Cottonwood Creek and 4500 South. These trails and
parks would also serve to increase shade in the area.

Ms. Chmielewski stressed the importance of bringing income diversity to the area, explaining that a more
diverse range of incomes would lead to increased spending power, decreased vacancy rates, and lower
turnover rates, while also providing an opportunity to increase housing ownership. She then addressed
the topic of safety, proposing youth programs focused on neighborhood improvement, a potential
community or neighborhood council to sponsor cleanups and community-building activities, and the
branding of Fireclay. Ms. Chmielewski emphasized that safety could be enhanced through both policy and
design, including proactive policing, improved lighting, and natural surveillance achieved through added
services and anchors like the Front Climbing Gym, which would increase activation during off-peak hours.
Other safety measures discussed by Ms. Chmielewski included filling sidewalk gaps, proper pruning of
trees and shrubs, and increased maintenance, particularly in the parking lot area. She also mentioned the
concept of shared parking in the core, which would involve reducing the existing parking lot and adding
three public parking areas. Ms. Chmielewski then discussed land use, identifying sites that could be
redeveloped or are currently vacant, such as UTA properties and the Salt Lake County property. These
sites would be used for various purposes, including housing, a grocery store, public parks, and mixed-use
development with shared parking.

Ms. Chmielewski concluded by emphasizing the importance of the proposed system of parks and green
spaces, which would not only provide amenities for residents to gather and build a sense of community
but also attract new residents to the area and allow for activities that are currently not possible due to
the lack of public spaces. She noted that these improvements would also contribute to the success of
local services and businesses.

Chair Patterson asked Ms. Chmielewski to confirm that there’s a long-term plan.

Ms. Chmielewski emphasized that the proposed improvements and additions to the Murray North
Station Area Plan were being considered through a long-term, 20-year plan. She noted that the
implementation plan, located at the back of the packet, outlined the anticipated timeline for the various
projects and developments. She acknowledged that many of the proposed changes were long-term in
nature and that the green space additions were being planned in accordance with the projected

population growth over the next 20 years. She explained that as the population in the area increases,
new parks and green spaces would be gradually introduced, allowing for a phased approach rather than
necessitating the simultaneous implementation of all the proposed improvements.

Mr. Smallwood acknowledged that while some aspects of the plan are long-term, the RDA is already
actively working towards implementing certain elements of the plan. Although he couldn't provide
specific details due to the sensitive nature of the real estate matters involved, Mr. Smallwood assured
them that the plan served as a valuable guide for the RDA, informing staff, elected officials, and all other
stakeholders of targeted areas and strategies for improvement. He emphasized that the RDA was
currently in the process of exploring and pursuing ways to put the plan into action, demonstrating their
commitment to the revitalization and enhancement of the Fireclay area.

Ms. Chmielewski emphasized that the Murray North Station Area Plan was designed to be a
comprehensive neighborhood plan, rather than just a response to state statute requirements. She
stressed the importance of creating a plan that would remain relevant and effective even if the state
statute were to change or be eliminated in the future. The potential development program outlined in
the plan demonstrated the projected growth in the area, with an increase in residents expected over the
next five years, and even more significant growth anticipated over a 20-year period and at full build-out.
Ms. Chmielewski noted that the plan was intended to be implemented in stages, ensuring a gradual and
manageable approach to the area's development.

Ms. Chmielewski highlighted three main focal areas within the connectivity section: north-south
connections, east-west connections, and connections within the station itself. She acknowledged that
Fireclay currently felt isolated and emphasized the need to improve accessibility and mobility both
through and around the station. The proposed Big Cottonwood Creek Trail and its southern extension
would enable Fireclay residents to easily access City Hall and other amenities in the area. Additionally,
Ms. Chmielewski mentioned the long-term goal of extending the Big Cottonwood Creek connection to
the Jordan River Trail and eventually creating a connection across the Union Pacific rail line to facilitate
development between the rail line and I-15, as well as improve safety by providing more access points to
the area between the Union Pacific line and the tracks station.

Lastly, Ms. Chmielewski discussed the livability and urban design aspects of the plan, stressing the
importance of branding the station as the Fireclay neighborhood. She proposed the addition of
wayfinding elements to help people navigate the area and realize the proximity of public transportation,
as many visitors to the Front Climbing Gym were unaware of the nearby tracks station. Improving
multimodal corridors along Fireclay Main Street and Central Avenue through the incorporation of
outdoor seating areas, banners, and wayfinding signage would enhance the walkability experience and
encourage people to spend more time outdoors.

Ms. Chmielewski concluded by discussing the implementation of the area plan, which outlines potential
funding sources, including grants, general funds, private developer funds, park impact fees, and UTA
funds. The implementation plan is divided into one-year, two-year, three-year, four-year, five-year, five-
to-ten-year, and ten-to-twenty-year goals, providing a flexible timeline that can adapt to changing market
conditions, real estate trends, and demographic shifts. She emphasized that the plan was designed to be
adaptable to meet growth and future needs.

Mr. Smallwood said that staff recommends that the Planning Commission forward a recommendation of
approval to the City Council for the Murray North Station Area Plan.

Commissioner Hacker asked Mr. Smallwood if their comments will be implemented before the plan goes
to the City Council.

Mr. Smallwood said, depending on the comments, planning staff will get those amended. He commented
that they will have comments from several city departments to take into consideration, as well as
Millcreek’s feedback. He anticipates an updated draft before the plan is presented to the City Council.

Commissioner Hacker asked if Ms. Chmielewski had a sense of how the Millcreek Planning Commission
feels about adopting this plan. Ms. Chmielewski said they feel strongly about implementing the plan.

Commissioner Hacker asked Ms. Chmielewski to talk about a cover over 4500 south as additional green
space or open space. Ms. Chmielewski said in order to justify placing a park on top, there needs to be
significant increases in density and services on both sides. Right now, there isn’t any on either side. At
this time, it isn’t financially feasible. If the southern area does begin to change significantly, to allow more
opportunities, that might allow for that concept to occur.

Commissioner Henrie asked why there was not much focus on the area south of 4500 South. Ms.
Chmielewski said they wanted to start directly around the Fireclay station at first, because that area is
the easiest to redevelop. Once the project gets going, they’ll move the focus further out and incorporate
more area into the Fireclay district.

Commissioner Henrie asked Ms. Chmielewski to talk more about why there is so much vacant retail and
commercial space in the area. Ms. Chmielewski said they discussed that with developers who were in the
steering committee meeting. They said that the residents in the area don’t have enough disposable
income to sustain those services over time. Developers and business owners also cited safety concerns as
a reason that they have chosen not to stay in the area.

Mr. Smallwood said that another issue is that the Fireclay area is secluded and doesn’t have a lot of
visibility, which keeps businesses from wanting to open there.

Chair Patterson said that while the implementation plan for the area included different phases, there
seemed to be no prioritization or weighting of the various elements within those phases. She suggested
that certain aspects, such as connectivity, bike lanes, and green spaces, could be given higher priority, as
they were more feasible in the near term compared to long-term projects like redeveloping buildings.
She proposed communicating with the Parks and Recreation Department to incorporate these priorities
into their master plan and budgets, and emphasized the importance of connecting the area to pedestrian
and bike paths, particularly to provide access to a grocery store on 4500 South.

Mr. Smallwood said they’ll make those changes to the prioritization of certain aspects of the plan.

areas as this is a high priority. Mr. Smallwood acknowledges that is a priority and that both areas want a
grocery store. He says there will be a pedestrian connection, but currently there is not a vehicular

Ms. Chmielewski clarified that phase one of the project will be building rentals. The next phase will have
ownership opportunities.

Mr. Smallwood asked Mr. Hackles to discuss the matter of the community garden after the meeting. He
said the RDA has been chatting with the property owner and could potentially use parks impact fees to
purchase the land for the purpose of a community garden.

Mr. Smallwood addressed the comment regarding traffic. He said the state has mandated that cities plan
housing around transit stations. Along with those plans, services will be provided in the area. Having
services closer to housing will reduce the overall amount of traffic.

Ms. Chmielewski said that part of the plan is to increase grid connection, so people don't have to be
circulating around in circles. She also said they did an extensive parking analysis (located in the appendix)
They determined that when developers incorporate parking fees into rental fees, the parking structures
are 85% to 94% utilized. When developers charge extra for use of the parking structures, they are only
46% utilized. They will be pushing developers to incorporate parking fees into rental fees to reduce street
parking.

Commissioner Hacker commented regarding the issues with traffic. He described how the process of
putting in infrastructure occurs within a city, stating that often the roads are built before the buildings.
This makes it difficult to accommodate future traffic needs as a city grows. He said a traffic study had
been done for the Fireclay area, but that was before the area was developed. Additional transportation
studies have been done suggesting improvements. He doesn’t know if any of those have been
implemented yet, but there are plans to do so. He said it’s a process that takes time and is difficult for
the Planning Commission (as citizens) as well as for the city transportation staff, city planners and UDOT
transportation planners.
