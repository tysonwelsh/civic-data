Utah Support Advocates for Recovery Awareness (USARA)
          Utah Support Advocates for Recovery Awareness (USARA) has served thousands of individuals recovering from the
effects of substance abuse since 2007. USARA has also provided critical support for families and the community at-large.
USARA's primary message is that "recovery is possible and there are no dreams that are unattainable."
          USARA's focus is on long-term recovery from addiction to alcohol and other drugs. Their goal is "to enhance the
quantity and quality of support available to people seeking and experiencing long-tenn recovery from addiction." USARA also
advocates at the local, state and federal levels to help individuals and families get access to the resources they need, including
access to effective health care, treatment, peer support and other recovery services.
          Trevor Cook and Charli Salas serve as Recovery Coaches at USARA. They would like to make the community aware of
their free services and provide information regarding upcoming events.
          Mr. Cook stated USARA is currently Utah's only recovery community organization; it is a non-profit entity that was
created in 2007 and an Ogden branch of the organization was recently opened. He stated he and Ms. Salas are certified recove1y
support specialists who guide individuals through their recovery process and through mental illness. USARA is heavily involved
in the community and hosts a wide range of free community events; they are affiliated with Weber Human Services and other
local treatment agencies in the area. Support specialists also participate with local task forces. He then discussed the case
management process facilitated by USARA and discussed upcoming community events in which USARA specialties will be
involved. USARA believes the opposite of addiction is connection and they want to offer opportunities for connection through a
community platform.
          Ms. Salas then added one of the unique things about USARA is that in addition to being a certified treatment agency
through the State of Utah, all ofUSARA's support specialists are also in long-term recovery. This gives them the ability to relate
to the individuals they are treating. She stated one event hosed by USARA that is very impactful is the monthly family fun night;
this is a fun social sober activity that anyone is welcome to attend with their family. Additionally, the upcoming Rally for
Recovery is a legislative rally aimed at adjusting laws in the State of Utah to give individuals a greater chance at achieving
recovery. She discussed the Peer Advisory Council (PAC) atUSARA, which is a group that meets once a month with leaders in
the community to detennine the best types of support that USARA can offer to local residents. USARA also provides suppmt for
loved ones of addicts by giving them the tools to create boundaries and avoid enabling a family member while helping them to be
more receptive to receiving treatment. Finally, USARA will be hosting its Recove1y Day in Ogden in the near future and all are
invited to attend; there will be many activities for attendees to participate in as well as a presence by local employers who can
offer jobs to individuals in recovery. USARA is always looking for ways to be more involved in the communities it suppmts.

          A memo from the Community and Economic Department regarding a proposed Lynn Community Plan amendment and
rezone at 140-152 West 2 nd Street came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated a petition has been filed by Shawn
Strong to amend the Lynn Community Plan to allow the option of either R-5 or R-3 zoning on approximately four acres of
property at 140-152 West 2nd Street. The Lynn Community Plan currently allows for the R-3/CO, P-1/CO or C-1/CO zoning
options on the property directly adjacent to the comer of 2nd Street and Wall Avenue but restricts the property to the west to low
density residential. Mr. Strong has acquired the property on the comer which recently received approval for a townhome project
under the R-3/CO zoning and is interested in acquiring the additional four acres to the west. The petition to amend the community
plan would allow expansion of an updated multi-family project. The initial petition requested the option of the R-5 zoning but was
updated to include either the R-5 zoning or the R-3 zoning with a conditional overlay approval. The Planning Commission
reviewed both options and is recommending denial of the petition for either the R-5 or R-3 zoning option.
          An additional memo from the Community and Economic Development Department explained a petition has been filed by
Shawn Strong to rezone approximately four acres of property located at 140-152 West 2nd Street from R-1-6 (Single family
Residential) to R-5 (Multi-family Residential). Mr. Strong has amended his petition to request the approval ofa rezone to R-3
(Multifamily Residential). The rezone petition was submitted in conjunction with a petition to amend the Lynn Community Plan
to allow the option of either the R-3 or R-5 zoning on the subject property. The Commission did not find good reason to
recommend approval of the community plan amendment allowing the higher density zoning, and, as a result, the Commission
found that the petition to rezone the property was not consistent with the existing community plan and has forwarded a
recommendation of denial for both the community plan amendment and the rezone.
          Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the
petitions. He presented an aerial photograph to orient the Council to the location of the property to the zoning request that is
subsequent to the Community Plan amendment. The Community Plan provides for R-3 zoning with a Professional/Institutional
(PI) land use overlay opportunity. The Council previously took action to adjust the zoning for the 3 .13-acre parcel on the comer of
Wall Avenue and 2 nd Sh·eet, but the petitioner would like to increase the overall property size by adjusting the zoning for the
adjacent 4. 72 acres of property and developing both parcels. This would provide for increased density in the area. He noted that
since the Lynn Community Plan was adopted, there have been 483 single family homes, 14 duplex, 66 aparhnent units, and a new
elementary school constructed in the Plan area. The Planning Commission has considered these trends and the petition's
consistency with the General Plan. They evaluated the manner in which higher density will reflect the neighborhood character, if it
will isolate neighborhoods, and if it will offer a transition between intense and less intense uses. They also considered whether 2nd
Street and Wall Avenue can be defined as a 'center' and Mr. Montgomery reviewed other areas in the City that have been
identified as having potential for future development as a 'center'. This includes, but is not limited to, urban mixed-use centers
and neighborhood village centers. He presented a slide that identified the density on properties in close proximity to the subject
property; most of the development that has occurred in the area consists of single-family subdivision projects. The Community
Plan discusses development options for the four comers of 2 nd Street and Wall Avenue; any zoning consideration should be
conditional overlay zoning with a development agreement to ensure design and use compatibility with the character of the area.
The zoning options identified in the Plan for the northeast comer include R-3/CO, PI/CO, or CI/CO. He then discussed zoning
considerations, noting the R-1-6 zoning designation allows single family detached homes and PRUD developments for properties
of five acres or larger. The R-2 zone allows single family or duplex development and PRUD developments if the property is five
acres or larger. Finally, the R-3 zone allows for single family to multiple family projects or PRUD developments if the property is
eight acres or larger. The subject property is 4.72 acres, meaning the petitioner could not pursue a PRUD project according to
current zoning guidelines. Mr. Montgomery then discussed the Commission's consideration of the plan amendment; they
contemplated whether the character of the area has changed from what was anticipated when the Community Plan was developed.
He offered a comparison of aerial photographs of the area from 1993 and 2019; the photographs illustrated the infill development
that has occwrnd in the area immediately sun-ounding the subject property. The Commission considered whether there are special
circumstances that wairnnt consideration of a Community Plan amendment; this includes things like regional changes,
