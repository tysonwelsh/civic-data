  APPLICABLE DEVELOPMENT CODE REGULATIONS

Section 04.060. Criteria for Approval.
No amendment to this Code, or the Zoning District Map(s), may be recommended for approval by the Commission
nor approved by the City Council unless such amendment or conditions thereto are consistent with the General Plan.
In considering a Code amendment, or Zoning District Map(s) amendment, the applicant shall identify, and the City
Staff, DRC, Commission, and the City Council may consider the following factors, among others:

    1.   The effect of the proposed amendment on the character of the surrounding area;
    2.   Consistency with the goals and policies of the General Plan.
    3.   Consistency and Compatibility of the proposed zone with the General Plan land uses of nearby and adjoining
         properties.
    4.   The suitability of the properties for the uses requested and their suitability for the existing uses identified by
         the General Plan;
    5.   Whether a change in the uses allowed for the affected properties will unduly affect the uses, or proposed uses
         for nearby and adjoining properties;
    6.   The gain to the public health, safety and welfare from the existing classification to the proposed amendment;
         and
    7.   The overall community benefit of the proposed amendment.


  REPORT ANALYSIS

Lehi City requests review and recommendation of proposed amendments to Chapter 12 of the Development Code
concerning buffering and screening requirements. One of the key changes includes the removal of the paragraph re-
garding compatibility, as it is difficult to accurately determine the compatibility between different land uses.

The proposed updates to screening requirements distinguish the fencing specifications for various types of uses when
they are next to residential. For Neighborhood Commercial and Commercial zones, a six-foot masonry fence is re-
quired, while more intensive commercial uses (such as heavy commercial and light industrial) will require an eight-
foot masonry fence. The Code also clarifies that this taller fence will not require a conditional use permit.

The buffering updates introduce a table outlining the minimum buffer width required for different uses. Neighborhood
Commercial has the smallest buffer requirement at 12 feet, while Light Industrial, Technical Manufacturing, and His-
toric Industrial zones have the largest at 30 feet. These buffer requirements apply to buildings up to 20 feet in height;

building height.

The revised landscaping requirements specify that trees must be planted 30 feet apart, with a minimum mature height
of 35 feet. It also includes guidelines for the types of trees and shrubs that may be used.

Additionally, the proposed amendments clarify that in Mixed-Use or Transit-Oriented Zones, when nonresidential
uses are adjacent to residential areas, the buffering requirements for commercial zones may apply.

The Development Review Committee (DRC) has reviewed the proposed amendments and had no further comments.


  SCOPE OF APPROVAL

Development Code Amendments are Legislative Items which provide the Planning Commission discretion if they
want to give it a positive recommendation or not. They also can include in their motion any changes they would like
to see included in the proposed amendment.

The public can give public comments on Development Code Amendments. These are great times for the public to
voice concerns and recommend changes.


  POSSIBLE MOTIONS AND FINDINGS

The Planning Commission may move to recommend approval or denial of the proposed amendments to the Lehi
City Development Code. Any motion should include findings. Possible findings include:

    1.   The proposed Development Code Amendment provides different buffering and screening requirements based
         on the intensity of use.
    2.   The proposed Development Code Amendment protects/ does not protect residential property owners more
         fully.
    3.   The proposed Development Code amendment is/is not in conformance with the purposes, intent, and provi-
         sions of the General Plan and its various elements.
    4.   Additional findings based upon information presented during the public hearing.
