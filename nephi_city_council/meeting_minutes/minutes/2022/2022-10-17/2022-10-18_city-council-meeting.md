RODERICK NEPHI BUSINESS PARK PLAT A PHASE 1 FINAL PLAT APPROVED:

   Mr. Atkinson updated the council that the Roderick Nephi Business Park development had submitted
a phased approach for the final plat of their industrial development. The first phase of the project will
include two lots. A light industrial building has already been approved at site plan for lot 1 at the
previous commission meeting before phasing was proposed. This final plat shows a 2-lot, phase 1
industrial subdivision along Highway 28, and Elevator Road. The property is adjacent to the property for
the future Jenson Refrigeration plant (to the west).
  Access for the subdivision will consist of a connection to Elevator Road on the north end.
Lot 2 will be used as stormwater collection for the proposed and subsequent phases of this
development. As lots develop, they will tie into the overall storm system and use Lot 2 for their
stormwater collection.
  The site contains natural drainage through the middle of the site running north/south. The DRC notes
from the preliminary and final reviews had stated that the developer must convey the water that uses
this natural drainage from one end of the site to the other. The current plans will only address this on
Lot 1, with future development addressing this issue further at later phases. This will protect the
planned lots from future flooding issues.

NORTH RIDGE PHASE E FINAL PLAT APPROVED:

    Mr. Atkinson reviewed with the council that Terry Messersmith had submitted a final plat for Phase E
of the North Ridge development. This development is a 2-lot commercial subdivision adjacent to Main
Street. The south lot is on the southeast corner of Main Street and 1500 North while the larger north lot
is on the northeast corner of the same intersection. The north lot is approximately 5 acres, and the
south lot is about 1 ½ acres. These areas are zoned Highway Commercial (HC-1) and are near what will
be considered a major intersection as the city grows. Utilities for the site have been installed when prior
phases were completed. It is possible that the north lot may subdivide in the future.
   The council discussed with Mr. Messersmith the closing of 1450 North and opening 1500 North. Cody
Messersmith said they are planning on running curb and gutter across 1450 North and Main to close the
road. The Messersmiths asked if they could begin developing the south lot because they have a buyer
ready to purchase the property. The council said they could as soon as 1450 North was closed because
the infrastructure for that lot was complete.
   Councilor Memmott moved to approve the final plat for North Ridge Phase E. Councilor Callaway
seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

ORDINANCE 10-18-2022 --222 BUSINES PARK REZONE TO HIGHWAY COMMERCIAL ADOPTED:

  Mr. Atkinson reviewed with the council that Alex and Peggy Johnson are seeking approval for a rezone
of a parcel of property located at approximately 618 W Sheep Lane that had recently been annexed into
the city for the Industrial - 1 (ID-1) zone. He stated that now the city staff and the Johnsons feel a
Highway Commercial - 1 (HC-1) zone would be more appropriate for this parcel. Mr. Johnson is
proposing an eight (8) lot commercial subdivision in conjunction with this rezone request. This would be
in keeping with other zoning and developments in the immediate area, and the city staff feels would be
the highest and best use of this uniquely shaped and located property.
   Councilor Worwood moved to adopt Ordinance 10-18-2022 changing the zone from ID-1 to HC-1 on
the Alex and Peggy Johnson parcel. Councilor Parady seconded the motion. The motion passed on a
unanimous vote.

JENSON REFRIGERATION EASEMENT AGREEMENT APPROVED:

  Mr. Atkinson updated the council that the City and Jenson Refrigeration have came to an agreement
on the easements for the electric service which will supply the new refrigeration plant as well as the
south redundant electric connection. In the agreement, the city will pay $8,500/acre for the easements
needed for the south redundant electric connection. Although this connection indirectly benefits the
refrigeration plant and all other large electric users, the costs should be borne by the electric system and
then dispersed through updated impact fees.
  The agreement also specifies that a portion of the easements will be absorbed by the adjacent
development being completed by Roderick Enterprises. Staff has reviewed the agreement and the
original draft was put together by the City Attorney. The agreement is recommended by city staff and
will assist in completing the city’s south redundant electric connection.
   Councilor Parady moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the easement agreement with Jenson
Refrigeration. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

TEMPEST ENTERPRISES AWARDED BID FOR NATURAL GAS GATE STATION AT OWENS CORNING:

  Mr. Atkinson reported to the council that last year, the city engaged in conversations with Owens
Corning to supply additional natural gas to their facility for an upcoming expansion. That expansion is
currently underway, and the city has gone out to bid to build a new gate station at their facility. This
allows the city to supply the appropriate level of natural gas to Owens Corning without jeopardizing the
pressure levels of customers downstream.

SCHOOL RESOURCE GRANT DISCUSSION:

   Mr. Atkinson informed the council that Chief of Police Morgan recently learned that the police
 department had received a grant from the U.S. Department of Justice for the hiring of an additional
 School Resource Officer. The grant was for 3 years and totals $125,000. This breaks out to
 approximately $41,000 per year for the 3-year period. The school district and the city have spoken
 about sharing the remaining costs and then paying a 50/50 share after the grant term has expired. If
 this were to happen, the costs would be approximately the following:

SRO Costs:       $120,132 $127,152 $132,684 $137,772 $143,064 $147,840
Federal Share:            $43,000 $42,000 $40,000                           $125,000
City Share:               $42,076 $45,342 $48,886 $71,532 $73,920 $281,756
School Share:             $42,076 $45,342 $48,886 $71,532 $73,920 $281,756
                          * Estimated Costs

  The costs assume an inflationary component for wages and benefits and an annual $3,000 budget for
materials and supplies. A car lease for the SRO position would be paid out of the Police Department
budget.
   The approximate $42k to $48k that the city would pay for their portion of the SRO position could
probably come from anticipated additional sales taxes and commercial property taxes, although sales
tax revenue can be a tenuous source of income. This is particularly true in times of recession and
economic indicators have shown that a recession is a high likelihood. The council may want to consider
instituting small property tax adjustments now and build toward a partial or complete funding of this
position by FY 2027 when 50% of the position will need to be funded without help from the federal
grant.
  Councilor Memmott moved to accept the grant contingent on Juab School District joining with us.
Councilor Callaway seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.
