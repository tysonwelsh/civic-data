REVIEW AGENDA FOR REGULAR CITY COUNCIL MEETING OF JANUARY
24, 2023
Eric Bunderson, City Attorney, stated that the Council requested that Starlink internet be
looked into for price comparison for internet services at The Ridge. He stated that the
service would be similar to what the City currently has. He noted that speed would slightly
improve and the cost would be less expensive than UTOPIA but added that there would be
other concerns such as environmental, wind, satellite issues, etc. Eric indicated that
UTOPIA did offer another proposal that would keep installation at the same cost but would
greatly reduce monthly costs from $700 to $50 by using services and connections from
City Hall. He noted the benefit of UTOPIA is that it is fiber which can be more reliable.

regarding items listed on the Agenda for the Regular City Council meeting scheduled later
this night.

RESOLUTION 23-11: AUTHORIZE THE EXECUTION OF A PROFESSIONAL
SERVICES AGREEMENT WITH HORROCKS ENGINEERS FOR A PAVEMENT
CONDITION ASSESSMENT
Dan Johnson, Public Works Director, presented proposed Resolution 23-11 that would
authorize the Execution of a Professional Services Agreement with Horrocks Engineers for
a Pavement Condition Assessment.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       Every three years, the City is required to assess the condition of its pavements to
       evaluate performance in pavement asset management and improve pavement
       treatment methods.

       The Public Works Department hires a third party to provide a road condition
       assessment. In 2020 the City hired Roadbotics, Inc. to assess the road condition
       using AI algorithms to analyze roadway images collected every ten feet. Prior to
       that the City contracted with the Utah Local Technical Assistance Program (LTAP)
       with Utah State University graduate students to drive and visually assess the
       pavement condition.

       Under this agreement Horrocks will drive each public street in the City collecting
       LIDAR (Light Detecting and Ranging, or 3-D laser scanning) data, and will use
       LIDAR data to perform the pavement condition assessment.

       The Engineering Division will have access to the LIDAR data which will be used
       as survey data on design projects.

The City Council will consider Resolution 23-11 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
February 7, 2023 at 6:30 P.M

RESOLUTION 23-12: AUTHORIZE THE PURCHASE OF CARDIAC
MONITORS FROM ZOLL MEDICAL CORPORATION
John Evans, Fire Chief, presented proposed Resolution 23-12 that would authorize the
Purchase of Cardiac Monitors from Zoll Medical Corporation.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as

follows:

       The West Valley City Fire Department responds on an average of 10,000 EMS
       calls each year. The West Valley City Fire Department operates as an Advanced
       Life Support (ALS) Service. As an ALS service working within the requirements
       of the Utah Bureau of EMS the West Valley City Fire Department is required to
       carry cardiac monitors that can read 4 lead EKG, 12 lead EKG, defibrillate,
       cardiovert and externally pace patients. The Zoll-X series monitor can perform all
       these required functions along with performing blood pressure reading, SPO2
       (oxygen saturation) readings, and CO2 level readings.

       The West Valley City Fire Department has operated Zoll monitors for over 10
       years and have found them to be reliable. Currently the department operates 11
       Zoll-X series monitors. Of the 11 monitors, 5 have come to the end of their
       recommended service life. With the approval of this issue paper proposal, 5 Zoll-
       X series monitors would be traded into Zoll Medical Corporation and put towards
       the purchase 6 new Zoll-X series monitors. These 6 new monitors will replace the
       5 monitors that have come to the end of their recommended service life and will
       also provide for one additional monitor to be placed on a Heavy apparatus that
       currently does not carry a cardiac monitor. This would ensure that all department
       front-line EMS response apparatus have a cardiac monitor.

       The proposed total cost is $215,000. The department wishes to enter into an
       agreement with Zoll Medical Corporation to have this total amount ($215,000)
       divided into equal payments over the next 5 years. This agreement has no
       additional cost and no interest tied to it and is a payment option that Zoll Medical
       Corporation offers.

       In 2017 the department entered into a similar payment agreement for the purchase
       of 5 Zoll-X series monitors. The cost was $32,112.31 / year. A final payment on
       this purchase plan was made in 2022. With the new proposed purchase of 6 Zoll
       X-series monitors the cost would be $43,000/year. This is a difference of an
       additional $10,887.69/year from what we have been paying. This price accounts
       for one additional monitor (6 total) as compared to (5) from the previous payment
       plan.

The City Council will consider Resolution 23-12 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
January 24, 2023 at 6:30 P.M
