Economic Development and Housing Director, Jeff Jones, said he is in attendance to give 
a presentation about the Regional Housing Needs Assessment for Summit County.  He 
said after the presentation, review, and public hearing, he hopes the Commission will be 
willing to submit a positive recommendation to the County Council to approve of the 
amendment to Chapter 7.    
 
Director Jones said that SB 34 is a merger of HB 259 and SB 136.  To give the bill more 
teeth, the State Legislature decided that each county must consider adopting SB 34 in 
their Moderate-Income Housing (MIH) plans.  He began a review of the bill and what 
this will mean for Summit County: 
• SB 34 requires counties to include at least four of the 23 possible strategies in 
their General Plan.  Summit County must submit these strategies to workforces 
services no later than December 1, 2019.   Reporting will be due annually going 
forward from this year.  This is important because it will keep the plans current.  
It will help to create a 5-year projection of what the need is.   
• Counties must be up-to-date on the MIH plan and the reporting requirements to 
be eligible for the Transportation Investment Funds (TIF) and the Transit 
Transportation Invest Fund (TTIF) monies. The TIF fund was $702 million in 
2018.  Director Jones said this funding is used by Summit County for the bus 
system in the Snyderville Basin and the connection to Kamas.  There are plans to 
move forward with a bus rapid transit system.     
• Tonight’s action is to bring Eastern Summit County General Plan into compliance 
with SB 34.   
• Each year, Staff will be required to demonstrate that the Planning Commission 
and County Council have reviewed the goals.  As part of this review, Staff must 
demonstrate what works and what does not work in Summit County.  The 
progress of the County must be shown.   Director Jones said it is his goal to have 
the report completed no later than August each year.

Director Jones said that state wide, home values have climbed 14% over the past 
year, which is double the national rate.  For the first time in four decades, Utah is 
facing a housing shortage of over 50,000 units.  He said that 70% of the growth is 
natural increase; 30% is the result of net-migration from other states.   
 
Director Jones said this is a planning goal, not a production goal.  The State wants 
the County to lay out a plan that can reasonably meet this obligation.  A slide was 
shown of the 23 strategic items outlined by the State.  Summit County must be 
aligned with at least four of the strategies.   
 
Director Jones read the items.  He said Summit County is already doing some of 
these strategies; however, this language has not been included in the General Plan.  
The definition of “reasonable” has not been given.   
 
Director Jones said from 2000-2009, the population growth of Summit County rose 
by approximately 20%.  He described the employment rate (34%) for the same time 
period.  Employment is outpacing population growth.  The number of building 
permits varied during that time.  The median was 640 per year.    
 
Director Jones said from 2010 to 2019, the population growth slowed slightly.  It 
was 16.8%.  Employment growth dropped 1% to a 33% growth rate during that 
same period of time; however, the annual number of building permits averaged 
about 196 per year..  The growth rate in authorized residential building permits 
declined dramatically.  This was during the recession years.   
 
Director Jones said the 5 year population projections shows growth will continue at 
a rate of slightly over 1% a year, adding ~460 people annually.  Employment growth 
will continue to outpace population growth adding approximately 745 new jobs per 
year.  This creates a housing problem.  He said a surprising statistic centers on the
