3.     Deer Acres Plat C 6-lot Subdivision - Jaren and Alyssa Lynn
       a.    Staff Presentation
             Shauna presented the concept vicinity plat and said there have been 2 previous
             phases of this development over a number of years. The first phase was done by
             the applicant’s father, Wes Lynn. The second phase had a few lots. Phase 3 will
             be called “Plat C” and currently ends in a temporary turnaround. They would like
             to do a new turnaround that will connect to the frontage road on the east side of

            the freeway. This subdivision is in the R1 zone, and will need a minimum of 80 ft
            frontage and a minimum of ¼ acre.

     b.     Commission Discussion
            Karl Brough asked about the length of the road to the center of the cul-de-sac.
            Shauna stated that it’s actually a turnaround. Karl also asked if parking would be
            allowed in the turnaround. Shauna said that a 120 ft diameter would allow for
            parking. Karl asked Brett Ludlow about the elevation of the water tank to the
            north of the property, to which Ludlow offered an explanation.

     c.     Action Item- Motion
            Ann Peterson asked the Commissioners if they had any questions. Seeing none,
            Ann asked for a motion to accept the concept vicinity plat. Karl Brough made the
            motion to accept the vicinity plat for Deer Acres Plat C 6-lot Subdivision with the
            DRC notes dated 4/3/23.

            Motion: Karl Brough Second: Heather Robertson Outcome: Passed Unanimously

4.   222 Business Park 7-lot Subdivision - Alex and Jared Johnson

5.   Electric Power Project for the Neph City - CAT Wheeler
     a.     Staff Presentation

            Seth stated that the city has been working with UMPA, the agency that Nephi
     City
            buys its power from. They would like to install a small power generation plant
            next to Substation #2, which is on the south side of our city. It would use 2
            natural gas engines to generate about 5 Megawatts of power, which would help
            the city be more self-sufficient. It will be operated remotely from the Spanish Fork
            headquarters, with occasional in-person visits.

     b.     Commission Discussion
            Ann asked about the projected cost to the city. Seth responded that he wasn’t
            sure because UMPA is covering the cost. Cory asked about a drainage pond
            mentioned in the DRC notes, which isn’t reflected on the site plan. He
            emphasized how important it is to be concise and to be an example to everyone
            else that is required to do the same. He expected that the city would be held to a
            higher standard and in turn, would probably vote against this because of those
            discrepancies. Seth replied that in a technical sense, the City doesn’t have to
            follow its own subdivision code. Seth and Brett Ludlow addressed Cory’s
            concerns by stating the fact that the city is ultimately held responsible for any
            liabilities. Ann questioned why the ground-breaking was done before
            Commission discussion and approval. Seth stated that you can dig a hole without
            getting a permit.

     c.     Action Item - Motion
            Ann asked for a motion to accept the electric power project for the City. Heather
            made a motion to accept the electric power project for the city, with the DRC
            notes.

            Motion: Heather Robertson Second: John Ford Outcome: Motion Carries*

            *All Commissioners were in favor, with the exception of Cory Thomson. Ann
            requested a role call. As the majority was in favor, the motion carried.


6.   Title 11 Updates
     a.      Open Public Hearing
     Ann opened the Public Hearing at 7:34pm.

     b.      Staff Presentation
     Shauna spoke to several conversations she’s had with Chet and Hailee Ingram. They
     have recently subdivided a property with a few existing buildings. She described the
     uniqueness of this property, and the proposed plan for subdividing the property. She
     stated they met all requirements for exemption from subdivision except the item that
     deals with the need for city infrastructure. While the Ingrams agree with the need to curb
     and gutter in front of the new home that will be erected on the property, they disagree

     with having to curb and gutter in front of the existing home, which is part of the original
     city plat. Shauna’s argument is that even though there is a code in place, it has never
     been followed. Brett Ludlow spoke to the fact that he’s never seen it enforced in 40
     years. He went into a lengthy explanation of the historical aspect of how this code has
     been interpreted. (Full conversation is available via transcript and recording). He feels
     that Seth and Shauna interpret it differently than it has been generally interpreted in the
     past. He also mentioned Taylor Warburton- another resident that is being affected by
     this policy of code enforcement. Shauna recognizes what has been done in the past, but
     she is bound by the plain language of the code. She stated that she is fine either way,
     but the code (as it’s written now) doesn’t give her permission to exempt it. Much
     discussion was had about the current pros and cons of curb and gutter requirements.
     Chet Ingram explained what they are planning for their property, and the reason why
     putting curb and gutter in front of the existing structure would be a detriment to the
     drainage system. Taylor Warburton, while agreeing to do whatever the city requires of
     him, expressed his concerns about the code. BriAnna Nixon said that she works with
     another city that doesn’t require curb and gutter unless the subdivision contains more
     than 3 lots because the cost offset doesn’t make sense for the developer. She feels that
     in a situation like this, an exemption should occur. Fran clarified that this is a parent lot
     that has been grandfathered. Ann Peterson expressed the need to know what the
     benefit of the exemption would be to the city, not just the homeowner. Brett Ludlow
     explained how the storm drainage system is supposed to work and that the water has to
     go somewhere. On this particular lot curb and gutter will provide the water with nowhere
     to go, therefore flooding the neighboring lot. Cameron Linford asked Ann if she could
     see that there would be 3 tax generating benefits. (Many questions, concerns, historical
     facts, and explanations were expressed by several Commissioners, Seth Atkinson,
     Taylor Warburton and Brett Ludlow and are available via transcript and recording).
     Ultimately, Shauna stated that she understands things have been done differently in the
     past, but there needs to be a change to the code to reflect what has always been
     considered policy. Shauna also discussed a proposed subdivision by Brett Wilkey that
     would potentially be affected by this code.
