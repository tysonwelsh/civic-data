Proposed Resolution 2020-21 awarding arts grants for Fiscal Year 2020-21
and setting forth findings pursuant to Utah Code Annotated 10-8-2
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed arts grants awards for Fiscal
Year (FY) 2020-21 came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated each year, the Ogden City Council appropriates
grant funding to various local arts entities and programs. The Ogden Arts Advisory Committee and Administration review each
application and provide recommendations to the City Council. The FY 2021 budget includes $100,000 for arts grants.
          Ogden City Arts Coordinator Buckley summarized the memo and explained the Administration, along with the
Ogden City A1ts Advisory Committee, has submitted its recommendations for arts grants. She used the aid of a PowerPoint
presentation to summarize the grants recommendation; for FY 2020-21, $100,000 has been allocated for arts grants. The City              .I
Council's role in the grant process is to make the determination that the value of the tangible and intangible public benefits
that will result from the proposed grants are equal to or exceed the monetary value of the grants. This year 38 grant
applications were received. Upon review the Arts Grants Review Sub-Committee, and the full Arts Advisory Committee
recommends that 29 grant applications merit funding. Seven general support grants totaling $40,000, and 22 project support
grants totaling $60,000 for a total of$100,000. She presented a spreadsheet providing a brief summary of the general support
grants and project support grants, after which she asked for Council support of proposed Resolution 2020-21, awarding arts
grants for FY 2020-21.
          Council member Stephens asked how a general support grant is defmed. Ms. Buckley explained that general support
grants require the applicant to be a non-profit arts-based organization.

recipient was required to return the money to the City. Since the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic, rules have been adjusted
slightly to allow for an extension of the time for specific projects to be completed. The grant application cycle was from January
to March 2020, so many of the projects were contemplated before COVID-19; those applicants are being given flexibility to adjust
their project plans or scope responsive to health guidelines.

Proposed Ordinance 2020-32 amending the zoning map of Ogden City to
reclassify 1400 West and 1475 West Midland Drive from Manufacturing and
Industrial Zone (M-2} to Single Family Residential Zone {R-1-6}
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed zone change for properties
located at 1400 and 147 5 Midland Drive came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated a petition has been filed by
David Taotua to rezone property at 3048 South 1475 West from R-1-10 to M-2. In addition to the petitioner's property, the
Planning Commission is recommending the rezone of eight additional properties in an area south of Midland Drive between 1400
and 1475 West. The properties were pait of the 2018 West Ogden annexation and were zoned R-1-10 when incorporated due to
the residential use. Prior to annexation, the properties were zoned M-1 under Weber County's zoning. Mr. Taotua has indicated
that he wishes to construct a contractor's shop building on the property, which the R-1-10 zoning does not allow. In reviewing the
petition, Planning staff and the Planning Commission determined that the additional properties that had been zoned R-1-10 upon
annexation should be rezoned to M-2 along with the petitioner's property. Inquiries were made of the other property owners and
all who responded indicated they were in favor of the rezoning.
          Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
intent of the petition. The subject property is currently zoned R-1-10 and the petition's original proposal was to rezone the
property to the M-2 zoning designation to allow for development of a contractor shop building. This original proposal was
considered during the December 4, 2019 Planning Commission meeting and community members who own property within the
R-1-10 zone along 1400 West and 1475 West voiced their desires to rezone their property to M-2. Following the meeting,
property owners were notified and given the opportunity to provide a response to the proposal with the explanation that if the
property were rezoned, residential units would become nonconforming. Five of the eight property owners responded and indicated
they would like their property to be rezoned to the M-2 designation; combined, the residential properties within this R-1-10 zone
occupy a total of 3.9 acres of land. Prior to 2018, the property was unincorporated and zoned M-2 by Weber county; it was
annexed into the City in 2018 and consisted of25 parcels with 10 single-family homes along 1400 West and 1475 West. The
homes were zoned R-1-10 and the remainder of the property was zoned M-2. During the 2018 annexation process, the decision
regarding zoning took into consideration the avoidance ofnonconfonnity and the need to offer some protection from surrounding
manufacturing uses for the existing homes in the areas. New owners have purchased these homes and have entered the area
knowing that it is largely made up of manufacturing uses. Nonconfonning homes lose their right of use once they have been
vacant for a year. The areas to be rezoned serve as a transition between residential and manufacturing uses; there are currently two
buildings under construction along 1475 West: a manufacturing building that fronts Midland Drive; and a pole barn located
directly north of the applicant's property in a R-1-10 zone. Mr. Montgomery presented photographs of the applicant's property as
well as properties surrounding it and indicated the Planning Commission's consideration of the application was focused on
compliance with the City's General Plan and zoning policies; they considered whether the rezone creates an edge or appropriate
buffering between types of uses and if the rezone request reflects the character of the area. The Planning Commission voted
unanimously to rezone the entire R-1-10 area to M-2.
          Council member Stephens inquired as to the uses allowed in the M-2 zone. Mr. Montgomery noted the M-2 zone is the
City's heavy manufacturing zone; it allows for any type of manufacturing use, including storage, salvage yard, and auto body
shops. To the northeast of the property there is a construction/asphalt yard. To the southwest there is a recycling plant. The area is
truly a heavy manufacturing area. Council member Stephens inquired as some of the uses not allowed in the M-2 zone. Mr.
Montgomery stated that any use that deals with hazardous materials is prohibited, as well as garbage transfer stations, and
chemical induction. He reiterated that the subject properties are surrounded by M-2 uses.
          Chair Choberka introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2020-32, entitled:
                    "An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the zoning map of Ogden City, as adopted by Section 15-3-3 of
                    the Ogden Municipal Code, to reclassify as manufacturing and industrial zone (M-2) property heretofore
                    classified as single family residential zone (R-1-6), generally located at 1400 West and 1475 West Midland
                    Drive, and providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final passage."
          A copy of the proposed ordinance was deposited with the Deputy City Recorder and ordered that the City Recorder have
at least one copy available for public inspection in her office during all business hours.

Proposed Ordinance 2020-33 amending the Ogden Municipal Code
subsections 15-15-5. B and 15-36-5. B to modify the building design
standards for accessory buildings under 200 square feet in the East Central
Community
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed zoning text amendments for
design standards of small accessory structures in the East Central Plan area came before the Council for consideration. The memo
stated Ogden City Planning is proposing the creation of design standards for accessory structures smaller than 200 square feet in
the City's East Central Community Plan area. The City's existing ordinances for the East Central community require all
structures, including main residences, attached and detached garages and accessory structures regardless of size, to adhere to the
design standards. This creates problems for residents who wish to construct or purchase small sheds or accessory buildings. The
proposed amendments would allow a wider range to siding materials and roof styles for small accessory structures in the East
Central area in order to accommodate things like prefabricated sheds. The proposed design standards would apply only to
accessory buildings smaller than 200 square feet.
          Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the
proposed text amendments; items being presented to the Council for consideration include:
               • Does the proposed amendment increase the types of material considered for small accessory buildings in the
                   East Central Community?
               • Does the proposed amendment detract from the historic integrity established by the existing buildings in the
                   East Central Community?
               • Does it set design standards which appropriately regulate the appearance of small accessory buildings in the
                   East Central Community?
          The East Central Community is an area of unique historical development relative to lot sizes, building architectural
features, and building materials. It is encompassed within the Central Bench National Historic District. All new buildings are
required to meet building design standards allowing only:
               •   Face brick;
               • Hardiplank, wood-lap or tongue and groove wood siding that creates horizontal lines;
               • Brick, stone;
               •   Stucco or stucco appearing materials so long as there are breaks in the wall plane with brick; and/or
               • A combination of brick as the base level material around all four sides of a single family or two-family
                   dwelling and stucco appearing material wood or hardi plank type materials above the brick.
          The current ordinance limits options community members have to install small accessory buildings, such as pre-
manufactured sheds and staff feels that enforcing current ordinances on buildings under 200 square feet causes undue burden for
City enforcement staff. He provided photographic examples of the types of buildings that would not be permitted in the East
Central Community, noting that the building would be allowed in all other residential communities in the City. Accessory sheds
are typically placed in the rear yard behind a dwelling and, subsequently, are screened from street view. The visual impact is
minimal in most cases. The ordinance amendments proposed by staff include increasing exterior material permitted for accessory
buildings under 200 square feet, excluding garages and living spaces; and regulating locations for accessory structures. Allowed
accessory structure materials include the materials cited previously in the staff report, as well as the following:
               •   Vinyl/resin siding, glass, stucco or stucco appearing material;
               • Metal siding that has a baked enamel paint or vinyl coating.No galvanized or bare metal permitted;
               • Architectural metal when approved by the Planning Commission on a case by case basis.
          There is no requirement for siding or stucco to be horizontal in nature. Roofing materials allowed would include:
               •   Composition asphalt/fiberglass shingles;
               • Wood shakes;
               •   Slate, tile or similar appearing materials;
               •   Standing seam metal roof systems and metal shingles;
               •   Vinyl/resin materials if part of a premanufactured building; and
               •   Galvanized metal surfaces, reflective surfaces or reuse materials that are not originally designed as an exterior
                   wall or roof fmish material are not permitted.

    Proposed Ordinance 2020-34 amending the Ogden Municipal Code 15-39-
    8.A to revise square footage for townhomes in the Mixed Use (MU) Zone
              A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed zoning text amendments to
    reduce the minimum size oftownhomes in the Mixed Use (MU) zone came before the Council for consideration. The memo
    stated a petition has been filed by Joe Torman with Lotus Development to amend the unit size requirements for attached row
    homes/townhomes in the City's MU zone. The proposal would allow up to 25% of a row homes or townhomes in a development
    to have units no smaller than 940 square feet with the remaining 75% ofunits at least 1,300 square feet in size. City staff and the
    Planning Commission have also proposed language to limit the smaller units to one bedroom. The amendment would apply to
    projects in the MU zone, located between 18th Street and 20th Street and between Wall A venue and Washington Boulevard. Mr.
    Torman's project is located on the property west of the Bingham Cyclery and Slackwater Pizza.
              Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to smnmarize the
    proposed text amendments; items being presented to the Council for consideration include:
                   •   Is the amendment consistent with the General Plan?
                   • Does the amendment meet MU zoning requirements and policies?
                   • Is there a good reason for the amendment?
                   • Does the amendment allow for the implementation of the Ogden River Redevelopment Plan?
              Mr. Montgomery presented an elevation rendering provided by the applicant as an attachment ofhis proposal; the City's
    current ordinance requires that townhomes be a minimum of 1,300 square feet in size and development options are restricted. The
    purpose of the City's MU zone is to create a variety ofuses within a geographic area and the applicant is requesting to reduce the
    minimum size for townhomes to 940 square feet; he has proposed that 5 of the 30 units in his proposed project have a reduced
    size. This equates to 17% of the development. Staffs recommendation is to allow smaller units for up to 25% of the total
    development. This could be accomplished by considering the following text amendment: "attached row homes/townhomes of a
    minimum of one thousand three hundred (1,300) square feet per dwelling for seventy-five (75%) of the development. The other
    twenty-five percent (25%) of the row homes/ townhome development may have a minimum unit size ofnine hundred forty (940)
    square feet." The effects of the proposed ordinance amendment include increasing the variety of residential townhome units,
    improving the stability ofneighborhoods, increasing buildable space within developments resulting in increasing the property tax
    base, incentivizing quality development for interior floor plan design and finish, and having a neutral impact on parking pressures
    as larger units have the same parking standards as smaller units. Mr. Montgomery offered a comparison of market studies from
    2013 and 2019 to illustrate the manner in which unit size requirements have changed in the City, after which he noted that the
    City's General Plan and Central Business District (CBD) Plan are intended to do the following:
                   • Encourage development downtown;
                   • Diversify housing types for all ages and incomes;
                   • Accommodate 4,700 additional units in Ogden;
                   • Revise ordinances to make downtown successful;
)                  • Promote sound urban design to match existing downtown;
                   • Promote tax base and investment in downtown; and
                   • Provide increased density housing in downtown .
