       e. Informational Presentation on the Salt Lake County (SLCo) Public Safety Bond
          SLCo Human Services Director Kelly Colopy and SLCo Sheriff’s Office Chief Deputy for
          Corrections Bureau Matthew Dumont presented information about a bipartisan Public
          Safety Bond that would be on the ballot in November. Ms. Colopy said the bond aimed to :
          balance public safety and human services needs; realign and set up the SLCo Jail and
          Human Services systems for success; create structured and secure environments to
          reduce repeat offenses and put people on a path toward self-reliance; fund a critical
          missing element of the broader system; and replace outdated infrastructure and
          treatment programs. The total capital cost of the bond would be $507,000,000, with a
          $4.91 per month impact on the average residence, and $8.93 per month8 for businesses.

           Council Member Bloom commented on the current shortage of needed medical and
           mental health personnel. Ms. Colopy spoke of a five-year plan to build additional work

       b. Ordinance No. 24-43 amending West Jordan City Code section 1-4-1 and 1-4-2
          regarding Elected Official Compensation
          Council Office Director Alan Anderson explained changes in State law regarding elected
          official compensation, and noted elected official compensation was last increased in West
          Jordan in 2014. During a previous Committee of the Whole meeting, the Council directed
          staff to draft amendments that would set Council compensation at 20% of mayoral
          compensation and add an annual COLA linked to that given to City employees, with an
          effective date of July 1, 2025.

           Vice Chair Lamb said he believed the proposed amendments would avoid some of the
           negativity associated with discussions of elected official compensation.

           Vice Chair Lamb opened a public hearing at 7:10 pm.

           Comments:
           Steve Jones, West Jordan resident, had a problem with the proposed amendments. Mr.
           Jones suggested tying Council compensation to mayoral compensation was a conflict of
           interest, and expressed the opinion the process was not supposed to be easy. He said
           residents should be consulted to determine if increases were warranted, and said he did
           not personally get a COLA. Mr. Jones said he believed there was a long list of individuals
           who would like to sit on the dais.

           Jim Bird, West Jordan resident, said Council compensation probably did need to increase,
           but expressed the opinion that the path chosen was not appropriate. He suggested the
           start date for Council compensation should be after the election cycle. Mr. Bird said he
           knew Council Members put more time into Council work than a part-time job, but
           suggested the Council needed to rethink implementation.

           Vice Chair Lamb closed the public hearing at 7:15 pm.

           Council Member Whitelock read aloud a statement. She was not aware that Council
           Members were compensated when she was first elected. She had been told the City would
           need to pay more to get better candidates, but did not agree. Council Member Whitelock
           did not want the pay and benefits to be the reason someone ran for office and would
           prefer a committee of residents be tasked to study compensation and provide a
           recommendation. She said all residents who had reached out to her on the subject had
           not been in favor, and she would vote against the proposed amendment. She would rather
           the funds were used for road maintenance.

           Council Member Green did not have a problem with developing a policy for regular and
           appropriate compensation increases for the Council. However, he did not like the idea of
           tying Council compensation to mayoral compensation and would not be in favor of any
           pay increase unless benefits were addressed. He wanted Council compensation to be
           reasonable and planned to vote against the amendment as proposed.

           Council Member Bloom understood public perception was crucial in the process and
           appreciated dialog with residents. She believed an adjustment was needed, having not
           occurred in 11 years, and expressed concern that if nothing was done an even bigger
           change would be needed in four or five years. She would prefer something incremental
           that would not affect the budget as much as if the issue were put off again. Council
           Member Bloom would like to readdress the matter and find a solution that did not have
           the appearance of a conflict of interest.

           Council Member Shelton did not choose to run for City Council for the compensation. He
           did not want to appear self-serving and expressed the opinion that tying Council
           compensation to mayoral compensation with a COLA would be inappropriate but a vote
           regarding Council compensation was difficult. He liked the idea of considering options
           that did not involve mayoral compensation and would likely vote in favor of the proposed
           amendments if the COLA were removed.

           Council Member Bedore agreed the subject was difficult and noted that giving a raise to
           yourself was an odd thing. He said that while he had not realized he would be paid as a
           Council Member, he had also not realized just how much time he would put into the
           position. He felt that ten years without an increase was tough and hoped tying Council
           compensation to something that would make it less of a political issue. He agreed with
           pulling back, but stated something needed to be done to ensure the Council was fairly
           compensated. Council compensation should not be something held against Council
           Members. He suggested there would need to be specific criteria for any citizen committee
           involved in setting Council compensation and was not in favor of the COLA. He wanted a
           policy for setting Council compensation and hoped the Council could come together on
           the topic.

           Vice Chair Lamb felt further discussion was needed. He said a policy was in place to
           discuss mayoral compensation every four years, but there was no policy in place to ensure
           discussion of Council compensation.

 6. BUSINESS ITEMS
       a. Ordinance No. 24-41 Amending Certain Sections of Title 1, Chapter 15 (Elections),
          including qualifications for office, campaign finance disclosures, and use of city logos.
          (City Code Sections 1-15-2, 1-15-4, 1-15-8, and 1-15-9)
          City Recorder Tangee Sloan introduced proposed amendments to City Code regarding
          qualifications for office, campaign finance reports, use of City logo, compliance with Utah
          election laws, candidate filing fee, and grammatical/clerical changes.

           Ms. Sloan presented two options regarding financial disclosure s:
              • Option 1: Allows compliance up until 30 days before the next municipal candidate
                  filing period.
              • Option 2: Prohibit a candidate from filing a declaration of candidacy at the next
                  municipal election if any late or unfiled campaign finance report was more than
                  30 days late.

           Regarding use of the City logo, Ms. Sloan proposed the following:
              • Exception: incidental use in a background or on a person’s clothing in a video is
                 allowed.
              • Not permitted: on any election or campaign material to infer endorsement of a
                 candidate by the City.
              • Enforcement: procedures in Title 16, fines listed in the Consolidated Fee Schedule,
                 and possible pursuit of other consequences.

           Vice Chair Lamb preferred Option 2 regarding the campaign financial disclosure. Council
           Members Whitelock and Green expressed agreement.

           MOTION: Council Member Green moved to APPROVE Ordinance No. 24-41
                   amending Sections 1-15-2, 1-15-4, 1-15-8, and 1-15-9 regarding
                   Elections, including qualifications for office, campaign finance
                   disclosures, and use of city logos adopting Option 2.
