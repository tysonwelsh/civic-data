   1. 6:00 PM - Work Meeting (Fort Herriman Conference Room)
Chair Chris Berbert called the meeting to order at 6:06 p.m.

       1.1. Review of City Council decisions - Michael Maloy, City Planner
Planning Manager Clint Spencer informed the Commission of a request to allow electric scooters
within City limits. This discussion was taken to the Council, which did not have interest in having them
sporadically throughout the City.

The proposed J & J subdivision which had yet to be heard by the Planning Commission, required the
vacation of right-of-way property. This request was heard by the Council who supported vacating the
necessary right-of-way to accommodate the proposed subdivision.

Manager Spencer reported on the General Plan amendment for the Rockwell Landing project and noted
the Council reviewed the Planning Commission recommendation; however, subsequently, the
applicant provided information on how the corner parcel could develop commercially and meet the
associated standards. The applicant also presented a potential site plan including connection options.
Manager Spencer relayed the position of the Council after receiving the additional information was
more receptive to the original request. City Planner Michael Maloy interjected the update would be

Manager Spencer reported the Council reappointed the Planning Commission Alternates Darryl Fenn

       1.2. Review of Agenda Items
Planning Manager asked the Commission if they had any questions on the conditions for the Hidden
Oaks subdivision and noted there were so many requirements due to the desire of the developer to
move forward with the development.

In response from a question posed by Commissioner Jackson Ferguson, Assistant City Engineer Bryce
Terry offered an overview of the clear view ordinance amendment which would restrict the installation
of a tree or fence within a sixty-foot triangle of an intersection to provide safety measures for
oncoming traffic.

       1.3. Discussion and presentation of architectural metal panels as a finish material in the C-
            2 commercial zone
Planner II Sheldon Sowa reported the current development standards in the C-2 zone required building
facades have a minimum of 60-percent brick or stone on all elevations and the remaining exterior
materials could include glass, brick, stucco, stone, decorative blocks, or stone panels. He recalled a
previous meeting with the Commission where there was an appetite to expand the list of secondary
finish materials to include metal products. He presented metal panels which had different profiles
including corrugated and box ribbed, tile profiles, and panel profiles with finishes which would include
anodized, painted, simulated, and galvanized. The 10–30-year warranties for the metal products were
observed.

The commission requested staff to bring back a proposed ordinance to allow the metal products except
for the metal panel profiles.

       1.4. Training - Michael Maloy, City Planner
City Planner Maloy distributed land use training books and suggested the Commission hold a special
meeting or extend a scheduled meeting in order conduct necessary annual training.

        4.1.   Recommendation to Amend the City Ordinance (10-29-48) Concerning Visual
               Obstructions at Roadways. (Public Hearing)
               Applicant: Herriman City
               File No.: Z2022-072
Assistant City Engineer Bryce Terry explained the current ordinance relating to visual obstructions
prevented developers and property owners to place anything which would obstruct the view at an
intersection including fencing, landscaping, or any other obstruction over three feet in height which
would block the view of a driver trying to pull out of an intersection. Assistant City Engineer Terry felt
the current ordinance was outdated compared to the engineering standards and wanted to clarify the
vague language of where the clear view triangle was measured.

Commissioner Sickles explained on a small lot, potentially no trees would be allowed and suggested it
was extremely restrictive. Assistant City Engineer Terry relayed his desire to create a safer
environment for the community.

Chair Berbert opened the public hearing.

No comments were offered.

Chair Berbert closed the public hearing.

Commissioner Jacobson expressed concern about the number of trees which would need to be
removed to be in compliance with the ordinance. Chair Berbert asked if those intersections
Commissioner Jacobson was referencing, had visibility concerns. He indicated from a safety concern
to avoid the possibility of a child being hit and killed by a vehicle, the City would need to prepare the
best way possible. Commissioner Powell reported this proposal was a nationwide standard for clear
June 2, 2022 Planning Commission Meeting Minutes                                Page 9 of 10
view. Commissioner Jacobson asked if complaints received were as to where a tree could be planted
or if the visibility surrounding a specific intersection was unsafe. Assistant City Engineer Terry
responded complaints are often received due to a vehicle pulling out of an intersection and having
visibility concerns. He further explained the City had received complaints of near misses as well as
accidents which had been attributed to clear view issues. Commissioner Powell interjected vehicles
parking in the clear view area make it extremely difficult to see oncoming traffic when trying to pull
out of an intersection, even local residential intersections. Commissioner Sickles stated a tree trunk in
the clear view triangle was vastly different than a vehicle parked within the same area. Commissioner
Jacobson agreed. Assistant City Engineer Terry explained mature trees whose canopy would be higher
than seven feet above grade, would be allowed; however, it takes approximately ten years for trees to
reach that type of maturity.

Commissioner Sickles agreed with the visual obstructions including the shrubs and fencing made
sense; however, was concerned with removing trees in the clear view area. He wondered if exceptions
for trees could be considered. The Commission asked staff to research what the proposed amendment
would do to current neighborhoods. Assistant City Engineer Terry agreed and added he would also
discuss the ordinance with legal counsel to determine if existing neighborhoods would be considered
legal non-conforming unless there was a safety risk.

Commissioner Garcia moved to continue item 4.1. Amending the City Ordinance (10-29-48)
Concerning Visual Obstructions at Roadways to the City Council. Commissioner Sickles seconded the
motion.
