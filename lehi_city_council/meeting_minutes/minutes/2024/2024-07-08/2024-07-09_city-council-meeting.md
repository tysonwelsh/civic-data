8. Hadco-Pacific Annexation with Light Industrial zoning on 25.69 acres located at
   approximately 2100 North 1450 West.
   John David Hadfield was representing the applicant.

    a. Public Hearing
    Mayor Johnson opened the public hearing. There were no comments from the public.

    The Council and staff discussed the process of annexation, zoning designations, and the
    ability to impose mitigation measures on the property through the Annexation Agreement.
    There were concerns raised about the mitigation measures included in the annexation
    agreement, with some council members feeling they were overly burdensome and arbitrary.
    The applicant also expressed concerns with parts of the annexation agreement.

    The Council discussed the balance between protecting the rights of the property owner and
    the concerns of the neighboring residential properties regarding noise, dust, and other
    nuisances. There was debate about whether the appropriate place to address those concerns
    was through the annexation agreement or through the city's zoning ordinances and code.

    b. Consideration of Resolution #2024-38 approval of the Hadco-Pacific Annexation
    Agreement.

11. Public Hearing to receive input respective to the LBR study, report, and
    recommendation for the Creation of a New School District.
    Mayor Johnson opened the Public Hearing.

    Molly Barrington, Lehi resident - Expressed support for the potential school district split,
    stating it is being done to put the children first. She appreciates the Council doing the
    research so voters can make an informed decision.

    Katrina Cummins, Lehi resident - Supports a three-way split of the school district. Cited
    concerns the lack of community connection and ability to pass bonds in the large district.
    Believes smaller districts would allow for better school board representation.

    Zaina Cox, Lehi resident - Stated it is time for the district to split, as it is too large. Wants
    local representation and the ability to solve local problems.

    Steven Smith, Lehi resident and Alpine School District employee - Expressed concerns about
    the impact of a three-way split on special education services and programs like Horizon
    School that serve students with severe disabilities.



Lehi City Council Meeting                           5                                         July 9, 2024
    Rachel Coxumaya, Lehi resident - Cited a state audit report that found districts with over
    20,000 students are too large to properly audit. Suggested shared service agreements for
    special education as a potential solution.

    Christopher Condie, Lehi City Council - Thanked the public for their comments and stated he
    initially favored a different option but has changed his mind based on the analysis done by
    his colleagues. He supports putting the issue on the ballot.

    Councilor Stallings stated that she appreciates all comments and noted that if it’s decided to
    place this on the ballot for voters, then they should carefully weigh all pros and cons.

    Kira Beck, Lehi resident and Alpine School District employee – Encouraged the Council to
    put this on the ballot for residents to choose. Concerned that local needs are not being met
    but also concerned about funding options for special programs in a smaller district.

    Representative Brady Brammer, Highland resident – Observed the Alpine School Board
    members work tirelessly but are unable to make constitutes happy due to current
    configurations. Believes that the interlocal agreement will foster connectivity and teamwork
    between communities. Thinks that teacher pay and benefits will not decrease with a district
    split based on other splits.

    Aubrey Sorensen, Lehi resident – Concerned that at risk youth and alternative programs will
    be cut due to funding constraints in a smaller district.

    Brianna Hugh, Highland Resident, teaches in Lehi – Concerned about district’s financial
    needs and the potential impact to students if the district splits. Recognized that there are
    different needs across the district. The unknown is concerning for district employees.

    Anthony Kretchmer, Orem resident – Dissatisfied with how Orem is being treated by Alpine
    School District. Commends Lehi for being part of an interlocal agreement to form a new
    district.

    Alyssa Facer, Lehi resident – concerned about the consolidation of power in the large Alpine
    School District and its negative impact on students and teachers. Is also concerned with how
    this may affect students. Supports putting the issue of the ballot.

    Mayor Johnson closed the public hearing.

    Councilmembers emphasized the importance of accurate information, listening to both pros
    and cons, and ensuring that concerns are heard and addressed. There was a strong focus on
    making informed decisions, especially regarding the upcoming ballot, with the
    acknowledgment that voters will ultimately decide. The Council expressed a commitment to
    a fair and thoughtful process, encouraging continued dialogue and community involvement.
