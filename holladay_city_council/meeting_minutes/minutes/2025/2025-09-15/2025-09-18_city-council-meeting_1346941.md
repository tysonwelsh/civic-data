Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District – New Director.
Mayor Dahle expressed support for long-time General Manager, Pam Roberts, and New General
Manager, Evan Tyrrell. It was noted that Ms. Roberts is retiring after 28 years of public service. She
shared information about her work background and stated that she feels comfortable with Mr. Tyrrell
taking over the position, as he is competent and qualified. She provided details about some of the
cost-saving measures that the district has implemented recently.

Mr. Tyrrell introduced himself to the City Council. He earned dual Master’s Degrees from Indiana
University. His home State is Indiana and he has degrees in both Public Affairs and Environmental
Science. He has worked in a variety of public sector roles throughout his career. More recently, he
worked as the Solid Waste and Fleet Services Director for the City of Flagstaff. Prior to that, he
served as the Executive Director for the Solid Waste Special Service District in Moab. He shared
details about his prior positions and was excited to work for the Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling
District moving forward.

He reported that Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling provides residential waste and recycling
collection services for over 86,000 residents throughout Salt Lake County, including residents in the
City of Holladay. He is excited to work with the organization and expressed appreciation for the
smooth transition that is taking place.

Council Member Gray expressed appreciation for the work that is done by Wasatch Front Waste and
Recycling District. She reported that Mr. Tyrrell was easily the most qualified candidate interviewed,
even though there were a lot of wonderful applicants. The district is lucky to have him as an
employee. Council Member Gray stated that the City of Holladay is well served by employees at the
district.

The City Council was concerned about growing mental health occurrences in the community. Some
of the other UPD partners were having challenges with their budgets, so Millcreek and the City of

services, probation, hospital social work, psychiatric social work, and skilled nursing social work.

Mr. Golden has done an incredible job so far.

Council Member Fotheringham expressed support for the program and the focus on mental health.

thanked Chief Hoyal and Mr. Golden for presenting this information.

Mayor Dahle reported that the next public hearing relates to a proposed Text Amendment to Title
13.86.020 to add 2052 East Arbor Lane to Holladay’s Historic Designation List. He explained that

Mayor Dahle opened the public hearing at 6:35 pm..

Kim Duffy- 2195 E Walker Lane. She stated that Camille Pierce and Steven Glaser have owned the
home for approximately 34 years and it is a piece of old Holladay that has been preserved beautifully.
In addition, the owners have been stewards of the land and the creek that runs through it. She hopes
the City of Holladay will reward owners like this for their efforts.

Camillie Pierce and Steve Glaser - 2052 East Arbor Lane. Ms. Pierce explained that an application
has been made to be included on Holladay’s Historic Designation List. She shared an image of the
back of the home. The intention has always been to maintain the original look and feel as much as
possible. Most of the windows are original and the original upper story remains. In 1939, Paul and
Betty Dinwoodey had their primary residence at this location. It is an English Cottage Style/Colonial
Revival house that was built on several acres. The features reflected the times, as the closets were
small due to the inability to afford material goods during the Great Depression. Almost every room
in the house has windows to look out into nature. As the family grew and their resources grew, the
home was expanded in 1956, using a Mid-Century Modern theme. This newer style provided
designated spaces, such as a large dining room, an additional bedroom, and two additional bathrooms.
Some images were shared with the City Council for reference.

Ms. Pierce shared additional historical information about the Dinwoodey family. The Dinwoodey
Home reflects the family history, as seen in the variety of woods that have been used: pine, fir, oak,
and mahogany. The current 1.5 acres is an expansive area surrounded by mature vegetation, which
is backed by Big Cottonwood Creek. Paul Dinwoodey was considered a master gardener, so changes
to the landscape have been made with the guidance of Landscape Architects and other professionals.
Ms. Pierce further discussed the home and explained that as long as they live on the property, there
is a commitment to maintain the home and the yard. A lot of work has been done on the house over
the years, including replacing the plumbing, mitigating radon, seismic reinforcement work, roof
replacement, repainting, and efforts to make the house more energy efficient.

There were no further comments. The public hearing was closed.

Mr. Gross explained that the City will lease the existing ground and/or buildings to the LBA and the
LBA then leases it back to the City. It also leases the constructed and financed improvements. There
is a construct under State Law that allows the City to finance this way. Essentially, the City creates

the LBA and leases what it currently owns. That is sub-leased back to the City and the improvements
are leased. Discussions were had about the LBA.

Ms. Lewis stated that the LBA issues the bonds. Should a future City Council choose not to make an
annual lease payment, then the Trustee, on behalf of the bondholders, will come in and seize all of
that. Under State Law, cities have a variety of ways to approach financing. For example, it is possible
to pledge sales tax or franchise fees. There could also be a General Obligation Bond or a pledge of
property taxes. There are many different options available to cities. However, if the intention is to
lease a property, there is a prohibition on entering into long-term debt. Since there is a right to walk
away and not make the lease payment, it is not considered long-term debt. This preserves the sales
tax for harder-to-finance projects. She shared some example scenarios with the City Council.

V.      Consideration of LBA 2025-03 – Authorizing the Issuance and Sale of Not More Than
        $11,500,000 Aggregate Principal Amount of Lease Revenue Bonds; and Related Matters.
Chair Dahle noted that the issuance is for not more than $11,500,000, but it is anticipated that
$10,200,000 to $10,300,000 will be used. Laura Lewis explained that a certain amount is anticipated
to construct the facilities and handle the City Hall renovations, but other costs need to be taken into
account. In the bond, it is possible to build the additional costs into the actual bonds. That is the
reason that the par amount is set higher than the $10 million or so for construction. The additives
include a Debt Service Reserve Fund, which is money borrowed that sits in an account with a Trustee.
There are Federal Tax Laws that govern how high that can be set. There is the $10 million for
construction, the reserve fund, and then the cost of issuance, which pays for the underwriter, ratings,
Legal Counsel, and so on. Ms. Lewis has advised that the par amount be set at $11.5 million. The
interest rate is not to exceed 6.5%, there is a maximum maturity of 25 years set, and a maximum
discount from par of 3%. The Resolution also sets forth that there will be designated Officers. The
Chair and Vice Chair of the LBA can grant the final approval within those parameters. There will
need to be a public hearing held, which is scheduled to take place on November 6, 2025.

Trustee Fotheringham asked about the reserve fund. He wanted to understand if that is interest-
bearing, which was confirmed. Ms. Lewis explained that it is interest-bearing, but there are
restrictions. Typically, the interest earnings on that do not match what the interest rate is on the bond.
A few years ago, short-term rates were high, and it was possible to earn some positive arbitrage. That
reserve fund is released with the final payment, so it effectively will make the final payment. Trustee
Fotheringham noted that interest rates currently have downward pressure. He asked if it would make
sense to consider buying down the rate by half a point. Ms. Lewis stated that it is not something that
can occur in this situation, but it might be possible to negotiate a slightly more aggressive call feature.
It is standard in the market that bonds will be callable at 10 years at par. While it is always possible
to negotiate an earlier call, it is a question of how much that will cost. Ms. Lewis reported that it
might be possible to negotiate a nine-year par call without a cost. Discussions were had about the
rates.

Trustee Fotheringham moved to APPROVE LBA 2025-03 – Authorizing the Issuance and Sale of
