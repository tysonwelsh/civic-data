III.   Public Comments
Allison Teller -2270 East 4500 South. She is a public educator and works as an Instructional
Specialist and School Counselor in local schools and is very passionate about her work. She wanted
to address the issue of law enforcement accountability. She commend on an incident on June 2, she
drove past an incident on 3900 South where a black man was lying face down on the sidewalk in his
underwear surrounded by a large number of law enforcement officials. She questioned whether this
person was treated fairly or if they experienced an excessive amount of force or brutality. She was
seeking a clearer understanding of policy measures the City of Holladay has taken. A list of
questions was presented to the City Council, which included:

   •   What initiatives are currently underway to make sure law enforcement officers in Holladay
       are treating people of color fairly.
   •   Do officers have training on bias-free policing?
   •   Are officers trained in responding to variable mental health conditions and situations related
       to those?
   •   What processes are in place to respond to internal and external complaints of misconduct.

Mayor Dahle reported that emails and documents had already been forwarded to the Police Chief,
Justin Hoyal. The City contracts with the Unified Police Department (“UPD”), an agency that
serves 200,000 to 250,000 people throughout the County. Mayor Dahle encouraged her to
participate in the upcoming UPD board meeting.

Several Council Members expressed gratitude for Ms. Teller’s email. They felt it raised questions
worthy of discussion, particularly as it relates to the influence the City has on policy in the City of
Holladay and contracted use of the UPD.

V.      Consideration of Ordinance 2020-09 Imposing a Temporary Zoning Regulation Relating
        to Retaining Walls Pursuant to Section 13.76.700.
Paul Allred, Community Development Dir. reported that an ordinance that requires certain types of
retaining walls in setback areas between the home and property are reviewed by the Planning
Commission. However, this was not found to be an efficient use of time. Mr. Allred considered
this to be an engineering issue that should instead be handled by the Technical Review Committee
(“TRC”). He recommended approval of a temporary regulation regarding Planning Commission
public hearings and certain types of retaining walls. A moratorium was recommended to prevent
additional retaining wall applications from being submitted to the Planning Commission.

Mayor Dahle believed that a temporary moratorium would be helpful as it could take several
months for the Planning Commission and City Council public hearings to be held.

Council Member Fotheringham asked if the moratorium would mean that no one could build a
retaining wall for the summer if they had not already obtained a permit. Mr. Allred stated that if
and when the City Council adopts a new ordinance during the moratorium period, the moratorium
would end. He estimated it would take approximately 60 days but would depend on the speed of
the Council. Mr. Allred clarified that the moratorium would not mean that retaining walls could not
be built in the City of Holladay. Rather, only certain kinds, certain sizes, and in certain locations.

Council Member Fotheringham reported that certain types of retaining walls are already being
approved administratively by the TRC. He asked if the purpose of the temporary regulation was for

retaining walls that exceed certain dimensions and if the goal is to extend what could be dealt with
over the counter. Mr. Allred confirmed this to be the case.

Mr. Allred stated that there was only one completed retaining wall application waiting to go to the
Planning Commission. However, because of a busy construction season, there could be more in the
future. He believed the moratorium would pause some projects but it would not be a huge
disruption. Council Member Fotheringham asked why a moratorium was necessary if there are so
few applications. Mr. Allred explained that each application takes a long time to process and a
great deal of City resources are spent dealing with these types of issues. He also believed that it did
not make sense for the Planning Commission to review technical engineering matters.

Mayor Dahle asked Mr. Allred if he expected any pushback at the Planning Commission public
hearing on June 16, 2020. Mr. Allred did not anticipate much public input. Mayor Dahle expressed
concern with the timeline of the vote. He stated that a public hearing could be scheduled with the
City Council for July 9, 2020, with a vote on July 16, 2020. Mayor Dahle questioned if it was
worthwhile to put a moratorium in place for one month.

Mr. Allred had a similar conversation with City Attorney, Todd Godfrey. Mr. Godfrey’s opinion
was that once the Planning Commission amendment is in motion, the City would be covered and
applications would not have to be accepted. That meant that a moratorium would not be necessary.
The Council Members agreed that a moratorium did not seem to be worthwhile for such a short
time.

Council Member Gibbons wondered about potential issues with neighbors and residential
complaints. He asked about the appeal process for an over the counter decision made by the TRC.
Mr. Allred explained that there is an appeal process. Residents or citizens who feel that an error has
occurred could appeal the decision with the Administrative Hearing Officer or the Planning
Commission.

Council Member Quinn asked if any pending cases fall into that category and wondered what would
happen to pending cases once the ordinance is passed. Mr. Allred stated that only one completed
application is currently in process, along with an application that was not yet complete. He reported
that once the process has begun to change the law with public hearings, any pending cases could be
on hold until a new ordinance is approved.

Mayor Dahle believed the issue could be addressed quickly and put on the books by July 16, 2020.
Due to the timeline, he did not believe a motion was necessary for this particular ordinance.

Council Member Fotheringham moved to table consideration of Ordinance 2020-09. Council
Member Petersen seconded the motion. The Council roll call vote was as follows: Council
Members Fotheringham, Gibbons, Durham, Petersen, Quinn and Mayor Dahle in favor. Ordinance
2020-09 was tabled by a unanimous vote.

        b.    Overview of Major Holladay City Contracts.
Ms. Chamness provide a presentation for the Council that reviewed three major contracts used in
the City. They included the UFA, UPD, and Salt Lake County Public Works.

She reported that the UFA is an Interlocal Agency that was created in 2004 with 443 full-time
firefighters and a total of 694 employees. The Board of Directors is comprised of elected officials
from the jurisdictions that are part of the fire authority. The board is responsible for the selection of
the Fire Chief. She reviewed the services provided by UFA that include but are not limited to: Fire
suppression, fire response, transport, fire rescue service, hazardous materials response, fire
investigation. The total cost of these services is approximately $2.6 million or approximately 17%
of the City budget. Ms. Chamness discussed the various fire stations that respond to calls for
service in the City.

Ms. Chamness reported that the UPD is also an Interlocal Agency. The City of Holladay
participates along with other cities including Taylorsville, Millcreek, and Midvale. She stated that
the UPD is governed by a Board of Directors. The elected Sheriff serves as the CEO of the
organization and each precinct has a Chief Deputy. There are two groups of services. The first are
the services at the precinct level, which include:

   •   Patrol;
   •   Property crimes investigations;
   •   Narcotics;
   •   Traffic enforcement;
   •   School Resource Officers;
   •   Domestic Violence Investigations; and
   •   Victim Advocacy
   •   Code Enforcement (for the City of Holladay).

Shared services include:
   • Violent Crimes Investigations;
   • Special Victims’ Investigations;
   • SWAT responses;
   • K9 responses;
   • Accident reconstruction;

   •   Technical services
   •   Records Management; and
   •   Administration.

The total cost of these services is $5.3 million or approximately one-third of the City’s budget. $3.8
million represents the precinct services and $1.5 million represents shared services. The shared
services are allocated by a formula at 20% for the Unincorporated County with the remaining 80%
divided between member cities and townships, based on a formula of 70% calls for services, 20%
based on population, and 10% based on taxable value.

Mayor Dahle discussed previous issues when cities like Herriman and Riverton left the UPD. This
created issues and required budgets to be reduced or additional funds allocated to absorb the costs
within the municipalities. He reported that when he was elected 6 ½ years ago, the Regional
Services component was approximately 28-29% of the overall budget. It is now approximately
34%. Mayor Dahle also stated that there is instability within the UPD due to municipalities
conducting feasibility studies.

Council Member Fotheringham considered this to be a critical issue since cities that abandoned the
UPD removed their access to higher-end shared services and as a result, those services now need to
be paid for by a smaller group. Council Member Gibbons believed in the model but agreed that the
City Council may need to look at other options at some point. Mayor Dahle felt committed to the
model as well and believed the newer levels of transparency and improved leadership had done a
good job of correcting certain issues.

Ms. Chamness stated that the City contracts directly with Salt Lake County for public works
services. The Salt Lake County Mayor and the Salt Lake County Council have oversight. Unlike
the Unified Fire Agency and the Unified Police Department, there is no board. For the City of
Holladay, Salt Lake County Public Works provides the following services:

   •   Snow Removal services;
   •   A certain amount of square footage for overlay, chip sealing, crack sealing, pothole repairs,
       street sweeping, streetlight maintenance and construction;
   •   Preventative and regular maintenance of traffic signals; and
   •   Administrative areas that handle hiring and training for these functions.

Previously, the City would only pay for services rendered, however, last year the model changed to
one that was based on the cost per snowplow route. That model would also purchase a certain
amount of pavement maintenance during the summer. The total cost of those services is $2.3
million.

Mayor Dahle asked if it would be possible to allocate additional funds if necessary. Ms. Chamness
stated that it is possible to contract additional services directly and due to competitive construction
rates, the rates would be more or less equivalent.

Ms. Chamness briefly highlighted other services the City contracts for. They make up an additional

        c.      Discussion on Tentative Budgets.
Mayor Dahle had questions regarding the Fund Balance. He stated that due to producing more
revenue than expected in 2018 and not spending the entire expense budget, this had produced
excess funding. He believed it would be beneficial to hold onto that balance for additional stability
or in the unlikely case the City needs to transition away from the UPD. Even with the potential tax
increase next year, it would be about one year before the added revenue becomes available. He felt
that it would be beneficial in the next fiscal year to put the fund balance into roads, so there would
be visual improvements to the infrastructure.

Council Members Petersen and Gibbons wanted to see Cost of Living Allowances (“COLA”) in
place for their employees. Council Member Petersen brought up concerns related to funds for Plaza
improvements. Council Member Fotheringham suggested discussing the issue at the next budgeting
season.

Council Member Durham asked if Ms. Chamness had new data on revenue, especially as it relates
to sales tax. Ms. Chamness stated that she would not have new data until June 20 or June 21, 2020.

Mayor Dahle asked if Ms. Chamness was able to make adjustments to the budget to include $500 to
the Interfaith Council and an additional $2,500 to the Chamber. Ms. Chamness stated that she had,
and along with the COLA changes, it was an additional $34,000 to use the Fund Balance for next
year. She also noted that there may be additional changes to include money from the Coronavirus
Aid, Relief, and Economic Security (“CARES”) Act and revenue from the Certified Tax Rate,
which could generate additional funding. Due to these potential changes, it was possible that the
use of the Fund Balance may come down.

Mayor Dahle questioned whether there would be enough money available to properly maintain park
spaces. Ms. Chamness stated that one of the priorities for the Public Services Director position that
is currently on hold is the development of a long-term maintenance plan for both the plaza and
parks. Mayor Dahle asked it would be possible to allocate funds into unrestricted capital projects.
Ms. Chamness confirmed that this was the case. Mayor Dahle thanked Ms. Chamness for her
excellent work on the budget and stated that the City Council can make final comments or ask
questions once adjustments have been made or at the meeting on June 18, 2020.
