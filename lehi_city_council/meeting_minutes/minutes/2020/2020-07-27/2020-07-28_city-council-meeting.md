2. Presentations and Reports
2.1 Waste Management Recycling Program.
Blake Leonelli, with Waste Management, reported on the city’s recycling program. He stated
that there are some good things that the residents are doing with recycling, but there is still room
for improvement. He stated that plastic bags are the biggest issue right now. If they could
educate residents not to throw the bags in the recycling that would make a big difference. Mayor
Johnson stated that he’s impressed with the work being done by the Lehi Environmental
Sustainability Committee to educate residents.

4. Consideration of Ordinance #41-2020, the Center Pointe Towns zone change, changing
   the zoning on approximately 2.25 -acres of property located at 1627 Center Pointe Drive
   from Commercial to R-3. (Tabled from the July 14th City Council meeting)
   Ken Burg was representing the applicant. There was discussion regarding the traffic study that
   was conducted, and suggestions were made to improve the safety of that road such as
   constructing a raised median.

    Councilor Koivisto stated that she was concerned about having high density in this location.
    She didn’t feel like it was conducive to the area and that there are better locations than this for
    high density. Councilor Albrecht stated that many of the concerns were addressed when they
    did the General Plan Amendment. Councilor Koivisto stated for the record that she is not
    against high density, she just wants it done correctly

                completion of all Development Review Committee and Planning Commission
                comments. Councilor Albrecht seconded the motion.

    Roll Call Vote: Councilor Albrecht, Yes; Councilor Southwick, Yes; Councilor Hancock,
    Yes; and Councilor Koivisto, No. The motion passed 3-1.


5. Consideration of Concept approval for the Traverse Mountain Multi-Family concept, a
   317-unit apartment development located at 3851 North Traverse Mountain Blvd.
   Devin Belnap was representing the applicant. He explained that this is for the density transfer
   that was done in the recent amendment to the Area Plan.

    Councilor’s Albrecht and Hancock expressed concerns with moving forward on the concept
    plan approval when the Area Plan has not been recorded. Mr. Struthers stated that they are
    close to getting it recorded, and that they are working with the applicant to resolve some issues.
    The Council agreed that they would prefer to table the item until the Area Plan has been
    recorded.

    Motion:     Councilor Hancock moved to table this item and directed staff to continue working
                with the applicant to get the Area Plan Amendment recorded. Councilor Koivisto
                seconded the motion.

    Roll Call Vote: Councilor Albrecht, Yes; Councilor Southwick, Yes; Councilor Hancock,
    Yes; and Councilor Koivisto, Yes. The motion passed unanimously.

9. Consideration of Ordinance #43-2020, a General Plan Amendment on approximately
   23.9-acres located at approximately 1402 West 900 North, changing the land use
   designation from VLDRA (very low density residential agriculture) to VLDR (very low
   density residential).
   Danny Gutierrez and Mike Dubois were the applicants. Mr. Gutierrez felt like other properties
   in the area have been granted higher density and felt that this area may not be a priority. He
Lehi City Council Meeting                        3                                     July 28, 2020
    felt that their request was reasonable and that there are similar size lots in the area. Mr. Dubois
    stated that they really aren’t asking for very much and these will be more affordable than the
    ½ lots. He also said that ½ acre lots are a lot to take care of for most people.

    Mr. Struthers stated that the property to the south is zoned R-1-22, but the lots are smaller than
    a ½ acre because the developer made improvements to the Waste Ditch for flood control. He
    said that the higher density zones nearby, including the nearby R-1-8 that was rezoned in 1998,
    was done before they decided that this would be a rural and less dense area.

    Councilor Southwick stated that some residents were concerned with the traffic that would
    come on 1220 North, because that road is small. Mr. Gutierrez expects most of the traffic to
    exit on 900 North. He stated that once they stub a road to the east and west, they are only
    asking for a difference of 7 lots. He also said that staff has requested that they bring water and
    sewer to the homes on 1220 North. Councilor Hancock stated that improvements to 1220 North
    would be helpful.

    Mayor Johnson wanted to clarify that with a PUD or a PRD, the residents will get some
    amenities with smaller lot sizes, but that doesn’t mean more density. Mr. Struthers stated that
    if they leave it zoned R-1-22, and the developer does a PUD, then they would get some
    dedicated open space, but if it’s changed to .33 acre lots, then there’s no open space
    requirement.

    Mr. Gutierrez stated that there isn’t a big difference from .5 acre to .33 acre lots and that most
    people are priced out of the ½ lot sizes and can’t afford it. He said most people don’t want farm
    animals and Mr. Dubois would put in the CC&Rs that there would be no animal rights.

    Councilor Albrecht stated that the rural feel is going away regardless of what the lot sizes are.
    Mr. Gutierrez stated that many ½ lots don’t even have animals.

    Councilor Hancock inquired about how much of the roadway would be left unwidened on 1220
    North. He suggested that it be a condition of approval to have the entire length of that road
    widened. Mr. Struthers stated that it would probably need to be a mix of the city doing the
    improvements in front of the existing homes and the rest would be done by future
    developments. Mr. Hancock suggested a condition of approval be that the road be widened to
    700 West and that the utilities be improved. This could be what grants the additional density.

    There was discussion regarding where the roads would be stubbed. It was suggested that there
    not be an egress to 1220 North. Mr. Struthers stated that they would need to have that access
    and also they cannot have double fronting lots, so some of the homes will need to face 1220
    North.

    Mr. Gutierrez stated that they are proposing this change because people don’t know how to
    take care of ½ acre lots and they are unaffordable.

    Mr. Struthers stated that this area stays rural in the new draft General Plan. He said that if this
    is approved then there is a high likelihood that the surrounding land would all want to do the
    same thing, so this would set a large precedent and they should consider what that would do to
    the surrounding properties. Mr. Struthers stated that there is an opportunity for some bonus

    they typically don’t allow density bonuses for that. They could do that with a development

    Mr. Gutierrez stated that if he is asked to make utility improvements to 1220 North, it would
