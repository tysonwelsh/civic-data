2. Discussion regarding the possible creation of a Neighborhood Mixed Use-I Zoning 
District- Patrick Putt, Community Development Director  
 
 NEIGHBORHOOD MIXED USE ZONE 
Director Putt said there is already a mixed use zone in the Code.  It is the Community 
Commercial (CC) zone.  As currently written, it doesn’t have the needed tools to create 
the desired type of design.  The CC zone allows outdoor storage land uses.  He explained 
these are things like storage yards.   
 
Director Putt said the NMU-I zone has been refined to create better tools in the right 
locations.  He listed the areas that might be eligible for the NMU-I zone as: Kimball 
Junction, Lower Pinebrook, Rasmussen Road, Bitner Road, The Canyons, Quinn’s 
Junction, and Silver Creek. 
 
Director Putt said the NMU-II zone is intended for large acreages on the perimeter of an 
existing development area.  It is targeted for a legislative discussion by the County 
Council.  It may be along trails, have transit, or high use areas. 
 
The NMU-I zone will be smaller in size, approximately five acres.  It could be located by 
commercial/residential areas.  This zone is about solving problems, better connection, 
Snyderville Basin Planning Commission 
August 28, 2018 
Page 14 of 22 
 
and integrating development.  He listed the criteria needed for property to be 
considered as NMU-I:   
 
 The property should already be identified in the General Plan as a mixed use 
area.  Examples of these were given.   
 The properties would need to be zoned Rural Residential (RR), Community 
Commercial (CC), Service Commercial (SC), Neighborhood Commercial (NC), or 
Town Center (TC).   
 Benchmarks would be established of when an application for the NMU-I zone 
can be sought.     
 There will be a Land Use Table with specific uses.  
 There would be a height limit of 45 feet.  Compact density might require greater 
heights. 
 15% open space would be required.  This is a lower requirement.  This would 
allow for more highly developed areas.  Public spaces will be encouraged.    
 Should there be a density cap?  The current CC zone doesn’t have a density cap 
but is controlled by building height, setbacks, parking requirements, and open 
space requirements.  Should this be the formula used?    
 
Another approach would be to create a yardstick that would identify or predict

(FAR) of 0.6 to 1 was used in a development exercise a few months ago.  He 
explained this is a ratio that permits a certain amount of building area in relation 
to the amount of land area.
