WORK MEETING - 6:30 p.m.

1. Discussion of agenda items, correspondence, and/or future agenda items
Mayor Silvestrini updated the Council on the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints
property the City was interested in purchasing for park use. He read an email from the realtor
stating that the Church would list the property for thirty days then entertain offers. He said
city staff engaged with Reagan Advertising and they have confirmed that they will not file any
billboard applications while negotiations with the City took place, though they already had a
pending application in to replace a sign on Highland Dr.

Mike Winder said one of the steps in creating a community reinvestment area is a blight
study, which may allow the use of eminent domain. He said Canyon Rim Commons and
Olympus Hills community reinvestment areas will not have a blight study done but Millcreek
Center will. The study was conducted by Lewis Young Robertson & Burningham and there is
enough blight in the Millcreek Center area to qualify. He invited the public to come and

Millcreek City Council Meeting Minutes 22 October 2018 Page 2 of 11

inspect the blight study at City Hall or on the City’s website for the next thirty days. For
residents that are in the blighted area, Winder said this will not affect their property taxes,
values, or records. He said there is a list of legal criteria in the blight study that blighted
properties are based on. Winder said the Council will be reluctant to use eminent domain and
it would be used as a last resort. Council Member Marchant clarified that the single advantage
of using eminent domain is to avoid having someone hold out on the price for the property,
such as what happened with the Grand America Hotel in Salt Lake City. Winder confirmed
and said this study puts the City on solid legal footing for the Community Reinvestment Area.
The study encompasses the entire Millcreek Center Community Reinvestment area, not just
the City center in the wedge. Council Member Jackson asked how many residences were
included in the area. Winder said they purposefully excluded neighborhoods that expressed
concern about being included. Mayor Silvestrini said residents expressed concern near Riches
Avenue so they were removed from the study. He explained that if the residences were in the
area, then tax increment money could be used to fix sidewalks and the like but since they
asked to be removed, improvements will not be done there. Winder said the boundary was
broad because it is a twenty-year tool. Mayor Silvestrini said he has spoken to a couple
property owners and the City’s intent is not to use eminent domain except for municipal
purposes, such as a street.

Mayor Silvestrini said he attended the retreat for the Central Wasatch Commission and they
discussed how to fund continued operations and fund public engagement. They discussed a
pilot shuttle service in Millcreek Canyon which involves using surplus UTA vans and a third-
party contractor driving service. He said the expected financial contribution for the City to be
on the Central Wasatch Commission would be $75,000 a year based on population. The
Central Wasatch Commission does not have the ability to levy a tax.

Council Member Catten said at the Mosquito Abatement Board meeting last week there was a
resolution about the Millcreek Community Reinvestment Agency. She said the Board did not
approve the resolution because they did not understand it. Winder said the Tax Entity
Committee make-up is that Salt Lake County gets two representatives, Millcreek gets two
representatives, Granite School District gets two representatives, the State School Board gets
one representative, and the other smaller entities together get one representative. The Unified
Fire Service Area appointed Ifo Pili to represent the other entities because they are the largest
small entity. Council Member Catten requested that Ifo Pili be introduced to the Mosquito
Abatement Board.

Mayor Silvestrini said the Unified Police Department (UPD) Board met last week in a retreat.
There were presentations from the captains that preside over the elements of pooled services.
They talked about the advantages of those services being pooled instead of being pushed out
to precincts, except for victim advocates. Lisa Dudley, CFO of UPD, is reconstructing the
budget to create accounts within the general fund. Mayor Silvestrini said any unexpended
portion of a precinct’s budget will go into a fund balance for that particular precinct. The fund
balance could be used as a credit towards the next year’s contract. Mayor Silvestrini said the
City pays for pool services based on a formula that is driven 70% by the number of cases that
are opened, 20% on population, and 10% on real property value. There were adverse impacts
to cities by changing the formula, so it will be revisited. Mayor Silvestrini said Millcreek is in
a good position because Millcreek’s cost did not change much based on the different factors
of changing the formula.

2. Planning Matters
2.1 Discussion and Consideration of Ordinance 18-60, Amending Millcreek Code
Chapter 19.73 Regarding the Town Center Overlay Zone
Mayor Silvestrini said the Council has initially reviewed this. Francis Lilly said items 2.1
and 2.2 come in a box set. The first item is an amendment to the Town Center Overlay
Zone to allow the Planning Commission to approve additional height subject to a
conditional use permit for potential development located on Gunn Avenue between
Highland Drive and Richmond Street. The second item is adoption of a development
agreement between Millcreek, Solstice Development, and Cottonwood at Brickyard LLC
regarding the development of projects located at 3089 S. Richmond Street and 1291-1315
Gunn Avenue. The developers at Gunn Avenue and 1300 E., Project 1, received a
conditional use permit for 188-unit mixed use building with a height limited at 40 feet.
Staff and the developers had a conversation about the image and future for Gunn Avenue.
The developers proposed changes to their projects. The entitled project for 188 units, and
11,181 square feet of commercial space is now proposed to be 330 units, 13,000 square
feet of commercial space. The 40-foot height limit will increase to 67-75 feet. The

exchange is that the developers are providing twice the recommended commercial
frontage along Gunn Avenue, angled parking on Gunn Avenue, height to 72 feet with the
lower end closer to Villa Theatre on the east, and a setback of 13 feet from the curb to the
building wall with an eight-foot sidewalk. The street cross section is in the development
agreement to accomplish a unified streetscape.

The second project is on the corner of Gunn Avenue and Highland Dr. These developers
collaborated with the developers on the other end of Gunn Avenue and have used the
same architect. Lilly said the developers are adding 8,500 square feet of commercial
space beyond what is required by ordinance, frontage on Highland Drive, angled parking,
and varied facade. The facade will be made from durable materials. The existing base
commercial zone allows for building heights up to 40 feet and would allow up to around
348 units. The applicants are proposing an additional 232 units, an additional 10,319
square feet of commercial space to total 28,000 square feet of commercial space, 50
percent additional commercial frontage beyond what is required on Highland Drive and
1300 East, and approximately twice as much on-street parking. Lilly showed the Council
a street site plan. The developers will enhance the Mountair streetscape by purchasing the
property and handing it to the City and the residents of Mountair will help to design it.
One of the developers has offered to donate a water wheel entry feature that will
welcome people to Millcreek. The developers came together at relatively the same time
and were able to work together. There is also a provision for a plaza in private ownership
on the south side of the Cottonwood Brickyard project site, but it will have public
functionality during daylight hours.

Mayor Silvestrini asked about bike lanes. Lilly said the streetscape does not call out a
separate bike lane, but the city center master plan will address where bicycle
infrastructure should go. The developers have agreed to utilize plaza space as a mid-block
access from Elgin Avenue to potentially 3300 South. The developers are also agreeing to
chip in to improve pedestrian crossing at Gunn Avenue and Richmond Street. The traffic
and parking study that the City does needs to be cognizant about pedestrian traffic and
safety. The long-term solution for pedestrian safety and bicycles will be contemplated in
the master plan.

The development agreement provides for the following:
1. An improvement to the Mountair Streetscape, to be constructed by the developer
with the input of community members. The Mountair Streetscape improvements will be
turned over to the city for ownership and maintenance. The developers agree to pay for
the improvements and pay for the maintenance for several years.
2. The developers will construct a public plaza at the south end of Project 1, to owned
and maintained by the developer. The plaza will be publicly accessible by virtue of a
public access easement. The design of the plaza will be approved by the City.
3. A Gunn Avenue streetscape that accommodates angled parking, which would
provide twice the parking than a parallel parking arrangement allows for. If the city is
to require commercial spaces on the ground floors of mixed-use buildings, the parking
needs to be designed to accommodate those uses. The City will ensure that the parking
is limited to 2-hour use during business hours.
4. The developers will construct the angled parking and dedicate it to the City.

5. The developers agree to impose parking limits in rental agreements, prohibiting
residents and guests from parking east of Highland Drive.

6. The developers agree to commercial space in excess of what is required by ordinance
— 100 percent of the ground floors of their buildings facing Richmond Street and
Highland Drive, respectively.

7. The developers agree to building designs that exceed what is required by ordinance.
In response to resident concerns about timeless materials and quality design, the
developers are proposing a series of materials that are durable and reflect the area’s
brick-making heritage. Moreover, the developers will not be using stucco or EIFS for
the visible portions of their buildings — these are materials found commonly on
multifamily buildings in Utah.

8. The developers agree to provide some funding a pedestrian safety enhancements.
The developers offered $5,000 in funds, and staff is recommending at least $10,000 in
funds.

Lilly and staff acknowledge that these projects will effect a significant transition for the
neighborhood, but this transition is in keeping with the intention of the General Plan, and,
through a development agreement, the City believes that the following benefits can be
obtained:
1. An improvement of the Mountair Streetscape - a long-neglected open space that will
enhance an existing buffer between commercial and residential uses.
2. Buildings that showcase quality design.
3. 28,000 square feet of commercial space, including commercial space along the
entirety of the project frontages on Highland Drive and Richmond Street, in excess of
what is required by code.
4. A Gunn Avenue streetscape that can accommodate roughly 50 on-street parking
stalls between the two projects, roughly doubling the parking that was planned and
approved by the earlier iteration of Project 1.
5. A significant catalyst to launching the City Center redevelopment and creating
helpful momentum as the City Center redevelops.
6. Nearly $100 million of investment in a new Community Reinvestment Area that do
not require any tax increment financing (TIF), but will generate significant tax
increment for the City to utilize elsewhere in the City Center redevelopment for years
to come.
7. Hundreds of new residents to increase the viability and success of supporting
existing retail and attracting additional retail areas in the City Center

Lilly said the city center has a lot of underutilized retail and to revitalize it, the City needs
to support residential development where it can be tolerated. A number of meetings were
held with the Mountair residents. Three of the four community councils voted to
unanimously recommend this item and the Millcreek Community Council had a tie vote,
4-4. Information about this project has been communicated to the public in the City’s
newsletters, and via email. Lilly said the Planning Commission unanimously
recommended approval, but they did express concern about the exterior design of the
project. There have been changes to the design in attempt to respond to the Planning
Commission’s concern. The building on Richmond has a stepback a hundred feet in and
along the street there are pedestrian overhangs. The designers have reoriented the open
