1B—- road construction and reconstruction is prohibited

1B-1 — recommended for wilderness designation in the forest plan and where road construction

10 — road construction and reconstruction is not prohibited (permanent roads not permitted;

Cache County Council
12-04-2018

Director Runhaar said his office worked closely with the Forest Service on the project. The
areas were looked at regarding topography and vegetation. Adjustments will be submitted to
the state and the state is building a statewide petition to submit to the Forest Service.

Chairman Erickson opened the Public Hearing and invited public comment.

Kari Prescott, J D Rhea, Bruce Pendery, Barbara Middleton, David Rosenberg, Kim Sullivan and Bryan

motion. The vote was unanimous, 7-0.

areas. Runhaar responded, “No.” These areas are not being removed from the roadless rule
designation; the change is within the designation allowing temporary roads. Eventually, the
county will have to approve a document notifying the state of the proposed adjustments. The
end result is the federal government will have to review and accept or reject recommendations
from the state.

Council member Worthen, in reference to Mr. Rhea’s comments, asked that a recommendation
for enforcement language be included. Director Runhaar said he knows the language exists,
but not sure where. He will act on Worthen’s suggestion.

Cache County Council
12-04-2018

Economic Development Director of Logan, presented a request for the county's participation in
a newly created South Main River community Project Area. Wasatch Development Group had
a proposed RDA project of 6 % acres and Logan City being a part of the taxing entity cluster,
added property to the project area for a total of 34 acres facilitated by an existing South Main
RDA that has about 4 % years to termination. Logan wants to capitalize on a 100 West street
extension and optimize the river location to make it more of a community asset/benefit with trail
connections. The current county revenue on the property is $32,000.00 a year on an $18
million tax base. During the 15-year interim the county will receive $350,000.00 and after the
15-year time period it will receive $130,000.00 a year. The trigger year is proposed as 2020 or
2021. Seventy percent will go to the RDA agency and thirty percent to the taxing entities split
by formula.

Chairman Erickson opened the Public Hearing and invited public comment. There was none.

ACTION: Motion by Council member Worthen to close the Public Hearing. Ward
seconded the motion. The vote was unanimous, 7-0.

In answer to Council member Worthen’s questions, Jensen said there will not be any properties
benefitting from the project and if a property owner wants to pull off a project, it will be fielded by
the Economic Development Committee with the Redevelopment Agency Board as the
determining body.

Council member Merrill asked what negative impact this will have on other property owners in
the area. Bracken Atkinson, Wasatch Development, replied there will be none. The only impact
will be if a property owner decides to sell, their property will have a higher value.

Mayor Daines pointed out instead of the usual 20 years; the county’s return will come in 15
years.

Chairman Erickson verified that oversight comes through the Clerk/Auditor’s office and the RDA
must file a November 1* report to all taxing entities detailing the status of the project.

RECORD OF DECISION
6800 SOUTH REZONE

WHEREAS, the “County Land Use Development and Management Act,” Utah Code Ann. §17-27a-101
ef seq., as amended (the “Act”), provides that each county may enact a land use ordinance and a zoning
map establishing regulations for land use and development, and;

WHEREAS, pursuant to the Act, the County’s Planning Commission (the “Planning Commission”) shall
prepare and recommend to the County’s legislative body, following a public hearing, a proposal that
represents the Planning Commission’s recommendations for zoning the area within the county, and;

WHEREAS, on November 1, 2018 at 5:50 p.m., the Planning Commission held a public hearing for a
request to rezone parcel 10-046-0010, 15 acres of property, from the Agricultural (A-10) Zone to the
Rural 2 (RU2) Zone, which meeting was preceded by all required legal notice and at which time all
interested parties were given the opportunity to provide written or oral comment concerning the
proposed rezone, and at which meeting a recommendation of denial was provided to the County Council
for final action, and;

WHEREAS, on November 27, 2018 at 5:40 p.m., the County Council held a public hearing to consider
any comments regarding the proposed rezone. The County Council accepted all comments, and;

WHEREAS, after careful consideration of the recommendation of the Planning Commission, comments
at the public hearing and other public meetings where such proposed rezone was discussed, and the
information provided by county staff, the Council has determined that it is not in the best interest of the
citizens of Cache County to approve said rezone:

NOW THEREFORE, on this the 4" of December, 2018, the Cache County Council denies the 6800 South

Rezone based on the following conclusions:

1. The Cache County Comprehensive Plan does not specifically address the Rural 2 (RU2) Zone and
does not make recommendations as to where the zone should be located. An update to the County’s
General Plan is necessary to better determine appropriate locations for this higher density zone in
unincorporated county areas not adjacent to a municipality.

2. The location of the proposed rezone would set a precedent for increased density and development
along this corridor.

Subject: 4600 West Design Exception request

Description
A request has been made by Jed Willets for a Design Exception on county road 4600 West (Exhibit
A), in conjunction with the proposed Wellsville Rising Subdivision (Exhibit B).

Design Exception Requirements
The Cache County Manual of Roadway Design and Construction Standards (Road Manual) section
1.8 Authority and Design Exceptions, specifies that:
“Full justification and documentation must be provided explaining the reasoning as to why the
roadway standards cannot be met, why an alternative design or construction method can meet the
intent of the roadway standards, and including any other relevant information.”

Proponent Justification and Staff Response
Justification 1:
This is a rural road because if provides access to farms, other agricultural uses, and dispersed rural
residences.

Staff Response to Justification 1:

A. This is not a Rural road. The Road Manual requires that county roadways serving 4 or more
single family dwellings meet the minimum standards for a Local Road. In this instance, the
structural requirements for a Local Road area a 22’ wide paved surface, 1° wide gravel shoulder,
14” of subbase, 6” of road base, and 2.5” of asphalt.

B. 4600 West currently serves 5 single family dwellings and agricultural property. Additional
development on 4600 West must meet or exceed the minimum Road Manual requirements for a
Local Road.

C. The depth of the structural fill and surface material of this section of 4600 West is unknown and
a core of that section of roadway must be completed to determine if the fill and surfacing meets
the minimum standard of the Road Manual.

Justification 2:
Heavy farm equipment that uses the same road has a more significant impact on the road than the
impact from four additional single family dwellings.

Staff Response to Justification 2:

A. Use of, and impacts to, the county road are cumulative, and the proposed 4-lot subdivision adds
additional use beyond the existing residential and agricultural use.

B. No supporting data or information has been provided by the applicant to substantiate the
justification.
