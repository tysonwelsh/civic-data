    Proposed Zone Text Amendments - Design Standards for Accessory
    Structures in East Central Plan Area.
              Council Deputy Director Symes explained Ogden City Planning is proposing the creation of design standards for
    accessory structures smaller than 200 square feet in the City's East Central Community Plan area. The City's existing ordinances
    for the East Central community require all structures, including main residences, attached and detached garages and accessory
    structures regardless of size, to adhere to the design standards. This creates problems for residents who wish to construct or
    purchase small sheds or accessory buildings. The proposed amendments would allow a wider range of siding materials and roof
    styles for small accessory structures in the East Central area to accommodate things like prefabricated sheds. The proposed design
    standards would apply only to accessory buildings smaller than 200 square feet.
              Deputy Planning Manager Spencer used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the proposed text
    amendments; items being presented to the Council for consideration include:
                  •    Does the proposed amendment increase the types of material considered for small accessory buildings in the
                       East Central Community?
                  •    Does the proposed amendment detract from the historic integrity established by the existing buildings in the
                       East Central Community?
                  • Does it set design standards which appropriately regulate the appearance of small accessory buildings in the
                       East Central Community?
)             Mr. Spencer noted that the East Central Community is an area of unique historical development relative to lot sizes, •
    building architectural features, and building materials. It is encompassed within the Central Bench national historic district. All
    new buildings are required to meet building design standards allowing only:
                  •    Face brick;
                  • Hardiplank, wood-lap or tongue and groove wood siding that creates horizontal lines;

              •     Brick, stone;
              •     Stucco or stucco appearing materials so long as there are breaks in the wall plane with brick; and/or
              •    A combination of brick as the base level material around all four sides of a single family or two-family
                   dwelling and stucco appearing material wood or hardi plank type materials above the brick.
          The cunent ordinance limits options community members have to install small accessory buildings, such as pre-
manufactured sheds. Staff feels that enforcing current ordinances on buildings under 200 square feet causes undue burden for City
enforcement staff. He provided photographic examples of the types of buildings that would not be permitted in the East Central
zone, noting that the building would be allowed in all other residential communities in the City. Accessory sheds are typically
placed in the rear yard behind a dwelling and, subsequently, are screened from street view. The visual impact is minimal in most
cases. The ordinance amendments proposed by staff include increasing exterior material permitted for accessory buildings under
200 square feet, excluding garages and living spaces; and regulating locations for accessory structures. Allowed accessory
structure materials include the materials cited previously in the staff report, as well as the following:
               •   Vinyl/resin siding, glass, stucco or stucco appearing material;
               • Metal siding that has a baked enamel paint or vinyl coating. No galvanized or bare metal pennitted;
               • Architectural metal when approved by the planning commission on a case by case basis.
         There is no requirement for siding or stucco to be horizontal in nature. Roofing materials allowed would include:
               •   Composition asphalt/fiberglass shingles;
               •   Wood shakes;
               •    Slate, tile or similar appearing materials;
               •    Standing seam metal roof systems and metal shingles;
               • Viny I/resin materials if part of a premanufactured building; and
               •   Galvanized metal surfaces, reflective surfaces or reuse materials that are not originally designed as an exterior
                   wall or roof finish material are not permitted.
         Mr. Spencer then presented a diagram illustrating the areas of building lots that accessory buildings would be allowed.
He concluded that the Planning Commission voted unanimously to recommend approval of an ordinance modifying the exterior
building, design, placement, and material restrictions for accessory buildings under 200 square feet in the East Central
Community.
         Council member Hyer asked if there are any garages in the East Central zone that are less than 200 square feet. Mr.
Spencer stated he suspects there may be some that are smaller than 200 square feet, but he noted that this ordinance should be
applied to new construction, not existing structures.
         Council member White asked if accessory structures may be attached to the home or if this ordinance will only apply to
detached structures. Mr. Spencer stated that if a structure is going to be attached to a home in the East Central Community, that
structure would be seen as a building addition and would be required to match the home materials allowed in the East Central
zone.
         Chair Choberka asked if existing structures will be grandfathered under ordinances that were in place at the time the
structure was constructed. Mr. Spencer confirmed.
         Mr. Symes stated that if the Council is comfortable proceeding, action on the proposed ordinance can be scheduled for
the August 4, 2020 business meeting.

Proposed Zone Text Amendments - Reduction in Minimum Size of
Townhomes in Mixed Use Zone (MU)
          A petition has been filed by Joe Torman with Lotus Development to amend the unit size requirements for attached row
homes/townhomes in the City's Mixed Use (MU) zone. The proposal would allow up to 25% ofrow homes or townhomes in a
development to have units no smaller than 940 square feet with the remaining 75% ofunits at least 1,300 square feet in size. City
staff and the Planning Commission have also proposed language to limit the smaller units to one bedroom. The amendment would
apply to projects in the MU zone, located between 18th Street and 20th Street and between Wall Avenue and Washington
Boulevard. Mr. Torman's project is located on the property west of the Bingham Cyclery and Slackwater Pizza.
          Deputy Planning Manager Spencer used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the proposed text
amendments; items being presented to the Council for consideration include:
               •   Is the amendment consistent with the General Plan?
               •   Does the amendment meet MU zoning requirements and policies?
               •   Is there good reason for the amendment?
               • Does the amendment allow for the implementation of the Ogden River Redevelopment Plan?
          Mr. Spencer presented an elevation rendering provided by the applicant as an accompaniment ofhis proposal; the City's
current ordinance requires that townhomes be a minimum of 1,300 square feet in size and development options are restricted. The
purpose of the City's Mixed-Use Zone is to create a variety of uses within a geographic area and the applicant is requesting to
reduce the minimum size for townhomes to 940 square feet; he has proposed that 5 of the 30 units in his pending project have a
reduced size. This equates to 17 percent of the development. Staffs recommendation is to allow smaller units for up to 25 percent

    of the total development. This could be accomplished by considering the following text amendment: "attached row
    homes/townhomes of a minimum ofone thousand three hundred (1,300) square feet per dwelling for seventy-five (7 5%) of the
    development. The other twenty-five percent (25%) of the row homes/ townhome development may have a minimum unit size of
    nine hundred forty (940) square feet." The effects of the proposed ordinance amendment include increasing the variety of
    residential townhome units, improving the stability of neighborhoods, increasing buildable space within developments resulting in
    increasing the property tax base, incentivizing quality development for interior floor plan design and finish, and having a neutral
    impact on parking pressures as larger units have the same parking standards as smaller units.
              Council member Hyer asked how the City can be sure that the quality of a unit will increase in conjunction with a
    decrease in square footage of the unit. Mr. Spencer stated that one of the selling points of the developer pursuing this amendment
    is that he is able to spend more money on the finishes of the interior of a unit if the minimwn size is decreased. Council member
    Hyer stated he understands that, but this ordinance amendment will apply to all property that is regulated by this ordinance and it
    is important to consider any future project that will be impacted by this amendment.
              Joe Tarman, Lotus Development, stated that the primary expense in a unit is for the kitchen and bathroom areas and those
    spaces will likely be the same size in both a small unit and large unit; what will be lost as a result of reduced unit size is likely a
    bedroom and possibly a bathroom space. However, he is likely spending more on the smaller units when considering cost per
    square foot. The biggest reason for pursuing the ordinance amendment is to allow for a diversified offering for people that want to
    live in the development, but do not need two or three bedrooms. He stated that a smaller unit is not a lesser unit.
               Council member Stephens asked how the 940 square foot minimum size was detennined. Mr. Tonnan stated that 940
    square feet is the most popular size and most leased size of units that he has built in some of his developments in Salt Lake City.
              Council member Hyer acknowledged that the smaller unit may be easier to market and lease; he simply asked that staff
    and the Council keep in mind that this ordinance amendment will not apply just to the applicant's project, but to all properties
    within the zone. Chair Choberka stated that smaller unit size does not necessarily equate to lesser quality in her opinion.
              Mr. Spencer then offered a comparison of market studies from 2013 and 2019 to illustrate the manner in which unit size
    requirements have changed in the City.
               Chair Choberka asked ifit is necessary to have an assigned number for minimum unit size; if the developer or another
    applicant is interested in building a unit smaller than 940 square feet, they would need to seek further ordinance amendments from
    the Council. Mr. Spencer noted that this amendment would only apply to townhomes; the minimum unit size for an apartment in
    the City is 716 square feet.
              Mr. Spencer then noted that the City's General Plan and Central Business District (CBD) Plan are intended to do the
    following:
                    • Encourage development downtown;
                    • Diversify housing types for all ages, incomes;
                    • Accommodate 4,700 additional units in Ogden;
                    • Revise ordinances to make downtown successful;
                    • Promote sound urban design to match existing downtown;
                    •    Promote tax base and investment in downtown; and

               Additionally, the Ogden River Redevelopment Plan calls for a mix ofresidential and cmmnercial type uses. The proposal

    bedroom requirement to allow flexibility and to maintain the character of the ordinance, The Planning Commission then voted
    unanimously to approve the petition with staff recommendations allowing up to 25 percent oftownhome units in the Ogden River

Council Business
          Rules/Norms Updates: Council Policy Analyst Mabey explained the City Council has established Rules and Norms to
provide an overview of the Council's purpose, vision, goals, priorities, expectations and relationships, and how the Council
conducts its day-to-day business and meetings. The Council generally reviews and considers possible changes to its Rules and
Norms at the first of each calendar and fiscal year. The proposed changes to the Rules and Nonns are intended to address the
following:
               •    Further outlining ethics and conflict of interest standards;
               •    Incorporating specific guidelines for electronic meetings;
               •    Proper use of straw polling in compliance with Utah law;
               • Allowing adjournment into a closed executive session prior to or following a regular meeting during a
                    separately noticed special meeting;
               • Removal of budget priorities which are adopted and included with the final budget document; and
               •    Minor stylistic and typographical changes.
          There was a brief discussion regarding the adjustments relative to ethics and conflicts of interest, with City Attorney
Williams noting that the City's legal counsel will be happy to help any Council member determine ifthere is a conflict of interest
relative to a Council member's or his/her immediate family member participating in a discretionary or non-discretionary program.
          Brief discussion also centered on the amendments relating to the properuse of straw polling and the timing ofconducting
a straw poll for a given item; Council member Hyer wondered whether it would be helpful to connect the straw poll regulations to
the process governing individual Council member initiative actions. Chair Choberka recognized that may be helpful, but noted
that straw polling can also be used for items that Administration may be interested in pursuing, but for which they would like to
know if Council will entertain the concept before spending too much time and effort. Council member Hyer agreed.
          Ms. Mabey noted that this item can be scheduled for action during the August 4, 2020 business meeting.
          Marshall White Center Advisory Board: Council member Stephens stated he heard form a resident in the community
about concerns regarding the process for selecting individuals to be appointed to the Marshall N. White Center (MWC) Advisory
Board. He asked for information about the application and selection process for that Board. Chair Choberka stated that Council
leadership has discussed this issue with Administration and she invited Council Executive Director Eller-Smith to relay the
infonnation provided by Administration. Ms. Eller-Smith stated the Council's role in the process is to provide advice and consent
for appointments. There has been some work done by Administration to adjust their committee recruitment process to ensure that
they are getting good information to the community and generating interest in the positions. She stated that she feels that more
improvements can be made, but it is her understanding that Administration is in the process of assembling a list of individuals
they would like to appoint and that will be provided to the Council in the next several weeks.
          Council member Stephens stated he would like to understand Administration's recruitment, interviewing, and selection
process. He asked if applicants are notified of the status of their application at any point in the process. This information would be
helpful to him in replying to comments he hears from constituents.
          Chief Administrative Officer Johnson stated that the City will notify all applicants of the status of their application. He
confirmed that recommended appointments will be sent to the Council in the next few weeks. Council member Stephens asked if
applicants who are not selected have been or will be notified, to which Mr. Johnson answered yes. Council member Stephens
asked if that is the case for all committees. Mr. Johnson stated that many committees commonly recommend individuals to be
appointed versus an open application process.
          Council member Nadolski suggested that Administration seek input from the Diversity Commission regarding the
selection of MWC Advisory Committee members. The Diversity Commission has asked for that opportunity and he supports it.
Council member Stephens agreed.
          Chair Choberka stated she is happy to hear that 17 people applied to be appointed to the Board, but noted that she would
like to see a more open process for the recruitment of members for this type of body. Diversity is very important for this group as
it is important to pursue broad representation of the community. There was brief discussion about the role of the Advisory Board
and the value that individuals with different social and professional backgrounds can provide to the group.
