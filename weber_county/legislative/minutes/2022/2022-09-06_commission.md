D. THOUGHT OF THE DAY: Commissioner Harvey: You know in our capacities as elected officials we have the
privilege of sometimes uniting man and wife to marriage, to be able to officiate at those wonderful gatherings. There
are times we get to counsel others on whether they should get married or should they not. Some young ones say it’s
too expensive, too hard to get married. There is a poem I use for those who wonder if it’s time or not for marriage. It
goes something like this:
                                                      The bride, white of hair, stooped over her cane
                                                      Her footsteps uncertain need guiding
                                                      While down the opposite church aisle with his one toothless grin
                                                      The bridegroom in wheelchair came riding
                                                      Well who is this elderly couple thus wed
                                                      Well you find when you’ve closely explored it
                                                      That here is that rare, conservative pair
                                                      That waited ‘til they could afford it
You know, when you find that right person, you just commit yourself to that person. The finances will work themselves
out. I’ve always found that two horses are more efficient pulling a heavy load than one.

F. PRESENTATION OF INVESTIGATOR OF THE YEAR AWARD TO DETECTIVE STEVEN M. HANEY. PRESENTER(S):
   COLD CASE COALITION
     Jason Jensen (Representative & Co-Founder of the Cold Case Coalition): We have gratitude to present this award
     and recognize one of your public servants. We are grateful for the members of the Ortiz family who are here, some
     of which traveled from out of state to be here. It took a couple of years of hard work to close this cold case. We
     wanted to recognize Detective Steven M. Haney for clearing a cold case of the 1969 murder of Leroy Ortiz, one
     of 400 cold cases in the state of Utah.
     Commissioner Jenkins: Detective, from the Commission, we want to congratulate you. It makes us proud when
     people such as yourself go above and beyond the call of duty. It makes us proud to say you are employed by Weber
     County. Mr Jensen we really thank you for spending your time in this organization and what you do.
     Commissioner Froerer: I read about this in the paper and was amazed at the diligence put forth by the Detective;
     50 years is a long time to dig that out. The family now has final closure they would not have had without his great
     work. Congratulations Detective. It’s people like you that make our state, our community, our county what it is.
     Commissioner Harvey: “Above and beyond” is not even close to what you’ve done. By your actions and deciding
     to do a lot more, you’ve not only honored the Ortiz family, but you’ve honored your family, your co-workers.
     Thank you so much for that extra (effort).

    2. APPROVAL OF ORDINANCE 2022-24 FOR A DEVELOPMENT AGREEMENT AND A REZONE
       FROM A-1 TO R-1-10 ON APPROXIMATELY 19.47 ACRES.
         Steve Burton (Planning): This item was presented in a public hearing on April 5th with a decision held until
         the Western Weber General Plan was adopted. The development is located at 2851 W North Plain City Rd.
         Approximately 20 acres currently in A-1 zone. 58 lots at about 10k square feet each with approximately 80
         feet of frontage. At the request of Planning and County Commissions, the developer was to tie in to the Nielsen
         Homes project to the south. Development Agreement will prohibit future property owners from protesting
         annexation to Plain City. Staff recommends following the recommendations of Western Weber Planning
         Commission:
         1. Owner will enter a Development Agreement showing agreed upon street and trail connection to the
            subdivision to the south.
         2. Development agreement will contain a statement prohibiting future property owners from protesting
            annexation to Plain City at any point in the future.
         Commissioner Froerer: I do not see any proposed trails in this plat.
         Mr Burton: There is no exhibit that explicitly shows it but the development agreement talks about a 10 foot
         wide asphalt pathway like the one proposed in the Nielsen subdivision. The trails will be part of the right away
         shown.
         Commissioner Harvey moved to approve Ordinance 2022-24 for a development agreement and a rezone from
         A-1 to R-1-10 on approximately 19.47 acres; Commissioner Freorer seconded.
         Roll Call Vote: Commissioner Froerer – aye; Commissioner Harvey – aye; Chair Jenkins – no

    3. APPROVE A CONTRACT WITH ZIONS BANK COMMERCIAL CARD PROGRAM – ZIONS
       WILL PROVIDE PURCHASING CARD SERVICES TO THE COUNTY AND PAY A REBATE
       BASED ON THE AMOUNT OF SPENDING.
         Scott Parke (Comptroller): Current vendor’s Purchasing card software has not been updated in ten years.
         Zions Bank has a strong platform allowing us to better monitor expenditures; it allows us to store images
         of receipts and attach them immediately in lieu of physical attachment. Instead of paying for this service Zions
         will pay us a rebate based on spending levels with no out-of-pocket cost to the county. Last year’s spending
         levels would have provided a $56,000 rebate.

         Commissioner Froerer moved to approve the contract with Zions Bank Commercial Card Program – Zions will
         provide purchasing card services to the county and pay a rebate based on the amount of spending; Commissioner
         Harvey seconded.
         Commissioner Froerer – aye; Commissioner Harvey – aye; Chair Jenkins – aye
