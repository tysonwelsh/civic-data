WORK SESSION – 4:00 PM

Call to Order

Mayor Buroker called the Work Session to order at 4:03 PM and conducted a roll call.
Councilmembers Haymond, Johnson, McDougal, Pierucci and Smith were present.

Presentations

       Edge Homes Park Design Presentation

Brandon Watson, Edge Homes, presented plans for a new 10-acre park in the Mountain Ridge
development area, located south of 13400 South and east of Mountain View Corridor. The
proposed park includes eight pickleball courts, shade structures, playground areas, splash pad,
multi-use play fields, and parking on both the north and south ends.

Dan Egan, Big T Recreation, the playground designer, described a large, inclusive play area
featuring a central structure and a variety of interactive and accessible equipment. The design
focused on durability, low maintenance, and creating an inviting space for families and the broader
community.

Discussion Items

         Budget Study Session on Personnel Requests

Nick Geer, Administrative Services Director, presented requests for personnel including the
following: IT technician, animal shelter technician, partial FTE count for facilities, and two water
distribution operators.

Mr. Geer presented a request from the police department to add a 12th step to the officer pay scale,
consistent with the existing 4% step structure, to address the growing number of officers who had
reached the current top step and were no longer eligible for step increases. He explained that the
change was intended to improve retention, remain competitive with surrounding agencies, and
provide retirement-eligible compensation increases, while emphasizing that this would likely
represent the final step addition.

         EV Stations Feedback

Matt Cassel, City Engineer, reported that the City received grant funding through the Wasatch
Regional Council’s carbon reduction program to install three to four EV charging stations, with the
City contributing a portion of the cost, and requested Council feedback on whether the stations
should be free or pay-as-you-go. A majority of the Council recommended installing level two
chargers and offering them free initially while monitoring usage and operating costs, noting the City
could later implement fees, usage limits, or operational changes if necessary.

Presentations

         Communications Department Presentation

Brook Bowen introduced Riverton City’s communications department, highlighting their goal to
create consistent, professional, and engaging messaging to connect City government with
residents. He discussed the newly redesigned newsletter and outlined plans to improve email
outreach.

Nate Slack provided an overview of Riverton City’s digital communications, highlighting trends over
the past five years across the website, social media, email, and text alerts. He explained strategies
to improve engagement, including fewer, more strategic posts, a recognizable blue stripe on social
media posts, consolidated messaging for community classes, and tailored content across platforms
to reach residents effectively.

         Transportation Master Plan

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, provided an update on the Master
Transportation Plan public engagement process, noting that the consulting team had completed the
resident survey and produced a brief video summarizing some initial results. He asked the Council
to provide input on the upcoming spring engagement, including whether to hold an in-person open
house, utilize an interactive online map for comments and project information, or a combination of
both.

The Council supported a hybrid approach to the upcoming public engagement for the Master
Transportation Plan that combines an interactive online map with an in-person event, ideally located
further west to attract broader participation.

         Riverton Police Department

Chief Shane Taylor reported that overall activity trends from 2022 through 2025 show a peak in
calls for service in 2023, followed by a decline through 2025, with property crimes decreasing by
37.8% over that period due to targeted patrols in high-crime areas. Arrests, citations, and traffic
stops have fluctuated year to year, largely influenced by available state funding for enforcement
programs, with proactive measures like DUI shifts and speed enforcement expected to increase as
funding improves.

Discussion/Action Items

         Grant Discussion

Cary Necaise, Public Works Director, explained that the City’s grant process is managed by a
dedicated grant administrator who researches opportunities using tools like Lexipol, with support
from multiple staff members. Grants range from federal, state, local, and private sources, often
requiring matching funds and support letters, and projects need to be “shovel ready” to ensure
timely use of funds. Once awarded, the finance team tracks spending and reporting requirements
until completion. Over the past three years, the City applied for about $8 million in grants and
received nearly $2 million, with roughly a 50% success rate, and staff plan to provide regular
updates on active and potential grants.

         Jordan Basin Improvement District 13400 South Project

Mr. Necaise reported that the City is coordinating with the Jordan Basin Improvement District
(“JBID”) on the 13400 South project, which is approaching the intersection of 2700 West. Two
options are being considered: a partial closure over four weeks with daily openings for school
hours, or a full intersection closure during spring break to complete the work in one week. Both
options will significantly impact traffic, particularly access to Walmart and surrounding areas, and a
traffic control plan with detours will be developed.

A majority of the Council supported a full one-week closure of the 13400 South and 2700 West
intersection during spring break, recognizing it would allow JBID to complete the work in a single
excavation and backfill cycle, reduce overall costs, and limit prolonged disruption compared to a
partial closure over several weeks.

         Capital Facilities Plan

Kevin Hicks, City Manager, outlined the City’s plan to create a comprehensive Capital Facilities
Plan which focuses on capital needs, consolidating past impact fee facilities plans, long-range
planning, and master-planned projects into a single, cohesive framework. The Capital Facilities
Plan will include transportation, parks, stormwater, and other infrastructure projects, prioritize
timing with construction seasons, and track external funding, impact fees, and City funds, while
excluding operating, staffing, or maintenance needs.

Councilmembers emphasized the importance of chronological organization and including grant
match amounts for budgeting clarity. Council discussed sustainability concerns for parks and
noted the importance of committing impact fee funds within required timelines to avoid returning
money to developers.

Mr. Hicks explained that the plan will continue to be refined and updated as additional studies,
transportation master plan updates, and funding opportunities arise.
