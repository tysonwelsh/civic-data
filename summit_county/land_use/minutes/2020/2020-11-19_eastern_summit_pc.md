1.  Cedar Crest Village Overlay Committee and Staff will provide an update and lead a 
discussion with the Commission on the Cedar Crest Village Plan. Cedar Crest Village 
Overlay Committee, representing applicants. – Kirsten Whetstone, AICP, County 
Planner; Peter Barnes, Director of Planning & Zoning and Design; Patrick Putt, 
Community Development Director 
 
Director Putt welcomed the large number of community members. This is a Work Session 
and no public comment will be taken at this time. This meeting is an opportunity to educate 
the public on the process of a Village Overlay Zone. A lot of material will be shown, but this 
is not a final product or final plan. Staff is asking for feedback from the Planning

Commission about the planning area, constraints, opportunities, types of land uses, 
carrying capacity, density, and the public process. The Development Code was amended a 
little over two years ago and the Village Overlay Zone process was created at that time to 
rethink how land planning is done. Property owners needed a stronger voice in the land 
planning process.  
The Village Overlay Zone process starts with a plan first, then determines how to 
implement the plan through zoning. The study area for Cedar Crest is a little over 1000 
acres, with about twenty landowners. Part of the purpose of this process is to conduct a 
land plan while keeping in mind the impact on the surrounding areas. Cedar Crest is a 
phased project that will be built over time. One goal is to maintain agriculture, with likely 
fifty percent or more areas left undeveloped. The applicants are looking for an overall 
density of about five units per acre. Some areas may have more density than others. The 
land plan will be created first, then the zoning will be applied. There will be a Development 
Agreement attached to this project. This is a complicated process that started about a year 
and a half ago for this project. 
Planner Whetstone shared an example of a constraints analysis that includes strengths, 
weaknesses, opportunities, and threats (SWOT). 
Director Barnes shared that theoretical planning has been used to get to the practical 
aspects of what the project looks like on the ground. It is critical to consider the topography 
for how the ground impacts development. Slopes greater than thirty percent cannot be 
built on. Drainage is necessary. Group areas have been established. Park City Heights is an 
example of comparable density. The bulk of the development is untouched.

Planner Whetstone shared the Draft Future Land Use Map showing the village core, 
village residential, village mountain recreation, village greenway, and village industrial 
areas. A Master Plan would be created by developers. There is discussion about a future 
Creamery Lane freeway on/off ramp. Staff would like to have another public outreach 
event before formal public hearings are conducted. The outreach could be virtual. 
In regard to the Constraints Assessment, Commissioner Sonntag stated he is comfortable 
with the proposed placement. Chair Sargent stated that density is placed in the most 
developable portions. 
In regard to land uses, Commissioner Clyde stated that the proposed Village Overlay Zone 
has big, scary density and looks different from the current agriculture uses, which was 
deliberate. The landowners want a complete community that is connected. There are many 
subdivision proposals that are small and make it impossible to have parks. Density is 
needed to pay for the infrastructure of a livable community. This proposal is using a quality 
growth approach.  
Commissioner Wheaton wondered if the density would pay for itself and asked what the 
impact would be to the school system. Commissioner Clyde stated there will be an impact. 
Many people in the community will commute. The impacts need to be considered. 
Commissioner Wheaton stated there are no sustainability elements in the plan. 
Developments need to be more creative and allow for increased density for smaller homes 
with pocket parks and gardens.  
Director Putt stated that the plan needs to start with land uses in the right location to deal 
with the carbon footprint. The goal is to have a birth to death community and establish

housing for younger families and have larger homes for more established owners. A mix of 
residential products is a main goal. There has not been a discussion about house size 
limitations. Planner Whetstone stated that sustainability will be addressed. As 
development arrives, if it is driven to centers then the community will be better connected,

In regard to carrying capacity and density, Commissioner Sonntag stated that Cedar Crest 
is proposing less than the overall density of Daybreak. Properly mixed and properly 
patterned development makes the density more than sustainable and provides a higher 
assessable value. Commissioner Wheaton stated that the impact on schools needs to be

In regard to infrastructure, Director Putt stated that ultimately public water and public 
wastewater will be needed. There is a possibility of tying into Coalville’s infrastructure but 
there have not been any discussions or a specific decision. Potential options are being 
weighed. Commissioner Clyde stated that there needs to be a more regional approach for 
sewer needs. Do not build an undersized sewer for what could potentially extend to 
Wanship. 
Commissioner Wilde asked when it is envisioned that the project will be turned over to a 
developer. A developer may have very different ideas for developing the land. There are 
three major parcels for sale in the Village Overlay Zone. If any of the parcels were sold to a 
farmer, the structure could be changed. The County has gone far enough in design.

Director Putt recognizes that a developer can buy at any moment. If there is a greater 
sense of predictability in the land plan, then the land is more likely to sell. There is a need 
to continue to refine expectations. Ultimate success will depend on convincing a buyer this 
is a community driven plan. There is the expectation that landowners will be approached 
by development firms. 
Commissioner Wilde asked how much taxpayers will continue to pay for the benefit of 21 
landowners. Director Putt answered that he believes work from the Staff on this project is 
a reasonable use of time. If this process was not gone through, then smaller MPDs would 
take up more time. The value to the community is a better integrated plan that future 
residents will want. 
In regard to the public process Director Putt stated that there would have been another 
open house but that is difficult at this time. Public outreach is still needed. There is an 
existing website that the public can access. Chair Sargent stated that the public needs to be 
engaged sooner than later to be sure the project is supported by the community. There 
needs to be clear and concise questions. There is a need to show if the project is good for 
the region and why. Commissioner Wilde stated an open house would be a great 
educational tool. Hopefully there will be the ability to have public meetings soon. Public 
input is needed as soon as time will allow. Chair Sargent stated that the open house was 
educational. There were people opposed to the project and the Planning Commission needs 
to hear why. 
Planner Whetstone stated that the public would like to talk to the decision makers. 
Director Putt stated that the opportunity to write in public input is a good idea. Staff will
