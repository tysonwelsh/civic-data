 F. PRESENTATIONS:
    1. REPORT ON THE SESQUICENTENNIAL CELEBRATION OF THE TRANSCONTINENTAL RAILROAD.
       Sara Toliver, of Visit Ogden, and Kim Bowsher, of the Ogden Downtown Alliance, reported on this huge
       undertaking of hosting a three-day (12-hour event days) extension of the Heritage Festival in recognition of the
       150th anniversary of the completion of the Transcontinental Railroad. The sesquicentennial event started just
       as an effort to support the Union Pacific and the Spike 150 State Commission in the steam meet, but it quickly
       became apparent that in order to really showcase our community, more than just supporting the effort had to
       occur. There were massive planning efforts, collaboration and joint communications with the many area entities
       that were also planning events here and in Box Elder County. They wanted to ensure that any event they
       produced supported our local businesses and created a draw to them. They also wanted to put our dynamic and
       vibrant community in the spotlight, celebrating where we have come from and where we are going, and to
       accommodate the public’s safety, which brought on the addition of numerous programming elements. Weber
       County was the first to agree to support this event, which opened the door to many other partners. Local
       businesses’ feedback indicated that they had record breaking sales, there was positive feedback from attendees,
       participants and the community, and the positive impact was felt throughout the community. This presentation
       was prepared in August (thus the numbers have increased since then) and only included Ogden’s part of the
       celebration: there were more than 2,100 news articles and more than 1 billion people reached with an ad
       equivalency value of nearly $11 million. They showed a video produced by Union Pacific with various leaders
       commenting on the event. Over the three days there were about 100,000 people in downtown Ogden and hotels
       were sold out for nearly a year. There were more than 1,000 volunteers. The commissioners expressed
       appreciation for the tremendous work done on this event. Commissioner Froerer referred to the number of
       people from outside of Utah at the event but he was amazed by the numbers from outside of the United States
       that he met.
    2. DICIO CONTRACT UPDATE.
       Sasha Clark, with the DICIO Group for county public relations, gave an overview of what DICIO has
       accomplished and their future strategies to communicate with the constituency, stating that the ideas came from
       weekly meetings with the commissioners, staff, the local newspaper, partners, and the community. In the past
       year they started instituting a project management software specific to the county, and were involved in 1,083

       collaborating and video filming/editing, and content writing. They will continue their scope of work, and new
       for the coming year is the addition of a project manager and a drone pilot to focus outside of the downtown
       area, secure more proactive press to increase transparency and knowledge of county government, etc. There
       are quite a few initiatives and proposals coming up at the legislature and DICIO will communicate those as
       effectively as possible. Chair Jenkins noted that when he came into office, he was opposed to this contract but
       has since recognized its value. Commissioner Froerer also noted that he had been opposed to certain contracts
       when he ran for office but has since been able to justify DICIO’s value to the taxpayers including what the
       county receives in economic development and that the county is more transparent with the public. The county
       recently launched a major effort for economic development and is receiving regional recognition. The county’s
       job growth is at 4.6% and Ms. Clark is a part of that success. He agrees with showcasing the county’s
       employees, which he feels are the best in the State. Commissioner Harvey did not criticized the contract because
       he has seen its value over the years and hopes that our 15 city partners feel the positive effect.

H. ACTION ITEMS:
    1. ACTION ON TOURISM TAX ADVISORY BOARD RECOMMENDATIONS.
         Sara Toliver, Tourism Tax Advisory Board Chair, presented the annual recommendations for the use of tourism
         tax revenues. In the past, the tourism revenues have not exceeded the expenses. She noted that the Conference
         Center bond payment expires this year. Past recommendations, along with Commission feedback, were that
         the Culture Parks and Recreation (CP&R) Department was to operate with 100% of its operation/maintenance
         (O&M) being covered by the tourism tax revenues, that of any remaining revenues 50% is to be moved into the
         Capital Improvement Fund to help with the necessary capital improvements on county facilities, and also that
         50% is to go into a Future Projects Fund. The 2020 recommendations do not vary much from previous years.
         With the 10-year projections, one change was made. She said that with the much needed capital improvements
         going into the county facilities, those dollars were allocated to the CP&R Department budget this year which
         made their operating costs appear as though they had grown significantly when they were actually for capital
         improvement projects (CIP). This Board’s recommendations are: to keep the CP&R funds separate to show the
         CP&R Department’s O&M actual costs; to continue with the recommendation for CP&R to be funded through
         the TRCC tourism tax dollars; to fund the Convention & Visitors Bureau at 70% of the transient room tax
         collection; that the GOAL Foundation maintain its 60,000 annual funding; to increase the Legacy Grant Fund
         by 10%/year; that 50% of any remaining revenues go towards CIP; and to allocate 50% to the Future Projects
         Fund. She addressed Commissioner Froerer’s question stating that for 2019 the TRT is at 64%. She opposes
         the proposed legislation to change some TRT recommendations because it becomes very attractive for elected
         officials to use tax dollars generated not by their constituents but by visitors, that anytime we deplete tax
         revenues generated by visitors into things that don’t encourage additional visitation, we eliminate our
         opportunity to bring people into our community and generate that revenue overall. A lot of communities driving
         this are in areas that have national parks and highly visited destinations and are feeling the overcrowding impact,
         but there is so much room to grow on the annual occupancy rate, and reallocation of where the marketing and
         promotion is spent to fill those high need periods would be much more valuable in the long term. Commissioner
         Harvey spoke of his confidence in Ms. Toliver’s cutting-age approach to what is happening in our community.
         Christopher Crockett, Deputy County Attorney, stated that this presentation satisfies the Board’s legal
         requirement to make a recommendation and there is no requirement to take action today.

    2. RESOLUTION REGARDING POSSIBLE ANNEXATION OF WEBER INDUSTRIAL PARK – RESOLUTION 56-2019.
         Kaitlin Morgan and Kassidy Black, Weber State University students, presented a non-binding resolution.
         Harrisville has been exploring expansion through annexation of the Weber Industrial Park. Ms. Morgan and
         Ms. Black oppose it and believe that in order to uphold the common good and support economic development
         of the county it is best to maintain the de-annexation of the Park as it was established almost 50 years ago, that
         society can benefit from it as a whole rather than just one small part of society, and it has been enticing for
         businesses on taxation. They asked for the Commission’s support. They had informed the related cities of this
         item. Chair Jenkins will make sure his office sends letters to the pertinent cities.
         Commissioner Harvey moved to adopt Resolution 56-2019 regarding possible annexation of the Weber
         Industrial Park; Commissioner Froerer seconded. He expressed thanks to Ms. Morgan and Ms. Black for
         bringing this forward stating that this Industrial Park was set up to make it a safe haven for taxation and
         encourage businesses to locate there, that businesses were not under any particular jurisdiction so that a
         municipality could not raise taxes anytime they wished. He is aware that surrounding cities have an issue with
         this since a majority of this property was de-annexed from Pleasant View to create the Park. This sends a bad
         message to businesses that located there only to have taxes raised on them; we need a stable environment that
         businesses can depend on for the future.
         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Froerer – aye; Chair Jenkins – aye
