REGULAR MEETING

Chair Erickson called the Planning & Zoning Commission meeting to order at 6:00 p.m. She
explained how the meeting would proceed. First, the Planning Department would brief the
Commission; then the applicant would speak to the Commission; the item will be opened to the
public for their comments on public hearing agenda items; the Midvale Deputy City Attorney
will then brief the Commission on their role on the given item; and then the Commission would
deliberate and decide.

APPLICANT:

Adam Anderson, the applicant, explains the inspiration for the outcome of the project. After
looking at the existing zoning and ordinances, the goal was to make an affordable family-oriented
homeownership neighborhood. The developers wanted to create a community feeling and that
heavily influenced the design that follows the route of the existing trail.
Parking was of significant importance, resulting in the widening of the road from 35-ft to 41-ft
right-of-way road. This will lessen enforcement issues with street parking. All units have a 2-car
garage and a driveway large enough to fit two full-sized vehicles. There are a total of 108 off-street
parking stalls for the proposed 27-unit development.
There will also LID (Low-impact development) procedures implemented with this development,
such as bio-swells and rain gardens for storm water retention.
The developers are requesting a variance on the façade. There is a proposal for a Japanese lilac
tree to be placed in between each home. The trees were placed strategically to beautify the space
with nature versus façade work. They are asking for an increase on front façade from 60% to 83%
and a decrease on the side-facing façade from 40% to 23%. Besides this reduction request, the
development follows all other ordinances.

Commissioner Snow asks if guest parking stalls are not required in this case.

Jonathan Anderson, Planner II, states that with master plan developments the parking standards
are set by the Commissioners in line with current parking standards.

Commissioner Snow requests clarification on the required versus proposed street width.

Mr. Anderson clarifies that a private road is required to be 35-ft wide, and a 41-ft width is being
proposed in this plan. The 50-ft requirement is only for public right-of-ways. In this case, the width
is considered wider than the requirement for a private road.

Commissioner Tippetts questions the possible concerns with the road width for emergency
vehicles if street parking is allowed.

Mr. Anderson refers Commissioner Tippetts’ questions to the applicant for a more accurate
response.

Chair Erickson requests that more information regarding the concerns for the concrete wash-out
area are discussed.

Mr. Anderson describes most of the issues related to the toxicity of the chemicals used in the wash-
out area and the chemicals in the concrete itself. The surrounding homeowners are the concerned
residents leaving public comments. The city’s SWPPP regulations are very closely monitored, and
this is already on their radar.

Commissioner Liedtke asks Mr. Adam Anderson to discuss the concrete wash-out area in more
detail.

Mr. Adam Anderson states that they have already submitted the SWPPP (Stormwater Pollution
Prevention Plan) application and received their NOI (Notice of Intent) permit from the State
Department of Environmental Quality (DEQ). The procedures are strictly monitored and inspected
weekly by City Staff and a third-party compliance group.

Adam Anderson, representing Anderson Development, states that the goal for the project is to offer
great housing at an affordable cost. In the existing space, the design is based on the square-footage
that would be accessible to young families. There is 18% active green space out of the 50% open
space in the development. He guarantees that the designers and developers have spent an extensive
amount of time with City Staff and each other to maximize the quality of housing and the green
space. The street-parking was heavily considered, the road width/right-of-way will measure to be
41 feet to compensate for cars along the street.

parking.

implemented.

Sean Pore, from Brighton Homes, states that these homes will be for sale and the developers are

Commissioners are also concerned with parking, especially during holidays and severe weather.
They compare similar projects with the proposed 27-unit development and discuss the current
parking issues currently being experienced.

Chair Erickson, weighing in on the façade portion of the requests, mentions that she prefers the
code/ordinance to be followed.

Commissioners agree that plants and trees are more temporary and fluctuate in coverage amount
based on age of the tree and season in the year.

Chair Erickson verifies that the parking in the proposal is more than is required. The façade will
be the only request out of the requirement for the ordinance.

Commissioner Snow requests that the Commissioners require additional off-site parking.

Elizabeth Arnold, Senior Planner, does clarify that the Commissioners have the power to make the
parking requirement for the development. She also states that with similar developments, one
additional parking stall is required per unit. For this development, seven additional parking stalls
would be recommended.

Commissioners discuss the possibility of more parking stalls being required. If families have older
children with additional vehicles, there could be a foreseeable parking overflow. The concern is

PUBLIC HEARINGS

      4. Midvale City initiated request to amend (1) Section 17-7-17 of the Midvale City
         Municipal Code entitled Transit Oriented Development Overlay Zone (TODO)
         and (2) Section 17-2-4 to clarify the definition of the term “Development”. These
         changes align the zone with state code and clarify when certain activities are not
         considered “Development”.

      The main purpose of this city-initiated code text amendment is to align the TODO zone
      with state code requirements for HTRZs (Housing and Transit Reinvestment Zones), best
      practices for walkable urban development, and general best practices for code writing.
      HTRZs are a state authorized tool to capture tax increment and use it to incentivize
      redevelopment. Midvale plans to apply for an HTRZ that uses the Fort Union Trax
      Station as an anchor.
    The following changes are incorporated into the new code text:

    - Section deletions:
            o Medium and high-density residential development standards
                    o Retail, office and mixed-use development standards
                    o Retail, office and mixed-use architectural standards
-   Section additions:
                    o Development standards
                    o Architectural standards
-   Use table deletions:
                    o Restaurant, drive-through; Animal Clinic/Hospital; Bed and Breakfast
                    Inn; Commercial Repair Services; Financial Institution W/Drive Up
                    Window; Heliport; Medium and High-Density Residential Developments
                    (replaced by Mixed-Use Commercial/Vertical)
-   Development standards section:
                    o This section synthesizes the previously existing standards sections and
                    adds required mixed-use development around the Fort Union and Center
                    Street Trax Stations as shown on the incorporated maps.
                    o An incentive mechanism (17-7-17.3(H)) that can be evoked with a
                    minimum of 25% office, retail, or restaurant use on the first floor or 10%
                    affordable housing was created in the zone to lead the market in areas that
                    do not fall into the areas of required mixed-use development. The areas of
                    required mixed-use development automatically qualify for the incentive.
                    The incentive increases the maximum building height from 5 stories to 10
                    stories and 85 dwelling units per acre to an unlimited density.
                    o A section on recreational amenities for residential uses was added (17-
                    717.3(J)).
-   Architectural standards section:
                    o This section builds on the previously existing architectural standards
               section and was modified to align the text with best code writing practices.
-   Required landscaping section:
                    o This section synthesizes the previously existing landscaping standards
               section and was modified to align the text with best code writing practices.
-   Parking standards section:
                    o This section is based on the previously existing section and was
                    modified to make it consistent with other sections in the code and align the
                    text with best code writing practices.
-   Renumbering of sections 17-7-17.7 to 17-7-17.12 to account for section deletions
    elsewhere in the text.
-   Definition of “Development”:
                    o The code language now relates the definition of “Development” to a
                    change of use and excludes minor activities such as replacing a water
                    heater.

    -ZONING CODE AMENDMENT CRITERIA-
    Midvale City Code 17-3-1(F) outlines the criteria necessary for amendments to the
    zoning code. A proposal may only be approved if it demonstrates one or more of the
    following:
      1. The proposed amendment promotes the objectives of the general plan and purposes of
      this title;
      2. The proposed amendment promotes the purposes outlined in Utah State Code 10-9a-
      102;
      3. The proposed amendment more clearly explains the intent of the original language or
      has been amended to make interpretation more straightforward; or
      4. Existing zoning code was the result of a clerical error or a mistake of fact.

      Staff finds that this proposal meets criteria 1-3 listed above.

      Criterion 1 is met because this code amendment promotes the redevelopment objectives
      of the West Gateway and Middle State Street Opportunity Areas of the General Plan. The
      purpose of Midvale Municipal Code Title 17 is met because this code amendment
      promotes coordinated development, redevelopment, effective use of land and site
      planning.

      Criterion 2 is met because this code amendment promotes many of the purposes dealing
      with general land use authority such as promoting prosperity and protecting the tax base.
      Criterion 3 is met because numerous changes were made that clarify the intent of the
      ordinance and express concepts in clear language that follows best legal and zoning
      practices.

      Staff recommends approval of the text amendment to the City Council with the following
      finding: 1. The amendment complies with Midvale City Code 17-3-1(F) and meets
      criteria 13.
