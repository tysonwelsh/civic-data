PUBLIC HEARING: MAY 08, 2018 — 5:30 P.M.-CACHE COUNTY SERVICE AREA NO. | ~
RESOLUTION NO. 2018-0] — APPROVING AN ADJUSTMENT OF SOLID WASTE
COLLECTION AND DISPOSAL FEES AND MANDATORY CURBSIDE RECYCLING —
Chairman Erickson opened the Public Hearing and asked Issa Hamud to explain the proposed
fee increase. Hamud attributed it to increased operating costs associated with the transfer
station and running two landfills. Fees were last raised in 2006.

Chairman Erickson asked if there are plans for any improvements at the North Valley Landfill
stating debris is blown in to neighboring farm fields. Hamud said the Sheriff's office is providing
pick-up crews for the debris and a higher litter fence is being built.

Chairman Erickson and Council member White expressed concern with the percentages of the
increase that go to the Logan General Fund and the Logan Enterprise Fund. Council member

Merrill said if the amount is simply “return on investment”, it is ok, but he can’t tell from the chart
given him.

Cache County Council
05-08-2018

Council member Worthen stated county residents are charged more than Logan City residents
for garbage services but 8% of that money is going to fund Logan City. She would like to see
charges equalized. Worthen also asked Hamud for a comparison of private recycling
companies’ costs.

Council member White said the difference in county and Logan City residents’ costs was agreed
on when the service was originally established and the percentages were agreed to bya
different Council.

Council member Zilles observed the County Council is the entity that must decide if there is an
increase or decrease, not Logan City. Maybe the Council would like to wait to see what Logan
City is going to do before making a final judgment.

Executive Buttars reminded the Council that this is a Public Hearing and no decision needs to
be made tonight. There is a 5th Tuesday on May 29, 2018 and he could contact Logan City
Council for a joint meeting to discuss this issue.

Vice Chairman Ward explained that 40% of the land in Logan City is nontaxable because it is
owned by government or educational entities. If the Enterprise funds go away, then property tax
on homes would have to be increased over 100% to cover costs.

Chairman Erickson invited public comment.

Mayor N. Craig Hidalgo, Clarkston, is displeased with the problem of blowing trash from the North Valley
Landfill. It is being picked up, but workers tramp all over the farmers’ fields. Will there be any
remuneration to farmers for that damage?

Alex Bearnson, Revolve Recycling, receives demolition waste at his facility. He understands the need for
increases, but it’s a struggle to compete with a government entity. It appears the current Logan City plan
is to spend $18 million of taxpayer money to build a new construction demolition transfer station on the
north end of the valley. If he were allowed to operate at what it costs him, he could employ up to sixty
people in the county and add 25 additional years of life to the landfill. He reiterated the difficulty of
competing with a government entity sustained by taxpayer dollars. He is asking that the city and county
charge a reasonable fee related to the cost. He is asking for fairness.

Jason and James Godfrey, spent $18,000.00 last year to have pallet debris received at the landfill. They
operate a pallet repair business — major customers are JBS and Schreiber Foods. Hamud explained the
cost used to be $9.00/ton received at the landfill with the understanding they could chip the waste and sell
as woodchips, but due to trouble with the chipping, the discount fee will be removed so the fee will be
$30.00/ton. Hamud recognizes this will affect the pallet business in the valley. If they are allowed to haul
directly to the landfill, it could be done, but the conditional use permit does not allow that. Godfrey's say
this increase will put them out of business. The savings to Schreiber’s was significant - $91,000.00 for
the Logan plant and JBS saved $86,000.00 a year using Godfrey's facility.

Bearnson interjected those businesses could come to Revolve at $9.00/ton, but he would need to be able
to haul to the landfill.

There was no other public comment.

ACTION: Motion by Council member Zilles to close the Public Hearing. Worthen
seconded the motion. The vote was unanimous, 6-0. Tidwell absent.

PUBLIC HEARING: MAY 08, 2018-6:20 P.M.-ORDINANCE NO. 2018-06 — OPTIONAL SALES
AND USE TAX TO FUND HIGHWAYS AND PUBLIC TRANSIT — Chairman Erickson opened
the Public Hearing and Director Runhaar explained Senate Bill 136 which removed the ballot
requirement for the local option sales tax. The county has two options: Option A — 33% of
CVTD portion to a grant pool to go back to communities where CVTD service is provided and
Option B — Work for possible legislative action in 2019 to change the percentage to transit.

Chairman Erickson invited public comment.

Jim Gass, Executive Director for CMPO, said the county has a serious problem with road funding and
spoke in support of the tax noting it is only 25 cents on $100.00.

Erik Ashcroft, CVTD Board, supports trying to get legislation passed to change the percentage allocated
to transit. He observed ridership of the CVTD has declined 30%, but revenues have increased over 35%
and the CVTD is carrying a $1 million surplus.

Vic Little, Paradise Town Council, supports the tax.

Norman Larsen, Nibley shop owner, not happy with it, but hopes money goes to the right place.


Cache County Council
05-08-2018

Representative Val Potter supports the local option sales tax ordinance. He will be happy to take it back
to the legislature next year if that is what the Council decides, but can’t guarantee the results. Potter
hopes the CVTD will be willing to work with the county as proposed in Option A.

Jim Gass pointed out that if the county elects to not pass this ordinance, Logan City will have the
opportunity to pass one on their own and the county will receive nothing.

There was no other public comment.

ACTION: Motion by Council member Worthen to close the Public Hearing. Ward
seconded the motion. The vote was unanimous, 6-0. Tidwell absent.

Council member Worthen stated she is also concerned that transit receives such a large
amount and has discovered that voters in some cities in Cache County approved a mass transit
tax of .3 or .25% for their city and wonders if this could be used as a negotiating tool assuming it
is possible for those cities to repeal their tax. As a result, transit would not be receiving so many
dollars.

Executive Buttars committed to working with Director Runhaar in negotiations with the CVTD
and/or going to the legislature for changes to the distribution of local option sales tax dollars.

Council members are not happy with the amount transit receives, are supportive of Option A,
but don’t want to lose $3 million and therefore are supportive of the ordinance enacting a local
option sales tax.

PROCLAMATION

WHEREAS, the Congress and President of the United States have designated May 15, 2018 as Peace Officers
Memorial Day, and the week of May 13 - 19, 2018 as National Law Enforcement Week; and

WHEREAS, the members of the Cache County Sheriff's Office play an essential role in safeguarding the rights
and freedoms of Cache County; and

WHEREAS, it is important that all citizens know and understand the duties, responsibilities, hazards, and
sacrifices of their law enforcement agency, and that members of our law enforcement agency
recognize their duty to serve the people by safeguarding life and property by protecting them
against violence and disorder, and by protecting the innocent against deception and the weak
against oppression; and

WHEREAS, the men and women of the Cache County Sheriff's Office unceasingly provide a vital public
service;
NOW THEREFORE, in recognition of all law enforcement officers and in particular the men and women in the Cache

County Sheriff's Office, be it resolved that the Cache County Council and the Cache County
Executive, County of Cache, State of Utah, hereby proclaim the week of May 13 through May 19,
2018 as

LAW ENFORCEMENT WEEK

and call upon all citizens of Cache County and all patriotic, civic and educational organizations to observe this week with
appropriate ceremonies and observances in which all of our people may join in commemorating law enforcement officers,
past and present, who, by their faithful and loyal devotion to their responsibilities, have rendered a dedicated service to their
communities and, in so doing, have established for themselves an enviable and enduring reputation for preserving the rights
and security of all citizens, and Monday, May 15, 2018 as

2

PEACE OFFICERS MEMORIAL DAY

and further call upon all citizens of Cache County to honor those law enforcement officers who, through their courageous
deeds, have made the ultimate sacrifice in service to their community or have become disabled in the performance of duty,
and let us recognize and pay respect to the survivors of our fallen heroes.

In witness thereof, as Chair of the County Council and County Executive, we have hereunto set our hand this
8th day of May, 2018.

ORDINANCE 2018 - 06

AN ORDINANCE OF THE COUNTY COUNCIL OF CACHE COUNTY, UTAH,
ENACTING CHAPTER 3.86 OF THE CACHE COUNTY CODE OF ORDINANCES,
NTITLED OPTIONAL SALES AND USE TAX TO FUND HIGHWAYS AND PUBLIC

TRANSIT

E

WHEREAS, in the 2018 general session the Utah Legislature has enacted Senate Bill 136
which authorizes a local option sales and use tax to fund highways and public transit; and,

WHEREAS, funding provided to Cache County and to cities located in Cache County

through Class B & C funds derived from the State gas tax are insufficient to maintain existing
infrastructure; and,

WHEREAS, maintenance costs continue to increase putting a strain on municipal and county
resources needed to maintain infrastructure; and

WHEREAS, the Cache County Council finds that this ordinance is in the best interest of
Cache County and its citizens,

NOW, THEREFORE, The Cache County Council ordains as follows:
SECTION 1:

Cache County Code Chapter 3.86 is enacted to read as follows:
3.86.010: STATUTORY AUTHORITY:

The authority for imposing this tax is derived from title 59, chapter 12, part 22, Utah Code as
amended.

3.86.020: PURPOSE OF PROVISIONS:

The ordinance codified in this chapter is enacted to provide a source of revenue to provide its

residents with safe highways and public transit and council directs that the provisions hereof be

interpreted and construed to accomplish this stated purpose.

3.86.030: IMPOSITION; AMOUNT:

Pursuant to Utah Code Section 59-12-2219, and in addition to the other taxes imposed, the

county does impose and levy for collection a sales and _use tax of one-fourth of one percent upon

the retail sales and uses in the county as such sales and uses are described in Utah Code Section

59-12-103(1) as amended, subject to the exemptions provided for in Utah Code Section 59-12-

104 as amended. The foregoing tax is imposed and levied for collection on a countywide basis,

including the areas within the corporate limits of the cities and towns of the county. The

provisions of this chapter shall be subject to the provisions of the sales and use tax laws of Utah

to which reference is hereinafter made in this chapter, and which are enacted and made a part of

this chapter as though fully set forth herein. The funds received from the imposition of this tax

shall be used and expended as provided for by state statute,

3.86.040: STATUTES ADOPTED BY REFERENCE:

The following requirements and provisions of law are adopted and included in this sales and use

tax chapter:

A. The appropriate provisions of title 59, chapter 12. Utah Code, as amended, are enacted and

incorporated herein by this reference thereto;

B. The applicable provisions of title 59, chapter 12, Utah Code, as amended, are enacted and

included in this chapter insofar as they relate to sales and _use tax, except that the name of the

county, as the taxing agency, shall be substituted for that of the state wherever necessary, that an

additional seller's permit shall not be required if one has been or is issued to the seller, and that

any retailer who has registered with the state tax commission shall not be required to repeat such

registration with the county.


SECTION 2:
EFFECTIVE DATE;

This ordinance takes effect 15 days following Council approval date. However, the tax imposed
by this ordinance shall take effect on the first day of a calendar quarter after a 90-day period
beginning on the date the Utah State Tax Commission receives notice as required in Utah Code
Section 59-12-2209.

PASSED AND APPROVED BY THE COUNTY COUNCIL OF CACHE COUNTY,

3. Full time public safety employees working for the County Sheriff accrue 4 hours of Special Leave each

b. An employee's balance of Special Leave may be reduced to a negative balance, but the employee
