          1. 2    Mayor' s Report



      Mayor Kristie Overson reported on a ribbon- cutting for the Larry H. Miller Softball Complex.
She was grateful to Salt Lake County as well as the Larry H. and Gail Miller Family Foundation
for the beautiful new facility.

The previous week saw the annual Night Out Against Crime held in conjunction with the weekly
Starry Nights on the Plaza. In addition to the usual food trucks, there was a police K-9
demonstration, a helicopter, the FBI BearCat, HamNet radio operators, and numerous public safety
personnel with various other pieces of equipment. The mayor said it was a great opportunity to
show appreciation to first responders and also to shed a light on what citizens could do to contribute
to public safety.

Mayor Overson recently attended a meeting of the Unified Fire Authority Board of Directors, of
which she is a member. UFA wished to have a way for their personnel to show the distinction
between the various communities they served. On one shirt sleeve they wore the traditional UFA
patch, but they had recently created a patch for each of these different communities. She displayed
the new Taylorsville patch that would be worn by those serving in our city.

School in the Granite School District had started earlier that day. The mayor expressed her
appreciation to TVPD officers for keeping a close eye on traffic around Taylorsville High School.
She appreciated everything they did to keeps students safe at all the schools in the city.

The week before, Mayor Overson had attended a large gathering of Granite School District staff
and faculty. It was held at the Expo Center and was a fun event held to show appreciation for all
those who took part in educating our kids.

Last years' sculptures were being rotated out and new ones would be installed the following Friday
evening. There was no cost to the city for this art, but the pieces were lent to the city for a year at
a time.



A great deal of construction was taking place in Taylorsville. Mayor Overson appreciated
everyone' s patience as improvements were made in various parts of the city.

         1. 3     Citizen Comments


IR Chair Knudsen invited any member of the audience who wished to address the City Council
to step forward and do so.

Linda Paulsen and Linda Snarr explained that the Utah State Legislature had designated the month
of September as American Founders and Constitution Month. They encouraged those gathered to
recognize and observe the occasion. Councilmember Harker who worked as an elementary school
educator, pointed out that she had been receiving emails and lesson plans to help involve school
children.




David Harris shared his concerns about parking and traffic on Simper Lane. He explained that
some townhomes had recently been built and for some reason all the potential parking spots in that
subdivision had been painted red which caused the residents to have to park on Simper Lane. He
explained that drivers parked on both sides of Simper, resulting in only a single lane of traffic
remaining open for cars moving in both directions.

Susan Ison also spoke regarding Simper Lane. She explained that when applying for a variance
before building the townhomes, the developers promised 1) to have sufficient on- site parking to
meet residents' and visitors' needs; 2) that the townhomes would all be owner-occupied; and 3)
that there would be plenty of green space upon which children could play. They had failed to
deliver on all three promises.


Councilmember Cochran inquired if the parking problem was worse at any particular time of day.
Ms. Ison responded that it was through the night as well as during the morning hours.

3.       REPORTS


         3. 1     Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District—Pam Roberts


                General Manager/ CEO Pam Roberts greeted the Council and explained that WFWRD
now had an apprentice program for those individuals 18 years of age and older interested in
obtaining their commercial driver license ( CDL). WFWRD would provide training and pay for
them to get their CDL for those interested in working for the entity. This was an effort to address
long-term staffing issues.

Ms. Roberts provided some statistics regarding what was collected during June' s city-wide annual
cleanup event:
         5. 71 tons of bulk waste
         3. 23 tons of green waste

         1. 11 tons of recycling( non- glass)
         240 pounds of glass


During the 2023 SCRP program( Seasonal Container Reservation Program), 1, 167 containers were
delivered over 19 days. Collected during that time was 885 tons of bulk waste, 265 mattresses, 22
refrigerators, and 37 tires.


WFWRD was currently ten drivers short and because ofthat, it was necessary to suspend the trailer
rental program until the end of September.
