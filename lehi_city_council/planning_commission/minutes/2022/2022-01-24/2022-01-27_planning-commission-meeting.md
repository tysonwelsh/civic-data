2.1) Consideration of Rage Development’s request for approval of revisions to the Atlas Cell
Tower adding collocation equipment for T-Mobile. (tabled from the January 13, 2022
meeting)
Sierra Pierson presented information on the agenda item.

Commissioner Nielsen clarified that this is not a public hearing item.

The applicant for Rage Development was present. He said the tower has been approved with this
design, but the ground equipment layout was changed. There was going to be an anchor antenna,
but because everything was put on pause, the criteria changed. There are eight total antennas, all
of them inside the tower but one. They have done their best to maintain stealth, but the main sector
needs coverage. Commissioner Nielsen asked if this would be a continual change, or just an

exception. The applicant stated this has been permitted and to meet the objectives and this is the
only antenna on the outside. The applicant was fine if this was a conditional approval, and if there
were any more changes he needed to make he was fine to come back to the Commission.

Commissioner Newall asked Staff if they were worried by allowing this exception that additional
requests would be made. Kim Struthers stated that this concern is outlined in his DRC comment.
Commissioner Britton stated when she read though code it was allowed as long as it was not
proceeded by 30 inches. This antenna was 37.8 inches tall and 29 inches wide.

        Motion: Commissioner Eyre moved to deny Rage Development’s request for approval
        of revisions to the Atlas Cell Tower adding collocation equipment for T-Mobile; with
        the findings with setting a precedent that it does not meet code and could set a dangerous
        precedent of unwanted development; including all DRC comments. Commissioner
        Newall seconded.

        Vote: Commissioner Nielsen, yes; Commissioner Eyre, yes; Commissioner Newall, yes;
        Commissioner Britton, yes. The motion passed unanimously.

The applicant stated this was approved with the ground equipment with zoning. Kim Struthers
stated he would need to look at the permit that had been granted to see exactly what was approved.
Commissioner Newall asked if Planning Commission approved something that was not stealth,
why is this item in front of the commission. The applicant stated it went before the design review
committee. He stated the antenna was on the outside originally and Staff recommended a revision
to the application, that is why it was sent to Planning Commission. The Commissioners are
concerned about a non-stealth antenna. Commissioner Nielsen asked if Staff was allowed to
approve something that does not meet code. Kim Struthers stated that they are not allowed to
administratively approve something that does not meet code and he will look into it. Kim stated
they do approve co-locations. He also stated there is an appeal process that can be taken to City
Council. Craig Chambers also stated if it was approved by Staff, it has to be honored.

2.8) Public hearing and consideration of Gardner Company’s request for conditional use
approval of the Lewis Landing Industrial site plan, an 11.81-acre industrial development
located at approximately 4150 West Waterbury Drive.
Sierra Pierson presented information on the agenda item.

Ben Seastrand with the Gardner Company was representing the Applicant. Commissioner Eyre
asked if the connection to 2100 North had been granted. The applicant stated that it has and they
are working with UDOT to be moved due to access convenience. Commissioner Newall asked
about bike racks. Ben stated that he believed there would be bike racks. Commissioner Nielsen
asked why the trail was so close to the parking lot. The applicant stated it was because of the
grade and slope. Commissioner Britton asked how much traffic was anticipated. He stated there

would be a truck routing plan restricting trucks coming from the west and that the access from
2100 North would act as the truck access point. Commissioner Britton stated trucks coming from
the North will be taking Redwood Road and they will take neighborhood routes and that is a
concern of hers, especially if there is a 30 acre park being considered in the future.
Commissioner Eyre also had questions about cut through on the property. The applicant stated
they would look at the traffic study and a trucking plan. Commission Britton asked what a
trucking route plan meant and if they had to adhere to that. Ben stated they could put a plan in
their CC&R’s. Commissioner Nielsen asked if it as an allowed use. Sierra Pierson stated it was a
conditional use. Commissioner Nielsen asked about the dumpster location and if the dump trucks
had the allowance to turn in and out.

Commissioner Nielsen opened the public hearing.

Chris Brown stated he lives in Holbrook Farms. He is also concerned about the traffic that would
affect 2100 North and Redwood Road. He thinks it is not appropriate to allow entry from 2700
North to Redwood to go to Woodbury and through the neighborhood roads. From the Holbrook
Farms perspective, with a school and neighborhood kids nearby, and a potential park to the
North West of the property, he is concerned. He feels the applicant and City need to give more
thought to the traffic. Mr. Brown feels there are certain things that can be done. He is concerned
about the enforcement of the traffic that will be brought in. He is asking the Commission to
reconsider approving for prevention of truck traffic into the neighboring streets especially from
2700 North and Redwood.

Commissioner Nielsen also recognized there were two online comments that echoed the thoughts
of traffic concerns.

Sierra Pierson stated she misrepresented and that this was not part of the Holbrook Farms area
plan. The piece is adjacent, not part of it, and is commercial and will fall under State Highway
guidelines.

Commissioner Nielsen closed the public hearing item.

Luke Seegmiller stated 2100 North will be a right in and right out. There will be about 2500 trips
daily. The other part of the traffic study only assume 5% comes from North and South. Luke
stated cutting through the neighborhood is not efficient. The turnarounds are more efficient to
take than going through the neighborhoods. Commissioner Britton stated because of the
congestion, the trucks will cut through the neighborhood. The study identified improvement at
the 3600 West signal. He thinks it may be a problem during peak hours. He also stated speed
bumps are not something that are installed as it is typically a liability and hazard for the
snowplows and drivers. The Commission discussed traffic concerns.

The applicant stated the use for the buildings are flex use and they want to attract office users.
They are marketing towards office tenants rather than truck manufacturing and heavy industrial.
He stated they are open to exploring other traffic options for access points and do not want it to


be a traffic nightmare and want to have happy neighbors.

The Commission discussed traffic concerns, zoning, and conditional uses. There was discussion
on how the traffic and uses would impact the neighbors.

The applicant stated he would like to withdraw the item with all the discussion that had ensued
and would like to come back at a later time.
