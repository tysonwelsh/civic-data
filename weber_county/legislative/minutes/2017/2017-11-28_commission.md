F. ACTION ITEM:
         RESOLUTION TO PURSUE LEGAL ACTION AGAINST MANUFACTURERS AND DISTRIBUTORS OF OPIOIDS –
         RESOLUTION 49-2017.
         Commissioner Gibson stated that the opioid crisis has been receiving a lot of national and state-wide
         attention. He read the letter from Utah Speaker of the House, Greg Hughes who encouraged counties at the
         recent UAC conference to move forward with their intent to take action on this critical issue and to confront
         the carnage that opioids have created in our community. In response to this crisis, President Trump declared
         this as a nation-wide public health emergency, and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention have
         warned that the U.S. is experiencing a public health epidemic. In Utah we know that 4 out of 5 heroin users
         started down the road of addition through prescribed opioid pain medication and over 30 Utahans a month
         are dying as a result of opioid overdoes. The State continuous to prioritize tens of millions of dollars
         annually to law enforcement and many other programs in an attempt to help those who are immersed in this
         crisis. Speaker Hughes continued that it has become clear that we need to take this fight to those who have
         profited by bringing 95% of their products to a country that makes just 5% of the world’s population. These
         drug manufacturers have marketed their products to physicians and the public with messages that they knew
         were false.
         Commissioner Gibson read the county’s resolution which included that: in 2016 there were nearly 215
         million prescriptions for opioids in the U.S.; in 2015 nearly 12.5 million Americans non-medically used
         prescription painkillers; everyday over 1,000 people are treated in emergency departments for misusing
         prescription opioids; 91 Americans die every day from an opioid overdose; opioids are responsible for ¾
         of all prescription drug overdoses; nearly 1 in 3 Utah adults were prescribed an opioid painkiller; and in
         2014 Utah ranked 4th highest in the U.S. for drug overdose. At least 12 states and many more counties have
         commenced litigation in the past two years against manufacturers and distributors of opioids and this
         resolution calls for Weber County Commission to pursue legal action against said drug manufacturers and
         distributors by 12/31/2017. Commissioner Harvey preferred having a broader date to be able to live up to
         the commitment. The other two commissioners and Mr. Baron felt that the date is non-binding and is a soft
         date. The commissioners expressed their strong support of this action.
         Commissioner Harvey moved to adopt Resolution 49-2017 to pursue legal action against manufacturers and
         distributors of opioids with the date of January 31, 2018 as the date to commence legal action;
         Commissioner Gibson seconded stating that with the Commission’s fiduciary responsibility for taxpayer
         funds and personal lives of individuals, we cannot afford what this crisis is doing to our communities.
         Commissioner Gibson – aye; Commissioner Harvey – aye; Chair Ebert – aye

   2. PUBLIC HEARING TO RECEIVE PUBLIC COMMENTS ON THE WEBER COUNTY 2018 TENTATIVE BUDGET.
        Scott Parke, County Comptroller, stated that the tentative budget was posted on the county’s website on
        11/1/2017 for review. Today’s meeting is to take public comment. He gave a slide presentation that
        included what property taxes pay for, how government is funded. He also showed the revenues and
        expenditures for the General Fund, Municipal Services, the Library Fund, Paramedic Fund, and Health

        healthy fund balance, it is able to put funds aside to cover debt service and plan for the future.
        Chair Ebert invited public comments and none were offered.
   3. PUBLIC HEARING REGARDING A PROPOSAL TO AMEND SECTIONS OF COUNTY CODE: APPLICABILITY (108-
      2-3) & OGDEN VALLEY RECREATION & RESORT ZONE (108-8-5 AND 108-8-13) TO ALLOW THE LAND USE
      AUTHORITY TO MODIFY THE APPLICABILITY OF CERTAIN PROVISIONS OF LAND USE CODE IN OGDEN
      VALLEY DESTINATION & RECREATION RESORT ZONE, WHERE A MASTER PLAN HAS BEEN APPROVED.
        Steve Burton, of County Planning, stated that the changes are minor and had been noncontroversial. Three
        sections allow the Planning Commission to modify the applicability of certain standards only for master
        planned communities in the DRR-1 Zone—parking, Ogden Valley landscape and Screening and Signage.
        Today’s amendments are specific to the first two standards. The most significant change replaces the words
        “Planning Commission” with Land Use Authority. Other changes simply provide clarity. On 10/24/2017
        the Ogden Valley Planning Commission recommended approval based upon the following findings: 1) The
        changes cause no adverse effect on the intent of the General Plan, 2) The clarifications will provide a more
        efficient administration with the Land Use Code, and 3) The changes will enhance the general welfare of
        the residents.
