     COMMUNICATIONS
     A.  CODE ENFORCEMENT POLICY AND PROCEDURE
         Councilmember Harmon stated that it has been three or four months since the
         Council began discussing Code Enforcement policy and procedure. He indicated
         that he is concerned that courtesy notices are not as courteous as they should be,
         residents have complained that they received multiple notices days a part, residents

   didn’t feel they were given adequate time to fix their issues, residents were unable
   to speak to Code officers on the phone, and residents expressed concern about being
   spoken to unprofessionally by Code Enforcement officers. Councilmember
   Harmon stated that he would like to move forward together to improve Code
   Enforcement and create full transparency for both residents and staff.

   Mayor Lang indicated that she received a complaint from a resident who received
   a notice that snow wasn’t shoveled within 24 hours. She stated that the resident
   received the notice in the mail the following week after everything had been taken
   care of. Mayor Lang indicated that another concern she has is that mailing time
   should be taken into account when providing deadlines. She suggested at least 3
   full weeks as a deadline for residents to bring their properties into compliance.
   Councilmember Nordfelt stated that snow removal would be different because it
   would need to be done within 24 hours. Layne Morris, CPD Director, explained
   that snow removal is an administrative warning and detailed how the process
   typically works. He noted that residents along safe routes to school are sent a
   postcard every year with a reminder to keep sidewalks shoveled within 24 hours of
   a storm. Councilmember Harmon suggested that Code Enforcement put a door
   hanger or notice on homes that haven’t shoveled their sidewalks. Wayne indicated
   that this would not be a good option for the safety of the officers. He noted that
   there are residents who don’t like Code Enforcement officers on their properties at
   all. Mayor Lang suggested that instead of issuing the administrative warning, the
   City could simply mail another reminder postcard and outline the safety reasons.

   Councilmember Whetstone asked if there was an industry standard for the number
   of Code Enforcement officers. Wayne replied that there are not many effective
   models across the country. He indicated that the City currently has 9 officer
   positions and one is vacant. He added that there are approximately 2500 homes per
   beat. Wayne stated that the need for Code Enforcement changes over time and with
   different Council’s. He indicated that staff is open to whatever direction the Council
   would like to go. He indicated that compliance has always been the goal, not
   punishment or collecting fees. Councilmember Harmon expressed concern about
   an accelerated time frame for landscaping violations that occurred last summer/fall.
   He indicated that the Code Enforcement officer only provided the resident 5 days
   and was told that he was given that direction by supervisors. Wayne replied that he
   isn’t sure how that would be misinterpreted because his direction was to gain
   compliance within 10 days without any extra allowances unless residents were
   willing and showing that efforts were being made to move forward.
   Councilmember Huynh stated that compliance should be the goal, not harassment.

   Councilmember Harmon suggested 17 days for compliance. He indicated that this
   would be 14 full days plus 3 extra days for the mail. He suggested that the courtesy
   notice be a postcard so that it is taken less harshly. Councilmember Harmon also
   suggested including a photo of the violation so that residents are aware of what they
   need to fix. Mayor Lang agreed and indicated that a photo would be extremely
   helpful and would eliminate a lot of questions from residents right away.

   Councilmember Huynh expressed concern about a home in the Chesterfield area
   that has trash in front of the home as well as someone living in a trailer on the
   property. He stated that this property has been reported multiple times but nothing
   has been done to correct the problem. Layne indicated that if residents suspect that
   someone is living in a trailer on a property, the Police Department should be
   contacted immediately so that can be addressed. He noted that he is familiar with
   the property and much of the problem is located in the backyard behind a fence and
   Code Enforcement has limited ability in addressing these types of problems. Layne
   stated that if there is trash in front of the home, this is absolutely something Code
   Enforcement can issue a courtesy notice for.

   Councilmember Whetstone suggested a Code Enforcement review board that
   consists of residents who can listen to hearings and provide a layer of oversight.
   Councilmember Harmon stated that he likes the idea of other residents holding their
   neighbors accountable and also being a liaison between the Code Enforcement
   officers and the residents. Mayor Lang indicated that she likes this idea as well and
   indicated that the board can provide explanation and guidance to residents.
   Councilmember Nordfelt indicated that this would be asking a lot of someone on a
   volunteer basis.

   Councilmember Harmon stated that he would like Chapter 10 ordinances to be
   more understandable to residents. He indicated it would be helpful if the language
   was less legal and simpler to read. Mayor Lang indicated that it has to be in legal
   format but suggested an improved website that breaks down the code and violations
   that shows pictures of what is and isn’t acceptable. She recommended a brochure
   as an option as well and indicated that pictures would go a long way for everyone,
   particularly residents who don’t speak English. Councilmember Nordfelt suggested
   including a QR code on the courtesy notices that take residents directly to the
   website.

   Councilmember Harmon asked if citations are issued for weeds. Wayne replied that
   the current standard is that weeds must be 6 inches or less. He indicated that
   landscaping is one of the most difficult codes to enforce because there are so many
   different views of what counts as landscaping and what doesn’t. Councilmember

   Huynh stated that if the grass is green, even if there are weeds mixed within it, it
   should be fine. Councilmember Harmon stated that he likes to keep a green grass
   with no dandelions and always makes sure that he maintains a clean yard. He
   indicated that he understands we live in a desert and xeriscape or desertscape should
   be allowed for those who want it. Councilmember Harmon stated that his biggest
   concern would be that the yard looks maintained. The Council discussed the
   definition of weeds and flowers. Councilmember Nordfelt stated that if it’s
   overgrowing onto the sidewalk, it’s a problem.

   Councilmember Nordfelt stated that residents will never be completely happy with
   Code Enforcement. He indicated that no one likes to be told they are doing
   something wrong. Councilmember Nordfelt indicated that there are always two
   sides to the story and the Council most often hears the residents side and not the
   Code Enforcement officers. H stated that there are things that need to be fixed and
   adjusted and there is always room for improvement but he noted that Code
   Enforcement is important. He indicated that he believes the program can be
   successful. Councilmember Whetstone agreed and noted that customer service
   training should be conducted and the approach of the courtesy notice should be
   softened. Wayne stated that he doesn’t believe there is an epidemic of issues with
   Code Enforcement officers but agrees there are complaints. He indicated that better
   ways of doing things should always be looked at and improved upon.
   Councilmember Harmon stated that the job of a Code Enforcement officer is
   difficult because they are constantly dealing with residents who aren’t happy. He
   indicated that training is needed so officers know how to diffuse difficult situations.

   Councilmember Harmon stated that he doesn’t believe Code Enforcement should
   occur in backyards unless there is an immediate health or safety issue. Wayne
   discussed the current policy and noted that Code Enforcement typically doesn’t
   enforce backyards unless a neighbor calls with a complaint and allows them access
   to view the concern from their property. After further discussion, the Council
   agreed to no enforcement of backyards unless there is a health and safety concern.

   Councilmember Harmon stated that residents shouldn’t get multiple notices that
   arrive days apart. He indicated that only one notice should be sent to residents
   within 30 days. Councilmember Harmon also suggested a hotline number that
   residents can call to reach someone who can answer their questions if the officer is
   out.

   Mayor Lang indicated that she believes each issue should begin with a courtesy
   notice and no automatic acceleration for every new violation. Wayne replied that
   this is an option but noted that the reason this is done is because there are chronic

      offenders who fall out of compliance immediately. Councilmember Harmon stated
      that 90% of residents will come into compliance and do what is right while the other
      5-10% will be repeat offenders. Wayne replied that calculation is correct.

      Councilmember Huynh stated that he believes proper training is important. He
      indicated that Code Enforcement officers shouldn’t use threatening language and
      shouldn’t tell neighbors who turned them in because this creates friction in the
      community. Wayne replied that this should not be occurring. He indicated that there
      are many Code Enforcement violations and notices issued each year and less than
      5% of those ever end up with an alleged negative interaction with an officer.

      Mayor Lang indicated that she believes abatements should go through the Council
      or a Code Enforcement Board. Wayne replied that the majority of abatements are
      for yard or material clean up but there are a couple that are for emergencies where
      someone is living in an abandoned house, etc. He indicated that he would suggest
      not delaying the emergency abatements. Wayne indicated that abatements are part
      of the Administrative Law Judge’s function. He indicated that currently the ALJ is
      an employee but the ALJ used to be contracted with an outside party.
      Councilmember Nordfelt stated that he doesn’t believe the Council should review,
      approve, or deny abatements. Mayor Lang stated that she would be more
      comfortable with an outside party or a board. Councilmember Harmon stated that
      it would soften the abatement process if it’s ordered by someone separate from the
      City.

      Mayor Lang stated that she believes landscaping requirements should be 30% at
      full growth. Lars replied that if a yard looks maintained, he isn’t concerned about
      how large a particular plant will grow. Councilmember Harmon indicated that as
      long as weeds are maintained at 6 inches and not encroaching onto the sidewalk, he
      doesn’t mind them. Mayor Lang indicated that she is fine with dirt-scapes as long
      as they are maintained.

      Councilmember Harmon stated that section 10-1-304 of the City Code be removed
      given that Code Enforcement officers are no longer post certified and don’t have
      the power to arrest.


MOTION TO ADJOURN
Upon motion by Councilmember Harmon all voted in favor to adjourn.


THERE BEING NO FURTHER BUSINESS OF THE WEST VALLEY COUNCIL THE
