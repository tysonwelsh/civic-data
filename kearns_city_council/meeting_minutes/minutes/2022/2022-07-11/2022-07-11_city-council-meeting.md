Olympic Resolution

               Mayor Bush stated this resolution just clarifies that Kearns supports the Olympic
Committee in its bid for the Olympics.

             Council Member Snow, seconded by Council Member Schaeffer, moved to allow
Mayor Bush to sign the following resolution.

                                         RESOLUTION

WHEREAS, Salt Lake County successfully hosted the 2002 Olympic Winter Games and
Paralympic Winter Games and the communities of Salt Lake City, Kearns Metro Township, and
West Valley City were critical to this success; and

WHEREAS, facilities put in service for the Games continue to be utilized to this day, including the
Salt Lake Sports Complex in Salt Lake City, Utah Olympic Oval in Kearns, Maverik Center in West
Valley City, and many more; and

WHEREAS, we desire to ensure that the next Games hosted in our communities prioritizes
diversity, equity, inclusion, and access, is climate-positive, and net-zero waste, and acts as a
catalyst for achieving major community initiatives; and

WHEREAS, we believe such a vision will be accomplished in a multitude of ways including
ensuring that:

   •   everyone employed to help prepare for the Games or employed during the Games will be
       paid a living wage;

   •   the Games are welcoming, inclusive, and accessible for all, with universally accessible
       venues and events, and disability awareness training for public-facility staff and
       volunteers, and that opportunities should be created for residents of all income levels to
       participate in the Games and accompanying events;

   •   prioritizing use of existing infrastructure and venues, developing a carbon-management
       plan for Games-related activities, striving for a Games that is net-zero waste, and forming
       a sustainability committee to ensure that sustainability goals are achieved for the Games;

   •   maximizing use of public transportation, including clean and active transportation modes,
       for all Games stakeholders;

   •   the SLC-UTCG will be a model of transparency, robustly engaging the public in the
       planning process, making regular meetings open to the public and sharing information.

NOW, THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED, that officials in Salt Lake County, Salt Lake City, Kearns
Metro Township, and West Valley looks forward with great anticipation to being selected as the
host city for a future Olympic and Paralympic Winter Games.

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that officials in Salt Lake County, Salt Lake City, Kearns Metro
Township, and West Valley are committed to working with our community partners and the SLC-

Emergency Alert Addition

               Mayor Bush stated on June 29, 2022, there was a police situation where reverse
9-1-1 had gone out. The reverse 9-1-1 did not reach everyone in Kearns, but it did reach people
through the state of Utah. Some people got the call three to four hours later or even days later.
She attended a debriefing with the Unified Fire Authority, Unified Police Department, Valley
Emergency Communication Center, Emergency Management, and additional people. At the
meeting it was suggested that Kearns invest in an additional emergency alert system called “Code
Red.” Many cities and government agencies go with this system; however, there is a charge
associated with it. She asked the Council Members if they would like her to get additional
information on this service and bring it back to the Council for discussion.

Council Members felt this was something they would like to get additional information on.

              Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, stated the Council
would need to go through the procurement process for this.

               Council Member Snow stated there were a lot of issues that went wrong with this
reverse 9-1-1 system. It has opened a lot of eyes to the problems that need to be fixed.

Purchase of Additional Automobile for Unified Police Department

                Chief Levi Hughes, Unified Police Department, stated the traffic officer would like
to be more effective in enforcing traffic violations not just speeding, but stop signs and those types
of things. If a cop sits at a stop sign in a marked vehicle, then everyone will stop, and he is not
able to enforce the law. If he was in an unmarked vehicle, then he is able to do his job. The
current vehicle is not up for rotation for two or three more years. He asked if the Council would
be willing to pay for an unmarked vehicle for the traffic officer.

             Mayor Bush stated she would support his request. She gets a lot of complaints
about speeding in the Kearns area.

              Council Member Peterson stated he liked the idea as well. He asked if the
purchase of the vehicle would go through the fleet department.

              Chief Hughes stated yes, that would be the best way to go about purchasing a
vehicle because he would get a better deal. Once the vehicle is purchased and build out. He

              Council Member Schaeffer asked what the vehicle would cost.

control in Kearns. The motion passed unanimously.
