Proposal 
Applicant, Copper Moose Farms, is requesting CUP approval of a “Farm Stand for Plant and 
Agricultural Sales” for the existing farm stand at 1285 W Old Ranch Road. The applicant is 
proposing to continue the use as is, with no expansion or change to the use. The reason for the 
CUP is that the Snyderville Basin Development Code was amended to make the use conditional 
in the Rural Residential Zone, which means the applicant would no longer have to apply for a 
temporary use permit each year as she has in the past.

Background 
 
The applicant operates a temporary seasonal plant and agricultural sales use on her property at 
1285 West Old Ranch Road. The farm grows vegetables, flowers, and herbs on approximately 3 
acres during the summer months and grows in an existing 2 ,400 square foot greenhouse during 
the winter months. The vegetables are then sold at an onsite farm stand or distributed to local 
restaurants.  
 
Each year, the applicant submits a temporary use permit application to operate between the 
months of May and October. The use was permitted as a “Seasonal Plant and Agricultural Sales” 
use. The use is listed as a “T” or temporary use in the Use Table, and defined as: 
 
“Seasonal Plant and Agricultural Sales The sale of agricultural or ornamental plants during 
the time of year in which the products are harvested.” 
 
4
3 
 
The temporary use permit criteria limit the duration of the use to one year and require that the 
site be returned to its original condition prior to the issue of the permit. The use does operate 
only during summer months, but it has become evident that the  use has expanded beyond the 
scope of “temporary” status.  
 
To remedy the temporary use problem, staff and the applicant began discussing the best way to 
ensure that the use can continue operations without expanding to a point where it has a negative 
effect on surrounding properties. It was determined that a code amendment creating a spot in 
the use table and criteria in chapter 8 would be the best way to allow the use and mitigate 
potential impacts. Consequently, the applicant appli ed for a code amendment including draft 
language. The Code amendment was reviewed by  the Planning Commission who forwarded a 
positive recommendation to the County Council who approved the ordinance in February of this 
year.

Recommendation 
Staff recommend that the Snyderville Basin Planning Commission (SBPC) review the proposed 
Conditional Use Permit, conduct a public hearing, and approve the application, pursuant to the 
following findings of fact, conclusions of law, and conditions of approval. 
 
Findings of Fact: 
 
1. The applicant is requesting CUP approval of a “Farm Stand for Plant and Agricultural 
Sales” at 1285 W Old Ranch Road. 
2. 1285 Old Ranch Road is on parcels PP-119 (1-acre) and CBE-2-AM (18.16 acres). 
3. 1285 Old Ranch Road is in the Rural Residential zone (RR).  
4. In March of 2025, the Summit County Council adopted an ordinance making a “Farm 
Stand for Plant and Agricultural Sales” is a conditional use in the RR zone.  
5. Prior to March of 2025, farm stands were processed as a temporary use in the RR zone.  
11
10 
 
6. The applicant has operated the use as a temporary use from May to October for the 
past 10 years. 
7. No changes to the existing site plan are proposed.   
8. There is an existing sales structure that is 1,500 square feet and greenhouse buildings 
that are 2,400 square feet in size.  
9. The sales building is on a Lot 1.09 acres in size.  
10. The proposed farm stand is entirely on private property. 
11. The proposed farm stand is adjacent to Old Ranch Road, which is listed as a Collector 
Road on the Summit County Road Classification Map.  
12. A maximum of 14 parking spaces are allowed. There are 10 existing parking spaces.  
13. Farm products are grown in a field adjacent to the sales building.  
14. The Lot has sufficient gas, electricity, cable, Wi-Fi, water and, sewer for the use on site.  
15. There are no steep slopes, ridgelines, wetlands, lakes, ponds, geologic anomalies, or 
year-round streams on the property.  
16. No critical lands will be impacted as part of this approval.  
17. Chapter 5 of the Snyderville Basin Development Code requires all new commercial uses 
to provide affordable housing.  
18. Chapter 5 of the Snyderville Basin Development Code also exempts the first 5,000 
square feet of a development from the affordable housing requirement.  
19. The proposed farm stand has a total of 3,900 square feet and is therefore exempt from 
the affordable housing requirement.  
20. No reduction in public services will occur due to this application.  
 
Conclusions of Law:  
 
1. The use is consistent with the Snyderville Basin General Plan. 
2. The use conforms to all applicable provisions of the Snyderville Basin Development 
Code, including, but not limited to, any applicable provisions of Section 10-8-19, 10-3-5, 
10-4 and 10-2-4, and State and Federal regulations. 
3. The use is not detrimental to public health, safety, and welfare. 
4. The use is appropriately located with respect to public facilities. 
5. The use is compatible with the existing neighborhood character and with the purpose 
provisions of the applicable zone district and will not affect surrounding land uses. 
 
Conditions of Approval: 
 
1. Sales of locally grown agricultural products or locally produced prepackaged agricultural 
products are allowed, however at least 50% of the products sold must be grown or 
produced on site. 
 
2. Food trucks, chef appearances, sales of cooked food and other similar activities are not 
allowed on the Lot unless it is part of a Special Event Permit acquired through the 
County special event process. 
 
12
11 
 
3. Large gatherings such as weddings, fundraisers or corporate parties are not allowed on 
site unless a Summit County Special Event Permit is acquired through the County special 
event process. 
 
4. Unless otherwise approved as part of a Special Event Permit, the sale of alcohol is 
prohibited.

Description of Copper Moose Farms Operation 
The Farm Stand at Copper Moose Farms proposes to operate during the spring/summer and fall up to 
three (3) to four (4) days a week, from 10am on weekdays and 9am to 5 pm on Saturdays. The days it will 
be open may change based on staff and produce availabil ity. During the winter the hours and days the 
Farm Stand is open will be reduced to an “as available basis”. 
The Farm Stand will have a maximum of four (4) crew members with varied shifts, depending on the day.  
During the time of year when and if snow needs to be cleared, Coper Moose will coordinate snow removal 
of the parking area with a tractor and staff will shovel the paths and the perimeter of the site.  
As the site plan depicts, parking for the Farm Stand is currently existing. There are ten (10) marked parking 
spaces available in the paved parking area.  There is additional overflow parking on a woodchip covered 
lot where trailers or large vehicles can be parked. The spaces provided have historically been adequate 
for the Farm Stand and no expansion of the use or parking area is anticipated.  
Vendor deliveries will be made the day before the Farm Stand’s operational day. All deliveries will be 
coordinated with the farm managers in order to ensure they occur during the hours of 8am to 5pm. 
The existing dwelling unit on the site will continue to be utilized. The Copper Moose Farm’s farm manager 
utilizes this dwelling. This provides continued security to the greater farm site, as well as the Farm Stand.  
Signage for the Farm Stand is limited to an hours of operation sign. The Farm Stand does utilize temporary 
“Slow Down” signs when the Farm Stand is open to aid in pedestrian safety. Additionally, two gates are 
opened during business hours, located contiguous to the pedestrian crosswalk on Old Ranch Road and on 
McLeod Creek trail to separate pedestrians and bikers from cars.

Proposal 
The purpose of the amendments is to amend the Development Code to replace the Board of 
Adjustment with an Administrative Law Judge (ALJ), to ensure that land use decisions are handled 
efficiently, impartially and in accordance with the standards in the Code.  
Background 
As part of the 2025 budget, the County Council provided increased funds for an Administrative 
Law Judge with the goal of changing how zoning variances and Code Enforcement violations are 
handled in the Development Code. This amendment would replace the current Board of 
Adjustment, a volunteer citizen board appointed by the County Council, with a professional 
Administrative Law Judge (ALJ). 
The purpose of the amendment is to improve efficiency, and legal consistency of variance and 
code enforcement decisions. While the Board of Adjustment has served the community for 
many years, increasingly complex legal and technical cases require specialized expertise that 
can place a heavy burden on volunteer board members. 
A land use variance is official permission to break a rule in Chapter 3 of the Development Code 
(usually height or setbacks) when following the rule would cause unfair hardship, but only if 
specific conditions are met. 
Per the Code, a variance can be granted when: 
1. Literal enforcement of the zoning rule causes unnecessary hardship (not just
inconvenience).
2. The hardship is unique to the property (not general to the neighborhood).
3. Granting the variance will not harm the public or neighboring properties.
42
2 
4. The variance is in line with the overall purpose of the Development Code.
5. The variance is the minimum needed to fix the hardship.
The Administrative Law Judge would be a legal professional with training in land use and 
administrative law. He/she would conduct hearings, evaluate evidence, and issue legal 
decisions relating to variances and code enforcement issues. 
This is not a unique proposal, as several counties and municipalities in the state have 
transitioned to an ALJ, including Heber City, Wasatch County, Salt Lake City, and Draper City, to 
name a few. 
Public Hearing 
Recent changes to the State Code by the Legislature have eliminated the option to hold a public 
hearing as part of a variance process. The rationale from the legislature is that these hearings 
are “quasi-judicial.” A quasi-judicial hearing is like a court process that is conducted by a 
government body (not in a court room). The decision is based on legal standards and not 
political preference or public opinion, hence the elimination of the public hearing component.   
A hearing may be open to the public (anyone may attend) but there will not be an option for 
the public to speak.
