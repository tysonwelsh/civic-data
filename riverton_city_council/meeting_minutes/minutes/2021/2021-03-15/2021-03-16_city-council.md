       Salt Lake County Update on Election Process 2021 - Sherry Swensen, Salt Lake
       County Clerk, and Lannie Chapman, Chief Deputy Clerk

Sherry Swensen, Salt Lake County Clerk, explained that they had received their equipment from
Dominion and the new equipment has the ability to do Ranked Choice Voting. She said that
Ranked Choice Voting was advantageous because it eliminates the need for a primary election.

Ms. Swensen said that the cost for a primary and general election in Riverton without ranked
choice voting would be $48,357. The cost for a general election only with ranked choice voting
would be $30,396. She explained that this cost included the additional ranked choice voting
software costs which will be split between all of the cities participating in ranked choice voting.

Mayor Staggs asked if Salt Lake County elections office had the software for ranked choice
voting and if they felt comfortable to move forward with this software.

Ms. Swensen said she felt confident that they will be ready to go with this new software.

Councilmember Buroker asked for clarification on how the ranked choice voting software costs

Ms. Swensen explained that the ranked choice voting software was $25,000 and this cost would

Councilmember Wells expressed concern over the cost to educate the public about ranked

Ms. Swensen said that the Lieutenant Governor’s office had $250,000 set aside for educating

Councilmember Stewart asked Ms. Swensen what her interpretation was of the letter that

choice voting.

Councilmember Buroker said that her understanding was that Riverton City had chosen to

         UTOPIA Presentation by Roger Timmerman

Roger Timmerman stated that Utopia is a political entity of the State of Utah and partners with
different cities to provide fiber. He explained that Utopia at the beginning had a negative image
because they were losing money. He said they now have a model that helps build fiber
networks to connect cities and does not lose money.

Mr. Timmerman presented a proposal for upgrading the core city facilities (presentation is in the
archived files for this meeting). He said the initial phase of the upgrade would cost the city
$7,160 per month with a 5 year commitment. He said this is probably not more than what
Riverton City is currently paying and the City would get a big upgrade to their fiber. He
explained that the fiber is an open access infrastructure and is available to anyone including
schools, businesses, and wireless providers.

Councilmember Stewart said that the parks in his area were not included in any of the phases.

Mr. Timmerman said that this was a mistake on his part and he would fix it and submit a new
proposal which included these parks.

Councilmember Buroker asked for clarification on copper versus fiber and also if fiber could be
used for 5G networks.

Mr. Timmerman explained that they would use all new fiber. He also said that 5G does not
compare with fiber and is for cellular devices.

Mayor Staggs said that in order for 5G to be effective it has to ride the fiber.

Councilmember Wells asked who owned the assets and how many of the cities that Utopia
works with have expanded the service to residents.

Mr. Timmerman said the assets are not owned by the cities. The assets are owned by Utopia
who has board members who make decisions. He said that most of the cities Utopia has worked
with have provided fiber to the residents.

Councilmember McDougal suggested that the Cares Act money could be used to pay for this
cost up front. He also asked if providing fiber to city parks was necessary to get fiber to the
residents.

Mr. Timmerman said that each phase was necessary to get the network in place to be able to
provide fiber to residents.

Councilmember Stewart explained what bilateral meant in the fiber context. He said the upload
speeds are very slow and that is why the fiber needs to be updated.

Mr. Timmerman said that with everyone sending video it will be necessary to upgrade the fiber
in all cities.

Mayor Staggs thanked Mr. Timmerman for coming to present.

Discussion Items

         Update on Recreation Master Plan - Sheril Garn, Parks and Recreation Director

This item will be discussed on April 6, 2021 due to time limitations.

Mayor/Council/Manager Reports

Mayor Staggs said that he had emailed the council regarding the $4.6 million dollars that the
City would receive in relief funds. He said that 50% will be distributed once the bill passed and
the other 50% in a year. The approved uses for these funds are any COVID related expense
including first responders, revenue shortfalls, and improvements to water, sewer and
broadband. In the past, he said the City has been hesitant about updating fiber because they
didn’t want to go into debt. He said he would like to gage the temperature of the council in
regards to fiber and said he could meet with each councilmember individually. He said they
could create a committee or have all of the council weigh in on the options and go over pros and
cons.

Mayor Staggs reported that he had a good meeting with Salt Lake County regarding the golf
course. He said they had discussed putting the golf course out for RFP to possibly renovate the
facilities and he said they discussed renewing the contract with the County. He said that Salt
Lake County was getting $225 million in relief money and the county is looking at using some of
the money to invest in a water source. The County has contemplated taking a pipe from the
green well and running it directly to the golf course. He said he would not like this idea because
it locks in a single user for all of the potential capacity of that well. He would like to improve the
well so that it could be used for the golf course and the residents.

Councilmember Stewart said during the Unified Fire Authority meeting they had considered
asking each city to contribute part of their relief money for the shortfall of UFA and he wanted
the council to be aware when looking at these funds.

Councilmember Buroker said she would like to have a discussion on the redistricting process
and a future work session on water efficiency standards.

Councilmember McCay asked if the relief money could be used for the secondary water
metering project.

Mayor Staggs said that he would see if the relief money could be used for the secondary water
metering project but said that Senator McCay was also looking at a money source for this same
project.

Councilmember McDougal said that the residential street, Creek Meadow Road, by Lowes has
had a lot of traffic because of the closure at 12600 South. He has been working with Trace to
provide some recommendations for traffic control.
