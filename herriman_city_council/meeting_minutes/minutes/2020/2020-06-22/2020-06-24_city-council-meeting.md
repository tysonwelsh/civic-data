The following are the minutes of the City Council Electronic Meeting of the Herriman City Council. The
meeting was held on Wednesday, June 24, 2020 at 5:00 p.m. in the Herriman City Hall Council Chambers,
5355 West Herriman Main Street, Herriman, Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting, as required by law, was
posted in the City Hall, on the City’s website, and delivered to members of the Council, media, and interested
citizens.

    2.1. Park Ordinance Update - Anthony Teuscher, Deputy Director of Parks, Recreation and Events
Deputy Director of Parks, Recreation, and Events Anthony Teuscher discussed the pump track project. The
project was designed to have wheeled, non-motorized vehicles, such as bikes or scooters, run on. The one being
constructed was a lumber frame with a composite top, although there were many different types of
construction. He presented a video from Centennial Park which highlighted their pump track to give the
Councilmembers a better sense of the aesthetics. He said Centennial Park had gotten positive feedback on their
pump track, so he reached out to their team to gather their recommendations on construction and
implementation. He displayed some conceptual drawings to show where the track would be located in the park,
and other details such as where parking would be available.

Councilmember Smith asked about the required maintenance if the amenity had lumber frame and Deputy
Director Teuscher said it would be low maintenance compared to other types of tracks, and noted the wood
base frame had a 20-year lifespan.

There was a question if there would be lighting installed on the track and Deputy Director Teuscher replied
there would not be since the park was open from 9:00 AM to dusk and so by the time that lighting would be
needed that the park would already be closed. Another question was posed if there was a concern about graffiti,
and it was noted the track was black and so would be easy to paint over any graffiti.

Councilmember Ohrn said she did not like the regulation requiring all riders must wear a helmet. She said they
would then be required to police that to ensure compliancy with the policy and asked if the verbiage had to be
that way for liability purposes. Deputy Director Teuscher explained the utilization of the track was at the rider’s
own risk, and noted if a rider chose to not wear a helmet and was injured, it removed the City from liability for
the accident. Councilmember Ohrn said she did not want to create rules that would not be enforced, and she
said it made more sense to only have the signs say, “ride at your own risk.” Deputy Director Teuscher suggested
it would be better to have too much signage rather than not enough, and to explicitly notate riders should wear
June 24, 2020 – City Council Minutes                                             Page 4 of 21
a helmet was to protect the City. Councilmember Ohrn expressed her opinion riders should have more freedom,
and felt this mandate was an overstep. Councilmember Henderson proposed they change the wording, since
they would not be able to enforce the rule that they wear helmets. It was discussed there would not always be a
police presence; however, when someone was there, they would be able to enforce the rule.

Councilmember Ohrn also questioned the hours of operation. Deputy Director Teuscher indicated all the parks
opened at 9:00 AM to give staff the time clean bathrooms and remove trash. All of the park facilities such as
the skate park all opened at the same time and staff was wanting consistency in regards to operational
hours. Councilmember Ohrn asked if the trails also opened at 9:00 AM and Deputy Director Teuscher clarified
the trails had different operating hours.

Councilmember Henderson asked why non-motorized scooters were not included in the list of vehicles allowed
on the track and pointed out that there had been a non-motorized scooter in the video presented. Deputy

Councilmember Henderson asked if only BMX bikes were allowed, and it was noted all bicycles could be taken
on the track and the language would be clarified to include all street bicycles. Deputy Director Teuscher asked
if there were any other questions and announced his intention to return to the Council with updated signage that
reflected the Council’s comments. There was a question about what the punishment would be for an infraction
of the rules. Deputy Director Teuscher said once the rules were codified, they could be enforced by the police,
and so punishments would be reflective of what the state laws allow for infractions. He added the fine for an
infraction could be up to $1,000, but usually people were given a civil fine, which was about $25 to $50.

    2.2. City Council Strategic Planning Discussion and future Work Meeting Schedule - Brett Wood,
         City Manager
City Manager Brett Wood reminded the Council the Strategic Planning Meeting date had been rescheduled
annually from January to November as it was logistically easier for the Council. He said the purpose of the
planning discussion was to solicit feedback on what the Council’s vision was for the City and observe the long
term projects for improvement that would best meet the City’s needs. He talked about having gone to many
different locations in previous years to hold the meeting, including going on a retreat outside of the City. He
also mentioned that in the past, there had sometimes been a facilitator, and other times they had self-regulated
the meetings. He said they needed to take the time to be completely dedicated and thorough in this meeting in
order to fully understand what their collective vision and goals for Herriman were and how they could best
achieve those ideals. He reminded the Council it was their prerogative how the money in all their funds was
spent, and so it was important that there was clear communication between the Council and the staff that created
their budget. He said he liked having the Planning Discussion meeting at the end of the year so staff would have
a baseline to work with as they began creating the budget for the next year.
Councilmember Smith opined having moderators at the meetings was not productive, and they would be better
served to have a meeting with only the City Council and staff as they best understood what topics would have
to be covered. He also said there were two distinct meetings that should happen, one to discuss strategy and
another one focused solely on the budget. He agreed with City Manager Wood that it made sense to have a
budgetary meeting in the late fall, just ahead of when the budget cycle would start. He then proposed they also
June 24, 2020 – City Council Minutes                                        Page 5 of 21
hold a meeting over the summer to focus more on goals and strategy for the City, and the meeting should be
spearheaded by the Council.

Councilmember Shields said he had some exercises that might be beneficial for the Council and staff, which
focused on articulating and writing down what their specific ideas for the City were and how they could work
cohesively on accomplishing those ideas. He concurred there should be a separate meeting for the budget.

Councilmember Henderson reflected over the years, there have been recurring themes and issues discussed in
the meetings, and began to pay attention to City Council meetings as a private citizen and he had noticed that
the conversation was often circuitous. He also agreed the budget conversation would have to be a separate
meeting, since the budget of the City was too large to be incorporated into another meeting. He said they needed
to have a conversation about how to hold the meeting in a way that would allow the Council to be as effective
as possible. He brought up the possibility of leaving the City to minimize distractions, as opposed to holding it
in the City Council building and said that they needed to put serious thought into where and when to conduct
the meeting. He reiterated the Council would all really need to commit their time and energy to this meeting,
and it would only be a productive meeting if everyone were fully invested.

City Manager Wood said he liked the tools and exercise that Councilmember Shields had spoken about. He said
he felt the meeting would be most effective if the Council took the helm on it. He reminded the Council they
were a legislative body and therefore had more authority than the City staff, and so it would be more effective
if the Council laid out their ideas and goals for the City for staff to take direction from, rather than having staff
lead the meeting.

Councilmember Ohrn agreed they should not just work off of a task list during the meeting as it was not
holistic enough to cover all of the topics needing to be discussed. She said they needed to create an agenda. She
said from the perspective of the staff, it was far more helpful for the Council to delineate their goals for the
City so staff knew exactly what the priorities of the Council were when they drafted the budget. Assistant City
Manager Tami Moody indicated these meetings used to be conducted this way; however, previous Councils
had drifted away from this structure which made it more difficult for staff to communicate to the public what
the intentions of the City Council were.

Councilmember Smith agreed with Assistant City Manager Moody, and said when the Council failed to give
the staff clear direction, there was no way to hold anyone accountable for projects that were not being
completed. He said it would be easier to make decisions when there was mutual understanding from both the
Council and staff about what the overall goals and trajectory for the City were. It was decided the Council
would think about how the meeting should be formatted and on what date it should be held, and they discussed
setting up a shared calendar to find a date that worked for everyone.

Councilmember Shields brought up the idea of holding additional work meetings that were shorter, as opposed
to having longer meetings on a less frequent basis. He suggested holding a meeting that was more concise might
be more effective for the Council, since in the longer meetings they covered so many topics it was sometimes
June 24, 2020 – City Council Minutes                                          Page 6 of 21
difficult to keep track of everything. He proposed holding one meeting every Wednesday evening, and also said
it might be helpful to avoid holding meetings that ran later into the night.

Councilmember Smith said he would be willing to hold additional meetings and agreed that it could improve the
efficiency of the Council to hold shorter meetings more frequently. He then said that if they could improve
their efficiency as a Council, then holding additional meetings might not actually be necessary. He said they
should consider if they needed extra meetings because there were additional things to discuss, or if they needed
extra meetings because they were inefficient in covering all of the items on the agenda.

Councilmember Shields explained his reasoning for proposing more meetings. He said generally what happened
was they took too much time in the work session and then they got to the general meeting late, and then returned
to the work session after the meeting. In his opinion, going from the work session to the general meeting and
then returning to the work session did not make sense and did not seem to be the best use of time. He said the
current structure of the meetings needed to be restructured.

Councilmember Ohrn agreed, and asked staff if they would rather come every week and have meetings be more
concise, or if they would prefer to meet less often but know that the meetings would likely run over time and
they would be there late into the night.

Councilmember Henderson said he felt the longer meetings were less efficient, and typically the longer the
meetings went, the less work the Council actually got done. He said he did not have time for more meetings
but liked the idea of splitting up the work sessions and the formal Council meetings. He also said there should
be more work meetings for specific projects that would require extra time. He said they had done that in the
past, and it had been more efficient to have dedicated work meetings for larger projects. He also pointed out
the confusion coming from the resident’s point of view for the Council to go directly from their work session
to the Council meeting, since the Council would be referencing things being discussed in the work session. He
asked the other Council Members what they thought about breaking up the work sessions from the Council
meetings.

Councilmember Smith recalled that recently, they had pushed the start time of the general meeting from 7:00
PM to 7:30 PM in order to give them more time in the work session. In his opinion, if they were going to add
another night to meet, it would be important that all the meetings be shorter. He said it would still be helpful
to meet briefly before the formal meeting to allow the Council to review the agenda and discuss any
questions. He proposed they add one additional work meeting on the third Wednesday of each month in
addition to continuing to meet on the second and fourth Wednesday of the month. He then proposed they
change the start time of all of their meetings and change the start time of the work sessions to 5:30 PM, and
then move the start time of the City Council meeting back to 7:00 PM. That way, both the work sessions and
the Council meetings would be shorter. He also said they would have the flexibility to cancel one of the work
sessions as they became more efficient.

Councilmember Henderson agreed with this plan and said they needed to evaluate how much they can cover in
one meeting and set the agenda accordingly.

    2.4. Herriman K9 Memorial Dog Park Update - Wendy Thomas, Assistant City Manager and
         Jonathan Bowers, City Engineer
Assistant City Manager Wendy Thomas said they had met with their contractor to try to lower some of the costs
associated with the project, as she turned the time over to City Engineer Jonathon Bowers. It was noted they
were going to meet with the residents the next day about to establish a nonprofit that could help cover some of
the costs associated with the park.

City Engineer Jonathon Bowers began his presentation, and reminded the Council of the original total cost for
the park had been $1.6 Million, and after an adjustment of $362,000 made by the Council at the last City Council
meeting that the new total amount was just under $1.2 Million. Their recommendation was to have a project
contingency of 10-percent, which would bring the new total back to $1.3 Million. He referenced a list of
eliminated items and said the largest item that had been removed was the splash pad. He said the utilities needed
to service the splash pad were expensive, and that it took about $80,000 out of the total cost to remove the
splash pad and its associated utilities. He said they had also removed a drinking fountain and were evaluating the
size of some concrete structures in the park.

The Council asked if they could see an itemized list of what was still going to be installed in the park and said
that she wanted to understand exactly how they had come to the total of $1.3 Million for the park. City Engineer
Bowers replied when they work with the contractors, they always ask for a bid schedule which shows the
breakdown of different aspects of the project. The bid schedule received from the contractor had 104 items on
it, each with an individual price so that they were able to see exactly how the money was to be spent.

City Engineer Bowers listed more of the expensive items in the park and said the costs for landscape and
irrigation took up a significant portion of the budget. A question was asked if it included the labor costs as well,
and the Council had a short conversation about how high the labor costs were, and if there were opportunities
to involve citizens with the care and upkeep of the park on a volunteer basis.

City Engineer Bowers said the work involved would be extensive and extended far beyond just laying down sod
since the ground needed to be leveled as well. He said cutting down on labor costs was not the most efficient
way to decrease the overall cost of the park. He also spoke about the way the park was funded and said that the
funding for the park came from impact fees and was not being taken out of resident’s property taxes. He
explained impact fees could only be used for the construction of new parks, and park fees could be used for the
maintenance and upkeep of existing parks.

Councilmember Ohrn agreed this was an important point to keep in mind since they regularly received
comments about the allocation of money, and how funds were spent.

City Manager Wood said constructing a dog park had been on their agenda for several years, and said it was
important they be fiscally responsible with the funding that came from residents, and also it was important that
the citizens understood how funds were allocated. He reminded the Council not to get fixated on the fact not
all citizens would utilize a dog park, since there were facilities throughout the City that were only used by certain
June 24, 2020 – City Council Minutes                                      Page 9 of 21
members of the public such as the marina or skate park. He mentioned the dog park was part of the overall
master plan for city development.

Councilmember Smith asked how much money it would cost to construct a regular park of a similar size as the
intended dog park, and City Manager Brett Wood said it would come to about $750,000 just for the irrigation
and landscaping of a normal park. They discussed how the public did not generally understand how expensive
the construction of public parks were, and it would be important if the public had a better frame of reference
for the general cost of such projects to help them better understand how the money was used. City Manager
Wood reiterated the dog park was a high priority for residents for a long time, and they would be able to gradually
phase in some of the more expensive components of the park, rather than installing everything all at once. It
was also clarified the $139,000 for delayed items had not been calculated into the new anticipated total cost. City
Manager Wood said the contractors had told them that they could hold on to the bid for 30 days, and after that
point they would have to commit to the plan.

Councilmember Ohrn said they would need to balance the need to be prompt in their response to the
contractor’s bids with also being sure that they took the time to obtain all the information and details that they
would need to give to the residents. City Engineer Bowers said he would meet with the contractor again and
said he was fairly certain that the contractor would be willing to extend the 30-day time frame if needed.

Assistant City Manager Thomas asked what the Council thought about doing corporate sponsorships in the
park, and if they would want to allow companies to put their logos on certain amenities in exchange for
funding. She pointed out they use sponsorships for other City events. City Manager Brett Wood added there
would be contracts between the company and the City that would have stipulations about what the companies
could include in their advertisements in the park. In response to a question about the distinction between a
sponsorship and an advertisement, City Manager Wood said that they were similar and that the main difference
was that a sponsorship was typically permanent.

Councilmember Henderson said he was hesitant to have advertising on City amenities. He said he would not
want to have advertisements be permanently placed in the park. Councilmember Smith said he was open to
sponsorships, and he thought it would be a fair trade-off to allow companies to have a small statue or plaque in
the park in exchange for the funding that it would bring in. He also said they should consider allowing people
to add plaques or signs in honor of loved ones that have died in return for a donation. Councilmember Ohrn
said she would have no problem with the suggestion, but they needed to make sure they had a very thorough
document with detailed parameters of what was and was not allowed.

Assistant City Manager Tami Moody said most legislation which had been passed did not impact Herriman
directly. She said there was a water conservation bill that had been passed, which she commented was a positive
thing. She listed some other updates, and said SB-5004 had been changed to remove the language limited to the
first class. She also said the Open and Public Meeting Act had been amended, but did not apply to the City
since they fell under the Emergency Quarter which was in place until August. She explained if the City still
wanted to hold meetings electronically after the Emergency Quarter expired, they would have to present a letter
of determination was signed by the Mayor detailed why they still felt holding a meeting online was
necessary. There were also Peace Officer amendments, and Assistant City Manager Moody said the Herriman
Police Department had been proactive about implementing the policies outlined in the amendments, and so
their police force was already in line with the provisions of the amendments. She added the police departments
