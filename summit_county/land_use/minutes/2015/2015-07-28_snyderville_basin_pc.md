1. Discussion of a proposed amendment to the Snyderville Basin Development Code, 
Section 10-8-11, Preservation of Historically Significant Structures – Ray Milliner, 
Principal Planner 
 
Planner Milliner presented the staff report and explained that this Code amendment request 
resulted from a six-month moratorium placed on this section of the Code due to the potential for 
abuse of that section.  He stated that an application was made that exceeded the standards of the 
Low Impact Permit in this section of the Code that is now going through the appeal process.  He 
provided photographs of some historic structures in the Snyderville Basin.  He reviewed and 
discussed the four sections in the draft language as described in the staff report, which include 
the purpose, determination of significance, additional uses allowed, and qualifying provisions. 
 
Commissioner Peck requested some grammatical and style changes to the amendment.  She 
asked if historic buildings would be limited to a deli with no drive-through, because she sees 
historic buildings used as cafes or other types of foodservice uses.  She suggested that they look 
at expanding that use.  She also questioned whether a teaching studio might also include recitals, 
which might be considered a public performance.  She suggested that they refine that language, 
because she would expect a teaching studio to have recitals.  She asked what is considered to be 
the work of a master.  Planner Milliner stated that language came from the Secretary of the 
Interior’s website.  Commissioner Peck suggested that, instead of using the word master, they 
say something like work that is distinctive in its field or significant in its field.  She commented 
that some of the language seems to be redundant. 
 
Commissioner DeFord asked what the term “significant” means and how they would determine 
whether something is significant.  Planner Milliner explained that Staff usually seeks information 
from the County Historian to help determine whether something is historically significant.  
Commissioner DeFord referred to Qualifying Provision XIII, which says that the change in use 
is necessary because the building cannot reasonably be used for its original intended use, and 
asked if someone would be unable to convert a house to an office use because it could still be

used as a home.  Planner Milliner replied that the applicant would have the burden of showing 
that it is not reasonable to use the structure for its original use.  He explained that they want to 
allow some limited uses in historic structures so they do not sit empty and eventually have to be 
demolished because of deterioration.  Commissioner DeFord recalled that the Planning 
Commission has not seen many applications for historic structures and wanted to be sure there is 
not a large inventory in the Snyderville Basin they might lose.  Planner Milliner commented that 
the Planning Commission is likely to see only one or two applications of this nature over the next 
several years.  Commissioner DeFord wanted to be certain that this is not so restrictive that they 
might lose historically significant structures.  Community Development Director Patrick Putt 
confirmed that will not be the case.  He clarified that they will not take away the ability to use an 
existing historic structure for existing purposes, and there are relatively few historic structures in 
the Snyderville Basin.  This ordinance is predicated on providing a reasonable level of incentive 
for someone to choose adaptive reuse to save a building and eliminate potential abuse by trying 
to develop the entire property beyond the original historic structure itself.  Commissioner 
DeFord asked if someone could demolish a historic structure.  Director Putt replied that they 
could, because the County does not have an ordinance that prohibits demolitions.  He 
commented that the County has been quite successful in preserving historic structures through 
public-private partnerships. 
 
Commissioner Lawson referred to Item 5 IX, and suggested changing the words “by 
considering” to “due to.” 
 
Commissioner Barnes reviewed the success of the Swaner Preserve and restoration of that 
structure with the ability to add uses to it.  He believed a deed restriction could be a negative in 
an adaptive re-use of a historic structure.  Director Putt explained that the deed restriction would 
assure that whatever benefit might go to the property owner or developer would not be lost 
through a radical change to the structure or a demolition.  Deed restrictions can be written to deal 
with the unique characteristics of the property and the structure, create a process for how future 
changes might be processed, and provide predictability.  Commissioner Barnes stated that he 
would like to be able to restrict it in such a way that the structure cannot ever be bulldozed.
