3.   Public hearing and possible action for potential amendments to Section 11-6 of 
the Eastern Summit County Development Code to create regulations relating to the 
preservation of historically significant structures. – Ray Milliner, Principal Planner  
 
Planner Milliner shared the background. The changes that are suggested for the historic 
structures section of the Code were to clarify changes made to Conditional Uses and clarify 
the definition of historic structures. Changes were also made to allow a restaurant to have 
an outside patio or deck not contained entirely within the historic structure.

Commissioner Wheaton asked for clarification on the patio/deck language if an outside 
sales rack would be precluded. Planner Milliner responded that the provision is not 
limited to just restaurants, so any store could have an outdoor sales area. 
Commissioner Sonntag asked if anyone would object to allowing the outdoor patio area to 
be enclosable in the winter so long as it does not detract from the historical character.

that analyzing additions on a case-by-case basis by requiring a low-impact permit is a

Commissioner Wheaton asked if disturbances to archaeological resources should not be 
allowed at all. Planner Milliner stated that a person could be digging and come across an 
archaeological resource and the State would investigate to mitigate what needs to be done 
with that resource without destroying the resource. Any resource shall be preserved in 
compliance with state and Federal law.

Findings of Fact: 
1. The purpose of the amendments is to provide owners of historic structures in Eastern Summit 
County with opportunities to establish an adaptive reuse in the building while limiting the 
impacts of the use on adjacent property owners.  
2. Goal 2.3. b of the Eastern Summit County General Plan states, “Consider development of a 
heritage preservation plan.”  
3. No Heritage Preservation plan has been adopted by Summit County.  
4. On June 7, 2018 the Eastern Summit County Planning Commission conducted a work session 
to discuss the possibility of creating a heritage preservation plan.  
5. The purpose of this language is to create incentives for owners of historically significant 
structures to use their property, with the end goal of preserving them. 
 
Conclusions of Law: 
1. The amendment is consistent with the goals, objectives, and policies of the General Plan.  
2. The amendment will not permit the use of land that is not consistent with the uses of 
properties nearby.  
3. The amendment will not permit the removal of the then existing restrictions which will unduly 
affect nearby property.  
4. The amendment will promote the public health, safety and welfare of the People of Eastern 
Summit County. 
 
Commissioner Sonntag made a motion to forward a positive recommendation for the

4.  Public hearing and possible action for potential amendments to Section 11-6 of 
the Eastern Summit County Development Code to create regulations relating to 
outdoor lighting of buildings and structures. – Ray Milliner, Principal Planner  
 
Planner Milliner shared the background. A presentation was made including examples of 
lighting causing disturbance to the dark skies in the County. There is still darkness that can 
be preserved in the County. Examples of full cutoff light fixtures were shown. The lighting 
ordinance would require full cutoff fixtures for new buildings. There is not a regulation 
being proposed to include warm versus cool lighting. Cool lighting is shown to be bad for 
human health and the health and mating of wildlife. Warm lighting is recommended. The 
lighting that will be prohibited includes flood lighting, architectural lighting, search lights, 
landscape lighting, up lighting, and blinking or flashing neon lights. The six rules of lighting 
from the Coalition of Night Skies was shared. 
Commissioner Sargent asked if there should be a standard for lumens, kelvin and wattage 
included in the Code. 65 watts, 4000 kelvins and 7000 lumens were suggested as standards 
found in research. There have been problems in Coalville because the standards were not 
in their Code.

included in new building. Commissioner Wilde argued that downlighting requirements

Commissioners Sonntag and Sargent argued that exterior lighting plans should be
