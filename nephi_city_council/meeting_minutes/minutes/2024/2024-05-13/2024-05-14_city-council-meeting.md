CONSENT AGENDA APPROVED:

minutes for the regular council meeting on 5-7-24 and a list of claims on the warrant register dated 5-
14-24. Councilor Parady seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

PUBLIC COMMENT:

  Mick Deschamps addressed the council about his interest in the planning of the park develpment on
the south end of the city. He suggested having pickleball courts at the park development. He stated
that our community has a lot of interest in pickleball.
  Steve Swan stated that he also supported the building of pickleball courts in the park development on
the city’s south end. He read aloud the goals stated in the Nephi City Recreation Master Plan. He
suggested that pickleball courts would be a great way to meet the objectives outlined in the master
plan. He stated that he had brain surgery a couple of years ago and that playing pickleball was a big part
of his recovery and regaining his eye-hand coordination.
   Donald Ball told those present that if any of them were interested in attending the Planning
Commission meeting, the meeting was held on the second Wednesday of every month.
  Councilor Parady asked how many participants were playing pickleball and where they were playing.
Mr. Deschamps answered that they play early in the morning at the Hive and at the churches and
sometimes they travel to Santaquin to play on their outside courts. Swan said that outside surfaces are
different than indoor surfaces.
   City Administrator Seth Atkinson said there has been talk of converting the horseshoe pits at Town
Square Park into pickleball courts.
   Councilor Parady said that the Crimson Flats development is probably a couple of years out and that
the city should work more on converting the horseshoe pits.

PARKS AND CEMETERIES DEPARTMENT 1990 BUCKET TRUCK DECLARED SURPLUS PROPERTY:
   City Arborist Gerratt Bethers had prepared a staff report stating that Altec in Salt Lake City had
inspected the bucket truck used by the Parks and Cemeteries Department to prune trees and install
Christmas lights. The inspection report stated the needed repairs to the truck were estimated to be
$30,000. Altec red-tagged the truck, making it unusable until the repairs were completed. Mr. Bethers
indicated in his staff report that the truck is now not worth fixing and that he and the Electrical
Department Director Rust Finlinson were in the process of looking for a replacement truck that both
departments could use and would like to sell the old truck at auction.
 Councilor Callaway moved to declare the 1990 bucket truck surplus property. Councilor Skip
Worwood seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.
