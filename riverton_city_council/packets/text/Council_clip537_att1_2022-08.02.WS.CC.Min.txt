38          Home Occupation Conditional Use Permit
39
40   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained the current home occupation
41   ordinance and exceptions.
42
43   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, said he had received an email that was sent from Commissioner
44   Cluff to Mayor Staggs. He said that a conditional use permit or home occupation is something
45   that can be done outside of the dwelling of the home. This must be designated and must go
46   through the approval process. He doesn’t see any part of the ordinance that would prohibit any
47   type of agricultural type home occupation.
48
49   Mr. Carter explained there is an application fee for a home occupation. This is a one-time fee,
50   not an annual fee. His biggest concern is short term traffic coming in and out of neighborhoods

16            Discussion of Eliminating Legal Nonconforming Use Rights Under Utah Code 10-
17            9a-511(2)(b) and Other Fee Corrections
18
19   Mayor Staggs explained that the Council has determined that pawn shops and title loan
20   businesses can only have one location per 10,000 residents and must be in commercial
21   regional zones. Some of these businesses are not in commercial regional zones but have been
22   grandfathered in, even though ownership has been transferred multiple times. Mayor Staggs
23   asked Mr. Carter to look into this problem and let the Council know if there are any options for
24   eliminating nonconforming use rights.
25
26   Mr. Carter explained the legislature had created a pathway to which cities can consider the
27   termination of legal nonconforming use rights. He gave the example of Provo City and its failure
28   to require owner occupancy for home rentals. Provo City created a formula to allow owners to
29   amortize out the properties that were not owner-occupied until they had to be.
30
31   Mr. Carter further explained that the formula for eliminating legal nonconforming use must be
32   sound and the calculation must give the benefit of the doubt to the property owner.
33
34   Mayor Staggs asked the Council to determine what uses and locations they would like to
35   amortize out. Mr. Carter recommended targeting nonconforming uses in the downtown area.
36   All Councilmembers agreed with this recommendation.

 8            Water Supply Issues – Report by Sherrie L. Ohrn of a proposed property tax
 9            Increase by Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District for Water Service Purposes
10
11   Sherrie L. Ohrn, Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District, reported that Jordan Valley Water
12   Conservancy will be having a tax increase this year and will have a truth in taxation hearing on
13   August 10 at 6 PM at the district offices. They will use the additional tax money to build new
14   infrastructure to accommodate the growth in the valley.
15
16   Ms. Ohrn said that residents need to start conserving water. She said the park strips and big
17   lawns need to go away. She asked that cities look at ordinances and standards to encourage
18   water conservation.
19
20   Mayor Staggs told Ms. Ohrn that Riverton City had adopted the water conservancy standards.
21   He also wanted the conservancy district to know that 100% of Riverton residents are connected
22   to secondary water and that the City has already installed 1/3 of the secondary water meters.
23
24   Councilmember Wells asked whether car washes recycle their water. Ms. Ohrn said that she
25   believes they do and that cities need to ensure their ordinances include water conservancy
26   standards for these types of uses.
27
28   Councilmember McDougal expressed frustration for the water conservancy messaging. He said
29   there is water for the existing population but not for the increased growth. He believes the
30   infrastructure costs should be passed on to the new growth. Ms. Ohrn said she will pass this
31   information on to the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District.
