     PRESENTATION – Central Corridor Transit Study

Mr. Eric Rasband of UDOT Region 3 stated he wanted to first thank Jamie Davidson for taking a
leadership role on the Central Corridor Transit Study from the beginning and being able to gain
support from 7 cities and worked on building a coalition which has made this project easy and fun.
He went on to introduce those non-municipality members that are also working on the project. He
continued to review the background for transit improvements form Lehi to Provo. He indicated
this started a little over a year ago and discussed who all was involved. He continued to explain
the preferred alternative route and some of the design options they have due to different rail
companies. This will be a rapid bus system from Lehi, tying into the frontrunner in Provo.
Vineyard was really the only community that did not have a connection to the system so they are
working on a branch at 800 north and State Street in Orem that will connect to the Vineyard area.
Generally speaking, they have a high level of support from all communities involved. They are
also looking at a link from the Lehi area north to the new development in the prison area. He
continued to discuss some of the proposed connections of other areas and communities within the

study area. He presented some design aspects of the cross sections and right-of-way’s. He
indicated they feel they have had some great public input, receiving about 750 overall comments
indicating residents feel the connections are important were well received. What they have left to
do is to continue to make this presentation to other City Councils, engineering work, cost estimates
and a few more open houses but feel they can close this project out come November. Ms. Mary
DeLamMare-Schaefer of UTA stated how thankful they are for Jamie and his drive to work with
his colleagues in getting this off the ground and all the work Mr. Rasband has done. UTA will
continue this partnership in moving towards construction.

Mayor Burnst asked how long the environmental study would take and how the funding estimate
will carried forward. Mr. Rasband indicated Zions Bank is helping with the financial plan and
they will have more details on the financing at their next executive meeting. He indicated that this
will not be a cheap project being over 20 mile in length and will be a big lift for them. The
environmental study will take 2-3 years. Ms. DeLamMare-Schaefer added they have seen the
importance for those using this type of service even with the decline and rise due to Covid.

Terry Peterson inquired how it would look down State Street and if they would lose any travel
lanes. Mr. Rasband showed the cross sections and explained the proposed three lanes of travel,
would include in some places some of the shoulder and the turn lane for the buses. He indicated
it would be similar to the current University Parkway. There may be some adjustments they will
need to make based on existing buildings.

Jamie stated it is important to reinforce that the city anticipated this expanded cross section when
they put together the State Street Master Plan, so they are not going to put themselves in the
situation where they are going to construct new buildings and place them too close to the right of
way. In addition to that they also anticipated future utility infrastructure as part of that plan as
well. They are doing their best to preserve the right-of-way in a way were expansion of the right-
of-way if necessary, can work while at the same time allowing businesses and uses along the State
Street corridor to continue to grow and develop in a way they need to.

Discussion continued regarding UDOT’s commitment to the 6 lanes of traffic and current funding
for planter dividers for safety reasons that may get in the way of future improvement of State Street
and possible disruption to local businesses.

Mr. Davidson indicated by the time they get through the environmental process, engineering
process, bid process and the financing process in order to afford the project he indicated the best-
case scenario is probably 5-7 years. Some of the improvements currently planned along State
Street are being done for safety reasons and need to be done immediately. This is something they
hope will come to pass if all the pieces come together.

Further discussion continued regarding the cross sections and how it would impact on street
parking.

Mayor Brunst thanked those that participated and stated they look forward to future information
on this project.

Mr. Ken Ransom stated the City of Orem initially received a grant for $411,983. With current
allocations totaling $209,950. The city will receive additional allocations but will not be received
until the end of the year at which time the commission with make further recommendations. The
funds must be used for corona virus related issues and the city has four priorities which were:
     Projects serving Orem residents
     First Responder support
     Mental Health
     Domestic Violence

Geneva Elementary has requested $4,500 to assist with Plexiglass dividers for the lunchroom
tables to help with social distancing. The commission has recommended the council approve this
allocation to Geneva Elementary.

Mr. Macdonald inquired as to why the city was funding a school district expenditure.

Ms. Mathews indicated the Alpine School District did not receive as much funding for Covid
related issues as the city did, and they are out of funds for items needed due to Covid-19.

Mr. Downs stated the CDBC Covid funding has very limited uses and sometimes it can be
challenging for the city to find uses for those funds. This school is a Title I school which they feel
is a hot area for Covid and they feel it is a way they can partner with the school district in a way
to serve the residents of Orem and their families in these areas.

Mayor Brunst indicated this is funding used for Orem residents and feels this is a good use of
Covid funds.

Mr. Ransom continued to state the commission recommends allocating $197,532.66 to public
services. He continued to discuss some of the future recommendations for the funding to be
received in the future.

       RESOLUTION – Designating 1200 West Street as Wolverine Way and 1200 West

Mr. Bench stated Utah Valley University requests the City Council rename 1200 West as
Wolverine Way and 1200 West. 1200 West provides access to UVU’s main Orem campus from
both Center Street and University Parkway. 1200 West Street from 800 South to the Lindon border
is a public street and the portion from 800 South to University Parkway is a private street owned

City Council Minutes – October 13, 2020 (p.6)    A complete video of the meeting can be found at www.orem.org/meetings
by Utah Valley University. Staff believes that renaming 1200 West to Wolverine Way will
promote community pride and spirit and will create a positive sense of place in accordance with
the Facility and Street Naming Policy that was previously adopted by the City Council. If approved
by the City Council, the 1200 West street signs will be changed to Wolverine Way with the
coordinate address of 1200 West remaining below the descriptive name.

Mr. Sumner inquired why they were continuing the naming up to 1600 North. Mr. Bench
responded that part of the reasoning was that if part of the street from Center Street south is
Wolverine Way and from Center Street north it is 1200 West they felt it may cause some confusion
as far as directions and simplicity and felt the entire way would be more consistent. The street
sign will continue to have the address of 1200 West under the name just as the city policy requires.

Tom Macdonald moved, by resolution, Designate 1200 West Street as Wolverine Way and 1200
West. Terry Peterson seconded the motion. Those voting aye: Richard F. Brunst, Jeff Lambson,
Debby Lauret, Tom Macdonald, Terry Peterson, and David Spencer. Those voting nay: Brent
Sumner. The motion passed.
