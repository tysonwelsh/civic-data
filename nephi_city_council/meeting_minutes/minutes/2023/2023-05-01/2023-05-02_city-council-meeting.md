CONSENT AGENDA APPROVED:

unanimous vote.

NO PUBLIC COMMENT

FY 2024-25 TENTATIVE BIENNIAL BUDGET APPROVED:

   City Administrator Seth Atkinson reviewed with the council the FY 2024-25 Tentative Biennial Budget.
The budget contained the priorities listed by the City Council for both general services and the
utility/enterprise funds. He explained that two new funds, the Hive Fund and the Fleet Fund, were
formed this past year. The Hive Fund is for the new recreational center shared by the Juab School
District. The Fleet Fund is an internal service fund to track accounts for the sale, purchase, and leasing
of administrative vehicles.
    Mr. Atkinson reviewed the following assumptions for the budget:
              A 4.5% cost-of-living adjustment in wages in both years to keep up with high inflation
                 reports for the area.
              Market adjustments in wages for some positions.
              No property tax increases
              $0.25 fee increase in the Public Safety Fee to replenish the Capital Project account that
                 had been saving to pay the capital fee charged to the city by Central Utah 911. These
                 funds were used to pay for new radios that will be required when the state changes
                 their communications system statewide to a new technology next year. Funding from
                 the county did not materialize and so savings needed to be used to pay for these radios.
                 In addition, costs have increased for Central Utah 911 and this fee increase will help to

 Councilor Memmott asked about the City’s plans for road maintenance and road improvements. Mr.
Atkinson said that there are line items in the budget that are going to be used for road improvements
and street rehabilitation. These two line items are increasing each year and as soon as the city can get a
pavement maintenance schedule from either hiring an engineering firm to prepare that schedule or use
a program from Utah State University called L-Tap, where graduate students survey the entire city and
give GIS information about the condition of all of the city streets and then use that data to put together
a pavement maintenance schedule work can begin on rehabilitating and improving the city’s streets.
   Councilor Worwood moved to adopt the Tentative Budget for the fiscal year 2024-25. Councilor
Callaway seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.

40-YEAR WATER RIGHTS PLAN ADOPTED:

   Mr. Atkinson presented the final draft of the 40-Year Water Rights Plan prepared by Sunrise
Engineering. He stated that Sunrise had added the Worwood Well as part of the inventory on page 11.
Last work session Councilor Ostler had noticed that the Worwood Well had been left out of the plan.
Councilor Memmott moved to accept the 40-Year Water Right Plan. Councilor Worwood seconded the
motion. The motion passed unanimously.
  Mr. Atkinson also mentioned that the state approved that our water rates were changed from a flow
rate to an acre foot.

CITY COUNCIL AND CITY ADMINISTRATIVE UPDATES:

   Mr. Atkinson reported that Public Works Director Kyle Marchant and himself have been monitoring
the flow rates of the Hollow and there are no significant problems. The Street Department employees
have been working very hard to clear the debris from the Hollow and the grate up the canyon. The
culverts are handling the water flow.
   Mr. Atkinson reported that he will be giving the State of the City address at the Chamber meeting on
June 1st at the Holiday Inn and invited the council to attend.
  Councilor Callaway reported that he has started working on the Mammoth Parade. He asked the
council if they had any ideas for a Grand Marshall for the parade to please get him those names in the
next couple of weeks.
   Councilor Callaway also mentioned that some residents had asked him about the striping of the
highway out by the hospital. Mr. Atkinson said that UDOT will be doing striping soon.
    Councilor Memmott said that there is a sink hole up by the Bart Jackson home.
    Mayor Seely mentioned that there had been complaints about speeding on 400 East. He has spoken
to the Chief about the problem and the Chief said he would have his officers patrol the area.
    Mayor Seely also mentioned that Seth had helped some sister cities with some budgeting questions.
    Mayor Seely informed the council that the Community Night Out will be held on June 5th at the City
Park from 5:30 to 7:00 p.m.
    Mayor Seely said that a Pump Track Committee has been formed and they are working on that.
    Councilor Parady reminded the Council about two ribbon cuttings to be held on Friday.

Noting no need for an Executive Session, the Council moved into Work Session. 8:00 p.m.

Work Session:

1. Mr. Atkinson reviewed some definition changes in the Land Use Code that the Planning Commission
   has been working on. Councilor Worwood asked about the definition of “Family”. City Attorney
   Wright offered some clarification. Mr. Atkinson said that the Planning Commission is working on
   repealing the Zero-Lot-Line overlay and incorporating twin homes into the R-2 zone. The council
   directed staff to bring back the changes in ordinance form at some point.
