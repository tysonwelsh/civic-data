G. ACTION ITEMS:
   1. REPORT FROM THE CHILDREN’S JUSTICE CENTER (CJC) ON VICTIMS OF CRIME ACT GRANT (VOCA)
          Roberta Dustin, from the CJC, reported that the new grant funding of about $243,000 will be primarily spent for
          client services, staff salaries/benefits, etc. They supplied 240 hours of direct therapy for children in the last quarter.
    2. CONTRACT WITH STATE OF UTAH FOR CJC GENERAL OPERATION ALLOCATION AMENDMENT #7 TO UT CONTRACT #160230
          Roberta Dustin, from the CJC, stated that allocation increased by $8,000 this year. They had underspent on the
          budget but had another area where they could defer about $25,000, which the State allowed.
          Commissioner Harvey moved to approve the contract with the State of Utah for CJC general operation allocation
          Amendment #7 to State contract #160230; Commissioner Jenkins seconded.
          Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Froerer – aye
    3. AMENDMENT #3 TO CONTRACT #180252 WITH THE STATE OF UTAH FOR FORENSIC INTERVIEWER EXPENSES
          Roberta Dustin, from the CJC, stated that this contract pays primarily for a fulltime forensic interviewer and does
          some match to VOCA funding for a part time forensic interviewer. It is the same amount as last year.
          Commissioner Jenkins moved to approve Amendment #3 to Contract #180252 with the State of Utah for forensic
          interviewer expenses; Commissioner Harvey seconded.
          Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Froerer – aye
    4. CONTRACT WITH KNIGHT IRRIGATION GROUP ON THE 12TH STREET PROJECT ALLOWING KNIGHT
       IRRIGATION GROUP TO PERFORM SEGMENT 3 TO AVOID IMPACTS TO THE IRRIGATION SYSTEM
          Gary Myers, of County Engineering, stated that one of the change orders on the project eliminated some work
          that the county’s contractor was going to do and allowed Knight Irrigation to install their own system. A portion
          of their irrigation facility crosses under 12th Street that serves some of their customers.
          Commissioner Jenkins moved to approve the contract with Knight Irrigation Group on the 12th Street project allowing
          Knight Irrigation Group to perform Segment 3 to avoid impacts to irrigation system; Commissioner Harvey seconded.
          Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Froerer – aye
    5. INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT WITH SOUTH OGDEN COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT AND RENEWAL AGENCY FOR
       THE CITY CENTER COMMUNITY REINVESTMENT PROJECT AREA
          Holin Wilbanks, of County Economic Development, via Zoom meeting, stated that this item has been worked on
          for several months, that it overlaps the City’s opportunity zone, and is an opportunity for the City to promote
          residential and commercial growth. The contract was reviewed by the county’s finance committee (including the
          Comptroller, Attorney’s Office). It is at 75% participation rate over a 20-year period with a $4 million cap.
         Matt Dixon, of South Ogden, said that the area is between about 36th and 40th Streets, Riverdale Road and
         Washington Blvd., and had been on the City’s radar for many years to find ways to revitalize it. He said that there
         is a high number of people who daily leave the county for employment and one lofty goal for this mixed use area
         (housing/commercial/retail) is to have up to 100,000 sq. ft. of new commercial office and to employ people here.
         Commissioner Jenkins moved to approve the Interlocal Agreement with South Ogden Community Development and
         Renewal Agency for the City Center Community Reinvestment Project Area; Commissioner Harvey seconded.
         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Froerer – aye
    6. CONTRACT WITH SUNRISE ENGINEERING, INC., TO PROVIDE ENGINEERING SERVICES TO DESIGN A STORM DRAIN OUTFALL
         Ashley Thoman, of County Engineering, stated that this contract resulted from a request for proposals. It is to
         take water out of the Eden Acres Subdivision area where there have been flooding issues, and discharge it to a
         more acceptable location. County Engineering had spoken with the county surveying department, which has
         agreed to perform the surveying services; this will help save money. They plan is for County Engineering to
         perform some outreach/custom engineering approaches and for the consultant to handle more of the design
         analysis to also save money. He addressed Commissioner Jenkins’ question stating that it is not so much flooding
         from the development that causes the issue but runoff from surrounding areas and the complexity with the area
         irrigation ditches. County stormwater impact fees will pay for this. Chair Froerer said that the issue is not from
         a particular subdivision but the hillside from Wolf Creek to the Browning ranch area, which then wraps around
         the Middle Fork area and impacts all of those subdivisions due to winter sheet flow impacts from the ice melt.
         Commissioner Jenkins moved to approve the contract with Sunrise Engineering, Inc., to provide engineering
         services to design a storm drain outfall; Commissioner Harvey seconded.
         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Froerer – aye
    7. FINAL APPROVAL OF FENSTER FARMS PHASE 2 3 SUBDIVISION, A NINE-LOT SUBDIVISION, INCLUDING
       ACCEPTANCE OF A PUBLICLY DEDICATED ROAD (560 NORTH ST.)
         Regarding the typo on the phase number, the information had been uploaded to the county’s website correctly.
         Tammy Aydelotte, of the County Planning Division, stated that this item includes creation of a couple of new county
         roads and continuation of an existing county road. It includes an improvement agreement for about $85,000.
         Commissioner Harvey moved to grant final approval of Fenster Farms Phase 3 Subdivision, 9-lots, including acceptance
         of a publicly dedicated road and improvement agreement with Peter B Development; Commissioner Jenkins seconded.
         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Froerer – aye
    8. POLICY GOVERNING DISTRIBUTION OF CARES ACT FUNDS TO SMALL BUSINESSES UNDER WEBER CARES GRANT PROGRAM
         Christopher Crockett, Deputy County Attorney, stated that as discussed last week, the State received federal funds
         and distributed a portion to counties and municipalities to cover necessary expenditures/losses due to COVID-19.
         He read the first three Policy sections which included that our county’s action is a permitted use of these federal
         dollars to reimburse certain small businesses. It was important as the county established the Weber CARES Grant
         Program that the process was outlined for receiving and objectively evaluating applications according to statute.
         Commissioner Jenkins moved to approve the policy governing the distribution of CARES Act Funds to small
         businesses under the Weber CARES Grant Program; Commissioner Harvey seconded.
         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Froerer – aye
    9. CONTRACT WITH WASATCH WIGEONS ASSOCIATION TO PROVIDE WATERFOWL NEST STRUCTURES, PREDATOR
       MANAGEMENT & REMOVAL OF INVASIVE SPECIES AT KINGFISHER WETLANDS AREA OF OBSERVATORY PARK
      Todd Ferrario, County Ice Sheet and Parks & Recreation Division Director, presented the renewal of this stewardship
      agreement at no cost to the county. Wasatch Wigeons, a non-profit, desires to promote conservation efforts for the
      benefit of waterfowl and wildlife in the Kingfisher Wetlands area. Commissioner Harvey said that this is a great
      group that cares about water fowl protection and Mr. Ferrario concurred, expressing his appreciation for their work.
      Commissioner Harvey moved to approve the contract with Wasatch Wigeons Association to provide waterfowl
      nest structures, predator management and removal of invasive species at the Kingfisher wetlands area of
      Observatory Park; Commissioner Jenkins seconded.
      Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Froerer – aye
