Public Hearing

            THIS BEING THE TIME heretofore set for a public hearing to receive public
comment regarding the following requests:

Application #31170 – Spencer Pack requesting approval of a zone change from R-1-6
(Residential) to R-1-3 (Single Family Residential) on property, located at 3027 South 9150 West.

                Ms. Diana Martinez, planner, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District
(GSLMSD) reviewed the application stating the property is just a couple blocks off Magna Main
to the south and in an area where the lots are smaller than typical zoning requires, which basically
means they are non-conforming lot sizes. This lot is a result of property being illegally subdivided.
It is very narrow and deep. The main difference between the R-1-6 to R-1-3, aside from the front
and rear yard setbacks, is the lot size, which goes from 6,000 square feet to 3,000 square feet.
The applicant is asking for a rezone to build a single-family dwelling on the property. The
GSLMSD Planning Division has been working with the applicant and thinks he can fit a home on
the property, although it will be hard to fit parking on the property.

The Planning Commission did not like the smaller lot size, but understood that nothing could be
done with the lot if it was not rezoned because it would not meet the 6,000 square feet requirement
and it did not qualify for any variances because it was created illegally. The Planning Commission
recommended that if the Council approved the zone change, it put two conditions on the property:
1) that a single-family dwelling was the only thing that could be built on the property; 2) that this
was an unusual circumstance and the rezone would not set a precedent for the area. The
Planning staff is okay with those conditions.

                 Mayor Peay asked how the property got rezoned illegally.

              Ms. Martinez stated it did not get rezoned illegally; it got split illegally. When
people separate their property, two legal descriptions are created. In this case, the Recorder’s
Office recorded it without having a review done by the Planning Division or the Surveyor’s Office.
This happens all the time and it is very difficult to have to tell an owner they cannot develop their
property because it is not a legal lot.

                 Council Member Prokopis asked when it was illegally separated.

              Ms. Martinez stated she thought it had been separated within the last 10 years. If
it had been done in in the 60s or 70s, it would have been legal.

                Mr. Spencer Peck, applicant, stated he purchased the lot with the intention of
building a single-family home on it, and was frustrated to find out it had been subdivided illegally.
The property is narrow, but deep. He was told there would have to be a minimum setback width
of 20 feet from each neighbor to the north and south. He found some modular home builders who
could build a home on as narrow as 15 feet and thought he could get a nice-looking house on the
property. There are other homes in the area on small lot sizes. On 9100 West going north, there
are two lots that are .08 that have homes on them, and then there are two more lots south of his

property that are .10 or .11. He asked the Council for its approval of the zone change based on

              Council Member Peel asked if this was going to be a personal use home, or if

                  Council Member Hull stated she agreed with Ms. VanRooosendaal in some ways;
however, she did not know what else the applicant could do with that property. If the property just
sits there, it will get weedy and be worse than having a small house on it.

              Mayor Peay stated if people get away with this, it will keep happening. He was
opposed to the rezone.

               Council Member Hull suggested talking to the Recorder to get these illegal splits
stopped.

               Council Member Peel stated he agreed there were problems and the buyer
should have been aware, but he did know how the Council could deny the applicant’s request to
build on his property, which was subdivided illegally 10 years ago. He was inclined to approve
the request with the two conditions.

                Paul Ashton stated the second condition has no weight. It could not bind a future
Council, so it would set a precedent. The Council can just go on record saying it will not do this
again in the future.

                 Council Member Prokopis stated there are some blighted parcels in that area,
which he thought the Council would have cleaned up in the future, and he wanted to find out
exactly how the property got illegally subdivided. However, he would consider this a test case to
see if the final product ended up being something the Council would want to do again.

              Ms. Martinez asked the applicant whether he was under contract on the property
or had taken possession of it.

               Mr. Pack stated he owned the property outright and had a vested interest in it.

              Council Member Pierce stated her concern was the space did not allow for very
much parking. She asked how neighbors felt about a house being so close to their property lines.

               Ms. Martinez stated all neighbors within 300 feet of this subject property were
noticed. She got one call from a neighbor who just wanted to know what was going on. She had
no opposition to it because she did not live close enough to be impacted. If this rezone is
approved, the applicant will have to go through the subdivision process to make it a legal lot, and
then submit his plan for approval. As to a precedent, putting an R-1-3 in the middle of an R-1-6
would have been spot zoning many years ago, so it is a precedent.

               Mr. Ashton stated he did not necessarily see this as spot zoning because it was
an issue that had to be dealt with, and it was not the only place in Magna that had small lots. The
Council ought to talk to the Recorder’s Office to make sure this does not happen in the future. It
would be good to get an inventory of how many of these types of lots exist, especially in the area
around Main Street.

               Mayor Peay stated adding another house will mean there will be more cars on the
street.

               Council Member Peel asked what the frontage was on the property.

              Ms. Martinez stated the R-1-6 frontage has to be 30 feet, and the R-1-3 has to be
20 feet. The R-1-3 width changes too from 60 feet to 20 feet at a distance of 20 feet.

               Council Member Hull, seconded by Council Member Pierce, moved to approve
Application #31170, with the conditions that it can only be a single-family use and that it is a
special exception. The motion passed 4 to 1, with Mayor Peay voting in opposition.

Reports from Municipal Service Providers

Unified Police Department (UPD)

               Chief Jason Ackerman stated he sent out the quarterly report for the last April
through June. The numbers are a little off because of COVID, in that detectives were working
from home to ensure their safety and some things were put on hold, such as search warrants and
drug cases. The County Jail was releasing low level offenders because of COVID, so it was
counterproductive to pursue search warrants considering those offenders would unlikely be
booked into jail.

               Council Member Hull asked if there has been a decrease in crime with Covid,
aside from the protesting and rioting.

                Chief Ackerman stated cases were just put on hold, but detectives have now
started working on those. He announced the Magna Precinct lost Lt. Levi Hughes who is now the
new chief of the Kearns Precinct. The Magna Precinct has not filled that allocation yet, but it did
move a sergeant over from the east side to take on some of those responsibilities. Also,
Taylorsville City has announced that it is departing the UPD, effective July 1, 2021. The UPD is
trying to figure out how it will right-size the department and trying to gauge how many current
vacancies it will need to fill without overextending itself. It will be losing detectives, so it is trying
to determine which areas it does not need to preserve. There will also be a decrease in pooled
services.

              Council Member Peel asked what was enticing communities to pull away from
the UPD since the point of it was to share resources.

              Chief Ackerman stated the biggest driving force for Herriman and Riverton to
leave was that they were paying more for pooled services than they were actually getting out.

                                                    5
DATE            TUESDAY                                   JULY                              14, 2020

Taylorsville is a very high user of pooled services, so its motivation is more about control. It has
been advocating for more aggressive compensation packages

               Council Member Hull stated she understood Midvale City was looking to withdraw
too.

                Chief Ackerman stated Midvale started a feasibility study to see what it would
take to start its own police department, but there was not enough support from its Council.
However, its Council did give guidance to continue collecting data for a feasibility study, which left
the door open. The reason Midvale is considering that is because it wants more control over what
officers get, but it is more conservative than Taylorsville.

              Council Member Hull asked if Chief Ackerman was concerned the UPD might not
be sustainable with cities leaving.

                 Chief Ackerman stated with every departure and reduction, there is a loss of the
benefit. The UPD is feeling the effects now from the budget cuts last year. It still has to provide
services. The UPD will have to make up for the money it is losing from Taylorsville withdrawing,
and it will be significant. However, services will not be interrupted because Taylorsville is a high
user of those services.

               Council Member Hull asked if the UPD was recruiting other cities to join.

                Chief Ackerman stated the UPD has not been actively recruiting. A lot of cities
are just waiting to see what the UPD is going to look like; they are not looking to join the UPD.

               Mayor Peay asked if there were any other updates for the Council.

               Chief Ackerman stated the Magna Precinct is adding additional officers and right-
sizing its patrol and resources. It has eliminated one of its property crime detectives and
consolidated that duty, so was able to add a juvenile crime detective to address a lot of the
targeted problems in the community. The UPD is also working hand-in-hand with the Magna
Coalition, and has created a code enforcement supplement, which will be dedicated to working
with the Salt Lake County Health Department and code enforcement to target a lot of the vacant
houses and nuisances that are happening on a regular basis, and looking for some long-term
solutions.

               Council Member Peel asked what happened across the street from the mortuary,
with the 15 patrol cars there.

               Chief Ackerman stated an officer saw a wanted felon in a car and began following
him. Then, the fugitive stopped and got out of his vehicle and fled. Officers pursued him and
were able to locate and apprehend him.

Magna Water District

DATE           TUESDAY                                 JULY                              14, 2020


               Clint Dilley, General Manager, Magna Water District, stated he took over as
General Manager in June and began working on three priorities. First, he filled several long-term
vacancies, including service maintenance technicians, hired a new water operations manager
with 40 years of operations experience, and is working towards improving the front office
operations and customer service, looking at economical ways to do inhouse inspections and
improve communication, which included expanding emergency contact information and updating
the website to alert customers of emergencies.

The water district has a Recreational Vehicle (RV) dump on 2100 South by its wastewater
treatment plant, which has historically been open 24 hours 7 days a week. Those hours have
changed to Monday-Thursday from 7:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. because he did not have enough staff
to manage that. That will allow staff to better monitor the RV dump to prevent illegal discharges.
If someone is unable to dump at the free dump, there are two other 24-hour dumps in Magna at
the Holiday Oils on 8400 West and on 7200 West that charge a small fee.

He thanked the Council for helping with the boiler alert and some excavation regarding the
cemetery and asked what the Council’s relationship was with the cemetery board.

              Council Member Peel stated the Council’s legal counsel is looking at what the
Council can legally do with the board and how that works with the water district.

Unified Police Department (UPD)



                                                7
DATE           TUESDAY                                  JULY                              14, 2020

                Council Member Prokopis stated the UPD board approved the budget, with a 2
percent increase for employees. Salaries for officers are still out of market. The UPD is hoping
to do a mid-year adjustment and will be addressing it again in November. One city wants to fix
pay inequities in one year to the tune of a 10 percent increase to each member. Other cities are
on a shoestring budget and feel they are being dragged along. He thought that was the case
between Taylorsville and Midvale, wherein Taylorsville wanted to fix things in one budget year,
but Midvale wanted to take things slower.

The interlocal agreement allows any entity to leave with twelve months’ notice, and that is
problematic. The UPD board wants to change the interlocal agreement to require a five-year
commitment at a minimum when joining the UPD, and maybe include a five-year exit clause. It
is difficult to do a budget because the board does not know which entities will be with it in two
years. He understood why Herriman and Riverton left the UPD, as they were paying a lot for
pooled services, but had relatively low crime compared to some of the other users of services.
However, prior to March, the economy was soaring, and every city had cash and revenues. If the
economy stays the way it is now and is slow to recover, cities will start to see budget shortfalls.
Then, the unified model will look more attractive for both police and fire services.
